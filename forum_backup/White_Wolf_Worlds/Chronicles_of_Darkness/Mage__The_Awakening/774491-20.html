        My personal thoughts on the Mage 2E.

        1.) The reach system is inspired, and is really cool.
        2.) Supernal Summoning. Lots of old myths and stories have magic-users commanding, summonings, and generally dealing with spirits and supernatural entities; you have the idea of demon summoning, the wizards summoning the djinn in arabian folklore, countless myths about invoking and calling down higher powers such as gods and spirits, and that's just scratching the surface. Any plenty of modern fiction about magic users has them invoking spirits and magical creatures as a regular thing, or even as the source of their power. (See the Bartimaeus trilogy, or Jonathan Strange and Mr. Norrell and that's just off the top of my head.) Mage: The awakening 1E never really was able to capture the feel of that "mages are people who deal with spirits and magical creatures" really well. Or rather, they could capture the feel of that well, but it wasn't flexible about it. Mages could be shamans and mediums, but they couldn't really be summoners.

        Let me explain. The Sprits of the nWoD, while very cool and works really well for the setting, tend to end up feeling somewhat limited in conceptual space. Summoners opened up the door somewhat, but never shook that problem of feel. Spirits were cool, giving Spirit magic (and the nWoD) it's own dark, creepy, alien feel. They made being a lowercase-s shaman be cool and dangerous, making breathing life in the idea of animist summoning, and making it feel part of the world of darkness. Ghosts were the same way, feeling creepy and alien and dangerous while still being ghosts. When Summoners brought up astral summoning, White wolf also did a really good job of making astral entities that felt odd and dangerous and inhuman but also like things of the soul and of the mind. And to people who wanted to be shamans, or deal speak with the dead, or futz around in the collective unconscious, those thing worked great.

        But none of those were demons. None of those felt like demons. You couldn't be a demon-summoner, or a man who called up the djinn to do serve him, a man dealt with fairies and the fey, or a man who invoked angels for strength and guidance. You couldn't call up the primal (but far from savage or animalistic) spirit guides of some Native american myths, you couldn't invoke the beaurucratic spirits and demons like the ones of Chinese and Bhuddist myth, or spirits like the ones of Japanese Shinto. You can't stand upon the banks of the river and scream out to the grim reaper, or the God of the Dead, or the judge of the Afterlife, to deal with him to bring your loved one back to life- or you could, but he had to be some spirit of death, or some thing of the Underworld, or the Astral spirit of death. Not something else, something magical, something mysterious.

        But with Supernal Summoning? Now you can. You can be whatever kind of summoner you want. Spirits of the shadow are free to be creepy animism spirits now, ghosts can be ghosts, and astral spirits can be the world inside all of our heads, and all those sorts of things are still playable. But Supernal spirits can be flexible and mysterious in a way none of those others can, magical in a way none of those others can match. They can be strange and weird and but still belong to magic, that mysterious supernal world, and be mysterious and strange in turn. It's made the supernal world closer in the coolest way.

        And it's simple. 3 dots, any any mage can summon these creatures. Imagine what that means. You don't have to build your character around summoning to being a summoner. You don't have to be a summoner to summon strange spirits, and deal with these things. You can send out spirits on missions to help you, do things, and still be able to do tons of other crap. I think it makes mages feel so much more like the sorcerers of myth, being able to do that. Mages could never really summon creatures of magic- their own magic- in 1E. It's given a spot for "mages as summoners of magical otherworldly creatures" a way to exist in the framework of the game that 1E never quite did.

        And imagine if something like 30 or 40 mages gathered together. Demons and totems and little angels running all over the place, serving their masters. Any place where you see a lot of mages gather or work is not just going to have people that do magic, but creatures that are magical serving them. Combine with the idea of the long term nimbus, and any place where mages gather and be mages is going to feel magical, full of strange otherworldly signs and otherworldly beings. It's cool.

        Before, mages could be mediums and modern day shaman and strange psychics. Now, they can also be summoners who deal with creatures of magic. And that's awesome.

        3.) There's a stronger, clearer idea of what the practices are about, what the arcanum are, and what they can do, especially at low levels. I like this a lot, since it makes it so much easier to make new spells, and gives so much more an idea of what the arcanum can do. In 1E, compelling and ruling were sort of ill-defiend this way. But with 2E, whe have much better examples to work from as starting point. With matter Compelling can cause water to flow downhill towards the character. Ruling can make it flow up hill, into strange shapes, and into any way you want. Mind compelling? You can make a person do something they were inclined to do anyway- and that fits nicely into the "emotional urge" they had as ruling in Mage 1E, where you can make people feel an emotion of your choosing, regardless of inclination. So another compelling spell would be to make people sad if that were at a funeral of someone they knew, while another ruling spell could be to make a guy go for a bite to eat even if he isn't hungry.

        Also, people now get cooler stuff at low levels in some arcana that lacked them. Space 2 Ruling lets you blip somewhere you couldn't because of an obstruction. So Space 1 lets you teleport anywhere you could get too normally. Don't feel like climbing? Blip, and you're there. The expansion of time to the sense of time means time compelling spells can actually do something, and at ruling you can make time go faster or slower, which was something that wasn't in the corebook of 1E.

        In addition, putting Truth and revelations under Prime's purview gives quite a bit more flexibility with it. Prime 2 Ruling, you make someone believe something you believe is true and that is objectively true, no matter what. So Prime 1 compelling could do the same thing, but on a person who might of believed you anyway. Prime 1 knowing turns you into a lie detector than works even if they don't know they're lying, going by the same conditions of truth under the ruling spell from the spoiler.
        ​
        Veiling spells have similarly been explained. In the "creative thaumurtagy" spoiler, they gave a couple of examples? With time, you can make a person loose all sense of time. Which means you can do all sort of tricks with it. With matter? Make a building impossible to notice.

        So you could make a person invisible with forces, since you're using the light...or you could use life, since he's a living being you're hiding. You can make a building hard to notice with matter (since it's an object), or you could do it with mind (since you're using mind-tricks to hide the building). Space veiling can make an area hard to notice or remember. Time veiling can give people fits of lost time, by concealing that period of time. Super lying skills? Prime 2, hide the truth. Or misdirect, using the truth to life. Fate 2 Veiling let's you hide that you've broken an agreement, if fate 1 knowing lets you detect if someone's has fulfilled one.

        I remeber once that Stephen Lea Shepard analyzied the capabilities of mages by path, to get an idea of what they could do at each level. So that's what I'm going to do, just with more speculation and less systimatically.

        So, in 2E, In addition to what they could do from 1E,
        Acanthus can cause a person to loose all sense of time, and probably mess with people's sense of time in other ways as well. And that's in addition to what they could do before.
        They can also speed time up and slow it down.
        They can know when promises are fulfilled, and probably influence promises that way.
        They can stop Damage over Time effects.
        And that in addition to all the fate-fiddling and minor prophecy they could do before.

        In 2E,
        Mastigos can cause people or animals to do something that they would normally do, like eat if they're hungry, and can make them do/feel things they wouldn't be normally inclined to do, causing impulses, thoughts, emotions and the like.
        They can also teleporting/bypassing an obstruction on a local level, and presumably bamf about like cartoon characters, using compelling spells to blip to places they could go normally (so easy local teleports) and Ruling spells to achive a "line of sight teleport" effect to warp to spaces they can see, but too far away to get to. You'd probably require reach to get to places like the top of a hill you can't climb, or places that are too far away to get too and have some other sort of obstruction like a fence.
        I can easily also imagine a zoom in effect that is a sensory version of scrying. ("You see that dot on the horizon? Now you see it better".

        In 2e,
        Obrimos can get people to believe the truth when they know it and are right, and probably act as living lie detectors and conceal truths on top of that.
        They can also futz around with any forces- light, heat, fire, gravity, motion- etc. to the exent of their power, right from the start. So they can probably make gravity pull more at lower levels, and definitely turn it off entirely by 3 dots.

        In 2e,
        Moros can make buildings and objects hard to notice. Or make them invsible. The could also probably move crap through telekineses, to a certain extent (if they can make water flow uphill, they can make air flow anywhere, they can make things with wheels roll uphill, and with enough reach they can probably throw around inanimate objects like a Obrimos can throw around anything else.)

        In 2E,
        Thryusus can change their appearance, and can probably hide people in any sorts of ways. Thrysus can also command people and animals like a mind mage could do with compelling and ruling, but wouldn't be able to alter memories or cap like at higher levels, and they probably couldn't change emotions flexibly, they'd just make people do stuff. They can also command around spirits real easily.

        So, looking at this...
        Acanthus could really easily futz with time in a serious way at low levels that wasn't apparent before. They can also explictly mess with people's sense of time, which is cool. They still have all the- "I feel like prophesy at will" stuff they have before. As for their powers affect mages daily life...well, they're lucky. Like all the time, and have perfect timing, and if something feels like it's taking forever or going by too quickly they can just mess with their own sense of time. Acanthus don't have to deal like petty things like "boredom"; they can just make the time seem to fly by!

        Mastigos, in addition to their advantages at understanding at manipulating people in 1E, are also a whole lot harder to keep out of everywhere without magical defenses. Doors, locks and walls mean virtually nothing. They can get into anyones house, easy. It really upsells the "privacy is nothing" angle. As for daily life, Mastigos can always know how everyone around them really felt, but now they can casually teleport on a local scale if they don't feel like moving around the normal way, and they could portal without being vulgar. That would make the idea of travel and getting around a lot more to deal with. It's easy go anywhere, get out of anywhere, and it's easy to escape if people catch you, either with "don't notice me" or just by warping into a closet, or behind them when they aren't looking. This lets you do all sorts of looney toons nonsense, and makes snooping absolutely trival. They can also get privacy for themselves trivally, by making themselves or their living area hard to notice.

        Obrimos always had all the little cool comfor spells- never to hot or cold, never to bright or dark. Now they can add "living lie detector" to they're resume, and don't really have to worry about people believing the truth of what they say; no Obrimos has to worry about being thought mad by their family or sleeper friends by believing in impossible things, they can just tell them! Being honest is always an option for these guys, no matter how much they lie otherwise so it would make being straightforward a lot more attractive. They can also tell when other people are lying to them, or just plain wrong and crazy.

        Moros can see dead people (duh). They can also talk to them, and know the presence of death around them, which is sobering stuff. They also get a whole level of cool things using matter ruling spells and compelling spells, like being able to cusing water to flow the way they want, make balls roll around, and cause all sorts of "poltergiest effect" sorts of things (plates falling of shelves? Matter ruling, baby!). This allows for all sorts of neat little fun with their power, like opening doors without touching them, start their car without their keys, and all sorts of neat little "lazy telekinetic" tricks normally reserved for Obrimos. They also have the ability to hide stuff, all the way up to buildings, so they're the easiest to have a "secret retreat nobody can find" right after mastigos. They can also hide objects, making them great thieves; when a moros doesn't want something found, it doesn't get found by normal people. So being a moros can be really sobering, but it's pretty trival to manipulate lots of stuff in you're environment.

        Thrysus are pobably going to be a lot better at manuvering animals and people, and can be incredibly sneaky simply because he can target himself with virtually any sort of concealment spell he wants, because he's a life pattern. hey can be another person, unoticible, or invisible, whichever one is best for him at the time.They don't need to be afraid of animals at all, either, since they can probably exert strong control over them with pretty simple, low-risk spells. Getting birds, cats, and other animals to do your chores is a distinct possbility now. As is understanding them, or even talking to them like Dr. Dolittle at a bit higher levels (Unveiling, target other, yeah sure!). They also have all the benefits they had last edition of being really healthy all the time, being able to purge poisions and stuff from their systems.

        So for 2E...
        Acanthus can cause weird time shit ALL THE TIME from awfully early, and never have to have time pass too fast or too slow, never have to worry about bad luck, and can always know both the outcome of a random event and it's impact upon her immediate future. Augury means that "Is this a good idea?" is a question you always have an answer too, at least with regards to yourself.

        Mastigos can go damn near wherever they please, screw security, locks, and probably other people, and probably the only thing to need to worry about are other wizards and video cameras. They always know how people really feel, and always know what people really care about by reading their sympathy. Hiding anything from them is going to be hard, and catching them is going to be damn near impossible. They can easily return home whenever they want, so "getting lost" and "too far away to go back" are concepts that no longer apply to these guys. Causal, paradox-free teleportation (even if it's short range until dot 4) promises to be amazingly fun.

        Moros can always see dead, people, talk to dead people, and probably move around objects without touching them. They can also play waterbender, earthbender, and airbender, and everything-but-firebender. They can proabably steal anything left unattended for too long with veiling/ruling spells to make it not noticible. I am betting they can also make give materials all sorts of crazy impossible properties, or do things like evaporate water at room temperature (or generally alter the phase of anything, to hell with melting temperatures). They'll probably loosing things like alter conductivity until 3 dots, though, when they get weaving spells, at which point you get all those property modifying spells.

        Obrimos never have to worry about being uncomfortable or about being believed. Like, ever. They can tell the craziest crap to people who don't know them, and if it's true, they can get their target to belive it. They may not want to, or try to lie to themselves after the fact, but never having to worry about the "this is going to sound crazy, but..." problem is awesome enough in it's own right.

        Thrysus don't have to worry about animals, and probably aren't afraid of even the most dangerous, since it's easy to control them. "Being sick" is something that happens to other people, and they can talk to virtually anything, or at least the spirit of virtually anything.

        So yeah, I'm excited.

                                ​Cons:
                                1. I liked it more when Atlantis was real, and mages were heirs to it. I don't like "maybe it is, maybe it's not" approach to setting building - I like having answers, because then I can choose if I like them or don't like them and decide if I want to use them. Otherwise I just have to create setting on my own. I also liked it more when Supernal Realms were realms. I find argument "but you can't travel to Biology" really stupid. Yes, you can't, but it doesn't change the fact that it's much more easy to understand information in terms of spatial relations. "Palaces of memory" are built exactly on this trick.

                                2. Paths are bad. They aren't really interesting, and aren't really playing to stereotypes they try to convey, because: 1) you can understand those stereotypes differently (for example, "witch" for me strongly associates with potions and commanding animals and weather, so Matter/Forces/Life, not Fate/Time); 2) your character is bound to those stereotypes in choosing Arcana, and that's just sad. I think it would be great to include at least one small section to playing without Paths, instead of making you buy Storyteller's guide for that.

                                3. Wisdom is a                                 mess. I don't think there really is understanding what is is in-universe, how it works and what is it represents. This theme covers it up better than me: http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/main-category/main-forum/the-new-world-of-darkness/mage-the-awakening/887107-understand-wisdom

                                4. Withstand/Clash of Wills system. First of all, Withstand is a joke. Strong mages can easily burn right through it with Exceptional Success, which is easily achieved either by Praxis or ungodly dicepools of 10+. Incidentally, it makes every other splat Mages underdogs, because they literally can't defend themselves, at all. Unless you use Supernatural Tolerance as a bonus to Withstand, and then you just can't burn through, unless ES again. And because ability to protect yourself with Clash of Wills is so limited, every other splat (including all kinds of spirits) is just clay in mages hands, killing opportunity to every crossover. More than that, two Masters with different Arcana and without Shielding practice in other will just have mexican stand-off: either Life master destroys Pattern of Force Master, or Forces Master destroys Pattern of Life Master. They have no way to protect oneself against each other magic, and you can't have cool magical duels between different mages - it's just "shot, shot, somebody collapses to dust". And that nicely ties to the next problem:

                                5. Mages don't have any other antagonists, except other mages. Every splat has "out of context problem", something they can't really beat by their normal means: Vampire have Strix, werewolves have Idigam, and mages have... nothing. Quite literally: all kinds of ephemeral entities are either destroy mage outright, because he lacks the corresponding Arcana and can't interact with them, or he have it and they can't do much about him, and he can lord over them quite easily. I'd imagine that Rank 5 spirit, a fricking minor god, would be more than a match for Spirit Master, but not - you could bind it, reshape it and banish it easily, and all it can do is Withstand you with a Rank. Laughable.
                                Okay, you are asking: what about the Abyss? Well: what about the Abyss? Acamoth and Gulmoth are treated like Goetia and spirits, respectively. You can use usual Arcana on them - all "anti-magic" jazz is part of the fluff, but not the crunch. Yes, they can warp reality - you can do that too. And by the rules, you are better at this.
