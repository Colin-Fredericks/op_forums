            Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post
            The fact that Dave considers the real world to be more horrifying than the horror-themed tabletop roleplaying game he wrote is pretty interesting.
            Dave didn't write Mage 1e or 2e. The game's primary themes were established well before he was one author out of many in later Mage 1e supplements (and virtually every other nWOD/CofD gameline). Dave simply didn't change the primary Mage themes after he became developer for 2e.

            It's also not clear that Dave find's the real world more horrifying than Mage/CofD. You're interpreting his matter of fact comments under you own standards and values concerning immortality and related issues.

            Dave stated that currently death is inevitable. Given our current state of technology and spiritual understanding, this appears to be a simple fact, although one you would like to change.

            Similarly, our world does not have the magic and other supernatural matter inherent in Mage that would totally alter any discussion concerning death, immortality, any afterlife, etc.

            Lastly, and to some extent, most importantly, the lack of easy immortality was necessary in Mage to correct one of the biggest problems of Ascension. Immortality necessarily creates innumerable ancient and super-powerful beings that often drive the plot rather than the PC's. By taking most of these entities out of the setting, it allows PC's much greater free agency and direct impact on their individual chronicles and setting.

                        Related to all this, I do still wonder: Why does the explicitly 'anti-death' position of the Silver Ladder not work for people whose mages want to defeat death? The Silver Ladder has a strong position against human potential being cut short, period, and they do a lot of a work in this regard, fighting to change the system and help people live longer, fuller lives.

                        They just don't as an organization exalt the individual route of 'I will spend all my energy and resources, and make some highly questionable decisions, to ensure I live longer' but rather 'I will work to ensure that someday, all of humanity attains command of the universe.' If you want to talk about the Culture, that's a pretty Ladder perspective - a civilization where humans command all aspects of their reality and are ruled by benevolent human-created superbeings sounds very much like Imperium to me!

                        So... why not do that? Is it because the Silver Ladder focuses more on improving everyone's lives, and in theory risking themselves to improve the world, rather than focusing on specifically acquiring individual immortality?
                        I would think that helping as many people as possible to live long lives and achieve their potential would be far more valuable to the Ladder than an individual living forever, but it's frankly the case in our world that people do a lot more good against human suffering with malaria netting than with cryonics. That's just a fact.
