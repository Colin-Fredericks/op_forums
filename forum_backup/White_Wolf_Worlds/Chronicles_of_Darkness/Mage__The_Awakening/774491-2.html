    Actually, starting Werewolves have 3 dots of Renown, 4 Gift Facets and also 2 dots of Rites.

    Vampires have 3 dots of Disciplines. 2 must be in-Clan, 1 dot free choice. This dot could be spent on Cruac or Theban Sorcery and using the BS:S&B rules that would net you 3 dots of Themes (Blood Sorcery Arcana look-alike of wich there's only 5 against the 10 Arcana).

    Mages have 6 dots of Arcana spread like: 2/2/2, 3/2/1, 2/2/1/1 or now in second edition also 2/1/1/1/1

    Unfair?

    Let's see what each of them gets for free, without spending a single dot in the powers department (I'll consider werewolves without any dots of Renown even if that's imposible):

    Werewolves get, for free:

    1) Auspice ability usable once per chapter

    2) Predators Aspect usable at will

    3) Passive Free Regeneration. Boosted Regeneration for a cost

    4) 5 different forms, each with an especial ability, 4 with bonuses to physical Attributes and natural weapons, 3 with increased Defense against firearms, 3 causing Lunacy, 1 with uber-Regeneration. 4 of them can be used freely while the most potent one is limited in its controlled use to a few turns once per Scene.

    5) 2 sets of improved senses, with bonuses to perception tied to the forms, above.

    6) Moderate Damage reduction (Aggravated to Lethal)

    7) Access to the Shadow (only at a Locus)

    Vampires get, for free:

    1) Regeneration for a cost

    2) Great Damage Reduction (Most damage to Bashing)

    3) Unaging

    4) Predators Aura usable at will or at a cost

    5) The Kiss, Its source of power also means damaging the enemy

    6) Vitae adiction and Vinculum

    7) Ghoul and Vampire creation

    8) Physical Augmentation

    Mages get, for free:

    1) Mage Sight at will and improved version at a cost

    2) Pattern Restortation (Regeneration at a cost, most likely worse than any of the others)

    3) Tilt-inflicting Nimbus, likely at will, maybe at a cost

    4) Access to the Astral (only at a Hallow or Demesne)

    Mages get more dots of "learned powers", mostly because the others have a lot of innate template ones making up for it. A Mage without access to Arcana is just a human with a broad Unseen Sense a supernatural aura and a ineficient alternate method of healing*.

    *At least in 1st edition it was a kinda underwhelming 3 Mana for each Lethal.

                        You know, I run a LARP with 30 mages and my friends run LARPs with 30 vampires, 20 werewolves and 35 Changelings. They are part of the same shared chronicle. So far after years of playing, there has never been an open war between the supernaturales (except for one time where a warmonger tried to create one, but it was solved after a few months). Do they still have conflicts? Yes. Do they have good motives to go to war? Not at all. Mages are quite busy with their stuff to bother about what vampires are doing. Werewolves have issues with two or three mages and don't care about the rest.

                        I am not speaking from any theorethical possition about how the game plays, I do this every month. Of course, right now we are mostly using 1e rules (Except I have started adding obsessions into the mix, which seem to work fine so far). I am eager to see what 2e brings that I can start to use.

                        So far, this experience has shown me that 6 arcana dots work perfectly with a cap at 3 dots for the start. I have done a few games where people start at 4 dots in an Arcana and they are fine too. I have even done a few Archmaster sessions. There are some players that do really hubristic things with their magic and abuse their powers. There are others that are really careful and avoid hubris. Each type of game has its highs and lows. As long as you and your players are on the same understanding of how to play the game, the kind of story you want and all that fine stuff, the actual amount of dots you have is not a problem.

                            I must note that I'm aware of the in-built drawbacks of the splats and was about to mention it in my post, but it seemed big enough as it was.

                            That said, if you analize it you will see that it follows the same logic. Both Werewolves and Vampires have in-built abilities that are not dependant on any purchase or investment of xp on part of the player, so they also get weaknesses that are in-built and independent of those powers. Mages get most if not all of their oomph from purchased powers and so their primary weakness is also tied to those purchased powers.

                            Also, some of the "disadvantages" of Vampires and Werewolves are more like double-edged swords. Both Frenzy and Death Rage provide significant advantages in the shape of increased resistance to mental manipulation (for both of them) and powerful boost to physical pools (for vampires) or free use and automatic activation of several powers (for werewolves).

                            Silver (for werewolves) and Fire and Sunlight (for vampires) being extra-damaging is balanced against the fact that most other forms of damage are quite ineficient. Add on top of that the quick healing makes recovery easier. On the other hand mages are just as vulnerable as any regular human, and heals as slow as one without asist of magic or a great investment of their fuel trait.

                            Note also, that Blood Sorcery is almost as flexible as mage Arcana at less investment, having only 5 discrete powers versus the 10 a mage have to spread on, and that you could get 3 dots of them on top of 2 dots of regular Disciplines at character creation.

                                                            To be fair, currently or in 1st edition, paradox doesn't really matter. I've run a couple year-long mage campaigns and... it only really came up when players went waaaaay overboard, as in casting 5+ vulgar spells in the same scene overboard. It's only at that point that the number of successes on paradox rolls become non-trivial to just take as bashing damage. So saying paradox is a real limitation is incorrect IMO, in 1st edition. It sounds like Paradox gets more teeth in 2nd, so I am looking forward to that.

                                                            As for balance between splats... I can't really see how to do it without fundementally altering the way arcana works. Werewolf, vampire, etc powers are specific, mage powers are broad. Once a mage hits 3 or maybe 4 in an arcana, they just outstrip everyone else. It doesn't matter if you have Celerity 6, when I can use Spirit 2 to get spirits to spy on you, find your name and a piece of cloth, then use Space 4 to teleport you to the moon using a sliver of rock from the moon landing. All from the comfort of my home.

                                                            If I ever had some guy wanting to play a vampire in a mage game, I'd throw something like 20 exp at him for starters (2nd ed rules). I mean, c'mon, this is white wolf games we are talking about, it wouldn't be white wolf if the authors actually understood how their rules worked. 2nd edition seems to be changing that, but still, don't expect miracles.
