    How do you show peripheral mage sight?

    08-19-2016, 04:52 AM
    I've got one player in a game I am running who has their peripheral mage sight manifest as a kindly old man who appears somewhere where he doesn't seem out of place within the scene but the character finds their attention drawn straight to the old man before he gives them a knowing look and disappears, another manifests it as a flash of light in their peripheral vision.

    So what ways have you shown peripheral mage sight in games?

              In my 1ed chronicle we were too busy trying to figure out how to play the game to put much effort in window dressing so I just told them their spidey sense was tingling. For my upcoming 2ed I wanted to move more into the feel of the setting and that includes trying to give each character a unique way of interacting with their magic. One of my players wrote up a budding musician so I intend to have Mage Sight come across as associated music, with their peripheral sense being music playing too low to really hear but catching her ear all the same. For our Arcanthus who is written up as developing an appetite for the rush of risk I decided peripheral sight will be the sound of shaking dice in a cup.

                      My intent, still haven't played it so we'll see how well it works, is to describe to each player their character's trigger. I won't mention peripheral sight but since the players are now familiar with the 'spidey sense' thing I will leave it to them to figure it out. The fact that each has a different way of perceiving it I hope will outline its significance until they do.

                          We have PCs from each Path, which helps a lot as each player contributes their own take on observing Mysteries:

                          Obrimos: Hears an angelic choir, or a floating hand inscribe High Speech on a wall. (We based it off an Old Testament episode). At higher Gnosis he'll begin to see sigils and mandalas of pure energy. (Prime)

                          Mastigos: Muffled voices or multiple whispers (Mind). Distortions of light around things which are 'wrong' (Space. Think Predator or how the Protoss cloak themselves).

                          Acanthus: Threads, ribbons, strings, and other materials of binding (Fate). Odd time shifts and split-second rewinds and flash-forwards. Visions of peril and promise. (Time)

                          Thyrsus: A loud heartbeat in the direction of supernatural acrivity (Life). Random sightings of benign and predatorial scavenger spirits. (Spirit) Also has a keen sense of smell.

                          Moros: Our newest player is a Necromancer who puts ghosts into bullet casings. She exhibits Sixth Sense behavior. I intend to let her see people age and wither in front of her ala Raistlin from Dragonlance.

                              I've been working on a mystery cult/nameless order/legacy (they evolved from an overgrown legacy organization that started admitting mages to the organization who didn't join the legacy, and then started admitting other creatures too) that are obsessed with, among other things, expanding their senses (they are actually obsessed with art, making it and appreciating it, more senses means more kinds of art).

                              I'm making a merit for them that mechanically just cancels penalties from additional senses (such as from active mage sight) but narratively what they are doing is learning to pull their supernatural senses off of their physical ones (so they can experience them "raw" and "pure"), such that they no longer experience them as a part of another sense, they are their own sense. (They also tend to have a lot of senses, as they will often have several safely relinquished indefinite unveiling spells cast on themselves).

                              A metaphor for this, that I like, is when someone has a heart attack, they feel pain elsewhere because the brain doesn't know what to do with the signal, but if their heart hurt on a regular basis they might be able to learn feel the pain in their heart directly.

                              They do things like cast Space Patterning spells to create a "sculpture" of warped space, in their sanctum, that, being made of space itself, can only be seen with certain supernatural senses, then cast an unveiling spell on the area such that anyone close by will have the ability to perceive it without needing an appropriate power.

                              Needless to say, the mages in this organization don't have high Wisdom.

                                  Originally posted by terpsichordean View Post
                                  We have PCs from each Path, which helps a lot as each player contributes their own take on observing Mysteries:
                                  .
                                  Each of our characters experiences it differently as well, always as a physical sense (though now that I think of it, never as sight oddly enough).


                                  Our Moros character smells odd, indefinite things.
                                  Our Acanthus feels cobwebs against his skin.
                                  Our Mastigos hears indistinct whispers.
                                  Our Thyrsus gets a strong, metallic/bitter tastes in his mouth.

                                  It is just coincidence that each experiences a different sense, and is of a different path. We don't have any convention that the sensation is path related for peripheral sight.
                                  We don't have variations for different Arcana. It is our understanding that, beyond a vague directional sense, peripheral sight doesn't really give specifics about what is happening, including what Arcana is most closely related.

                                  The exception is for attainments that call for specifics. The Moros has the Death 2 attainment that lets peripheral vision detect ghosts. When a ghost is present, he gets a specific smell of freshly turned earth that he associates with ghosts (I'm not certain that the Death 2 attainment is actually meant to indicate that peripheral sight lets the mage know when a ghost is around, or if it is just supposed to adds ghostly presence to the list of things that give the mage an indistinct ping, but we have decided to play it as if it clearly identifies that there is a ghost about). Getting specific information on other incidents of peripheral sight requires that he use active sight or specific spells, which have their own descriptions.
