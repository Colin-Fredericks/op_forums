            Viewers of Chaos: The most miniscule act can have dramatic, far reaching consequences. That is the essence of the Chaos Theory, and it is the philosophy that the Viewers of Chaos follow. An Acanthus Legacy, they use their powers to view the fates of others and take note of how changing even the tiniest little incident can drastically alter the course of events that unfold. Contrary to what you might believe, the Legacy is not inherently evil, they are simply curious to see how their actions affect the world around them. Sometimes these changes are beneficial, and sometimes they are disastrous. For the Viewers of Chaos, the joy of their craft lies in watching the events unfold, not about their eventual outcome. The Viewers of Chaos have begun to study the Arcana of Space, which allows them to alter their environment from a distance, as well as plant and remove objects and people from the locations they wish to manipulate.



            Bayou Brujah: From taming alligators, to turning people into frogs, the Bayou Brujah (otherwise known as Swamp Witches) are a Thyrsus Legacy who choose to live in the marshes, swamps, bogs, and bayous, defending the murky waters and overgrown foliage from all those who would bring them harm. As a society of swamp based Shaman, the Bayou Brujah have a strong connection to voodoo rites and practices including the forging of cursed dolls and other less familiar activities. In addition to their standard Arcana of Life and Spirits, they have also begun to study Death, giving them such terrifying abilities as reanimating zombies from those who meet their gruesome end among the swamps!

                Writeup of the Disciples of Rathma


                Disciples of Rathma


                Death is not the end. Death can never be the end.
                Death is the road. Life is the traveller. The soul is the guide.
                - Sri Chinmoy





                Most consider Life and Death to be eternal enemies. Mutually exclusive states of being. Forces in fundamental opposition to one another. Enlightened in equal measure by sleeper science and certain sleeper cultures, the Disciples of Rathma know that this is wrong. Where others see conflict, the Disciples see symbiosis. Two forces codependent on each other and driving each other forward. Life begets death, Death begets Life. Without this unending circle there would be no progress, only stasis and entropic decay.
                The Disciples of Rathma as a Legacy arose not long before the Nameless War It was born amidst new scientific findings in biology at the beginning of the 20th century and certain sleeper cultures’ view of life and death as being so intimately entwined as to basically being the same. With each generation that passes species improve themselves molded by the forces of natural selection and necessity. Life overcomes, life adapts, but only through the fires of death that get rid of the old can this evolution take place.
                So the Disciples ask: what if you could turn yourself into a furnace of evolution? Make yourself able to evolve, without needing to die. Control death and life such that small parts of your being continuously die and are reborn, improved and adapted. Disciples of Rathma seek to understand and master the balance of life and death. Once true mastery is achieved, it is said, a Disciple will be able to enter a spiral of Selfevolution.
                To reach this point, a Disciple of Rathma starts by becoming truly aware of the cycle of life and death around them. Once they are aware, they adapt their body using this knowledge, reaching a balance between true death and being alive, becoming able to evolve themselves
                The unique viewpoint on the relationship between the Life and Death Arcana means both Thyrsus and Moros often find themselves attracted to the Legacy. The different viewpoints both Paths provide to the Legacy can lead to conflict, but in a typical Free Council approach this is seen as more of a chance than a problem.
                The Disciple of Rathma’s founder was Rising Sun, a member of the cabal of the Black Mast, one of the original founding cabals of the Free Council. Originally from Zanzibar, the Black Mast eschewed the traditional form of Sanctum. Instead they lived on a Dhow that travelled the trade route that goes from India, along the coast of East Africa, down to Tanga. Rising Sun had always been curious and inquiring, and the cabal’s travels provided her with access to all manner of cultures and lore. Interested in both religions and science, Rising Sun happily merged sleeper beliefs about the nature of life and death with Darwin’s theory of evolution to create the Disciples of Rathma legacy.

                Theme: Science and Discovery between Life and Death

                Parent Path or Order: Moros or Thyrsus, Free Council

                Nickname: Boneseers, Corpsewalkers, Seeders (new, first used around the turn of the millenium)

                Appearance: Many Disciples of Rathma emulate dress and style of sleeper groups focused on the life/death duality and the erosion of the borders between them. Many like to dress like stereotypical Necromancers or take after the looks of spirits and gods of life and death found in sleeper cultures.

                Background: The Disciples of Rathma draw members that are inquisitive, willing to question established knowledge and often have equal interest in science and the occult. Experimenting with one's own body, improving oneself and broadening what it means to be human is a central part of the Legacy, and members are often extraordinarily driven and adventurous individuals. Many have a background in medicine or biology coupled with an interest in the occult.

                Organization: There are normally two teachers per student, one Thyrsus and one Moros, though the legacy’s low numbers mean one is often only marginally involved and might live far away. Also, there is often a visible rift between the Thyrsus and the Moros that make up the Legacy, but as the unity of Life and Death is one of the core beliefs of the Legacy’s members there are also many that actively try to foster working together.

                Suggested Oblations: Ritual Trance induced by careful use of dangerous poisons, activities with the aim of improving oneself (training, learning something new, etc), helping with a birth or death and watching the process with the first attainment.

                Concepts: Researcher into matters of Life and Death, travelling Medicine Man or Wise Houngan/Mambo, explorer of the Underworld, researcher of the Undead, mad scientist experimenting on themself.

                Attainments:
                Similar to the Celestial Masters’ relationship with Forces & Matter, both Life and Death can be the Legacy’s Primary Arcanum, depending on the Willworker’s Path. Moros tread Life as the Primary Arcanum, Thyrsus Death.

                1st: The Struggle for Life
                Prerequisites:                 Gnosis 3, Death 2, Life 2, Occult 2
                The first lesson a Disciple learns is open their perceptions, to feel Life and Death in all their forms interacting around them, permeating their surroundings. This attainment provides the a Disciple with an always on version of the Life 1 Spell Sense Life, but the Disciple can also feel the Undead (including Ghosts in Twilight) and any corpse that has been dead for less than the Disciple’s Life Arcanum in days. If successes are required to notice someone, treat the Disciple as having rolled their Life Arcanum in successes.
                Additionally, the Disciple can activate Speak with the Dead towards any Ghosts that enter the Spell’s Safety Radius as an instant action.

                2nd: Experiments on Human Hybridization
                Prerequisites:                 Gnosis 5, Death 3, Life 3, Athletics or Medicine 3
                After adapting their perceptions, the second step for a Disciple of Rathma is to accept the unity of Life and Death as a part of them. By understanding how their body carries both, the Disciple of Rathma is able to integrate them more fully within themselves. They balance the two forces’ power over their pattern, becoming neither fully dead or alive.
                This Attainment replicates the spell Corpse Flesh. The dicepool to activate the attainment is Medicine + Stamina + Death. At Death 4 Life 4 the attainment no longer has a set maximum duration. The Disciple of Rathma basically always decides themselves if they want to to be under the Attainments effects.

                3rd: The Origin of Species
                Prerequisites:                 Gnosis 7, Life 4, Death 4, Medicine 4
                At this point, the Disciple of Rathma becomes able to actively evolve themselves, easily adapting to changing circumstances.
                This attainment allows the user to change themselves, as with the spells Transform Self and Two Faces, but the mage never needs to roll to get features right.
                Activating this attainment is an extended action that takes 10 minutes. The Mage gains one feature per activation and cannot have more activations than the lower of their Life and Death running at the same time.
                If the second attainment is active when the third is used, the list of possible features extends beyond just normal biologically possible features to a few specific ones related to Death and the Undead. The exact extend of possible changes using this is not yet known, as Disciples are still discovering new ones. Known ones are the ability to draw sustenance from eating ectoplasm, do photosynthesis in the unnatural light of the Underworld or smell ghostly Anchors.

                                I don't like the mechanics of most of these, but they seem a place to start for some more ideas.

                                Spectral claws (+2L) that affect both material and twilight.
                                Stillness of the Grave: The character with this trait can double the time he needs between breaths by activating undead metabolism in areas that are inactive. This removes any penalty due to personal shaking or instability while the character holds his breath. This grants a bonus of +2 to resistance traits that attack such susceptibilities, such as choke holds.
                                Rigourous Grasp: The character gains the ability to lock their grip without needing to expend energy to maintain it. This improves effective Strength and Stamina by 1 for purposes of maintaining a hold or pin. (Technically, this could be done with just Life, since Mollusks do this.)
                                Ablation: Some superficial skin can rot, providing the character the ability to slip free of bonds and holds much easier. The ability itself causes no harm, but using it causes a Bashing level unless an Exceptional Success is achieved on the escape.
                                Driven: Will or magic drives an undead creature beyond natural limits. This feature taps that to drive the body beyond its normal limits. Gain a health level that lasts until the undead state is removed. This power allows one health level of damage to be healed as if Bashing,regardless of actual severity. This can only be used once per scene and once per activation of the Dead Flesh. While this feature is active, the character also reduces wound penalties by 2.
                                Ear of the Tomb: The character has the ability to challenge ghostly concealment effects with his basic senses. Success on perception must exceed the concealment. If the character has another roll that can penetrate the effect, roll that with the 8,9,10-Again qualities, rather than rolling twice.
                                Quiet Mind: The character can voluntarily dampen his mind as an Instant mental action. The result is the same mind as a Quicken Dead spell creates. The character may issue a single event or time-limit that restores consciousness, or their mind returns at the end of the attainment. While invoking, the character sets up actions per commands to a revenant ("Zombie" see the spell M:TA pp140-1) or simply be still. In this suppressed condition, their mind is dead, and cannot be targeted. Act committed in the dead state are easier to rationalize, granting a bonus die on degeneration checks.
                                If so desired, this can also be activated when meditating, in which case the character's mind can, in fact, be targeted per standard rules, just not through their body.

                                        Order of the Vibrant Soul: This legacy of the Mastigos focuses on internal power. By combining magical talent with their emotional spectrum, they can improve the effectiveness of their spells, and over time they learn to harness the emotions of others to even greater effect! They have begun to study the Arcana of Prime, learning to harness magic in its purest form.


                                        Attainment 1 - Strength from Within:
                                        Requirements: Gnosis 3, Mind 2, Prime 2

                                        The Mage is able to harness their own emotional state to enhance the power of their spells. If the Mage is feeling an emotion that would normally be attributed to the effect the spell is intended to produce, they gain a +2 when casting it.


                                        Attainment 2 - The Bonds we Share:
                                        Requirements: Gnosis 5, Mind 4, Prime 3

                                        The Mage no longer has to fight his battles alone. With this attainment, he can now draw upon the combined emotions of those around him. However, they must all share the same emotion as the one he is attempting to invoke for the spell, and their participation must be willing, meaning he can't draw power from an enemy. Every person who lends their strength to the spell provides an additional +1 on attempts to cast it.


                                        Attainment 3 - Hopes of Those Who Came Before:
                                        Gnosis 7, Mind 4, Prime 4

                                        Upon reaching this attainment, the mage no longer requires living beings to supply the emotional power for their magic. They can now harness the emotional resonance that lingers in places strong with it's connection. The site of a great war may hold strong hatred, fear or sorrow. A whore house may hold great pain, lust, or regret. A child's playground may hold joy, freedom, or whimsy. All of these being just some possibilities. When casting a spell that draws upon an emotion that the area might possess, the mage is able to gain up to a total of +1 to casting equal to the location's size.

                                        Unfortunately because the area is not actually a living being, it can not replenish the emotions that linger there on its own. For each + the mage draws upon, the emotional power for the area is depleted by an equal amount in size. When the emotional resonance reaches 0, the location is dried up and the mage can not harness it again for quite a while. The emotional energy is replenished at a rate of 1 size each year (though not exceeding the locations size) if the location continues to see regular use.



                                        The following is a list of examples for possible combinations, but is by no means a complete list.

                                        Offensive - Anger, Pain, Love (for another), Despair

                                        Defensive - Fear, Love, Pride, Mercy

                                        Transportation - Joy, Free Spirited, Impatient, Terror

                                        Transformation - Rejection, Uncertainty, Whimsy, Disgust

                                            The Celestial Covenant
                                            This Legacy was created by a Mormon Obrimos in order to reconcile his religion's teachings on marriage and the family with the demands of Magehood.                                                 The Hands of Atlas

                                                When I raise up this stone, sister, I raise up my soul.

                                                This legacy was founded by an illiterate Libertine Ecstatic in the early 20th century, who worked as a pig-iron hauler at a steel mill before his Awakening. Unable to participate in the more intellectual discussions of his peers, he decided to develop a mystical practice doing the only thing he had ever really excelled at: picking heavy things up and putting them down. Though mocked at first, his contemporaries began to find themselves in awe of how he ritually transformed this simple labor into a reflection of Supernal power's capacity to transform reality. One day, as he set down the last load of his shift, he turned to find a seasoned Adamant Arrow warrior bowing humbly before him, asking to be taught. And so the Legacy began its dissemination to the present day.

                                                Parent Path/Order: Thyrsus Libertine or Arrow

                                                Nickname: Lifters

                                                Appearance: The Hands of Atlas are generally quite muscular, and tend to have rather calloused hands. They prefer sturdy boots and clothing that shields their skin from the sun while not constricting movement.

                                                Background: Many Lifters are blue-collar workers or body-builders, but some are mages who have spent a considerable portion of their careers doing what they perceive to be un-fulfilling office work, and who look to embrace a lifestyle where the product of one's efforts is considerably more visceral.

                                                Initiation: An apprentice must spend an entire week working from sun-up to sun-down, hauling heavy objects or doing some other physically strenuous activity, and stopping only when their mentor signals them for momentary rests, water breaks, or meals.

                                                Organization: Lifters tend to form gangs who rally whenever a project appears that requires the efforts of more than one of them. Other than the customary deference of apprentice to master, Lifters view one another as equals, and while jests are often made amongst each other accusing one member or another of laziness, they are loathe to comment on one another's relative competence to outsiders, asserting that each does "the best she can."

                                                Theory: Lifters believe that as they move things where they need to go, and pick up the stones that have been torn down, they slowly bring order to the Universe, and more perfectly align the Fallen and Supernal. When something is carried, it is transformed from its inert state- it gains potential energy, and its relationship with the rest of the world can be redefined to something more ideal.

                                                Oblations: Helping someone move, lifting weights, working as a day-laborer, carrying a cabal-mate an extended distance

                                                Yantras: Holding the subject over one's head provided it weighs at least as much as a backpack full of books +1 or +3 if the spell lasts only as long as the subject is lifted and the subject weighs at least as much as the caster, stacking stones or other heavy objects +2.

                                                Attainments: (Primary Arcanum: Forces) A newly initiated Lifter can tell how heavy something is at a glance, and where its center of gravity is with a touch. A student can reduce an object's weight as long as they are bearing it themselves. A master can lift things and move them with the slightest touch, levitating objects even if they themselves are not standing on the ground. (Secondary Arcanum: Life) A newly initiated Hand of Atlas can increase their own endurance; a student can heal themselves and others of injuries gained through physical labor, and a master can heal at an accelerated rate simply by getting back to work.

                                                        Originally posted by Thorbes View Post
                                                        The Hands of Atlas seem quite interesting, Ghost Turtle. I like their concept a lot.
                                                        Thanks! When I've tried to design legacies in the past, I usually started with their philosophy and then attempted to extrapolate practices. With the Hands of Atlas I did the reverse: I started with the activities the mage would undergo in search of Supernal Truth (picking things up and putting them down), and then tried to consider what sort of world-view would develop from doing that a lot, studying it closely, and contemplating its symbolic significance. This also made thinking about Attainments a lot easier, since you just pick things that facilitate what the mage was doing already.

                                                        Initiation and Oblations ended up becoming key to thinking about the Legacy, because the former is what you do to start being a member, and the latter is what you continuously do as a member. For whatever reason I used to think of oblations as sort of in-theme side-quests that give you an optional reward for playing up your Legacy, but any Legacy member worth her salt (or at least the founder, whose stamp will be on the legacy forever) is going to be doing them as often as possible- they will be a key part of her magical lifestyle.

                                                        I also just loved thinking up a legacy that was about becoming a transcendent mystical paragon by way of "unskilled" labor.
