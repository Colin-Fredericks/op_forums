    Focused Mage Sight - Scrutiny: removing the Extended Action

    01-13-2020, 06:47 AM
    Extended actions really don't work well for my group - it feels too much like rolling dice until a problem goes away, plus the issues with narrating every single roll, plus remembering "so, this is Opacity 4 for player A, player B already brought it down to Opacity 3, Player C still has it as Opacity 7..." is just too much for me.

    So, this is what I'm considering using instead:

    Focused Mage Sight (Scrutiny)
    • Dicepool is Gnosis + Arcana (+1 per Mana spent). The roll is considered a success if it rolls more successes than the Mystery's Opacity (basically, Opacity works like a Withstand).
    • Costs 1 WP (in practice, it's the same WP for keeping up Active Mage Sight up for an extended period of time).
    • Single roll, but the action itself takes a Gnosis Ritual Interval to complete.
    • Each spent Mana gives +1 to the roll. If the roll succeeds, partially recover some of that Mana (up to total spent -1). Succeed or fail, there's a small window in which the Mana can be recovered in other ways (such as Channel Mana).
    • Spending more Mana than your Gnosis is the equivalent of going over the safe number of rolls.
    • Success (rolling more successes than the Mystery's Opacity) gives access to Deep Information (it's the equivalent of reducing Opacity to 0 under the other system).


    I can see some issues with this: we lose out on the ability to dole out partial information (for reducing Opacity for half, for example), and there's no good way to make "slow" progress.
    Would you use this system? Any suggestions on improving it, or is there some other obvious flaw I'm missing?

      I would say that for your group that may just work. Different groups are going to rule away different things based upon the needs of that group.

      Now, as to what you might miss, I would say locked and linked opacity from Signs of Sorcery. Those two tools give you the ability to truly control your big mystery whereas without them you're mostly just relegated to raising the opacity of the main mystery to absurd levels. Directly related to that is the fact that your case is going to be even more exasperated by that problem since characters HAVE to try to do it in one go.

      Another big issue would be the disparity between acquiring successes vs raising your dicepool. It takes, on average, 3 dice for every 1 success that you want to have a decent shot at. Forcing a Mystery to be done in one go means that some Mysteries could be nigh on impossible to understand since you can only raise your dicepool so high whereas the opacity can be arbitrarily high.

      To give an example of the first issue:
      Let's say that your game is set in Los Angelas where the Astral realms are invading normal space. You've got all this cool information about what's going on that you can learn from various different npcs in addition to observing the phenomenon yourself. Because this is a main mystery it's base opacity is probably 5, raised to at least 8 though possibly as high as 10 or 12. Now, in your case that mystery could be (and in fact MUST be) resolved in one single roll. This means that anyone who is lucky could (and in fact MUST) just solve THE mystery that the entire concilium is based upon in a single lucky chance. Where this gets strange (and, unfortunately, poorly done) as related to something like a main mystery as big as the Astral realms invading LA is the sheer number of factors involved with such a Mystery. How could someone possibly just understand everything about something as widespread, convoluted, deep, and involved as an entire REALM invading another with just a single roll? No, this is where locked and linked opacity comes into play. With those two tools you slow it down so that the players are instead forced to understand multiple parts of the Mystery as well as related Mysteries in order to progress with the city wide, BIG Mystery.

      Now, having said that there is a possible compromise: only use the normal system for your main Mystery. This way you can still stretch out that plot without the players just getting it in one go while still allowing for a much simpler system for the rest of the Mysteries that you create. Also, take notes. Yeah, it can be hard to keep track of everything in your head (I wouldn't want to do that either), but if you take notes about where the players are as related to the main mystery then you don't have to worry about each one being at a different stage. Also if you're only doing the normal system for main Mystery and your own for other Mysteries then you have a lot less to keep track of.

      Alternatively you may consider just lowering the opacity a bit for all of your Mysteries. If your intention is for characters to generally be able to understand things in one go then perhaps you just need to lower the opacity.

              The only thing I would change is that if you want to treat Opacity like Withstand then don’t require successes to get over it. Treat it exactly like Withstand, letting your players take dice penalties as if they were increasing their Sight’s potency, and then when they roll treat a single success as getting at the deep info. This makes pass/fail less wobbly, and prevents any confusion about how Withstand works for spells when you introduce this house rule to your group.

                                          After using this for a few sessions, we came to a conclusion: we don't like it. It felt like the best approach to a Mystery is "dump Mana all over it".

                                          This is what we're using instead:
                                          • Dicepool is Gnosis + Arcana (+1 per Mana spent) + progress bonus.
                                          • Costs 1 WP (in practice, it's the same WP for keeping up Active Mage Sight up for an extended period of time).
                                          • Single roll, but the action itself takes a Gnosis Ritual Interval to complete.
                                          • Each spent Mana gives +1 to the roll. If the roll succeeds, partially recover some of that Mana (up to Opacity or successes rolled, whichever's lower).
                                          • Spending more Mana than your Gnosis is the equivalent of going over the safe number of rolls (after the roll, add half your Gnosis to Opacity, rounded up)
                                          • Each success peels back one layer of Opacity. Rolling more successes than Opacity fully Scrutinizes it, while rolling under the required number gives only partial information.
                                          • Failing to fully Scrutinize the Mystery gives a progress bonus. For example, rolling 3 successes on an Opacity 5 Mystery gives you a +3 bonus to further Scrutiny rolls - if on your next roll you get 4 successes instead, the bonus "updates" to +4.

                                          This feels much more variable, and gives us room to experiment with Prime spells to further improve on Scrutiny (granting dice tricks, to bonus dice, to flat-out adding bonus successes).
