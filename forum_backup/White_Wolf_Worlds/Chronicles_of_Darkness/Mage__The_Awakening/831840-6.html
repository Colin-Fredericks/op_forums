                Lots of misunderstandings we got here, serves me right for just rambling on. To disentanglement!

                Originally posted by MCN View Post
                .... Yeah, no. First of all, that's not how abuse actually works. Secondly, no, it wasn't a Lie. That sidebar actually refers to the Exarchs and Seers ruling the world, and suggests the Pentacle (and thus the PCs) is at war with them. Not that Sleeper Science is wrong. In fact, for someone who's also a part of Free Council who believes that science is an echo of seeking Supernal Truth? Frankly, I don't see my PC having any qualms with making peace between "newly discovered power" and being a doctor. She had qualms about the people close to her being subject to Long Term Nimbus (thus the Wisdom). But the power itself? Not really.
                First: Abuse works perfectly fine with the abuser not considering themselves an abuser. Believing they're doing the best for the victim, whom they don't see as a victim but someone they're helping. Whether or not something is abuse is entirely an experience of the one who is in a position of less power.

                I was too vague with the 'a lot of it was a Lie.' You learn that a lot of what a whole hospital has to deal with, short to long-term, could be handled in a few seconds by a single experienced Life mage. Hell, a newb with an imbued item. All the suffering, needless. There be exarchic influence at play.

                I did not mean to imply Sleeper Science is wrong, my bad if it turned out like that.

                And again to the 'Science is not wrong,' did not mean to imply the power is antithetical to being a doctor, but rather that being a doctor, one who deals with injury and illness and all kinds of suffering on a very likely daily basis, it will be exceedingly hard to resist using a tool that can just make it all go away. Not that it's hard to make peace between having/using the power and being a doc, but rather between being a doc with this power and not using it. Which brings Wisdom into play, a mechanic that is on the boundary of preparation and consequence.

                Originally posted by MCN View Post
                Secondly, choosing to not to help people is not a "monstrous choice." There's fairly well established ethics in the medical community about responcibility to patients as well as to the self. That's why the Wisdom - the understanding when its best to apply the balance of the personal life and the duties as a doctor and the life as a mage. Its knowing that experimenting on living people is kidn of a bad, immoral thing that shouldn't be done. Its knowing that there are times when casting magic shouldn't be done.
                Sure it is! It's the scale that gets ya, or me as it were XD
                It's not a monstrous choice to not help that specific person in that specific situation. It is a monstrous choice to have power on a dramatic scale and not use it to help people.

                Certainly ethics of medical practice is very important, and while I am not informed enough to go into detail, I don't really have to. Medical ethics is by necessity formed by medical professionals in context of what medicine can do, and the consequences thereof.
                As a code I can only presume is quite ingrained into doctors during their training, what happens to that when your tools to heal suddenly surpass your previous ones by leaps and bounds? But also come with different risks - specifically patients' integrity breaking points if they witness the healing, and forgetting or rationalising parts of it if exposed to it.

                So you are now a medical ethics panel of one, because telling sleepers is a no-no. And informed consent is very tricky, if at all possible even in a roundabout way.

                Originally posted by MCN View Post
                Third, "wanting to help people" is not a cliche. I went into social services because I want to help people in real life. Its called "this actually happens with a lot of frequency."
                Apologies if this was offensive, but yes it is. By definition, because it's very much overused in fiction.
                I did mean specifically in fiction, as per talking about a Mage game, in real life it's obviously much more meaningful, but that it happens a lot is what makes it a fictional clichee, certainly not the first or last one to be important and good in real life.


                Power corrupts has been scientifically proven false? Not being facetious here, please link to the studies, I'm very interested, in PM if it's too off-topic here.
                Because I don't see it that way for CofD. If studies show it, that's delightful for us, but I'll be throwing that out wholesale for the World of Darkness, or Chronicles thereof.

                I see you're trying to find monstrousness in mechanics, and I'm fine with it not being there. Not the way you want it to be.

                Also significantly, a Changeling can be a not-monster. Because why not? There's addiction to Glamour, easily sated through positive dreams and periodic nomnoms of positive emotions from someone you care about and who knows about this ... and there's what else? Being hunted. Being a victim. The powers of the wyrd are, as you say, ripe for abuse (but much less so than Mage ones), but do not equal abuse perpetrated, even more so than for Mages.

                Vampires too, mind. They need blood to live, but hey, blood is not a thing in scarcity. Put some Merit dots on character creation into a loving family that knows about vampirism and provides blood enough to live. The vamp then follows the path of humanity in whatever version or edition you want.

                It's certainly much easier for mages, if the only thing you consider are the punishment mechanics. We seem to disagree strongly here, but I am much in love with the 2E orientation away from punishment mechanics, and going towards rewards of story/experience Beats and willpower and whatnot.

                I will again point out my favored fluff, which is addicted to Mysteries. Your proposal of the Obsessed Condition works quite nicely too. Even if it's less of a stick than you mentioned wanting to the carrot of Beats and Mana. Consider perhaps the Addicted series of Conditions.

                Final thoughts: They're not inherently monsters. But they're immensely powerful humans. So very likely to become monsters in the CofD. Even/especially if they don't see themselves that way, or even any of their Awakened peers.

                Turns out I have many thoughts on this and they're less coherent than I'd like. More to follow I'm sure

                                A few fun examples of monstrosity that have occured in my last few games are:

                                - Tearing the Goetia of someone's affections for another person out of their mind, because they thought that relationship was weighing the other person down
                                - Disguising oneself as another person, the living their life with their family for the next day, building up their hopes that the person had changed by acting strangely nice, then simply leaving without an explanation
                                - Sending an ephemeral entity off to kidnap children so you could sacrifice them for mana
                                - Mind-controlling civilians to kill Seer agents, simultaneously enhancing their mental attributes so they have a better chance of coming out of Quiescence unscathed
                                - Inuring Devouring the Slain, then repeating its use, combined with Mend, upon an unconscious individual within the mage's own Demesne, to reap ludicrous amounts of mana and willpower
                                - Summoning the Goetia of one's own wrath, and then leaving it alone in a room with someone he completely and utterly hates while the now calm collected Mage goes and has coffee
                                - Tricking an entire tribe into believing that the hostile nature of a local spirit is their own fault, and that only through appeasing it can they hope to live in harmony, then collecting the boons from that Spirit for finding it a group of follower
                                - Corrupting a Spirit of Bravery into a spirit of a Selfish Motivation by binding it to a mask and then starving it of Essence until it was at the brink of discorporation/death, and then offering it Essence with increasing amounts of selfish urges over and over.
                                - Manipulating a Changeling's memories to believe that a) he has a daughter b) that you have kidnapped her and will torture her and potentially hand her over to a privateer if he doesn't do what you want. Thinking he'll be fine when he does what you want, and realises it was all a trick. Not realising how agonising it is to grant him hope tinged with fear, only to strip it all away and leave him with nothing but an aching loss.

                                I mean, only couple of those were by PCs.
                                But I hold out hope for the future.

                                                            I usually play Hunter, while dabbling in other splats, so the concept of "at what point are you a monster yourself" is very near and dear to me. On top of that, you've also given me an idea for a new antagonist for my Mage Hunters but that's just how my warped Hunter mind sees things now 😀. I'd say what really makes a Mage a monster is their separation from mortal thinking. A vampire thinks in the terms of a predator, with the beast altering their perceptions of the world around them as they grow more callous towards mortals, werewolves start to think more like spirits like one werewolf pack I made for a friend who's totem had them going out and slashing people's tires and vandalizing property for the spirit's amusement and they saw themselves as perfectly noble, even as their totem pushed them towards the pure and made them more beast than man. Likewise mages just don't think like normal people anymore and thoughts like "I can just undo it" will pretty much always crop up sooner or later, and I find among many Mage players that I've met a certain amount of "all you have is a hammer" mentality. What I mean by that is that they get addicted to using magic to solve problems and start to only think of what new spell they can do to fix a problem, which tends to lead to a large amount of paradox before they know it. Sooner or later their actions are going to hurt people, and just because they can make the consequences disappear doesn't mean they didn't do the action, and as they become more callous they stop caring about the bad they do to those around them, they no longer see it as all that bad compared to other things they've done. It's only a matter of time before their actions catch up with them though.

                                                            In many respects it's similar to Hunters but with even more power perversion potential.
