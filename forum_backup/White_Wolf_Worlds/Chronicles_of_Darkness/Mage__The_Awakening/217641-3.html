    Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post

    Simply using The Code to protect yourself from Integrity loss takes off the biggest edge. The Code and Willpower 5 should in theory be enough to play a Witchfinder; if you see magic and don't attack there and then you'll be left with a sense of unease which can lead to an investigation and witnessing magic again. Or if you have a film and The Code, you can remind yourself again and again without hitting a Breaking Point each time.

    Everything else is just a cherry on the top.
    That is not how Quiescence works going by the FWC spoilers. Witnessing the magic again or reviewing film won't help. You'll continue to forget the second it stops. Sleepers cannot remember Supernal magic. Period. Willpower rating does not matter. There isn't a way to bypass that without Sleepwalker status.

    But you can certainly build to the point where a Hunter cell recognizes the signs that they, or someone else, is being impacted by Quiescence, and they can certainly try to figure out who the Witch is from that, by looking at who tends to be around those places when those effects have been occurring. They're just as likely to not get the right person, especially if the Mage in question is on to them, but that brings a nice thematic element of over zealous witch hunts; the Hunters can never really be sure they got the right person, and if they're fanatical enough, they can easily slip into casting an overwide net, and even then are just as likely to be targeting entirely innocent people on the basis of coincidences that line up with a flawed understanding of the effects of Quiescence.

    Edit: Supernal Magic can be thought of a bit like the Silence from Doctor Who, without the loopholes that're used to defeat them on the show. But even without those workarounds, there's still a way for a properly attentive person to start to notice that something is going wrong, even if they'll never be able to say what that is precisely, and if they can associate a person with that sense that something has been happening, and killing them makes it stop? Well...

    So there's a pattern of too perfect coincidences, or of sudden mass memory loss, or everyone has a significantly different recounting of events of some tragedy, or everyone has exactly the same recounting, it'll twig, and then they start researching for common elements. And this one guy has been involved in half a dozen such events. So they burn his house down while he's asleep. Etc, etc.

                                                            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                                            I take issue with the premise that a person could follow that chain and still remain fully functional.

                                                            They might become fairly effective at killing mages (and probably a few innocent people who unfortunately seemed to be mages), just at the expense of almost all other coherent social engagements.

                                                            Nobody ever said that being a light shining in the darkness meant you get to still be happy or fulfilled.
                                                            True enough. More often than not, it means that you’re a torch, burning yourself up to provide the light that drives away the darkness.

                                                            Going a bit less poetically, my main concern is with a hard line being taken that says that Sleepers are metaphysically incapable of filling any role in the story other than “victim”. I’m concerned that the attitude is “Sleepers are intended to be victims; so any proposal of how they might be able to fight back on their own needs to be shot down.” As opposed to, say, “yes, there are ways for Sleepers to cope with the Lie and to be effective without resorting to undergoing some sort of supernatural transformation; but those ways exact their own toll from the Sleeper in question.” The Code? Exempts you from Breaking Points at the cost of picking up some psychological problems of your own. Recording devices? Lets the determined Sleeper learn what actually happened; but can lead to reliance on said recording devices, which can fall into the wrong hands and/or be tampered with or destroyed. After Action Reports? Requires a lot of work to recreate the events that the Lie expunged — time that could otherwise be spent doing something more useful than damage control — and even then fails to recover everything that was lost. And so on.

                                                            Becoming a Sleepwalker should be the only option open to a Sleeper for combatting the Lie that doesn’t have a downside (unless you count the fact that Sleepwalkers no longer cause Dissonance or Paradox Risk); but that’s different from saying that becoming a Sleepwalker should be the only option open to a Sleeper for combatting the Lie, period.

                                                            Going back to the original post: another way to view the Vigil in Mage terms might be to say that Practical Experience is Arcane Experience for non-Awakened people. That is, a hunter who earns Practical Experience is getting vague hints of the Supernal Truths that the Lie seeks to conceal, enough to enable the hunter to chip away at the Lie.
