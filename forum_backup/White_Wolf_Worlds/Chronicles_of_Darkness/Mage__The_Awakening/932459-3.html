                                                Yeah but at the end of the day what my character would do is up to me. What my character is obsessed with is written on the sheet, sure, but that doesn't mean I have to engage with the obsession if I feel that it suits how I want to play. Let's say my Mage has the chance to go and take care of a friend in need, or indulge in their obsession. I might choose to have them take care of their friend in need. Sure, there's a cost. That obsession gives Arcane Experience! But if I feel that my character would deny that obsession to go and take care of that friend, to try and be better than their own basic desire to explore the mysteries, then that's fine too. It's the denial of the carrot that makes the sacrifice an interesting roleplaying action; sometimes to be a good person you must turn away from something that gives a reward and indeed the game mechanises this by not giving me the Arcane Beat I would get for going after that mystery.

                                                Just because I write it on my character sheet doesn't mean that it's what my character needs to do. My character's form exists in my head, and the sheet is merely trying to express that to the best of the mechanic's ability (how suitably Platonic for Mage!) I like getting XP (my friends joke that I am a total XP whore haha) but at the same time, I like playing my characters as I feel they should be. Why do it? Because I find it more satisfying to sometimes not indulge and obsession or a vice than to indulge it, indeed for some characters, deliberately denying their desires when they clash with other priorities can be something they choose to do on purpose. And that doesn't mean that those desires are actually a cloaked need for denial. I had anger management problems, I spent a long time getting over them. I deliberately try to avoid losing my temper and indulging that desire, but that doesn't mean that I secretly deep down don't really want to be angry, I want to be disciplined. No, I do want to be angry. I just choose to try and not to be.

                                                Anyway;

                                                Mages can ignore their Obsession and avoid committing monstrous acts in the name of their obsessions in Mage the Awakening 2nd edition as it is written right now. That's playing the game, black and white (more a bluish-grey, really) in the rulebook. I can indulge my obsession, and I can choose to say my character really likes to investigate this mystery but won't do so for whatever reason. There's no mechanics to force you to do that. There's plenty of rewards for investigating those mysteries, but no way to force the character to do it or inflict negative consequences simply as a result of no doing so. You can say "you aren't playing the game!" all you like, but I am. The rules. As written.

                                                Now, I feel like actually the vast majority of characters will never go down this route. But I think it's important that they can. Because at the end of the day it means a Mage is responsible for indulging their obsessions. There's no excuse of "oh well if you don't you get forced to do so by X Y Z" like how a Werewolf can just be in a situation where they go into Kuruth because you didn't roll enough successes on your pool to resist. And that's cool, that's part of Werewolf, but equally part of Mage is how you could choose to not investigate this mystery. The only person forcing you to do it is you. And therefore when people get hurt as a by-line of a Mage's obsession, that's on them. That's their deal. You cannot blame a thing you are not responsible for, the Great Curse, Kuruth, whatever. You choose to indulge Obsessions.
