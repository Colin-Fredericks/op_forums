                                                            Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                                            I have another suggestion/request, but it would take more significant alteration to the sheet, so maybe it could be an alternative version. Sorry I'm late to the party here, I understand if this would be a lower priority. Personally, I don't see myself getting much use out of the Yantras and Magical Tools lines. Maybe one line for "Deadicated Magical Tool", but that'd be it. Everything else in that left-hand section could be shifted up, and the bottom of the second page could become a section labeled Attainments. On the left there could be a sort of table with 10 rows (each labeled with the name of one of the 10 Arcana) and 4 colums, labeled "Counterspell", "Mage Armor", "Targeted Summoning", and "Create Rote", and the fields could just be checkboxes. To the right of that table you could have three colums with 10 fillable lines each, labeled "Lesser Utility", "Greater Utility", and "Legacy".
                                                            Yantras and Magical Tools would work great written on cards - you could write down commonly used Yantras (or even their types) and Tools, and apply them to your spellcasting visually, by adding them next to each other on the table. Moving dice in pairs as a visual help for setting Spell Factors works very well*, so I think this could be a similar case. I love such things, because you can signal your mechanical actions during narration without breaking the flow - you talk and describe, while physically moving "the things", and everyone know what is going on and how many dice do you sell and what tools do you apply.

                                                            Plus a lot of people would love to have their favorite tools on a card with some sweet description or drawing on it.

                                                            *Wyrd's Cheat Sheet is great for this, as you can just put the number of dice you trade for factors on a Factor Table, so it's visually clear how you spend them and what you get in return. It help cast things on fly, because as silly as it sounds, it's easier than keeping the math in your head. It also adds a certain...ritual to the game.
