    The Bannerets of Ameretat, a Thyrsus Legacy of Deathless Warriors

    05-24-2020, 12:20 AM
    In short: A Legacy of warriors that focuses on psychological warfare and relentless resilience. The Attainments and yantras reward them for embodying the persona of an unnamable and unassailable nightmare. They utilize phylacteries to reform after death, which synergize with their other Attainments to grant them unyielding defenses and maximize the effectiveness of guerilla tactics. By skillfully striking fear into their opponents, they cut a path through battlefields with unstoppable momentum. Do you want to roleplay an agent of fear such as the Nazgûl, the dullahan or the Witch King of Angmar ? Perhaps an immortal with fearsome determination, such as Connor Mac Leod or a Dark Souls character ? Then this is the Legacy for you.

    Greetings everyone, this is an attempt to make a purely martial focused Legacy. They are inspired by the Nazgûl and Gilad The Eternal Warrior (see the Resurrective Immortality trope for more details). They specialize on psychological warfare and survival. I also added design notes for those interested. Feedback is very welcome.

    The Bannerets of Ameretat

    Origins

    "No passion so effectively robs the mind of all its powers of acting and reasoning as fear."
    - Edmund Burke

    Nicknames: Athánatoi, eternal warriors, dread marshals, undying, simurghs, dauntless
    Parentage: Thyrsus, Adamantine Arrow

    Background:The earliest mention of the Eternal Warriors can be traced to a free company cabal in the Achaemenid Empire.

    In order to compensate for their small numbers, they utilized shock and fear to rout their opponents. Those who were courageous enough to fight them often fell against the unyielding defenses of the warriors.

    Over time they became renowned for defeating powerful foes both mundane and supernatural. After witnessing their skill surviving overwhelmingly impossible odds unscathed, emperor Cyrus the Great believed them to be servants of Ameretat, the personification of immortality. He elevated them to nobility and commissioned the creation of a force of elite soldiers.

    So it was that they developed a training regiment to forge great warriors, which would become the empire's famous Immortals heavy infantry force. They further codified their martial doctrine of psychological tactics and relentless endurance into a Legacy, forging a path to immortality so the empire would always have champions ready to defend it against supernatural dangers.

    The ages passed and the empire has long since crumbled to dust. Yet the Simurghs continued to move through the battlefields of history, continuously fighting for causes they believed in.

    In time they would join other warriors whose oath of service and honor resonated with their ethos, becoming founding members of the Adamantine Arrow. Although their focus on immortality draws unease from mage society, its non-parasitic nature and their member's honorable behavior have so far allowed them to avoid being labeled as Left-Handed.

    Appearances: The Dauntless tend to favor those who demonstrate tenacity towards their goals, although this often comes with a degree of stubbornness.

    While knowledge of martial disciplines is desirable, much greater weight is placed on those that demonstrate a mix of pragmatism and honor. Mentors are loath to equip the bloodthirsty with the means to rampage through the ages.

    Members can often be found studying new combat techniques, whether it's by questing to rediscover lost systems, apprenticing under masters or developing their own personalized style, a drive to seek perfection of the arts of battle permeates the Legacy.

    In everyday life, there is as much variety in the manner in which the Simurgh dress as anyone else. When they expect battle, they tend to favor sturdy and utilitarian clothes, allowing them to embark in long quests unimpeded.

    Doctrine

    "Those who have the courage to conquer fear are made free and those who are conquered by it are made to suffer until they have the courage to defeat it, or death takes them."
    - Alexander the Great

    Prerequisites: Death 2, Air of Menace, Intimidation 2, two dots in one of the following Skills; Athletics, Brawl, Firearms, Stealth, Weaponry.
    Initiation: The prospective member prepares an iron sagaris axe with a wooden handle (symbolizing Ameretat as patroness of plants). This is traditionally customized to represent the mage and their line of mentorship.

    The candidate then imprints it with their Nimbus. Before it fades, they must use it to defeat a worthy opponent while driving fear into their hearts.

    Once this is accomplished, they must fully submerge themselves in the waters of a river of the Underworld while holding the axe (symbolizing Haurvatat, the personification of wholeness and an ever present ally of Ameretat). The ritual protects the candidate from any deleterious effects, finishing the shaping of their soul and perfecting the materials of the sagaris into the signature weapon of the Undying.

    Organization:


    Because they have a penchant to outlive their mistakes, many Athánatoi tend to become excellent teachers, having honed the craft over several lifetimes.

    It often falls to them to train new talons in the combat techniques of the Arrow. Great variety exist in their favored disciplines. Martial arts, HEMA, ballistics and more are covered based on the instructor's preferences, although veteran masters tend to draw from all fields.

    Members are sometimes called to mentor problem students, ones whose stubbornness often matches the Simurgh's own. While their teaching style can be seen as unorthodox or even brutal, it still closely adheres to the philosophy of the Order.

    Prospective initiates are trained in a variety of fields of martial combat. Common to all is an emphasis on fear tactics, on making opponents perceive them as a ghost, reapers, dullahans, a terrible thought. Destabilized foes can be more easily routed and survivors can spread the tale, possibly preventing further casualties. If all else fails, they are prepared to fallback on their resilience, to make sure they can outlast their foes.

    It's not unusual for mentors to take students along on assignments, to help them gain practical experience. Sparring via the Duel Arcane is also commonplace, teaching them to be ready for anything. By the time students are elevated to full membership, often a strong bond is forged through mutually earned respect.
    While the Legacy found chapters based on convenience and available numbers, their global headquarters are located in the Alborz mountain range. Deep within the caves lies the Keep of Mithra. Here they develop new training regimens, compile their history and test themselves in trials.

    The Keep is also employed as a safe research ground to develop new species, field supplies and combat medicine. Extensive research is focused on cryptids and ghosts, which is then leveraged by the grandmasters to create new beasts of battle and living weapons to aid in their most perilous confrontations. This gives the area the appearance of an alien ecosystem.

    Theory: When taking up arms, a warrior must become death itself. They must strike suddenly and decisively, having honed fear into a weapon. The enemy that believes the battle is lost walks the path to their defeat, and those too afraid to fight may be neutralized without unnecessary bloodshed.

    Yet a warrior must also be relentless, able to endure as they pass through a thousand battlefields, championing their causes. In the end, the best techniques are passed on by the survivors.

    Seize the power of death and forge it into the blade that safeguards life, earn the wisdom of the ages and use it to create a better future for the living.

    Magic

    “You must not fear death, my lads; defy him, and you drive him into the enemy's ranks.”
    - Napoleon Bonaparte

    Ruling Arcanum: Death
    Yantras: Chilling atmosphere (+1), succeeding in an Intimidation roll related to the spell (+2), nervous or frightened target (+1, +2 if under a fear related Condition), ritually maintained personal weapons or armor (+2), confronting a target without them knowing the Undying's identity (+2), unaware target (+2), symbols of immortality or fear (+1), drawing blood from an opponent (+2), finishing a round of combat where the Dread Marshall hasn't taken damage from attacks (+2, +3 if this was done without focusing on dodging).
    Oblations: Writing a journal or memoirs, ritually maintaining equipment, practicing combat maneuvers (martial arts katas, shooting training, war games, etc), endurance training, researching interrogation techniques, conducting an interrogation, learning or teaching combat techniques, sparring or dueling (including the Duel Arcane), investigating ancient or esoteric weapons / combat styles, researching the psychology of fear, developing new tactics based on fear.

    Attainments

    First: Banner of the Frozen Breath
    Prerequisites: Initiation

    The first step to defeating an enemy is to make sure their will crumbles and fear replaces reason. After a scene preparing to drive fear into their enemies' hearts (taking an oath, making a pre-battle speech, setting up traps, sharpening their weapons, etc), the Dread Marshal is able to amplify their killing intent. This allows them to detect all feelings of fear or unease around them, amplifying these impulses greatly. Indeed, being able to sense these fears greatly aids in hunting their foes down.

    They can selectively impose [Death] Conditions, such as Cowed, Spooked, Frightened, Madness or Nightmares, on any nearby target that feels even the slightest bit nervous. This is normally applied on themselves and their allies for maximum effect on the field, some describing the sensation as the feeling of death's shadow gripping their soul.

    This is a Potency primary Compelling of Death, which is Withstood by Composure. Reach is assigned to advanced duration and advanced scale, with the restriction of only affecting fear making up for the extra Reach.

    Optional: Life 1

    The Eternal Warrior has sharpened their very presence into a weapon, capable of commanding obedience from the weak willed and severing a foe from any aid.

    Whenever using the benefit from Air of Menace, the Eternal Warrior can choose to instead bring the entirety of their fearsome presence to bear, substituting the bonus for the lowest of [Life] and [Death].

    In addition, whenever a crowd is surrounding or protecting their target, they may roll Presence + Intimidation versus their target's Composure + Persuasion. Upon success, they will back down or offer them up.

    In combat, this can be done after hitting with an attack, the brutal demonstration being enough to make allies reconsider standing up for the target. Many a seasoned warrior lose their morale when abandoned by their comrades, who cower in the face of the wrath of an Eternal Warrior.

    Second: Cloak of Mēnõg
    Prerequisites: Death 2, Intimidation 3



    The darkness is one of mankind's oldest fears, for it hides predators within its depths. In the hands of a skilled warrior, it can conceal them from their foes or transform them into nightmares.

    After a scene of weaving the energies of Twilight into an invisible shroud, the Dread Marshall becomes able to turn invisible any part of the target that is partially obscured. Should they be fully obscured, they are rendered undetectable by mundane means and any supernatural powers provoke a Clash of Wills.

    They have fine control over the parts affected and can reflexively suppress this ability if they wish. If their face is lit only by moonlight, for example, they can make only the head invisible. This ability is sometimes attributed as the origin of tales of the dullahan or headless knight. Some use a hood to make their faces invisible, others make their eyes' pupils vanish or their hands alone manifest in midair. This is as often a tool of stealth as it is of psychological warfare. Utilizing the ability in this way grants 9-again to intimidation rolls, or 8-again if the subject is also under the benefit of the first Attainment.

    This is a Veiling of Death with Reach assigned to advanced duration.

    Optional: Life 2

    The first step to become immortal is to fortify the body, allowing it to operate in peak performance, maintain focus and stand firm against the most taxing trials.

    The Eternal Warrior spends a scene using ectoplasm to draw an icon of a winged sun disk upon a subject, with the High Speech rune of Endlessness inscribed in the center, allowing them to suspend all negative passive entropic effects upon them. Subjects do not bleed out, illnesses do not worsen, poisonous effects are suspended, they do not need to roll to stay conscious due to damage, wound penalties do not apply, they do not require food or sleep (although they may partake if they so wish) and they do not age (or decay in case of objects). Apart from granting their longevity, this ability to bestow a limited form of eternal youth and extra time is usually leveraged as a bargaining chip.

    This is a Shielding of Life and Death with Reach assigned to advanced duration.

    Third: Aegis of the Simurgh
    Prerequisites: Death 3, two dots in a second Skill among the optional prerequisite Skills

    After tempering the body, next comes fortifying the soul. Some opponents are like snakes, fear is not enough to dissuade them and they will slither away to continue causing harm. When the need is dire, more permanent sanctions might be in order. Like the legendary bird they are named after, the Simurghs of this level have resolved themselves to eliminate these threats and push on towards victory.

    Killing no longer counts as an Act of Hubris and the mage can choose up to [Death] kills to give the same amount of mana as a ritual sacrifice. This amount resets at sunset, for the Dauntless are able to find the strength to persevere even at the time where other’s spirits start to wane.

    Optional: Life 3

    Legend has it that the Simurgh is immortal, being reborn in flame upon death. While the existence of this being is debated, it has nonetheless inspired one of the Athanatoi's most famous means of immortality.

    After a scene of ritually marking a target with sigils, they can make them into a phylactery. If the Athánatoi's health pool is full of lethal or aggravated damage, they automatically spend a mana to Discorporate and reform immediately in any phylactery of their choosing, or the closest one if this happens while they are unconscious. They are fully healed and awake, even regenerating lost limbs. This can be consciously activated by paying a mana. They can have up to [Life] phylacteries at a time.

    Veteran members usually anchor one of their phylacteries to the Keep, cherishing the opportunity to test their mettle against new creatures or pass on their knowledge to others.

    This is a Duration primary Weaving of Life and Death (giving the ability to Discorporate like a ghost) with Reach assigned to advanced duration.

    Fourth: Paths of the Fravashis
    Prerequisites: Death 4, Intimidation 4

    The Fravashis guide the spirits of the dead to the afterlife and allow an echo of them to return as ghosts, to resolve unfinished businesses. A sagacious Dauntless can leverage their secret roads to great effect.

    Some utilize them to bypass enemy fortifications, springing forth ambushes out of thin air. A few members suggest this gave birth to the myth of dullahans effortlessly passing through gates.

    Others utilize them to escape explosions or dangerous environments. There are even a few that use them as part of their psychological warfare tactics. One famous case involved terrifying a target by utilizing it alongside the Cloak of Mēnõg to create an effect of "nothing under the mask", similar to the ending of the Mask of the Red Death.

    This replicates Ghost Gate with Reach assigned to instant use and sending a target to Death attuned Twilight without a portal.

    Optional: Life 4


    The Dread Marshalls have learned how to amplify their power by channeling it through their phylacteries, allowing them to assume a form that embodies fear and martial prowess.

    When at least one phylactery exists, they can use an action to become a being halfway between flesh and ghost, Materialized by default. They are not bound to an anchor, do not possess a Ban, Bane, bleed Essence or have an Essence pool. Any Life or Death spells that seek to affect them require equal aptitude in both Arcana, although the Legacy’s Attainments affect them normally.

    Their Rank is half their Gnosis, rounded down. They receive the max number of Numina, Influences and Manifestations for their Rank, which are fueled by Mana instead of Essence and are activated by rolling Gnosis + [Death]. Their stats are derived by dream form rules.

    In addition, they envelop themselves in dread armor, which grants them [Life]/[Death] points of Ballistic/General armor that stacks with physical armor, spells and other Attainments.

    They have greater control over their weapons and can cause them to inflict bashing or lethal damage (even fists and guns).

    If their last phylactery is destroyed, this Attainment is forcefully ended.

    This is a custom Patterning of Life and Death with Reach assigned to instant use and advanced duration, with the restriction compensating for the extra Reach.

    Fifth: Decoction of the Gaokarena

    Prerequisites: Death 5, two dots in a third Skill among the optional prerequisite Skills

    The Gaokarena is a tree whose essence is fabled to bring back the dead, grant immortality and cure any disease. The greatest masters of the Undying have survived enough to gain a deep understanding of the borders between life and death, becoming able to channel a similar power to heal, restore youth or afford some time for the dead to put their affairs in order.

    This replicates Deny the Reaper with Reach assigned to instant use, advanced duration and bringing back the recently dead.

    The resurrected do not suffer the effects of soullessness (although they still lack a soul). However, the revival requires a target to be assigned as a phylactery sustaining the subject's life. Should the target be destroyed, the Attainment ends and the subject dies again.

    Optional: Life 5


    Even the greatest combat virtuosos cannot win every battle alone or be prepared to champion every cause. To this end, the grandmasters of the Eternal Warriors have learned to create comrades in arms, bringing forth mighty champions to overturn even the most dire situations.

    After using a point of mana and spending a scene sculpting their design into existence, they are able to create a being halfway between ghosts and living beings. They possess sentience and the stats of ghosts, but the body of living beings, having no Essence or anchors. They have Numina and Influences, but fuel them with Willpower. Affecting them with Life or Death spells requires equal dots in both Arcana. Their Rank is equal to half the Athánatoi's Gnosis, rounded down. Rather than Manifestations, they are granted [Life] extra features as per Transform Life.

    However, once created, they are independent beings and the mage is therefore unable to simply end the Attainment. While they are initially loyal and obey the mage, this may change depending on how they are treated. Sometimes the Eternal Warriors must take on the responsibility of putting down a creature twisted by the callous actions of one of their own.

    This is a Making of Life and Death, with Reach assigned to Lasting duration and the restrictions making up for the extra Reach.

      What were you thinking design wise ?

      The first Attainment was inspired by the Black Breath, the aura of fear that the Nazgûl project. Creating those effects normally would require a Ruling, so instead I focused on the ability of Compelling to create a condition by intensifying existing phenomena, in this case the sensation of dread.

      But utilizing sensory range would not have the battlefield shaking effect I was looking for. To solve this, I turned to the Oath of Conquest paladin for inspiration and decided to make it into an AoE effect. A restriction on intensifying only fear would provide the extra Reach for it to be able to affect a reasonably large zone. By selecting which targets to afflict, it avoids friendly fire. Allowing them to "smell" fear also felt very thematic for a hunting nightmare.

      The name for the Attainment itself was inspired by the Lothric War Banner of Dark Souls 3. A magical banner that inspired fear in a mercenary company's enemies seemed very appropriate. Pirate flags served this purpose, inspiring dread in the targeted vessel's crew, sometimes enough to make them immediately surrender.

      The first optional Attainment was a challenge. Heightened Senses, which is duration primary, didn't seem very appropriate. Speak with Beasts would be to a degree, allowing them to literally send the hounds and crows upon foes or even spy on them via birds or rats. But there were no other Attainments to build on this angle.

      The solution was to make it intensify the Legacy's specialties. Now a skilled Eternal Warrior would be able to get a nice bonus on their intimidation rolls by using Life and Death to optimize their body language to seem even more intimidating. They also benefit from a similar ability as Dalu form werewolves, being able to separate their opponents from help, building on the sense of fear by feeling like they are being hunted while no one dares interfere.

      Adding Air of Menace to the prerequisites also has the benefit of complimenting the themes. Now they are able to deescalate potential fights by just being that intimidating. But it also means they have to deal with the social consequences of being a terrifying individual, which is part and parcel of embodying the Legacy's Truth.

      The second Death Attainment was inspired by the Skulker feat from D&D, which is a ton of fun since it allows rogues to sneak around in more scenarios. Death governs darkness and deep shadows can hide a person, making them visually absent. So you could therefore use a Veiling to intensify absence when some is already present (in this case, the target being partially obscured). When seen through the lens of a fear focused Legacy, giving fine control of the effect opens up all sorts of creative uses (such as making sure the enemy takes down one of their own that has been disguised by it).

      When you see the Nazgûl attack at Weathertop, you see them fade in as they start encircling the party. That seemed like a good sign that I was on the right track. The name for this Attainment comes from the spirit world in Zoroastrianism.

      The second Life Attainment covers eternal youth. It's inspired by Veil of Moments, but utilizes both Life and Death to fine tune the effect such that beneficial things like healing or spending XP are not arrested. Now they can march through a desert without food or water, train as much as they want or obsessively research through the night. It's a first minor step towards immortality, but also a useful tool and powerful bargaining chip.

      The third Death Attainment comes from the earliest iterations of the Legacy. It focuses on the trope of the hero becoming a one man army and pushing through the hordes with sheer determination. Being able to gain ritual sacrifice level of mana should be able to grant them the breather to win or survive, but only if they play it smart. However, this has a clear potential for abuse and therefore was restricted to a couple of times a day. The restriction itself is inspired by the challenge kirins face to tame youma in The Twelve Kingdoms.

      The idea is to give them some aid in standing stubbornly against the enemy, even if it means killing. It's not supposed to incentivize murderhobo behavior, as the Appearances section subtly hints.

      The third optional Life Attainment is the whole reason for this Legacy’s existence. It's inspired by how Sauron uses the One Ring as a lifeline. While it is a very potent effect, it's surprisingly a Weaving. Discorporation is a way ghosts and spirits suffer a "small death", so to add that to the mage simply requires adding that characteristic to a living being's pattern.

      The phylacteries act as a point of reference to where they will reform. The simplest use of the active discorporation is as a means to escape captivity. It also allows inventive players to quickly teleport around by moving them, but this requires them to take on board the risk of something destroying them. This opens up some possibilities of creative problem solving while presenting an interesting drawback.

      The cost is inspired by the Cult of the Doomsday Clock's own death defying Attainment, although this one has a much more powerful healing effect. The maximum number of phylacteries is placed so this Attainment is not powerful to the point of being broken. Opponents can kill them, but it takes a lot of work (or someone well versed in Space magic), which is exactly the feel I am aiming for.

      The fourth Death Attainment is simple, yet remarkably useful. Reliability entering Death Twilight allows them to walk through all sorts of fortifications and dangers. It also permits them to sneak up on targets. Now they can, for example, unpin a grenade, teleport it to the Material and then move the cabal into the fight. The shock and awe means they can open conflicts by disrupting an opponent's cohesion and dominate both the practical and psychological aspects from the first move. A fringe benefit is that now opponents that intimidate using a blade to the throat or a gun to the head are rendered laughably ineffective against them ("what happened to my weapon ?").

      The fourth optional Attainment is a stealthy reference to how Sauron used the One Ring as an amplifier for his might. I really enjoy how Legacies such as the Katsinam Suukya and Bene Elohim have transformative Attainments.

      This specific variation focuses on defense. Allowing them to take a Manifestation form means that anything but specially prepared, anti-supernatural gear will only do bashing damage. The necessity of both Arcana to affect them also grants extra safety.

      The dread armor serves as a way to reinforce the theme of being these impossibly resilient juggernauts, especially when stacked with the Manifestation's damage degradation, defensive spells, mage armor, defensive combat styles, etc. If they reinforce this image, the yantras are there to reward them.

      The ghost powers and Manifestations allow them great flexibility, but require high Gnosis to reap the full benefits. Dream form stats encourage them to diversify a bit. I see them as a Physical Attributes primary, Social secondary and Mental tertiary Legacy. This encourages them to invest a bit in being charismatic or intelligent warriors. The control of the damage type gives them a lot of flexibility when they want to send a message. The phylacteries continue their role as an exploitable weakness and counterbalance to the benefits.

      Ideally, if someone is brave enough to engage a Dread Marshal who has mastered their battle form, they will feel like Fingolfin facing Morgoth. Getting to that point, however, will require a lot of dedication from the Eternal Warrior.

      Deny The Reaper seemed like a perfect fit for the capstone Death Attainment. It heals all damage at no cost, can restore youth (an extremely valuable bargaining chip) and generally allows the mage to recover very quickly in combat. If their companions have any breath of life left in them, that is sufficient to fully revitalize them.

      Being able to bring the dead back seemed like a very appropriate expression of mastery for immortals. So the extra Reach could be justified by creating a lesser phylactery for them. Even when this would have to be reinforced once a week at best (using the extra factors' pseudo-dice on duration), it would still be very useful.

      The final optional Attainment, in its most benign form, extends their power outwards. It allows them to make sure life endures and thrives, no matter the challenge. This can allow them to create crops that resist every climate or grow any produce the farmer wants, restore extinct species or generate ones with novel abilities, such as plants capable of filtering sea water or curing a disease. At its most hubristic, it allows them to make the ADAM producing slugs from Rapture as a means to grant humans immortality or the ability to evolve on their own terms. Others may construct the bioengineered beings that the Venusians utilized in Battle Angel Alita: Last Order.

      When this ability is turned to combat uses, it allows them to bring forth fell beasts and any other types of monsters, mighty creatures or living weapons they can imagine, such as the biots of the Yuuzhan Vong. Because they are complete beings, it means they can pass on their traits forward, allowing the Undying to create more advanced ones over the generations. These beings wield fierce abilities and are tougher to affect with magic due to their dual nature.

      The balancing factor is that they are independent. While they initially start with a positive relationship to their mage, those that mistreat them or guide them incorrectly will have to slay the dragon they created themselves.

      The original idea for this Legacy was inspired by Gilad The Eternal Warrior, who embodies the Regenerative Immortality trope. The first iteration was based on Oisín, its founders having delved into the Hedge far enough to learn the truth of his legend and decide to outshine his exploits. They would make it so their soul would be linked to any part of their body that remained and then regrow their body from it. Determining how that could be judged mechanically proved difficult, so I tried a solution akin to the Clone spell from D&D. But the mechanics for growing the clones also proved complicated to codify.

      The Dark Souls series provided a solution. By setting a respawn point, it allowed them to be easily restored. The Discorporate Manifestation proved an elegant way to do this and the Shifting Sands death defying Attainment from the Cult of the Doomsday Clock provided an effective framework for it.

      The Oisín based Legacy just used healing and transformation, making them feel like a variation of the Orphans of Proteus. Redesigning them was done by realizing that an immortal would use every possible trick to survive. If they could be intimidating, they could drive the danger away. They would make themselves into reapers, become death itself to demonstrate their mastery over it. But they are warriors first, not scholars of fear akin to the Fangs of Mara. The Nazgûl proved a perfect framework for this, since they are warriors that specialize in unraveling their foes’ minds with terror and they utilize the aesthetic of the horseman of death.

      The next step was to create a background for them. I wanted the Legacy to feel ancient and make this traceable even in the feel of the Attainments. The first thing was to determine what would cause its development, which could be explained by a small number of mages in a dangerous lifestyle, such as town defenders or mercenaries. The Persian Immortals were an ancient group that existed in a time of constant expansionism, so they provided an interesting historical connection. Zoroastrian mythology helped flesh out their mystical correspondences.

      The signature weapon of the Immortals, the sagaris axe, proved to resonate well with the theme and so was integrated into their initiation. An axe is not as expensive to fabricate as a sword, it can be thrown (although the sagaris is usually not weighted for it) and is a versatile survival tool. The sagaris have the added advantage of possessing a cutting (or sometimes a hammer) side and an armor piercing, ice pick like side. It's very utilitarian and practical, which fits their style of combat pragmatism well.

      Their oblations reflect their stubborn dedication to martial arts, psychological warfare and outliving their foes. The memoir oblation is there to create plot hooks. It could, for example, allow the cabal to discover the journal of a particularly old Athánatoi, written in Egyptian during the Bronze Age Collapse.

      Lastly, their yantras incentivize striking suddenly and becoming a horror that a foe cannot name. A Dread Marshall may do something as simple as use a microphone with a voice distortion to speak to a target. But they may also dress like a Nazgûl, activate partial invisibility, disguise their voice with a spell and come to them in this terrifying visage (or from Twilight to speak with a Moros). In combat, the more panic they sow and the more they sell this image of being an unstoppable force, the more they are rewarded.

      And that’s it, hope you had as much fun reading it as I had writing it. Thanks in advance for the feedback.

                                  Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                  (I do wonder sometimes why more people don't seem to Google these terms out of curiosity for their origins).
                                  I did, which is what I based my post on. The original ancient Greek just means amulet, and when it was later borrowed into latin it came to mean reliquary. Its use in judaism came from a greek translation of the New Testament, probably based on how the writer/translator viewed the use of tefillin. (Phylactery is also used as a term for speech scrolls in art to illustrate speech or song, but that's mostly irrelevant here.)

                                  But out of curiousity I researched how the word came into tt-rpg jargon, which actually is a fifth meaning separate from the four mentioned above. My current understanding is this:
                                  Phylacteries showed up for the first time in AD&D where they were visually described identically to tefillin, but their usage were as religious treasures that could be worn by clerics to gain some benefits (still, YMMV on how appropriate that is) with only an offhand mention saying that liches had one (probably because some of them used to be clerics when alive). Over time and editions phylacteries became more and more connected to liches and became the source of their power rather than just a piece of magical equipment they happened to have, but they also had their descriptions slowly revised to include amulets and regular jewellry as potential forms. Unfortunately fifth edition actually took a step back to make them more similar to tefillin rather than trying to match one of the more generic meanings of the word.

                                                  Leave it to me. I appreciate your bravery and valor, but you are too... mortal for this one. Only immortal can kill another immortal.

                                                  Those Who Tore Out Their Death
                                                  There are some people who just don’t die. They refuse to follow the rules written for everyone else. Many Awakened studied them; some of them tried to replicate their feats with their magic. Usually those attempts are failures; while Supernal magic proposes many ways for immortality, it's not good at imitating purely Fallen ones, making those a curious Mysteries for mages obsessed with secrets of life and death, but not more.

                                                  Once, a long time ago, a man have seeked a way to become warrior immortal. He found one of Eternals, learned his secrets and created a phylactery, containing his death inside. He went on living and fighting for centuries, drifting from one culture into another, being part of countless battles, amassing riches and losing them to ravages of the time, all while his phylactery was safely buried in a hidden cave. Eventually Eternal was tired of his life, and was prepared to leave this existence, but one last mystery called to him, leading him to his Awakening. After returning from Stygia, he started to study the world and himself with his newfound Mage Sight, and found out, to his great surprise, that he was still immortal. He tracked his phylactery once again and found out that it has transformed; not only did it contained his death, it now contained his own soul, transformed by the container, warping it. In it, former Eternal, still Immortal, read the secrets of his new Legacy. Back then, being a Lich wasn't a stigma amongst Awakened as much as it is now, in modern times. First Immortal was willing and able to join mages who were ancestors of first Adamantine Arrows. Ever since then, Immortals were on the frontline of every major conflict in history, fighting wars both mundane and occult, testing the limits of their own immortality and finding ways to cut others short.

                                                  There are requirements, of course. Immortality is something many Awakened wish for, and Legacy carefully chooses candidates to avoid those who could choose a path of Left Hand. Almost all candidates come from Adamantine Arrows; they have to be brave, driven and loyal. They also should not be afraid of death; a point where many potential Liches are cut off. Immortals are quick to point out that they are not, in fact, truly immortal; it's very hard to kill them, but possibilities of Arcana are endless, and joining the Legacy all but guarantees that there will be a lot of those who will try to. Candidate also have to have exceptionally strong willpower to withstand trauma of being killed again, again and again on the battlefield. There is another reason for such high requirements; if Legacy would grow too big, Pentacle mages would raise their heads and ask, why near-heretical Legacy is allowed to prosper. There shouldn't be too many immortals.

                                                  Within their Legacy, Immortals contain many dangerous secrets. They know much about immortality and immortal creatures - including ways to cut such immortality short. Only real ending will do, though - Immortals despise options such as trapping their opponents indefinitely, transforming them into harmless forms or putting them into vegetable states; they are quick to point out that those always can be reversed, slinging problem into the future - which Immortals are likely to see for themselves. Immortals are known for being a Liches who excel at killing other Liches - reputation they are taking pride in.

                                                  Legacy also often deals with it's own kind. Awakenings amongst fellow immortals are extremely rare due to them barely counting as human for mysterious and elusive perceptions of Watchtowers - but they do happen, as it happened with Legacy founder. Legacy always seeks those exceptions out and studies both their Awakening and their condition, if it survived the Awakening. There are rumors that Immortals are seeking something; a final theory of death and mortality, which could grant eternal and perfect immortality for anyone worthy enough... or ensure that everything and everything will, eventually, pass on.

                                                  ​​Origins
                                                  Nicknames: Immortals, Puppeteers, Koshei, Puppet Knights (derogatory)

                                                  Parentage: Moros, Adamantine Arrows

                                                  Background: Immortals come from all walks of life, but those with military and law enforcement backgrounds are most common due to Order bias and requirement with marital skill and exceptional willpower.

                                                  Appearances: While younger Immortals still associate themselves with their puppet bodies and don't differ in looks from any other mage, older ones often grow beyond them, having lost many and being sure they will lose even more. They dress functional and live in spartan-like conditions; pleasures or pains of the flesh don't sway them, often making them uncanny and scary.

                                                  Doctrine
                                                  Prerequisites: Death 2, Resolve 4+, Composure 4+, Occult 2 with specialization in Immortality, Soul Stones or Souls.

                                                  Initiation: To join Legacy, future Immortal should create his own phylactery. To do that, he must successfully create а soul stone and imbue it with the blood of immortal creature. Any creature unable to die from old age will do: vampire, Tremere, other Liches, but future Immortal is unable to use blood of another member of his future Legacy. If imbuement is successful, soul stone becomes a phylactery containing soul of future Immortal, which is changed by it's container. Immortal can take that blood by force; this is traditional way, and Immortal is expected to permanently kill creature before or after draining it of blood. There is an alternative way to create a phylactery, which is to find phylactery of an Eternal and attempt to make it the soul stone of mage. If future Immortal is successful, his soul defeats death of an Eternal, killing him and inducting now-Immortal into the Legacy.

                                                  Organization: Immortals stick to each other; while they are valued in the Arrows, other Pentacle mages see them as dangerous Liches, and won't tolerate them, if they start to grow in political power. Immortals respect each other on Gnosis and age within Legacy; mentor-student bonds are strong and don't dissolve long after students grow beyond apprenticeships. That being said, they also try not to form Legacy-exclusive cabals and spread out far enough so that any witch hunt won't put whole Legacy into danger.

                                                  Theory: Immortals believe that to fight in Awakened conflicts, they have to be bodily immortal; everyone mortal just doesn't have the chance to raise his Gnosis high enough to be a real participant in fight for the humanity. They also believe that mortality is necessary for souls to be able to be released from bodies and purified in Stygia, but frailty of human body is imposed by Exarchs to keep humans afraid and within grip of the Lie. They believe that perfect, final theory of death and mortality exists somewhere within Supernal, and finding it will heal the wound deal to the Fallen, shaking the Lie and restoring pre-Fall cosmic order of life and death. Then, they can finally allow themselves to pass away.

                                                  Magic
                                                  Ruling Arcana: Death/Life

                                                  Yantras: Phylacteries, symbols of immortality and durability, works of art and artifice

                                                  Oblations: Near-death experiences, killing and resurrecting their puppet bodies, honing and repairing their phylacteries, creating works of art and artifice with their hands

                                                  Attainments

                                                  First Attainment: Soul Gem
                                                  Prerequisites: Initiation

                                                  The very first Attainment makes Immortal worthy this title. During his initiation he creates phylactery, which becomes vessel for his soul, while his body becomes a mere puppet for it to command. This gives a number of benefits:
                                                  • Puppet body does not age and is are immune to all natural illnesses and diseases.
                                                  • Puppet body regenerates slowly, healing one point of Bashing damage every two minutes, one point of Lethal damage every 8 hours, and one point of Aggravated damage every day.
                                                  • Puppet body regrows lost limbs and organs as well, regrowing a lost limb in two months and a destroyed organ in weeks.
                                                  • Puppet body is able recover from death, coming back to life weak and injured, but alive as soon as it restores at least one point of aggravated damage.
                                                  However, this is not without downsides. Immortal how has it's phylactery, which is vulnerable, providing following downsides:
                                                  • If their phylactery is destroyed, Immortal instantly dies in a fashion that echoes the destruction of their phylactery.
                                                  • If their phylactery is damaged, the Immortal suffers a similar level of damage that cannot be healed except by repairing phylactery with either Matter magic or skilled and patient hands.
                                                  • If phylactery is removed from puppet body for more than a hundred feet apart, puppet body goes comatose. Immortal's consciousness still resides within phylactery, but normally lacks any sensory input.
                                                  • Phylactery now counts as permanent Soul Jar for soul of the Immortal. If soul is removed by any means from phylactery, Immortal instantly dies as if it was destroyed.
                                                  • Phylactery is considered to have Connected sympathy to all current puppet bodies.
                                                  • Immortal is not able to create a soul stone by any means, but phylactery itself is counted as one; Immortal is able to create Demesne from it, or use it as magical tool, if he wishes.​
                                                  Second Attainment: Enshrine the Spark
                                                  Prerequisites: Death 2, Occult 3

                                                  At this point, Immortal perfects the act of creating phylactery and is able to transfer his death and the soul from one phylactery from another. By spending a scene of effort and point of Mana, Immortal creates new phylactery for his soul; he is able to instantly transfer his soul and death from old phylactery to new one by touching it. Old phylactery is still able to hold his death, and Immortal can switch phylactery as many times as he wants to. Phylactery must not have a Size over 5 or lesser than 1; it can be made of any material (most Immortals use works of art, jewelry or magical tools).

                                                  ...and then I hit the writer's block and never wrote more Attainments. Will surely return later, when at least one Immortal appears in my games, but that's it for now.​

If future Immortal is successful, his soul defeats death of an Eternal, killing him and inducting now-Immortal into the Legacy.

                                                      Organization: Immortals stick to each other; while they are valued in the Arrows, other Pentacle mages see them as dangerous Liches, and won't tolerate them, if they start to grow in political power. Immortals respect each other on Gnosis and age within Legacy; mentor-student bonds are strong and don't dissolve long after students grow beyond apprenticeships. That being said, they also try not to form Legacy-exclusive cabals and spread out far enough so that any witch hunt won't put whole Legacy into danger.

                                                      Theory: Immortals believe that to fight in Awakened conflicts, they have to be bodily immortal; everyone mortal just doesn't have the chance to raise his Gnosis high enough to be a real participant in fight for the humanity. They also believe that mortality is necessary for souls to be able to be released from bodies and purified in Stygia, but frailty of human body is imposed by Exarchs to keep humans afraid and within grip of the Lie. They believe that perfect, final theory of death and mortality exists somewhere within Supernal, and finding it will heal the wound deal to the Fallen, shaking the Lie and restoring pre-Fall cosmic order of life and death. Then, they can finally allow themselves to pass away.

                                                      Magic
                                                      Ruling Arcana: Death/Life

                                                      Yantras: Phylacteries, symbols of immortality and durability, works of art and artifice

                                                      Oblations: Near-death experiences, killing and resurrecting their puppet bodies, honing and repairing their phylacteries, creating works of art and artifice with their hands

                                                      Attainments

                                                      First Attainment: Soul Gem
                                                      Prerequisites: Initiation

                                                      The very first Attainment makes Immortal worthy this title. During his initiation he creates phylactery, which becomes vessel for his soul, while his body becomes a mere puppet for it to command. This gives a number of benefits:
                                                      • Puppet body does not age and is are immune to all natural illnesses and diseases.
                                                      • Puppet body regenerates slowly, healing one point of Bashing damage every two minutes, one point of Lethal damage every 8 hours, and one point of Aggravated damage every day.
                                                      • Puppet body regrows lost limbs and organs as well, regrowing a lost limb in two months and a destroyed organ in weeks.
                                                      • Puppet body is able recover from death, coming back to life weak and injured, but alive as soon as it restores at least one point of aggravated damage.
                                                      However, this is not without downsides. Immortal how has it's phylactery, which is vulnerable, providing following downsides:
                                                      • If their phylactery is destroyed, Immortal instantly dies in a fashion that echoes the destruction of their phylactery.
                                                      • If their phylactery is damaged, the Immortal suffers a similar level of damage that cannot be healed except by repairing phylactery with either Matter magic or skilled and patient hands.
                                                      • If phylactery is removed from puppet body for more than a hundred feet apart, puppet body goes comatose. Immortal's consciousness still resides within phylactery, but normally lacks any sensory input.
                                                      • Phylactery now counts as permanent Soul Jar for soul of the Immortal. If soul is removed by any means from phylactery, Immortal instantly dies as if it was destroyed.
                                                      • Phylactery is considered to have Connected sympathy to all current puppet bodies.
                                                      • Immortal is not able to create a soul stone by any means, but phylactery itself is counted as one; Immortal is able to create Demesne from it, or use it as magical tool, if he wishes.​
                                                      Second Attainment: Enshrine the Spark
                                                      Prerequisites: Death 2, Occult 3

                                                      At this point, Immortal perfects the act of creating phylactery and is able to transfer his death and the soul from one phylactery from another. By spending a scene of effort and point of Mana, Immortal creates new phylactery for his soul; he is able to instantly transfer his soul and death from old phylactery to new one by touching it. Old phylactery is still able to hold his death, and Immortal can switch phylactery as many times as he wants to. Phylactery must not have a Size over 5 or lesser than 1; it can be made of any material (most Immortals use works of art, jewelry or magical tools).

                                                      A fascinating Legacy, they probably have a deep psychological effect when they react to lethal injuries and great danger with an unnerving calm.

                                                      The possibility of becoming a lich hunter if you defeated one must be a double edged sword, anyone who pulls it off would earn the caution, if not the outrage, of other Legacy members. Being both hunters of immortals and seekers of a better cycle of mortality must place them in direct conflict with the Psychopomp.

                                                      Having the phylactery within 100 meters must be nerve wrecking, you need to plan carefully to make sure it isn't destroyed. Anyone who is good at Space and knows what to look for must be terrifying.

                                                      I think you could build up on both the puppet angle and the immortal hunter one. An example would be a Ruling of Death that prevents healing for its duration, it would be scary for a vampire if all blood healing required a Clash of Wills.

                                                      At high level, Control Instincts with instant use, advanced duration, affecting supernaturals and changing primary factor to Potency could be terrifying, allowing them to puppet others with the same ease as their normal vessel. It requires an aimed spell or touch, but its still a very powerful tool to disarm a target or turn them against their allies.

                                                      They could use a Weaving of Death to make a voodoo doll, linking the damage of an item to the subject, similar to what happens with their phylactery. An example would be burning a letter in the middle of the night, which would make a blood bather suddenly be on fire.

                                                      The appearances section suggests they might develop a way to Possess others or create decoy bodies that could allow them to venture beyond their phylactery's range.
