                                            Sorry, East Coast, of course - early morning writting.
                                            So, couple things I want to point out, because I live in western Massachusetts and have lived in Vermont, and have an insider's knowledge (although I admit I am very, very shaky on my knowledge of Mage and of Boston proper).

                                            -While Montpelier is the capital of Vermont, it's not the largest city. That belongs to Burlington, which has more colleges, more culture, more people, more shopping, it's just generally more in every way. I'd guess that, unless the local Mages are real sticklers for keeping their head city the same as their capital, they'd choose Burlington instead. (The order of city-sizes goes Burlington, South Burlington, Rutland, Barre, Montpelier. South Burlington is really just an extension of Burlington. Rutland is a scuzzy city that I'm pretty sure is run by vampires. Barre and Montpelier are also basically the same city.)
                                            -I'm pretty sure Maine is similar to Vermont, in that it's capital Augusta is not the most populous or a good place to rule from. I'd pick Portland, Bangor, maybe Auburn. For New Hampshire, you'll want Manchester. Providence for Rhode Island and Hartford for Connecticut are both the largest and the capital, so those work fine.
                                            -Splitting out areas by state makes a kind of sense, until you look at population. Vermont has very few people in it; their total population is only a little more than the city of Boston not including suburbs (0.6M each). New Hampshire (1.3M), Maine (1.3M), and Rhode Island (1.0M) have about twice as many people as Vermont; Connecticut has triple that again (3.6M); and Massachusetts has double that (6.7M). It might make sense population- and area- wise to group Vermont, New Hampshire, and Maine into one area, Connecticut and Rhode Island into another, and Massachusetts into a third, at least starting out.
                                            -Of course, all the states are super independent; I know Vermonters get pissy when they're forgotten about or glossed over because they're so tiny. It could easily be each state has it's own deacon. If you go that route, though, western Massachusetts also gets pissy when Boston/eastern Mass gets all the money and attention. They should either have their own Deacon, or be trying to create one. (Plot hook?) The area has very different interests and needs than the city, and it's a real thing that we get overlooked.
                                            -While New York isn't part of New England (because it was settled by the Dutch first), upstate New York has a lot more cultural similarities with Vermont/New Hampshire/Maine than with NYC proper, and might make sense to include as a region. (This would be the area of New York located north of the city, hence "up".)
                                            -There's a lot of MA cities that are very full of crime. Holyoke, Springfield, New Bedford, Chelsea, Brockton, Worchester (pronounced wu-ster), probably more that I'm forgetting. But yeah, if you want peaceful small towns, you can do it in Mass, buuuuut be careful of exactly where. Vermont is much more back-country, but there's a lot of drugs. (Fun fact: Vermont has the least strict gun laws in the area, due to all the game hunters in the state. So there's a whole back market of getting guns there, then driving them down to states like Massachusetts that have much stricter laws. New Hampshire has the least strict fireworks laws, so the same thing happens with them.)
                                            -Awakening in Vermont leads to a higher-than-average chance of being a Thyrsus. Seriously the state is so full of hippies. It's against the law to have billboards there. But hunting is still a huge thing.
                                            -Please please please don't use the witch trials as a thing. There's loads of actual historical reasons for things to be weird in the area, given who settled where and when, how we drove out/infected with plague the native folk, how there was a fair amount of eugenics going on after WW1, etc. The witch trials are overdone, and were honestly about claiming land and political power. If you've got a chance to look at the Dark Era's stuff for that, it treats it pretty well, but I strongly encourage you not to make the witch trials a "real" thing. (I'm still pissed at Rowling for doing that. Also for her entire treatment of US magical history. It is some painful bull. But that's offtopic.)

                                            Sorry for the spew, but I'm happy to add/clarify if there's anything you want to know more about.

                                                    I realize that Boston Unveiled uses the witch trials, and I don't like it. I have not read it, but it sounds like it's making the witch trials "real" (in the sense of "there was actual magic and said magic-users were persecuted and the cause of this"). Which, no, historically, that's completely wrong, and it's been done a lot, and only rarely well. If you go to Salem, and go on a tour, they'll tell you this, that the trials were about politics, about the conflict between Salem Town and Salem Village, and that deaths happened when trials got out of hand. The whole thing gets a lot more attention then it needs (similar to Roanoke colony, but that's another tangent), while other options remain unexplored.

                                                    Like, if you want New England to be a land of mystery, how about this: Most of the native tribes in New England are extinct. Before white folks came to settle, a lot of them got wiped out by a plague. Once white people got here, they started expediting the process, trading tribes blankets infected with smallpox, enslaving or killing those who were left. For tribes that got wise, they ended up getting driven westward, through broken treaties and bloody bloody Andrew Jackson.

                                                    So, there's this whole land who's magical experts have all been killed or driven off. Of course there's mysteries aplenty; none of the settlers know jack about the area, and so you have your mystery-rich area for Mages. And the first folks to "discover" the answers get a lot of prestige, which gets passed down through history, and so they become a big deal. (And you have to take care to treat this one with respect, too, but this is a whole area of history that's been looked at a lot less than the witch trials.)

                                                    The witch trials for New England are like Vikings for Iceland, or the Inquisition for Spain, or like Guy Fawkes for England: yes, they are a thing that happened, but they get waaaaaaaaaaaay more focus then they proportionally deserve, at the expense of a lot of other history, and have been done and done again with varying levels of respect and accuracy.

                                                    Rowling tangent: the MACUSA can't exist. The timeline doesn't work. Unless the wizards of the 1700s were better at seeing the future than Trelawny was, they couldn't have known how the war was going to turn out. You can't govern an area the size of the US with the structure that only barely works in a place the size of England. The whole thing is just...My dislike is not because it's cheesy, my dislike is because it can't work, not even with the normal suspension of disbelief I grant such things. The reason a pyramid-like structure kinda works in Mage is that 1) it's not actually ruling everyone, and 2) all mages have a vested interest in staying hidden because reality punches them in the face if they don't.

                                                    But that's the last I'm going to say about witch trials, outside of any factual stuff, because while I'd like to convince you otherwise, I don't want to derail the thread or spoil the fun of your game or tell you there's things you can't do. Honestly, I'm pretty thrilled that you're running a New England game, and I'd like to help with authentic stuff that makes the game more fun.
