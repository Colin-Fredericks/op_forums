            The notion that horror requires strong mechanics to showcase itself is completely missing the point. ALL the game lines have their own forms of horror and how well the storyteller displays this has absolutely nothing to do with the mechanics of the game. It has everything to do with how the storyteller describes situations and circumstances in the game. In Mage, it's the storyteller's job to get the players to be disgusted with their own choices and thereby showing how mages are monsters. It isn't the job of the developers of the game to scare you, that's your job. It isn't the job of the developers to make you disgusted with your own choices, that's your job. Furthermore, attempting to place more stringent mechanics on the setting to enforce the horror of the setting actually detracts from said horror when the type of horror you're trying to present is that of how people hurt other people by being selfish! You don't get players to be horrified by what they make their characters do by placing stronger mechanics that detract from their capability to do horrific things. Nor do you get players to be horrified by what they make their characters do by introducing mechanics that "present horror." Trying to enforce horror through mechanics simply is a losing battle. Mechanics are like facts whereas horror is emotional. If you're trying to get your players to be horrified (no matter where that horror is coming from), you're shooting yourself in the foot by trying to do so with mechanics instead of vivid descriptions, plot twists, and graphic details.

            If showcasing the consequences of horrific actions isn't your cup of tea, fine. But please don't suggest that there isn't horror within Mage or that it isn't actual horror. Normal people doing evil things is quite horrific as can be seen whenever you look long enough at history.

            As to the topic on hand, yes, I believe the op has it wrong. He has it wrong within the very title of his post. There is no line for Mage to be brought into. That implies that the developers of the game went rogue. Chronicles of Darkness is specifically designed to not have game lines that are balanced against each other. Suggesting that one game line needs to be strengthened or nerfed to bring it into line implies that you don't understand the intent of the developers and what the games are actually trying to do. Every game has its own themes and moods and any time anyone decides to play a crossover game doing so will explicitly detract from at least one of those themes or moods, which goes directly against game design.

            Now maybe you want to go against game design. Maybe you really do want to have that crossover game. Sometimes that is fun, and honestly, the point of these games is to have fun. So you decide to have a crossover game. Ok. Fine. Great even. Here's my point though: if you don't know how to change the abilities of one splat to make them weaker or stronger you probably haven't played the game long enough to appreciate it for its own merits. Now, it's perfectly possible that you just don't like a game for its own merits. However, by the time you've played a game long enough that you COULD appreciate it for its own merits (presuming you don't have a natural inclination against said game, which of course just begs the question of "Why did you want to play this game again?"), you should be familiar enough with its mechanics that you can easily see how strengthen or weaken the splat. And THAT isn't even considering how ALL these games use xp to purchase higher dots of abilities (i.e. just use a weaker mage, or a weaker werewolf, or a weaker vampire, etc).

                                                        I suppose my assertion is a little simplistic, and upon reading this response it occured to me that I have been meaning two seperate things in one go. Now, that's not to imply I haven't heavily been relying on the first of the two meanings, as over a decade of dealing with this particular fanbase has made it abundantly clear to me that People Not Getting It is a deep and afflicting malady, and I will actually stand by it against your assertion that this can't be brushed off with saying "people don't get it" because too much of my experience to the contrary makes me skeptical. I could be wrong though, so now it's time to examine that second thing I've been saying.

                                                        As mentioned, over the years of dealing with this fanbase, I've come across this iteration of problem quite a few times, and what occured to me on reading the response above is that the question is not "What is the horror of this game?"-at least, not directly-but more "How is this horrifying?", and that has interesting implications. Like, even when questions are phrased as the former, it's often with an intonation with the latter, a searching for something they missed because what they found evokes a very strong "What, that's it?"

                                                        Because, yes, it would be crass to assume everyone who asks these sorts of questions simply can't see it, this leads to the natural conclusion that they have seen the branches of Mages horror, recognized them for what they were...but didn't actually comprehend how that was supposed to be horrifying. And, at it's heart, that's the most dehumanizing and monstrous part of Mage, that "so what" reaction. It belies a disregard for other people who have to suffer through your nimbus and the consequences of your spells, successful or not, it belies a disregard for the self-mutilation and warping that will invariably happen starting with Awakening and getting worse down the line, it belies a disregard for the world they exist in as they casually wreck any number of things in flow, all in favor of the obsession with knowing things just to know and exercising power for their own benefit, which often includes mere convenience or amusement. And all of this gets magnified because it happens within a "false space", it's not like any of this has reprecusions on the "true world", as though we are not responsible for the entertainment we create or ingest without even some small consideration.

                                                        And putting aside the spiritual-to-physical ways that Mages are monsters, that's the main way of how Mages are Monsters. It's people power exercising power without even the slightest regard for how that power spills out, the 1% approach to and lack of care for power, all because they don't even see how any of the bad stuff is actually bad stuff-not because they don't see it, but because they don't actually care about their consequences.
