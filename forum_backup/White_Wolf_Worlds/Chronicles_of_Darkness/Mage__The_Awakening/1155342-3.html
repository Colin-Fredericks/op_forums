                                    Originally posted by Dark Archon View Post
                                    I banned Steadfast (automatic successes? Not in my game) and forbidden Fate to give dice bonuses on spellcasting rolls (but allowed to give 9 and 8-again). So far it works, but mostly because player with Fate never ever uses it (because he have no idea how to do that).
                                    Same here, i understand some people like the narrative snowball approach. "This power seems OP but is the DM responsibility to DM fiat it to have consequences" which, to each their own i guess.

                                    I am not a fan, in my experience it always leaves a bitter taste on the players mouth with their reasoning being "why punish me for using a power that you allow me to buy with consequences that arent in the description?". And it generates (in my experience) a derailment problem. So player A does uses X power but I, the DM, make the situation more complicated for it to have consequences which is gonna derail the main plot with them dealing with it. But then Player B also uses X spell or similar Y spell, now I gotta keep derailing the game with consequences within consequences or i can just say that Player B cast and we continue but then, player A gets angry because he felt single out for a house rule which is basically DM-Fiat the rule.

                                    This is also why I am not a fan of touchstones and vice/virtue equivalents for splats.

                                    Before doing that i prefer to nerf the whole arcanum to manageable levels and then (the players) using it at their hearts content but i am a mechanics oriented person more than a narrative on the fly one.

                                        Originally posted by totalgit View Post
                                        tldr, Agree with Johnny, Consquences should happen at any powerlevel, and you shouldnt feel afraid to let your PC's let rip once in awhile because there will always be something or somebody who may want to change whatever the players do and they too are probably OP mages or similar.
                                        This is, in my opinion, "the answer" to the dilemma posed by OP: The PCs may well be able to thwart any obstacle you define in static terms, so instead, oppose them with obstacles that are as versatile as they are. Having problems with a Fate mage abusing the Arcanum? Sic a Time mage who knows the ins-and-outs of manipulating history on 'em, or a Prime mage with an expertise in reading and countering reality-unravelling effects, or a Space/Mind mage who can hit them from afar while manipulating the ability of their target to even perceive their imminent assault.

                                        After all, if your Fate mage is so powerful that he or she is giving YOU, as the Storyteller (and ostensible God of the whole game) any real concern... then you obviously have sufficient justification to say that same character is earning attention from some very powerful NPCs.

                                        TL,DR: There's always a bigger fish in the sea.
