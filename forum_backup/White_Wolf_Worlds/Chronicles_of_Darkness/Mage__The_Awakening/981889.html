    Gecko's Awakening Hack, Take Two

    10-04-2016, 09:42 PM
    Making a new thread, as was suggested:

    I've been wondering if there was a way to accomplish my goals for Mage: The Transgression on a smaller scale, by slightly hacking and nudging Awakening. The primary goal isn't just to make it more fun or flavorful or mad science-y, but to emphasize the personal horror elements as much as possible. The most important thing is, what would be the best way to run or hack Mage in order to emphasize the ambiguity and horror of the setting? To really make the players question everything, and to create the genuine possibility that a lot of the principles of the setting are either false or much more complicated. The books raise this, but what would it take to make it a central feature of the setting?

    Here are my goals/ideas so far:

    • The main thing I want to do is present being Awakening as a cursed, monstrous condition rather than a blessing, and make it plausible that close to or more than half of Mages wish secretly or openly that they were normal humans again.
    • Paradox and Obsessions both being much more dangerous and punishing would help.
    • Archmastery is equivalent to becoming a True Fae- the Mage becomes an utterly inhuman entity.
    • There's a lot of Genius influence. The Pentacle, for example, gets a LOT of influence from the Lemurians.
    • As Mages gain power and lose Wisdom, they might take Bans and Banes related to how they can perform magic, like other supernatural creatures.
    • The subjective nature of the Supernal is emphasized, as well as the possibility that it's essentially a shared delusion that Mages can force into reality. Players only have to pick one of their Path's Arcana, and choose any other as their second ruling Arcana. Experiences of the Supernal realms are vastly different and subjective.
    • Some Mages have no Path. They are stereotyped as being more likely to become Scelesti or Banishers, but the truth of this assumption is questionable.
    • A significant and ever-increasing number of Mages Awaken as Gifted Scientists from the Mage Chronicler's Guide, because I really really want to emphasize mad scientists as much as possible, since they're pretty much the modern version of the "sorcerer as monster" archetype.

    Feedback and suggestions are welcome.

      • Many Mages deliberately try to avoid gaining power, because gaining power inevitably removes the Mage more and more from humanity. Obsessions get stronger, Bans and Banes develop, and ultimately the Mage risks becoming an utterly inhuman Archmaster.
      • Mages are stuck between the risk of higher power and the painful compulsion to seek it- their Obsessions constantly force them to delve into paranormal phenomena and seek knowledge, no matter how much they try to resist.
      • The Nimbus grows more powerful as a Mage gains in power as a representation of their growing distance from humanity, acting similarly to the Wake from Leviathan.
      • It's quite hard to effect powerful supernatural creatures, and some are immune to direct uses of magic entirely. Creativity and problem-solving are key.
      • More pessimistic Mages prefer the terms "Hollow" or "Open" to "Awakened", because they more accurately describe what happens. A Mage blows a hole in his soul and desperately fills it with chunks of occult symbols, grabbing onto a watchtower to survive having their mind cast into primordial chaos. The "hollow" nature of Mages is why they're so sensitive to the occult and potent with it: they have "open space" with which to connect directly with the supernatural. Obsessions play into this, too: every Mage feels a deep and aching pain, as if they are missing something precious, and Obsessions are an attempt to fill that hole.
      • The factions now have more of a resemblance to the Covenants in Vampire- they primarily exist to help Mages justify and survive their cursed state.
      • The Silver Ladder is sort of equivalent to the Invictus, using elaborate hierarchies and rituals to manage their condition. They try to frame the Awakened state as a honorable burden, something given only to the worthy. Members of this group are most likely to be Lemurians- those who are trying to bring back a lost magical golden age.
      • The Mysterium seek knowledge with a fervor that surpasses that of other Mages. Some seek it for its own sake, while others look for the secrets of the world in the hope of regulating or removing their condition. They study the past to learn from it. Most historically significant Mages, after all, are primarily famous as cautionary tales.
      • The Guardians of the Veil know just how dangerous the supernatural is, especially the volatile powers of their own kind. They dedicate themselves completely to protecting the Whole not just from direct menaces but from knowledge that might lead them down the same self-destructive path the Hollow tread.
      • The Adamantine Arrow are not simply about warfare or strength. They focus on control and refinement as a means of honing mind and body to withstand the ravages of the Awakened condition. Many are in some way ascetic, seeking enough control to keep the fires raging inside them under control.
      • The Free Council are no longer the primary technomages at all. Every order has a fair proportion of scientifically-focused mages and Gifted Scientists. The Council's connection to mortal occult traditions is much more important to them, because their focus is connection: remaining in contact with the mortal world and humanity so as to avoid drifting away from it.
      • I've always disliked how the most human splat was the most powerful one, so having to choose between being human or being powerful is a major theme.
      • Hm, maybe The Hollow Chronicle is a good name for it.
      • No player is allowed to make a character inspired by Dr. Who or Benedict Cumberbatch's Sherlock. Any character who qualifies as a sarcastic british man is to be subject to close ST scrutiny, and is only allowed a maximum of three quips per game.

              Like I was saying at the end of the previous thread, if magic is cursed and something Mages deliberately want to avoid then why do they even cast spells? What stops them from just not doing magic? The changes you seek to make need to be gameable, and this question and its answer is the foundation to everything else you should be editing. It's summed up in this point you post:

              Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post
              • Mages are stuck between the risk of higher power and the painful compulsion to seek it- their Obsessions constantly force them to delve into paranormal phenomena and seek knowledge, no matter how much they try to resist.
              I asked why do Mages even cast spells and your answer, seemingly, is that Obsessions push them to do it. Fine; someone did suggest using Obsessions as a 'stick' rather than the 'carrot' they're currently written as. There's nothing inherently wrong with this.

              The important point to realize here is that it needs to be gameable. Will your players want to play someone who is forced to use magic that is cursed and that they would ideally seek to avoid? Will Obsessions as a stick be fun for your players (or other potential audiences)?

              Vampires, werewolves, and other monsters who have inhuman compulsions are fun for players because, long story short, XP incentives (narrative incentive exists but is rewarded in CofD by beats). Is it fun for players to be near-forced to have to use their magic? Can they choose not to use their magic despite the stick of Obsession?

              If that's the case then it's a game of choice and what your players' Mages choose to do with their magic, making the best of it. Isn't that just what Mage the Awakening is?

                  The first Mage book I worked on back in 1st Ed. was Night Horrors: The Unbidden. It was mostly a monsters book, but sort of the central inspiration was that magic wasn't something mages had all figured out. Sometimes it came unbidden into the world, and mages had to sort it out. It sounds like your notion of mages as cursed even more than they are blessed follows that particular train of thought (albeit not one I've seen a whole lot of in the past or present line). Reseru is correct to point out that the trick is finding a group that will find that kind of spin evocative and fun and not needlessly punitive or adversarial. Assuming they're on board, here's one I'd consider low-hanging fruit:

                  Take a page out of Beast's Satiety system. When Wisdom gets low because the mage is flinging around sorcery in ways that endanger everyone around him, that's its own kind of problem (amply covered in the current mechanics, really). However, make it so that if a mage goes without using magic (and without Reaching regularly) her Wisdom keeps climbing. That's good, right? No. That's bad, too. A mage is a pore between the Fallen World and the Supernal World, and if that pore gets clogged by a wizard who refuses to function properly (using magic), it becomes infected and pressurized until, eventually, it explodes in an unsightly and painful flow of puss and blood.

                  This is the point at which it resembles Beast. A high-Wisdom mage's unused magic eventually leaks into the world, causing trouble in the mage's vicinity that reflects her Long-Term Nimbus as it tear tiny holes in reality through which unguided Supernal power and the poisons of the Abyss leak into the world. Mechanically, Anomalies start showing up the region where the high-Wisdom mage lives. The higher her Gnosis, the wider the range of those Anomalies. The higher her Wisdom, the worse those Anomalies (gradually worsening into Manifestations) become.

                  This one would be comparatively easy to implement without a ton of mechanical changes. It doesn't require you to create Bans/Banes or rewrite the magic system. That said, you can certainly hammer out some mechanical reflection of the Supernal "pressure" building up in a mage.

                  You can head off the "obvious" solution by saying that whenever one mage dies, another Awakens. Yeah, it still behooves you to off particularly bad mages, but when facing sliiiighly awful ones you're taking the risk that the ones who replace them will be worse.

                  Okay, here's one more random one - not as thought-out, however: Maybe just using magic alone isn't enough to keep Wisdom under control. Perhaps Obsessions function a little bit like Shadow Names - but instead of keeping Nimbus from leaking out into the mage's mundane life they give her an excuse to use lots of magic to accomplish some moderately Quixotic goal. Maybe just throwing down and winning the lottery isn't enough of an act of hubris to keep the monsters from crawling out of the walls, but using magic to manage the lives of a family of Sleepers so that little baby Kate grows up to marry a Charlie (your obsession doesn't care which Charlie she marries - only that his name is Charlie) - or something equally arcane - helps reduce the magical pressure. Is it better to brainwash dozens of innocent people to prevent an Abyssal pandemic from sweeping across the country, or do you feel better about yourself if you're just manipulating one specific person or family in a really up-close, personal, and in-no-way-accidental way?

                              This is a "setting"/storyteller suggestion, but amp up the Long-Term Nimbus so that it starts doing stuff at lower levels of Gnosis (start it at 3 or something maybe rather than 6?). Write up a list of examples for yourself as a push-off point for every "Path" (Primary from a Path + that one that people can pick for themselves) increasing in severity in accordance to gnosis, as this is the mechanism by which you're suppose to have mages unconsciously warp reality around them into doom. Based on how wisdom works with nimbus, starting-level mages have it taint and curse their siblings and friends and objects they have emotional connection with already. An acanthus's baby sister seems to not get any older than 3, and his dead father's watch seems to keep stopping on the exact second you first found out about his demise. A magistos finds her girlfriend suddenly unable to feel an emotion like fear, because the mage kept thinking "I wish she could stand up to her jerk of a boss" until their nimbus slowly started to chain up the girlfriends fear-goetia. A moros starts to become depressed and broken as all the mementos and photos of his lost family members start decaying, rusting, and fading. Obrimos accidentally gives her husband heart problems as his nervous system keeps having minor surges of electricity out of nowhere. Thyrsus's life energy slowly draws in insects until every inch of their family home's walls hides some form of creepy crawly.
