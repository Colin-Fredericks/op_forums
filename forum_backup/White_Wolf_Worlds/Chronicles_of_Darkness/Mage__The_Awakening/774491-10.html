                        The Sleeping Curse is one of the reasons I feel that any mortal seriously attempting to Hunt mages needs a dedicated scribe/information keeper. I'm talking about someones whose dedicated role is to stay back at base, have constant contact with the agents in the field, and have the active hunters call in what they're seeing so that they can record it. If I recall correctly, I'm fairly sure that Quiescence only affects those who actually perceive Supernal magic in some way and only takes hold after the scene is over with. So I'd assume that someone who only hears a secondhand account of, but is never actually exposed to, the magic itself would be relatively fine.

                        So you have a guy sitting back at base with some sort of radio or other communications device, or hell, maybe with some sort of machine with speech to text so he couldn't even get any actual audio, receiving constant reports of what the people in the field are seeing at the moment. So this guy, who wouldn't be affected by the information he's handling, would keep track of all records and suspect data, only telling the guys who were exposed to magic who they needed to take down, what tactics to use, and what equipment to bring. Never hinting that magic was a factor so as to not cause a breaking point recursion, but supplying the Hunters with everything they might need to be able to react appropriately to what they're dealing with.

                        Now there are still problems with even this, and the people doing the actual visual investigation are going to need therapists to deal with the landslide of breaking points that this sort of job is going to put them through, but it's pretty much the best they're going to get.

                                On 4chan, Dave has offered some additional clarification about the Quiescence and Hunters' use of notes, recording, etc.

                                DaveB !!kn3vuELOelB 12/03/15(Thu)05:50:08

                                In the scene directly following exposure to the Supernal or Abyssal, a Sleeper forgets what she saw, or else changes how she remembers it toward the mundane. It is as harrowing in memory is it is in reality. Quiescence, like Dissonance, is not limited to the spells of mages; Sleepers’ memories are clouded by the interior of ruins of the Time Before, the presence of the Mad, Emanation Realms, and sighting Supernal or Abyssal entities.
