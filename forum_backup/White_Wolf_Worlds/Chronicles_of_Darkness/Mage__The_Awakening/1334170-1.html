                Reading this thread made me want to look over what the main book says about how time works, and how definitively it's presented. Mage 2nd edition does say on p. 186 that "The Fallen World has the point at which the future becomes the past, which constantly advances." But I think you can argue that there's a slight amount of wiggle room in terms of whether to take this as the gospel truth, since p. 185 says "Mages who study the Time Arcanum have practical experience to marry to Sleeping theory, and quickly discover that while some thinkers have approached something like the truth, thought experiments don’t fully prepare them for the reality of temporal magic." You could read this as saying that mages have inferred the idea of an objective present from how they observe various time spells to work.

                So, if you don't like the idea of an objective present, and you can come up with an alternative metaphysics of time that fits with the facts derived from mages' "practical experience", I think that isn't fundamentally changing the setting. The observations I see that seem to support the notion of a single objective present would be these ones from p. 185-186 (if anyone can think of any others let me know):

                1. A mage who repeatedly looks into the future or past will find a given set of past event happens the same way each time since the past is already "spun", while the future seem to be in flux because you may see multiple possible futures on different occasions when performing the same spell repeatedly (or in some cases a single spell like Choose the Thread may allow the mage to see multiple possible futures).

                2. Mages who actually travel back in time with the Corridors of Time spell will have a "telltale temporal aura", as will anything they interact with to change its destiny, but this aura will disappear when they return to the present.

                3. "Mages who travel back in time and look forward find that the intervening span is still set"--so even if you are changing things in the past, it's as if you're just changing things in a new "draft" of history but when you look ahead you'll still see the original version of history prior to the present you departed from, with the altered draft only displacing the original once you return to the present. It isn't 100% clear from this whether other mages who are "native" to that draft of the past would also see only a single possible future up until the point the time-traveling mage had come from, or whether they might see multiple possible futures, reflecting the fact that the altered timeline of the draft could go in multiple different ways.

                4. "While still in the past, if the spell that projected the traveler backwards is dispelled, he returns to the present but any changes he made to history are reversed." So this also supports the idea that when in the past you are only altering a kind of draft of a different history, and all your changes can be undone if someone dispells your magic while you're in that draft.

                Could there be a way to account for all these that doesn't involve an objective present? For #1, maybe we could imagine that time has a branching structure, so that there really are multiple possible futures from any given point, but only one unique past. For the others, what if using Corridors of Time to go backwards in time created a sort of magical tether with the mage on one end and the moment/timeline they departed from on the other, such that if Corridors of Time was dispelled they would snap back to the point on the tree that they left rather than staying in the altered timeline? And this tether could also cause the temporal aura, and could determine what they see when they look into the future up to the point they departed from. Any holes in this as an alternate explanation for #2-4?
