    Has Immediate Nimbus come up in your games?

    09-19-2016, 02:56 PM
    Just noticed that after a good dozen 2e session, immediate Nimbus has never come up in our games. I think it's a mix of being one more thing that makes you roll dice (and Mage has far too many of those already), its effects feeling extremely minor coupled with a duration that is too short to reliably take advantage of them, and very rarely being in a situation where the dicetrick/bonus/malus it applies would be helpful. Long-Term and Signature Nimbus have come up, but not really Immediate Nimbus.
    So, has it come up in your games? If it did, did you feel it added something that was, to put it very brutal, worth the wordcount it uses up and the time spent rolling dice?
    I'm mostly curious if we are missing something about the Immediate Nimbus that would make it more worthwile.

    My custom legacy (2e)- The Disciples of Rathma - Life/Death focused Moros/Thyrsus Legacy, comments appreciated

                                      Every time you cast a spell, your Nimbus flares.

                                      Attainments are not spells, so countering doesn't flare as far as I know.

                                      When you flare due to casting a spell, only people who can see you cast with Active Mage Sight are affected (and they can choose not to withstand in case they know your Nimbus is beneficial).

                                      You can also flare by spending a point of mana. This affects even people who are not using AMS, meaning it can affect Sleepers. You can only do this once a scene.

                                      It's impossible by RAW to have a Nimbus Tilt anywhere near as potent as Heavy Winds. They only give penalties or bonuses to attributes and skills (and skills also remove 10-again or add 8-again). The total penalties/bonuses add up to half gnosis, plus one or two for Potent Nimbus. Immediate Nimbus can't deal damage, and it'd take absurd Gnosis to get the sort of penalties to rolls it grants.

                                      Basically, Immediate Nimbus tends to only have a mild effect even if it does affect other mages. It's not nearly as severe as you seem to believe.

                                      That said, when I played... mage vs mage combat was pretty rare, since it's roughly akin to nuclear war. Nobody wins a mage war. The only time my character's Nimbus ever came up was because her's gave the sensation of peace reminiscent of getting high, and another character would deliberately expose herself to my character's Nimbus because she liked it. Never came up in an actual tense situation.

                                                  ---

                                                  Speaking of house rules, this might be the place to get feedback on this particular one.

                                                  During the dev cycle of Mage 2e, I distinctly recall Dave describing the Immediate Nimbus as existing to "convince hobbits you are no conjurer of cheap tricks", and that stuck with me. It's why I always liked Nimbuses; they were a fun little aesthetic power that let a Mage show off their Mage-ness without having to throw out some meaningless spell. In 1e, at most, your Nimbus granted a small social dice modifier because you were doing the magical equivalent of title-dropping or flashing a wad of bills.

                                                  I wanted to take a stab at revisiting that feeling; making a Nimbus something that players would want to throw around a little. So, to that end, here are the house rules I've been using for my games:

                                                  Immediate Nimbus

                                                  The flaring of a Mage's Immediate Nimbus is a glimpse of their Path and the Truths therein. It is a flash; a brief, momentary insight; an epiphanic shiver, gone after an instant but never quite forgotten. This is not a glimpse of the Primal Wild or Arcadia, per se; it is far more intimate and personal; a glimpse of the specific symbols the Mage holds closest to them and with which they have the greatest sympathy. The stronger the Mage's sympathy to these symbols, the stronger a glimpse the flaring of their Nimbus provides.

                                                  Dim Awareness

                                                  Choose descriptive terms for the Mage's Nimbus as you would a Shadow Name (if the Mage has the Shadow Name merit, the two can - and probably should - be the same).

                                                  Any time the Mage wishes, they may flare their Nimbus reflexively as a part of a social roll. Doing so grants (Gnosis / 2) bonus dice to that social roll; giving all present the sensation of just what sort of being they stand before, and adding resonant power to their words or deeds. The social roll need not be a spoken action, either; an Arrow who holds the severed head of a foe high above a battlefield might boost an Intimidation roll to frighten off the rest, say.

                                                  A Mage can flare their Nimbus in this manner as many times as they like in a scene, but it's really only effective once for any given group of witnesses. After you have smiled wickedly and shown yourself to be Lucifer's Fallen reflection, everyone present knows it on an intimate level. Further shows and displays don't tell them anything they do not already intimately know.

                                                  As a drawback to this benefit, a Mage's Nimbus is always somewhat obvious. People see it; they recognize it just by looking at them. If asked to describe them, they'd use the words that fit into their Nimbus. A strong Shadow Name limits the effects of this - though people suddenly change how they react when the Mage is going around in their magical persona with remarkably speed - but every Mage feels it to some degree. After all, what others are seeing is the Truth written into the journey that is their own soul. While there are sometimes benefits to this, a Mage whose Shadow Name is Prometheus is going to get looked-askew at by police as a troublemaker, even as the average person on the street finds them oddly charismatic.

                                                  Flash of Enlightenment

                                                  Of course, sometimes a Mage wants to do more than just give a subtle hint. The reflexive flaring described above isn't usually what one calls subtle, of course - a Thyrsus's flaring Nimbus may cause objects to rattle as unquiet spirits stir in their sleep; a Moros's Nimbus causes shadows to draw near and all the world to feel dark as the Moros's voice booms, echoing off unseen walls - but sometimes even that isn't enough to get the point across.

                                                  Design a custom Condition for the Mage to represent the symbol they have etched of themselves into the Supernal. Use the same guidelines as for designing a Nimbus Tilt. The Condition's resolution should be something reasonably onerous or burdensome related to embracing the truth the Mage represents. It fades without resolving after a number of days equal to the Mage's Gnosis.

                                                  By spending a point of Mana while taking an action in keeping with their soul's Path, the Mage may flare their Nimbus even brighter. While one might call the lesser flare reasonably subtle, this is not. An Obrimos's head is surrounded by a blazing halo; a Thyrsus's hands drip crimson with blood; an Acanthus drips mist wherever they walk. This flare is visible briefly to anyone, and inflicts all who witness it with the Mage's Condition. Those who wish to deny the Truth do so with a reflexive Resolve + Composure, penalized by the Mage's Gnosis. Dramatic failure on the roll extends the Condition's duration to (Gnosis) months.

                                                  Example: Charles's Obrimos is a thearch who goes by the Shadow Name of Sophia. Her magic is a mixture of Gnosticism, Kabbalah, and various Neo-Platonist ideals. Charles decides Sophia's Nimbus is saintly and enlightened, giving her the aura of a wise mystic. At Gnosis 5, Sophia is a respected famulus and head of her own Illuminated cult. He decides to give her a Condition that leaves those who witness it inspired; those affected gain a +2 to Expression, a +1 to Presence, and resolve the Condition by proselytizing high ideals to others they consider less enlightened than themselves.

                                                  One night, Sophia is visiting the underground cathedral that is the centre of her followers' faith, and overhears two of her priests having a doctrinal dispute about the best way to go about recruiting the unenlightened. Since this bickering is in front of neophytes, Sophia opts to step in.

                                                  "Sister, brother; do not fight. Know you not that you are of one family?" Sophia speaks, flaring her Nimbus. She appears to them caught in a shaft of moonlight falling through the only opening to the sky in the chamber; seemingly lit by the will of the heavens. Charles rolls Presence + Expression to calm the bickering, and gets a +3 bonus for the roll. It succeeds, and the bickering calms.

                                                  After listening to the arguments from both sides and weighing each, Sophia delivers a passionate speech; emphasizing the insight each side shares and ultimately delivering a ruling on the course of action the cult must take. Charles asks the ST if that's fitting with Sophia's myth and the ST agrees it is, so Charles spends a point of Mana. Forked tongues of blue flame appear above the brow of the Mage, and it spreads to the brow of all those whom the Nimbus affects. They are filled with a proselytizing vigor; eager to go out and spread the Word.

                                                  Of course, the ST knows Brother Maximillian finds this course of action foolhardy. He tries to resist, so the ST rolls Resolve + Composure, but with a -5 penalty, he just can't muster the reserve. Maximillian can't deny the inspiring power of the words and is filled with the urge to speak up. Of course, that doesn't mean he's convinced by Sophia; just that he finds her words inspiring. They might just inspire him to begin to foment a heretical sect working to circumvent their saintly leader...

                                                  Sleepers and the Nimbus

                                                  So, what happens when a Mage flares their Nimbus before Sleepers? Does the Quiescence Curse crush it from their minds? Well, yes, but quick as it is, there isn't usually that much to suppress. Even if a Sleeper ignores that all the timepieces in their office are all now reading at different times after that strange man came by and talked something funny about thorns, she still remembers that he looked and sounded like he knew way more about the world than she did; like a spider at the heart of a web.

                                                  Less so when it comes to more forceful flaring. The sensory hallucinations they induce trigger Quiescence as if the Sleeper had just been witness to a spell. They still gain the Condition, however, and some Awakened believe that inducing such experiences imparts Truths so directly as to bypass the Lie; providing a path by which a Mage might guide Sleepers to Awakening, or at least to stir more freely in the Sleep. Whether they're correct or are simply subjecting hapless victims to psychic torture is an unresolved question. Even those Awakened who believe such might be a means to aid the Sleepers aren't gung-ho about utilizing such methods, though; Sleepers who internalize the Truths of a Mage's Nimbus from repeated exposure without achieving Sleepwalker or Awakened status tend to become... Unhinged. More than one charismatic cult leader has been slain for insufficient piety by an over-zealous Sleeper acolyte.

                                                  ---

                                                  Edit: A small switch for Signature Nimbuses as well: The sort that Mages imprint using Mana and a direct act of will - as opposed to those caused by incidental use of magic - ping the Peripheral Sight of any Mage who passes by them. In this way, the Awakened may "mark their territory" and give those who pass by a reminder to bugger off.
