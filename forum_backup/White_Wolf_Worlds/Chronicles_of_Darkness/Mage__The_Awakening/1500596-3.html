                        Great job on Shackle cutting that deal. The Grigori, the Prelacy Temple, the Supernal beings, they all indicate Singsong is not only focused on long term gains, but also in prestige, in showcasing his Exarch's favor. What could be more prestigious for a Panopticon member than having access to information no one else does ? Plus the deal will pay many dividends in the long run.

                        Simultaneously, Singsong also did a great job at using coldly calcuated intimidation. The implied threat of "every Space spell cuts both ways", the pressure for Shackle to deliver more value than other bidders, disarming him unknowingly, pointing how he is helpless and isolated at the center of the Seer's power, all while remaining professional.

                        But the best one was the clear message that he will game this bargain for all its worth, immediately getting Shackle to use his veto and getting the information he wants. But Shackle cannot do the same, since he has the most to lose from this arrangement ending. In other words, its a power move that points out how he entered a Faustian bargain.

                        Despite all that, Shackle stuck the landing and walked away with an invaluable source of information, provided its carefully managed.

                                    Temporal Summoning can't be used to bring back the dead, you're right.

                                    Unfortunately Chron was using a pre-errata version of that spell back when he brought that Seer back to life in the Athanaeum and I only realised after it had happened that it shouldn't have been possible. Since then I've made a rule that it CAN be used to bring the dead back to life, but it only lasts a minute and you can only use it once per corpse. It made more sense to allow it with limitations than to retroactively rule that it wasn't possible.

                                    In theory there's no reason you couldn't reverse someone's timeline to bring them back to life with Weaving. In practice, the Exarchs have built a reality where EVERYTHING DIES is coded into the foundations of everything so my headcanon is they've set things up so that immortality is extremely difficult to obtain. Death is a Lie, after all, and the Lie is omnipresent. So that's my story and I'm sticking to it.

                                                They DID consider trying to be more discerning with their summoning, but then I ran them through the summoning mechanics and they realised that without the name of an entity it would be difficult to summon a specific one. And each summoning takes 5 hours, plus a mana, plus the time to set up the space, and also Shackle spent 5 willpower on the rolls, and even then success isn't guaranteed...

                                                ...so they decided to pick one from Antenora's book and save them some hassle. Also, Shackle categorically has not gotten over his wife's death and point blank refuses to deal with it in any way that can remotely be considered healthy. So the idea of killing an entity that represents, on some level, him 'letting her go' or 'getting over it' definitely appealed to him. Which is pretty messed up, if you think about it...

                                                ...but that's the Last Call Cabal!

                                                        Given how he is trying to aim the chaos of the Abyss, I could see Shackle being a weird edge case that would be allowed to join the Secret Order of the Gate, to the great confusion of the Seers. It would make him into the Shackle that keeps the Abyss in check. Maybe swapping their primary Arcanum to Spirit rather than Space, so he would have a third Ruling Arcanum.

                                                        Its too bad the House of Ariadne only uses the Arcana of the Acanthus. They have some very flavorful Attainments that follow a theme of omen divination, which allow both proactive and passive flashes of insight, doing so in a way that often takes you on an interesting journey. The same journey can also be leveraged to setup other scenes, which is great for both players and GMs.


                                                        I feel reasonably confident that Chron Legacy is so deep into the Cult of the Doomsday Clock that he found out they didn't tell you everything when you joined, and is now desperately trying to break free before Chron Legacy becomes an Anachron. It also explains why he stayed away from Shackle, who is using antinomian magic. Chron joining the House of Ariadne is a big win for Chron Legacy, but its not over until those timers are off him.


                                                        Some very cool visuals for the Illuminated Path, they have some Attainments which both make it easier for Sleepers to run into the occult and grant some level of safety. Their lore is fascinating as well, unorthodox enough to rattle the Guardians, but also providing a unique take on a Cryptopoly.
