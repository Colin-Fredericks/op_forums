            My preference for shadow names has shifted over the years, from the more punchy, thematic or 'superhero' like titles to something that a mage can use as a cover identity and give to a hotel clerk without too much of a problem, even if they seem like obvious fake names. My thinking is that if you've got a shadow name, you're going to need to use it for extended periods of time because there's all manner of different ways to perceive, detect, trace or connect a person with magic through time, fate, interconnections, etc. It wouldn't do to leave a place where you've been performing magic and going around calling yourself "Nightbane", only to have someone follow your resonant traces through Time back to your apartment later that night, where you're getting mail as Dave Johnson. At the very least, you'd want to call yourself "John Nightbane" so you can get a driver's license or rent a motel room in town after you've spent the day throwing around perceiving and knowing spells, even if you also get a few stares or eyebrow raises. And maybe you're just NIghtbane to your wizard friends, but when you make a dinner reservation they might think it's a prank.

            I feel like operating as a mage would have to be like the most extreme version of going deep undercover in enemy territory, if you really cared about your name or true identity being used sympathetically, or enemy mages. As a mage, you're going to do some interacting with the mundane world. It would be unavoidable. And if you're going to be in the mundane world doing things, you're going to want a name you can drop there.

            The example in the Mage 1e core under Shadow Names seems reasonable in that sense: Glorianna. Some Sleeper asks for your name, you just tack a 'Smith' onto that and you're good to go. "The Adversary", though... how would you get anything done in a day? You'd be acting through intermediaries or using magic for everything. Seems a bit excessive when you could pick a name that could "pass" at least a little in Sleeper society.

                                            I definitely don't know enough British/English/London history to help from that point of view, but 4 of the 5 have clear links to bombing incidents. Since it sounds like Mage2E will allow some sort of Yantra merit based around Shadow Name / Cabal identity, it might be worth pursuing something thematically linked.

                                            I would tread carefully, especially since these are real world tragedies that affected real people, but in a world of darkness I could see each of the bombings actually being frame of jobs, with the attacks actually being being something along the lines of extreme actions taken to destroy Abyssal intrusions. The bombers could be patsies for the actions of extremest mages preventing even worse tragedies. In a case like that the bombings could be seen a symbolically cleansing and erasing that which cannot be known, which might feed into the themes of the cabal. In that case taking the bombers names as Shadow Names might fit. It also lends it self to some pretty extreme modus operanti for the cabal as villians.

                                            I would just caution that something like this should be used cautiously cause it has real potential to offend.
