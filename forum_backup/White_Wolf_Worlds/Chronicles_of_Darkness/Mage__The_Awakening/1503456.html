    Home brew merits vs Arcane attainments

    02-19-2023, 12:18 PM
    Something i was reading about a 1.5e homebrew got me thinking about custom magic 'styles' which could be added (or removed) from the game.

    Throw spell could have been a supernatural merit, and a pre-requisite for fast spells.

    Counter spell, likewise

    Or a Highspeech, Runic writing, Second Person High Speech, Invisible runes series of merits...

    I'd like some alternatives to the Order specific merits. Maybe an Oneiromacy series starting with Astral Adept?

    What are the relevant advantages of custom merits over custom Arcane attainments/alternative utility attainments? / Legacies?

    I think i want something like a set of minor legacy 'skill trees' which don't lock you out of other learning (With or without Arcana pre-reqs)

      Legacy Attainments have one huge selling point: consistency. You can be in an Abyssal Verge where time is inconsistent and your Legacy's time travel and divination Attainments will work fine whereas everyone else has a terrible time. You could be in an Ansho and your Legacy's physical enhancements will remain steady, the spells under your Hung Spell Attainment ready to go and your Co-Location Attainment connecting to the Sanctum still available. They cannot be counterspelled, Dissonanced away or effected by the Lie, they are always there when you need them.

      Utility Attainments are something that I imagine is rarer, you would need to quest to an ancient ruin, the depths of a Realm Invisible or complete some kind of great work to learn one beyond the ones in the core book. They make a major difference in your toolkit and should be considered a rare and precious prize.

      Style merits are just a body of knowledge that has been refined and codified into teachable skills. They should be easier to learn, should you find a teacher or reference containing them. But they are subject to being manipulated like anything else, such as a Prelate of Fury instinctively countering combat styles in just the right way.

      Overall, I recommend not giving out spells themselves in these skill trees, but rather supernatural moves that can help specialize your approach. Try looking at how Hurt Locker specializes combat styles and try to translate the spirit of it into these styles, I did something along those lines when making a fighting style version of the Adamant Hand.

          The advantage of custom Merits is that Merits are the design space the game has set aside for "use this space for any mechanical widget that you're not building a whole new subsystem or powerset for."

          Arcanum Attainments aren't framed as something for which alternatives typically occur — the training the Orders give mages to learn to use them is described in terms of basic athletic conditioning or fundamental mathematical principles, not side paths for One Weird Trick like Discipline ladders in Requiem.

          What do you want alternatives to the Order-specific Merits for, exactly? Like, "custom magic styles" is doing a lot of the heavy lifting in the conception of this thread.

                      You could use the system described in page 44 of Hurt Locker, which allow you to pick one maneuver out of many for each tier of a fighting style. Each style has a tag, so if you have enough dots in a style with a similar tag, you can buy a maneuver for one XP or even buy one of the alternative maneuvers in your current style.

                      Which means that if you have 3 dots in Martial Arts, you have 3 maneuvers, one for each tier. So for one XP you could, for example, get another tier 3 maneuver, purchase the fourth dot or, since Boxing also has the Striking tag, you could purchase one Boxing maneuver up to tier 3. Which thematically it means studying mixed martial arts or retooling the technique to work within your main fighting system. The same can be done with supernatural styles, provided they share a tag and the character meets the minimum requirements of the other style.

                          I'm curious what more you think could be done with magical combat style that a)Isn't covered by Adamant Hand, Between the Ticks and Fast Spells, b) accounts for the way combat in Chronicles works, c) Maintains the vibe of that mainly being the purview of the Adamantine Arrow, d) doesn't break the balances and feel of magic, and e) doesn't break the balance between magic and mundane violence in combat.

                          Magic, in the context of combat, is intended to be something of a high risk, high reward scenario, something that's a viable action, but not strictly more viable than just cleaving a mother fucker or lighting them up with your SMG. Resources are neccesarily stretched thin for magic as a combat option, with Reach being limited to your own mastery or if it's a Rote and conventionally only being able to pull off a single Yantra, unless you decide to give up the sacred resource of time. It's not like diving into the thick of things and going slap happy like a spell-bender or Gandalf on a staff-happy day* is inviable given the touch option for spell casting, nor is slinging spells like Harrius Barius Potternicus took lessons from Vash the Stampede, particularly since every Mage has Mage Armor and spellcasting lets you make use of your Immediate Nimbus, but given that you can only do magic and use basic defense, it's not anymore encouraged than other combat options. This is on purpose, both to keep mundane physical combat viable and to keep magic apart as a special thing. Adamant Hand bridges the divide between the two by making it easier to transition between the two between turns with notable increases and permission for better defenses, all without neccesarily incentivizing one over the other.

                          If you mean "magical style" in a non combat way (because you describe them like fighting styles but then make it about being distinctive from each other), the game already has that in Praxes, Rotes, Dedicated Tools, Shadow Names (and Cabal Themes), Legacies, Nimbus, and a lot more, all built on the foundation of Path and Order to help hone towards particularly distinctive styles. As Players go along, they're going to pick up a lot of things that will be signature to them-it's just something that takes work. It's not the sort of thing that you can hand players on a platter, it's something they have to curate, often with guidance and assistance from the Storyteller in the course of play.

                          Magical Traditions, which is what I assumed you were going after with the magical styles, are a terrible mistake that the game has done a lot of work in running away from and is not benefitted from going back to. As for magical combat styles, I think my main comment on that is that I idly considered allowing a merit for being able to swing a punch or stab a motherfucker AND unleash a spell through it, and immediately realized that that would be broken as fuck.

                          So, uh, yeah, I dislike flat out saying a homebrew is a bad idea, because often there's something interesting in them and they just happen to be poorly implemented, but this one. THis one feels like it's running straight into things that weren't good when we had them and won't be better now.

                                          Originally posted by orathaic View Post

                                          Same reason "your magic benefits from your engagement with an established real-world cultural magical tradition" is just part of Techné: as a general phenomenon, insight into things that resonate with Supernal Truth is not unique to occult traditions; symbolism can be found and drawn upon from anything, which is why Techné affords the same benefits to computer networking and knitting and why the Diamond just focuses on developing their own mythology and practices.

                                          There's a comparison to be made with Deviant here: Awakened spellcasting is an incredibly versatile system where each spell can be made to work differently in terms of where it gets its bonuses from, and belonging to a major occult conspiracy or adhering to a focused magical philosophy can give you some additional tricks, but none of that is going to fix the fundamental problem you have of "two characters from different traditions use the same basic systems for magic that accomplishes the same result with the same Arcana."

                                          Some of this is down to Mage not having the space to really emphasize improvised casting as being more difficult than using Rotes or Praxes, but if you want an example of something off-the-cuff, you could do something like this thing I did for changelings personalizing their Contracts and build a set of Merits whose prerequisites are knowledge of a specific spell as a Praxis (or a Rote, depending on what you want the Merit to represent). Again: custom Merits are pretty much the path of least resistance for building this sort of thing.

                                              There's also the factor of "if you have a special paradigm that is meaningfully distinct from others, then you have a tool everyone is going to use over the standard system, at which point there's no point to the standard system." The M:tAw sourcebook Magical Traditions, the nearest mirror to this concept, didn't make sense for the "symbolism in everything" magical jackdaw style of Awakening and mechanically injured gameplay by having players who weren't using it be left behind so bad it basically had to be hard disawoved from the canon in the buildup to Second Edition's release. Fortunately, we're now at a point where no one really mentions it, and what good was in their concept being appropriately spread across the order mechanics, with the most superficial benefits going to Techne, and the Nameless Order Merit and Legacies that are particularly devoted to it.

                                              Players can develop their own individual style, and there are benefits of empowerment to do so baked into the game, but it falls on them to build it up, and part of that is understanding the tools they have to make that work. That's, in fact, a big part of the narrative, each mage making their own face. I wouldn't short cut that.
