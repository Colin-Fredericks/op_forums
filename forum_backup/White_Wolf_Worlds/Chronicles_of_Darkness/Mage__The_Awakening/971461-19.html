                    Originally posted by ManusDomine View Post


                    I'll admit that I've spent infinitely more time theorycrafting than I've spent playing games.

                    I'm just tired of the attitude that there is anything objectively wrong with Mage as written, because Mage is a perfectly functional and well-written game.

                    Sure, you can disagree with it on a lot of points, and I do have my disagreements as well, but I don't shout them open for everyone to hear and see so they can know how much I dislike feature X or feature Y.
                    Oh, no, I'm with you - I love Mage, and I love it for all the things Gecko says are problems. I love that it's a game about power and responsibility, about the difficulty of wise choice-making even with good information and good aims, rather than a game of passively sitting there while the GM fiats you to death. I love that players have agency to engage, or not engage, with Hubris and Wisdom loss as they will, and can tell stories about people fighting to hold on to their humanity, people gleefully letting it go to become something else, or people who find some kind of separate peace in between.

                    And this is what all the people I've played with have liked, too! They love the freedom of the Arcana, how the game rewards lateral thinking and creative play. At the end of the first session of my newest chronicle, one player, who was new both to Mage and the CoD, saw how another player overcame an obstacle and turned to me, eyes wide. "Wait a minute," he said, "it's like we can do anything?" I grinned back.

                    Every World of Darkness game has, along with horror, some kind of basic elemental appeal to it - a selling point, a reason to play. Yeah, vampires are cursed creatures who suck blood, but they're also sexy and cool and transgressive. They get to do all the stuff teenage you wished you could do. OG Werewolf let you play ultraviolent biker terrorists fucking up the Man. New Demon is like, hey kids, want to be Arnie in Terminator 2 mixed with a Doom cyberdemon and also, for the more RP-inclined, you can trick people into unwise bargains? Even in a mortals game, you can be a brave mystery-solving cop or a crusading journalist or a wily thief.

                    These games give you cool shit to do because they're games and they're meant to be played. And this is the element that is missing from so many gaming discussions online, which so often get sidetracked by white room situations, prescriptive definitions ("um excuse me sir this TVTropes link defines horror as blahblahblah"), and the eternal question of how to enforce a certain outcome.

                    And I feel like this has been missing since page one. The motivations here seem to be entirely to do with making Mage more exactly like (how the OP perceives) the other CoD games, which...why? Why am I, a hypothetical player sitting down to play a game, supposed to care about someone's sense of persnickety cross-game-line correctitude? That's not a bullet point! Or when Gecko says they don't want even the possibility of positive, helpful mages, and that it's a game about 'delusion' - what kind of appeal is that supposed to hold? "Okay, guys, this game is called Mage, but in my superior version, you play powerless mentally ill people who are constantly subject to GM fiat! Are you scared yet? This is horror!"

                            Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post
                            Here're some more:
                            • Many Mages deliberately try to avoid gaining power, because gaining power inevitably removes the Mage more and more from humanity. Obsessions get stronger, Bans and Banes develop, and ultimately the Mage risks becoming an utterly inhuman Archmaster.

                            • The factions now have more of a resemblance to the Covenants in Vampire- they primarily exist to help Mages justify and survive their cursed state.

                            • More pessimistic Mages prefer the terms "Hollow" or "Open" to "Awakened", because they more accurately describe what happens. A Mage blows a hole in his soul and desperately fills it with chunks of occult symbols, grabbing onto a watchtower to survive having their mind cast into primordial chaos. The "hollow" nature of Mages is why they're so sensitive to the occult and potent with it: they have "open space" with which to connect directly with the supernatural. Obsessions play into this, too: every Mage feels a deep and aching pain, as if they are missing something precious, and Obsessions are an attempt to fill that hole.
                            This would have to rewrite all the order books or better put make them incompatible with your hack fluffy wise. As the way mage society work in a way is for them to gain more power. And the third point would make banishers redundant or at least ideologically correct. If all mage thinks this banisher status as "heretic murderers" is downgraded to "just murderers" and even then.

                            Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post
                            • The Nimbus grows more powerful as a Mage gains in power as a representation of their growing distance from humanity, acting similarly to the Wake from Leviathan.
                            I will recomend resonance rules in, of all places, Las vegas source book for mage ascension page 122. Its has rules for a more "proactive" nimbus that is similar to this. Fair warning as many things from ascension is dependent of DM fiat extremely.
