        Originally posted by Cauthon View Post
        "Teleport to the sun" does not have to literally be the surface of the sun. As with the satellite in near-earth orbit, you could do the same thing with a probe that's in the solar neighborhood for effectively the same result.
        And a similar degree of legwork, neither of which comes implicit in the usual statement of "teleport it to the Sun to kill it."

        And if we're really being pedantic, this I'd a Legacy that descends partially from the Panopticon. The Prelacy Crown of which is pretty darn terrifying.
        We can get even more pedantic and point out that a location is not a subject, but also this sounds more like an argument against a Teleportation-based killing Attainment where the non-Seer half of the Legacy still exists.

        My point was the less dramatic "teleport the body to a different location". I chose "the sun" or "the Mariana Trench" because the hyperbole is far more evocative.
        I would recommend avoiding it in the future because of this specific derailment. Enough people overestimate the baseline capabilities of the Awakened template without immediately Going To Space with your examples. "Your Sanctum's incinerator/drowning tank/death trap" gets the point across.

        Besides which, the main thrust of my arguement was that "My character / the DMPC kills this character and disposes of the body with a single touch" is far less interesting than "I killed this person with a touch and now need to dispose of the body". Yes, disposal in the second case still only takes a few turns, but it has more flavor and chance to get caught in the act than a disintegration ray that doesn't even line up with the proposed application of Forces.
        Blades in the Dark, a game where bodies need to be specially cremated to prevent ghosts from rising and magical animals can track the location of the recently deceased, provides its Assassin crew type with advancement options to circumvent both of these through "hard-won experience or occult ritual," and there are systems in that game specifically built around minimizing the attention you draw (when killing happening at all during a heist you were on draws attention whether you're the ones who did it or not). Much as I would love for Chronicles to have a generalized attention/exposure system to hook stuff like the Masquerade and the Veil and Cover and whatnot into, it is not a system whereby the default assumption is that extremely powerful monsters still need to go out of their way to hide a body, especially when those monsters truck with the lore that bends light and stokes flames and holds things together.

        The thrust of your argument is "A Gnosis 8 Master of an Arcanum at the apex of their Legacy's Attainments can disintegrate someone with a touch and that's less interesting than needing to worry about body disposal in a game where magic can do a great deal and I've just spent multiple posts talking about how with a bunch of previously-unstated prep you could get better mileage by teleporting them somewhere unpleasant that you have the sympathies for."

        I agree that From Dust to Ash is a bit out of left-field after the preceding Attainments. I strongly disagree that fatal use of Teleportation is a superior use of Legacy-building resources to the same end.

                                    I know this is a long over due response but I moved on to other things over Christmas while the forums were down and then forgot to come back and give an update, so here I am as I'm working on finishing this up.

                                    Magic, to The Glasaugen, is an expression of knowledge. Not abstract or philosophical knowledge, but useful, practical knowledge about a someone or something. You study a person, place, or thing (from the shadows) in order to build up a parasocial sympathetic connection to them so that you can preform magic, but the construction of that sort of sympathy is also magic. Parasocial sympathy is something I will be going back in the original writeup to add in to their philosophy.

                                    "Magic is holy when only the one doing the magic can see it
                                    When the person who is being affected by magic has no clue the magic is happened, that is divinity. It is not divine in the sense that it is next to god, inspired by god, or a reflection of divinity. It is divinity within the fallen world" - This is still true for them

                                    Magic is parasocial and unseen by all but the one casting, that is where you will find Truth.

                                    Still a work in progress but now that I'm back to working on this I believe I'll get it more fleshed out. ​
