    Touchstones?

    01-01-2020, 06:44 AM
    I know mages are human and Touchstones are supposed to be the things that keep inhuman supernaturals grounded. But then look at how werewolves have a Touchstone for both their flesh and spirit sides, granting their Touchstones a different context to how vampires use them. I think mages could use Touchstones in a similar way. Mages may be human, but they experience things most humans don't. Magical awareness tends to isolate them from the common man, especially considering Quiescence. Touchstones could provide an anchor to humanity and aid their Wisdom. Directly causing a Sleeper to experience Quiescence is always an Act of Hubris, but doing it to their Touchstone would provide a powerfully emotional context to their hubris.

    Just an idea at this point, I don't have any hard mechanics on it or even specific examples. I just thought of throwing it out there to see what others think about it.

          I'm inclined to agree with Heavy Arms. A Mage isolating himself from humanity has very little to do with Wisdom. That's more a product of their Obsessions. Like, Harry Dresden is a low-Wisdom Mage, but when he's not on his bullshit he remembers and interacts with regular folks pretty easily.               Look at how the gamelines with Touchstones operate in terms of Integrity-equivalent:

              • Vampires and changelings literally risk Humanity loss or Clarity damage just from spending too much time without human interaction, period, and their Touchstones in particular keep them from edging closer to frenzy or getting attrition'd into a coma. They're built to be social creatures that interact with humanity on a semiregular basis, or at least have developed odd periodic workarounds to the fact that isolation starts to tilt them a little bit sideways.

              • Deviants incur Instability over time if they aren't actively pursuing the relationships their Touchstones currently represent, and the only other means they have of restoring their Stability besides aiming down those sights is to roll well with their volatile superpowers, a state that they mostly need to be stable in the first place to cultivate. They start with nearly as many Touchstones as a vampire or changeling can mechanically have and maintaining them is the core focus of the game's default arc to such a degree that "how many Touchstones you still have" is a secondary Integrity analog.

              • Werewolves start with two Touchstones, can't get more than that, can shift the benefits of one over the other, mostly use them to slow their jump up or down the Harmony scale or quickly correct a shift in the wrong direction, and have a slightly stronger rationale than usual for having their Touchstones be things that aren't creatures. They detach their Touchstones at points near the ends of the scale they're ideally trying to keep to the middle of, which makes it easier for them to get stuck at the ends and effectively trapped in one of the two worlds they inhabit while more susceptible to Death Rage.

              • Sin-Eaters start with one Touchstone, get another one and then another one as their Synergy rises (which they lose access to if their Synergy falls), naturally raise their Synergy by resolving the issues at the heart of their Touchstones, temporarily raise their Synergy in the pursuit of those issues, and run into trouble when two or more of their Touchstones come into conflict with each other. It's entirely possible for them to deal with their Burden and Remembrance in order and wind up with only one Touchstone left to worry about — the one that represents their vision for the Underworld — and in fact this is encouraged by the fact that the only benefit to having multiple Touchstones is being able to pursue both at once for a larger temporary Synergy boost.

              • Mummies pick up Touchstones electively as a source of Reminisce Beats, and notably they're the only such source that is both inexhaustible and not tied to destroying relics. They are valuable for their impact on the ability to cultivate Memory rather than their utility in staving off its loss, and much of their other benefits are inherited from the other social connections they have to the mummy, like holding their Pillars or being their Sadikh.

              • Hunters (assuming the 2017 preview rules still hold more or less true) have Touchstones in the same model as vampires and changelings do (start with one, pick up more as a Merit, having multiple attached gives you an extra die on breaking point rolls), have converted their Integrity to a Wisdom-esque loosely-tiered set of breaking points that you can be hardened to, need to have a heart-to-heart with their Touchstones to buy back Integrity, roll Resolve + Composure for breaking points with a penalty from the breaking point and bonus from their Touchstone(s) alongside other modifiers, and get a little weird on exceptional successes to keep their Integrity. They're very likely to be under a Persistent Condition that makes them Spooked fairly frequently, and the key concept surrounding their Integrity as a social widget is more or less "trust."

              Mages already track things and people that are important to them through sympathy, can't meaningfully tell most people about the things they see for reasons that have nothing to do with how important that person is to them, and are host to a power that not only doesn't get worse with isolation but passively steers complications towards their sympathetic connections in a way that is only partially managed by maintaining a separate magical identity to ground those complications towards. In other words, they don't have Touchstones for similar reasons that the Begotten, the Unchained, and the Created don't have Touchstones.

              You'd need to determine what Awakened Touchstones are actually for, because transplanting the rationale from another gameline outright simply doesn't work and in some cases is directly antithetical to how some existing fact of Awakened existence already works — for example, your first Touchstone in Geist is built around an Aspiration, but Touchstones don't help you maintain Synergy in Geist and Mage's equivalent (Obsessions) is actively detrimental to your Wisdom even as it's necessary for you to buy Wisdom back up. There's more to Touchstones than "thing that keeps your Integrity stat from falling."

                                                          Arms has brought up the main other thing I'd thought to say, which is that proper maintenance of your Shadow Name is the primary means of doing what you seem to want Touchstones to accomplish — the thing about Wisdom and the Awakened is that it's there as a measure of how well you're able to moderate yourself away from the extremes of Obsession and how concerned you are for the consequences of being a magical actor.

                                                          In other words, the problem mages face is not the problem monsters with Touchstones face — they aren't dealing with intrusive thoughts from alien parapsychology or a the significance of things on the borders of a driving purpose or the flailing attempts of a broken soul to find stability, they're humans who've made themselves into conduits for unadulterated god-magic that is dangerous but thrilling to use, which means they're struggling to various degrees with managing an addiction rather than holding themselves together on a day-to-day level.

                                                          A healthy priority for the Awakened is keeping the supernatural as far away from any mundane life they care about and/or cultivating enough discipline that they can let incidental contact with specimens of the occult go instead of diving into scrutiny and experimentation, which if anything suggests the thing you want to do to encourage mundane living is set up something like Hunter's Persistent Conditions with an early-exit clause of "gain a dot of Wisdom."
