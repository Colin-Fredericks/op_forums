                                        I personally think you should consider that by considering how many mages you think exist in the world or in the country of your setting, and then the surrounding area of the main location. I personally consider anyone whose dice pool with improvised magic to be 6 dice to be a "professional" for whatever magical tasks they get up to. Gnosis 3 is when your rituals get really short, and you've finally got access to combined spells, as well as decent headway for legacy progression, with that in mind, I personally consider mages with gnosis 3 and path arcana 3 to be the meat of any consilium--anyone above that are major players and actively shape the politics and research and captain the resources for the local area. Even if you have thousands or tens of thousands of mages world-wide, I personally think that Masters of any arcana should be rare enough that you could count them on your hands.

                                        The more important the players should be in addition to making a suppressive, "we're alone or outnumbered" kind of scenario, the less powerful mages you might want to have. If you want Mage society to be positively active and exciting, like a secret world where there's constantly stuff going on and lots of interesting people to meet, then you might have dozens of isolated Masters who specialize in specific things and pull the weight of mentor's and teachers and top-researchers in various fields--but I personally feel that runs the risk of sometimes making the player characters seem unnecessary if they get caught up in a conflict that would be better resolved by the masters.

                                        Unless the conflict is totally personal and related to the PC's obsessions so there's a clear reason why only the PC cabal(s) are involved in the drama of your chronicle. I dunno if that is helpful or too much to take in, and it is all just my opinion, but if that helped at all...

                                            I use two guidelines for generating NPC stats in my game. These are totally just a model that I almost always fine-tune, but it helps to have some kind of skeleton to work from.

                                            First, decide if the NPC is simple or complex. This isn't so much a reflection of importance to the story as whether or not I expect to roll complex dice for them during a scene. My PCs have/had an important and powerful mentor who I never fully statted, and a more minor mage who's just their friend and sometimes joins in during action scenes, so I gave them full stats.

                                            All named NPCs (ideally) get a Concept, Virtue/Vice, one Aspiration and one Obsession.

                                            In both cases, arbitrarily rate their power from 1-5 dots. 1 dot is equivalent to a freshly created PC, 3 dots is a "rival" whom they can be challenged by as a party but expect to win, 5 dots is a major force.

                                            Simple NPCs: use Retainer as a guide for dicepools. Choose a number of tasks/abilities equal to their dots that they're skilled at. Choose Gnosis/Arcana ratings based on the Mentor merit relative to a generic starting character.

                                            Complex NPCs: make a starting character. Add Gnosis dots equal to NPC's dot level. Add Experiences as follows:
                                            • 1 dot: 5 XP
                                            • 2 dot: 10 XP, 5 AXP
                                            • 3 dot: 15 XP, 10 AXP
                                            • 4 dot: 25 XP, 15 AXP
                                            • 5 dot: 30 XP, 20 AXP

                                                        My simple question is:

                                                        How should you handle legacy attainments that make sense to be something that gets superceded by straight arcana attainments at higher levels? (say, a first attainment that unveils something that the arcana at 2 just allows.

                                                        (I'm leaning towards making the legacy attainment improve on learning a later attainment that guarantees that the attainment has become redundant, but not using up that attainment to achieve it, but this seems like stretching or breaking the rules as stated. Alternately, you could word the attainment in such a way as to have it slide things over naturally. E.G. 1st Attainment improves the ability to see spirits and related phenomena without a spell by a step. If the mage normally requires deep scrutiny or an active spell to see a thing, it is now visible to Active Mage Sight If they normally can see something with Active Mage Sight, the attainment boosts that up to Peripheral Mage Sight, but with full clarity.)

                                                        Wait: Better questions:

                                                        Where (or what) are the rules for when a Yantra can be used?

                                                        Area isn't consistent about mentioning the rules. Demesnes seem to assume that you will cast from inside to get the benefit, but I don't see it actually state this anywhere. Environment actually says you need to cast from within in its Benefit section. Supernal Verge makes the point only in the first bit, but it actually says so. Presumably, that's intended, but it could help to say so.

                                                        Conceivably, some area Yantra effects could apply based off of the location of the target, though, rather than the caster. Still, skip area.

                                                        Actions seem pretty clear all the way through.

                                                        Tools are where intent becomes vague. Pretty sure that actually holding a tool was intended by "use" but that is far from clear. This applies to pretty much all of the Tools section. There's a bit of debate over whether or not a guy counts as Representational Yantra of himself. Reading that section, the only suggestion that such a maneuver might not work is:

                                                        Originally posted by "Mage: The Awakening 2nd Edition
                                                        ...allowing the mage to build it into the Imago or access an Attainment to use her own sympathetic connection to the subject in order to cast across Space or Time.
                                                        Also, without any particulars on how specific "use" is supposed to be, we're left with a lot of calls by ear.

                                                        Incense would be a sacrament, presumably. A wand should be waved around deliberately and appropriately for the spell, but couldn't be used if someone knocked it out of the mage's hand. A lucky coin might need to be flipped into the air, so somebody grabbing it could cancel it.
                                                        None of those is very well-defined by the rules.

                                                        If a player can argue that their wand is symbolic for a spell when it's in an enemy's hand should it still apply?

                                                        I get why this is vague---authors didn't want to paint people into a corner---but it's a bit much.

                                                        What are valid conditions for Keys?

                                                        There is almost no explanation in the Space section on the topic. Is there someplace that actually explains more?

                                                        An example given is "Those of my bloodline". "Those who mean me well and are under their own control."---Would that be a 2 Potency Key? If I include "Only open for people I want admitted." would that be legitimate? The clauses being limited by Potency is a good idea, but the scope of the clauses is not particularly defined, there.
