        Hold your horses. I am not employing deductive logic.

        To be clear, I am not trying to prove anything or make a logical case for anything. I was not attempting to deduce the existence of non-human mages from what is stated in the books. I apologize if it seemed like I was attempting to make the case that there must be Ape mages because human apes can become mages. As we have seen in pretty much ever speculative thread on this forum, the authors get to decide what is absolutely true based off the themes, and everything else is spooky vagueness. This isn't a "fan theory" that I hope DaveB is going to come in and confirm for me.

        The only thing I am trying to get out of this thread is to explore a potential chronicle spanning mystery on the relationship between Humans as animals, Mages as Humans, and other animals. I thought an Ape Mage or Proximus would be a really really weird and could be a good central draw for a chronicle. This thread has so far been pretty productive because people had brought up various aspects of why a non human ape shouldn't be a Mage. So putting one in my own setting makes it an even stranger mystery.

                        A few random facts:

                        1) The Omphalos protects the collective thoughts and dreams of humanity by isolating them as a "Temenos". Omphalos is also an artificial construct.

                        2) When you "lose soul", you lose, among other things, your capability to connect with other people.

                        3) There is a big ass spire growing from Omphalos up to the Anima Mundi, symbolizing mankind's connection to the Dreamtime and was supposedly used as a scaffolding for the Celestial Ladder.

                        4) Well, it grew once. Now it's being reabsorbed by typhonides of the Aeon of Death, supposedly by the order of "gods". Typhonides would devour Temenos as well, but can't because of Omphalos.

                        5) There is a new connection to the Dreamtime, known as the Swath, established on July 16, 1945. It becomes more pronounced as time goes on, and there are rumors about a realm existing in it, that symbolizes the fixing of Swath's diseased nature and essentially perfecting humans' connection to the Dreamtime.

                        6) Spires called the Watchtowers grow, too, out of the Supernal Realms on the other side of reality, though unlike the neolithic mages, most modern Awakened probably don't notice it. It's also relatively less stressful for mages to sign the Towers today.


                        Make of it what you will.

                        ----

                        As for "losing souls", I would say that their connection to Temenos and Omphalos is essential for today's humans' normal perception of life. There was a time when Omphalos didn't exist, with all the resulting consequences.

                        There is also a place in the Dreamtime called The Wellspring, where animals, supposedly including humans, can gain sapience and even Awaken, without needing Temenos. Or Omphalos.
