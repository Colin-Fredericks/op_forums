    Dissonance Edge Cases

    05-13-2017, 11:38 AM
    I use Fate to give a Sleepwalker Electrokinesis. This merit is non-supernal, and is 100% Fallen (Supernatural) Phenomena.

    When that Sleepwalker runs around zapping people with lightning bolts, does Dissonance kick in? Does Quiescence? They aren't actually witnessing anything Supernal happening, but they are witnessing something obviously supernatural, and that supernatural effect's root source was Supernal Magic.

                                  The sidebar on what the definition of "obvious magic" is bears remembering — a ghost manifesting is certainly clearly, openly supernatural and a Sleeper who is lucid and capable of putting two and two together re: spectral apparitions appearing out of thin air is by definition conscious and cognizant of that not being something that just happens — nothing about any facet of the Curse requires that a specific actor be intuited as responsible for the impossibility that confronts the Sleeper witness.

                                  It serves as a decent anti-shovelhead measure, but it also lends to the isolating effects of Awakened magic if its visibly fantastical products are more or less consistently kept to the shadows — a fetish or a Fated psychic or a constructed ghost need not be imperishable to be useful as a Paradox-free supernatural instrument.

                                      I actually did read that before considering. The thing I keep in mind with that however is that while yes, something supernatural is obviously magical, only supernal things are what actually are affected by dissonance and the Abyss. The curse strikes sleepers after obvious magic, but only "Magic" in the sense of mages. Sleepers don't deal with the curse when seeing obvious fallen magic. By this token, what's obvious must be the magic of the spell itself - not the magic of the individual or being affected by the spell.

                                      Take the ghost. Say I cast a spell to force a ghost to materialize. I have cast that spell - this will create dissonance in anyone who sees it, because by it's nature it's supernal. _I_ did that. If a ghost had materialized without it, it would not have this issue. But if I were to -command- a ghost with psychic domination to materialize, it would be an aftereffect of me casting that spell - but just because I had given him the command which leads to a sleeper seeing an obviously supernatural effect does not really mean that my mental compulsion over the ghost wavers if they do not recognize that I am, in fact, controlling the ghost. It's still obviously magic, but it's not my magic, and it's not supernal magic. Similarly, if I granted a spirit the ability to possess someone, I would not immediately assume that when the spirit did the spell granting it that ability would be subject to dissonance - even with the mortal a puppet in their own body. Because while its my spell giving it the ability to possess, the possession itself is not the spell - the granting of it is.

                                      In this case, you are granting a person something supernatural - but it's not Truth. Because it's not Truth, when Sleepers witness it they don't have to pit it against the Abyssal Lie.

                                          Two problems with this:

                                          1. Dissonance and Quiescence occur for more than just Awakened spellcasting even though they aren't brought on by less concentrated forms of supernatural power — most notably Supernal and Abyssal beings and Verges. Further, the effects of Dissonance on ruins of the Time Before are more or less flush with the effects of Disbelief on Atlantean ruins in 1e; surely "orphaned" supernatural inhabitants of such ruins would have merited mention somewhere if they weren't subject to the same vulnerability to exposure as their homes and wards?

                                          2. The example of mind-controlling a ghost into manifesting doesn't work as a point of comparison to outright giving someone an obvious supernatural power that they didn't have before — the effect of mind-controlling an invisible monster into revealing itself is not "clearly, openly supernatural" without the context of a controlling actor. By a similar token, the way ephemeral possession works means by definition anyone under spiritual control is not conscious or cognizant at all, much less specifically of the impossibility of what's happening — if the indwelling spirit spent Essence to heal its host body and other Sleepers witnessed the open wounds closing, meanwhile, that's an obvious supernatural effect that relies on sustained Awakened magic to happen and therefore ought to prompt the same risks as regular spellcasting.

                                          Again I must point out that the only thing this reading really does is cut down on the extent to which heavily-altered overt supernatural proxies are a viable way for Masters and Adepts to conduct their business relative to taking on apprentices and/or getting a familiar or three; high-level magic is often more durable than the average Integrity rating in isolation, and the ability to more comprehensively declare one's available resources is advantage enough without extending the Subtle Arcana's lowered susceptibility to the Curse from their purviews' reduced visibility to outright immunity to the usual consequences of those facets of their Mysteries that would be visible.

                                                          1) Dissonance effects anything Supernal. It does not actually affect the Abyss. Verges are explicitly immune to Dissonance, and in fact prevent spells cast in them from being subject to them (assuming they are natural - Demenses are very much not immune to Dissonance). In this, the question is whether or not Dissonance is a factor to the effects. As far as the ruins of the Time before, I'll be honest - I'm not exactly sure what your bringing to the table in this part of the discussion or what your referencing so it's hard for me to understand the point you are trying to make with that reference.

                                                          2) Fair point on the possession bit basically being something the mortal can't notice (though I don't agree with the essence-to-heal bit causing dissonance), so I will address the issue with the comparison to using Mind to create the supernatural effect. The problem here, is that to the mortal there is something supernaturally occuring and obviously supernaturally occuring in both instances. In both instances, the mortal does NOT know or sense the mage's part in this play. In both situations, the Sleepers do not witness "Obvious Supernal Magic" - they simply witness obvious magic. In both cases they see a supernatural effect, but it is not the mage's supernatural effect - even though the mage's supernatural effect is the reason they are seeing the effect they do see. Because it is not the mage's effect that they witness, it is not the mage's spell that would be the target of dissonance. Because the effect that they DO witness is not a viable option for dissonance, then dissonance should not be an issue. Yes - the major difference is in this case the mage is granting a gun, rather than telling a person to fire it. But in both cases, the person shooting the gun is not the mage.

                                                          I get and understand that this particular reading doesn't really make much of a difference in the long run. Dissonance takes time, and really you are going to need to get 6 successes on that dissonace roll at the end of the scene to even touch a person's Fate Spell. This discussion is fairly academic. I still enjoy having it though, because as small a change as that is, it does alter methodologies of many mages - even if only slightly.
