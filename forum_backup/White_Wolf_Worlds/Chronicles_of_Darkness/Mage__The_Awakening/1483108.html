    Fantasy Races and Mage

    06-05-2022, 02:35 AM
    This is essentially an attempt by me to combine Mage the Awakening's mechanics with a standard fantasy setting/RPG. Tired as some of the tropes may be, the concept of elves and dwarves and all that jazz is still a timeless one, and so I wondered what they would look like in a world using Awakening's magic system. It's not a perfect concept, but I think it could be an interesting one.

    I'll gladly admit that the humans were the ones who gave me the most trouble, along with the orcs, so if you have any suggestions for changes I'll gladly take them. For each sub-race I've listed a source of Inspiration that helped shaped my image and view of the sub-race's appearance and culture.

    Rules: Every race has 2 "Typical" Sub-races, each of which is associated with a given Path and Watchtower. Mages in these subraces are substantially comprised of spellcasters from their given Path, though exceptions exist. Every race also has 2 "Rare" Sub-races, which are less common and embody different Paths. This leaves 1 Path left, which the race very rarely has members for.

    The Races Include:
    • Humans
      • Typical Sub-Races
        • Nomad: Mastigos
          • Description: A group of explorers and trailblazers, seeking new lands to visit and colonize. In more sedentary groups, they drive large swathes of livestock in their travels so that the animals have the food they need. Their talent with Mind allows them to understand the languages of any newcomers, and Space assists them in getting around from place to place.
          • Inspiration: Pioneers/Wild West (American History)
        • City Folk: Obrimos
          • Description: People born, raised and dead in large city-states which at times unite together in alliances against greater foes. They use Forces to keep themselves comfortable regardless of location, and Prime to protect themselves from the hostile magic of others.
          • Inspiration: Grecian History
      • Rare Sub-Races
        • Desert Dwellers: Moros
          • Description: A culture of desert folk who assist those lost in the sands and quickly lose their temper with liars. They use their skill with Matter to provide assistance generously to those in need, and control the burgeoning population of ghosts that wander the dunes.
          • Inspiration: Egyptian Mythology
        • Druids: Thyrsus
          • Description: A group of forest dwellers who find harmony with nature and protect it against predation. Their reliance on Life can heal others and change themselves to create a greater connection with the world, and their ability to communicate with Spirits allows them a wide variety of potential benefits at any time.
          • Inspiration: Druids (Dungeons and Dragons)
      • Unusual Path: Acanthus
        • Oracles and Witches are quite uncommon among humanity, and though they are respected they are also feared for their control over the invisible hand of destiny.
    • Elves
      • Typical Sub-Races
        • Faeries: Acanthus
          • Description: The most capricious of the elves, the Faeries (known for having the longest ears of the race) delight in tricks and subterfuge. Their talent in Time allows them to take the measure of any situation to know when the timing is right for a prank, and Fate is useful for ensuring that what little there is of their central culture remains as an institution.
          • Inspiration: Fae (Mythology)
        • Wildkin: Thyrsus
          • Description: The elves of the woodlands, their mages sometimes grow horns as they increase in knowledge. They despise outsiders to their lands, and utilize their command of Life and Spirit to gather an army of walking trees and animal spirits to chase them out.
          • Inspiration: Wood Elves (Warhammer Fantasy)
      • Rare Sub-Races
        • Cave Elves: Mastigos
          • Description: At times the most prideful, at others the most humble, the Cave Elves are always the most religious of the long-eared peoples. They banish their inner demons with Mind for short spans in order to realize their inner failings, and dash through cave systems with Space in order to escape the dangerous wild life that dwells below.
          • Inspiration: Drow (Dungeons and Dragons)
        • High Elves: Obrimos
          • Description: The High Elves are the most magnanimous of their kind, practicing generosity tainted by hubris. Their command of Prime makes them among the greatest spellcasters in the entire world, and they rain down lightning and fire with Forces as they believe them to be under their jurisdiction.
          • Inspiration: High Elves (Warcraft)
      • Unusual Path: Moros
        • Brimming with life, Elves find it difficult to manipulate the dead. While they are associated with nature, they do not guide the waters or shape the earth, instead singing plants to grow to fit their needs.
    • Dwarves
      • Typical Sub-Races
        • Halflings: Acanthus
          • Description: A simple group of farmers and chefs, the Halflings are down-to-earth and the most hospitable and friendly among the shorter races. They divine the weather with Time to understand when best to grow crops and plan events, and the good luck provided by Fate allows them to get out of less-than-fortunate circumstances, or avoid them in the first place.
          • Inspiration: Hobbits (Lord of the Rings)
        • Mountain Folk: Moros
          • Description: The Mountain Dwarves build fortresses out of hills and palaces out of mountains. They produce some of the finest tools with Matter, and use Death to communicate with their ancestors as part of their religious services.
          • Inspiration: Dwarves (Lord of the Rings)
      • Rare Sub-Races
        • Subterranean: Mastigos
          • Description: Dwelling in tunnels far below their Mountain-dwelling cousins, the Subterranean Dwarves are more practiced with guile than is typical for their kind. They protect their homes and cities with wards constructed through Space, and warn others away with feelings of anxiety provided by control of the Mind.
          • Inspiration: Orzammar (Dragon Age)
        • Gnomes: Obrimos
          • Description: Gnomes are the one race of Dwarves to live in cities and larger populaces, using social connections to make up for a lack of physical prowess. They construct magical items for practically every need with Prime, and control flying machines through the use of Forces.
          • Inspiration: Gnomes (Warcraft)
      • Unusual Path: Thyrsus
        • Dwarves understand tools, they understand minerals, and they understand people. But it's a rare dwarf that knows how to talk to trees and beasts, or who knows how to see the spirits around them, as they rely more on guidance from their ancestors for their day-to-day life.
    • Elementals
      • Typical Sub-Races
        • Solid: Moros
          • Description: Whether built of earth or frozen water, the Solid are a stubborn, but dependable lot who support their friends. They command corpses and constructs of stone as "lesser golems" used for labour. After 2 decades of service, such golems are granted sapience and made an honorary citizen among their people as a reward.
          • Inspiration: Earth/Water Elementals (Dungeons and Dragons)
        • Flowing: Obrimos
          • Description: Shifting in appearance with the temperature, the Flowing Elementals drift through life, passing from friend to friend in pursuit of seldom understood personal goals. They maintain their existence through a dance with universal Forces, and dispel any attempts to disrupt their spark with Prime.
          • Inspiration: Air/Fire Elementals (Dungeons and Dragons)
      • Rare Sub-Races
        • Stuttering: Acanthus
          • Description: Resembling flickering, grayed-out silhouettes, the Stuttering are a people who speak little, especially in regard to their existence as the flow of time personified. They maintain control over their chaotic existence through binding oaths of Fate, and "repeat" actions to different results through their innate connection to Time.
          • Inspiration: Codex (XCOM 2)
        • Dryads: Thyrsus
          • Description: Trees who have grown enough in sentience to develop a second humanoid body, they defend their main bodies and fellows against lumberjacks and other land desecraters. They maintain the health of their woodlands through a relationship with Life magic, and speak to the slumbering Spirit present in younger trees.
          • Inspiration: Dryad (Greek Mythology)
      • Unusual Path: Mastigos
        • Elementals sometimes find themselves at ill ease with the living (or in the case of the dryads, the sentient living). Understanding how people think or talk is difficult at times, and being as tied to the elements as they are, separating "distance" from material landmarks can be a struggle for them.
    • Orcs
      • Typical Sub-Races
        • Peace-Makers: Mastigos
          • Description: A collective of individual pacifist scholars, the Peace-Makers engage in diplomacy that is thankfully backed by the sword rarely. Their great Minds allow them to master a variety of skills and subjects, and communicate with distant relatives along Space-backed lines of sympathy.
          • Inspiration: Ogier (Wheel of Time)
        • Trolls: Thyrsus
          • Description: Large giants who hate the sun's light, they rarely interact with civilization in favor of simpler lifestyles. They grow and collect food for themselves with a ruling stake in Life, and cultivate Spirit friends to warn them of enemies approaching.
          • Inspiration: Trolls (Lord of the Rings)
      • Rare Sub-Races
        • Goblins: Acanthus
          • Description: Smallest of the orcs, the Goblins are actually the most populous of all the races. They received their "rare" status due to the lack of Mages among them, after all becoming a mage generally requires a soul closer to adulthood and goblin children, though numerous, die young and fast. Their combination of Prime and Forces allows them to construct some of the most powerful and hilarious weapons the world has ever seen.
          • Inspiration: Goblins (Warcraft)
        • Barren: Moros
          • Descriptions: Orcs have a tendency to survive in less than ideal circumstances, but the Barren exemplify this to a tee. They reside in inhospitable lands where no one else can go for safety. They protect themselves from toxic materials through Matter and hunt their prey through command of Death itself.
          • Inspiration: Super Mutants (Fallout)
      • Unusual Path: Obrimos
        • The Orcs find difficulty in imagining the "perfection" which the Aether represents. They can change what they can grasp, but who can grasp the wind? Who can get a hold on magic's strange inner laws?

              I would like to recommend avoiding using specific philosophies or lifestyles, and instead to paint with very broad strokes the ways an environment influences a culture. For fantasy races in particular, try drawing from several very diverse global cultures, and use loose enough descriptions that all of them could be distinct nations within that sub-race.
              The Paths already have a philosophy they’re centered around; further aligning that to a particular belief system within a race starts leaning toward some flat and problematically specific stereotypes.
              In my opinion, each race should ideally be diverse enough that people could awaken to any Path without it being out of place, even if the culture overall resonates with one in particular. Like, the vast majority (of the already rare Awakened) might fall within one Path, but we can see how any Path would fit in and still emphasize that race’s themes.

              I’d also suggest showing a broader variety of expressions for the Paths. It seems like you’ve repeated a particular emphasis on Thyrsus = Forest = Druid, for example, but there is incredible diversity in what Life magic can accomplish, and even more so with spirit magic. I’d recommend instead have each race’s approach to a Path totally different, as a way to both show the diversity of the Path symbolism and make each race more distinct.


              As examples, here’s the ideas for Humans that I’ve been mulling over:

              The Sun Cherished (Human - Thrysus)
              People from great plains and marshy lowlands who developed remarkable agricultural practices - building their success by transforming the creatures and plants around them, along with the landscape itself. They depend on vast fields and herds; their people spread wide across their territories, staying connected through seasonal celebrations.
              Relying on an abundance of only a few crops can lead to entrenched traditionalism, but at the root of the Sun Cherished is valuing the wisdom and patience to recognize and cultivate the potential in new plants and animals. Similarly, being so spread out can incentivize insular, self sufficient groups, but the most successful clans foster interdependence and generosity, ensuring that everyone can thrive when disaster devastates lone farms at random.
              • Inspiration: slash and burn agriculturalists, pastoralists, Great Plains settlers, terraced farmers
              • Presenting Human thrysus as domestication, to contrast with the Elven druid-like thrysus

              The Stone Drinkers (Human - Moros)
              Hailing from deserts, mountains, and wastes, these resourceful clans were able to find hidden bounties in the harshest environments. They concentrated around oases,mountain springs, and even learned to call water up from beneath the desert dunes. They survive through relentless curiosity, searching everywhere for something useful, and experimenting with everything they find. Unlike Hill Dwarves, which flourish on precious gems and metals, these human clans master clay and stone, finding uses for ground minerals that can nearly be called magic.
              Driven by the scarcity of their homelands, many Stone Drinker clans either cling fiercely to the refuges they find (or make), or travel across an extensive territory between sparse watering holes. Having little to make vellum or paper with, they prefer oral storytelling to communicate, although have several exorbitant cities of stone around their most verdant oases.
              • To get a little specific here, limestone is basically magic - used for carving huge stone monuments, plaster/cement (and thus really good kilns), water and air purification, improving soil Ph for crops, calcium supplements, and glass making - and usually has large deposits of oil and metals in it.
              • Inspiration: Middle East pottery cultures, Saharan nomads, Ancient Egypt, Puebloan First Nations, Aboriginal Australians
              • Presenting Human Moros as transmutation and impermanence, in contrast with Dwarven Moros as wealth and legacy

              The Wave Riders (Human - Mastigos)
              Seafaring peoples, who, settling in lands of mostly barren hills and unforgiving jungle, spread across vast island chains and archipelagos, primarily relying on small fishing villages clinging to inhospitable shores. They were the earliest and most daring explorers of the seas, staking a village on nearly every shore they touched - but, remarkably, have retained many united clans. Through remarkable ships, uncanny mastery of the stars and wind, and more than a little magic, merchants and explorers regularly circle every port in their sprawling empire.
              Wave Riders don’t avoid the land exactly, but they don’t expand very far from the shore: usually, another race is already there, and they’d much prefer to trade and negotiate than make any enemies. But they don’t shy away from building wherever there’s room, and have several huge kingdoms on large islands and peninsulas. While other races do sail, the Wave Riders invest in making every port accessible - and ruthlessly controlling narrow straights and channels to ensure everyone has to use their ports eventually.
              • Inspiration: Phoenician & Aegean sea traders, Polynesian explorers, Srivijaya and the India-to-China trade routes in general, sixteenth century maritime empires, Norwegian fishing towns
              • Presenting Human Mastigos as boundaries and connectivity. There’s not really a good contrast point for them yet, but possibly Orc Mastigos as self mastery.

                          Ah, I misspoke in one section. I recommend making every *sub*race broad enough that any Path could still reinforce their themes, even while basing them on one in particular.
                          That doesn’t require pointing out specifically how those Paths fit in, just making sure every subrace has a full range of lifestyles and philosophies, without getting too prescriptive.

                          In my Wave Rider example, the Mastigos resonance at their core is mostly reflected in the Boundaries created by the sea and the Connectivity maintained by traveling merchants (plus a sprinkling of diplomacy). But the majority of the people would be in mostly sedentary fishing villages and large trading ports (where a Moros might be more valuable), and they also claim whole islands - possibly with farming towns, and definitely harvesting various fruits and spices (where a Thrysus could shine). Obrimos might be common on volcanic islands, not only mastering the elements, but imposing strict social hierarchies to manage resources and respond to danger efficiently; while navigating by stars, living in rhythm with the tide, and divining the best times to sail would best benefit from Acanthus.

                          Moreover, different Wave Rider nations could be characterized as bitter self-sufficient fishers nestled in frozen fjords and rocky cliffs, island plantation farmers, freedom-loving surf and sun resorts, diplomatic but greed obsessed merchant kings controlling a necessary and fiercely coveted port, pirates and explorer-marauders, or evil empires believing that an iron fist is the only way to keep people united and safe.
                          All of those expressions keep the core themes of the subrace, and the Mastigos. Being broad enough to encompass all those (and more) helps them feel more like a “race” in a fantasy setting, rather than a particular monastery.

                          So while the seed that inspired the culture is Mastigos explorers keeping a people unified as they spread across distant islands, be sure to grow and expand on that until every Path (and by extension, a variety of lifestyles or personal philosophies) could fit in.

                              Honestly, I think you're onto something. Let me try and give a small example:

                              Dwarves

                              The Dwarves are among the shortest of the five great races of the world, and oftentimes the stoutest, but their iron wills have ensured their survival throughout the generations. This willpower, their Perseverance, is one of the defining traits of Dwarven culture. Dwarves are generally associated with the element of Earth and the minerals within it, using the bounties of the ground below to sustain their lives. All dwarves are trained to be excellent in at least one craft, with certain sub-races having much more of an emphasis on one in particular. Other crafts are still useful to a society, and so they have their masters as well. Finally, even among the civilized races as a whole, Dwarves respect and revere the family above all other institutions, for good or for ill. Ancestors are respected and in some culture worshipped as bringers of good fortune and wisdom. The family is the group you stick by no matter what, even if that means at times performing criminal acts for its sake. Going against the family's customs then is a betrayal on par with blasphemy, and many such traitors are punished with at minimum exile, if not greater acts.

                              Altogether, the defining traits of Dwarven kind are:
                              • Perseverance
                              • The Element of Earth
                              • Craftsmanship
                              • Families

                              Dwarven Sub-Race: Halflings

                              Halflings are a group of dwarves known for living on the surface and being forever cheerful, at least in appearance. They are slightly thinner on their limbs than most dwarves, and maintain shorter beards for style and ease of maintenance. Their idea of Perseverance takes the form of boundless optimism, remaining steadfast in the belief that the future will get better with time and effort. They interact with the Earth through the growing of crops and by making pottery from clay. They build homes out of hills or even just holes in the ground, finding a security within earthen walls that are supported with wood or iron beams. The best known Craft among the Halflings is their ability to cook up a great meal for themselves or visitors. They often gain a position as world-famous chefs, when they bother to travel beyond familiar homelands. Another common craft are the diggers, those who build homes for others out of the soil. Its a traditional gift in halfling marriages for someone to provide the lion's share of work in constructing a home for the happy couple, though its considered good luck for both partners to contribute when able. To Halflings, one's Family is greater than simply those they share blood with. It also includes friends and visitors, which has led many Halflings to adopt great standards of hospitality. Having a messy home when visited is a great black mark against an individual, and so they keep their homes as tidy as possible in case unexpected individuals drop by.

                              Relation to the Paths

                              Acanthus (Main): Halflings draw closest to the Path of Arcadia, as its oaths and good fortune help them remain enthused about the future. Halfling diviners bless marriages, give advice to the young and old and maintain the public order through sworn oaths. At greater levels of the Arts Arcane, they can speed up the flow of time to grow plants faster or to heal wounds at a greater rate, and can sever away terrible destinies for their children so that they might have a better chance in life.

                              Mastigos: When one typically thinks of a "Soothsayer," they imagine an oracle or perhaps a shaman in the role. Halfling Soothsayers are quite literal in their title, they say soothing things to help ease tension in the community. With their ability to listen to the emotions of others and see the connections between individuals, a Soothsayer can turn a grudge between two families into peace or even marriage. A great Soothsayer often makes a greater homedigger as well, as they can lay enchantments that expand the size of a room or home beyond what it should be, saving on space and property for a couple.

                              Moros: Halflings who take up the Leaden Coin are among the greatest cooks Halflings have ever known, which is really saying something considering the general excellence among the race. They often break down a problem into it's primary components or "ingredients," changing things step by step by adding an element here, a lone word there and so on. Stygia's chosen also assist in funerary affairs, hoping to bring peace to the dead or, if a venerated ancestor, at least get them in an agreeable mood when possible. Halflings do not maintain cemeteries, instead burying their dead in their fields and having the Moros break down the bodies in the soil, transmuting flesh to minerals so that it nourishes the plants for generations to come.

                              Obrimos: The Aether's selected often help out in homes, providing fire and heat during the winter or a cool breeze in the summer. In harsh seasons, they manipulate the temperature and weather to make days always pleasant for people in the community. The Obrimos often have another role which is sadly necessary in a hospitable community such as this: Guards. There is always at least 1 Obrimos present as part of the town guard if available so that someone is on hand to detect whether a given visitor is being truthful in their invitation or whether they have worse intentions. If need be, the mages protect their communities with strikes of lightning and anti-magic wards around the towns perimeter to prevent spying from outside forces.

                              Thyrsus (Unfamiliar): It might sound strange that a community which often farms for its sustenance has little connection with the Primal Wilds. To become a Thyrsus often means leaving a community and venturing out into the great unknown for large spans of time, or a large degree of isolation among nature, something unheard of for a community-oriented culture such as this. Still, when a Croptender is present, they are the best for diagnosing health problems in people and identifying blood ties among the orphaned or abandoned children. When spiritual influence turns sour, it is they who bargain for renewed assistance, or who lay the hammer down on malefic entities.

                                      I think that’s an excellent example!
                                      I particularly appreciate that you spelled out several key, broad themes for Dwarves that both anchored core parts of their identity and could be expressed very broadly - and then the first subrace example immediately took them in an unexpected to emphasize their breadth.
                                      Put another way, I think those defining traits both really exemplify the dwarven archetype, and leave a lot of room to have complex societies. Same thing with Halflings - but writing them first and showing explicitly how they hit the defining dwarven traits was an excellent way to showcase that diversity.

                                      Giving space for every Path did a lot to expand on Halflings. From some of the lines you included, I can easily picture how “breaking the mold” personalities (grumpy, selfish, suspicious, adventurous, antisocial) would not only be expressed in a Halfling, but it feels like there’s room and roles in the community (and sometimes outside of it) that make that kind of diversity a natural fit - creating a well rounded culture.

                                      Spelling out how every Path gets expressed definitely helped. The format worked great, if you want to keep to it. I worry that it could get to be a little much (in both word count and creative effort). If you wanted to give yourself a little slack, the alternative I would propose is coming up with just one or two sentences per Path, and put them all in the same paragraph addressing various cultural trends and social roles. That said, if you want to aim for a “relation to the Paths” section for every subrace, I think it will pay out, and the presentation format works well.


                                      I’m very curious to see what you do with Orcs. They are a fantasy race I have never been able to understand, and have always struck me as rooted in extremely racist reductionism. And years of WoW have not helped much.
                                      Like, I’ve seen more nuance than just “savage warrior race guys” - usually as “noble warrior race guys”, “warrior poets”, “shamans”, or “but this monastery chooses peace” - but the only defining trait I can identify is “rage issues”, and I still find that deeply problematic.
                                      The only well-presented fantasy race I’ve been able to understand that hits those notes is Werewolf (forsaken).
                                      1. Thank you for the compliments. I kind of drew on Signs of Sorcery's examples of Arcana through the lens of the different paths as an idea for how I should break down the quintessential "Dwarf" traits, using the Halflings as a sort of lens on how those traits are viewed. If the main racial traits could be considered as close to a symbolic form as you can get, then the sub-races are how those traits are interpreted in the world.
                                      2. Same idea as above for the Halfling Paths. By taking the traits established for Halfling society and then looking at them through the lens of each Path, I was able to get some interpretations that surprised me with how they fit.
                                      3. As for the Orcs, well, my sort of outline for "Main Racial Traits" is as follows:
                                      • General "Character" Trait
                                      • Element/Material Association
                                      • General "Proficiency" Trait
                                      • Type of Group they focus on

                                      So for example, going from the bottom, I sort of view each race in terms of what "group" they define themselves by. For Dwarves, the family is everything, you help your family and stick by them when the going gets rough. For Elementals, I figured that they would define themselves by their elemental associations: It doesn't matter how far you travel, whether it be in the jungles or the frozen tundra, a water elemental that draws association from "rain" would group together with an ice elemental, a mist elemental, an ocean elemental and so on, they would still work with them. This of course leads to issues when dealing with Elementals of other elements and especially other races who aren't so tied to the elements as they are. An earth elemental might be fine with standing by a Dwarf, but feel lost on what to do with humans or elves, and especially out-of-place with other types of Elementals. Obviously that kind of overlaps with the second point, I'll figure something out.

                                      Humans define themselves by kingdom or "national" identity. For example, you can't really lump in a human from "Valenkia" let's say with another from "Markuu" (I'm just spitballing names here). There's too many differences between the two, from language to location to geography and so on that separates them from each other. Elves in turn are defined by Philosophy. You know how each Order as a motto along the lines of "Magic is ___"? They tend to take a similar approach with different subjects, viewing themselves through the lens of a philosophical rules and archetypes. This can in turn extend to religions, or remain purely secular depending on the group.

                                      Finally, for Orcs, I sort of view them as eschewing the concept of "Groups" altogether in favor of the individual. An Orc recognizes themselves as part of a family or kingdom if that group recognizes them in turn, but ultimately they define themselves by their relationship to individuals. An Orc is not part of a kingdom, he serves a king; he isn't part of a family, he's his mother's son. If that individual is no longer part of the group, he leaves that group to go along with them. If that individual is no longer worth the Orc's service, he'll find someone else more worthy of his work. This in turn leads to the Orc's Character Trait: Respect. Orcs depending on the sub-race can respect a lot of things, from base "strength" or "cunning" to "skills" or "wisdom." What matters is that someone is respected for they can do and achieve, and in turn respect those above and below them for their work. Without respect, a chain of command is nothing, a family is nothing, a nation is nothing. If a person has the proper respect for the Orc's efforts and in turn displays traits the Orc respects, then the Orc will work with him or even follow where he goes. Its... I hesitate to say "mercenary" of them, but it is a bit different from most people's viewpoints.

                                      I'm still working on the other two points for them, but that's what I have thus far for them.

                                              A few reasons: Giants feel sort of redundant when we already have Orcs, and I'm not sure how to really take them into multiple different directions from that point. Aasimar and Tieflings can theoretically be divided into subraces based on virtues/vices, but with Tieflings especially you'd have to somehow work that vice into a good thing, which can be touchy at times. Aasimar are also weird because you have a person who is supposed to be the child of objective good, and it feels weird to have absolute good and evil be something guaranteed in a setting, when these races should be able to be placed in many different settings with little issue. Dragons you could probably do if you have European Dragons, Eastern Dragons, Mesoamerican Dragons and African or Middle-Eastern Drakes as the different sub-races, but it still requires defining how precisely they group together (dragons are traditionally VERY individualistic, which would infringe on what's going on with the orcs), their primary characteristic (greed? Hoarding?) and so on.

                                              Technically speaking, I could reflavor the Elementals to instead be Genasi, which are DnD's humanoid children of elementals, one part flesh, one part element. But ultimately, there's one all-important reason why I chose the elementals: I like'em.

                                                  Dwarven Sub-Race: Mountain Folk

                                                  The Mountain Folk are a sub-race of Dwarves who specialize in turning geography into architecture. They toil on the surface, and inside, of mountains to construct internal fortresses and cathedrals that beggar the mind to envision. To the Mountain Folk, Perseverance means working on a piece, ironing out its flaws until the inner perfection is drawn without. A Mountain Dwarf will spend weeks examining and fixing every minute crack in a piece of stone or metal until it becomes a perfect representation of what it should be, a true Supernal example of the element of Earth. Mountain Folk thus have become one of the greatest sources of perfected metals and other materials in all the world. This attitude doesn't just apply to minerals of course, Mountain Dwarves are (in)famous for constructing some of the greatest works in any profession... at a rate ten times slower than anyone else. Thus, despite Mountain Craftmanship being known for its superb quality, especially for blacksmiths and architects, it's also rarely sought after except for projects one expects to never see in their lifetime. While all Dwarves place a great degree of importance on the Family, only Mountain Folk place an equal amount of focus on deceased relatives, more so than any other race. Ancestor Ghosts are always around, kept in this world through constructed Anchors and worship, and they reward this influx of Essence with the wisdom of someone who has seen several generations pass by.

                                                  Relation to the Paths

                                                  Moros (Main): The Moros are part craftsmen, part priest. They lead religious rites as often as they work materials into a more perfect state. It is the honor of these Dwarves to have access to the secrets of how to create Perfected metals the natural way, through passing the materials back and forth through the Underworld in a ritualistic manner. The belief is that the Underworld "kills" the vulnerabilities in the metal, slowly over time removing its imperfections until only perfection remains. Often times apprentices will be the ones acting as the ferries for these materials, a sort of combination of pilgrimage and exercise to help them with their physical fitness and to hopefully "perfect" themselves just as the metals they carry are perfected.

                                                  Acanthus: When dealing with large amounts of rock, it's best to have someone on hand who can figure out when things are going to go wrong or not. Acanthus Mountain Dwarves serve as "detection" systems, either divining the likely results of striking a certain rock in this stone wall or triggering internal alarms through Oaths Fulfilled to detect cave-ins even from great distances away. This process of safety ensure minimal deaths in a community, but it has a habit of slowing down an already snail's pace to a drag that would bore any lesser individual. The Mountain Folk would rather trust in a safe process than a fast process however.

                                                  Mastigos: If the Acanthus serve as canaries in the coal mine, then the Mastigos are the engineers and mathematicians. Using their command of Space and distance, they measure the length of tunnels and buildings and then use telepathy to communicate these distances to other workers. An ordinary individual can't see the sights a Mage can, but approximations can always be communicated mentally, which helps the project stick to its blueprints.

                                                  Obrimos: Generally speaking, the Mountain Folk prefer honest toil before magical solutions, but even they can admit that a pickaxe enchanted to strike with precise force can be a life-saver when it comes to more delicate work. Thus the Obrimos have become equipment suppliers, providing a standard array of temperature regulating amulets, bracelets that make a worker waste as little energy in their swing as possible, or provide emergency force shields in case of a cave-in. They also serve as part of the business side of society, examining coinage for false copies and making sure that both sides of a deal are honestly resolved.

                                                  Thyrsus (Unfamiliar): Living within a giant rock means that Mountain Folk typically regard anything to deal with forests and jungles as outside their area of expertise. There's even a bit of stigma against the Thyrsus by the Moros since the Shadow can also theoretically be used to perfect metals. As tradition states, metals exposed to the Shadow multiple times become imbued with a more "chaotic" energy, improving the good points of a piece while also making the weaknesses more extreme. But this idea has yet to actually be tested in a scientific manner. Regardless, the Thyrsus do have their uses in serving as spirit police, bringing rampaging entities to heel in order to avoid damage or new flaws in perfect works of art. They also serve as veterinarians, though this rarely comes up as the Mountain Folk have a distrust over living changing things as opposed to perfect Matter.

                                                          Dwarven Sub-Race: Gnomes

                                                          The Gnomes are the race of Dwarves who most often reside in the cities and towns of other races. They are a more cosmopolitan sort, providing wares for others and other services. They view Perseverance in a similar fashion to the Mountain Folk, but with one key difference: The Mountain Folk seek to make their creations perfect, but the Gnomes look to make the process perfect. A Gnome will repeat experiments with different materials or mechanical designs until they are as sure as mortally possible that they will produce the same excellent results by the end of it. Their connection with the Earth is perhaps the most destructive: Whereas Halflings renew it with good agricultural practices, the Mountain Folk use only what is needed and the Subterranean Dwarves employ it mostly for bartering, the Gnomes consume vast amounts of rock, metal, gems and especially coal in their experiments to produce the perfect procedure. Their focus in Craftsmanship is with mechanical systems such as clocks and windmills, making them some of the best repairmen you can find. For the Gnomes, Family is who you teach and pass knowledge down onto. This has led to issues where Gnomes Mages, disappointed that none of their children have Awakened, or Awakened to their same Path for the more stringent, will adopt someone into the family who is of the correct specification, even if they aren't a Gnome. This adoptee will often become the new family heir or at least receive the mystical side of the inheritance while all of the other children are granted mundane portions. This say that this practice has led to feuds down the line is an understatement, and there are more noble houses of Gnomes due to all of the branching out than most other races despite their smaller population.

                                                          Relation to the Paths

                                                          Obrimos (Main): Through the Obrimos, Gnomes have become the premier crafters of Imbued Items. Let the Mountain Folk seek perfection in their minute pieces, the Gnomes instead look to emulating the perfect state of the Aether with their methods. Their works are fantastical, Wands of Firetossing, Rings of Greater Leaping, even airships constructed by a select few Masters. Many a Noble from outside the race come to them for commissions, and it is the Obrimos Gnomes that demonstrate this most of all.

                                                          Acanthus: Sometimes the right tool for the job works wonders down the line, and the Acanthus know this lesson well. Their job is in the placement of materials and people where they are best needed. This can take many forms, sometimes the right man working at a specific job or time will lead to massive boosts in production down the line. A few coins placed in the right hands can speed up projects by an astounding degree, while a dagger given "for emergencies" can help a lady protect herself from hidden attackers... or enable her to stab her cruel husband in his sleep. The Acanthus navigate bureaucracy like fish swim through water, and they know just what you need to do your best, or your worst.

                                                          Mastigos: Those touched by Pandemonium are looked down upon in Gnomish society, for 1 simple reason: Their ability to steal the knowledge of others right from their heads. This potential violation of privacy and intellectual property enrages most senior mages, who have over the years passed down false stereotypes of Mastigos Mages to ensure that public opinion is turned against them. Thus to Gnomes, the Demon-Branded are lowest on the magic totem pole, even lower than the Thyrsus who they easily outnumber. Sadly, this environment of rumors have led some Mastigos to go down that very road that they are branded with. Others seek to do better, whether that be through advocacy or hidden charity work.

                                                          Moros: While the Obrimos work on their enchantments, the Moros among Gnomish Mages practice Alchemy to supplement their trade. They obsess over chemical formulas and reaction responses, looking to find just the right amount of sulfur or silver for their creations to work. They clutch onto their knowledge the hardest of the Paths, to the point of death and beyond. It is often only when a Moros Mage has died and left behind a ghost that they ever willingly "surrender" their knowledge to their heirs, let alone anyone else. This has caused problems where the Mage before dying did not prepare their ghost enough for it to retain all of the necessary knowledge, and thus great discoveries have had to be rediscovered from scratch or remain forever lost to the void.

                                                          Thyrsus (Unfamiliar): The Primal Gnomes are surprisingly enough the most sociable of the 5 Paths. They look to perfect methods of communication and bargaining, not for the purpose of obtaining the greatest personal gain, but rather so that both sides walk away satisfied with what they received. This has helped them immensely in negotiating contracts with spirits, and it's not uncommon to see an advanced Thyrsus walking around with multiple Familiars at their side. They also utilize their knowledge of the living form to analyze micro-expressions and tells, letting them figure out whether a given offer is good or not within a microsecond and pivot away if necessary.
