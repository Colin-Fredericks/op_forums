                                    Originally posted by orathaic View Post
                                    Just one question this raises:

                                    Pattern Restoration allows a Mage spend three mana to (among other things) remove a mental condition (without gaining a beat).

                                    So while sub-optimal (for XP gain) can't and rampant/megalomaniac mage just spend 3 mana (if the character realises they are suffering from a mental condition)?
                                    My reading is that this is an obviously non-permissible resolution for the same reason that Paradox Conditions cannot reasonably be concluded to be resolvable by scouring them before they've settled into your Pattern, as well as because the book never fully defines a Mental Condition and Mind is only one of three Arcana with a core published spell that messes with Obsessions as both Rampant and Megalomaniacal do.

                                    Awakened magic can't remove Scars from Deviants, directly mess with a Sin-Eater's Synergy, or give direct increases to a soul's Integrity or Wisdom, so I wouldn't allow a template feature that exists well below the level of spellcasting to get rid of a Condition as entwined with the game's systems for how your magic spreads and affects how much control you have over it. Pattern Restoration can help you purge a lingering Guilty consciense or being Swooned by a mind-twisting dream-creature, but the direct consequences of a failure of your magical self-control should not be able to be bought off for the price of healing a minor animal bite.

                                                    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                                    I think there is a necessary place to reconcile "self-absorbed power tripping and being less than sensible" with regaining control of faculties and emotions.
                                                    Yes, and getting an Obsession rewritten, gaining an extra Vice, and not being able to successfully use your normal Empathy are not what I would term a loss of faculties or control of one's emotions. These are Conditions that can be made Persistent and obviously are not constantly affecting the character's thought process outside of extremely broad and deep magical talents, which definitionally have more room for other Obsessions to operate.

                                                    I really don't see the use in trying to find other examples of things to establish some sort of baseline value to units,
                                                    That is not my intention, else I'd be going over the fact that Improved Pattern Restoration only affects the costs for healing damage.

                                                    Whatever it costs a vampire to heal the damage inflicted by one of their Banes, that's a thing that purely exists in its own narrative space of how they deal with physical injury and the necessities of recovering their power points, it doesn't establish some common baseline across all games.
                                                    I make the comparison because I consider the consideration of a Condition that is only acquired from failing the roll from an Act of Hubris to be closer in intended frequency to a vampire taking the kind of damage they need to spend an entire day healing (which they are incentivized to avoid) than it is to the Conditions that vampires get from succeeding or failing at a Detachment check (which fade without resolution after a number of nights and whose resolutions render their subject immune to the Condition for a month, when the game's diceless feeding mechanism prompts Detachment checks at a minimum threshold of right below a starting character's Humanity rating, one of the core clan Banes is built to make them gravitate towards desensitizing themselves to a comfortable Humanity rating Aloof from the living, and it has been pointed out that the frequent checks one is likely to make at high Humanity are fairly likely to succeed), not because three Mana is worth less than five Vitae.

                                                    Are Horror hungers another take on Aspirations or something they have in addition to Aspirations that gets included in their rules in Hunter Second Edition? Although you're saying the blue book, but I can't find any description there of them having anything other than Aspirations (even if the animalistic ones might merely Aspire to satisfy certain appetites). Either way, I'm not really seeing the case for that being anything other than a psychological phenomenon in its own right.
                                                    They're Aspirations, and the ones related to feeding specifically produce Willpower that the Horror can retain outside the confines of the scene in which it is earned.

                                                    Same with what I am inferring about the fact that some Deviants have multiple consciousnesses that come with separate Aspirations.
                                                    You infer incorrectly to the degree that there is a sidebar underlying the difference. The Symbiote form is "your powers have an agenda, represented as one or more Aspirations, which can benefit or penalize attempts to use your Variations in support of or opposition to them" as distinct from "you have an Alternate Persona with its own Aspiration."

                                                    And sure, Obsessions don't always represent conscious, deliberate goals and drives of a character, but they often do. Considering the elements described in those Conditions, I think the Obsessions they force of "dominate others" and "use magic indiscriminately" can be considered wilful rather than signalling the Storyteller to bring things into contact with them.
                                                    And I am purporting that this change is sufficiently adjacent to the deep subconscious to not be considered a phenomenon on the same level as "someone messed with my memories" or "I have been made to feel despair," particularly given that I do not believe you are going to also suggest that Mystery Commands can be shrugged off in this manner since it involves Exarchal influence (despite the Exarchs not being wholly external forces due to the same cosmological placement of the Supernal that the Mastigos and Acanthus writeups allude to in their Stereotypes sections and one of the overarching problems of trying to reform the Enraptured).

                                                    Are the impulses of Horrors and that subset of Deviants not the kind of thing that is subject to the Mind Arcanum? Are they off limits to Psychic Reprogramming?
                                                    Would you treat the Fate-based inclinations of the Principle and the God-Machine upon the world as psychological phenomena? Are you legitimately comparing Psychic Reprogramming to Pattern Restoration?

                                                    They're Conditions modeling the consequences of losing the bit of your soul that keeps a lid on your magic's ability to ride your unconscious impulses and fine social-mystical connections due to carelessness or malice, rewriting an element of your character sheet that serves as a source of Mana and affecting your ability to regain or retain Willpower. These are deeper changes than being befuddled by a cryptid or addicted to vampire blood, and they come from the character themselves — if they had these Conditions or similar ones as a Sleeper and underwent the Awakening, I don't think it would be reasonable for them to be subject to the "external supernatural effects are removed" effect, so I would definitely not let them be removed by spending less than half the character's maximum Mana on Pattern Restoration.
