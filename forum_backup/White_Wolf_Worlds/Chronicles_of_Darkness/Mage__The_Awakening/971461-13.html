                Originally posted by Justin Sane View Post
                Posting my own perspective here: Unlike other splats, Mages have choices. A Mage has a choice on whether he'll blindly follow his Obsessions into the rabbit hole. A Mage can decide never to overreach. In fact, with the exception of the Long-Term Nimbus (which has no mechanics attached to it besides "Your ST should make something up"), a Mage can decide to live as a Sleeper.
                And again, personally? I think the occasional "saint" does wonder to highlight the depths some Mages fall to, so I like the system as-is. It works, it does what it's supposed to. It's just neat.
                But then, I get to the table. Now, I know my players, and they're enjoying the story I'm presenting them -- it's definitely a Mage story, but it isn't a horror story. Why? Because Mage is all carrot and no stick, and my players are allergic to carrots. Seriously, after 20 sessions, not once was a Paradox voluntarily released, actions that could cause Wisdom loss have been avoided like the plague (even after reminding them it's a good source of Arcane Beats), and so on. If it wasn't for the external horror I bring to the story (Lovecraftian entities and time travel, mostly), there would be no horror whatsoever.
                So I understand why Gecko made this thread. I believe that, for some tables, the system needs more teeth.
                I dont see a problem with this. You said it yourself, your players are having fun, so why force things just to make mechanic that your players are clearly not interested in engagin relevant. The mechanics should serve the fun of the players no viceversa and sometimes the mechanics serve the fun of the players by getting out of the way.

                                Mage is about the evils you choose to commit, not the ones forced onto you. If your players choose to play boy scouts, they've pretty much decided on the type of character they want to play. If they wanted to play someone conflicted or morally compromised they wouldn't actively avoid all attempts to bring that to the forefront like the plague.

                                If you want them to face situations where they have to break moral codes and lose Wisdom, put them in situations where they have to make the hard choices. Lose-lose situations exist, as do situations where there's only a choice between two evils, and it's up to the player to decide which evil they consider the lesser of the two. Put them in a situation that forces them to go beyond safe boundaries or lose something they feel they absolutely must save above everything else, even if that something has to be their own lives.

                                        I absolutely disagree. I think the Wisdom system is 100% in-character and doing its job. In this case, the Wisdom system is making the players think again about what they're about to do and ask if it's really the right choice, even if for the wrong reasons.
                                        Then everything work fine, nothing to change and anyone disagreeing obviously doesn't GET the game. *eye roll*

                                        However if you dont agree with the wisdom system then its feel arbitrary.

                                        Example i think by the description of wisdom, that being the responsible use of magic power, then mundane killing should either not be a sin (because its not magic) or be in higher grade than say "change the timeline", "read/modify minds" or "create life". Killing is always wrong says the objective morality of wod, i think killing is wrong depending on the context.

                                        Because morality or in this case wisdom is relative to each of us but the game gives us an objective definition of it when you dont agree with it, as player no PC, it cause 2 kinds of problems. Either you fight it, say instead of

                                        The Wisdom system is making the players think again about what they're about to do and ask if it's really the right choice, even if for the wrong reasons.
                                        They think "how can i off this guy without getting screwed by the arbitrary morality system" or on the other extreme is your situation, players just dont do the things that are a nono and use the system just like the alternative morality paths of vampire the masquerade, the famous "path of what i was going to do anyway".
                                        All of that couple with the regression of wisdom to the carrot and the stick extremes. Having low wisdom, as far i could find, is ALWAYS bad so there is little temptation of power there. Even Werewolf got the good idea to add some advantages to having high end spectrum of morality (To those than dont play werewolf, forsaken morality is about reaching the perfect balance at 5 with 1 and 10 being the equivalent of wisdom 1 or integrity 1). So as player unless the DM force the situation, "commit a wisdom sin or you gonna lose" and gives you no way out them there is little player incentive to have low wisdom.

                                        Unfortunately im preparing a Forsaken campaign and dont have time to devote to this but an idea i had is using Resonance from Mage the ascension revised instead of Wisdom because to me it was the better morality system i seen in WoD.

                                        The basic idea was that depending on how you use your magic and what acts you committed you gain points in one of 3 resonances (Entropic, dynamic and static) and it would "color" your magic as you get more.

                                        Example, we had a verbena who kill all his enemies in messy brutal ways as the game continue he gained more entropic resonance (pain) which meant all his spell would cause searing pain, the player like that because it help his character to kill better until her try to heal someone with magic and realize that his healing also causer mind shattering pain.

                                        And thats the beauty of it, because is not an objectively "bad thing" but instead it was a show that action have consequence both good and bad. The game wasn't saying "what you were doing is BAD" but instead "you got even better at what you were already doing at the expense of other things......so what does it say about you (pc) that your magic is tainted with pain?" and thats what a morality system should do, not punishing you because of what it perceives at bad but showing you that choices opens some doors and close others.

                                        Honestly the system of resonance is interesting to explore in mage awakening as resonance was greatly expanded from the 3 categories of ascension and it fits better with the inverse paradigm of awakening as in ascension you start with a paradigm and supposedly, as you raise in power you discard it until you just blink and magic happens. While Awakening you start blinking and doing magic and as your raise in power you get more trappings of a paradigm (More yantras, a legacy, praxies) and getting a resonance that affects all your spells would fit nicely on that.

                                        My original idea was to make nimbus more mutable and instead of affecting the people near you, it would affect your magic. Thats my suggestion.
