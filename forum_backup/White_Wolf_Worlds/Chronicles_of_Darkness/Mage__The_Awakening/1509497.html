    Redeemed Tremere (What if the Tremere had remained in the Pentacle?)

    04-24-2023, 01:53 PM
    Hi everyone!

    Having read the most recent post about the Tremere in the Tome of the Pentacle, I was struck by the question "What if the Tremere had remained in the Pentacle?" Would there be a way to capture the general vibe of the Tremere, as outlined in the above post, but also removing their soul sucking providence? After a bit of drafting, I think I've managed to get a good Order style write up for a redeemed Tremere.

    Please let me know what you think! Do you dig it? Do they have a distinct enough identity from the rest of the Orders? Would you want to play as them? I think I'll wait until TotP is out before I try and write up an Order merit at least!

    Thanks for reading!


    The Tremere


    Magic Demands Conquest


    Don’t come here just because you liked me pissing off the Hierarch. Yes, he can be a stubborn old coot, but I knew him when he was the rising star of the Ladder. Can you believe he was accused of being both a Guardian plant and a secret Libertine? I keep at him so he has to remember those days, remember what it was like to shake things up.


    That’s what’s important, memory. We all once were Sleepers, ignorant to the Lie around us and unable to do anything. After Awakening too many forget that fact, in the Diamond and out. The Tremere don’t forget, even if the memories are painful. Instead, fight for the dreams you had when you were Asleep, fight for the dreams you had when you first Awoke and when you’ve reached further than you could have imagined, go beyond that.


    The Hierarch hasn’t forgotten how to sulk like a teenager, so we’re not going to wait. That verge needs mapping and whatever’s been eating ghosts in there needs its ass kicked. Want to make some new memories?


    The path to enlightenment is a blazing trail which others can follow. The Watchtowers are merely the first step on the path to Ascension, of unleashing the infinite power of the Awakened soul to conquer Truth and dethrone the Exarchs. The Fallen World is in shackles, but these chains reflect the Tyrants’ fetters on the Supernal, a hierarchy of control by autocratic sub-souls and mystic satraps who believe themselves unassailable, the populace appeased with meaningless victories and easy pleasures within their designated realms. The Tremere defy this, assailing Mysteries and cracking open the walls of heaven, so that Truth will pour out unimpeded like blood from a wound. Like Prometheus, the Tremere fight the gods to steal Divine Fire so all the world can feel its warmth. They are dedicated iconoclasts, reformers and devil’s advocates, often the loyal opposition within the Diamond rather than its leaders. The Tremere know the dangers of unchallenged authority better than any Order and will fight to keep the Awakened nation vital and strong.


    Mages join the Tremere when they want to seek challenge and danger, share Truth with others, challenge the structures they support, atone through acts of valour, or hunt monsters and Reapers who threaten the souls of Humanity.


    Core Beliefs: Shaking the Boundaries

    The origin of modern Tremere philosophy is derived from a sacred text, the Pure Suspire written by the Seven Martyrs, the founders of the renewed order who gave their lives to purge it of sin. It is derived from the original secretive text of the first Tremere order, who’s opening treaties on struggle and enlightenment hid the literal soul devouring practised by its leadership. Not only is the new text purged of the prideful nihilism of its predecessor, it is freely available to any Mage who wishes to read it so that the virtue of the new Order can be seen by all. From the Pure Suspire and nine hundred years of internal debate, several tenets have coalesced by general consensus.


    Break the Gods

    The Awakened soul possesses limitless potential, but it is hemmed in on all sides by the hierarchy of the Exarchs. Supernal gaolers leer down at the Fallen World like gargoyles, wielding power they have not earned. Agents of the Tyrants seek to conceal and destroy what Mysteries they cannot hoard for themselves. Those with the strength to do so must bind and defeat these foes, smashing apart these pantheons of oppression and claim the secrets they guard.


    Scatter Their Thrones

    It is not enough to claim the Mysteries; strength must be mediated by righteousness and benevolence. Supernal Truths should be distributed to all those who need them, so they can never again be used as leverage against the ignorant. This does not advocate for Sleepers to have unfettered access, the Tremere respect the threat of Paradox, but the proliferation of any revealed knowledge is seen as a virtue. The majority of Temere preach the belief that every successful Awakening makes subsequent ones more likely.


    Protect All Souls

    Within all souls, from the deepest Sleeper to the grandest Archmaster, is power to change the world, if it can be unleashed. To remove, damage or worst of all destroy a soul is to serve the Sixth Watchtower, the tower of monsters and the Abyssal reflection of Awakening. Any who do so must be hunted down and destroyed. This does not preclude research into the soul for the purposes of defence, healing or strengthening, but this must never be at the detriment of another’s. Some Tremere see the undead, shapechangers or other once-human creatures as being victims of soul afflictions and seek to cure them, whether they’re asked to or not, though hardliners see more worth in cauterising the spread of these curses.


    Seek the Seventh Watchtower

    The five paths are like pure white light refracted from a prism, each one aspect of Truth, with the Watchtowers the first revelation a Mage achieves. The Seventh Watchtower, in Tremere philosophy, is the pure expression of Truth, the Platonic form which each Path derives from, offering Ascension to those who conquer it. The life of a Tremere should be the following of this Seventh Path, fighting towards greater truths as they fought to Awaken from the Lie.


    Origins

    The Tremere order has a long shadow over its history. Regarded first as monster hunters and soul researchers by the others of the Diamond, in the 12th century the Order was revealed to be headed by foul soul stealers, who had devoured other Nameless Orders and Legacies in their pursuit of dominion over the soul and magic. Luckily, the influx of new recruits, most willful and iconoclast, granted by admission into the Diamond led to a cracking of their secrecy. Information and lore began to leak quicker than could be contained, the final blow being the dissemination of an unredacted copy of The Suspire to the other Orders.

    After the purge of the House masters, the question remained of what to do with remaining members who were judged to not have taken part in the leaders’ excesses. Ultimately a cabal of seven Tremere mages, the very same who had leaked The Suspire to the rest of the Diamond, volunteered to be executed as symbols of the Order’s past, to allow its rebirth. These were the Seven Martyrs and the Pure Suspire they wrote formed the basis of the redeemed order.

    In modern times the Order is treated by some with suspicion, though this is mediated somewhat by the Tremere’s openness about their dark origins and their continued reputation as monster hunters. In fact, their transparency (at least compared to the rest of the Diamond) has lent them an expectation for more “Left-Handedness” with the other Diamond Orders. While this is by no means a licence to commit crimes against Wisdom, often a Consilia will be more lenient with a Tremere member’s choice of Mysteries than they would others, if only with the expectation that they will confirm why such topics are better left alone. The Order is legendary on how brutally they will come down on members discovered to be falling into the same corruption as their old masters.


    Mysteries

    The Tremere seek Mysteries that actively resist comprehension, are protected by powerful guardians or involve Supernal entities. Second only to the Mysterium, Strugglers are avid explorers of the ruins of the Time Before, Supernal Verges and stranger realms still, though they’re less concerned with preservation than defeating their guardians and taking their treasures home. Tremere also often aggressively targets Seers and the Left-handed, driving them away from Mysteries and stealing their Artefacts for their own study. The more well defended it is, the more it must be worth.


    Magical Symbolism: Hunger

    The Tremere are never satisfied, their passion never waning, always seeking worthy challenges. They believe that Truth resides inside the precious soul and all answers can be found within. The Order’s magical tools often incorporate hair, nails, spit or even blood and bone. These grossly physical implements ground the spiritual in the passion of the mortal. Maps, blueprints and records also feature, to better plan for the next challenge, or bugles, banners and even loud declarations to clearly state the intention of the Mage. When it comes to weapons, they range between obvious enough to display utter confidence, or concealed to surprise an unwary foe.


    In the Diamond’s draconic symbolism, the Tremere embodies the Sanguis Draconis, the Blood of the Dragon: the motive force and ambition that drives the Awakened city and its people forward, purging stagnation and cleansing corruption. Blood must flow, or the beast will die.


    Hubris

    Tremere philosophy is predisposed to biting off more than it can chew and seems built to encourage Hubris in its adherents. The Order encourages seeking challenges, but bystanders can be caught up in the consequences. Raiding a Seer’s Sanctum sparks reprisals, defeating a powerful spirit creates a vacuum in the Shadow and harrassing vampires or werewolves is a surefire way to get someone killed. Tremere fall when they forget the reason they are struggling is so that those following will not have to do so. But a saviour or martyr complex can just as easily lead a Tremere to ruin. Some Mysteries are too corrosive or demand utter immorality for the Wise to understand them safely, but Tremere can convince themselves that if they could master it, they could reverse the ill effects and return, triumphant, only to become another victim or a vector for the pain it spreads.

    Concepts

    Disciple of Jacob: I know the creature I’m following isn’t human, despite the form it wears. When it thought it was alone, or only viewed by the faithful, it revealed its truth, the secret body of wires, circuits and stained glass. It can perform what the cult calls miracles, but only in service of the occult geometry it’s trying to twist the new highway into. I can think of better uses for them. I’ll have to rely on my magic in the end, but the classes in Judo, Sambo, Sumo and MMA are the foundations of my victory.

    Unioniser: I must have worked at six to eight different warehouses in the last year alone. I’m in good with lady luck and always have experience needed somewhere else. Management’s getting antsy, but with a quick tug of probability they’re never around when my co-workers are reading the leaflets I’ve given out. The spirits of conformity the Seer flunky sent turn tail and run when they see me. With one strike, we’ll topple the pylon. Next, the whole damn pyramid.


    Stereotypes

    Adamantium Arrow: Service is mastery is just a fancy way of denying your own autonomy. A righteous battle is still righteous, even if nobody asked you to fight it.

    Guardians: We have to give them credit for taking the fight to the Thrones and our predecessors. But we admit to our mistakes.

    Mysterium: They claim to oppose Pancryptia, yet keep the lore to themselves.

    Silver Ladder: We’re here to keep you honest.

    Free Council: The enemy isn’t hiding in plain sight, little cousins.

    Seers of the Throne: Your thrones are first.

                                  Originally posted by Michael View Post
                                  It's well put together, with some clever ideas, such as the Tremere being super-open to combat their reputation. However, it honestly makes me think the remaining Tremere would, if not simply dissolved into, have become a faction of, the Silver Ladder. Hell, maybe even a legacy within the Ladder. The similarities are just a bit too strong in my opinion.
                                  By contrast, I've been sitting here wondering why there's a need for revision at all from the medieval forward-face order of the Tremere-Cut out the Suspire and the rest is pretty operable.

                                  While the Tremere and the Silver Ladder agree on the worth of ambition, the difference is to what end they work it towards.

                                  The Silver Ladder are primarily interested in perfecting society-put the true king on the true king's throne, and the true tailor in their clothing store and the true farmer in their field, and the world will submit itself to humanity, and tyrants will have no purchase upon the diamond kingdom. The Silver Ladder believe that if a wizard is to be a wizard, in an ideal world there would be no obstacle to that, that Awakening would just be given to the deserving. Their paternal interests lie in then constructing their diamond-they're the ones interested in cooperation, coordination, and assigning everyone to where they will do best.

                                  The Tremere, by contrast (based on what we have for the medieval form so far), have no interest in sociological concerns. THey're concerned with the individuals and with the world, and that buffer between that we call society will just sort itself out-it doesn't matter if a "true king" is on the throne, the world will submit itself when you tame the land, slay or corral the beasts, and otherwise make it submit. If a wizard is to be a wizard, they'll take the light of the Watchtowers as a rope and bring it to heel. If a person is truly to be a king, then they must be willing to claim the kingdom and make it an extension of their will. And mind you, that's assuming kings are desirable at all-their missives bring them against gods and monsters, but rulers do just as much to wield power they don't deserve and keep it from others. This is why the Tremere markedly aren't systematically Seers.

                                  The order on the whole leans more libertarian*, even objectivist (as much as those terms can apply to a medieval organization), being more concerned with making wholeness in the self and destroying nexuses of stagnancy for the flow of power. Their individual ambition is to seize what is theirs, and if nothing can be seized from them, then that's where they're supposed to be for now. If they make a perfect society in their practice, it's because they have made a consumate whole soul, starting with themselves and then making whole the soul of humanity, and then the world. They have more in common with the Adamantine Arrow and Guardians of the Veil than the Silver Ladder, but prioritize the world against enemies as their targets, and believe the way to fight the Abyss is not treat magic as frail, but as something to keep a firm grasp on.

                                  THe SIlver Ladder believes Magic is Humanity's Birthright, and things will be right when everyone take's their accordant place on the Silver Ladder. The Tremere believe Magic must be Tamed, and things will be right when all of magic no longer bucks against those who would grasp it.

                                  *as it's usually meant, not how Americans use the word.

                                                          It could be neat to have redeemed Tremere maintain their tradition of subverting hostile Legacies.
