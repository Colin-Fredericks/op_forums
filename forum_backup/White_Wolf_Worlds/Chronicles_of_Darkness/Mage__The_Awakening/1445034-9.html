Being able to automatically exceptionally succeed at any type of ritual magic is beyond the scope of a Legacy Attainment. The main point of a Legacy is that you belong to or have developed a magical school of thought that gives you additional magical insights and lets you tweak the work in less disastrous ways than dragging raw magic into the world across the Abyss. Attainments are there, magically, for ease of use and reduction of complications compared to the rest of the Awakened toolbox, not to remove any need to deal with modifiers for a rolled process.

                                    My "toolbox argument" is that the tools Legacies provide in the larger toolbox of "powers available to mages" have the advantage of not being subject to the core problems spells have, chiefly the risk and unreliability presented by Paradox/Dissonance/Quiescence and the need to roll.

                                    Legacies are not a toolbox in this sense. Legacy Attainments are reliable tools that always work the same way instead of having to drag Truth across the chasm of impossibility with mnemonic aids to minimize the risk of the products of your enlightened imagination picking up weird glitches at best and catastrophic errors at worst. Legacies are you picking an area of focus for reliable powers of supernatural origin and accompanying magical theory. You get no benefit from refusing to specialize, because the benefits of specializing in anything else do not manifest without actually committing to the specialization.

                                    Yes, the right sort of Legacy Attainments can trivialize certain types of problem if you define "trivializing a problem" in the vein of "being able to suspend your vital functions allows you to remain in a place with no breathable air." This is not on the same level of problem as "the full scope of ritual magic available to mortals — Phenomenal, externally-sourced power — can be rendered consistent and reliable by an internal power this Legacy can cultivate in a character before they're even far enough into the Legacy to initiate new members."

                                    A certain stripe of mage will turn up their nose at a Legacy for having Attainments that don't impress them. Other sorts of mage will be more interested in the Legacy for its oblations, or its Yantras, or its Ruling Arcanum, or its magical theory, or the networking opportunities it presents, or because someone they care about is involved with it, and so on. The fact that Legacy Attainments are not significantly stronger than spells built on the same principles prevents the first sort of mage from twisting the game into an absurd shape by making them objectively right instead of simply focused on a particular type of optimization.
