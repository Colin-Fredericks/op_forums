    Originally posted by Mrmdubois View Post

    How do you figure?
    As I said, this is how I feel about how the "mechanics and narrative" works together. Caladriu's example really helped seeing it.

    When I look at the rules/narrative in the book:
    * There is the writing and there is Scribe Grimoire.
    * There is no real interaction or meaning between the writing and Scribe Grimoire. It all comes down to the spell in the end.
    * How badly or well written the text is has no effect.
    * What language it is written in means nothing.
    * The Rote is solely created by Scribe Grimoire

    This doesn't fit the narrative that I want for mage. And removing the spell makes it flow much better.

    Originally posted by Caladriu View Post

    And I also think it could be something that could happen allot in a game. Depending on how often the ST hands out content with Grimoires offcourse. It shouldn't however be a normal thing :P

    I could set it up where she has to write the text, make the research, etc.. But it still all comes down to the spell in the end.

            Originally posted by Caladriu View Post
            There's a sourcebook from 1E, Grimoire of Grimoires, which includes a lot of examples of what they look like. Most of them are along the lines of a full book discussing the author's ideas and Obsessions and approach to magic, with a few rotes encoded throughout the text. It's often primarily a memoir or manifesto or treatise, just like any other book a person might write. Even once it's encoded, you can't just pass the rote directly into someone's mind; the reader needs to read and understand the spell and how to cast it. You're teaching the reader how to do something.
            I should also point out that some of the entries in Grimoire of Grimoires aren't exactly what people might usually think of when envisioning a book of spells. There's an incomplete set of scrolls encoded with Arrow martial spells, of course, a "cursed" book of shapeshifting magic written by a Thyrsus studying werewolves, etc. The really odd and innovative stuff comes from the Free Council, naturally: the original manuscripts for a popular series of fantasy novels, the Electric Grimoire "written" on a spirit of electricity contained in various formats over the years, a vinyl record produced by a Satanic cabal who are also a death metal band (you have to play it backwards to hear/learn the spells).

            Although the strangest one in the book seems to have been made unintentionally, on a recording of an encounter with an Abyssal entity.
