                I'd let that work. Using symbolic logic, a heart is something that beats. It beats at different rates depending on the situation, but if it functions as it's supposed to it is going to beat. The rate, however, is something you could almost certainly fuck around with. It is still beating, still fulfilling its symbolic purpose, but it happens to be doing so in such a way that screws up everything around it.

                The logic for water flowing uphill and the like is that symbolically, water flows. That's what it does. It flows until it can't flow anymore then it pools. Now normally it only really has permission to flow downwards, led around by gravity and the like, but using magic you can grant it permission to flow elsewhere. It still has to be able to flow, or pool if it is unable to do that, but the limitation of where it is allowed to do so can be made less restrictive.

                                    It seems to be a general problem with mage where they want some spells to be certain level, but that doesn't fit into the system of practices they made. Example: Animal Minion (Life 4). Practice of Patterning. You take over a body and puppeteer it. It definitely seems like Ruling effect to me, since Patterning is about transformations.
                                    Patterning spells allow a mage to completely transform a target into something else that falls under the Arcanum’s purview. A memory can be replaced wholesale (Mind), the mage can turn herself (or a target) into an animal (Life), or she can teleport by “rewriting” her own location (Space). A spell that transforms the target into something that falls within the Purview of another Arcanum, like transforming into a living pillar of fire (Life and Forces), requires a mage to know the Practice of Patterning for the destination Arcanum.
