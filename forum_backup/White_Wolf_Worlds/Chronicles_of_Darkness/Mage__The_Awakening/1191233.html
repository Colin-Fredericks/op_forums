    Holy Crap is it different now!!

    03-03-2018, 12:29 AM
    Hello! Some people may remember me from back in the day. Others not so much. So again, Hello! I'm Deimos Masque and: Finally! The Masque has come back to Mage the Awakening.

    And holy crap is it different.

    I did a similar thread back in the Blood and Smoke era of Vampire 2nd edition. I've got the Mage the Awakening 2nd Ed all printed out and ready to go. I've read through a decent portion of it, and have been lurking in here on and off for the last month or so. But a question is in the back of my mind constantly, even as I re-read the new core book.

    As a Mage 1st person, what changes should I be noticing besides the obvious stuff like Wisdom, the Beats, the better histories of the Orders, etc. What nuance is there that I'm going to miss until I pick up the dice and roll them?

    Thanks for you help in advance!

      Thanks for you help in advance!
      • Arcana dot ratings are now locked to the Practices. No more 5 dot Weaving spells.
      • Vulgar and Covert are gone and each spell can either be cast safely or risk paradox based on the difference between your level of knowledge and and the kinds of complexity you introduce into the spell.
      • A lot of things that used to be spells, such as Mage Armor and Counterspelling, are now "attainments", abilities with their own rules that you unlock by advancing in the arcana.
      • There's a dedicated subsystem for designing Mysteries for players to learn and uncover. Made much better by the suggestions in the Signs of Sorcery preview.
      • You can now cast indefinite spells on living patterns.
      • Also, there are 3 historical settings in the dark eras books. Sundered World has Neolithic Mages and features a still living Father Wolf, To the Strongest has the new history of the Orders, and Princes of the Conquered Land has the Mutapa Empire and crossover rules with Mummy. Those are the only supplements that exist for Mage 2E.

              In addition to serving as prerequisites for Order-specific Merits, Status in Awakened circles is now distinctly and specifically useful for getting ahold of magical resources without doing a lot of legwork.

              Related to the above, what a Consilium is has been refocused to "local judicial court" and the Orders' sub-global granularity has express backing in the form of regional Caucuses in which a character's Order Status is more or less effective.

              The Orders now include Sleepers and Sleepwalkers among their membership up-front (albeit at some remove from the activities of Awakened members) in a carryover from things like the Silver Ladder's Cryptopoly; there is a mage-specific variant on Mystery Cult Initiation whose effect is essentially "take the same benefits as having this many dots in MCI, but don't worry about the demands of cult membership."

                  I think a big difference before you pick up and play is the interplay of Reach and Paradox. Paradox is no longer about this spell being too obvious, or too many people seeing you, but how hard you push your magic compared to your skills. There's a lot of neat mechanical impacts of this, but as a game element mages being pushed to Reach is important. While some players will avoid over Reaching as much as possible (there is incentives in the Beat XP method to counter this though), the game really shines when mages are tempted to toss a few extra Reaches into the situation. Gone are the days of, "mages are dangerous if they're prepared, but squishy if they're not." In many ways, catching mages unprepared now is when they're at their most dangerous, because if their life is on the line, why not toss in three more Reaches than you can safely use for a super potent spell?

                  And it doesn't have to be combat. Any situation where a mage feels cornered or highly challenged makes it a tempting choice to grab some more Reach. It's not about being clever with hiding your spells (not that casting in front of normal folks isn't without consequences), or just being at the whims of the dice on Paradox rolls. Magic, and hubris, and consequences feel a lot more personal because they're mostly about choices. Which means its much easier for the ST to bring the negative sides of magic into the game because it's easier to build them organically off of player choices.

                      Spellcasting has been revised to obey the general Storytelling System roll structure, where rolls that measure numbers of successes are rare. When you cast a spell, you envision modifiers or scaling factors before you assemble your dice pool, and apply them as dice modifiers. The actual dice roll only cares whether the result is a dramatic failure, a failure, a success, or an exceptional success. That means things like necessary Potency need to be considered rather than sorted out after you roll, even for direct damage spells: you decide before you cast Celestial Fire how much damage you intend to inflict, and that will determine how easy or difficult it is to cast the spell.

                      Mage Sight and the treatment of the Supernal World are a particular element to focus on, but aren't really a "won't know until you roll the dice" topic. Still, worth mentioning.
