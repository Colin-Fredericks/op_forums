            I'm currently running a game, and the main way I'm maintaining horror for it is twofold:

            First, learning that something is there that nobody else can detect. That blood-soaked carpet upstairs was made as a result of actions from a thing that is still there, not some mass murderer. Not something nearly so trite. You have some idea of what it could be, but then again, there's a dozen things that fit that bill. But you're the only one in the house now.

            Secondly, knowledge does not guarantee respite. You find the thing that caused the mass murder is still there, watching you with insectoid eyes. And once you see it it begins talking to you. You cast your spells and learn just how threatening it is. How absolutely certain it is that if the monster is left unchecked it will tear a hole in reality and usher in something worse. You see just how many dice it will roll, how badly it'll hurt you. So you try to find another way to deal with it. And you do. You learn human sacrifice is the only way it'll go for good. Or so you think. Maybe there's another way? Maybe you overlooked something?

            And now you have a moral dilemma. And you let the PCs feel it.

                    Monsters are mysterious unknowns, but Mages have the ability and resources to learn about the unknown.
