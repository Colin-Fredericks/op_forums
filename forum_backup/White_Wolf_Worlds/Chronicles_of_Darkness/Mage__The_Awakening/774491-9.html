        Originally posted by Juhn View Post
        Sure, but Quiescence having graduated effects wouldn't actually contradict the general rules there - the book itself might get more specific.

        And even if it doesn't, I imagine it wouldn't break the game to houserule it.

        Also, having just looked it up again, it appears that the "Sleepers forget magic" was part of "Disbelief" in 1e, which explains why it's actually called Disbelief - it's less about the unravelling spells and more the part where Sleepers disbelieve what they see and substitute it either with nothing (if their willpower score is low), or remember it as a dream or substitute a rational explanation (if their willpower score is middling). Based on 1e core, the Quiescence is just "the general tendency for people not to Awaken to the truth", which I believe has been folded into the definition of The Lie in 2e.

        I like 2e's terminology better, if only for separating the terms for "Sleepers forget magic" and "Sleepers unravel spell effects".
        They changed it to clarify it actually has nothing to do with belief or disbelief of magic or a person's strength of mind (willpower). It is a curse and there is no way around it beyond either being some level of supernatural or being a sleepwalker.

        To me that makes far more sense then "I disbelieve!!!!" "Or if I try really hard because I have a really strong mind I won't forget". Because it just is, Now it makes total sense that there can be a globally networked society completely hidden from everybody without the CWoD solution of having everything run by some supernatural group while everybody else is painted as being willfully ignorant or that mundane law enforcement wouldn't have clue one about what is actually going on at some level. Especially with things like cell phones, computers, tablets, intranet, apps for social media that keep us all networked up as a matter of daily life.

        Dissonance is completely seperate and is the term for "unraveling" spells.

                BTW, for Hunters vs Mages thing - I think Hurt Locker will make you people happy. It seems that it will contain a lot of power house, mortals-only merits. Punch Drunk, for example; another one, relevant to this, is a merit that allows you to ignore tilts for a number of rounds. It scales with your stats, and starts with a minimum of 3 tilts for 3 rounds, I think. Why is this relevant?
                Because most of mage abilities to disable you in one move are translated to "inflict really nasty tilt". Want to fight mages? Get that merit. Blindness? Ignore it! Shadowy tentacles trying to immobilize you? Punch them and go for the mages throat! Sure, it's only 3 rounds and only 3 tilts, but mage is going to inflict one tilt per round at best; and if you can't kill them in 3 rounds, then you are fucked either way.
                Another thing to remember is that mages need to use yantras in their spells. Yantras take time. Spells with only one Yantra are not that strong, especially if they are contested or resisted; another way of beating a mage is superior numbers, because adding people to the mix means that mage can't put as much power into potency, and needs to spread the dice around. And if mage is arrogant enough to try casting spells longer than instant casts, then well, smash them.
