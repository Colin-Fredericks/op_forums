                                                        Many mages don't have any kind of alternate Sleeper identity - they just take the lumps of people finding out their Sleeper name, and possibly from that their sympathetic name. That sympathetic name is not always the name on your driving licence - it's what the people with sympathetic links to a baby think of them as while their sense of self develops, so looking through someone's mortgage paperwork is not a surefire way to get it.

                                                        In mage society, referring to someone by a Sleeper identity except in intimate relationships is downright rude, as rude as deadnaming someone in our world. Some Orders outright punish people who do it - and not always the ones you expect. The Arrow think it's rude and unprofessional, for example, but it's grounds for murder among Seers of the Throne, who are if anything more serious about their Shadow Names than Pentacle mages are.

                                                        So, the game assumes that most mages maintain a healthy separation of their magical and mundane lives. Not having a Shadow name, or performing too much magic in your mundane persona, can screw you up. Being really invested in your Shadow persona, to the point of buying the Merit, gives Occultation-like benefits to resisting sympathetic spells when you're not "in character".

                                                        Some mages, though, require mortal identities that aren't their own in the course of their studies. They might have things in their pre-Awakened lives they're trying to put behind them, a need to move in radically different social circles among Sleepers, that kind of thing. For those characters, that's what the Alternate Identity Merit is for. It's a multiple-level Merit depending on how elaborate the background is. A fake ID doesn't even need the Merit, but if you're running for political office or facing federal investigation, the higher dots of it will protect your cover.

                                                        Five example characters, and how they treat their lives:

                                                        Herophilus never really had a choice: he was a public servant (he's a coroner) and MD before he Awakened, he's kept his old job, his Hallow's in his morgue and everyone knows it. Despite this, other mages (even Seers!) stick to calling him by his Shadow Name (or, in mixed company, "Phil") rather than his real name, despite his real name being the one on his door. To do otherwise would simply be rude. Still, assuming someone wanted to do his a mischief, he's relatively vulnerable - he doesn't have the Shadow Name Merit, as he himself calls himself "Phil" and doesn't use the symbolism of his Shadow Name in spellcasting. He just thought it was historically appropriate when he picked it, which is fine by the Free Council's standards.

                                                        Tallulah is in a position where she's losing track of her various identities and who knows her as what. She was on the run and then a sex worker before she Awakened, so was already under an assumed name. The Silver Ladder fixed her up with documentation so she could get a job in a casino (the Alternate Identity Merit, at a mid level) but she's now moving up in the world thanks to increasing Status and membership of the Sisterhood of the Blessed, to the point where the Order is having to deepen her background to fend off private investigators, represented by buying the Merit up. The rest of her cabal know the name on her mortgage, but also know the real name beneath that. Complications include the Seers knowing an earlier alias but not her current one, and the fact that she has family - it's not just her the Ladder are maintaining Alternate Identities for, it's her (Sleepwalker) sister and infant son as well.

                                                        Cowl, as a Guardian, takes operational security extremely seriously. He insists on being called by his Shadow Name on business, uses different cellphones for "work" and "Work", and takes precautions to not allow his magical work to follow him home. This is seemingly contradicted by the fact that he uses his Sleeper identity and career to run his Labyrinth, but it's a blind - the name on his business cards is not his real name. He actually has the maximum level of Alternate Identity, and not even the rest of the cabal know who he really is. That's how the Guardians roll.

                                                        Ibn Awoke while serving in Iraq. To the Sleeper military, he left as an invalid. He doesn't have any kind of fake papers, but as he takes on undocumented work anyway, it doesn't really matter - he's taken the broad strokes of his background and uses it without the specifics like his name, lying with the truth and vanishing into the statistics. What's an enemy mage trying to find him going to do? sympathetically scry on every single homeless and minimum-wage veteran in Vegas?

                                                        Tanashiriki is in a position many mages find themselves in - her Awakening Mystery Play was so traumatic it basically burned her Sleeper life down around it. She was picked up, homeless and ranting, by the Mysterium several hundred miles away from the city where she'd fled her job and immigration status while in the grip of Pandemonium. Her Order have set her up with the basics - a driving licence, a bank account for expenses - but she lives in a Sanctum and basically doesn't have a Sleeping identity any more. Even if someone connected the Vegas Mystagogue to the three-years-missing low-level Interpreter in New York she used to be, it wouldn't exactly help. To get her sympathetic name, you'd have to track down the correct Kenyan village. And good luck with that.
