                    Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post

                    The main one is that Promethean disfigurements are not inmediately visible, no one would see the Galateid as a stone woman first hand, also Galateids are not stone-like they are called Galateids due to the beauty more than the origin, also the Sand effect? Nothing at all to what the disquiet or torment would do. Maybe a specific wasteland would do something like that, but that is streching the rules.
                    I actually do have an answer for some of that! The Infrastructure in the facility does a few things (apart from preventing those caged from escaping), and one of them is revealing those trapped in their true forms (see also Tinker the Changeling in Containment upstairs as well). Mr. Pocket gets a free pass because his Alistair form is not an illusion per se - it's a literal piece of someone's soul he's stitched together. But mostly it's cos there's so many cryptids here that disguising their true forms was far too intricate for what I had in mind - they needed to be instantly recognisable as cryptids, because there was a good chance the players would only speak to them briefly at best, which makes it hard to weave in all the layers of lore each splat has.

                    The Infrastructure has also contained all of Clague's Wasteland for about a decade now, the cumulative result of which is now about to be unleashed. The result is certainly a lot more violent and explosive than is normal, but the situation is quite unusual...

                    ...her being made out of stone is definitely a mistake. I must have conflated the original Galateid/Pygmalion myth with the book. Oops!

                    Then again, the idea of someone loving a statue so hard they use unholy Alchemy to bring them to life as a Promethean is pretty cool!

                                            Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
                                            Phoog
                                            Great as usual.

                                            Couple of questions:

                                            1. Are your players not questioning the fact that 2 Chron exist is paradoxical (in more than one sense) and skirts on Archmastery?
                                            2. If Chron the Elder is working with Seers, why dont they make the connection with the cabal? They are at this point a very obvious target.
                                            3. Love the bowler hat, most likely related to certain Clock.
                                            4. Can you expand on the triggers for all the paths? I meant this part " Curious, it is usually the death of the self (or another) that kicks off a Moros Awakening. And it is the death of personality, of identity, that often kicks off a Mastigos Awakening."
                                            1. The players don't really know much about Archmages or high level time travel in general. There's no indication that Chron Legacy is an archmage however, so the implication is that he found another way to send himself back bodily. They also know it's a core part of Chron's story (he came up with the idea, not me!), so they haven't questioned it too much. In-game, all the other Acanthus Pentacle mages are dead, so they don't really know much about time travel in general. They're not really sure what Chron Legacy is, beyond some kind of alternate, older timeline version of Chron.

                                            2. The cabal is indeed a very, very obvious target. Ever since the Athanaeum attack, they've had a target painted on their back, with Praetorian and Hegemon especially keen on hunting the cabal. Mammon are mostly keeping out of it, partly because Chron managed to Hex their leader with a Paranoia Condition, so he's more worried about other Seers than the Pentacle at the moment. There's a few reasons the other Ministries haven't been hit by Seer squads yet. For one, Panopticon is actively trying to protect them, and have made a deal to keep the other Ministries away from the Sickle. They also have the most info on the cabal, which they arent sharing, because Singsong finds Shackle to be quite useful to him. It also helps that the players have only really made trouble with minor Ministries recently (Horologion with Phemonoe and Sycorian with Salut). The main reason is that the Praetorians and Hegemons have been trying to wipe out the Guardians of the Veil, who represented the main active threat to them (the Arrow are focusing on trying to take down Noface, with little success). The Guardians are now wiped out, but Voidfisher's timebombs (and foul weather) have kept them busy, for a time. On the Chron Legacy side, he's not interested in helping the Seers hunt down Chron's cabal, so he's playing it cool for the moment. But Bagman has definitely taken notice of Dio. Now that the dust is beginning to settle (and the week of storms is almost over), the Seers are about to get more active...

                                            3. You guess correctly. Dexter Nuremberg, at your service. It's about time he showed up!

                                            4. So this is mostly based on stuff DaveB wrote in the Soul Cage Actual Play. I can't find any reference to this in the books, though I haven't scoured all of 1e yet. Signs of Sorcery DOES say that it's possible that the 'Confrontation with the Lie' that kickstarts Awakenings *may* be related to the Path symbology of the Awakened, but it's hard to tell because as soon as the Awakening starts the Path symbology floods everywhere anyhow.

                                            In general, it's a trend I've seen in quite a few Actual Plays that just feel *right*:

                                            Acanthus - Tend to Awaken when confronted with impossible odds, million-to-one chances, strange coincidences, or distortions in time or anachronistic (or ancient) encounters with the Lie.

                                            Obrimos - Lots tend to have religious Awakenings, but a fair number seem to Awaken in storms or fires, miraculously surviving the flames, like Dio. Dio is a Thyrsus, but she's the most Obrimos Thyrsus I've ever heard of (and one day I may even explain why). Most of my players have had atypical Awakenings, come to think of it. Except Chron. Chron is straight up Acanthus.

                                            Thyrsus - Encounters with wild animals, fights, or epiphanies in nature. Spirit journeys are a common theme here. Sometimes drugs are involved. Or actual encounters with Spriits.

                                            Moros - Near-death experiences, actual-death experiences or murdering someone will usually do it. Or encounters with unusual chemicals, alchemy, or any other great change or death of a thing.

                                            Mastigos - Mindfucks, basically. Psychotic breaks, drug-fuelled ego-death, Viceful behaviour, deep meditation, that sort of thing.
