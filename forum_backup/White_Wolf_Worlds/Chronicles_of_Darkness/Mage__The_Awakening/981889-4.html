    Alright, edited to make sure I wasn't assuming too much.

    Again, Dave, I don't hate or even dislike you. One of many reasons for that is that if I couldn't rail against you without being gigantically hypocritical, because I'm not currently in a place to accuse anyone else of being insulting and condescending. I can't wag my finger at you, because I've done everything I could accuse you of far more often (I can't exactly act shocked and offended for you saying you're smarter than me, because of how often I've taken the "I'm right and you're wrong and this is empirical truth" position. In fact, the fact that I immediately zeroed in on that part of your post as a personal attack against me, when it was likely just sarcastically using the wording I had used, says a lot about me, and indicates that I'm still in the mode of looking for fights with people). If I write you off as an asshole for disagreeing with me and having different ideas, then I can't exactly complain when other people do that with me. Not to mention, all of the massively worse people there are out there.

    I apologize again for being overly negative, unproductive, and confrontational.

    Back on track, about the "cursed" theme and establishing that Awakened are not "humans but better", there are some ways to establish it. I want to make it super clear that Awakening is not a reward or a sign of virtue or even ability, and obsessive pursuit of a goal beyond the limits of sanity is the most likely way to reach it. Being a moral person, a determined person, or even an intelligent or creative person does not make you more likely to Awaken. As an example, Stephen Hawking is unlikely to Awaken, because when one of his theories is proven wrong he accepts it and keeps going instead of screaming "No, you're wrong!" at the universe. Awakening comes to those people willing to burn their clothes and lock themselves in a shed for weeks to tattoo lines from the Iliad on their bodies to protect themselves from the chemtrail goblins. Another thing I'm borrowing from Genius is that it's very hard for the Awakened to make genuine lasting contributions to human history, society, and culture. (Genius gives Da Vinci and Nikola Tesla as examples of the few historical figures who were likely Geniuses, and they could easily have been Mages). This is part of the overall philosophy of ChroD, if I'm not mistaken, and I always thought Mage made it a bit too easy to say that all or most of human history is the result of magical manipulation (though I liked that Mage Noir made it clear that mages didn't invent the atom bomb). Going back to what I said about awakening, mages tend to have a very hard time functioning in normal human culture. The Seers are the ones who are the best at it.

    About the Seers, I kind of like the idea that they're actually less numerous than the other factions, and I'll put it in the storytelling section as a potential reveal the ST could pull. Mages are meant to be fairly rare, and I also like the idea that the Seers use subtle and far-reaching manipulation to indirectly influence people, and deliberately play on Awakened paranoia to make themselves seem larger and more omnipresent than they really are. Part of the retooled Seers is that they're very aware of how warped and broken the Awakened are, and they use that to their advantage: deliberately seeding clues to play into the Obsessions of Pentacle mages who try to follow them, until those mages start seeing Seer agents and plants everywhere.

    I was struggling with what to do with the Free Council, since they've always been a bit of a stumbling block for me (too many fans portray them as unambiguously good gee whiz too hip science heroes), and I've decided to give them a major and explicit connection to organized crime, to the point of having their internal organization closely resemble the mafia, pirate crews, and other criminal organizations. This is the model they use to influence Sleeper culture, similarly to the way the Diamond uses mystery cults and fraternal organizations.

    I also want to introduce a "Nemesis" condition, representing a character having a Venture Bros-style "arch".

        Currently stuck on how to work out the Tremere and archmasters. I kind of like the idea that all archmastery is basically a form of lichdom, since ChroD is all about exploring monster archetypes. Here's one nerf I'm considering for archmasters based on that idea: each archmaster has an object called a Phylactery. They can only manifest in the physical world with one, or else they start causing major paradox backlash almost immediately. Destroying it bars them from the world until they can use Quintessence to make another one. Destroying an archmage's phylactery inside their realm, while difficult, permanently kills them.

        Also, does anyone know the relative power level of archmasters, as compared to, say, the True Fae? I'd like them to be roughly equal when it comes to power, with the main difference being that archmages can't influence the world as easily as the Gentry can (assuming both exist. If only archmages exist, then sure, have them fill the role of the Gentry).

        Also, I'm going to emphasize the visual bang you can get out of spells and nimbuses. Since they're invisible to normals and they can look like whatever the player wants, why not give emphasize how cool they can look? In fact, mental image already has a strong connection to spellcasting, so I'll play that up, make it so that a Mage's spells appear in a way resonant with their paradigm, and this appearance is vital to even being able to cast them: an Obrimos who saw the vast machinery of the cosmos when she Awakened can't not make golden gears appear when she casts a spell. Or, to put it another way, she can't effect reality without imagining golden gears, and because the reactor of magical potential burning inside her works by turning symbols into reality, actual golden gears appear.

        I prefer nimbuses to be pretty dramatic by default. "Moros might cause subtle rot around them, or melancholy. " I get that Awakening often goes for subtlety, and that's cool, but a nimbus is a magic effect only supernatural beings can see. If you asked me to give an example of a Moros Nimbus, I would say: "The skin appears to rot off her bones, and her skeleton snaps, reforms, and sprouts extra bony limbs like a grotesque undead tree. In the moment she casts the spell, she is visually transfigured into the image of a multi-armed and horned skeleton dripping with black tar. Thin black veins shoot out from the fingertips of her bony form, piercing her target and pumping the rot that surrounds her into them."

        I probably would have loved Mage from the beginning if someone had told me I could totally make it Persona, since even in the base rules there's nothing that says a Nimbus can manifest as the Mage summoning an ephemeral being that represents their inner self.

        I don't want to say "I have trouble with Mage because I'm autistic", but I am, and that would pretty much be the shorter version of "I have trouble with Mage because it's so frustrating trying to fit every game and setting element in ChroD into one single perfect organized, internally consistent and perfectly balanced crossover setting where everything is explained and everything has its own perfect thematic niche, and that I can't is not a negative reflection on the design of the games or on their creators, but I just can't get over my frustration over not being able to make all the pieces fit ahhhhhhh".

        Something I feel perfectly justified in ranting about, however, is the fact that "autistic" has been so widely used as an insult that I still feel awkward using it to describe myself. I hate that we're still in this fucking Victorian mindset where being neuroatypical is seen as something shameful and temporary. But that's off topic.

            Big news! I'm canceling this project, and I couldn't be happier. I forced myself to confront some truths that I had been ignoring in my pursuit of a Perfect Coherent Balanced Setting. I realized that I want three different things. 1. I want to play a minor template sorcerer or mad scientist or a Deviant. 2. I want to play Mage, but not bogged down by my own desire to force it into crossover viability and thematic horror purity. 3. I want an over the top superhero game based on The Venture Bros. So I'm not longer trying to force Awakening to be all of those things.

            My new project is to let Awakening be Awakening. I have revised my personal canon, so that mages do not exist in my ChroD setting. The only sorcerers there are desperate hedge mages, because that was what I preferred to mages in that setting and trying to force mages to be that just wasn't working. I'm now making Mage its own setting, one in which Mage reigns supreme and I can go all out with its mad, brilliant ideas. I've made another thread for it, but basically my new Mage hack and new project is an anti-crossover: it outlines a world and cosmology defined purely by the Supernal, where the focus is entirely on the Awakened and their adventures.

            I'm jettisoning a lot of the rules that have been proposed or formulated to make mages more monstrous or less powerful or make the game grittier. The Awakened get to be the Awakened, not my attempt to force them into basically being a minor template. I might carry over a few of the ideas here, like weird science and path flexibility, but mostly I'm focusing on letting Mage be Mage. Letting Awakening be the most Awakeningy it can be.

            It's been to long. I've spent too long lying to myself about Mage and telling myself that it's a bad game, or even one I don't personally like. I've spent too long condemning it for not fitting a very narrow expectation. I'm done with that. It's time to revel in Mage for what it is.
