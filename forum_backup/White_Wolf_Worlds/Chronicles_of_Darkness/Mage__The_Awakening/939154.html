    Summoning Supernal Entities: How Often does this come up?

    07-28-2016, 05:46 PM
    I am struggling to get my head around how frequently mages make use of their ability to conjure Supernal Entities. In 1e we only found out about it in the Summoners sourcebook and my take on it then was that it was a rare, esoteric discipline, the type of thing mages quested after to discover the necessary techniques in order to further some greater goal. I am totally open to the possibility of this new interpretation, hell I love the drama and tension of a big occult summoning scene! Its just now its smack bang in the middle of the Core book I am unsure just how frequently its meant to come up?

    Do NPC mages in your games conjure and converse with Supernal spirits on a regular basis? How often have your players turned to the higher realms for answers to their questions?

              I wouldn't say Supernal summoning is something that is, or ever should be, done with any degree of regularity or any sense of the commonplace. It is a thoroughly dangerous action; a slip-up invites an Abyssal manifestation into the world or, worse, sees a Supernal being fed to non-reality. Yes, measures may be taken to prevent this but no guarantee is perfect. This, all before consideration that a Supernal entity in and of itself, even when friendly, may be actively harmful to everyone and everything around it.

              All that said, however, Supernal summoning is far from unheard of in my games. The risk is very much a thing measured against a reward. Supernal entities are glorious and powerful, and can offer insight into the grandest Mysteries of all as no other being can. Even the weakest and most insignificant can be powerful allies far in advance of others.

              One of my Moros players has an ongoing relationship with a Stygian entity known as Gur Aryeh - the Young Lion; the same entity who taught the Maharal how to construct golems with which to defend the Jewish people of Prague - who acts as a frequent advisor on Supernal matters and provides insight into alchemy. The Moros in question doesn't summon him often, but the central importance of the entity to his mystical endeavours have seen him dedicate an entire room in his sanctum to the sole purpose of calling Gur Aryeh. In the past, Gur Aryeh has directed the Necromancer to find his first Hallow; provided him with the location of an ancient alchemist's workshop; guided him to a stockpile of thunderbolt iron; and explained the nature of a Bound. On occasion, Gur Aryeh has provided material aid too, in the form of the occasional Supernal artifact. Every one of these caused the Necromancer to uncover some old, stagnant relic and make it into something new, which is in keeping with Gur Aryeh's transformative nature.

              Supernal entities are powerful and deservedly so, but shouldn't come up too often; they should be treated as the dangerous but awe-inspiring beings they are; even the most familiar treated as a wise mentor atop a high mountain, whose climb is at times risky and dangerous. Calling one down should be a momentous occasion, done when nothing else will satisfy the need for insight only they can offer.

                  Well yes and no. The great thing about summoning is, try as you might, you don't quite know who or what will answer your call. We have an Obrimos PC whose Obsession is communicating / interacting with Angels. When he finally succeeded, it was a Rank 2 Cherub named Eblis (Prime / Life) that materialized; a giant raven with six eyes, human hind legs, and claws on his wings. A golden mandala halo shimmered behind its head.

                  The thing is, the PC was trying to invoke a higher ranking cherub. Eblis turned out to be a servant only, and a particularly prickly and condescending one at that. (It kept on making snide remarks about humans.) Nevertheless, the angel aided our Obrimos after snacking on the stored Mana in his Demesne.

                  We also have an NPC Thyrsus who serves as a herbalist, poisons master, and ally of the group. She has been known to summon plant Atavisms, and keep specimens for study and even trade in the black market. She has performed several summonings in the past, but nothing on a regular or habitual basis. Not only does it risk the attention of the Abyss, it also lays open the Fallen World to the untamed forces of the Primal Wild.

                                  As written in core, there are following ways to provoke Abyssal slip up:
                                  a) You roll more times than your Resolve+Composure (so, in generally, around 4-7 times; if you are smart and ask your Mastigos to get you buffed Resolve or Composure, this can go to 10+), and *then*, your GM rolls your Gnosis every next roll to check if intrusion happens, and even *then*, he needs to accumulate successes equal your Gnosis + Primary Arcanum (so minimum of 4, usually something about 5 or 6). This means that you need to fail summoning for 4 extended roll turns, then roll your Gnosis enough times to gather 4-6 successes, which should take either about 15 or 10 turns of extended roll. Unless you are super unlucky, you will probably either finish the summoning, or flat out run out of extended roll turns to finish it before it can get corrupted.
                                  b) You *decide* to take a Dramatic Failure on a summoning roll.

                                  More powerful entities don't bring greater risk; they only need more successes to summon. And because you have limited number of turns to summon the entity...
                                  Like, you get minimum of 4 rolls (gnosis 1 + arcanum 3), and maximum of 10 rolls (gnosis 5, arcanum 5) to summon an entity. Even if you are something like Gnosis 3 + Arcanum 3 Summoner with Resolve+Composure total of 4, you *still* only risk two times, and both of these times you roll 3 dice and need to accumulate 6 successes. 6 successes on 6 dice.

                                              Seems to me a way better option here would be to remove the roll cap on Supernal summonings.
