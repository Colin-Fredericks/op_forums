                                                    Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
                                                    Mechanically, what does a brand new, freshly Awakened mage look like?
                                                    Dave mentioned it some time ago on 4chan. If I remember correctly, the stats are (in addition to standard mortal template and changes in it based on previous play before Awakening):

                                                    One dot in one Ruling Arcanum, two dots in the other, one dot gained in a resistance attribute, Gnosis 1 and the relevant derivatives, Wisdom 7.

                                                    EDIT: I intentionally didn't mention merits, because not only you should have at least 7 dots from before Awakening, which would mean you may get only three during Awakening, but you can also normally spend 5 dots to get 1 Gnosis, which should be only at one dot immediately after Awakening. This would imply that you had to earn those merit dots to get to at least 5 dots to be able to buy Gnosis with it and this implies training, not inherent ability gained during Awakening.

                                                        Originally posted by minightengale View Post
                                                        So the term "Rote Quality" shows up a bunch in this book.

                                                        The first instance is on page 92, where receives the text reads, "the player the rote quality on the roll" when discussing Clash of Wills and Scrutiny. There is no example to delineate what Rote Quality is or how it is determined. It appears later in the rules section under spells for adding Rote Quality rolls. In the Luck section, there is a rote that reads: The subject gains the rote quality on a number of mundane dice rolls equal to Potency. So here I'm thinking Rote Quality is a number. Later, in Time, it states that +1 Reach: For a point of Mana, Dodge actions taken while under this spell’s effect have the rote quality. That reads like it isn't a number, but rather a condition? It isn't very clear.

                                                        What is Rote quality, and where is it derived from?
                                                        Like all other dice tricks, it's on page 214. It means "reroll failed dice once."

                                                        Rote quality actions have been in the Storytelling system since the first World of Darkness corebook, and they've been called that since then too. The idea was that mage's rotes would use them - but then, by the time Awakening 1e came out, they didn't (which was needlessly confusing). Some Fate spells in 1e granted the rote quality on dice rolls, Ochemata and other high-end Supernal beings had it on mundane tasks, that kind of thing.

                                                        In 2e, if you're a master casting a rote you made yourself or a mage using a grimoire, rotes actually do now have the rote quality.


                                                        So... It's a system term, that this is the 13th corebook to use, and is explained in the systems chapter.
