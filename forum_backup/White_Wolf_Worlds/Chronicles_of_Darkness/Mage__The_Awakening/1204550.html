    invalid targets

    04-08-2018, 11:05 AM
    What happens when a mage targets something with a spell that's inapplicable? Both in terms of the effect of the spell, and the qualia of casting the spell for the mage.

    For instance, what happens when a mage tries to use a Life spell on a Mind-induced hallucination?
    Or, as was the case in the game I was running, tries to use Forces to turn down a Mind-induced painful screeching noise?
    Or someone pulls the old "blanket over pillows" trick and a fooled mage tries to cast a Fate spell to prevent the "sleeper" from waking up?

    My biggest concern as a ST regarding this is wanting to be consistent and figuring out how much doing something like this should hint to players that something is off.

          The operative parts of the answer to this question are whether a) the mage in question Reaches for Sensory Range — an Aimed spell will simply establish an area in which the effect would happen if there was a valid target — and b) the mage in question is in the habit of using their Active Sight prior to casting to avoid exactly this conundrum.

                      I could see an argument for the Forces using Mage realizing that the sound doesn't fall under Force's purview, in other words realizing that it isn't really caused by vibrating sound waves, but he wouldn't be able to use Forces to stop it and he wouldn't automatically know it is a hallucination. By the same token I could see a Mage trying to use Mind to affect an android, which would require Forces or Matter, realizing that it doesn't work because the target doesn't have a mind, but again he wouldn't automatically know what it is. A Life Mage would realize that it isn't a true life form, but wouldn't know an android from a ghostly manifestation.
                      As for the pillow trick, the Fate spell did work and the pillows didn't awaken.
