    Originally posted by lnodiv View Post
    Ran some numbers just for funsies. Only using published spells because honestly, Creative Thaumaturgy makes this way too easy.

    You can hit 10 in every physical attribute plus the relevant combat skills with 3 combined spells. These are going to need to be ritually cast, and you're going to have to be a Disciple of Life and Mind, but it's totally doable assuming you have 3 in the relevant traits beforehand.
    This is going to give you 15 health, an average of 12 defense (20 if you enhance wits to the same degree, but that's an extra spell control slot, you could combine it with enhancing Resolve for an average WP pool of 12).


    What's not going to be doable reasonably is the regeneration. You'd need 19 Potency to regen one lethal per turn with Body Control, although every point of Potency above and beyond that is going to have massive returns. To even have a hope of doing this, you can forget being a Disciple - you want Mastery for the free Potency.

    Change the Primary Factor, and you knock the number of 'additional' Potency you need down to...14. That's -28 dice. Even assuming you can deploy 4 yantras, You're looking at, at best, +4 Persona, +2 High Speech, +2 Runes, +5 Rote. That's only +13 Dice, and after factoring in Gnosis 6 and Life 5 you're still 4 dice in the hole. Ritually casting it brings your total dice up to...1. Spend willpower, and you can get a respectable 4 dice.

    Except if you want it to last any reasonable amount of time, you're still going to need to add a bit of Duration. Making it last a day is going to cost you two dice, for a final dicepool of two, which fails more often than it succeeds.

    tl;dr while adding massive numbers to mundane combat dice pools is super easy, matching one of the most basic features of the Forsaken template requires Mastery and hours of ritually casting and still doesn't get results reliably.

    And it still doesn't hold a candle to Gauru regen.

    I think the system is beautiful for having ways to accomplish this feat. Also, your (and many others) number crunching really helps to show some perspective. The sheer dice accounting is enough to keep most sensible mages away (conserving resources; getting the most bang for your buck; striking when the time is right; element of surprise, etc.)

    Some other solutions listed are off-the-cuff solutions to give a werewolf the impression their opponent isn't squishy. These snappy solutions are probably more akin to what a mage under surprise attack would do and I can readily appreciate them as well.

            Ran some numbers of my own on a couple previous approaches, restricting myself to victory through melee damage (as opposed to floating the enemy in the air and blasting them in a leisurely manner). Same disclaimer applies, these aren't guaranteed kill alls, they have weaknesses and can be overcome. And no build survives contact with the plot. However all three fare extremely well in CQC vs a Werewolf, even one spec'd for combat, and would make menacing antagonists for a Forsaken game.

            Enter the Dragon (Acanthus, Gnosis 4)
            Attributes: Dexterity 5, Wits 5
            Skills: Weaponry 5 (Nunchucks)
            Merits: Armed Defense 4, Defensive Combat (Weaponry), Fighting Finesse, Adamant Hand (Weaponry), Shadow Name 3 (The Dragon)
            Arcana: Time 4, Fate 3, Spirit 2
            Rotes: Acceleration, Monkey's Paw, Shifting Sands
            Strategy: Before combat the Mage precasts a really high potency Monkey's Paw on the weapon of her choice, let's go full Enter the Dragon and use nunchucks. She bumps the weapon bonus up to +10 and make this last at least 24 hours (indefinite might be better). Next she casts Acceleration with a high potency, lets say 10 . She'll add the extra reach to make all Dodge actions Rote while she's at it. Now the Mage's Dodge pool is ~40 dice, 30 vs Gauru. This can be bumped to 60 (50 vs Gauru) with dot 4 of Armed Defense turning her weapon bonus into a defense bonus. Spirit 2 is there to grant vision of spirits, and the ability to answer Numina if necessary. Shifting Sands is there to bug out if things get hairy and do a fight reset. For extra safety the Mage can hang a casting of Temporal Summoning to restore her to peak condition if she's struck, or cast Past as Present in turn 1 and foresee everything her opponent(s) is going to do.
            Combat is mostly spent waiting for her opponent(s) to line up for a good attack. Starting with Present as Past guarantees she'll know what the Werewolf (or pack) is planning, and gives her the means to answer it. When the time is right she uses dot 3 of Armed Defense to turn her Dodge pool into a counter attack. With Rote Quality she can expect damage returns in the range of 20+ successes. Enough to pour past most Werewolves lethal track and begin to deal aggravated damage.

            Iron Man (Obrimos, Gnosis 3)
            Attributes: Strength 5
            Skills: Weaponry 5 (Poleaxe)
            Merits: Heavy Weaponry 4, Defensive Combat, Adamant Hand (Weaponry), Shadow Name 3 (Iron Man)
            Arcana: Matter 3, Force 3
            Rotes: Kinetic Blow, Aegis
            Strategy: First, become Iron Man. The Mage gets himself a suit of full body armor and uses matter to reshape it such that it has zero openings, then casts Aegis to give it a huge armor rating. 10 is fairly doable. Next get a big weapon, a poleaxe will do, he casts Kinetic Blow to boost its weapon bonus to +10, also use some reach so it stuns and knocks down on every hit. He makes the weapon silver while he's at it too. Now he's headed to combat with 10 Armor and +10 Damage, but it gets better. With Heavy Weapons 4 the Mage can use that weapon bonus as yet further armor, hiking that value up to 20 (without sacrificing the damage). With Armor that high he can afford to take risks until he knocks down or stun the Werewolf, from there it’s pure beating with a +10 Silver weapon.

            Rawr I'm a Dinosaur (Thrysis, Gnosis 4)
            Attributes: Whatever, he's turning into a dinosaur
            Skills: Brawl 5 (Megaraptor)
            Merits: Relentless Assault 4, Techne (Jurassic Park), Shadow Name 3 (Megaraptor)
            Arcana: Life 4, Mind 3, Death 2, Time 2, Spirit 2
            Rotes: Shapechange, Hone the Form, Enhance Skills, Mend
            Strategy: Start by turning into a megaraptor, gaining all it's presumably high attributes, natural weapons, and dinoness. Next hike physical attributes and Brawl as high as possible. Assuming Gnosis 4, Life 4, Rote +5, High Speech +2, Persona +4, Legacy Yantra +2 he'll be looking at a dice pool of 19 dice, willpower takes it to 22. One could reasonably boost attributes to +5 across the board, albeit the roll is dicey so he'll want to give himself the steadfast condition to guarantee success. Mind 3 can achieve that. Brawl & Wits needs to be boosted similarly, which is entirely doable. He'll be out a bunch of mana and willpower by the end of the process, but has the joy of being a superpowered magical dinosaur.
            End result looks something like: Attack - 25 dice, Defense - 20 (10 against Gauru), Health - 18...which still doesn't look great matched up against Gauru. To turn the tide he'll need Mend, cast with high potency hung with Time. Death Armor will also help, effectively doubling his health track by converting everything to bashing. Finally Relentless Assault lets him make huge all-out attacks without losing defense, and grants a little boost to damage and armor.

            Between the three builds I'd give best odds to The Dragon, mostly because it leverages highly magey tactics for which Werewolves have very few answers (fucking with time and being precognizant). Of all of them she's probably the best suited to beat more than one Werewolf at a time. Iron Man is insanely dangerous to Werewolves as well, but slightly more prone to being caught out on a weakness he doesn't have covered (for instance, if his Armor can be negated for even a single attack he's in trouble), but if the pack doesn't have a solution to his armor Iron Man will be essentially invulnerable. Dinosaur is closest to the original question, and can win, but I still feel one more Arcana (probably Fate or Forces) would be needed to tip the advantage to the Mage. However, it's key to note that in all three cases the Mage is dumping major time, mana, and willpower into the fight and relying on tools ritually prepared well in advance. A Werewolf can be a ripping, killing machine at the drop of a hat.

            Verdict: Mages can win in CQC, but they have to be built for it. However, a Mage built for CQC can be an unholy terror on par with even the fightiest of other splats. Conversely werewolves will dominate in CQC even with very little investment. Indeed one has to purposefully build away from combat to make a Werewolf bad at CQC.
