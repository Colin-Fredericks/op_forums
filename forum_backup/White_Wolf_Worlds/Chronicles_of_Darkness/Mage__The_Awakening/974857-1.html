    Originally posted by Ashenrogue View Post
    Yeah, but that's because people generally know exactly what zombies are capable of. They're well known, and information galore exists on them, so of course they lose a bit of that scariness of the unknown type thing, at this point they represent a completely different type of horror, which is fine. But what happens when the zombies aren't doing what you expect, and exhibit powers not native to them? How much of what you know of zombies can be relied on at the point? What happens when you shoot them in the head, destroy the brain, and they keep on coming?     And what happens when whatever is responsible is aware of and uses observation/awareness of itself to spread/attack? When Knowledge Itself is the danger?

                    It seems like boiled down to its basics, the problem is simply:

                    Monsters are mysterious unknowns, but Mages have the ability and resources to learn about the unknown.

                    Monsters are dangerous, but so are Mages, especially prepared ones.

                    ...but it seems like in both of those sentences "Mages" could be replaced with "Hunters." Mages have more tools to learn about things and more tools to fight them, but the basic shape of things is the same, and it seems straightforwardly the case that if you can menace and horrify Hunters and their players then you can do the same to Mages.

                        The sad truth is real horror comes not from just the unknown but from a sense of helplessness, something I haven't seen any RPG outside of Cthullu achieve (or want to). Yes Mages can identify the threat, feel out its strengths and weaknesses, and then have the power to carry the fight to the enemy but then so do Vampires, Werewolves, Changelings and the monster of the week. For me the game works best when most of the story is about going through those steps and then taking out the baddie. In my experience the best monsters are ones intelligent enough to return the favor. I also recommend evil minions or multiple, yet subtly different, monsters, so your characters can take some out and enjoy the battle without derailing the entire plot in the first act (no monologue-ing).

                                        On the issue of helplessness, while a vampire or werewolf aren't likely to be bothered much by an individual human, a mage can still just be shot in the back of the head or hit by a car when unexpected. This is obviously not a building-up of dread situation, but it could be the climax of a series of threats or insidious warnings about something bad going to happen.

                                        However there's a lot more in the universe than just humans. And even if a mage was able to recognize a threat, find out the individual supernatural being that might cause it, and find out its weaknesses, and even gather them up, keep in mind that's a lot of steps and not something a mage is guaranteed to be able to do, they still would have to use said weaknesses against the being. As it is ephemerals of Rank 5 have weird banes that are not going to be easy to reproduce, and in the game of mage, the concept of dealing with even higher Rank creatures is a possibility.

                                        Yes, you can have a game where a mid-to-high 'level' mage punches out Cthulhu. But in the larger picture of things, Cthulhu is a high priest of Azathoth, who mages won't be able to punch out, and who is likely to be the kind of threat a high 'level' mage will deal with.
