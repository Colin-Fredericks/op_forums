                    Originally posted by MCN View Post
                    I don't want someone to just tell me mages are monsters because they abuse power, then insist that my non-abusive Enlightened magus is still a monster when she engages in none of the abusive attitudes.
                    The abusive attitudes are partially enforced through the fact that failing a breaking point roll gives you Conditions that render it impossible for a Gnosis 1-2 character to pursue higher Wisdom until they are discharged (when discharging those Conditions de facto involves more risks to your Wisdom). Mages who lose Wisdom become selfish and careless and get pips of the resource that lets them buy Wisdom for things as simple as casting in front of Sleepers — and start getting incentivized to inure spells and let Paradox become more of an issue than Wisdom loss, which in turn means they've either become more Mana-dependent or locked off a spell for special occasions.

                    For that matter, having the necessary Obsession to cultivate Wisdom inherently raises the risk in every Act of Hubris you engage with, and it takes an entire story of fruit-bearing actual-work enlightenment-seeking to get the opportunity to spend those ten Arcane Beats that you will almost certainly earn faster if you court degeneration to woo Sophia. If you feel confident making that many unboostable two-dice rolls at the risk of losing more Wisdom, your secure place in the cosmos could well be one that you got to by leaving a trail of devastation in your wake, and you'll be a lot better off than the folks who're just going cold-turkey.

                    Yes, low-Tolerance-high-Integrity supernatural beings like most starting characters are going to be able to act in a manner acceptable to mortal society without much distress. This is not a new phenomenon — it's an entire easily-viable playstyle in Demon, for instance — and it changes very little about the all-consuming lore-hunger squatting at the center of your web of occult sympathies waiting to poison your life and your home if you break character with the wrong people while pretending not to be carrying one of the most invasive investigative implements conceivable by human thought.

                    As a point of curiosity, what's the fuller context of the enlightened doctor's quest for Wisdom?

                                        Your non-abusive Enlightened magus might try not to engage in abusive attitudes, but how does she make peace between her newly discovered power and her drives as a doctor? You don't become a doc because you were a bit bored and took a course, it takes years of dedicated study, and this takes drive. And now you learn a lot of it was a Lie? (see the 'The World is a Lie' text in the book)
                                        What is this drive? Is it the clichee 'I want to help people?' or something a bit different? Does she use magic to heal the sick? If not, why not? How mean. If yes, why just those? Don't others deserve the same help? Could you wipe out diseases on a global scale? Could you enhance people's immune systems on that scale?
                                        .... Yeah, no. First of all, that's not how abuse actually works. Secondly, no, it wasn't a Lie. That sidebar actually refers to the Exarchs and Seers ruling the world, and suggests the Pentacle (and thus the PCs) is at war with them. Not that Sleeper Science is wrong. In fact, for someone who's also a part of Free Council who believes that science is an echo of seeking Supernal Truth? Frankly, I don't see my PC having any qualms with making peace between "newly discovered power" and being a doctor. She had qualms about the people close to her being subject to Long Term Nimbus (thus the Wisdom). But the power itself? Not really.

                                        Secondly, choosing to not to help people is not a "monstrous choice." There's fairly well established ethics in the medical community about responcibility to patients as well as to the self. That's why the Wisdom - the understanding when its best to apply the balance of the personal life and the duties as a doctor and the life as a mage. Its knowing that experimenting on living people is kidn of a bad, immoral thing that shouldn't be done. Its knowing that there are times when casting magic shouldn't be done.

                                        Third, "wanting to help people" is not a cliche. I went into social services because I want to help people in real life. Its called "this actually happens with a lot of frequency."

                                        I feel like you're trying to tell me that my character is going to absolutely make "wrong choices" because she's a mage. That's kind of circular reasoning. Mages are monsters because they make wrong choices, and they make wrong choices because they're monstrous mages. Everything you're suggesting is a choice. And, you know what? Even when picking between bad choices, that doesn't make someone inherantly a monster. It just makes them a flawed human with others being judgemental.

                                        Do Obsessions really need to have a punishment mechanic for them to be played as intended?
                                        No. Does it make them more effective and evocative? I find that to be a yes.

Paradox, Nimbus and Obsessions give me the tools for that. Or, at least, they should. Without them? *shakes head sadly*

                                        The fact that their power is inherently easy for them to use is part of the setting's horror because it also means it's ripe for abuse.
                                        Ripe for abuse does not equal actual abused. That's nothing more than the "power corrupts" line that has been scientifically proven false and is not part of Mage 2e by default.

                                        How does having Wisdom as an Obsession render you a monster? It means you'll be experimenting with what's Wise,
                                        No... it means you'll be studying what is Wise. Obsession does not equate to experimentation in every situation. It can, but its not a given. There are many ways of achiving an Obsession.
