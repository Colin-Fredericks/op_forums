                Originally posted by CreatorFounderMaker View Post
                I have a hard time putting any validity in the beliefs of literal soul-eating psychopaths.
                Tremere can be ruthless and cultivate a certain amorality within themselves, but I feel as though we've gotten enough books right now that lend depth and nuance to their beliefs and practices that would make them more engaging characters than a line like this might convey. Even the notion of "soul eating" needs to be put into context, since not only is it perfectly within their power to replace the souls that they take with their spent ones (with no indication that possession of such a thing does long term harm to the human who receives it), but they have certain incentives to do so by way of not giving away their presence by leaving behind a trail of people without souls and general sustainability. One could even have some Tremere who interpret their creed in terms of encouraging a kind of fair exchange in souls.

                As far as the Seventh Dragon, I feel as though that gets complicated by a question of whether the Tremere philosophy should be presented as completely delusional, or no less well-reasoned and researched than the beliefs of any given sect. I think that's supposed to be a recurring element to the horror features of Mage antagonists; the potential that they might be correct about some of what they espouse, or at least have a credible basis to believe that they do.

                Even if the Seventh Dragon is not Supernal directly, if it is a most profound expression of something that the Tremere believe, with cause, to be the core truth of the Supernal, then they might have basis to regard it as something that deeply resonates with the Supernal regardless of where it comes from, which might have greater implications for their model of the cosmology. After all, the Second Edition core presents such questions in terms of "reality seems to incorporate some influences from alien universes, and mages can't discount the possibility that such things manage to rise up to become part of the Supernal".

                Alternatively, the question of "what Realm does it come from" might be obviated by the fact that distinct Supernal Realms are largely an illusion for the benefit of Awakening mages anyway, and there's some understanding that the Supernal beyond the influence of the Watchtowers (which itself is not fixed) is more undifferentiated. Such a thing would match with the Tremere belief that the Paths are traps and impositions.

                Indeed, considering what they believe the other six Dragons to be, the answer could well be "apparently another Watchtower". Is it a Watchtower that has been somehow forced out of the Supernal? That raises the question of exactly where it is supposed to be right now. The question of "if it is a Watchtower, who made it" is mysterious in the way that the question of who made any of the Watchtowers is mysterious.

                I have approximate knowledge of many things.

                    The pre-Chur Tremere were operating under a philosophy of "someone needs to be bold enough to make the spiritual sacrifices needed to push humanity's magical power and enlightenment". As ambitious as the Silver Ladder, following what could be seen as an offensive version of the Guardian's tenet of "Sins for a just end grant Wisdom".

                    The current Tremere are closer to the Sith. They do not stifle individual ambitions, they exalt them (so long as they don't conflict with those of other Tremere). If the truth is that magic hungers for souls, then they shall embrace it to throw off another insidious shackle of the Lie. Succeed and survive, or fail and become an empty husk.

                    Well, at least that is how I imagine they justify their philosophy to themselves. As was mentioned above, whether they are correct or corrupted is left deliberately nebulous.
