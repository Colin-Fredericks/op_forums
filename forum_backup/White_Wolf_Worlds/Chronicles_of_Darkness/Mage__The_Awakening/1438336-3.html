        Originally posted by LadyLens View Post
        How in any world does changing a solid into a liquid not alter the function of what that solid can do?
        Because conservation of energy does not apply to that transformation. If you liquefy a rock, you will have a room temperature liquid rock rather than magma. You can create ice gas, an oxygen sword or skates made out of solid water (rather than ice). The mage's Gnosis simply says "You are in this state of matter now, this is your new reality. Rules of physics, go cry in a corner until I release this Imago". The trick is that any properties that are tangential to the main effect are not affected, such as elasticity or electrical conductivity. Which means traditional physics will not help you make sense of how the effect happened, but allow you to come up with clever applications for its consequences.

                                        Ok, so while everything about the subject seems to have been more or less said, but here is something to throw out there- did you know that there are a number of phases for ice? Like, some phases of ice can only form under great pressure, and the properties of the different phases are different from one another, so even saying "I transform water into ice" is not trivial. Would anyone here have a problem with the same Practice which allows you to turn water into different phases of ice, or even turn ice from one phase to another? If not, the same holds true for coal and diamonds In the end, what we call "states of matter" (which are simply approximations which are generally good to day to day life) simply measure how well defined is the structure of the material- or how much entropy it had. Diamonds are one such structure, which is very unfavorable because you may say that its crustal structure is too "perfect" (it is very close to a perfect crystal, which entropy really doesn't like). The other, more common crystal structures are simply more stable because they have a greater entropy. Now, while Mage clearly doesn't care about physics, if you decide that a certain Practice is enough to change water into ice, and "all that it does" is changing the arrangement of particles, the change is much less fundamental than, say change water and CO2 into sugar, or worse, change carbon into iron. Diamond and graphene are not the same material, but they are built using the same units, and as such a transformation of coal to diamonds may be more difficult than changing water into ice, but it is definitely the same process.

                                        So yeah, while you don't need two degrees in chemistry, do your M.Sc and PhD projects in a solid state laboratory and dedicate your life to investigating the contribution of entropy to the stability of materials, it does help to say "yeah, that's basically the same thing- like, it is waaaaaaaay more difficult because, like, ENTROPY, but you don't give a damn about the conservation of energy anyway so... yeah, magic"
