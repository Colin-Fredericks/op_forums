        With the exception of a couple of almost game-breaking loopholes being closed off, I'd say Mages are a little more powerful overall in 2nd Edition than they ever were in 1st Edition.

        There are a few things that make mages less powerful in some ways. You have to define your Imago up front, with the exception of a critical success it doesn't matter how many successes you roll, this stops spells having unexpected extra effect. The extra time it takes to include multiple Yantras means it might take a couple of rounds to get off a really potent effect. You need to include spell-factors from the start and that can be costly when you don't have a lot of dice to spare.

        However the ability to cast "vulgar" magic privately in your own home without worrying about Paradox is a lovely change that helps Mages actually do magical things. That's both a power boost and a major thematic win. The free reaches that you get for Rotes makes them legitimately worth obtaining for starting mages, since suddenly those spells are much more useful. The removal of artificial "road-blocks" allows even low level effects to be frighteningly powerful when used tactically. These all make mages more powerful and more magical.

        The main difference I would say is likely to be in combat (though I haven't tried a combat in 2nd Edition Mage yet). I think 2nd Edition mages are far more powerful with preparation, even a couple of rounds, but might struggle a little bit to pull off the same sort of flash-bang instant magic that you could sometimes manage in 1st Edition. The Mana cost for mage-shields also makes it a little less practical to wear your shields up at all times, which was a classic 1st Edition thing to do.

        I hate to use this word, because it's such a subjective term. However I feel that 2nd Edition is a little more balanced. It's not been reduced in power, but some of the spikes and dips in power have been evened out into a smoother curve.

            Clash of Wills is probably the greatest relative strength reduction in 2e. Shielding limits after that.
            You can't simply see things anymore. The 1e trick was to ritually cast Mage Sight and/or ephemeral detection along with some sort of shielding and go about your magey day. Thataway, you could simply see hidden stuff with regularity.

            2e makes both of those systems (Mage Sight and Armor) transient and cost more. So, in some very basic ways, mages are less powerful than they were. You can't plan around seeing anything not exceptionally well-hidden and being less vulnerable than normal folk unless you know it's coming. Since those were things most mages walked around with per the book, well, it stands to reason people would miss them.

            That said, the upper end of the spectrum is significantly higher. Thus, calling things more powerful is rational, too.

            Grump, grouse, and/or gripe.

                                    Where's your evidence?
                                    You should never confuse Lasting and Indefinite. In real life they may mean pretty much the same thing, but Mage systems have very distinct meanings for them. Indefinite is a Duration from the Advanced Duration Factor table. Lasting is not a duration, Lasting spells can be of any Duration, however their effects will happen again every ritual casting period while their Duration is still running. All spells can be made Indefinite. Very few spells can be made Lasting, and they say so in their description, or in the creative thaumaturgy sections by type of spell (healing spells, damaging spells, etc).

                                    Confusing these terms leads to arguments between people who don't actually disagree.

                                    Stealing Fire can be made Indefinite (as can all spells) but cannot be made Lasting by RAW, because, its description doesn't say it can be and it isn't one of the types that are automatically Lasting.

                                        The big thing here is that once something is Lasting, you can't go back - dispelling it won't work.
                                        This also means that very few spell effects are safe from Dispelling - look at the formula.

                                        Spell has a base Withstand of It's Arcanum Dots - so it's anywhere between 1 (easy) and 5 (still relatively easy). Then, you check and add 1 for every other Arcanum involved in the casting. Then,you check for Advanced Potency. What does it mean?
                                        It means that very few spells will be outside of "dispel range" of a starting Obrimos. The biggest thing stopping them is not knowing Arcanum involved. And of course, Dispel Magic is a perfect praxis spell, because why bother selling your dice for Potency? Better amass casting pool of 12 or something, and then just go for a Exceptional Success.
                                        Supernal Magic is extremely fragile.

                                            I did some number-crunching and figured out that: a Gnosis 1 mage who is instant casting a Rote (at +5) with an Arcana of 3, burning max mana (1), risking a paradox pool of 3 can pull off a 7 Reach spell (one of which is instant casting) with 3 spellcasting modifiers and a 6 die pool (after spending a WP). If they are really willing to push their luck, the same mage, risking a paradox pool of 7, using a Rote (at +5), can pull off an 11 Reach spell (one of which is instant casting) with 3 spellcasting modifiers and a 6 die pool (after spending WP).

                                            Oddly enough, mages overall "raw power" for the single instant spell example above dips after Gnosis 2 (but goes back up at 9 and 10), but the extra perks such as increased spell capacity, combined spells, Legacy attainments, extra Yantra, greater Mana capacity, shorter ritual duration, etc. are enough to offset the dip.

                                                    Both 1E and 2E Mages are pretty weak when they are caught with their pants down.
                                                    2E Mages feel considerably stronger than 1E Mages when they have a few minutes to an hour of prep, because of all the free dice and reach they can wrangle.
                                                    2E Mages feel somewhat weaker than 1E Mages when they have plenty of time to prep, because they cannot do the same extended casting shenanigans that 1E Mages could do.

                                                    On average, they feel considerably stronger than any other splat when they are going on the offensive and have some time to prep (due to so many vectors of attack, and layers of offense that are capable). But they are just as weak or possibly a little weaker when they are on the defensive.
