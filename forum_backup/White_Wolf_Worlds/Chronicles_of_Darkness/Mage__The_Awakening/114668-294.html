                            Okay, here's a question: how do you keep Lucky Number (fate 2) from breaking the setting? I just ran a Mage game where one of my players had it as a praxis. It started with him wanting to use it to call the phone nearest to their quarry and use his hacking skills to find the phone's location (the spell itself doesn't tell you the location of the phone, but he had really high computer skills). Of course, then he realized he didn't need to call the bad-guy's phone, he could just open google-earth, cast the spell, and randomly punch in coordinates that "just so happened" to be the bad-guy's exact location. Once you realize that this sort of shenanigans is perfectly valid within the spells limits, you realize all sorts of crap is possible. Like randomly punching on a key board until you've typed out all of the weaknesses of the spirit you're hunting. Luckily, I realized the implications and ruled that the spell did, in fact, run off of some sort of sympathy, but neither he nor I were happy with nerfing one of his signature spells, and it was still up to me to ad-hoc whether "yes this incredibly unlikely thing is a thing you can do" or not.
                            For reference, the odds of "just happening" to punch in the correct, 12-character password on the first try is of the same order of magnitude as "just happening" to be struck by lighting on any given second (which, incidentally, isn't possible without Fate 5 and +2 reach).
                            Sorry if that was a little rant-y. Does anyone have any better ideas for how to deal with this spell? Did the two of us just read it completely wrong?

                            The longer I study science the more I am convinced that it is functionally indistinguishable from what our ancestors would refer to as sorcery. And I would know, being both scientist and sorcerer.

                                Veiling spells. Especially if word gets around that he's using Fate spells to find people a lot. Lucky Number is not an Unveiling spell, so any Veiling magic that hides the subject will simply cause Lucky Number to report wrong information. Space would probably be able to do it, as well as maybe a creative thaumaturgy Fate Veiling spell (Warding Gesture itself doesn't really seem to deal with probabilities). Also, I run my games with the idea that the players specifically need to be Unveiling something or be using magic that very directly clashes in order for Clashes of Wills to trigger against Veiling magic. Some people may interpret Lucky Number as Clashing against relevant Veiling magic, but I would not allow that in my games.

                                While I would probably allow the GPS thing, I think that randomly punching the keyboard to find Spirit weakness is outside the limits of what this spell can do. It's more about entering small pieces of data such as phone numbers and passwords, not entire paragraphs with detailed pieces of information regarding things under the purview of another Arcanum. The spell works on probability, so while you could argue that randomly typing out spirit weaknesses should be possible, I would counter with the argument that such a manipulation of probability is beyond the boundaries of the Practice of Ruling. Ruling is about minor alterations, not sweeping changes. Finding information about a spirit through random chance is either a Weaving or Patterning of Fate, in my opinion.

But even if you don't go with that interpretation, you may have another thing to fall back on: the Imago. Mages can do basically anything with magic. The key is: they have to know how to do it. A mage can create Vitae with magic. 99% of mages don't know what Vitae is, much less have studied in-depth enough to form a proper Imago that creates proper Vitae. A mage with casual knowledge about Vitae could probably create an imitation, but it wouldn't work like actual Vitae.

"Guy with black hair that shot a fireball at me last week" is probably not enough. You would need things like Shadow Name, maybe even associates, or last known location. One thing that trips mages up a lot is completely mundane solutions. Most mage players are completely gung-ho about using magic to solve everything that they often forget simple mundane techniques for hiding or spying work very well! Can't form an Imago to "randomly" locate someone if you barely know anything about them.

                                            Of course, maybe your players already know a lot about the bad guy. And if they know a decent amount of information, let Lucky Number find the bad guy. Mages are very good at finding information and blowing through most puzzles in a couple of turns. Just because they can find someone, though, doesn't mean it's not dangerous as hell for them to approach (especially if they're unprepared).

                                                        Here's my take on why Lucky Number can't do that:

                                                        Guessing a password/phone number/etc. is a straightforward probabilistic event. The thing you're guessing is (in theory) at least pseudorandom, lies within a well-defined sample space, and the act of guessing it has an immediate outcome (you're in or not, you reach the right phone or not, etc.). Conceptually it's not all that different from pouring a bucket of dice on the floor and hoping to get all sixes.

                                                        "Guessing" a location or a bane is none of those things. The thing you're trying to guess is not random, the sample space is ill-defined beyond (e.g.-) "probably somewhere on Earth," and the result must be verified by other means. Altogether you're talking about something much closer to divination than to rigging an RNG. Not that divination methods based on supposedly-random processes aren't very much a thing, but you need a higher practice (probably Weaving?) for that.
