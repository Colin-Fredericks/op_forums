    Chapter 2: Ripper Man(?)
    Part 2

    “All I’m saying is Tartarus probably sent us on this to try and get me killed,” Ash remarks as he wipes powder from his nose. “Guy has it out for me.”

    “I wonder why?” Kismet ‘asks.’

    “Yeah, you would take his side despite going along with it all, wouldn’t you?” Ash grouses before taking a swig from a flask.

    The elevator they’re riding dings and opens, and the cabal exits into the grungy hallway of the third floor of an apartment building. As they reach the studio apartment Tartarus had told them about, it’s hard not to notice the faint red stains just beneath the door.

    “Whoah, what’s with all the-“ Midas opens the door to the studio apartment, revealing a place splattered in red, with a body pinned to the wall that’s been cut open from crotch to sternum “-blood,” Ash finishes after a second’s pause. He reaches into a pocket, to the small pouch of powder there, and takes another snort.

    There are two mages already present, both dressed far too professionally for the work they are doing, as the blood-splatters on their clothing show. They give the simple lowdown: they’ve been following the Ripper, mostly through an unreliable combination of precognition and resonance-tracking, but somehow the two things keep ending up running them in the wrong directions as much as the right.

    Midas and Ash are offering their own perspectives on it when Kismet speaks up. “He’s still alive,” she says.

    Everyone in the room immediately stops talking, and shares a series of horrified looks. The body isn’t breathing. The clearly-visible organs aren’t pumping, but at the same time, they now notice, pallor isn’t draining from the ‘dead’ man’s face, and his eyes haven’t gone glassy in their frozen stare of horror. The man isn’t there, his soul is also gone, but somehow those connections still remain.

    “I think I can…” Kismet trails off as she sets a hand on the victim’s forehead, her eyes suddenly focusing on something far in the distance, trying to follow everything that man is missing to the source. Her mind trails into the gaping chest cavity, into the empty non-spot his essential spark would’ve been, until suddenly…

    …it comes out the other side. The landscape is protean. The skyline is a mess of strange buildings and odd landscape formations, cascading from one into another with a strange transparency effect. The skies themselves are empty, a blackness that the horizon seems to be bleeding into, evaporating into.

    At the edge of everything on the verge of the abyss, is a pillar of pale green light. It bends perspective—it doesn’t change size in Kismet’s view even as her perceptions get close to it, going from a thick thing dominating a chunk of the horizon to a column a little wider than a person, and it stretches so high into the darkness that it seems to bend overhead when looking up at it. Around it stand four people in green cloaks. In the hands of one is a ball of shifting light. The cloaked person squeezes, and massages, and it distorts and grows into the shape of a man—the same man, Kismet’s recognizes, that’s sitting torn-open.

    The other cloaked figures place their hands on the soul-shape of the not-dead man, and he flails ineffectually in their grasp. “Where am I?” he screams. “Is this hell?!” As if in response, something inside the pillar strains, human outlines pressing against the insides of that shifting light, and Kismet realizes just where the undulations on its surface are coming from, and starts seeing it all: the skull-like screaming faces in it, the arms stretching out from it, only to be reabsorbed into the mass. And as she watches, the soul-shape is pressed into it and is pulled into it, howling in terror, by a score of desperate, grasping hands.

    The leader who’d been handling the soul-shape—or who she assumed was the leader, based on the intricate golden filigree on the robe’s cuffs and hems—dusts their hands off. “Good work,” he tells his associates in a deep, masculine voice. "Especially you, Miwok," he says to one who clinks when they move.

    The leader suddenly freezes “That’s… familiar,” He remarks to himself. The other three figures turn to look at him, and Kismet sees their faces: two women, one man. One of the women has chains around her face--Miwok. “Not since…”

    Slowly, the leader turns, and Kismet sees his face. Young, handsome. Sharp features. An ashen-grey color to his skin, like a corpse. His eyes are a deep, unnatural green, and as they turn towards where Kismet is viewing, she sees them begin to blaze, sockets dimming as pinpricks of green fire begin to burn in them. His mouth hangs open in shock for a moment, and there’s no doubt in Kismet’s mind that he’s looking directly at her.

    “After all this time,” the man says, lifting a hand to reach for her. Instantly his expression goes wild, and his mouth twists into a thin rictus grin. “Found you.”

        Chapter 2: Ripper Man(?)
        Part 3

        The blackness of those eyes expands further, drawing Kismet in, until all the universe is just those two small green fires. “Come back to me,” she hears “Come to Malthus.” She’s drifting deeper, and there’s a pressure in her chest, a light guttering out, and then-

        “That’s enough of that!” Ash’s voice cuts through it all.

        The world snaps back, and she’s falling away, falling out of that darkness, and before the hellscape around her vanishes into the distance she sees the face of the leader—Malthus—twisted in fury. When Kismet comes back to herself, she’s on her back in the apartment, suddenly gasping for air. It feels like somebody has snapped on her diaphragm like a rubber band. “Wha-“ is all she makes out before falling into a coughing fit.

        “I broke the connection,” Kismet hears Ash say. Midas kneels over her, looking apologetic, and adds “And I sorta… punched your soul back in place.”

        “And we need to get the fuck out of here,” Ash remarks. The two Guardians are already prepping to leave, casting spells to wipe away the blood and the evidence. “Call went out to the PD a second ago—the second your chest began lighting up.”

        “Flatterer,” Kismet dryly remarks as she gets back to her feet, rubbing her throat. Ash rolls his eyes, and stalks towards the door.

        Ash can already hear the sirens approaching by the time they hit the narrow alley through the fire escape, his skill over Forces carrying further than the doppler effect would normally allow. He stifles the sudden urge to giggle at how utterly insane what just happened was.

        “Hell of a series of events, right?” Ash remarks over his shoulder to Midas and Kismet. He says it just in time for the manhole cover he’s walking over to blow open and send him sprawling to the ground. It sends a gout of steam into the air which quickly fills the alley, refusing to disperse properly. He’s already scrambling when he hits the ground, pulling himself up on his elbows and glancing frantically up and down.

        They all spot it at the same time. Its skin ripples, adopting a kaleidoscope of colors and tones, and there’s the distinctive sensation from the way its body seems to undulate in place that the form it’s currently wearing is temporary. For the moment though, it’s noticeably feminine. The thing is nude, and its arms and legs are bending at the elbow, making it cling to one of the alley walls. The sunken eyes, the wild hair, and its current pose scream ‘Exorcist.’

        Dumbstruck, Ash yells the first thing that comes to mind: “Is it weird I feel this is kinda hot?!”

        Despite his sexual confusion, it’s not hard to decide on a course of action. Immediately, Ash draws Winona—his pistol—and fires. The bullet catches the Ripper in the stomach. All that does it make it angry, and to his special sight he can see the beginnings of what must be an imago. He braces himself for what pain might come, and…

        Ash’s clothes fall empty around a chicken. It looks around, then begins to run and flail in panic. The thing—the Ripper—leaps.

        Midas reacts more smartly. He raises an arm and clasps his hands together, and a small geyser of sewer-water pulls itself from the open manhole. It and the steam condense around the Ripper, forming into a cocoon. The cocoon hardens into a dense, reddish-orange powder, entombing the Ripper in a packed shell of capsaicin.

        The effort, which creative, only slows the monster down for a second. It bursts from the powder like someone who’d been buried in the sand, skin a burning red and pulsing, and makes for the Moros. Somehow, though, its feet and hands (which are now feet-hands), stumble over each other, and the Ripper goes careering face-first into the side of a dumpster. Kismet glares at it, hands in the form of a mudra.

        This distraction is enough to break its concentration, and suddenly Argentum Everto is back in the alley, naked and very shaken up. “I am a Nazi chicken!” he yells out. Even still collecting his wits, he instinctively rolls and grabs Winona from his pile of clothes, and this time when he pulls the trigger fire explodes from the barrel, lighting the Ripper on fire.

        The Ripper screeches, gouging chunks of metal off the dumpster as it rights itself. Its head turns independently of its shoulders, and its sunken eyes glare murder at the cabal. Despite this, it dives for the manhole, its pulsing form beginning to suppurate in places, and its head still on fire. The monster vanishes beneath the street, leaving the three mages confused and concerned.

        As they have a change to collect themselves, the cabal hears what it is they’d forgotten all about in the scuffle: the police sirens. Ash scoops up his clothes and sprints for the edge of the alley. The light around him shimmers, bending until he’s no longer visible.

        “I’ll run ahead!” Ash yells behind as the other two mages run for the opposite exit of the alley. “I’ll tell Tartarus!” he promises, streaking naked and unseen through the city streets.

            This might be a spoiler, but here's a thing about the Ripper: After this, it never got mentioned again. A part of me feels that's fair - after all, the Ripper was this terrifying murderbeast; why would rank apprentices be sent after it? - but another part of me is sad it was never given more depth. Then again, by that point, the game wasn't terribly far from dying, so it's not surprising.

            I did later go back and invest more time in the Ripper as a concept in one of the solo game spin-offs from this, retroactively adding more detail to it. I won't go into the why of it yet, since that'd give away spoilers, but Malthus didn't have his named disciples until much later on, except for the Ripper.

            Eventually, we ended up with the Third Disciple, Ripper of Essential Form; the Fifth Disciple, Wandering Dark Star for Whom the Void is Reserved; the Ninth Disciple, [Name Redacted]; and the Twelfth Disciple, Miwok, the Burdened Scribe of the Rite of Carrion. When we get more detail on Malthus, I'll do my own post on each of them.

            ---

            Something else I want to add: Games finding their feet. A lot of an early game is finding who the characters are and what the tone of the game is. The more experience you get with this stuff, the less time it takes, but there really is a lot of time spent with both the players and the ST figuring out who the characters are. Early on in this, I was pulling a lot of stuff from The Phantom for Kismet, Indiana Jones for Midas, and I am pretty sure the reason [REDACTED] happened with Ash is because Biston and I were both watching Farscape and reading Transmetropolitan around this time.

            It's way easier to get a mental image of Ash if you picture Spider Jerusalem running around naked.

            Nowadays, games tend to find their feet faster because I do way more groundwork on them, but even so, there's a lot of feeling out, especially if you're a player, to get to know the characters.

                        Chapter 4: Ripper Man(?)
                        Part 4


                        By the time Kismet and Midas return to speak with Tartarus, he, Ash, and Orcus are already in a shouting match, exchanging incredulities, insults, and passionate attempts to get things back on track (respectively). Ash hasn’t changed back into his clothes, apparently feeling this news was more important than his dignity. When the two mags approach, Tartarus storms off, pausing for a second to offer them a nod and a perfunctory “good job.” Ash continues to solidify his position on everyone’s shitlists by giving Tartarus a parting finger, which earns him what he’s sure is a glare from Orcus.

                        “Ash, if you don’t calm down, I will take away your ‘happy powder,’” Orcus threatens, with more than a little strain. Ash huffs at him, but doesn’t challenge the Councilor—the man looks old enough to turn him into sand, and the last thing he wants is for his stash to suddenly be turned into drywall. “I want to talk to you before you go,” Orcus continues, then departs before Ash can think of some excuse to not do so.

                        Midas clears his throat to try and get Ash’s attention. Ash waves over his shoulder, bending over to rifle through the pile of clothes on the floor beside him.

                        Kismet grimaces. “Could you please put your pants back on?” she asks.

                        “Sure thing, stormtrooper,” Ash grumbles, stopping his search for whatever to pull his pants from the pile and tug them back on. A second later he pulls what he was searching for out of his pocket: a packet of cigarettes.

                        ~ ~ ~

                        “According to Tartarus, he put Malthus in the ground decades ago. Stabbed a spear through his heart,” Ash says, lighting a cigarette with a burning thumb. “A Tremere, pretty powerful, and with a hell of a cult of personality. From what I picked up on, Tartarus was taking care of some other people in green cloaks while we were following up on the Ripper.”

                        The cabal has taken a seat in a conference room with a small rectangular table and an unused wall-length whiteboard.

                        Kismet frowns. “The sign says ‘no smoking,’” she says. Ash blows a cloud of smoke into her face, which she doesn’t react to.

                        “Anyway,” Ash continues, “they were saying things the two of them really wished they hadn’t around me. Got very upset when I suggested we could do something to help.” He frowns. “There was plenty they weren’t saying, but I picked up on some of it. Tartarus thinks Malthus is trying to build a… well…” Ash leans in, whispering. “A Ladder.” He shifts uncomfortably.

                        A silence falls over the three of them. Ash’s eyes dart expectantly between the two of them, looking for a reaction.

                        “It’s not our business,” Midas finally declares, though he doesn’t look committed to it. “I know you might have reservations, Ash, but I’ve picked at things I’m not suited for, and-“

                        “I agree,” Ash interrupts, and Midas exhales. “I don’t know much, but… this is really far above what we’re good for. And it has nothing to do with us,” he insists.

                        “Right. Nothing to do with us,” Kismet echoes, staring off into nothing.

                        Ash falls back into his seat, spouting twin gouts of smoke from his nose. “Oh thank God, I was afraid one of us was going to be stupid about it.”

                        There’s a moment of awkward silence, all three of them knowing their business is concluded but that they have no common ground to make small-talk with. Finally, Midas speaks.

                        “We should find a place to work,” Midas blurts out. Ash gives a curt nod. They both look towards Kismet.

                        Kismet sighs. “Fine, I’ll see what I can… see,” she offers, awkwardly.

                        As Ash stands to go have his chat with Orcus, Kismet resumes off into space. She thinks of the man who claimed to be Malthus, unable to forget those empty eyes or the green glow in their depths. The resurrected master building some horrible bridge between heaven and earth. ‘Come back to me,’ she hears again in her head, and she can imagine the Tremere sitting on the edge of oblivion, a writhing pillar to his side at he stares into the absolute ends of everything. He’d be wearing a thin-lipped smile on his corpse-skin face at their recent encounter, having found whatever it was he’d been looking for in her.

                        “Nothing to do with us,” Kismet says again, as Midas watches her with concern

                        "Adelaide was asking about you," Midas says, and Kismet focuses on that, doing her best not to imagine what the Tremere's laugh would sound like.

                        I would include Ash's chat with Orcus, but I remember little of it, and what I do remember is that overall it was just Orcus trying to convince Ash of the worthwhileness of Ash's own order. It provided some insight into the characters, but to read and play it was just several hours of Orcus going "come on" and Ash responding with canned iconoclasy--not fun to read or reconstruct from memory. What did come out of it was Ash actually trusting Orcus more, but there are other places to showcase that.

                        What is on display here is Ash's contempt for his own cabal, a running element in this chronicle and beyond. At this juncture, he at least has some respect for Midas as someone who has accomplished things and has a visible moral compass, but Kismet... well, Kismet never had a chance. She was an outlet for his hatred of mage society, focusing on her affiliations as a Guardian--views put in his head by Goch, a man whose father and father's associates were affiliated with actual Nazis and were directly and indirectly responsible for the difficulties of Ash's formative years.

                            I am the Midas in this story.

                            At the encouragement of Biston and against my better judgment, I decided to make an account and throw some of my perspective into this bubbling cauldron of weirdness. I think Biston prefaced that he’s telling his version of the story with some retcons that were appropriate to Ash’s continued solo adventures. I’ll probably end up doing the same, but I don’t think any of these changes are major. From what I’ve seen of Biston’s adjustments so far, his changes just smooth out minor wrinkles.

                            Let’s give it a go.

                            Biston’s description of Midas in Chronicle I is accurate. Midas, real name David Snow, was a teacher who Awakened as an alchemist and joined the Mysterium under Adelaide’s tutelage. He’s my first Mage character and really my first attempt at a World of Darkness experience, period. Biston was also right about struggling to nail down themes and figuring out how to make this all work in a game with a cabal.

                            Blood and Spiders seems like it had some red flags. I really enjoyed the adventure and still think fondly of it, even if I know better that wizarding usually doesn’t work this way. Like Biston said, Midas was actually the first of the cabal to be created; Axelgear ran this as a solo game for me for a while before bringing in Biston and our other player. I think this explains some of the design choices made on that adventure. By this point, Midas had been adventuring enough that he had some reasonably established themes, and one of those themes was a cavalier Indiana Jones adventuring style. Most of his work in the Mysterium has been artifact-hunting, and by the time this game started, I think he’d had at least two major adventures in that vein: uncovering an ancient alchemist’s laboratory near the library of Alexandria and tracking down a similarly ancient monastery in Tibet. Both were great fun, globetrotting adventures, and gallivanting off to Central America to recover an artifact from a Mayan temple was right in line with this. Midas had taken on the Victorian-era explorer look for wizardly meetings because of these travels; dashing coat, sturdy boots, even a fancy cane that he enchanted to fight with if he had to.

                            When they came out of it, though, Midas’ spirit was really at odds with Ash’s. All the fear and blood dusted off of Midas amid the joy of discovery, and he came out of the portal all smiles. Ash, though, was the one who ended up vomiting/crying/bleeding spiders, and I think part of the disconnect was that I as a player just forgot that part by the end. When Ash went off angrily, rightly traumatized by what had happened, it was too late to act more sober. Still, we tried to make it seem friendly enough that the three of them had a good reason to stick together, and the cabal tentatively took shape.

                            We know in hindsight now that things don’t usually go this way with wizards in general or the Mysterium in particular, but I found the adventure fun at the time and sure wasn’t gonna retcon it away. I remember hoping for the best at the time. Ash caught on that Midas was good with making things, and Midas agreed to make some parts for his magic machine. Seemed like a sign things might be okay.

                            Things weren’t okay.

                                Interlude IV: Dream Machine


                                When Ash tells Pathos about his dreams and their vaguely-prophetic bend, she immediately takes interest. The look on her face is one he’s seen before, on the faces of his former colleagues; men and women who’ve found something to bite into, to pull apart and learn from.

                                “So they guided you to the club?” Pathos presses one night as they’re leaving after shutdown.

                                “I like to think they brought me to you,” Ash replies with unwitting earnestness, scratching at the red ring left on his forehead by his frizzy-haired wig. It’s become easy to rely on her, even over just a few months, and even these strange moments of openness have been becoming comfortable to him for the first time in his life.

                                Pathos smiles at it, and thankfully doesn’t acknowledge it any further. Instead, she proposes something.

                                And that is how Ash found himself strapped around his chest to a padded wooden chair. “I didn’t figure you for the type,” he jokes.

                                Pathos snorts. “Don’t you start,” she says. “I want to see how your brain works when under like this,” she explains as she polishes Ash’s temples and attaches two discs to the sides of his head. “Witcher wouldn’t break us into the hospital, so I’m improvising. These things will monitor and record, and I’ll be doing one of my own using a spell and myself as a relay.” She ruffles his hair. “Try not to go insane in the middle of it.”

                                Ash frowns slightly, and swallows his instinct to ask ‘who’s Witcher?’ He finds something else to talk about. “We could try to build an EEG machine,” he says.

                                Pathos quirks a brow at him.

                                “What? We have friends. could get some to make materials, we could easily make the right tools, download or divine plans…” Ash trails off, now down a road he’s never thought on before. “Hell, what couldn’t the right spells build?” he wonders. He stiffens for a moment. “A bunch of college kids made a working nuke frame,” he remembers.

                                Pathos stares at him for a second. “Now I’m going to have trouble sleeping. Thanks, Ash,” she remarks with a strained smile. “Now stop talking. I’m going to take a final check, turn this thing on, and then we’ll have you use the powder.”

                                Pathos drops a small purple pouch of off-white powder on the endtable beside Ash’s chair before turning to do a last-minute go-over and calibration on the two computers she’s set up for the sake of this project. When she turns back around a minute later, the entire pouch is empty, and Ash is sporting a heavy dust on his upper lip.

                                Suddenly, both of them can hear a pin drop. Pathos’s eyes are wide, staring at Ash. The small grin on Ash’s own face is beginning to fade.

                                “You were only supposed to have a pinch,” Pathos remarks.

                                “Oh,” Ash says, before the sensation of falling starts to overtake him. The last sight he has before the world fades is Pathos running to his side.

                                ~ ~ ~

                                The world fades away from him, but Ash keeps falling. The blackness around him twinkles with stars, a firmament littered with four-pointed diamonds, and that ripples and undulates and begins to give way. Colors bleed in, slowly at first, but eventually they roll past at immense speed, a rainbow that slowly blurs into white. It’s blinding.

                                Ash flexes his wings, and soars towards it. That light is in front of him. It’s at the end of the tunnel, and as he approaches it he watches it shrink, shivering before his approach. He hears the whine of exposed electricity, and as he does so an immense pain fills his chest. Loneliness, longing, regret. The pain of sin, the weight of guilt. It intensifies the closer he comes to his goal, the nearer he gets to holding the sun in the palm of his hand, all while it retreats into something smaller and smaller.

                                Tears stream down Ash’s cheeks. The memory of it, of all these things, His hand grasps. He screams in emotional anguish, and that weight on his chest explodes. Shards of obsidian tear free from his heart, forming a mushroom cloud of jagged glass that is stained with his blood. The sun explodes outwards as he closes his fingers on it. He feels heat and pain as it blows him past, singing away his body hair, his wings melting away in the fury of it all. Once again he is tumbling.

                                Ash looks to his chest, and the glass falls away to reveal a large phage. Even without eyes he can feel it looking at him. It leaps from his chest, and he watches it and the glass scatter as a crater before Olympus Mons. He tumbles through the volcano.

                                “You deserve this,” Ash hears himself tell himself before he crashes into the sky, into a sea of tass. Above him hangs a moon, and on it an oversized ziggurat. It bleeds shimmering rainbow liquid wastefully into space. Before he knows it, Ash is crawling towards that bloody bridge.

                                With reverence, with longing, with desperation, Ash reaches towards that impossibility with stained hands. He stumbles, weeping. When he looks back up, he’s in the jungle, at the foot of that temple. Beyond the treeline, he can feel them there—friends, Seers, spiders. He wipes away his tears, and as he stands the residual tass dries across him in whorling, runic patterns.

                                It still hurts. Ash’s body hurts, his heart hurts. Somehow, that feels fine now. It feels right to feel this way. It feels necessary.

                                “You deserve this,” Ash repeats to himself as he begins to climb the ziggurat.

Ash would have been damn well hard-pressed to have ever said it was going to but it feels like it has. There is the thought, however intelligent or silly it might seem, that some Asimov-style psychohistorian with sufficient mastery of the right Arcana might've pieced it together; enough outsider calculation to predict the world before it happened. Maybe it’s just a memory adding up from when he saw the mathematics in everything and realized how it could all, in fact, fit together. Not in the ways he ever expected, but it could.

                                                            With distant light shining across Winona’s new barrel, cut into a dozen different rainbow streams in Ash’s mage sight, he can feel the edge of understanding - of enlightenment - just at the reaches of his perception.

                                                            It has all led here. He can see the shadows of his equations in the sand before him as new numbers drift into being outwards from the Winona Mk. II. His dreams, his battles, his struggles.... He has seen spiders pour of his eyes, he has felt the horror of his family life suddenly clicking and making sense, has found friends... of a sort. People who can stand him, at least, and vice versa. His dreams of standing against the storm suddenly feel like they make sense. He can feel an... alignment. An alignment of the planets and the stars. A sensation that his next act will be portentous; a foundation stone for something bigger than he has ever imagined possible. The universe seems to hold its breath.

                                                            Argentum takes a deep drag off his breather, and telekinetically lifts the rocks from his pile. No more borrowing. It can't be that anymore. His symbols need to be his own. Furrows carve themselves across the regolith of Umbriel, rocks setting themselves in places along it. Slowly, Ash marks the surface with a small message.

                                                            There's too much that people can do, too much they have done to lie to the world like that, to treat the unaware like gawkers at some vulgar show. There's worlds for them to explore, to control, and to master. There's a future to build, and they deserve to see what they might build in it. With a bit of guidance, of course.

                                                            'Alone my ass,' Argentum mouths to no one, turning back to what rocks he hadn’t used. The small message sits in Umbriel’s dark surface. One day, absent magical interference, their probes and their telescopes will see it. And if the war isn't won by then, whatever wide-eyed and sharp-minded people will find it will see:

                                                            You are never alone. You can join us in the sun.

                                                            And Ash realizes, he is different. He’s shaped more than a gun, carved more than a message; these things have left their own indelible marks on him, and this one moment has shaped him. The vessel of his soul is different, and in it germinates a new legacy.

                                                            Ash is left in silent contemplation in the remaining moments on Umbriel. He doesn't know how long it will last, or whether this will be the place he spends what’s left of his life. Marx, when she comes around, might not be willing to risk another doozy of a Paradox like that. This could be how Ash ends his life. Worse ways to go, truthfully, and he’d like to think he’d be sure to cause an Awakening in a few decades time if/when a probe comes by and sees his naked corpse clutching a gun on the surface of a moon.

                                                            Come what may, his return or his death, nothing can take this away from him.

                                                            Suddenly, there is a faint shimmer before him and he sees those iron claws peeking through the gap in space. He sees ice form on their edges. Marx's face silently strains through the crack until Ash sees her force wide the gates once again. Waiting on the other side is not the welcome committee he had expected. Instead, there is Tartarus and four others, black keffiyehs obscuring their faces. They each are holding weapons, swords and long knives and guns with silencers. All are looking in his direction, weapons pointed towards him. Tartarus himself is at their head, two long cleavers clasped in his hands, and in a battle-ready stance.

                                                            Argentum stares at the sight presented to him, his face blank of any sort of reaction. He doesn't rush for the portal, doesn't beeline in. He takes a final breath from his breathing tank, and reassembles the rods and supports in it into a crude frame, on which he hangs the breathing mask, his shades, and his clothes like some sort of MiB scarecrow. Next, he slides the camera and the rocks through the portal Marx has opened.

                                                            One last time, Ash looks up towards the sun. That proves a small smile in him; it’s billions of miles away, and yet right here in front of him. As a final gesture, he snags some of the background noise of the universe and twists it; a timed message, garbled just enough that it could be noise, but too distinct to just be dismissed: We are with you, and we are proud of you.

                                                            With everything done, Ash hefts the last two remaining rocks. The two biggest, saved just for this moment. He hoists one over his shoulder, tucks the other near his hip, and steps through the portal.

                                                            Exposed before his peers, much like last time, Ash shouts.

                                                            "Look at the size of my stones! Am I not the sexiest man alive?!"
