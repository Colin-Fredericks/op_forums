      Originally posted by orathaic View Post
      So, I happen to like the Strix rather a lot, great piece of storytelling equipment for any Requiem game.

      But also I dislike the "they are actually human spirits, or whatever happened to human spirits..."
      The events HIJACK: The Writer's Guide to the Strix attributes to the departure of humanity from the Strix are so far back in the history of the real world that they pre-date the earliest point on Tome of the Pentacle's timeline that hasn't been erased from history by an order of magnitude, and that itself pre-dates the Sundering by an order of magnitude.

      Signs of Sorcery describes the "root peoples" in terms that make it plain that weird spiritual phenomena relating to human prehistory are far from unheard of, and Tome of the Pentacle likewise reiterates (by way of remnant Neanderthal populations) the reoccurring notion that spending long enough in the Shadow can turn a creature into a spirit. On the other end of the age gap, their weird relationship with time notwithstanding, the immortality-granting alien beings known as the Visitors have emerged/spawned at least as recently as the Trinity nuclear tests, where they were created as part of the event's impact on local spirits.

      What I'm getting at is that it's not exactly a wild or diminutive theory to have traction.

      So what if a Mage (Thyrsus) gets this idea of human spirits and the Strix, and in exploring and learning Death makes a legacy with Shadow Flesh as the capstone.

      Now the Spirit master has a plan, to explore Dis (in shadow form) and hopefully find the truth ...
      What we know about the Lower Depths and the Dark Places both establish that, unless you have a plot device for making it back to Earth, this is functionally an elaborate form of suicide to attempt for longer than a scene. Is the idea that this is part of a Threshold Seeking or did you have another method in mind for making this relevant beyond "a powerful mage wants to enter a place none return from"?

Dealing with the Lower Depths is rightly looked upon with suspicion by conventional Awakened society. Records of obscure knowledge come from somewhere, and that obscurity tends to lend weight to the names of the people who went to the trouble of finding it, particularly among people as prideful as mages.

                              Again, we're talking about the bogeymen of a more accessible supernatural culture that have naturalized themselves into the material world and sprung forth from a variety of sources, which may be able to access a realm of primordial darkness at the height of their power in conditions that preclude visually-oriented beings from seeing it do so.
