    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
    The thing is, I think what you're perceiving as misplaced emphasis is merely the result of the text being written from a Mage's perspective. Mages are presented as these awesome higher beings that everyone should want to be because that's how Mages want to see themselves. All you have to do is take a step back and look at it from a more objective standpoint and you see that they're terrifying, dangerous monsters. The Banishers are presented as insane for their self-loathing, but the thing is they're right. With their human Integrity, they have a clearer view of what Supernal magic really is than any Mage; they are the ones with the objective view, and what they see traumatizes the hell out of them, it scars their very souls. Mages are horrifying, and that horror is only made all the more frightening by the fact that they are so utterly oblivious to it.

    As an aside, I don't know why this is such a sticking point for so many people when it comes to Mage specifically. Every line presents its monsters as awesome. Vampire tells you "hey, you may be Damned, but at least you'll be young and beautiful for ever. Besides, you can't go to Hell if you never die!" Werewolf tells you "We're the righteous inheritors of a sacred duty to protect the world by maintaing the border between spirit and flesh." The books making it look awesome to be a monster is nothing new. The horror has always come in the form of that fridge logic "Wait... What are we doing?"
    Yeah, you know, that's kind of a good point. Vampire 2e, which I love, is written from a very pro-vampire perspective. I just feel like the audience is already accustomed to see the "curse" aspect of vampirism, whereas having magic powers and being Literally Neo isn't something people automatically perceive as monstrous, so it takes some more work to make it so.

    Also, a lot of the lines aren't nearly that positive. Changeling, Mummy, and Demon hit the notes of "this really kind of sucks but I guess you can make the best of it" that I'm looking for, and Promethean is honestly too negative about being a Created for me.

    I can see Geist, but how the hell could someone not see the horror in Changeling?

    And this isn't a new game, it's a hack. I still genuinely think that Mage: The Transgression would be a much better fit for ChroD, but even if I actually write it out that won't magically insert it into canon. I've decided it's easier to just look for a compromise that would let me enjoy and use the existing Mage material.

                            A setting document is purely a page or several detailing how you want to run/play the game you envision as an ST. For example, say I'm running a Mage game wherein I want the players to awaken during the game and not already be mages. To emphasise this, I might say that merits can't be swapped into gnosis and that order/consilia status can't be purchased during creation. Or, in another game, I might want to heavily emphasise that being a Mage is bollocks and that the abyss is always working to counter a Mage's work, so in my setting document I house rule that paradox is always a minimum of one dice and can't be reduced to chance die.

                            That being said, you should also make it clear in your setting document that you have a strong dislike for pretty much every core conceit of Mage and would much rather be playing a hybrid of Genius that emphasises how much being a Mage sucked. If I was uninformed and sat down at your table to play Mage, I'd likely leave the table once I realised your vision of Mage was nothing like the game I intended to join. If I was in the mood to play a game involving having everything I do end up with me sucking and weird science, I'd have looked for a Promethean game :P
