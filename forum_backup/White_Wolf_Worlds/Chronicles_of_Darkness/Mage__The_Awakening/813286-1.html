                Originally posted by ShadowKnight1224 View Post
                Right now, my tentative houserules is to start every single spell with baseline Paradox (so instead of a free Reach, you always risk Paradox no matter how casual the spell is). In 1e, I'd get rid of the Vulgar/Covert system and run all spells as automatically risking Paradox at all times. For Quiescence, I'd run it closer to Lunacy, with the idea that mortals with high Willpower (or that succeed at their Integrity roll) can actually remember what happened, and are likely to become Hunters. I might steal the 2e Lunacy rules and have dramatic failures in the Integrity roll bestow the Proximi minor template.

                EDIT: I also might tweak the Disbelief rules to make sure that mages can't feasibly cast anything that would have any long-term permanent consequences on humanity, like mind-controlling authority figures or any such shenanigans. I'll probably adjust Disbelief so that it can reasonably keep magic in check.
                I dunno about the omnipresent Paradox risk. It discourages the subtle approach to magic somewhat. I think making Paradox backlash deal resistant lethal instead of resistant bashing is already a big increase in the danger. I also disagree with giving the Proximi template as a result of an Integrity Breaking Point roll, as Proximi are almost always the result of heritage. I'm fine with giving the Sleepwalker Merit, but my hunch is that a Breaking Point roll isn't the best way to determine when to give it.

                Originally posted by ShadowKnight1224 View Post
                In terms of fluff, my baseline assumption is that mages are completely deluded about the nature of reality. Everything in the Mage books is true, when observed by a Mage. For everyone else, the Supernal Realms are nightmarish hell planes of soul-murder, and the Abyss is the stalwart guardian of humanity against the incursions of the aforementioned planes. Since humanity has a sympathetic connection to the Abyss, I reasoned that the Abyss is probably a very good thing for humanity. I also have Abyssal entities consider regular humans to be kin, to drive this point home. Where Mages see their souls as having "awakened into the truth", everyone else sees their souls as missing a vital component, which was replaced by a shard of the hellish realms. Paradox, then, is the Abyss trying to protect humanity from the Supernal soul-murder.

                Of course, Exarchs are still the bad guys (because they use the Supernal to actively oppress humanity) and Mad Ones are still terribly dangerous, but even Pentacle mages are hubristic and misguided. Banishers are the closest thing to "heroic mages", from humanity's perspective, and Scelesti are... complicated.

                This viewpoint emphasises an aspect of hubris that I thought wasn't terribly well explored in either edition of mage (though I'd have to read 2e properly to say for sure): the idea that hubris blinds. That mages are convinced that their delusions about reality are true, and that their hubris blinds them to the idea that they might be wrong, that their actions may not, in fact, be justified. That maybe humanity, in its powerlessness and sympathy for the Abyssal, might be right. That way, the personal horror for Mage is less "the price of power" and more "holy shit what if I am the monster???"
                While I am very fond of giving reality checks to Mages and other supernaturals who never bother to put themselves in another's shoes, defining the Abyss as a guardian of anything is a really big stretch.

                All of that being said, I still like your post.

                Bye.

                                                            Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                                                            Narrative question, typical in magic related fiction - The Sleeper comes to character and says:" I believe in magic. And I know you are mage/witch/wizard."

                                                            Question: What now, in Mage setting?
                                                            It depends on a lot of things, to be perfectly frank. Sleepers now apparently are allowed to be "in the know" about Supernal magic, but still trigger Dissonance. I'm personally still fuzzy on a lot of details on the difference between a Sleeper and Sleepwalker. Not the mechanical difference, the story/background of it. What the line that makes that "abyssal shard" go away. In 1e, I would have said that your Sleeper was either 1) lying to themselves (want to believe, but actually don't), or 2) is actually a budding Sleepwalker.

                                                            In either case, my suggestion would be to put them through the local Msytery Cult and see if they can Awaken on the path. Some of the more secrecy oriented mages (GotV?) might just deny it, and walk away, try to get away, since they don't want someone paying attention for magic and potentially causing Dissonance at a bad time.



                                                            Now, as much as DaveB has been trying to make magic as "objective" as possible, Dissonance is still very much a subjective thing. It still relies on a magically enforced Doubting Thomas effect. They see something magical, the Dissoance kicks in, and suddenly they are dissapointed because the so-called magic is clearly faked, because that's how Dissonance works. Makes you doubt.

                                                            Now, in your crystal ball gazer example? What makes you so sure the fortune teller doesn't have an attainment that lets them fortell the future without risk of Paradox or Dissonance? Personally, I would say that a Sleeper that came in to see a non-Legacy fortune teller, obstinably to see the future, would invoke Dissonance if the reading was too accurate, making any more spellcasting dangerous.
