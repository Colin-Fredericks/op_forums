            Keep in mind there are guidelines for Creative Thaum-ing published spells. For example, changing just Primary Factor is a no-no. It is reasonable to conclude that just adding a Reach effect that was intentionally excluded when the spell was written would be disallowed as well. Especially because, as I mentioned before, they explicitly went through and removed a lot of Lasting effects in the errata. That was a conscious choice on the part of the developers, and differs from adding something they just might not have thought of (eg. a Reach Effect for Speak with the Dead allowing you to speak with Mages in Death Twilight)

            That said, it Creative Thaumaturgy does encourage a degree of messing with published spells - the system encourages you to try to accomplish effects with alternate Arcana, but if you want a Lasting effect on a non-Lasting spell you need to reimagine how the spell accomplishes its goals, in a way that is compatible with the rules regarding what spells should be Lasting.

            A good example of how to envision things like this is Ghost Gate. The default, portal-style spell is designed in such a way that the effect would (arguably, lets not have that argument here) be Lasting. The spell creates a portal that shifts things that pass through it into Twilight. When the spell ends, the portal goes away. The side effects of the spell (things that went through are in Twilight) do not, just as the ground a fire burns doesn't become unburnt when the magical fire goes away.

            The reach effect works completely differently - it directly shifts a subject into Twilight, so when the spell ends, the subject would cease being in Twilight.

            If you want to make a non-Lasting spell Lasting, the process should be more in-depth than just slapping on two Reach and calling it good. Figure out how to accomplish the same effect in a Lasting manner, and do that. Sometimes the result will be an alteration of the published spell, sometimes it will be something brand new.

                                    The issue with adding Lasting to published spells is that all of the writers had that as an option. They used it where they felt it was appropriate, to the point that the devs then had to go through and remove instances where it was used incorrectly.

                                    It is very reasonable to operate on the understanding that, among the published spells, if it's meant to be Lasting, or to have a Reach for it, it does. Some spells are described in a way that they would have Lasting effects without actually saying they're Lasting. See Ghost Gate, Shaping, Corridors of Time.

                                    Others have the Reach for it.
