    Making life challenging for Mages

    06-20-2023, 11:22 AM
    I normally hang around the Werewolf Forms, but I have a keen interest in all the Chronicles of Darkness games. I wanted to ask how to make life…challenging… for Mages. I get that Mages are arguably the most powerful entities in the Chronicles of Darkness, but I imagine that they must have some weaknesses. I am sorry if this thread was done to death in the past but I am curious

    1/ Mages are Squishy: The most obvious point is that Mages are just ordinary people when you look at it and break easy. Sure they can get Mage armor, but some of the mage armors are tougher than others and using Mana to heal is very inefficient compared to other splats.

    2/ Mages need to prepare: Mages are virtual demigods when they have time to prepare with rituals, but when caught unprepared they are vulnerable

    3/ Mages have blind spots: My impression is that Mages can usually do some things really well, but are not that useful in others. For instance, a Mage with a high value in the Death Arcana would be great against Ghosts, but pretty hopeless when faced with a Spirit.

    4/ Mages need Mana: My impression is that Mana is mainly used to prevent paradox due to reach (correct me if I am wrong), and that Mages can burn through it quickly through spell casting. They also need it for Healing (however limited). However, if they burn through their Mana reserves they are forced to confront Paradox which might blow up in their faces, or scourge their bodies which is very unhealthy.

    Please let me know if I am missing anything.

      Any good mage is certain to make their own life difficult through their own actions by merit of being mages - whether through the consequences of pursuing their Obsessions and being rewarded with arcane beats for making breakthroughs with them via doing dangerous or dubious stuff, being drawn into figuring out dangerous Mysteries and becoming involved in competition with other mages or supernatural communities, or through acts of Hubris when they get too comfortable or risk-prone with their magic.

              Originally posted by maryshelly View Post
              2/ Mages need to prepare: Mages are virtual demigods when they have time to prepare with rituals, but when caught unprepared they are vulnerable
              I wouldn't say they need to prepare so much as preparation can be highly beneficial to them. Like, the right assemblage of Yantras and the time to make the ritual casting extensive, or the correct rote, can let them cover a lot of the bases that they want in a spell. But instant casting is always a possibility if necessary (and in an emergency, is likely to be the only thing you need to spend Reach on, so not necessarily a Paradox risk), and even if the dice pool is small there are a lot of impromptu situations where the factors don't need to be too high (especially if they get some steps in their primary factor for free). Besides, High Speech always affords a +2 dice bonus that can be utilised in practically every spell, albeit with the complications of losing the reflexive benefit of one's first Yantra and the likelihood of drawing attention from a Sleeper.

              Really, I'd say being unprepared is less a matter of the mage being especially vulnerable (although I've heard accounts of how players can just plain panic when caught off guard and forget about some of the effective countermeasures their Arcana might afford them, which seems to work very well in character) and more that there's higher risk of doing something that counts as an Act of Hubris. Getting one of the Conditions that comes with a failed roll there can cause... complications. As well as making the decision to contain potential Paradox more threatening (although the Condition you risk from it will last less time).

              Originally posted by maryshelly
              3/ Mages have blind spots: My impression is that Mages can usually do some things really well, but are not that useful in others. For instance, a Mage with a high value in the Death Arcana would be great against Ghosts, but pretty hopeless when faced with a Spirit.
              Really, I'd ask first if the mage even has an Arcanum at an especially high level; given the Gnosis requirements to go above three dots, let alone all the way to five, it takes a lot of effort to become especially powerful as a mage.

              But sure, not every individual character can cover all of their bases, not every player group will have the full spread of Arcana, and while two or three dots are quite versatile they hardly cover every eventuality or let them exert themselves to the fullest.

              Originally posted by maryshelly
              4/ Mages need Mana: My impression is that Mana is mainly used to prevent paradox due to reach (correct me if I am wrong), and that Mages can burn through it quickly through spell casting. They also need it for Healing (however limited). However, if they burn through their Mana reserves they are forced to confront Paradox which might blow up in their faces, or scourge their bodies which is very unhealthy.
              I think one of the greater drains on mana might be casting improvised spells from outside one's Ruling Arcana, and it also provides a lot of use when scrutinising with Focused Mage Sight. Still, I think mana is primarily a matter of long term logistics; casting safely within one's Ruling Arcana is probably often straightforward enough, unless something particularly dire catches them by surprise.

              I think the major things that should be complications for the Awakened would be:

              * An assumption that they're generally going to want to understand Mysteries rather than treat them as things to break down, which can mean continuous contact with something that can be kind of hazardous, or at least make their lives more complicated.

              * Being involved with scenarios where there are a lot of moving parts, several of which involve people, in which there's not exactly some simple solution to line things up as they wish that won't cause a major disruption. Even if the characters/players are not very concerned with the ethics of that, such behaviour should risk their Wisdom (with attendant complications as mentioned before) and piss off other mages.

              * Other mages have their own claims on something that the player characters are interested in, or that are in the way of something they're interested in. Other spells, Consilium procedure and law, and simply the personality and agendas of those other mages can be something that players crash into in a manner that can cause blowback for them. You want to examine the ghosts in a graveyard, but there's a powerful Veiling over the place preventing that. You try dispelling it, and that's not just a crime against the mage who cast it and their cabal, it causes problems when something that wasn't supposed to be able to look at the graveyard now has a clear view of it. Maybe be cautious about any of your own critical long-term spells being abruptly broken in future.

              Besides all of that, I'd say that there are sometimes supernatural powers in the world where it's not entirely apparent what kinds of Arcana and Practices might provide countermeasures for what it might do to you or others, and even then the Clash of Wills roll might favour them. Plus, well... a lot of supernatural powers can be deployed more quickly and hit harder than an Awakened spell can in short order; there's no fiddling around with factors and Reach, no balancing bonuses and penalties to get all the moving parts in order, there's just an instant action, a strength based on number of successes rolled, and a tendency towards large dice polls to potentially afford a lot of them. And we've got enough examples of how exception based Mysteries can be that one can have cases where walking into the heart of something strange and fascinating to examine it can severely impair something that might be critical to a character.

              Never mind the likes of ruins of the Time Before, which seem to be built around an ethos of "Indiana Jones traps where the trigger isn't stepping into the light, it's using certain spells" and are structurally a bit proofed against the kinds of spells that would let one sequence break, before even considering the powerful guardian entity within that will have both Influence and Arcana and a free option to be either physical or ephemeral.

              Don't get me wrong, the capacity and versatility of Arcana are supposed to be things that let mages have their say or work their will in quite a lot of normal situations. It's just that being Addicted to Mystery inevitably draws them away from their comfort zones and across boundaries where the certainty of their power is less assured, for any number of reasons.

              Don't think of it as looking for the mage bane. Think of it as the things that might slap them across the face in their own particular idiom if mages get too close without proper precautions, and the consequences of their bad decisions (and how good decisions can be limiting in a way that frustrates and tempts).

                          I glanced over the rules to check what might happen.

She just needs to sacrifice 10 dice to overcome the rank 5 potency, but still doable at 6 dice. And all she needs to do is add 2 reach to issue a complex command, plus probably a bit to increase duration, but she will essentially have a rank 5 spirit to command?

                          That is pretty crazy, did I miscalculate anything? Yes, I agree, that would certainly ruin a Werewolf game
                          Casting from a grimoire doubles the casting time, which means your hypothetical god-leashing Disciple of Spirit needs to get their target to stay within her sensory range for ten hours for this brilliant plan to work. (If she doesn't know the rote, she can't use Scribe Grimoire to make a grimoire to get the rote quality on the casting in the first place.)

                          If she's casting from across the Gauntlet without an open Iris to the Shadow, the Withstand for targeting a Rank 5 spirit needs Potency that ends up at more than 0 after subtracting 6 (and that's another Reach besides).

                          The needs of the dicepool can be reduced somewhat by spending a Reach to switch the primary spell factor from Duration to Potency, but that's trading more dice for more potential for complications.

                          The +2 Reach effect indicates that Spirit Summons is the spell we're talking about here, which means that the complex command is a single task described within a sentence or two; if you want to do a lot of different things with this near-godlike spirit, you need another casting or a different spell — the spell for "make a spirit do as you tell it to" is Command Spirit, which is Potency-primary but only allows you to command the spirit to perform a number of tasks equal to Potency (when Withstand reduces effective Potency, all the casting-time concerns for Spirit Summons still apply, and there's no inbuilt Reach option for casting across the Gauntlet, meaning you have to go to the thing or deal with the fact that casting on a target you can see across the Gauntlet costs 2 Reach for moving from Touch/Self range to Sensory range and casting remotely.)

                          Add to this the fact that most Rank 5 spirits out in the wild are not solitary creatures casually discovered on the street and it is reasonable to say the amount of legwork necessary for this nominally simple act of spellcasting to work without issue is the stuff whole stories are built out of.
