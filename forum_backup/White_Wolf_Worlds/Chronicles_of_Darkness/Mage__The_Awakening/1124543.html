              I ran a game that was set in a consilia next to London. I worked out a vague outline for the city not based on any other material, the idea being to contrast it to the comparatively quiet consilia the game was set in. The players never went there (and that was partly the point of the way I wrote it), but it loomed over the setting.

              To summarise, I figured that London would be a major centre for Seers, to the point that they have the numbers to be an open threat to the Pentacle. As a result my consilium was based less on regulating access to mysteries and more about co-ordinating the war effort as the place pulled in large numbers of Guardians and Arrows. There wasn't open warfare (generally), but more a frozen conflict between the two sides.

              Tourists were pretty much non-existent because you ran the very real risk of wandering into Seer territory and being murdered/disappeared and the local Pentacle were pretty unwlecoming and very authoritarian.

              The minor detail that really influenced my game was that as a result of the long running tensions, the Mysterium didn't keep any athenaea of note in London. Anything valuable was shipped out to neighbouring consilia where it wouldn't be under immediate threat. So the consilia I ran had an athenaeum far out of proportion to the size of the population.

              I didn't have Shadows of the UK at the time, but I had the local tetrachy call itself the Shadow Cabinet, seemed like the obvious pun.

                  I grew up in London so I use it as a setting often for my WoD games especially Mage for nearly twenty years. The way i have presented it has changed a lot over the years including new inspirations for media and fiction. And i have never run a "standard" game i.e. my version of Mage has always been more like Dresden and Buffy with Mages being focused on protecting humanity against dark forces rather than some of the more high concept aspects of both Mage the Ascension and Awakening.

                  All that being said here are some major elements that influence my version of London and some resources i haven't really incorporated but could be used by others who have a different approach to their campaigns.

                  1 WoD - There is some material in NWoD Shadows of UK (although not as much as one might expect for UK's capital city!) and in the Second edition of MtAw. Also some material in the old CtDr source book Isle of the Mighty dealing with the UK in general for that setting but also with elements of MtAsc. Also some interesting material from the dark ages line both in Dark Ages British Isles and in the two Dark Ages mage books. Obviously this is historical but good stuff to draw on for background of forces that could be brought into a modern age London/UK game.

                  2 Other RPG's - Fantasy Flight games has an odd but interesting game called Fireborn about Dragons in the modern world and connecting to an ancient mythic age (including a version of Atlantis!) through roleplayng flashbacks. the modern setting is modern London and so has lots of interesting background. The GM guide is especially useful. There are a variety of historical London settings especially for Cthulhu all of which provide a good overview often older parts of London (which is lots of it!) including supernatural inspiration and are good background for both modern games and obviously Dark Eras games. The best ones i have found are from Cubicle 7's Cthulhu Britannica and World War Cthulhu lines which include an entire London box set for the 1930's and a London book for the city during WWII. Cthulhu by gaslight also covers lots of London background from the Victorian era.

                  3 Other media - Obviously Neil Gaiman's Neverwhere, either the book or the TV show. I have used lots of inspiration from the idea of a London Below where spirits, Changelings, mages and other supernatural beings exist in a parallel spiritual realm adjacent to mundane London and connecting to te names of prominent place names and tube station names in the London Underground (Earl's Court being a Changeling Court, Black Friars being a group of Lancea Sanctum Vampires, All the various 'gates" being gates to spirit realms, Oxford Circus being a supernatural Circus etc)

                  In books there are a few interesting urban fantasy series - some better than others - that have interesting inspiration and are set in London. The best two and the ones i have drawn on in my game are ben aaronovitch's Rivers of London series (now also a comic book as well as book series) and the YA novel trilogy by Tom Pollock starting with City's Son. Both really focus on making London a real spiritual and supernatural place where the supernatural is intricately connected to the city rather than just being supernatural urban fantasies set in london. Same can be said for the works of Kate Griffin although i personally find her writing style a bit too weird for my tastes and so not my favorite. Also of interest are The Wolves of London by Mark Morris, drake the Burned man by Peter Maclean, and the Shadow Police series by Paul Cornell.

                  In terms of how all this translates into my game a few things:

                  1 London Below (or my version of it) is deeply connected to the tube system which is where it can be entered and has become a place for many supernaturals to escape the mundane world. Changelings have particularly made it home as a place that has spiritual protections from the interference of the True Fae.

                  2 i use place names as inspiration for the spiritual and supernatural elements of London a lot like Neverwhere and the other books I named above.

                  3 Mages interact a lot with changelings who have gathered in London below and with the spiritual aspects of London. Ghosts are also very significant in such a history filled city. Werewolves are less significant in my version of London although they are certainly there. Vampires are enemies now since the Mages discovered the existence of the mass Blood farm described in Shadows of UK. As mages in my setting are focused on protecting humanity that was not something that could be ignored! Vampires in UK are currently in civil war over this discovery (which was kept from many vampires too) and mages are currently unsure whether they can or should interfere or just let them at it!

                                  Originally posted by RadioFreeDeath View Post
                                  Out of curiosity, what have you estimated your mage populations to be your london games? .
                                  Honestly never really thought much about it! But i would argue it would be above the average percentage of population because of the nature of London and its relationship with the rest of the UK. There tends to be a brain and talent drain towards London because it is the center of so much political/social/cultural/financial aspects of UK life. I would suggest therefore that Mages in UK tend concentrate in London the same way that people in finance or media or culture etc. do in the UK. That also probably means that Mages outside London are more committed to the locality and rooted in it whereas there are more tourists and newcomers in London. That also probably means that like mundane London society London Mage society is split between those who are there for "work" i.e. because that's where the magic action is!) and "Londoners" who pride themselves in being local and coming from generations of locals. This provides an cultural conflict within Mage society that is really nothing about magic but about the regular cultural pattern of the city and its general population.

                                          I'm currently brainstorming for a possible upcoming chronicle in London, so I'm kinda looking for feedback as well. If some of my players happen to find this thread, please try to avoid spoiling it for yourself.

                                          As a longtime metalhead I was inspired by the Iron Maiden song "Reincarnation of Benjamin Breeg", whose publication was advertised with a fake website about a man investigating the mysterious disappearance of a relative of his, mentioned in the link above. In my Awakened London this website would've been real, and Benjamin Breeg would be a human somehow doomed to reincarnate again and again, haunted by nightmares and visions that urge him to do something awful. Hierarch Civitas and the Guardians of the Veil would be connected to his disappearance, as they dealt with him before he could complete his purpose in his last incarnation. The main story of the chronicle would be about his return, figuring out who he is in this reincarnation, and what to do about him.

                                          I've been thinking of having him as some sort of threat that will open a portal to Hell if left unchecked, but Mage doesn't really have the Christian themes for it. Maybe the Lower Depths could work? Also, he doesn't really want to do anything bad. It's his curse that leads to him doing it.

                                          Anyway, my ideas about London itself include having the shadow name of the Hierarch being hereditary. Civitas means "social body of the cives or citizens, united by law" in Latin, so in a way, the Hierarch is the city. Another thing I've been thinking about is the differences between American and British urban fantasy: According to some forum discussions the UK is so full of towns and people that distances are much smaller, and various supernaturals in London probably know each other. One example that was given on rpg.net was that if there was a "police organization dealing with supernatural phenomena", they would probably have some usual suspects who they'd interrogate if something happened. I guess the movie Snatch would be a good example of this, with various criminal groups knowing each other by reputation at least.

                                          What do you think of these differences between American and British urban fantasy? Should these be taken into consideration when running a chronicle in London?
