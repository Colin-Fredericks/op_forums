    That wasn't aimed at you, Gecko, rather the people who appeared to be feeding you their headcanon, which you've had trouble parsing from the books before.

    So, changelings don't have anything to do with the astral. As of 1st ed, they even physically bounced off portals to Temenos. I don't think going that far is necessary, but the relationship between dreams and the hedge is like programs running in a holodeck: Changelings can view dreams from the hedge, and step into them such that they're in physical form unlike everyone else.

    Well, the viewing part works from the Onieros, too: You can squat in someone's soul and watch their thoughts and dreams, assuming their soul's inner ecology of Goetia don't turn on you like in Inception.

    This does not mean that the Hedge and Oneiros are connected. Just that they both view dreams. The Hedge and Faerie could be off on a right-angle to the Astral.

    So, a few options suggest themselves:

    The Hedge thorns hurt souls, and the Gebtry are voracious consumers of story. Perhaps it is a Lower Depth, and Faerie is part of it.

    The contract-based nature if changeling reality and the appearance of the Hedge might suggest some connection to Supernal Arcadia even if they're not the same (Mage-Arcadia's "thorns" look like silver cracks in reality, the name being an apt description that confuses first-time crossover readers). Perhaps, then, Faerie-and-the-Hedge is a particularly large and active Emanation Realm.

    When mages enter the Astral, they pass through a symbolic barrier between dreams and the Onerios that hurt like hell to move through. In the same way that Mages and Werewolves are aware that the Gauntlet has stuff (even native life forms) in it but their powers treat it as a barrier to be crossed, perhaps the Astral Barrier is actually the Hedge? It is dream-adjacent, a realm of narrative would seem logically placed between dreams and the personal soul, and the pain mages experience when crossing seems analogous to the thorns.

    The third one came to me long after changeling 2e was written, and I'm reasonably proud if it. I have no intention of making any one of them canon. Some things *are* best left multiple-choice.

                    Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
                    For clarity I think it would be better to say that the Lower Depths have Some Things, and the Abyss has No Things (though it has some things that aren't things, possibly all things that aren't things).
                    Or to be more precise, that which is from the Lower Depths is inimical because it lacks sufficient thingness, and acts like a vaccuum, drawing whatever thingness the particular manifestation lacks out of the world around it. This can be a problem when that thingness is something like "location" or "identity."

                    That which is of the Abyss is inimical because it has incompatible thingness. Abyssal manifestations exhibit properties that contradict sense and causality. This can be a problem when it means multiple timelines and histories begin to coexist and overlap one another, or when competing laws of physics take on the contagious properties of disease.

                    Direct submergence in the Supernal Realm, without the protection of a lustrum, is inimical because it has excessive or overwhelming thingness. This can be a problem like when your computer overclocks and runs operations it's seriously not equipped for, overheats and crashes, and your hardware is damaged and your data destroyed. In this analogy, the data is you as a person, your entire existence, backward and forward.

                                This discussion has made me like 2e even more, because it really does resolve a lot of misconceptions and complaints. It does so in a fairly complicated way that I didn't initially "get", but that's not really a criticism. I'm fine with the game about wizards and cosmic secrets being deliberately complicated and somewhat obtuse. It really makes the players feel like they're solving mysteries.

                                I guess another thing I don't like about the "source code" example, and why I prefer the "sun" or "turning lies into truth" analogies, is because reality hacking is kind of already Demon's hat. Part of what threw me on that was misinterpreting the whole Supernal vs. Fallen terminology as "the Supernal is the true world and everything else is fake garbage", which I see now isn't how it works.

                                To put it another way, my initial conception of the how Awakening worked was "there are five Magic Space Heavens with magic pieces of paper in them and if you write your name on one you turn into a Mage", which is also not how it works at all.

                                The Awakening and the whole "name writing" thing are metaphors/analogies, but they're metaphors and analogies that mages make real, because that's what magic does. The Supernal realms could be seen as the Mage imposing order on the raw power of the Supernal and turning it into a form they can comprehend, which allows them to form a connection between it and their soul. To use another example, when a Supernal summoner summons a Fate entity that resembles an elven sage (that happens to resemble a Fae), he's not actually opening a portal to the Elf World and summoning someone from there. What he's doing is imposing the analogy of "elf sage" on the raw concept of fate. Or, to put it another way, "raw fate" can't just exist as raw fate or be understood in our reality, but it can be understood as being symbolically and conceptually similar to an elven sage, so it manifests in the shape of one. It's similarity to the Fae isn't because it is one of them as much as because it possesses the same symbols that that define them. A Mage could summon an entity from a Phenomenal realm, but would do so by using the Symbols connected to it. Sorry if all of this is obvious, I'm getting a handle on it and it's pretty cool.
