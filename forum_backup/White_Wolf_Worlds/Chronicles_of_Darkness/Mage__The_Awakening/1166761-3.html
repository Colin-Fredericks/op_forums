                    Also, I play Mage for the philosophy. We tend to have a lot of fun debates around the table that we don't always have with, say, D&D. (Not to disparage D&D, I love 5th edition).

                    I agree that it also means that maybe not everyone wants to play Mage all the time because it requires more work than other games from everyone. And that's fine. I am one that thinks the complexity of 2e should be toned down a notch, but not entirely and certainly not in the sake of balance.

                    Typically what I do when other supernaturals show up in my mage game is that I decide if they are a challenge or not, up front. If I want them to be a challenge I think a number of things that will create that challenge, beyond just stats.

                    For example, in one of my games, a pair of mages were investigating a Sleeper and discovered she was turned into a Vampire. They didn't know enough about vampires and tried to shadow one to learn more. This eventually lead to combat, for which they were not well prepared. The mages completely obliterated him but took some claws to the ribs in the process. In another scene, they interrogated a Vampire by holding him in place with Space and letting him know they could keep him there until sunrise. But these vampires were not "challenges" they are just small speed bumps (and a way to allow the players to better explore the setting).

                    The real challenge came when an Ordo Dracul Ventrue Elder had designed a Scale to travel the Astral in torpor and kept spying on their secrets (and Dominating their Astral forms) while developing another Scale that turned Sleepwalkers into hosts of Abyssal intruders. Also, they needed the help of this vampire to retrieve something the Concilium had taken from them. And because he was a potential ally against a large-scale confrontation between vampires and mages that may or may not have been caused by Concilium officials and/or Strix. Also, their constant meddling with vampires helped the Kindred locate their sanctum (which made them risk the safety of their retainers, and this after they had to leave their previous sanctum due to certain activities that attracted werewolves). You will need more than combat spells to untangle that one (unless the solution is destroying the city). It was fun!

                    So, now that I think about it, Mage is a great game if you like convoluted storylines and combing through weird situations like I do.

                                                Mage on Mage combat seems to be a dish best served sparingly, but it can definitely be fun. I tend not to play my NPCs 'optimally' or whatever, because they are flawed human beings, much like the PC's (who themselves usually make a mess of things.) The trick to making it all worthwhile is to have some kind of interesting 3rd (or 4th) element to the scene beyond the simple fact of a confrontation. Perhaps the clash is happening in a crowded street, forcing the PC's to consider Wisdom, Paradox and the potential of dozens of innocent casualties while their Scelestus antagonist cares for none of these things.

                                                Perhaps the 'Mage' they are fighting is actually a helpless Sleeper possessed by a Seer using a Profane Urim. Then the question isn't 'can they defeat him' its 'how much are they willing to risk to save this man.'

                                                As has been stated, Mages are capable of a great deal. That means the challenges you throw at them have to be quite hefty too, don't be afraid of chucking in moral conundrums or odd requests, working out an elegant solution to such things is half the fun of the game.

                                                For example, in a Halloween themed session a few weeks back, my Moros and Obrimos PC's had to work together to gather a laundry list of items for a unique ritual (otherwise the Iris in the basement of their house was going to condemn all of southern England to a new Ice Age, but thats another story.) This list included.
                                                • The skull of a murderer, the blood of a victim.
                                                • A sliver of captured moonlight, a newborn bird that has only seen one dawn.
                                                • The ashes of an ardent love letter, and the written bile of a man consumed by hate
                                                I had only faint outlines as to how they would achieve this, but the proceeding couple of hours worth of scenes where they worked out various schemes to gather the reagents was a blast.

                                                    I think you're missing the point: There is no such thing as instantaneous transitory spells in 2e so there's no such thing as a spell that wouldn't be suppressed by an AOE dispel during the AOE dispel's duration. In 2E, "transitory" means spells without Advanced Duration... of which the minimum "transitory" duration is 1 turn.

                                                    Example with Thyrsus Mage 1 and Obrimos Mage 2:
                                                    Mage 1 has Potency 20 W/S up from his buddy with lots of time on his hands and Prime Arcana 4. Mage 2's initiative comes up first and they cast an Advanced Scale area combined praxis spell of Celestial Fire and Supernal Dispellation with a reach for Aggravated damage at Mage 1; the spell has a potency of 7 and a minimum duration of 1 turn and Mage 1 gets an exceptional success with a roll of 3, opting to ignore Withstands.

                                                    Mage 2 uses 4 Mana to power the 2 Reach and doesn't need to use Sensory Range because he can just chuck the spell with an attack roll of Gnosis + Firearms/Athletics. With Fast Spells, Mage 1 could only apply defense to a chucked spell if they could apply defense to bullets; even if they could, they can't apply defense to or dodge an area spell anyway. Just for giggles, because Mage 2 has Fate 1, they specify that it will only suppress spells that don't hurt Mage 2.

                                                    Mage 1 can't dodge this and, of course, can't counterspell it without Prime magic. His withstand of the CF from Potency 20 W/S is ignored because it's an area spell. Even if it didn't, it would be ignored because of the exceptional success. Even if it didn't, the Supernal Dispellation beats any Withstand of W/S. Even if it didn't, the Potency of 7 means it would dispel W/S even if it had been cast with Advanced Potency. Even if W/S was somehow from an attainment and thus couldn't be dispelled, its withstand rating would be ignored by Celestial Fire because of the exceptional success.

                                                    So Mage 1 is in a "large house or building" sized area in which all spells are subject to ignores-withstand Supernal Dispellation and everything is subject to 7 points of aggravated from Celestial Fire (including beings in Twilight). Arguably neither his Spirit Armor nor Life Armor apply against this damage. Thankfully, Mage 1 has Life 4 and Mend as a rote so they cast Mend with a reach to heal aggravated damage... unfortunately the area dispel is still in effect and nullifies Mend because it is within the area during the duration (1 turn). The Supernal Dispellation would nullify ANY non-archmage spell because of the ignoring of withstands from exceptional success and, even if the Storyteller houseruled against that, Mend's Withstand wouldn't beat SupDisp's Potency of 7 even with Advanced Potency.

                                                    Mage 1's player says: "But wait! Mend is a transitory spell! It takes effect instantly and can't be dispelled." The Storyteller says: "That is literally not what Transitory means. If you look at the rulebook, transitory just means it doesn't have Advanced Duration. The only reference to 'immediate' effects are on page 113 in a section saying what happens when a heal or damage spell etc is cast with an extended duration. If the spell takes effect immediately, it still gets dispelled immediately. The book does not say what you say it does."

                                                    Supernal Dispellation:
                                                    "Supernal truths can never truly be unmade, but with this spell
                                                    the mage may cast them back across the Abyss, effectively erasing
                                                    any spell she comes across. This spell is not potent enough to
                                                    dispel archmages’ spells.
                                                    A successful casting suppresses the spell for the Duration of
                                                    Supernal Dispellation."

                                                    If an area spell treats all possible subjects within its area as its subject which each withstands separately, then it suppresses every spell cast in the area during its duration. Period. The only way for Mage 1 to escape Mage 2's spell is to escape the "large house or building" sized area if their Speed allows.

                                                    This is why I think area spells treating everything within AOE as subject, which is otherwise limited, is bats*** f****** insane. If it's RAW, that's cool. Still insanely OP.
