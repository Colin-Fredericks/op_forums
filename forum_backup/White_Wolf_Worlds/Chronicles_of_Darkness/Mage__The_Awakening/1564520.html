    Artifacts of Prosperity

    10-04-2026, 03:04 PM
    Across the archipelago of Japan, folk tales are told about sacred relics that beckon good fortune. Legends say they are drawn to the driven and intrepid, those who would brave the storms of the unknown toward the shores of prosperity. Some believe they are treasures from the vaults of the mythical ship Takarabune, blessings passed down by the gods of luck to overcome misfortune and turn away calamity. Even now they await the call of those who stride boldly into the future.


    Fukuju (福寿, "Content Longevity", ••••• ••)
    Gnosis: 4
    Arcana: Death 5, Life 2
    Mana Capacity: 14
    Effects: Analyze Life, Deny The Reaper, Purge Illness

    Description: A scroll with a flexible case made of imperial jade, stamped with a carved yìnzhāng of the South Pole Star's High Speech rune and adorned with intricate kin-urushi renditions of a crane, deer, and tortoise.The case unrolls directly to reveal a pristine washi scroll with a timeless feel. Within it are incantation formulas for longevity, healing and good health, written in a flowing calligraphy that is always perceived as being in a language the reader knows. When someone holding the scroll recites a formula, its abilities activate, giving them insight into diseases, curing illnesses, healing the most grievous wounds in moments and bestowing longevity.


    Hōjō (豊穣, "Bountiful Harvest", ••••• ••)
    Gnosis: 4
    Arcana: Prime 5
    Mana Capacity: 14
    Effects: Create Truth, Sacred Geometry
    Utility Attainments: Everywhere

    Description: A wooden Uchide no Kozuchi mallet crafted to function as a damaru (pellet drum), finished in a lustrous jet-black kuro-urushi. Its rounded, rectangular head is adorned with an intricate, overflowing hanazume floral pattern along the sides, while the dual drumheads bear the swirls of the tomoe.

    When swung so that the pellet beads rhythmically strike the drumheads before hitting a target, its Create Truth effect activates. The surrounding area becomes blessed with an abundance of Supernal power: Mysteries multiply, storehouses overflow with tass, and prosperous Resonances permeate the area.

    Alternatively, if the user strikes the mallet's head directly with their palm, a sound like a taiko drum thunders out, activating its Sacred Geometry ability. This opens the user’s senses to the flow of the ley lines. Through their diligent work, crops can be made to grow overnight, structures and tools become sturdier, and machines are supplied with an endless flow of clean energy.


    Kenninfubatsu (堅忍不抜, “Steadfast Fortitude”, ••••• ••)
    Gnosis: 4
    Arcana: Space 5, Fate 2, Time 2
    Mana Capacity: 14
    Effects: Ban (with Fate and Time Keys available)

    Description: A highly ornate, fully functional jumonji yari featuring a vermillion shaft and a central speartip resembling a trishul. The gleaming blade, fittings, and ishizuki (butt cap) are meticulously detailed with golden koftgari inlays. When pointed downward and forcefully thrust with both hands, its abilities activate. The energies of stasis and order surge forth with the force of a waterfall, forming a pagoda-shaped barrier, bathing the surroundings in gold and saffron hues. Within these boundaries, the wielder dictates who may enter, who may exit, and who is frozen in time - allowing them to create an unyielding bastion of safety for those they protect, or to seal away great malevolence.


    Kisha (喜捨, "Joyful Giving”, ••••• ••)
    Gnosis: 4
    Arcana: Matter 5
    Mana Capacity: 14
    Effects: Endless Bounty
    Utility Attainments: Permanence, Everywhere

    Description: A large hemp cloth sack tied by a 3-coin feng shui charm. On its surface sumi-e of rice stalks are painted. Whenever a user laughs heartily while touching the sack, its abilities activate, making it so a container never runs out of items. While it's normally used on the sack, it can also bestow its blessing on other objects, ensuring that food, water, toys, clothes, medicine and many other treasures can always be given freely and generously.


    Kitsutabi (吉旅, “Auspicious Journey”, ••••• •••)
    Gnosis: 5
    Arcana: Fate 5
    Mana Capacity: 16
    Effects: Forge Destiny, Strings of Fate
    Utility Attainments: Time in a Bottle

    Description: A tsuko-tegata travel pass featuring bold black shodō calligraphy, stamped by a vermilion hankō seal of the Seven Lucky Gods, granting the bearer passage onto their treasure ship, Takarabune. On the back side, the Seven Lucky Gods are depicted in a nishiki-e, traveling the seas on the Takarabune. When a holder speaks a wish to the pass, it activates its abilities, helping them travel towards a thriving future.


    Mako (誠呼, “Sincere Voice”, •••••)
    Gnosis: 3
    Arcana: Prime 4
    Mana Capacity: 10
    Effects: Words of Truth
    Utility Attainments: Sympathetic Range

    Description: A suzu bell omamori made of a flawless alloy of white gold and silver, perfectly polished to never tarnish. It bears a delicate kebori engraving of a hakuko, suspended from a braided vermillion silk cord tied in an agemaki knot. When rung, a crystalline sound pierces the air, allowing its user to become a herald of truth who speaks with clarity to those who need to hear it most.

    In addition, if the user claps their hands twice in a kashiwade, they may utilize it at sympathetic range, carrying their message across vast distances.


    Ninkin (忍勤, "Patient Diligence", ••••• ••)
    Gnosis: 4
    Arcana: Fate 5
    Mana Capacity: 14
    Effects: Serendipity, Shifting the Odds
    Utility Attainments: Everywhere

    Description: A sturdy Edo-wazao fishing rod finished in a brilliant vermillion urushi lacquer. Its handle features a golden maki-e design of seigaiha waves, and it is rigged with a lure shaped like a red sea bream. The fishing rod aids those who persevere. If the line ever nears its breaking point, it harmlessly slips from the target, preserving the tackle and granting the angler a future opportunity to try again.

    When cast by a smiling wielder, its abilities activate, luring in opportunities, attracting customers, and drawing fortune — paving the way to success through hard work. If a wielder claps their hands twice in a kashiwade, they can activate Everywhere, spreading bountiful blessings to foster a thriving community.


    Ryūshin (流心, "Flowing Heart", ••••• ••)
    Gnosis: 4
    Arcana: Mind 5
    Mana Capacity: 14
    Effects: Social Networking, Emotional Urging
    Utility Attainments: Time in a Bottle

    Description: A saraswati veena, its wood is coated in a soft, warm tan lacquer that defies the rigors of weather and water, and its kudam and sorakkai resonators are adorned with fluid sumi-e paintings of rushing streams. At its head, the traditional yali beast is beautifully carved into the likeness of a coiled Japanese dragon. When its strings are plucked, its notes flow effortlessly through the air, allowing its abilities to be used to touch hearts and beckon solidarity.


    Tokowa (常磐, “Endless Vitality”, ••••• ••)
    Gnosis: 4
    Arcana: Death 5, Life 4
    Mana Capacity: 14
    Effects: Renew Life, Soul Jar
    Utility Attainments: Time in a Bottle

    Description: A long walking staff masterfully crafted from durable peach wood. Tied to its top by a vibrant vermillion silk cord is a chestnut-brown ki-urushi hyōtan (gourd). The gourd's surface features a togidashi maki-e design of a soaring crane, as well as an incantation in flowing script, which always appears to be written in a language the reader knows.

    Should a user intone the incantation and touch a vessel with the staff, it glows softly with a golden inner light and becomes capable of housing a soul. Furthermore, when a user holding the staff recites the incantation and uncorks the gourd, its abilities activate and a golden elixir flows forth, breathing new life into the deceased, giving them a second chance at life, or the opportunity for closure.


    New Spell: Renew Life (Death ••••, Life ••••)
    Practice: Patterning
    Primary Factor: Duration
    Withstand: Rank (for ghosts)
    Suggested Rote Skills: Academics, Medicine, Occult

    An ancient rote created to bring back the deceased by transforming their death into new life. A subject is revived with their body fully restored and in perfect health, at any point in their life cycle chosen by the caster. When the spell's duration ends, they die again and their body returns to whatever state it was in at the moment of casting.

    Because the souls of the dead have already moved on, any human revived by this spell immediately suffers soul loss.

    It can also be used to transform a ghost's Corpus into a living body for its duration, though human ghosts suffer from soullessness as well.


    What were you thinking design wise ?

    The Artifacts are thematically rooted in the Seven Lucky Gods and Inari—popular Shinto kami of good fortune. Each of them bestow a grace through their chosen Artifact, with their innate abilities coaxing the user to foster it, thus paving their path to prosperity. The Artifacts for the Seven Lucky Gods are all 7-dots (the ones for Inari and the Takarabune have different dot ratings), resonating with their lucky number. In addition, since six of the Seven Lucky Gods came from outside Japan (with Ebisu being the sole indigenous kami), they also incorporate symbols of both their originating culture and their Japanese incarnation.

    Fukuju is a scroll of Jurōjin, its ability to diagnose illnesses, cure diseases, heal without expending mana and rejuvenate ties into his blessing of longevity. The imperial jade, scroll case and seal allude to his Chinese origin as the Taoist immortal of the Southern Polar Star. The three animals are symbols of longevity in Taoism, but were only added in his depictions in Japan. The washi paper is Japanese and does not yellow with age, reinforcing the theme of longevity. The way the scroll unfolds and its general appearance are inspired by Oogway’s scroll from Kung Fu Panda 3. The fact anyone can read it is a reference to him being a patron of knowledge.

    Hōjō is the lucky mallet of Daikokuten, which is said to grant wishes and make treasure appear. One possible approach was to have it cast spells like Miracle and Ex Nihilo, but instead I chose to focus on his role as a god of wealth and agriculture. In the case of the Awakened, that means mana, so being able to create Hallows with advanced scale means it will be plentiful. Since using the power of Nodes allows one to benefit from their innate energies, including being able to grow plants faster and have more harvests, it seemed fitting for it to grant Sacred Geometry as well, making it so both Sleepers and Awakened benefit from a bountiful harvest. The damaru and black lacquer allude to his origin as the Hindu god Mahākāla, an aspect of Shiva. The taiko is a Japanese drum that symbolizes the rhythm of life and the spiritual connection between humans, nature, and the divine. The hanazume floral patterns allude to his role as a god of agriculture. The tomoe symbolizes abundance, vitality and life, linking to his syncretization into Daikokuten.

    Kenninfubatsu is the spear of Bishamonten, since his role as a guardian of the righteous and to suppress evil, therefore his spear accomplishes that by using Ban to create a mighty kekkai. The Fate Key determines who can freely enter, exit or be spared from its sealing power.The Time Key creates a seal of temporal stasis fit to hold the mighties foes. The koftgari inlay represents his origin as the Vedic god Kubera. The trishul alludes to his Buddhist syncretism as Vaiśravaṇa, and the vermillion shaft to his role as a war god. The jumonji yari is a type of Japanese spear that is extremely versatile and practical, just like Bishamonten, and whose design could easily be blended with a trishul. The pagoda is an item carried by Bishamonten, which symbolizes the divine treasure house and the containment of Buddhist teachings, with the colors gold and saffron being linked to holiness and enlightenment in Buddhism. In this way, the kekkai essentially declares an area as being under his protectorate.

    Kisha is the sack of Hotei, which can produce unlimited mundane goods via Endless Bounty. By incorporating both Permanence and Everywhere, it can easily apply its effects to both mundane items and large containers, giving it a wider range of applications without the need to ritual cast. The 3-coin rope is a good luck charm that alludes to his origin as the Chinese Zen Buddhist monk Budai, and the sumi-e of rice stalks symbolizes humility, prosperity and a bountiful harvest.

    Kitsutabi references the Takarabune itself, the boat in which the Seven Lucky Gods travel. The boat itself was said to be full of treasures, which would probably make it huge, so one possible approach was to make it able to cast Quarantine, while having some way to have a Key, and possibly Time in a Bottle and Everywhere as well. In addition, it was said to be full of magical items, which would involve making more Artifacts and go beyond my intended scope. Instead I chose to represent it via a tsuko-tegata, a type of travel pass from the Edo period, which are used in modern day as lucky charms, and some modern versions have images, which was represented in this case via a nishiki-e. Its abilities allow you to travel the paths of fate towards your dream of prosperity, allowing you to even take an express route if you invest mana.

    Whether it can lead you to the Takarabune or not is something I leave deliberately open, but I would probably have it require a key to be able to take a user to the ship, such as taking inspiration from the custom where an image of the Seven Lucky Gods traveling on the ship is placed under one’s pillow in New Year’s Day to beckon good luck for the year. Unlike the other Artifacts, this one is eight dots to represent the Seven Lucky Gods and the user traveling together in the Takarabune.

    Mako is an Artifact of Inari, embodying her role as the goddess of divine messengers. It allows the user to communicate the truth clearly in any situation and have people believe them, even reaching people far away via sympathetic casting or remote viewing. The silver color symbolizes Inari and gold her messagers (the hakuko kitsune), which is depicted as white gold to match the color of their fur. The vermillion cord and agemaki charms reference its nature as a good luck charm suzu omamori. The kashiwade is used to represent beckoning the kami to deliver your message. Lastly, since in this case Inari is acting as a messenger of the Seven Lucky Gods, the base casting pool is a reference to their lucky number 7.

    Ninkin is a traditional Japanese fishing rod and the Artifact of Ebisu. It lures in a path forward for the user, as neither Serendipity nor Shifting the Odds immediately solve problems, they require you to take initiative and work diligently using whatever aid they provide. Being able to use it on multiple targets via Everywhere symbolizes Ebisu’s philosophy of building a thriving community via diligence, perseverance, and community welfare. Activating it via kashiwade references how the tradition is said to have originated with Ebisu, as worshippers believe he is hard of hearing, using it to firmly signal their presence and catch his attention. The red on the lacquer, as well as the red sea bream are symbols of Ebisu. The seigaiha waves are symbols of endurance, resilience, good fortune and prosperity, which match his role as a god of the sea and hard work. The property of the fishing rod not snapping upon a failure (or dramatic failure) is a minor effect that embodies his philosophy of constantly trying again to make steady progress over time

    Ryūshin is the musical instrument of Benzaiten, the goddess of everything that flows, which means her portfolio includes a wide variety of different things, such as eloquence, rivers, oceans, lakes and inspiration, just to name a few. So I decided to focus on her role as a deity of love, music and happiness. Her Artifact conveys sentiment via Emotional Urging and allows the user to have the community empathize with them via Social Networking. Time in a Bottle allows the magic to flow all the more easily and naturally. The instrument is a saraswati veena since it not only alludes to her origin as a Hindu river goddess, but the veena is also the ancestor of the biwa, her signature instrument. The waterproof lacquer allows it to be used in places with high humidity or at sea, without worry of damage. The sumi-e emphasizes her role as the goddess of all the flows and the yali beast being a Japanese dragon alludes to one of her greatest victories, where she redeemed it through empathy.

    Tokowa is the walking staff of Fukurokuju, which focuses on his unique ability amongst the Seven Lucky Gods—he is the only one capable of bringing back the dead. The Renew Life spell made for it was created utilizing the Reach effect of Deny the Reaper as the baseline, but made into a duration primary Patterning of Life and Death, with the additional ability to “resurrect” ghosts since you are transforming a target saturated by death into a living being, so being able to affect ghosts seemed mechanically consistent and thematically fitting. A master level application of it was the most sensible approach I could think of to bring back the dead without risking Paradox (even if it can still be dispelled), either temporarily at no cost or at indefinite duration by paying mana. Time in a Bottle allows it to be cast at indefinite duration instantly, it also functions as a way to free Reach so it can be used for other cool applications, such as reviving someone at sensory range or resurrecting a large number of fallen comrades via advanced scale, making it a very versatile Artifact. Since it still had the issue of humans being revived without souls, including Soul Jar in its toolkit seemed like the logical next step. Now someone who is heading into a deadly situation can carry a soul jar, such as a bamboo canteen (or even enchanting the Artifact’s own gourd), so they can be easily revived if their body and soul are recovered.

    Figuring out the dot rating of the Artifact was a challenge. Page 100 of the 2ed core book says to use the highest single Arcanum dot rating of all the effects as the base (in this case Death 5), which I believe was a deliberate design choice so Artifacts with cool multi-Arcana effects could be made. I then added half the dot cost of Soul Jar (Death 2) following the rules from the same page, bringing it to six dots, and spent one more dot to give it the Time in a Bottle Utility Attainment, arriving at the target total cost of 7-dots.

    The walking staff is made out of peach wood, since peaches are a symbol of immortality in Taoism. The crane is a Taoist symbol of longevity. The vermillion color of the silk cord is an auspicious symbol of life force and vitality in Shintoism. The gourd canteen is said to hold the elixir of immortality in Taoism and is a symbol of longevity in Shintoism. The elixir itself is called the Golden Elixir in Taoism, which is alluded to in how the abilities activate. The ki-urushi lacquer on the gourd is a symbol of endurance, fortitude and timelessness in Japan. The incantation always appearing in a language a reader knows alludes to his role as a patron of scholars, and the fact intoning it is necessary to activate its effects is a reference to Taoist magic.

    That concludes the design notes, hope you had as much fun reading them as I had writing them.
