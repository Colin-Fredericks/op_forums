    Originally posted by EW-Matias View Post
    I don't understand. Could you give an actual example? I don't get what's the utility of doing that.

    Imagine that you find yourself casting, let's say, Incognito Presence upon yourself pretty often. It's a spell that benefits a lot from Super Duration, but it's also very inconvenient to leave it "on" for large periods of time, because it interferes with your activities quite a bit. You don't like the idea of burning a spell control slot on it - maybe your Gnosis is low, or maybe you expect yourself to cast other long duration spells (maybe you want to increase your skills, for example). You like the idea of Unsafely Relinquisinh the spell to save on spell slots, but you don't have Prime - so you can't dispel the spell when you are done with it.
    So you get yourself a ring with a movable segment and carefully place Runes on it, reflecting imago of this specific spell. The ring won't be helpful for any other spell, but you don't mind.
    Next time you cast the spell, you use Runes placed on it as a Yantra. This gives you a +2 Yantra that can be used only for this spell (already a pretty good benefit for spending 3 hours working on a piece of jewlery), and only when spell targets "anything encircled by the Runes" - which, in this case, will be you. After casting the spell, you spend a Willpower point to unsafely relinquish the spell - you probably invested a lot of duration into it, because you wanted to be safe against Clash of Will detecting powers. Normally, this would be really unfortunate, because being invisible for a year is actually a curse, and if you are a Mastigos, your starting Arcana spread won't allow you to dispel it! But that's why you are using Runes.
    You see, spells that used Runes as a Yantra stop working the moment Runic circle is disturbed. That's why you specifically picked a ring that has some kind of movable part - when the spell outlives its usefulness, you can move the movable part so the runic circle is broken. This terminates the spell, and you can reuse the Ring in future castings of Incognito Presence, because you actually didn't destroy the runes - you just temporarily disturbed their continuity.
    If you were, let's say, a Gnosis 1 Mage, this allowed you to have Incognito Presence running without interfering with your normal spell casting, as your Spell Control limit is pretty small at the beginning phase of the game. This saves you from having to Reach to simply cast a spell.

    This tactic loses some of it's potency at Gnosis 3, where you probably already picked up Dispel Magic. It also dosen't play well with Combined Spells - simply because you would need to create new items for each combination, which limits your flexibility a little.

    I like the image this invokes, though - mages creating and carrying bags fulls of trinkets. It also gives you an interesting trade off, because by anchoring spell in this way, you are risking someone or something figuring it out - or worse, something happening by pure accident! - which in turn allows them to potentially disrupt your spells by targeting the trinkets. Which...is good, as it creates temptation of lazy hubris. It encourages you to rely on unsafely relinquished spells, too, which is another temptation that helps Mages hang themselves with their own hands.

    This also seems to have great affinity with Matter magic, as you can reinforce the trinkets, change their shape and do other interesting things that allow introduction of "magic items" into Matter playstyle from the get-go, even if they aren't really magical items. Artificer archetype!

                                                    Originally posted by Fortunatus View Post
                                                    WHW and falco1029

                                                    Sounds like two different uses of Scale: one to make X number of subjects into zombies, and one to make an area of X size where people become zombies as long as they're in it. Both effects last for the Duration of the spell.
                                                    It's little different than that. Using are as a Scale Factor doesn't mean you are targetting an area - it means that you are targeting everything inside that area. For example, it allows you to do a DnD-esque "Burning Hands" cone effect without making the spell Sensory. Trade off here is that you have no power over what get's targeted and what doesn't - anything that's a viable subject for the spell in the target area (which probably can't be shaped into swiss chesse pattern; I'm running under assumption that the shape has to be balanced and with no holes) is getting targeted. Which means possibilities of friendly fire and other unfortunate implications. It's a trade off that allows really cost efficient use of Spellfactors and Reach, in exchange of being very dangerous and leading into Low-Wisdom behavioral patterns. Again, it does that Mage 2nd Edition thing where you totally can be a diligent spellcaster and cast all of your spells as Sensory or Touch spells, but once you figure out that you can not only skip Sensory by doing a "kamehameha spell cone" using area, but also potentially hit extra targets, you will cringe every time you will have to Reach for Sensory. And some day, you will be tired of doing so and just unleash collateral damage. Which is perfectly good emerging gameplay feature.

Could someone give me like 10 different examples of the thing? Mostly about what carries over with the subject to the past and what does not.                                                             The subject of shifting sands arrives at the past in whatever state it was when you sent it back. The only exception are spells. Spells afecting the subject cast by the caster remain, while spells actively effecting the subject not cast by the caster disappear. You'd have to be a Disciple of Time to rationalize why it works like that aside from meta reasons like playability, book keeping and game balance.
