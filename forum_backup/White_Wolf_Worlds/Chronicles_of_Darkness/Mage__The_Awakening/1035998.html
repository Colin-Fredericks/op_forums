    Mystery in Nameless War?

    01-22-2017, 06:07 AM
    Having free weekend, I start to wonder over my next Chronicles ideas I got prepared in 'spare time', for future games. One of which is far in future episodic game about rise of Free Council in the Nameless War from French Revolution events till the Great Refusal, with many different characters ( and players ) across span of over 110 years and around the world. ( I will probably run it after premiere of Tome of Pentacle to have all bolts and screws in setting. ) I would see this game as running on pulpish 'Viva la Revolution' kind of zeal, divide and cooperation in Nameless War, but with 2E Mage center of uncovering Mysteries - I wonder if some grand Mystery should not be behind the scenes.

    With other Orders, I would simply center game on the Order's Special Merit and his Supernal implications, but Libertines have Techne and it's... well, to varied? It's basically stating that each Sleepers technique or practice can have Supernal effects. So it's 'Oh, you can use voodoo and psychic projection as your Yantras in magic? That's great!' and Mystery is solved. It does not seem as lasting Mystery too dwell in to, at least for me now.

    Other idea I was having showing of Sixth Watchtower, but it seems less about Free Council and much more about 'those characters are so special they found new Path'.

    And here I'm in a pinch - basically, I have problem with uncovering something very 'magical' in Free Council to throw this portion of Order to players as Mystery to pull by characters.

    P.S. I think also on giving characters possible options of choosing Gifted or Psychics mage template alternatives from Mage Chronicle's Guide.

      The big thing I think of with the Free Council and techne is that they view magic and the Supernal in a radically different way than the Diamond or Seers. I run a game in the modern LA Assembly where I like to emphasize that the Atlantis/Time Before/Exarchs mythos is only one way of interpreting magic, and it doesn't adequately explain the world on its own. The excitement and power of the Nameless is that they're living in a world dominated by people who have a very rigid and outdated and elitist perception of magic, and they can challenge their authority with things that wouldn't occur to the Atlanteanists.

      If you wanted a big Mystery, maybe start with some phenomenon that's big and far-reaching and subtle and exists in the blind spot of the Diamond and Seers alike, something that wouldn't make sense in a rigid Atlantean cosmology- some kind of change and growth and conflict within the Supernal Realms themselves, maybe. Then, taking a cue from the whole Locked/Linked Mystery rules on a larger scale, establish that the diverse Nameless magical traditions and Sleeper scientific and cultural practices can glimpse this Mystery in new ways by asking questions and Obsessions that wouldn't occur to (or are banned by) the Atlanteanists. No one group has all the answers, but the PCs have an incentive to seek out Nameless mages around the world and bring them into contact with each other by seeking their perspectives on this vast Mystery.

      There's plenty of inspiration for a framework like that. "I'll show those fools at the Academy who laughed me out the door!" Many scientific innovations in Europe had that kind of reaction, from natural selection to continental drift. Or something like Mass Effect where the established authorities loudly ignore the threat posed by the Reapers because it doesn't fit in their worldview, and you have to seek allies elsewhere.

          The problem you're having with Techne is that you're looking at what the PCs do with it rather than why the FC uses it. The FC has observed that humanity appears to -change- the Supernal via their beliefs and rituals. They delve into Techne in order to figure out how the fuck that works. So, you have the one overarching Mystery of the FC trying to figure out the exact mechanisms that allow humanity to shape the Suprrnal. You have another set of Mysteries with the FC trying to figure out what's been changed already, or reacting to changes that they discover have been made. Finally, you have another set of Mysteries in attempting to discover what the heck humanity is going to do to the Supernal next, and can they shape it into something useful? You need to stop looking at just Techne's utilitarian ends.

          The 6th Watchtower is the Tremere one, the Tower of the Soul. The FC used to be a lot of very young Nameless Orders. The Tremere used to be part of the Diamond and know all the terminology and bring a sense of legitimate, long lasting history. So, what if, instead of just the Seers trying to seduce the Nameless the Tremere were as well? They could do it without having to really worry about the Diamond or Seers noticing. If you really twist their ideology in knots they even have a sort of FC view of the Supernal, they believe that slowly "transforming" the souls of humanity by creating a "connection" between those souls and the 6th Tower will eventually bring about a major change to the Supernal capable of doing things like merging all of the Subtle Arcana together into one super Arcana. Anyway, point is, the Nameless War could have had a lot of Little Refusals to various corrupting influences before getting up to the Great Refusal.

                                                          Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                                                          I have just problem with seeing lone PCs at start of the Nameless War to start look in to this phenomena as PC level Mystery and starting the wheel of Techne idea in Order.
                                                          To me, I see Techné as an emergent property of the Order's global ascendancy. There is no single "techné" that the FC practices: it's both a philosophy and a general approach to magic and culture, one with many many differences. It's the proverbial elephant and the blind men arguing about it. The answer to this mystery is "Humanity is magical", but it's not the whole answer, and even to reach there the Nameless columns had to collate and compare many praxes to finally come up with that answer.

                                                          I imagine that there would have existed all sorts of columns, each with its own little nugget of insight. Reaper columns, anarchist columns, scientific-minded columns, masonic lodges, archeomancer columns, "rediscovered" traditionalist cabals, etc. Each and every one of them bring their own obsessions to the nascent Order, but in the end is the political necessity and the historical context that bring the core ethos of the Free Council into being: democracy, revolution and the mystical exaltation of cultural diversity. All other concerns become secondary afterwards, and even violently purged from the Order once the original leadership start consolidating their power.

                                                          My advice is that you should consider the idea that the exploration of techné is really an exploration of magical society, and how mundane society push and pulls against the constraints of accepted magical inquiry.
