    Mine Soul Stone (Death ••)

    Practice: Ruling (Souls)
    Primary Factor: Potency
    Withstand: Composure

    This spell enables the caster to create a soul stone using the soul of the spell's subject. As with the process by which a mage normally creates a soul stone, the spell requires an object of size up to 2 to house the fragment of the subject's soul and the expenditure of a Willpower dot by the spell's caster. The soul stone created by this spell functions equivalently in all respects to one created by the subject from her own soul (e.g. for sympathetic magic targeting the subject).

    At the end of the spell's duration, the object housing the soul fragment ceases to be a soul stone (but is not destroyed) and the caster regains the spent Willpower dot. Destroying the object housing the soul fragment ends the spell immediately.

    Use of this spell is not intrinsically an Act of Hubris, but may constitute an Act of Hubris at the Storyteller's discretion. Mining a temporary soul stone from a mortal enemy to use for sympathetic casting? Probably not an Act of Hubris. Casting it on herself with Indefinite Duration? Definitely an Act of Hubris.

    Mortal souls can't be turned into soul stones, and messing with a Mage's soul is Death 5, and considering the effects it has on Gnosis this is probably in the realm of Archmastery.

    Soul Shaping (Death •••)
    Practice: Weaving (Souls)
    Primary Factor: Duration

    To join a Legacy is to reshape one's soul by changing one's Gnosis. A Disciple of Death can contort her soul to match the shape of member of a specific legacy without changing her Gnosis and thereby gain a few of the benefits of belonging to that legacy.

    The mage must have ready an example of the form into which she wishes to sculpt her soul, which can be the soul or a soul stone from a member of that legacy.

    For the duration of this spell, the mage may utilize the legacy's oblations (although still limited to dots in the legacy's Ruling Arcanum of mana per day way from a Hallow). She also serves as a sympathetic Yantra worth +2 dice for all members, daimonomika, and soul stones of the legacy. Another mage may join the legacy through study of the caster's soul or soul stones. The caster gains none of the other benefits of belonging to the legacy.
    Legacies aren't tied to the soul, they're tied to the vessel of the soul, which is under the purview of Prime, and this effect is also probably beyond the realm of normal magic.

        I'm wondering if you have anything definitive you can cite for those assertions. I don't know anything in the core rulebook itself; possibly there's something from the secret midrash halacha posted long ago on 4chan or whatever.

        The only thing I know that supports your first assertion is that Sever Soul and Sever the Awakened Soul require Death 4 and Death 5 respectively; I don't remember any general rule stating that Awakened souls always require higher-level practices to manipulate than sleeper souls, and to me that idea stands in contrast to the firmer delineations of the practices in 2E.

        Regarding legacies, the word "soul" pops up frequently enough in the write-up for me to feel comfortable letting the reshaping of a soul provide some legacy benefits. I included the benefits that I felt would best preserve the balance of the game although the write-up would indicate that the attainments themselves also are tied to the soul.
        • "Unlike ordinary Oblations, the mage does not need to perform Legacy Oblations at a Hallow — her soul becomes the 'sacred place' being drawn upon"
        • "All Legacies teach special Attainments, built from Praxes the Legacy’s members have internalized. The mage invokes them from her reshaped soul instead of reaching into the Supernal"
        • "Legacy Attainments issue from the mage’s soul instead of the Supernal Realms, so Mana is only used..."
        Based on statements like these from the book, my interpretation of the mechanism behind legacies is that joining and progressing in the legacy changes gnosis, changed gnosis reshapes the soul, and the reshaped soul actually provides the legacy benefits. It's a grey area though.

        EDIT: Also, manipulating a soul in a way that has a side-effect of diminishing potential gnosis is not the same as directly manipulating gnosis. We judge spells based on what subject they manipulate and how they directly manipulate it, not based on the ripple effects and consequences of those manipulations.

            Remember that souls in CofD are plug'n'play. Lost your soul? Just get another. The new soul will reshape itself to fit the hole where your old soul used to be, which is why we're talking about the "soul vessel" because it's the shape of the hole that forms the soul, not the other way around.
            That said, I don't necessarily agree with the notion that you need Prime. Forcing your soul into a specific shape may lead to your "soul vessel" temporarily "deforming" to fit the shape you want. It's crude, but I don't think it should be impossible. In fact, messing directly with the "soul vessel" with Prime skirts quite close to the kind of metaphysics that require Archmastery. Also, the "soul vessel" is not a term in the game, and not listed under Prime.

                            For second edition the soul was defined pretty well in the God-Machine Chronicles, but already in that book (and it's "update", CofD) the word soul was used poetically more often than not (most often in relation to ghosts). Other games know that they talk about things that aren't the same when it comes to souls. Geist even had a sidebar explaining that they don't refer to the soul as defined in CofD most of the time, but rather use it poetically to refer to ghosts.
                            Mage, the game that most of all deals with souls, was developed primarily by Dave Brookshaw, a guy who really had that metaphysical part of the setting pinned down. He was involved in Beast, Geist and Deviant, and is the reason the titular characters' souls (or lack thereof) were explainable and compatible with the soul as defined in GMC/CofD/Mage. He's also worked out how Reapers (more specifically the Tremere) interacts with the souls of all the major templates, which we will see in the upcoming Nameless & Accursed. He's also explained quite a lot of this in this very forum, which is where the fan term "soul vessel" comes from in the first place.
                            So while there are countless inconsistencies regarding the soul, it's primarily either from poetic language being used or because it's written from an in-setting point of view; not because there wasn't a shared definition from the start of the current edition, and Mage is by far the least likely game to unintentionally mess up how souls work.

                            Fun fact: The soul as described in Mummy is divided into five Pillars; each of which corresponds to and can be manipulated by each of the subtle Arcana (as described in Dark Eras Companion). This actually ties neatly into the fact that the soul in Mage is believed in-setting to consist of each of the subtle Arcana, which in turn is supported by the fact that the Mage Sights of all five Paths can see a soul when not inhabiting a person.
