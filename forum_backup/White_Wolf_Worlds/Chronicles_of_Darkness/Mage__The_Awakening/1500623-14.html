    Suppose the Silver Ladder has a Proximus family focused on using Fate/Mind to "inspire" people and whose curse is that they have to take credit for anything good that happens as the result of their inspiration.     That is very easy to deal with. Nothing forbids them from mentioning the curse. Even if the curse did, an undocumented Proximi Dynasty would make mages curious. So at the very least someone will study the curse with their Focused Mage Sight and some Knowing spells.

    After that you just say "they are a Proximi whose curse makes them do this. Don't judge their behavior based on how they deal with the curse, keep an eye on the full picture". Even the Guardians wouldn't really be annoyed if they knew its they need to filter that out during interactions. Do you need to find out what they really did ? Read their minds, use postcognition, ask the people they know.

    However, the way it was presented suggested that you intended them to take credit for everything good that happens in the faction they are in, rather than anything good that happened due to using their blessings (which would be fair enough).

This "off screen" casting is part of the downtime system I sometimes use and was there to streamline play. I can reference this sheet to see what to expect.

                The idea here is that it isn't an extended roll, but is using the ritual bonus to get the +5 dice, and then taking time to get the best yantras possible. This, plus the abuse of exceptional luck to add dice, allows for really high potency spells with 1 year duration. I was wondering what other thoughts of this or if they allowed these kinds of things at their tables.
                The thing about this is that if you're "streamlining play" by handwaving the fact that characters are spending several hours to set up something they intend to have last for a month, you can afford to have that preparation take a short scene and have them do the roll, particularly if you're specifically concerned about them walking around with absurdly powerful spells that you apparently are just letting them sideline the primary risks of creating.

                Like, I want to be quite plain here, pushing the limits of your Gnosis and the potential issues that can lead to are part of the reason Paradox works the way it does and why spell factors just apply penalties to a roll you only need a single success on. Dice are being rolled because there's a chance that you'll fail and have wasted six hours or a valuable reagent or a rare opportunity.

                If you want to math it out and remove the chances of interesting failure, a moderately reasonable shorthand is that if you still have four dice left in the spellcasting pool after penalties, you have roughly a 75% chance of rolling at least one success; if you have six or seven dice, you have roughly 90% odds of success; nine or ten dice gives about 96% odds; and at 13+ dice you're just adding decimal values to the end of 99%. Pick a probability threshold and let them put as many spell factors as they could fit into their dicepool above that number of dice onto the spells they're doing during downtime; anything more intense will require a roll.

                            Originally posted by jsnider3 View Post
                            The Guardians are dicks, yes, but letting a family of Proximus get themselves killed seems a bit of a stretch for them. I was thinking of it as being more towards the "problem" end of the spectrum where there are essentially a few dozen people willing to cast Superlative Luck or Augment Mind on command, but who keep bragging to local news about it. I just wanted to make sure the Guardians couldn't trivially solve the problem I'm trying to give them.
                            ​
                            While I generally agree with these being a problem for them, with my notation on that making it clear that the problem is less one of PR and more one of literal livelihood, it's important to note that it's not particularly off the table for them either. Sins for a just end grant Wisdom, after all, and sometimes the problems solve themselves.

                            With the context you added, it's not like their options get any rosier. If they're not flat out breaching the Veil and the Precept of Secrecy, they're sure as hell flirting with it, and with the idea of the curse in particular, what you have here is not so much a moral quandry, as it is a beast begging to be killed so it may understand the world as being capable of mercy. This is the sort of scenario that really invites the Guardian's particular hubris without being challenging about it.

                            If I were going to run this scenario, I can think of one easy fix to make it all the murkier-make it the group of Proximi's inclination, not their curse. That gives you the room to play with and manipulate the groups actions and reactions towards the Guardian's responses and efforts, which in turn makes a clear answer one way or the other more muddled as gentle guidance or misdirection can sometimes seem to work only to be completely ignored, or for the threat of the scenario to kind of get it sinking in, and of course makes it all the more muddled as a issue of dealing with a group verus dealing with the individuals in that group. The curse really just makes it seem like lemmings near a cliff scenario, and even with my general role of being the one who continually makes it clear the the Guardians are indeed merciful instead of perpetually cold-blooded killers would be hard pressed to not argue that it'd be easier to let that problem solve itself.
