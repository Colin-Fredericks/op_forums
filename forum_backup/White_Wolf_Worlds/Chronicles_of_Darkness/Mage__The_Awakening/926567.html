    [2e] Why Mage?

    07-05-2016, 03:39 PM
    I couldn't see one of these for Mage, so I thought I'd start it. Can you sell Mage to me? I own 1st ed and I have the advance preview of 2nd. I've loved the three games released so far in the 2nd edition rules (Vampire, Werewolf, and Demon) and I was looking forward to Mage. However, something about it is just not clicking in my head. The magic system is great, that's without question. But I just can't seem to break in to this one.

    How would you encourage someone to pick it up over the other CofD games and run it instead?
    What amazing things have you done or seen so far?

    I look forward to seeing what you have to say.

      While I can't speak on 2e directly as I haven't ran it yet (and not sure if I will. There are things I like and things I hate about it.), Mage in general is a game that is heavily a shared experience. The ST can't describe to you how magic looks as it is different for each mage. The players have to add their own flare to such descriptions. This aspect is what I like about mage. It can be as ugly or pretty as the player is able to speak about their magic. This can make for a really beautifully game if you have people who can describe things well.

      The personal horror nature of the game (the fact that most mages can't really still be around Sleeper friends and family, or at the very least having this secret life that none of them know about) is something that when is played on, and done well, is something that can be rather scary. Stories about how a mage tries to maintain relationships with Sleepers while trying not to invoke paradox (and thus the attention of the Guardians of the Veil) could be interesting.

      Mage, to me, asks two questions mainly: 1) 'should I do this?' (rather than 'can', because chances are you mostly likely can do it) and 2) 'What happens when you give x person the power of god?'
      The first is more for character development and perhaps is a minor point, but the second? Scary things can happen if you include this question as a plot point(ex: 'What happens if Hitler was a mage?')

      In all, Mage provides the most choices for stories, how to do things, I think. In some ways, too many choice and that trying to figure out just what you want to do in a game can be the hardest part.

              Mage enables you to get involved with a wide range of mysteries and weirdness. To explore stories about what separates you from others, and how you would apply your power. It is great for telling stories of slow spirals into obsession and madness, where your PCs can start with the best of intentions, and look back at the Chronicle's end to see how they became it's villains through small choices.

              Leverage the damage you can inflict upon others unwillingly or unwittingly with Paradox, Dissonance, and Nimbus.
              Add in some Lovecraftian Horror with Paradox, Abyssals, and Lower Depths.
              Play out classical Mystery stories that only a Mage could solve through their talents - and show them what happens when they grow too cocky and leap to conclusions.
              Add some sense of Cold War, opposition, and oppression with the Seers of the Throne.
              Throw players broken mirrors and curve-balls with the Mad.
              Run a spiritual trial or vision quest with Supernal Entities.

              Malkydel: "And the Machine dictated; let there be adequate illumination."
              Yossarian: "And lo, it was optimal."

                  Mage is a game about mystery more than any other line in CofD. So, for people that love multiple ongoing mysteries, that's great.

                  Having played...about four sessions so far in a 2e game, I have to say that it's been a blast. We have a hilarious player that has an Acanthus character and he really plays up the whole fool archetype. Also, none of us are very much into horror, so the ST plays down that aspect of the game.

                  In all honesty, if it isn't clicking for you AND you've played a session, then it just might not be the game for you. If you haven't played a session, I'd give it a try just to see if there's an aspect you might like.

                              Sure, vampires can hypnotize you into being a walking blood bag and make you enjoy it. Werewolves can tear you limb from limb. Prometheans poison the environment and warp your mind by simply existing. Changelings feed on your emotions. Demons steal bits of your identity that you sell to them.

                              But anything any other splat can do, mages can do. Maybe even better. They walk around, routinely rewriting the laws of reality to suit their purposes, and the universe sometimes doesn't even fight back. What else can you call a being who can cast a spell that makes it so that you suffered a third-degree burn a week ago, can conjure gamma radiation ex nihil, can return the dead to life... but a god? Yes, in many ways, a mage is a god. The best ones realize that it's probably not courteous to carve your name in North America so big that it's visible from orbit. The worst ones practically see it as their birthright to do that.

                              Yet at the same time, Mage 2e has a strangely hopeful tone. It's remarkably egalitarian: anyone, regardless of race, creed, nationality, class, sex, or sexual preference can become a mage. The bad guys want to create a system of haves and have-nots because they have delicate egos. A wrong has been written, and best-case scenario, you are taking a big, magical eraser and righting that wrong one person at a time.

                              Haberdasher's Requiem Conversions and Homebrew
