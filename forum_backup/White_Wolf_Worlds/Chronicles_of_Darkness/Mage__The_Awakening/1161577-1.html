                            Originally posted by Maris Streck View Post
                            This is just your opinion and I believe it makes no sense, no offense meant.
                            Actually, no, it's the Awakening RAW. Awakening has no learning times either mechanical or vague-narrative ones.

                            It also makes perfect sense in the context of how magic works in Awakening. Magic is not simply a skill you improve with practice and practice, and more practice. As a very Gnostic themed approach, Awakening handles magical advancement in terms of experiential knowledge of the Supernal via Mysteries rather than traditional concepts of skill improvement. You improve your Arcana and Gnosis by lived experiences (generating Beats and Experiences) that expand your intuitive conceptualization of the Supernal Truth. There is no logic or reason to how fast someone can accomplish this, because it isn't a matter of logic or reason.

                            Mages that go out and investigate the Mysteries of the world develop much faster than a mage that sits around studying ancient tomes, because they open themselves to a much wider array of mysteries and insights leading to gnosis (instead of regular knowledge). Reading books about magic is a narrow set of experiences and thus only allows a narrow trickle of insights.

                            Also, it's a game and a setup that makes going out and doing stuff better than sitting around studying in a library is kind of a 'duh' thing to include.

                            Your take is something more in line on how videogames work rather than role playing games and it's bound to lead to weird and shonen-like situations where the 15-years old boy is just as powerful as the ancient masters.
                            Mysteriously potent youths are a very common trope in the mythologies and media Mage is based on; enough that any overlap with another genre is irrelevant.

                            Awakening is not a game about playing a novice magician who might overpower any mundane individual through magic, but is dwarfed by the ancient masters in who's games you are but a pawn.

                            Awakening is a game about the line between a novice and true master is not the magical feats they can achieve, but the wisdom they have acquired in how to wield the power they possess. It is the Sorcerer's Apprentice writ larger, where the apprentice can enchant his cleaning equipment to life to do his chores for him, but lacks the understanding of consequences to realize why he shouldn't have done something beyond so beyond his current skills in controlling.

                            You get 1 beat every scene where a mentor teaches you. If you had a lessons for each in-game day, like Hogwarts, you'd reach your master's level at the end of the first three months. Three scenes with three different masters on three different subjects, like it would properly work in an organized school, and you'll have six arcana at 5 by the end of the year. Am I the only one to see how it would makes no sense at all?
                            You're not honestly applying the rules, which is why they don't make sense in application.

                            First off, this rule only applies to a Legacy tutor and their initiates. So you have to join a Legacy (costs 1 Arcane XP and Gnosis 2 in the first place...) before this comes into play. Hogwarts isn't a Legacy.

                            Second, Scene-logic is not meant to be applied to downtime-logic. 99% of class time in Hogwarts in the Harry Potter books is not in any scene, because that class time doesn't matter. So none of that class time would count. Only Scenes count, which means only narratively significant events count. Three months of Divination classes with zero events worthy of a Scene means zero progress towards another dot in Time or Fate.

                            Third, gaining an Arcane Beat in this fashion doesn't just require a Scene of instruction it requires, "...they interact over some mystically meaningful or emotional and intimate matter..." Since learning the Arcana and developing Gnosis is a matter of direct experience with Mysteries, the tutor's role is to help guide their students to Mysteries (and specifically the ones the Legacy was built to engage with), and shape those explorations into a meaningful experience. Harry showing off his learning of a new spell to Snape's disapproval of his arrogance of naivety might make for a Scene, but despite Snape being a teacher and Harry a student, it doesn't mean an Arcane Beat would be generated.


                            Exactly. You can't just skip common sense because you have an high enough number (=exp).
                            However, there's a big difference here. In the CofD 2e advancement paradigm, having a high number of Experiences is directly related towards not having to worry about "common sense," esp. regarding Arcane Experiences and raising Arcana/Gnosis. The player-facing methods of generating Beats means you end up with high numbers by doing the stuff that would pass the common sense smell test for you learning a new trait. The ST only needs to step in when players try to buy things that don't make sense with the events of the game, otherwise the learning experiences happened when you gained all the Experiences.

                            ---------

                            Also, you're wrong about the RAW for Ascension anyway. While Ascension has provided optional estimates of how long it takes to learn things, they are clearly not supposed to apply to player characters, and NPCs break them all the time. The actual RAW in the WoD regarding how fast it takes to learn something is that you can't raise something by more than one dot at a time; and generally not more than once per story. Even in Ascension character advancement follows the will of the plot, not some appeal to "realism."

                            No, it doesn't.

                            There is no XP system that makes sense from a realistic perspective on learning. Humans don't learn in a fashion that's conducive to a mechanical advancement system in a game.

                                            The linear-chargen/quadratic-advancement combo in the oWoD and nWoD 1e systems imply that the only practical way to get a trait above 3 dots is by buying it in character creation - and therefore the mechanically optimal PC starts as an absurdly narrow specialist, only to broaden in play. Using linear methods in both character creation and advancement avoids favoring specialists over generalists; a starting PC can be a generalist without being doomed to watch the specialists zoom past him to greater power. That's the real reason why CofD advancement is linear.
