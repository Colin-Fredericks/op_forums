    Wouldn't surprise me.

    The short version: Inferno covers demons who sort of operate by treating the material world the way archmages treat the Supernal — as a more-"real" source of power and influence whose inhabitants can be leveraged to grow.

    Every Infernal demon can Fall to Whispers, effectively becoming a Rank 0 parasitic thoughtform encouraging its human(/like) host to commit evil deeds and create or amend the demon's written description for greater power; some demons start out as Diaboli — corrupted ghosts, spirits, or goetia — though past a certain point it becomes unlikely they won't have progressed to the true demonic state of a Dominion.

    Dominions who used to be Immundi (corrupted spirits) make up the bulk of the loose category of true demons who shift the world toward sin by interfering with its material conditions — the way they push people to do evil is mostly by making the world a worse place to live in so that it's easier to abandon compassion and do things you know are harmful and wrong.

    For Inferno's purposes, the Maeljin are particularly powerful true demons; in Werewolf, their modus operandi is to empower agents from deep within the Wounds formed by atrocity and overwhelming negative resonance, which jives with the Lower Depths' main tactics of draining things out of the world and/or turning natives of reality into ways of draining things out of the world.

    And going back to the Hedge, but trying to keep it Mage lol.....is there a way that a Mage can protect himself from the Debt
    By not making deals with goblins. The only other regular source of it is from Goblin Contracts, which non-fae can't get without a stint as a hobgoblin.

    You might make an argument for allowing the Unbound Fate Attainment to let you try and shrug off incurring Goblin Debt, but since it's an abstraction of the broader phenomenon of "this is a paid service, however that manifests," it's hard to invoke it under any rationale besides potentially eventually becoming a hobgoblin constituting an alteration of your destiny —you're generally not tricked into it so much as offered the bargain under duress.

    or navigate the Hedge himself? For example, will Fate Arcanum help with that? Or Prime even? Or is that something that will problem addressed in Contagion?
    It's literally just handled with regular Chase mechanics, except that even without another person-scale participant the Hedge counts as a sort of loose time-limit, and also navigating the Hedge or otherwise interacting with it can prompt sudden changes to your surroundings.

    In general, if you're not a changeling then your best bet for moving quickly through the Hedge is — surprise! — stay on the path. The Hedge is "slower" on trods, especially powerful ones, and its Hedgespinning is most hampered on the main roads, where its consistent pool of eight dice doesn't get the 10-Again quality (it gets 8-Again in the Thorns and 9-Again everywhere else, and this is a roll that specifically requires it to get more than one success to accomplish anything). The old warnings get passed along for a reason.

    Without fae-specific advantages, your best bet for navigating the Hedge as a mage is going to be anything that makes winning a Chase easier — high Skills and Attributes, high Speed and Initiative, the ability to quickly gather information about your route and surroundings, and so on. Above all else, move quickly — the Hedge's dice pool for counter-navigating gets bigger with every turn of the chase.

        As to ways of dealing with fey things:

        In 1e, a mage with Fate 3 (IIRC) could use mana to create and use Glamour. (There were rules for most interactions established, actually, though some were better than others. At least it wasn't "No.")

        2e hasn't, to my knowledge, mentioned making Glamour, but Prime 1's Word of Command + Fate 1 can trigger things like gateways and tokens. Fate can manipulate an oath. Space 1's Correspondence can be used to identify a Key.

        Space is really good for navigation, in general, it bears noting. Ground Eater would be quite helpful in Hedge chases. Ban could be mighty useful with Fate to keep out naughty things. (perhaps naughty things like Hedgespinning in general. Surely a Pocket Dimension should provide some respite.)

        An argument can be made that Chaos Mastery and Strings of Fate (Fate 4) both serve as a means of manipulating the Hedge in a manner similar to Hedgespinning. The Unbound Fate Attainment would certainly apply versus some Hedgespinning.

        Depending on how you are interpreting "location" in the Hedge, Teleport or Co-Location hold some merit.

        Any magic that resists a hazard of the form spun would work against it, too, it bears noting. Thus, there probably shouldn't be a "shield vs. all Hedgespun attacks" under any Arcana. Protection from being Hedgespun, on the other hand, sounds reasonable for Fate 2. Control Heat could be brought to bear against a blaze. Ban could provide shelter from a wide array of things with proper Arcana backing it up.

        Biggest crossover headache is figuring out how to apply Withstand ratings versus Clash of Wills. In most cases, I suspect a Clash wouldn't apply because both effects occur, regardless. If the mage has some spell for locating something or navigating without fail, the Hedge should have some sort of Clash of Wills against that effect.

            Clash of Wills are for mutually exclusive supernatural powers. If the Hedge itself provides resistance against anything it would do so through an added Withstand rating.

            Shielding spells vs Hedgespun attacks by others grants a CoW like most or Shielding spells unless the nature of the spell and the attack means the spell should auto-succeed. I believe the latter is more common since CoW Shielding spells mostly apply against supernatural powers used directly against you, and Hedgespinning is more about shaping the environment to cause relatively mundane attacks.
            Shielding spells don't use Withstand ratings as part of their protection (unless they protect against other spells, in which case they add one instead of having to overcome one).

            Also, don't forget Mage Armour, which works likes usual.

                    They're above the pay-grade of mages to summon in any fashion that isn't equally accessible (and dangerous) to everybody else; the Faerie Favor Merit represents one of the safer methods, but otherwise you're going to have to catch the attention of a god and convince it by hook or by crook to give you lent access to things it owns and try not to get screwed over by the fine print.                                         Huntsmen aren't True Fey; they predate the True Fey in Arcadia. Some were bargained with and others coerced into service. Some may run free and others may operate in a mercenary capacity, presumably, but the ones that get sent out to hunt have been locked into service by removing their heart and replacing it with the will of their master.

                                        A mortal Bastion contains the Huntsman's heart. If removed, the Huntsman can't enter the physical realm. Discovering this detail would be a pretty difficult Mystery on its own, I imagine, but it unlocks a handful of interesting possibilities. Presumably, that heart is Connected to the Huntsman, so summoning one via Sympathy of its heart should be possible. This would obviously violate the rules of the magic keeping the Bastion hidden from the Huntsman and arguably qualify as forcing them to violate the will of their master. Clash of Wills would be involved and one could argue the Bastion protections would always go in the Gentry's favor. That doesn't prevent a dreamwalker from removing the heart from the Bastion, first, however, and avoiding that matter.

                                        Summoning an untitled Huntsman from Arcadia seems like it would either require a very special Mystery or visiting and making a deal or both. Not convinced there should be such an option. Mage being what it is, pulling out the heart and replacing it probably should be possible, but would be seriously wacky magic---well into homebrew territory. I'd probably consider that Fate-dominated soul manipulation, so Death and Fate would figure prominently. (Bare minimum of 4th dots for that, too, unless you assume that Hunsman Hearts are easy to extract and a normal part of what they are.)

                                        (Described as it is, the "undeveloped lands" in Arcadia of 2e sound and act a whole lot like a Lower Depth. Even with the True Fey presence, Arcadia fits that motif rather well, what with the hunger for mortal dreams. That could be an interesting theme to play with.)
