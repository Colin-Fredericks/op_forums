    Greetings,

    I am trying to make heads and tails of the changes to the Free Council and Silver Ladder in the second edition. It is my understanding that the main gist would be:
    • Silver Ladder is presented as meritocratic but non-democratic.
      Singapore. Meritocrats. ¨Although we all have human rights, we are not all equal in capabilities, and we have different roles to play in an Awakened society¨
    • Free Council is presented as democratic but non-technocratic.
      Sweden. Egalitarians. ¨All mages are created equal and we ought to be one mage, one vote society, inequalities strengthen the Exarchs¨
    Those are the main tenet that I see on both orders... Am I right? What I am missing, ignoring, or misrepresenting? Can someone explain in detail how both see Mage and Sleeper society functioning right now and posterior to the Exarchs defeated?

    Even better can someone align them to a real-world political party, movement, or ideology? (Classical Liberals, Utilitarians, etc)

    If they were newspapers or magazines, who is closer to The Economist, The Times, or The Financial Times?

    Please correct my assumptions and mistakes.

      Been awhile since I last checked ‘the big stuff’ like this, but…

      They have contrasting views on the cosmological role of Sleepers.

      The entire Diamond, plus the Pyramid, follows As Above So Below; that Supernal Symbols give substance and meaning to Fallen Patterns. They hold that Sleepers’ cultural ingenuity is not ‘true’ ingenuity, but results of being inspired by tuggings of Supernal resonance. Of course, they don’t dismiss the entire thing out of hand, because as far as anyone knows humans (Asleep or not) are the only beings who actively ‘refine’ the Supernal to the Fallen world that way, but the limit is obvious.

      The Council, along with a number of Nameless Orders (including the Tremere) (and I think the Scelesti?) follow As Below So Above. Human activities are not mere mirrors reflecting the Supernal, but are like lanterns and lighthouses to guide it to the Phenomenal World. As mages they of course admit how mighty the Supernal is, but they hold that without Patterns, its Symbols lose context and coherency. Which is why the Council values human culture and traditions and genius so much — they’re the frontiers of the Supernal, not its patsies.

      For political spectra… I’d frankly avoid that line, not because of worries of misrepresenting any ideology (though you should care about that too!), but because I think each of the Orders can potentially find a niche comfort spot in any human politics.

      For example, while the Free Council is often associated with liberal democracy and left progressivism in general, you could find a Libertine vehemently defending some ‘hidebound conservative’ thing like [insert here], because of its cultural significance.
      Because, hey, if something persisted throughout the ages despite wear-and-tear, surely it must have both Supernal and Fallen meaning, right?

      Likewise, the Silver Ladder is usually thought of as the conservatives, but a Theárch will likely support cases of ‘overthrowing the old order’ because anyone who has the guts and prowess to do so is clearly worthier than the now-stagnant ruling class.
      And hey, it’s not like they’re alien to cases of entitled elites forming out of the so-called enlightened revolutionaries — more-holier-than-thou, replaced by more-correct-than-thou. It’s just the Symbols of the Supernal reflecting itself in the social Patterns of the Fallen World.

      (If you find anything contradicting with the above from the written official material, that’s likely cuz 1) I’m writing this from memory in the subway without referring to the books, and/or 2) I forgot about them, so feel free to point things out)

          This seems pretty correct as far as I’m aware. The Silver Ladder believes all humanity deserves to be able to become a Mage, (and being a Mage is better than not), and that the person who leads should learned and wise, and have the ambition to overthrow the gods. They don’t like the state of the world, and are the only faction I’m aware of that has improving the Fallen World and other humanitarian efforts as a mission goal and ethos. In the perspective of the Silver Ladder, Sleepers are Mages that have been forcibly lobotomized or disabled by the Exarch’s curse. Unless they can overcome it, they need to be guided by those who can see and know the Truth. Those who are wise should guide those who aren’t. (This is the part that usually aggravates everyone else, since it can be easily interpreted as patronizing and self serving. Mind you, the Ladder can and will play second fiddle, they just usually are the only ones who want Consilium leadership positions.) A post Exarchs world probably has the Silver Ladder in the place of the Exarchs, and anyone can climb the Ladder to claim their birthright as a God if they wish. Think of it like all those stories where a evil tyrant is overthrown by a good Monarch, who then reigns wisely and well. Only the evil Ruler in question is God.

          On the other hand, the Free Council is believes everyone to be equal if not all in the same way, and are relatively fine with the world, if all the Seers were dead. Sleepers *are* Mages, just ones that are passively using their magic to create technology and learn science or folk beliefs. Honestly, Sleepwalkers probably get as much of a vote as Mages do in most Assemblies. A post Exarch world under the Free Council would probably not be that different, except there would be no formal government outside of votes, and being a Mage would be just a extension of another profession, like being cop or being engineer. Or potentially it could end up with Mage: the Ascension metaphysics, where belief and consensus is reality.

          I find the two Orders fascinating, because both hold rebellious ideologies, but in practice they are almost nothing alike.

          Oh you edited to ask about similar stuff IRL. I’m not particularly great at this, so please take what I say with massive grains of salt, but the Silver Ladder is kinda like hardcore Humanitarian, and Pro-Supernal-Magic for everyone, where the Free Council is more True Democracy than anything else.

                      Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
                      So does it make sense to have a Silver Ladder member very involved in scientific innovation?
                      Practical experts are the second-highest unAwakened rung on the Ladder's sorting hierarchy, below spiritual/philosophical leaders and above administrators and bureaucrats. Figuring out how to learn and manipulate the rules of the world is something the Order wants people to do.

                      Or a Free Councilor making cases for meritocratic governance?
                      "Trust the experts" is literally Free Council doctrine. Leadership positions just tend to be situational. The position of Strategos is not a universal one, and it exists where and while there's a need for it. Syndics likewise are just the thing that the moniker denotes, which is "people with enough sway in the Assembly to convince other Voters to vote one way or the other.

                      It's important to remember that Consilia and Assemblies aren't modes of government except in the broadest sense of "way the mages in an area have agreed to set the rules relating to stuff that goes beyond your personal affairs." They're local courts and forums, not different wings of the Bureau of Magical Vocations.

                          The Silver Ladder and the Free Council are very alike. They are the two Orders most concerned with the well-being of Sleepers. They are both host to great numbers of visionaries genuinely and self-sacrificingly committed to a utopian dream to empower and free humanity. As each of the two Orders tries to compensate for their characteristic flaw, they can even drift towards resembling the structure and practices of the other Order.

                          The Silver Ladder values solidarity. Thearchs believe in revolution that can construct an ideal and benevolent order which requires cooperation, commitment, and resistance to not only oppressors but to the systems that reinforce and proliferate the oppressive structure itself. They recognize the need for high-level consideration in order to carefully craft that order, but sometimes fall prey to a bias towards authoritative hierarchy and a preference for philosopher-kings.

                          The Free Council values self-determination. Libertines don't believe in trusting any form of wisdom that they cannot test against what they know. Wisdom does not come from one source but can and must be gathered from all levels of the community collectively. They're as passionate as the Thearchs in the need to demolish both oppressive groups and the structures that recapitulate that oppression, but are skeptical of hierarchies that could grow over time into pernicious structures themselves. The need to remain fluid, adaptable, and responsive to all those their community serves also makes them vulnerable to more dissolute focus and disorganized movement.

                          The Silver Ladder are communists. The Free Council are anarchists.

                                          The Mysterium is an academic and religious entity more than a political one. The Free Council, the Seers of the Throne and the Silver Ladder are all Orders that are basically defined by their 'grand vision' of how society should function, but the Mysterium isn't really all that concerned about 'society'. The Mysterium is mainly concerned with the acquisition of knowledge, the protection of their 'sacred mysteries' and the pursuit or spiritual enlightenment. It's not a political party, it's a mystery cult.

                                          Meanwhile, the Adamantine Arrow is more concerned with one's own conduct and the general 'way of the warrior' philosophy than any larger Order-wide vision for the world. 'Existence is War', which means that you must learn to fight, so you can protect yourself and what matters to you. 'Service is Mastery', which means that an ideal Arrow warrior must fight for more than just themselves, but different Arrows all pledge themselves to different purposes. 'The Supernal is the Self', which means that your conduct is echoes out into the world beyond you and is reflected in the Supernal, which also lends further importance to the Order's general quest for personal excellence. 'Enlightenment is Honour', which again means that your worth as a warrior is determined by your conduct, your ability to do your duty and overcome challenges the 'right' way. 'Adaptability is Strength' is just generally good advice to keep in mind.
                                          The Arrow is an army, in a sense, but it might just as well be compared to martial philosophical school-... One which places a particular focus on the means, more than the ends.

                                          I'd get into the Guardians, because they are somewhat more political than the Arrow or Mysterium, but I need to get to bed. it's also worth remembering that the Silver Ladder serves as the political core of the Diamond and thus their ideas for how 'Awakened society' should function have some degree of influence over the other three orders.
