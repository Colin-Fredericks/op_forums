    Originally posted by rwknoll View Post
    Dumb question time! What happens if you cast a spell like Divination with advanced duration? Do you get to ask a new set of questions again at a later time based on your ritual casting interval? Or could you choose to structure the Imago in such a way that you can ask your questions later rather than at the time of casting? Or would it be both, if the duration is long enough (say, a day or a month)?
    Looking at page 113 in the core book, it says "If a spell would logically have an immediate effect, but is cast with advanced duration, the effect recurs at every multiple of the character’s Gnosis-based ritual casting time, until the Duration runs out". I always ruled the answers to Divination as happening instantly, so you don't need to extend duration just to hear longer answers. In this case specifically, I would say you can ask up to [Potency] questions at any time and you get a refresh on the number available on your Gnosis based interval. So if its one hour and you casted at Potency 3, you can ask all 3 instantly and make the spell "do nothing" for an hour or spread your questions around during that interval or use less questions than the max and get back to 3 every hour. The benefit is that you are able to divine what a target does during that time, even when they are outside of sensory range, which can be a major advantage to any chessmaster type of mage.

                        What you describe has nothing to do with Sympathetic Range unless you're targeting the familiar itself at Sympathetic Range. Nothing it sees or interacts with has any inherent sympathetic connection to the familiar, and much less to you.                                     In page 185 of the core book, under "The Past can be Rewritten", it suggests that the mage would return to a present that doesn't know them, even if they died. In this way, their Time magic defies causality and resolves the temporal paradox.

                                    Because Unmaking can do everything Patterning can do and more, it means that based on Rewrite History, that the caster can choose to send either to the past. The main restriction is that they cannot make this choice for those born to their powers, such as werewolves or spirits. So they can choose to wield magic when they would have been Sleepers.

                                    What happens if the moment they are in overlaps with their time of Awakening and they aren't in the right circumstances? Probably nothing to them, since an Awakening is an act of Imperium done by one of the mightiest Talismans in existence. Some random Traveler's meddling in Time magic probably stands no chance of messing with a sealed rite. So they likely "don't experience it" because the Watchtower already has their signature.

                                    It would, however, likely create a big domino effect on their history. "No Awakening" means no alert sound to nearby mages, leading to no Order finding them, locating them later or someone/something else getting to them first. It could also lead to a very confused past version trying to solve this Mystery.

                                    On the other hand, if they somehow figure out how to experience their Awakening again in the past, but this time with the answers, then it likely leads to one direction: Bad Things happen. Very, very, VERY Bad Things. Exarchial interference, archmage meddling, Harrowing or worse. Then their biggest concern then would be fixing it somehow.

                                        I play it as a flashback where the player controls their historical self as they were at that point in time. The only thing they bring from the future is their knowledge of what choices they wanted to make differently. Thematically, the point of the spell is to make different choices, not overcome obstacles using power or skill you didn't possess at the time.

                                        I'll also note that sheer dice pool and reach restrictions make it inconvenient to visit a point in time when the subject was vastly different for more than a couple of turns anyway.

                                        As a general rule I employ the most restrictive interpretation of time travel rules I can. Players tend not to like it when the cabal Acanthus goes on a substantial solo mission to the past to upend large parts of the story that the cabal has built. Nothing ruins a story quite like "Oh, he's behind it all? I time travel back to that scene a few months back where we were all chatting in the pub and murder him."

                                                12-15-2020, 11:18 AM
                                                The Reach effect for Serendipity is a Lasting consequence of casting the spell. Casting Serendipity with the Reach effect causes the subject to know something about how a skill may come in handy in the future in an unexpected way. Mechanically, that results in the skill substitution whenever that foreseen circumstance arrives. From the in-game perspective of the character, the omen foretells exactly what skills will come in handy and when. However from the player's perspective it's not decided until they want to spend the bonus.

                                                It's a Knowing spell. To quote a meme, "if you know, you know."

                                                            The main things to consider when deciding on Verge vs Emanation:
                                                            • City-sized at maximum, or boundless?
                                                            • Accessibly by walking into it, or only by Thurae or other rare circumstances?
                                                            • Inhabited by (mostly) cryptids, or by supernal entities?
                                                            • Shadow, or no Shadow? Note that this is super important for Spirit mages because Emanations just don't have native spirits. There's no Shadow and they're inhabited by supernal beings.
                                                            • The rules don't mention this, but I personally also rule that Emanations are disconnected from the Underworld and there are no ghosts.
                                                            Regarding the last two points, I guess you can say some Emanation has it's own versions of the Shadow and/or Underworld and it's own versions of spirits and ghosts for Spirit and Death magic to work on if thematically appropriate. But I keep those as facets of the Fallen World only. If I want a ghost-themed setting I would just use the Underworld itself rather than an Emanation anyway.

                                                            The narrative purpose of both Verges and Emanations is to enable the ST to have weird settings with fun themes like "Dinosaur World," "Steampunk City," etc... Like if as the ST you want something akin to the Shadow or the Underworld but with a different theme and you don't want to have to come up with the whole cosmology of its complex relationship to the Fallen World. So first you design your setting and then you pick whether it's a Verge or Emanation according the above factors.
