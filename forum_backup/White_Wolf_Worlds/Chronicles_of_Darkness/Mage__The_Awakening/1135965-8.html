Given that I don't like combat heavy games, either to play in or run, that makes paradox kind of iffy.

    As for what I have issues with? Paradox is a joke. Obsession needs a stick. Nimbus is a pain in the butt to track in combat, meaningless out of combat. Scrutiny is too time consuming and needs to be group oriented isntead of individual. Some order merits need revamping. Too much number crunching. Active spell limits are too forgiving. I don't like having mages and the abyss be the only real threat to PC mage cabal. I don't like how so much of the game seems to be balanced around combat.     And has been proven grossly false by the scientific community. It is, to put it bluntly, a Lie. Some people might have the inclination towards corruption, but others will not. Those latter who Awaken will not become corrupt.

    Furthermore, the type of corruption the saying is refering to (rulership) has nothing to do with mage hubris. Mage hubris is a result of Reach, risking 'dox and carelessness with magic, with a touch of "mind control and murder bad," which, given the mage toolset, is generally easy to avoid. You can be a corrupt despot, have Enlightened Wisdom and not fall into hubris.

    Some are, some aren't. Like all organizations, you have to take each one individually and see if its scary/bad or not. Generalization is generally (ha!) bad.

    I'm not understanding how Paradox is a joke. From my reading, Paradox is as big an issue as the ST is willing to make it. Meaning the ST can add more modifiers or generate circumstances that make Paradox a bigger issue.

    I don't understand why "obsession needs a stick." Does it really need anything? It works as a lead or direction tool. Nimbus can still transmit conditions and communicate influence outside of combat. Don't get why scrutiny is "too time consuming" or "needs to be group oriented," by that I mean, why does it "have" to be that way - when it has already been established and has reasons for working the way it currently does?

    Too much number crunching seems highly subjective. Don't crunch as many numbers? I think we're still better off than 1e and where Exalted was (Pretty sure current Exalted resolved those issues, but I haven't played it to figure out if it has less crunching than Mage currently has.) What makes you think there are too many numbers to crunch in the current system? I found it to be vastly reduced from the previous iteration, let alone the chaos of Classic WoD. I suppose it has less crunching than D&D's "choose a spell, roll the dice, do the damage" formula - but even then those games can be pretty complex if the DM sets up the challenges appropriately.

    Active spell limits are too forgiving? These all seem like personal issues that don't necessarily equate to a less horrifying game. You might personally feel that way or want a grittier game, but it seems like you're free to make it grittier based on your own understanding of the rules and what you want out of a game.

    Why are mages and the abyss the only real threats to PCs? Is your ST not utilizing other splats or creatures? Your ST is literally able to design any bizarre or enigmatic nightmare and pump them full of abilities and countermeasures. Your ST could even throw them within a magical deadzone and have them exist as supernaturals outside of magic. Other splats can come at you in complex waves of political intrigue and resource management. Again, it seems like you aren't challenging people as an ST, or you aren't being challenged enough by your ST.



    The whole Power Corrupts thing is sort of a misnomer. Corruption either needs to be defined within the context of the law, and even then some people can be said to be "corrupt" as a consequence of exceptions or overriding the current conception. It could literally mean anything from money misappropriation to willfully not fulfilling campaign promises to thirst for war to commanding the execution of others, etc. Who gets to use the label of corrupt? Often times it is some set of observers, not you. Meaning you could understand your actions as corrupt, but you could also understand them as permissible. At the end of the day this depends on how you personally feel about stuff and how your ethics work, that doesn't make you any less or more corrupt in the eyes of observers. Your ST can put pressure here on your actions based on the perceptions of NPCs, politics, and established Orders or groups.

    Your ethical behavior is not necessarily a resolved issue because it can be influenced by so many things within the context of CofD. There is tons about "doing the right thing in spite of the consequences" when talking about virtuousness.

    The scientific community has shown that people are more likely to take advantage of things when they aren't accountable. Whether or not this translates into them being "good or bad" is highly subjective, though. People can also be influenced by their environments to commit decisions they otherwise wouldn't, as is the case with the Milgram experiment (or the culture of mage society the ST has set out for the PCs.) In CofD, people can be influenced by supernatural circumstances or beings to commit actions they otherwise wouldn't. Those acts don't translate into nothing. They could very well land within the context of breaking points.

    Crime statistics show most people that steal are not necessarily rational thinkers, but opportunists who see something and take it in the moment: a rush, the thrill, intrigue, what-if, etc. Rational-thought thefts are a thing, but they aren't the majority of all theft related crimes. The same can be said for many violent crimes or manslaughter cases of murder (which make up the majority of murder.)

    People unintentionally hurt each other or kill each other often. This is often in spite with the knowledge that, typically, these people are perfectly "well-adjusted" or non-murdering types.

    People don't always rationally think through the processes and instead are many times accidental contributors. These are examples against rational choice theories, or the belief people are bad or making poor decisions intentionally. This says to me that "corruption" is liable to surface without intent. It could even surface directly within the context of NOT trying to be corrupt.

    What does this translate into gamewise? An ST staying on top of your decisions and making it hard to make the right choice. Or, that the right choice has unintended consequences.

    It seems like you're looking at the game from a purely mechanical standpoint, but I don't think that does it justice because the ST can lend a lot to make it more forgiving or less. Mage, in and of itself, probably works fine for people that aren't calculating all the ways to ignore penalties and reduce the mechanics to what you've outlined as your gripe with Hubris degeneration.
