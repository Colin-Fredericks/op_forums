                Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                Ruins of Time Before seems to not agree with you on that.
                If we go into real philosophy here: it's a question of semantics, but Elfive is right and the book is wrong . Per definition there cannot be anything "before" or "after" if no time exists. Time exists because of the expansion of space. So there can be something outside of time, but not before. Tachyons from the big bang, if they exist, would be an example.

                Back to the creation myth of CoD. I honestly din't read into this, but what I can fathom from this thread gives a couple of logical problems. If the Supernal is basically the sum of everything that can be (can come or is in existence) and the Abyss is the sum of everything that cannot be (is contrary to existence), the Supernal cannot logically have emerged from the Abyss. What might have happened is that before existence, there was a state of every concept that can be and cannot be together (Supernal + Abyss) and as the first act of creation those two split from each other into the Abyss and the Supernal. This is in fact pretty much the base line for every creation myth, that ever existed. It has also to be noted, that it is impossible to define "what cannot be" without defining "what can be" so before the split, there cannot have been an Abyss nor the Supernal.

                                                            At this point I'm honestly curious and baffled - what's the meaning of all this?

                                                            Some mages in the past decided to name the not-yet-named toxic anti-reality from outside proper reality with the names of the primordial progenitors of the world from their own cultural mythologies, because that's honestly what it seems to look like back then.

                                                            It's like claiming Indians (Native Americans) really are from India, just because some Europeans in the past decided to call them Indians because they believed they were from India. And after a nuclear apocalypse or something, researchers in the far future dig up our age's documents, and think Indians are a really well-traveled people who lived on two continents at once.

                                                            Sharing names does not make two things the same. Sure, maybe they were named that way in the first place because they had common characteristics, but that's where the similarity ends.

                                                            If we can claim the Abyss is the alpha-and-omega of the world because people named it after the primordial forces of the world which it shares common behavioral patterns with, then why can't we just go back to the old Arcadia Problem? Or better yet, claim all the Acathartoi, Abyssal Intruders, Goetic Demons, Pandemonic Entities, and the Unchained are from the same source because they're all called "demons?"
