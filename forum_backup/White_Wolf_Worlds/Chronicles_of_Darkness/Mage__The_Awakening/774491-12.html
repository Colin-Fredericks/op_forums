    Originally posted by Tambov View Post

    Really? Name one theme, just one, and it will invariably apply to Werewolf as well.

    First, I never said that the games might not have some similar themes, but the stresses and importance of different aspects are important.

    In Mage, a primary theme is mages' alienation from humanity; only mages recognize the prison of the Lie. Every sleeper has a shard of the abyss infecting their very soul. This not only causes increased risk of Paradox and Dissonance, but Quiescence, a injury to the actual souls of sleepers and the absolute inability to remember supernal or abysmal effects. The Quiescence has nothing to do with willpower or resolve. It's an extra-dimensional curse upon all of sleeping Humanity.

    "Lunacy is the deep down, ancestral fear of the wolf at the door." (p.100). It reinforces the themes of werewolves as the ultimate hunters, Werewolf's primary focus. While similar in that it alienates werewolves from humanity, it's not nearly as fundamental to the game line as the Lie is to Mage. Notably, unlike Paradox, Dissonance and Quiescence, Lunacy and other inherent aspects of Humanity do not negatively affect werewolf abilities or powers.

    If you require some mechanical balancing for justification, while Lunacy offers humans some opportunity to resist, it similarly offers nothing even remotely analogous to the detrimental effects of Paradox or Dissonance, which automatically affect mages without a roll to resist.
