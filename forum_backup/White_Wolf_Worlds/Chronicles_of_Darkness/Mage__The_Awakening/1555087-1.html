    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
    Personally, I find some of the premise of Annunaki as bubbles of coherence within the Abyss to kind of shift it away from what I regard as some of the more intriguing prospects of that plane.
    It is, in fact, objectively the case, because, to use the most direct example, that's how Gulmoth work. Abyssal beings as discrete phenomena are not ruinous to reality because of some intrinsic protean quality; they are ruinous to reality because the principles they work on are antithetical to the portion of reality that needs to not be impossible in order to function Anumerous does not need to be able to pull a heretofore unforeseen aspect of his powers out of his back pocket to continue to be an anti-symbol of Numbers Gone Wrong that paves the way for the unmaking of ordered reality. The Third of Two is not going to become the Gears of Chaos, because it is the Third of Two and nothing else. The Dur-Abzu exist to negate the symbolism of their Supernal counterparts and they aren't going to change the thrust of that counter-symbolism purely for the sake of embodying the destruction of reason, hope, and being in a broader sense.

    Greater Abyssal Beings are universes unto themselves; not all of them are hospitable to Phenomenal humanity, but the ones that are are so in a consistent fashion, and that does not stop them from destroying the poor fool who pokes around the wrong part of their awful systems any more than the Earth is unable to consume an amateur volcanologist.

    None of this detracts from the Void itself being the chaotic boundary between the Ideal and the Real — "sufficiently powerful beings can create self-contained worlds where things are more predictable, but all of them ultimately degrade reality" is straight from Imperial Mysteries, and it echoes similar circumstances in Arcadia and the Underworld re: bubbles of more ordered (albeit strange) existence amid a shifting bed of chaos far from material reality. Yes, it might all collapse some day because its god got bored, but that's still the end of a world, not business as usual within its context.

    I've mulled over the Abyss's presentation in the books as specifically more than just shapeless cosmic horror for long enough to recognize that "anti-symbol" isn't a meaningless abstract concept when placed next to the aggregate trends of Abyssal beings and antinomian magic. You have to be able to use the tools the game gives you in a more robust sense than implying a Rank 9 pure sodium hypercube the size of the Sun is minutes away from popping into existence from pure possibility-space if the intention of a game is to engage with the Abyss as a narrative device with variety and nuance.

            Originally posted by Rosencroft View Post
            Thank you for all answers, and if I may, can I ask this?
            What are the odds of humanity surviving/existing in a Lower Depth realm that lacks Death ( nothing stays dead ) compared to a crafted pocket realm a size of a small planet?
            Provided, there is water and local "flora" and "fauna"
            Lower Depths realms are governed by Death Gods, and it isn't entirely clear if they have a hand in their creation, but if not they're entirely natural.

            That means if the ST wants such a place to exist, it exists. If they don't, it doesn't. And on top of that they'll have their Laws.

            Human colonizers don't have any means to either create, promote or locate such places. And Dead Dominions are finite in number, so just because something is possible it doesn't mean it exists.

            So... the odds of surviving there depends entirely on the specific realm, but the odds of such a place existing to begin with are extremely low, the odds of it being without exceptional danger that may impede a large colony entirely are also extremely low, and the odds of finding it are also quite low. The journey will also be dangerous. And then you have to ponder your relationship to local gods.

            And in every case it all depends on the denizens of the area. The truth of the matter is that, for one reason or another, truly hostile environments are actually rarer in the otherworlds than on Earth. Extreme weather, dryness, exposure to sun or wind, all those are at most as common than on Earth, frequently rarer or more manageable in some way. What really makes them dangerous are the denizens, and those can usually get pretty strong.

            Whenever you go, you can expect Ephemeral Entities of Rank 1 or 2 to be around regularly and be frequently hostile, and Entities of greater rank are close enough to take notice of you sooner or later, all the way up to Rank 5 at least, or the equivalent in power in non-Ephemeral entities. In a Dead Dominion you'll have to deal with an average at least 1 Rank higher (more the deeper it is), which is a problem, but they're also the most human-like in the otherworlds, so you can at least try to talk with many of them.

            It all depends, then, on whatever "lives" in the Dominion. Living humans have a lot to offer that they may want to take, or may want to negotiate so it isn't eliminated. Depending on many things the colony may be either quickly under siege, have a harsh but manageable life, to even raised as cattle or actively patronized by local powers. Learning about the local denizens and how to deal with them is an important thing here.

            Now, all that is before adding the "lacks Death" part. That's as Satchel said, it will be a very weird environment and probably quite dangerous and toxic.

                Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                Lower Depths realms are governed by Death Gods, and it isn't entirely clear if they have a hand in their creation, but if not they're entirely natural.
                Chiming in because this looks like a mixup I've seen before: The Lower Depths are extremely inhospitable portions of thin reality that are generally missing one or more things necessary for life, as metaphysically divorced from the rest of the Phenomenal World as the Phenomenal is from the Supernal — they're the Hell to the Supernal's Heaven, to the point where the Inferno was originally what "the Lower Depths" referred to before some circles needed to be squared re: different books describing them different ways, and the other semi-defined parts of the cosmology that share the label now are Duat from Mummy and the place powerful Strix can use the Dark Places Manifestation to open a gateway to in Vampire.

                "Off the map and wants to eat us" is the irreverent shorthand that was used at one point to describe Lower Depths on the forums, and the Alexandrian Dark Era mentions them in its cosmology primer with the distinctive phrasing of "Not all of them are evil, but none of them are good." What little we know of most of them doesn't really involve capital-L Laws.

                Dead Dominions are part of the Lower Mysteries, the part of the Underworld past the Rivers that separate them from the Autochthonous Depths on the top layer of that world (not counting ghostly Twilight/Neter-Khertet); the chthonians that can be found in the Rivers and Ocean of the Underworld may have some connection to the Lower Depths, but they inhabit a part of the cosmology that isn't directly antithetical to life and can be consistently returned from by anyone with the ability to open its doors with freely-used magical powers (as opposed to ritual Keys).
