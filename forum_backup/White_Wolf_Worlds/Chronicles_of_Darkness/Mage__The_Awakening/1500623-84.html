    Mage in particular has been able to organise pretty globally for as long as there has been mages (mostly via the Astral, meeting and sharing knowledge without physically being in the same place, overcoming language barriers with idea, but accessible to any Mage who knows about the Temenos...)

    Which likely helped the proto-Seers of the Throne organise.

    Rapt seem unlikely to organise on any scale. Neither do Banishers (though I care very little for Banishers). The Tremere might have a global scale, but are likely busy hiding/erasing their presence...

    Most mages are busy with their personal Obsessions, and only interested in global events if it leads them to further their study. Sure some might be interested in Order aswell, and that can have global consequences, but usually that can make up an Obsession aswell. Fundamentally Mages are written as addicts, addicted to mysteries, and most mysteries aren't global.

    The God-Machine as an Obsession is liable to drive most Mages Rapt if the pursue it deeply (or killed/assimilated by Angels). Similar issues with the Judges, or trying to fix the Underworld (Reapers probably aren't the only agents working for the Chthonic Gods).

                                                        Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                                        I mean more in the sense that you are limited in how many Yantras you can benefit from but you are not limited in how many bonus dice from teamwork you can benefit from.
                                                        Yes, but let's take an example.

                                                        You have a dice pool of 4, (after all of the yantras, ritual durations, willpower, whatever else is accounted for).

                                                        You have one assistant who has one less gnosis and one less of the relevant Arcanum. So they have a dice pool of 2, right?

                                                        Either they fail (and in two dice that is 50/50), in which case you added some paradox risk for no gain. Or they succeed, in which case they could have simply cast the spell seperately and given the full effect to the imago.

                                                        Alternatively, you really want to push things. So you go to -1. Your assistant is at -3.

                                                        Now you get a there more assistants, and hope that all of them rolling chance die. You hope that they roll enough successes to bring you up to more than a chance die and get a real shot at successfully casting the spell - you are in the same situation as above, except now you risk them getting a dramatic failure and giving you a -4 penalty (according to the teamwork rules).

                                                        Again, you are better off having everyone cast seperately and hoping one person succeeds.

                                                        Alternatively (and it has been pointed out to me that this may be me misreading the rules), if you have assistants who don't have the require Arcanum dots just roll [Gnosis -3] (possobly adding willpower) - without adding yantras or subtracting spell factors - then you could have a Gnosis 5 character actually adding to your chances of having a better than chance die dice pool (when they can't simply cast it by themselves). But this makes assistants who aren't full participants better than assistants who are full secondary casters.

                                                        So that is arguably not the rules as intended.

                                                        So finally i conclude, you should have every secondary caster rolls [Gnosis + Arcanum/Gnosis -3] add those dice to your dice pool after deciding spell factors (or possibly before deciding spell factors) - so you are playing in to the risk of failure vs getting the most bang for your buck, but now you are risking being on a chance die and hoping the secondary casters add enough dice to get a real chance of success.

                                                        And I'm happy that this house rule incentives ritual teamwork without making it either pointless or massively overpowered (which I think is that case if you let the secondary casters roll Gnosis + Arcanum + Yantras, but choose their own spell factors or not take any spell factor penalties...).

                                                            Originally posted by orathaic View Post
                                                            Yes, but let's take an example.

                                                            You have a dice pool of 4, (after all of the yantras, ritual durations, willpower, whatever else is accounted for).

                                                            You have one assistant who has one less gnosis and one less of the relevant Arcanum. So they have a dice pool of 2, right?
                                                            Why, in this hypothetical use of a system where you want the secondary actors to roll more successes, are you assuming that the point is to maximize spell factors through penalties, much less that the primary actor is working with what can rightly be called the bare minimum reasonable odds of success to cast a spell that their less-talented secondary actor is able to cast normally?

                                                            Just because the spellcasting rules lean hard into the "only one success is needed to succeed" part of ChroD's base system doesn't mean the other reason you want more dice is gone, particularly when the leader still benefits from having the spell as a Praxis or Rote.

                                                            The two core Merits that affect teamwork spellcasting remove the "unskilled penalty" for secondary actors (and increase the bonus they contribute on success by one) and grant the primary actor 8-Again, respectively. Setting aside the options that provide extra steps or Reach to the primary spell factor, an exceptional success can bypass Withstand, generate Mana, or create Conditions that grant Arcane Beats, all of which are sufficiently major for a mage to tap their fellows for assistance, particularly if they don't have the Gnosis, Yantras, or stamina necessary to build a more conventional Imago on their own in a reasonable timeframe.

                                                            I am also curious why the assumption is that the casters will roll for and release Paradox in this situation. By definition, any teamwork casting is working with one more Reach than would be available to a mage casting in an action scene, and containing a Paradox does not penalize the spellcasting roll. The most likely situation where Paradox is an issue, outside of hypothetical wildly inadvisable constructions, is that the primary caster's Imago uses more Reach than one or more secondary casters can safely incorporate, whether because they're an Adept upscaling an Apprentice-level spell or because they're using a Rote the rest of their team haven't learned.

                                                            Like, the point of teamwork casting is very much not to keep your net dicepool as low as possible and flip a coin on whether the firework explodes as planned, it is to compound the advantages of ritual casting to allow uncommon breakthroughs or cut down on the overall number of ritual intervals needed to achieve a desired result.
