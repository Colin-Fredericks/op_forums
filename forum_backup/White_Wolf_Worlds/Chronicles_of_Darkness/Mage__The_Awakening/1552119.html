                      The obvious problem you're going to run into is that Arcana are generally not abstract and powers like Embeds are built on complex frameworks of the sort that take archmagic to create and mess with, because the Arcana are building blocks and spells meddle with concrete phenomena, even if those phenomena are esoteric things like sympathetic links.

                      No amount of spellcasting is going to allow a mage to e.g. simply reach out and target the concept of their credit card debt without going through the vectors of who they owe or where that information is stored, at least not with any kind of fine control — Fate can be used to accomplish desired outcomes as long as you aren't fussed about the methods or consequences, but spirits form a useful resource base for mages to draw upon even without their powers being Paradox-proof.

                      The other problem is that… well, not to put too fine a point on it, conceptually it sounds like the Arcanum is too general to be balanced; its Mage Sight gives you information with no constraints on type and its hypothetical spells either require another Arcanum to do anything or target abstract phenomena too diffuse to fit into conventional Scale factors. All Arcana are tools for understanding and manipulating the world, so what can Data do that can't be easily done by another Arcanum, given the limits of Awakened spellcasting, and what mechanical systems might you have in mind that it would introduce and/or work with, if necessary?

                                  I think it keeps running into the problems Satchel described.

                                  Every Arcanum has practices about information. How does Data differentiates from them, so that it can acquire information, but doesn't simply trumps every other Arcana in doing so?

                                  What can it do in practice that is distinct from other Arcana?

                                  Basically, to make an Arcanum you have to go through each and every Practice and think how it would work in concrete use. It has to fulfill four criteria:

                                  1 - It has to clearly do something with each Practice. Not just an abstract explanation, but an actual, concrete use, with clear targets, effects and use of the basic game mechanics;

                                  2 - It has to be complete in itself. While some effects would certainly need other Arcana, you can't be required to take any other Arcanum to be able to use this one with every Practice;

                                  3 - It has to be distinct. As many Practices as possible should be able to do something no other Arcanum can, or at least different Practices shouldn't do things the same Arcanum does;

                                  4 - It has to be limited. Unless you plan to overhaul the other Arcana, the new one shouldn't be clearly superior to others, preferably at no Practice whatsoever.

                                  So, for example, it has to have Knowing effects, at least one that it can use alone, but this Knowing effect shouldn't simply acquire the same information other Arcana acquire, much less be better at it than any particular Arcanum at their own purview.

                                  Finally, we should keep in mind that the purview of an Arcanum is the phenomenon it acts upon, not the effect it produces. A Data Arcanum shouldn't acquire information about anything, but acquire information about information itself. How does it look like? What concrete, easy to grasp examples can be given?

                                  Unless you can work at least half the Practices through this, I don't think you have a fleshed enough concept, and I'm having problems seeing this particular concept through.

                                  Data wasn't a very good idea back in Ascension. In Awakening it seems even harder to work with.

                                      Ok
                                      - Knowing: Detect and interpret social or symbolic data in the environment. Recognize someone’s official status, affiliations, reputational echoes, or the social meaning of a document, crest, or inscription.
                                      Example: Be able to see things a good deductionist would see looking on a person like trauma and attitude - from their body language, resources - from their atire, ocupation from behavioral quirks and so on.

                                      - Unveiling: Be able to decrypt and understandhidden data like cyphers or unknown languages both in voice and writing
                                      Example: Decoding a ancient tablet in a forgotten language.

                                      Compeling: Be able to nudge a bit the meaning of a data structure like a document or program.
                                      Example: Changing the name on the document to be interpreted with someone else (not physicaly just in understanding)

                                      Veiling: Encrypting your messages and worlds. Hiding the meaning of the trail you left behind.
                                      Example: making so your muddy shoe tracks are interpreted as animal tracks or as simple debree.

                                      Shielding: Prevents the data a character left behind from forming sympathetic connections with him and being connected to him through association.
                                      Example: The signature he left in the hotel cannot be traced to him and is unreadable.

                                      Fraying: Damages the data making it hard to decypher.
                                      Example: Corrupts a computer file so the system registers it as unopenable.

                                      Perfecting: Restores data to its former shape. Refines the information allowing to read between the lines. Gain dots in abilities.
                                      Example restoring the information from a burned note.

                                      Weaving: Change the interpretation of the given information.
                                      Example: Appear to be rich, important, one of the factory employees.

                                      Unraveling: Distort the meening of a given information
                                      Example: Disolve the conotations of what a wanted poster means.

                                      Patterning: Allows creating new relations between data structures
                                      Example: The barista recodnises the characters watch as the markings of his hiden cult.

                                      Unmaking: Deleting information.
                                      Example: No one knows this man.

                                      Making: Creates new data.
                                      Example: Fireballs are normal in this town.
