            Claiming is a fuzzy manifestation. You could have a perfect merging of two disparate entities (In which case you have a third personality that emerges from the other two being suppressed or similar), or you could have one entity being more in control than the other. You could have a Claimed person able to recover from having been Claimed, or the Claiming entity could have done so much damage that the person they Claimed will die without that bond. Mechanically the effects of being Claimed are fairly similar, but exactly how it works and what it looks like narratively will be dependent on the entity that uses the manifestation while always being consistent for that entity.

                    For comparison, the big meta reason Awakened magic can't force an angel to betray its Mission is because that would be giving mages who hadn't crossed the Threshold the ability to make Unchained demons.

                    Mage doesn't include Claim on its list of Manifestation Conditions, doesn't allow Awakened magic to raise an ephemeral being's Rank by more than 1, addresses Geister as a subset of Rank 4-5 ghosts, and cannot provide any spell as precedent for "give this entity a Manifestation that allows it to, with no prerequisite Condition, touch a dying mortal to forge an agreement represented by a template on the mortal and a Condition on the geist, both of which only go away when the host dies for good."

                    That's not a Master Moros maneuver, that's stuff that archmages fuck around with for an art project.

                    It takes a very sideways reading of Mage to interpret "this other gameline uses a nonstandard Manifestation to represent the thing that makes its PCs" as "Mages, who don't even have the more common restricted Manifestation for weird pseudo-permanent merged possession included in their copy of the rules, can't make permanent alterations to the basic makeup of a target with magic, and mathematically can't turn a normal ghost into a geist under their own presentation of what a geist is, can give a ghost the ability to provide themselves as the basis for an indefinite and undispellable gameline-level supernatural template to a mortal character that will last beyond the spell itself."

                    That's… extremely not how the Bargain works.

                    "Make the Bargain with yourself" is only ever speculated to have happened with characters' own Doppelgangers, which are not subject to the same temporal constraints as dying characters themselves on account of being separate entities. Even if a dog had the capacity to receive and accept the offer, it's not getting that offer from its own ghost; the best case is that that ghost might Possess or Claim its own dead body like human ghosts regularly do.

                        To be fair, it was stated during the Geist 2e development process that having the Bargain Manifestation was what defined a ghost as a Geist. That was changed sometime before the Kickstarter preview. Now the definition is Rank 3+, and does not have to include the Bargain Manifestation. I hope these instances are addressed before the final release of Geist 2e.
