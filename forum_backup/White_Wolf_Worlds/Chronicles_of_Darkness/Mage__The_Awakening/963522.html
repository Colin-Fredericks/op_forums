                                  I believe the official history of the Seers and the Ladder is that they were a single organization that split: They were the mages who went seeking after the hidden powers that had caused the Fall. They found the Exarchs.
                                  The Silver Ladder responded to the discovery of the tyrant kings of the Lie with defiance. The Seers of the Throne decided they would rather serve.
                                  I find it very strange how the Mage forums have an underlying current of 'let's see if we can justify the Seers of the Throne.' Their justification is the same many people in history and the present would give: Personal security and profit. Any corporation that uses child labor, any corrupt politician, any human trafficker: They justify themselves with self-interest. That's one major foundation of what the Seers are: Mages who chose self-interest and power over freedom and defiance. The Silver Ladder is still human - Thearchs have flaws, the organization has some ideas as a whole that I profoundly disagree with, but they do stand for human self-determination in the face of tyrant symbols of literal oppression.
                                  In short, the Ladder has ideals. It stands for a better world. One can disagree that the 'better world' of the Thearchs is better, in fact, but one can't argue they don't pursue a vision of utopia. Moreover, their utopia generally looks pretty good - it's a humanist one.
                                  The Seers stand for themselves and only themselves. Many individual Seers have ideals and justifications, but as an organization they are totally unprincipled. I mean, come on: They are literally a mystery cult that worships Oppression! They have faith in the power of xenophobia, violence, and surveillance to crush human aspiration, that is literally the cause they serve. An idealistic Seer is the most terrifying kind of mage - a self-interested one is far more comprehensible.
                                  The fact that the Seers are, in total, a minority of mages is a statement of faith in humanity that the game makes, and one I appreciate.

                                                          Originally posted by Mrmdubois View Post
                                                          The main reason that people feel a need to come up with more justification for the existence (via idealism or whatever) of the Seers is because they think selfishness is a flat motivator that can't do a perfectly good job of explaining a person's motives for doing evil. They think selfishness is too mustache twirling and cliche so they try to add more "humanizing" motivations. Personally, I'm fine with selfishness as a motivator, it's very, very human.
                                                          I would agree with you if it weren't for the fact that the book flat-out says that all seers know the Exarchs are evil and serve them anyway. Cults don't work that way. Cultists convince themselves that they are the good guys, that their leader is perfect, and condemn all evidence to the contrary as heresy. It's all about isolating yourself from dissenting opinions so you can convince yourself that you are part of a utopia.

                                                          Someone who joins the Seers out of selfishness or fear is not going to just become a sociopath because the Seers tell him to. He'll either have a nervous breakdown, or will rationalize everything he's doing as being necessary for the greater good. This is where the complaints about the Seers are coming from. People don't consciously accept that they're evil because they're greedy. They block out all evidence that they're evil, condemn everyone outside of their group as corrupt, and convince themselves that their greed is part of a holy purpose. They do everything they can do to protect their self-perception as a good person. That is not consistent with the book's portrayal of the Seers. Take this quote for example:

                                                          "I have spent the best years of my life giving people the lighter pleasures, helping them have a good time, and all I get is abuse, the existence of a hunted man."

                                                          - Al Capone

                                                          Al Capone, a vicious gangster, convinced himself that he was a public benefactor - a person working for the greater good. THIS is how the Seers should see themselves.
