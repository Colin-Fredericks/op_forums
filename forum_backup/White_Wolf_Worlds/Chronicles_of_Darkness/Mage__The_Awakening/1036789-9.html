The link between the Abyss and "morality."

    All things in the abyss do not have intelligence, and whether the Abyss itself is intelligent is up for debate elsewhere. The sapient things in the abyss want, yes, but things like the abyssal spiders just are. And a vacuum hungers as well, not consciously, but it sucks everything into it through pressure.

                            I don't know if this thread has resolved the nature of Wisdom, but doesn't it work as intended?

                            I know it has more or less come down to an argument of moral cause and effect, but doesn't the fact that it smells like imposing a value system kind of make sense?

                            It could be argued that the mage continues to believe that her actions were appropriate, as I believe the Mad will attest; however, one could say that there is sympathy between AoH and the Abyss. The act is not decidedly good or bad, but for the purposes of the Abyss as described, it resonates therein.

                            A mage deleting an SUV full of people for no other reason than just because might peak the theoretical intrigue of the Abyss (From over the shoulder it remarks, "I agree/I liked that.") Not to argue that the Abyss is any measure of sentient, but that the ST can direct the attention of the Abyss to actions you take on a case-by-case basis. Degradation occurs when you fail and even if you (and your mage) disagree, the Abyss has established some sympathy with your character. (It is worth it to note that I'm also not using sympathy in the Space arcana sense. I merely wish to convey relation or synchronicity, but perhaps it is all the same.)

                            A player can also argue their actions aren't reckless enough to warrant Abyssal sympathy, then bonuses could be given or the roll could be waived.

                            The fact that Wisdom works the way it does seems indicative of the trappings of the Fallen World. A disagreeable circumstance of being human and both interpreting and being interpreted by forces far beyond your control. The Exarchs presumably reside beyond the reach of the Abyss and can dabble in whatever they damn well please without judgment. They are the strange Old Ones, that don't see themselves as evil, but unlike you they don't have to deal with the incessant shittery of Paradox trying to befriend them.

                            I think it's even cooler to translate the idea of "I'm not fucking crazy!" directly into the character and to live that Madness. To thoroughly feel your character is right in his conviction and to be twisted by Paradox into a shape that other mages lament. The Mad are alien because their ideas are perceived as far removed, not because their ideas are decidedly evil. The consequence of perceiving an alien idea (or set of ideas) that could potentially harm you is the judgment that it is perceptively unpredictable and perhaps dangerous and/or evil (at the very least bad/negative/unwanted to some such person or article.)

                            Cthulhu is, as I've interpreted, a being that by virtue of existing is understood as foreign and evil. This is more or less, as someone else noted, how I view the Abyss and why I believe attributing Wisdom loss to sympathy with the Abyss (or loss of control) is appropriate.

                            The whole Inuring thing could be understood as coming to terms with that sympathy, or obscuring it from the Abyss, perhaps even a sort of contract with the Abyss. ("We agree on this much, but nothing else!")
