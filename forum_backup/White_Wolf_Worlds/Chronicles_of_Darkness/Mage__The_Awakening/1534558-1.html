    Originally posted by Yumlum View Post
    I know some people who have played Mage the Awakening complain about how it's too eurocentric with it focus on atlantis, which trivialize other magical tradition around the world. While I don't really see how it could trivialize other magical tradition, but I couldn't agree on whether it eurocentric or not.
    It does not "trivialize other magical traditions". It outright says that Sleeper magical traditions are wrong - all of them, Western and Eastern. Then it presents the universal Truth with capital letters, and says that everything all Sleeper cultures are doing is seeing glimpses and parts of it, while distorting it with Lie anyways. Then it says that some people do see the Truth, and understand which parts of their cultural heritage were right all along.

    It doesn't spend too much time describing how exactly different cultures see their Paths and Orders, but it does a good attempt in Tome of Pentacle. There probably would be more of it, but sadly, game line inofficially ended along with CofD at large. The best we can do is try to paint those pictures themselves, while trying to be as true and respectful to the cultural sources as possible.

                Verlan, not for the first time it would behoove you to clarify whether you are talking about Mage as it currently exists or as it existed when Second Sight was released eighteen years ago with an altogether different metaphysical framing and design philosophy.

                Second Sight spends most of the first six pages of its Thaumaturges chapter presenting options for what the true nature of thaumaturgy is, and its subsection explaining the difference between thaumaturges and mages relies on an explanation of how Supernal magic works that hasn't been accurate since at least the start of Second Edition if not sooner — the Supernal Realms are only places when perspective is being used to experience them as one and the Atlantean traditions are ahistorical creations derived from commonalities between different cultural mythologies and magical discoveries.

                There's magic in the world that people can perform without being Awakened or otherwise made intrinsically magical. The Awakened know this. They also know that a lot of the narratives people have around magic is urban legend and broken telephone and disinformation by virtue of having tools for magical scrutiny as the easiest forms of magical power available to them, so they can usually tell pretty easily when the overactive window spirit that's rattling your porch glass is not, in fact, Grandpa's ghost trying to send a message to his grieving children. Much of this has little to do with saying Jainism Is Totally Wrong, Actually, and it is going to take more than vague gestures and unsubstantiated claims for statements like "Awakening suggests that mortal magical traditions are almost completely wrong" to go unchallenged if your metric is more stringent than "characters with some amount of filtered divine power can exploit the plasticity of phenomenal experience in a way not accessible to those more firmly anchored in reality."

                                            Originally posted by Verlan View Post
                                            It is true that Mage: The Awakening demonstrates an exceedingly patronizing attitude towards mortal magical traditions
                                            You could remove "magical traditions" there. You could say that Awakening demonstrates an exceedingly patronizing attitude towards Sleepers in general. Because, well, they are Asleep. They don't see the Truth. They live in the Lie. And, mostly, they can't do anything about it - other than Awaken, which sufficiently removes them from normal human experience so that they become patronizing towards mortals in general - at best, because, you know, they could just go into direction of not even bothering to patronize and just exploit.

                                            That's the horror of that game.

                                            Originally posted by Verlan View Post
                                            but the text of Mage: The Awakening does not really line up with some of the other Chronicles of Darkness books. It seems to clash most obviously with Second Sight. The text of Mage: The Awakening suggests that mortal magical traditions are almost completely wrong, but Second Sight does not depict them in that light. Indeed, thaumaturges are depicted as having greater insight than common mortals, and their magic is often depicted as being different from Supernal magic.
                                            First of all, as you probably know, Chronicles of Darkness is a constructor. Second Sight is not written with Mage: The Awakening at mind; the reverse is true as well. Different game lines often contradict each other factually and thematically, being their own universes. If Storyteller decides to bring them together, it's up to him to resolve those conflicts.

                                            Second, there is wrong and wrong. From point of view of Diamond, Fallen magical traditions are wrong, because they are not True with capital letters - they are not Supernal, or at least, their Supernal origins are sufficiently distorted by Lie and Pancryptia to deny them status of being True. From point of view of Free Council, Fallen magical traditions are wrong, because they are not yet there - they are seeds of inspiration which could one day become powerful enough to change Above, but not yet.

                                            Both of those do not deny that Fallen magical traditions exist, and sometimes they do real (still Fallen, but real) magic. Of course, thaumaturges have greater insight than common mortals - not only because they have said real magic, but also because by virtue of having it, they are Sleepwalkers and able to see and remember Truth. But both of those doesn't change the fact that in Awakening, there is objective Truth written in Heavens, and Fallen magical cultures isn't it - it can't be, otherwise it wouldn't be Fallen.

                                            Originally posted by Verlan View Post
                                            Frankly, the portrayal of the Awakened as being more correct about the true nature of the universe than mortals are is ridiculous.
                                            Well, that's the basic premise of the game. It's right there, in the words "Sleep" and "Awaken". Awakened are more correct about the true nature of the universe, because they saw the Truth of it, and now they can't unsee it. They see through Lie built by tyrant-kings of the Creation, and because of that, they can override it, if only for a while.

                                            That's not a suggestion to feel smug and superior in that knowledge (though many many Awakened do exactly that). It's a suggestion to feel the terror of it. You know exactly how broken, unfair and hostile the universe is, and you know exactly how powerless individual person is against that. The question is - what are you going to do about it?

                                                    The Supernal Realms constitute a "Truth" with a capital letter in a kind of cosmological sense, but I think it's a mistake to read this as a definitive statement on Supernal things having more reality than things of the "Fallen" World. Even mages with the greatest level of investment in the idea that the Supernal is innately superior have to recognise that there are things that originate within their world (or possibly other neighbouring ones that made their way in), and the relationship these have with the Supernal can be difficult to grasp and raise a lot of questions. It's a complicated world out there, and they've gathered enough information on it to not just dismiss everything that isn't Supernal as patently inferior. This is reinforced in the text of the Second Edition core book in the opening paragraphs of the section titled "Fallen Worlds".

                                                    It should also be noted that "Fallen World" is an ideological term for what mages otherwise describe cosmologically as the Phenomenal World. That the world they inhabit exists physically isn't what makes it Fallen; mages understand that there was always a Phenomenal to a Supernal, and that indeed the two are interdependent on one another rather than set in a strict hierarchy. Presumably those who go deepest into the weeds of theory and exploration think that the Phenomenal World always contained occult phenomena distinct from direct Supernal power.

                                                    The world is made Fallen only by the rise of the Exarchs and end of the Time Before. The presence of the Abyss and oppressive presence of Supernal Tyrants creates a more inhibited world, but the hold of those things is not absolute. And while mages might think that the influence of the Lie cannot be discounted from ideas and institutions that rose from under it, it's really only the most extreme of them (largely disavowed by their fellows) who think that absolutely everything from Sleepers is tainted because of that.

                                                    Because that's another thing that's part of the equation; while there are things that manage to have varying degrees of Supernal influence, for various reasons, in spite of the reign of the Exarchs, things don't only have value if they evince Supernal understanding. There are plenty of mages who can look at a belief system in magic or spirituality or what have you and think that it has value in its own right, even if only for what it means to the people participating it, let alone if it's capable of having phenomenal results.

                                                    Besides, it should be noted that the nature of the Supernal is technically more of a theoretical framework that has vogue among the largest segment of Awakened society than something described to the reader as objective fact. It's a highly sophisticated theory, benefiting from centuries of refinement and with a lot of practical applications to show for it. But it doesn't cover everything, and Tome of the Pentacle not only depicts a part of the world that sees it in very different terms, it includes some Mysteries that give some legs to that as a very alternative idea of what mages see with their Sight and accomplish through their imagos.

                                                        In a sense the Supernal isn't a worlds it is merely a description of the Phenomenonal world. It is a Truth in that it describes things on a deeper level than usually accessible, but then each path will see that Truth differently, and take different meanings away from the same thing - at least for things that aren't Abyssal Untruths - and the powers of a Mage are to alter the Truths that they can see, imposing new Truths.

                                                        This doesn't make any claim about the truth of sleeper beliefs, just as describing the mechanism of action of flowering plants attracting pollonators doesn't change the enjoyment a person may get from smelling the roses once in a while.

                                                        Those are two seperate and unrelated things. The Phenomenal actually exists, meanwhile the Supernal is merely a set of symbols describing the Phenomenal... An infinite set of symbols filtered through the paths and individual limits of a mage.

                                                        And even with that supposed Truth, mages don't know what happens to souls when a person dies (or where they come from when someone is born). There are real limits to what a Mage can do/comprehend.

                                                        But mainly, I don't see describing something (Supernal Truth) and being something (the Phenomenal existence of sleeper culture) is somehow in conflicted...
