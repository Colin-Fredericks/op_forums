                    Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post
                    the only problem there is that might fall under the purview of the Nameless Empire based on its timing and location.
                    As astonishing as it is to say this, Gobleki Tepi predates Irem to a significant degree, according to my shoddy pencil-drawn timeline. Perhaps not the Shan'iatu, however! They were around for a REALLY long time and the story of Irem only begins in the last chapter, really!

                    It's all really vague but more or less by my notes this is what I got from Decieved : In the 'first age', the Shan'iatu were crazy powerful. This is the era before they discover necromancy and get kind of corrupted by it. They discover humans and create a civilization with them that is NOT Irem ; this may, in fact, be the origin of Gobleki Tepi and other sites. This culture would be a more 'pure' version of the Nameless Empire, theoretically, as it would predate much of the later corruption the Shan'iatu clearly experienced interacting with Ammut.The end of the first age seems to be when the Shan'iatu try to test the might of Anpu and get unmade. Who knows how long they were gone? When Anpu unmakes you, it might take a while to reform your Temakh!... so from the perspective of human followers, they must have seemed to just vanish. Whatever happened to those cultures is very mysterious, right?

                    When they came back, some are humbled and they become students of Anpu. They learn more about Necromancy. This is the second age, where the Shan'iatu understand necromancy and accept (at least temporarily) that they aren't allowed into the afterlife. A few of them bow to Anpu and learn many secrets, but others have learned concepts like 'hate' and dislike Anpu immensely. Listening to Ammut, even just a little bit, has changed them already quit impressively.

                    At some point the masters of the Shell decide to 'educate the other Shan'iatu', and we have no idea what that means, because they don't really go into it. I like to imagine that the Shan'iatu were fighting over something at this point, quarreling amongst themselves in a crucial moment when they should have been minding the store. The Master Amkhata appears and almost destroys the world. This is a huge, huge deal that marks the end of the 'Second Age' of the Shan'iatu. This is implied to be a massive ecological disaster that all but wipes out humanity (I usually pin it to the desertification of the the Sahara, circa 7000BC). The Shan'iatu 'die' again (or sleep), and when they return, they are 'wounded'. These wounds never go away.

                    SO! The Shan'iatu sleep for a long-ass time before they wake up, sacrificing an entire tribe of people to do so. At this point it's mentioned that those who are wounded are more powerful than those who aren't (strange huh?). These Shan'iatu are a lot less powerful and not at all the Gods they used to be. Still, they're pretty smart and potent cuz they manage to kill Azar in a big ritual that's sorta a foreshadowing of the Rite of Return, when you think about it?

                    When it fails they go hide in the sand for 'centuries' so that's even more time. Eventually Anpu guilt trips them (if that's accurate) and they come back. THEN they finally, finally form the idea of Irem. This is not the first civilization the Shan'iatu attemped! It's more than likely not at all as ideologically close to the original intentions of Azar as previous efforts, either.

                    So, Irem... believe it or not... in the timeline I've jotted down for Mummy that has all of these ages outlined, is actually pretty recent! As crazy as that is. Lotsa epic stuff in the world of Mummy happened before them, it's got a very rich almost Tolkein esque background which is really cool.

                    Because the Shan'iatu are described as having spread all around the world in this early age, I like to say they scattered all around the world and kinda monstrously bred humans into a perfect 'toy breed' like we do now with dogs... which is why many other types of hominids die out rather suddenly, including the neanderthals. Humans in that period live like pets because all powerful Shan'iatu obsess over removing suffering and never letting people die or experience emotional or physical pain. This doesn't work out all the time, as you might imagine. Early Shan'iatu while well intentioned and all powerful understand humans not at all, while later Shan'iatu understand humans great but also are much less powerful and prone to wanting to have sex with and/or enslave humans rather than shelter them.

                    TL;DR - Gobleki Tepi is really, really old and way older than Irem, though it might be evidence of a 'prot-Irem' attempted by much less corrupted Shan'iatu in prehistory.
