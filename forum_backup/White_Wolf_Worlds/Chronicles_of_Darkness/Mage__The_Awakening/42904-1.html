            The vast majority of mages aren't going to try to live past old age. That said, "old age" for a mage is quite a lot older than it is for a Sleeper, because any Thyrsus, mage with a Thyrsus in their cabal, or other mage with a middling amount of Life will never suffer from long-term diseases and physical conditions. "Natural lifespan" for mages is around 90-100.

            Some mages use Life magic to push on past that, but the body has limits. To live past 130-ish, you're looking at alternatives. Most of which involve screwing someone else in the process or giving up your humanity, and are thus decried as lichdom.

            Socially, mages don't go for immortality (even most archmasters don't!), because the common means of doing so are, as you'd expect, in the Death Arcanum's purview. And Moros are predisposed to accepting death as a transformative state, not fighting it.

            The Free Council goes easier on liches than other orders because they didn't have the tussle with the Tremere the Diamond did in the middle ages, because thanks to that tussle lichdom is seen as Left-Handed so most research into it went on in the Nameless orders - some sects that went on to join the Free Council were originally kicked out by the Diamond for being overly interested in it - and because during the desperation of the Great Refusal, many of the oldest and most powerful Nameless (who were reaching the end of their useful lifespan after being among the big population boom in Nameless in the 19th Century) took to looking at Seers of the Throne's souls and bodies as the spoils of war. Steal Body was a terror weapon and a way for the LIbertine generals to carry on into the 20th Century.

                                                Honestly, developer's statements or not, the notion most archmasters reject immortality is as utterly outlandish and implausible as most citizens of an industrialized nation forsaking the comforts of modern technology and medicine. An extremist fringe might do it, but the vast majority of the community is going to have much more pragmatic a viewpoint.

                                                This for various reasons: 1) there are plenty of ways Imperial magic may be used to gain immortality without harming anyone 2) by its godlike nature, archmastery already bestows a transhuman perspective 3) Path restrictions to Arcana are much less meaningful to archmasters, so they can master Death if they wish even if they aren't Moros; moreover several other Arcana may be used for immortality besides Death 4) immortality is not a violation of the Pax 5) archmasters may easily decide they need as much time as necessary to accomplish Ascension and their other various personal goals and plans for the Tellurian and they won't in all likelihood achieve them within a human lifespan 6) immortality may be an ideological statement for various kinds of archmasters 7) even for archmasters that believe in death as a transformative experience, it is very easy to reason out their current status is as much mystical/spiritual development as they are going to get this side of Ascension; in all likelihood, this is their final and most enlightened life unless they really, really screw up, so there is little spiritual gain or merit in waiting for another incarnation.

                                                To a less outlandish degree, the notion also defies credibility that the Diamond Orders would remain rather ideologically hostile to immortality because of a minor scuffle they had with the Tremere about a millennium ago. Especially since we are also expected to assume very few Diamond mages from that era remain alive to embody and enforce that viewpoint. On its own, time would have corroded that kind of bad blood to nothingness long ago. The lasting features of mage society that endure the ages like the phoenix typically do so because they have some Supernal entity actively supporting them, such as Exemplars propping up the Orders, the Exarchs propping up the Seers, and so on. There may certainly be some very-high-Wisdom mages that are entirely unwilling to screw up someone else (including various kinds of assholes) or give up their humanity for immortality, but just as surely they do not make up the vast majority of Awakened society or define its mainstream policy and ideology.
