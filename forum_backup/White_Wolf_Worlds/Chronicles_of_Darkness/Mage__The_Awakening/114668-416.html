    Originally posted by Tessie View Post
    Yet the number of charges varies because one of the targets has Composure 1 and the other Composure 2. For a Potency 3 Grave Misfortune they would have 2 and 1 charges, respectively. How can the spell have a varying number of charges shared across all secondary targets?
    The only way to resolve it simply would be to ignore Withstand for the number of charges, which would be another exception to the rules just to make it work your way, and another situation where Potency is treated differently depending on whether it's a charge or not.



    "the area is the subject"

    The subject is defined.

    "and the +2 Dex is the effect."

    The spell effect is defined.

    The next step would be that the spell effect is applied to the subject.

    "This area gives +2 Dex,"

    And now you've added that the subject pushes the effect forward to a secondary target. That's not what Hone the Form does. The spell adds two dots of Dexterity to the subject. What you're describing is the subject adding two dots of Dexterity to a secondary target.
    I've been thinking about this the last little while, but haven't had a chance to respond til now. I'd say that the number of charges isn't variable, even if the number that can be applied to a given individual in the area isn't, and that isn't a problem since I've already said that while everyone has access to the charges they may not be evenly used among everyone before running out. In this case, Withstand protects you by limiting the number of charges that are negative to you that can be applied to you.

    As for the rest, I can see why you'd say that. Area works like that though, if you set the area on fire, everything in the area is on fire. If you give the area +2 Dex then naturally everything in the area also receives +2 Dex. It may be simpler to say that people or whatever inside the area, are considered part of the area until they leave it.

                                                At that point, they might as well just use Life 4 to regenerate it. A simpler solution is to perhaps use Life 3 Weaving to aid in transplanting a living replacement, but that depends on low their Wisdom is. But it will still look unnatural, no matter how much the arm looks like the mage's.

                                                Permanent solutions include a Weaving 3 to gain a starfish or lizard's regenerative healing, added to body control to regrow the limb the long way (unless you have a master of Time).

                                                Another option is to summon a Life Supernatural Entity to do it for them, or give instructions on a way to do it. They can also bargain with a high Ranking Healing spirit (like that of a famous hospital or medical school). Last option is to give the Life sight to a Matter mage so he can construct a perfect, custom made prosthesis. Attaching it would require an extended medicine roll and would probably be agonizing, but at least they become a Fullmetal Alchemist.
