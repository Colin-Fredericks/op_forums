                    My To the OP's original question -

                    No and yes...and no...and sort of yes...

                    No - Flipping through the book, the power level of many spells have definitely ratcheted up. Mage Armor is sweeter. Scrutinizing is much better. Some other aspects of the base template are much better.
                    Yes - The new Reach mechanics seem a bit more confining (especially for buffing spells), die pools seem a bit smaller (especially now that the errata has capped the maximum bonus from a single Yantra to +5), the number of spells that a Mage can carry without worrying about consequences is lower.
                    No - Mages are SUPPOSED to be squishy if they are walking around unprepared and oblivious to danger.
                    Sort of yes - Paradox was more or less of a joke in 1e - a good idea, but it never had any real teeth. The overwhelming temptation to screw oneself over by attempting too much is a central theme of the game. Still, a Mage with loads of time and Mana on his/her hands can still accomplish some really big things if they're willing to take chances.

                    Personally, I think the power level of the average Mage took a hit, but I can justify that by thinking - "This is the Exarchs' doing."

                        I'm playing a Master of Forces right now.

                        The Consilium had some problems with vampires, so my cabalmate found a vampire victim, traced a single vampire down with Space and found all other vampires in the city by abusing that vampire's sympathetic connections.
