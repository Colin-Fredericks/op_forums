    Originally posted by Satchel View Post
    Since this is by no means an uncontroversial reading of the mechanics, I feel it necessary to reiterate that there is evidence to suggest that the most flexible applicators of success-altering Conditions don't actually boost spellcasting.
    Considering Exceptional Luck is the only spell that specifically has the +2 reach for "the boon/hex can affect spell casting rolls" then its safe to assume ALL three applications of boons can be applied vs a spellcasting roll when using EL. Other spells that give boons without this specific +2 reach option (ie monkeys paw, sworn oath etc) can still all all 3 applications of boons to a roll, but can only add the "grant 9/8 again" boon option to spellcasting because THAT specific application of boons has its own +2 reach option that this boon option can apply to spellcasting rolls. The other 2 boon options such as granting bonus dice or giving conditions cant be used on spellcasting rolls when using "monkeys paw".

    But this is off topic.

    I quickly came to the realization that the best way to balance encounters in mage was to balance the repercussions after whatever encounter. Usually once the PC's had planned for abit the actual "encounter/conflict" was over quickly and i then had to really think about how whatever the PC's had done affected everything around them. Eventually the PC's stopped wondering IF they could do something but considered SHOULD they do it.

    Not had much experience with 2e though, the ES bypassing withstand does indeed make a master with unmaking Life for example a serious threat/encounter to a group of PC's but only if that master has reason to antagonistic to the PC's. Its no wonder masters should be treated with some respect and perhaps people spend time considering if their current action will piss any of them off or not. I like to think most sane Mages dont automatically jump straight to ES unmaking problems away though but my PC's always seem to end up being the insane types who try to violence away problems..

                I agree with those who say the game should focus mostly on investigation and discovery, rather than direct conflict.

                Nevertheless, when it happens I agree that the best ways to make it challenging are:

                - Sleeper witnesses
                - Strange immunities and abilities from Temple Guardians, Abyssals, Spirits, Cryptids etc...
                - Other Mages with high initiative (many ways to do this like Body Control, Acceleration etc...) and Wards and Signs

                Just remember there's a reason that people talk about the Cold War between Seers and Pentacle Mages: It really can result in a nuclear style exchange with devastating results if people don't respect the "rules" of the game, so to speak.

                What I've done in the early going in my Chronicles is put players up against Sleeper gangs, The Shinje (Boston Unveiled), the Red Word (BU) or low to mid-rank Spirits, Ghosts, Goetia etc... Most of my players get a good idea of the deadliness of the game by that point and usually don't go looking for unnecessary conflict.

                                I think it's important to always keep in mind this fact: World of Darkness is not World of Warcraft.
                                That's not saying anything bad about WoD or WoW; what I'm saying is that this game isn't primarily geared towards combat as the engine - battles are not the driving force behind what makes this game interesting, fun, or compelling.
                                Mages start off, innately, the weakest of all the templates: barely more than human, they have no access to the incredible raw power of gauru shapeshifting or the intrinsic resilience of undead flesh and vitae. But Mages seek understanding as a path to power. A Master mage flips the paradigm on it's head: where once the werewolf and vampire had the advantage, they scaled rather linearly, and the mage exponentially. The Master throws around ranged agg damage, and/or has insta-kill spells, and a myriad of utility to support his power. The "payoff" comes late, as mages are slow bloomers. But the payoff is there.
                                The continued growth beyond Master is a perfect illustration of this point: Archmages exist, whereas no equivalent for Werewolf or Vampire exists. This fully fits the theme of mage the awakening. Werewolf and vampire do not have such themes as transcendence and transcendental power; they grow strong, but stay the same. Improvement is not the same as metamorphosis.

                                Which brings up the ultimate point I'm trying to make: balance is not found in crossover. Mage isn't "balanced" against Vampire or Werewolf. The Arcana are only "balanced" against each other in a vague, guideline sense of the word. What is balance in World of Darkness? It's found in story: in consequence, in player satisfaction, in the effect of hubris, vanity, and becoming a human who is not humane. Mage combat is more all-or-nothing than any other template, which is to say either your spells work or they don't, and it's often more efficient to use a gun than a bolt of lightning. Mage isn't built around the combat system. Vampire and Werewolf are more combat-oriented, and engaging/rewarding, if you're looking for combat. Mages either win or lose, often very quickly.

                                Don't try to balance Vampires and Werewolves and Mages. It will never happen without homogenizing and nerfing and stripping away most of what makes each character unique. Instead, make sure each player has a niche that they enjoy and effectively fulfill, create an engaging story, and use each splat's built-in challenges to the fullest (when necessary): Mages often require time, time, time to prepare; knowledge of the situation; mana; normal human sleepers interfere with casting; paradox; reach for the best effects; a risk of wisdom loss for the most abusive spells (by abusive I generally refer to the spells people like to complain about).

                                    Originally posted by Vent0 View Post


                                    Wards and Signs is Prime 2 that grants Withstand equal to its Potency (Duration is the Primary Factor). If a Mage opponent cast upon a group of disposable minions, that is going to make any magic cast upon them harder. Minions get eliminated? Use the freed Spell Control to enchant the next wave and send them in. Attrition is a villain's greatest tool. Remember also that multiple Withstand values add +1 per to the highest value. Even a Potency 1 Wards and Signs with make a Withstand 2 [for average Resolve, Composure, or Stamina] into a 3.
                                    According to p114 this isn't true. A spell applies it's Potency to subject's individual withstand ratings. The +1 comes from the attainments. (ie if you're casting through Sympathetic range use whichever withstand rating is higher, which would be either their resistance attribute(s) or the sympathy itself +1.)

                                    The group I play with are staunch believers that a character isn't a person unless you start with double or triple experience, which makes balancing notoriously difficult and you run into things like "It's Fae/God Machine/whatever therefore any relevant traits are infinite" Which has it's own pros and cons. That being said, we recently fought a D&D inspired mob that was basically a master of Time and clashing with it (even with 4 dots of time myself) was utterly futile. The "Big Stick" approach to balancing may be inelegant but it can certainly work once in a while to just make something absurd with absurd stats (17 defense, lol) forcing the party to work together against a common enemy.
