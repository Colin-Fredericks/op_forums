        Originally posted by quelsec View Post
        How do time sympathetic connections work exactly? the descriptions in the book are unclear for me.
        What exactly is unclear? They work as connections between objects and a past version of themselves.

        You don't need a Sympathetic connection to (for example) look back as far as the unchanged past - though looking at the past of an object when it hasn't changed probably isn't actually useful, unless you can see other things changing around it.

        Beyond that you need a Temporal sympathy. And that then depends on how much they have changed, look at a mage who has just awakened, and they likely have a weak connection to the Mage they were just before awakening. So any attempt to use post cognition to look at them before their awakening will have the highest possible withstand.

        Look at the past of anything ancient, and the absolute further you can see is the last Lacuna where the world dramatically changed (to the point of breaking, the Sundering event is something Mages can't use time magic to research before... This is an event from Werewolf which changed the nature of the world, involving the death of a god).

        I guess a person's birth is also a Lacuna, you can't see back before the person was born.

        But everything in between is smaller or larger changes over time...

                Given that a thing cannot have temporal sympathy outside of itself, I think it's ultimately just a way of saying "spells to see or affect the past are Withstood by the extent of how much they have changed over time" that is rooted in some existing jargon.

                The main ambiguity that I read myself in the rules is what exactly constitutes a valid yantra to use for the temporal sympathy attainment. Personally, I'm inclined to be more restrictive with it than a straight reading might imply; the yantra needs to not only symbolise the spell's target, but the time period that you wish to work with. If you want to see a person's childhood, you need to have something evocative of them at that time.

                I'll admit, this is mostly because I want postcognition to be something that can't just readily solve mysteries by looking freely at the timeline of any person or place. You need to have an idea of where you're looking and something that can connect to that time. I would have it that this can be surprisingly tricky for times and places that don't really have anything obvious that would be symbolic of them, for how prosaic they are.

                Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                I think Rewrite History is better explained as "simply" dimension hopping: you're not changing the actual past, you're bringing an alternate version of the target from an alternative timeline.

                The corollary is that the timeline in question must "exist" on its own, so to speak, and you need to know the point of divergence enough to have an idea of which "timelines" to look at.
                I think that's just exchanging one complication re: whether or not a subject can be granted additional features or possessions for another one that is even bigger while also being less consequential.

                I also don't really care for the restriction it would place on the casting mage.

                                    I don't think they are that similar, the Ocean of Fragments is made for dissolving the memories of ghosts so they can finally let go a pass on (the good ending?) - the Hermit is sitting by presumably watching things enter the ocean, then picking the shores for interesting bits and pieces and maybe going for a swim when it has too much and dissolving some bits of himself (pretty sure the 1e write-up mentions him not having any way of knowing his original sex).

                                    The Abyss is a place of unreality, only things which don't exist are there, so becoming unreal and possibly leaving your Shell of a body as a conduit for unreality has a very different feel. The Old man of the Abyss, is more of a tempter, probably promises all the things which do exist only in the Abyss, all the possibility that can't be realised in the real world. He seems humble compared to the other Aeons, those that represent all of reality, while he merely represents things unreal.

                                    The Hermit seems to just be another ghost, the Old man of the Abyss is a dangerous Aeon trying to unmake reality.
