    ⯎ INTRODUCTION

    ​
    Hello, all, and welcome to my Actual Play! I'm a newcomer to Mage, having discovered the game a few months ago after randomly stumbling upon Ascension. I was immediately hooked by the freeform magic, the simplicity and richness of the Storytelling System, and the evocative fiction. Some of my favorite pieces of media were listed as inspirations, and a lot of the lore shared elements with a world me and some friends had built collaboratively around 10 years ago. DaveB's fantastic writeups only made the obsession worse.

    I had to try it out for myself. It wasn't long before I got into two weekly games, which I've been enjoying immensely. But still, something was missing… Don't get me wrong, I love me a tabletop game. But there's nothing like being on the other side of the screen, crafting a world, and watching the pure alchemy that happens when you drop a group of players right into the midst of things and see the chaos that ensues. I used to GM Pathfinder and D&D, and even worked as a professional DM for some time. Some of my good friends mentioned having a free day and being open to playing something. The signs all pointed to me running a game. I just needed to figure out what kind of game I wanted to run.

    I'm the kind of person who doesn't do things by half measures, so I immediately got my hands on every book I could, both 1e and 2e. And the more I read, the more ideas began to percolate. When I saw the description of the Los Angeles setting (where I happen to live in real life), I knew I had to use it. But for me, a lot of the fun of Mage is in exploring the contrast between Sleeper life and Mage life, especially at the start. The seemingly endless potential, taking first steps as a mage, navigating the Orders, and the "street level" stuff. I think it's a shame that most games start using the standard template, skipping past all that juicy exploratory content. As I thought about that, a most devious idea blossomed in my mind.

    What if I kept the entire premise of the game a secret from them? None of them have read the books or know much about the game, and I wasn't about to force them to binge the books like I had. This would be the one and only opportunity to experience the world of Mage for the first time, just like their characters. So I decided I'd keep them in the dark. I told them we were playing an investigative horror game, gave them some character-building questions, and a prompt. And that's it.

    Here's the character building advice I gave, for those interested:

    ⯎ THE CAST | Who exactly are you?
    ​
    ⯌ You are in Los Angeles, a beautiful, bustling, horrifying metropolis. It's August 2025.
    ⯌ Whether you're a shut-in conspiracy theorist, a politician with aspirations for the Oval Office, or the current hottest TikTok Influencer, you've got dreams of your own.
    ⯌ You are about to be drawn into a series of mysteries that you can't even fathom yet.

    ​
    ⯌ THINGS TO CONSIDER

    Genre​ Mystery / Urban Horror / Slice-of-Life / ____​
    Tone​ Tragic / Tense / Slow Burn / Paranoia-Inducing​
    Inspirations​ House of Leaves / Dresden Files / Memento / Haunting of Hill House​
    Themes​ Truth vs. Lies / Identity and Transformation / Echo Chambers ​

    ​
    ⯌ ​ADVICE
    • You can create any type of character. This is a very freeform and flexible storytelling game. Whether you're a celebrity, a grad student, a member of the FBI, or a local chef, you'll fit in the story just fine. Just make a character whose story you'll enjoy playing and exploring.​
    • In fact, I'd encourage you to step out of your comfort zone or go for a concept that you think is fun but that you'd normally not get to play in another system.​
    • Who your character is before the game starts will greatly influence the resources, knowledge, and connections you have access to. Do you want to have lots of allies and enemies? Tons of money and creature comforts? A secret life that nobody else knows about? Knowledge about a super esoteric subject? Fighting ability that would scare Bruce Lee? Build your character concept based on what you want to actually play once the story gets rolling.​
    • Your character needs to have a Vice that they're strongly tempted to indulge in and a Virtue that they hold dear to them. This should be a real source of conflict for them!​
    • They also need to be curious and interested in solving mysteries. Characters who are uninterested in the world around them probably aren't a great fit for this chronicle.​

    Name:
    Age/Birthday:
    Gender/Sexuality:

    Vice/Virtue:

    RELATIONSHIPS AND CONNECTIONS
    1. Who are your people? Could be biological / chosen family or not, but no man is an island.​
    2. Who are not your people? Could be nuisances or enemies, but nobody is liked by everyone.​
    3. Who do you wish was in your life? An old flame, a departed relative, a dream collaborator?​
    4. Who have you all but forgotten? Someone who you just barely crossed paths with.​
    5. How did you end up at the funeral?​
    PERSONAL QUESTIONS
    1. What makes you feel out of place or like you don't belong?​
    2. What's your morning routine—and why do you stick to it?​
    3. Who in your life have you disappointed? Who has disappointed you?​
    4. What is the worst thing that's ever happened to you?​
    5. What is something you wish someone would ask you about?​
    6. What's a place you go back to even though it's unpleasant or painful?​
    7. What is the worst thing you've ever done?​
    8. What do you believe that you'd hesitate to admit to others?​
    9. What is a weird thing that has happened to you that you don't talk about?​
    10. What is a mystery that you NEED to solve?

      ⯎ THE PREMISE

      ⯌ ⯎ ⯌

      Your story starts and ends at a funeral.


      You are all here for a very specific reason, even if you don't realize it at first. The simplest explanation is that you knew the deceased—one of his friends, a member of the family, something like that. But there's no fun in an answer that simple, right? That's not to say that can't be the case. Just pointing out that a mystery should be a little more fun. Then again, that might be enough of a mystery to start, since regardless of who you are and how you got here, one fact is true for all of you.

      You don't even know his name.

      Perhaps you just work at the funeral home and you're here to make sure everything goes smoothly. Maybe you received a text with a time and place from a contact you don't remember putting in your phone. It could be that you're not even here for the deceased, but someone—or something— else. A cascade of unexpected circumstances could have landed you here, certainly. Weirder things have happened. And there's even a chance that you simply don't know why.

      Regardless of the circumstances of your arrival, there is one thing that I can say for certain: This moment is going to change everything. You might not know it ahead of time, but this is just the first of many dominoes to fall. Where the story goes I cannot say, because the future is a mystery. What exactly happens, how you shape it, and who you become are all up to you.

      Your story starts and ends at a funeral. But it doesn't have to.​



      ⯎ THE SETTING

      ⯌ ⯎ ⯌

      Los Angeles—The City of Angels—August 2025.​


      Los Angeles is the most populous county in America, surpassing the combined population of 40 other US states. The county's seat, Los Angeles, is the second most populous city in the United States behind New York, and is the heart of the film and TV industry. Though it's called The City of Angels, people who live here know that it's really The City of Dreams. Whether you're a transplant or LA-born and raised, if you live in the county, it's more than likely you at one point held a dream that was larger than life. Maybe you still do.

      But LA has its problems. Despite being one of the most diverse places in the US, it too falls short of the ideal of America as a melting pot. It's a city filled with as much ugliness as beauty, where the crime rate is high, economic inequality is rampant, and the echo chamber is vast.

      But this is a story about dreamers. Of people with a deep curiosity. A need to know. Some truth that they are searching for. Answers to a mystery.

      This is a story about you.




      ⯎ THEMES

      ​ ⯌ IDENTITY


      I knew from the start that identity was one of the main things I wanted to focus on for this Chronicle. I love character-driven stories, and the various Chronicles of Darkness game lines and the Storytelling System as a whole is geared toward exactly those kinds of stories. In particular, Mage centers the question of identity and how it is performed and mediated over time through the use of Shadow Names, the way Mage Sight is filtered by the Mage, and their Long-Term Nimbus. In Mage, everyone is given a Sympathetic Name, generally the name they were most often referred to as they first began to develop their own sense of consciousness and form connections to others as an individual. But that name is a product of the Lie, something ascribed to them to box them in. It's not until someone Awakens that they can, for the first time, decide who they are and who they want to be without being completely blinded by the world and its apparent limitations.

      Because of that, the choice of a Shadow Name and the ways it informs your Nimbus and Destiny are really special. Taking a Shadow Name is and should be a momentous step for any mage. It's not simply an alias or a nickname. You are informing the Supernal and reality exactly who you are and who you want to be and, consciously or not, inviting that into your life. But a Shadow Name and Nimbus aren't just something you choose once and never reflect on again. The way you act as a mage, the kind of spells you cast, and the Mysteries you choose to explore or ignore all come to refine and define your identity as a mage. There's a reason a person no longer has strong temporal sympathy to who they were before an Awakening, and the same should be true for a mage at the end of their career and at the start.

      For this Chronicle, I want to explore what it means for the characters to have this rare chance to define and redefine who they are on a symbolic and metaphysical level, as well as all of the trials and tribulations that come with that sort of ability.


      ⯌ FREEDOM VS. CONTROL


      This is the quintessential struggle in the Mage universe. There is no Ascension War, not unless you're an Archmaster navigating the Ententes and trying to seal your Omens to bring about the exact kind of world that you want to see. The grand struggle for the future of the Fallen World has long since been fought, way back in the Time Before. What we live in is the result, a world redefined in the aftermath of a cosmic war where all are slaves to the inexorable god-tyrants that rule from the Supernal. The overwhelming majority of people will never even know how much they've lost to the Abyss and the Lie the Exarchs have crafted. But mages have the rare blessing and curse of Awakening to the truth, of being able to peer past the Lie and see the bars of the cage. But for most mages, that Awakening is the first and last time they will ever touch the Supernal short of Archmastery.

      You've seen through the Lie, the glitch in the Matrix, and now you're back in the cage. What do you do? Like the man from the Allegory of the Cave, do you struggle against your chains, trying to enlighten your fellow prisoner and raise them up so that they, too, can one day touch the fundamental truths of the universe? Or do you throw in with your jailers instead, trading servitude for temporal power and the potential for eventual enlightenment at your masters' side?

      It's not just a long-term question, either. As a mage, you have the power to bend the rules of nature to your will. But you're not the only one. Mages form communities to mediate disputes, trade favors and knowledge, and keep each other in check. Everyone, regardless of their Order Status or lack of it, has some opinion on how mages should operate in the world. Should you be free to use magic as you wish in pursuit of your Obsessions?

      The thing about mages is that, unlike Sleepers, there's very little that they can't do if they apply themselves. No thought can be hidden, no hypothesis untested, no truth undiscovered. And that's why the question of freedom vs. control is such an important one. You and your kind can achieve just about anything you'd like.

      But should you?


      ⯌ THE ECHO CHAMBER


      The phrase "echo chamber" is not one that's easy to avoid in the modern day. In the last few years, especially, it's become a common buzzword in both academic and popular discourse. And with good reason, because echo chambers are everywhere you look. We've all heard the term, know what it's referring to, and understand more or less what the problem is with falling victim to one. But how does one identify an echo chamber in the wild? If you're born into, raised in, or grow up in one, how do you escape? And when you do, what exactly does life look like on the outside?

      Mages call themselves The Wise, a term that is half aspirational and half ironic. Yet, despite being gifted with near unrivaled information gathering and truth seeking power, mage society is no less fractious than mundane society. At times it may even seem more so. For people with Platonic truths at their fingertips, why is it that mages can't ever seem to agree on anything?

      Magic is humanity's birthright. No, humanity is magic. Magic is a responsibility. No, magic is payment. Magic is a struggle. No, magic is alive.

      For all the power they wield, mages are humanーoften frighteningly so. The ability to manipulate the Fallen laws doesn't remove people's flaws. If anything, it only seems to heighten them because of the greater power mages have and the fewer restrictions on their actions. And what is more human than the desire to define oneself by what one is and is not, to draw lines and boundaries and categorize things in neat boxes in service of understanding? For all the discourse on echo chambers, academics fall victim to them just the same as the general public. Mages are no different.

      LA, in particular, is known for its political divisions. Instead of a unified Pentacle, it is governed by a Consilium and an Assembly locked in a bitter rivalry–they fight over the ideal state of governance, Mysteries, and prospective members of all Orders in equal measure and ignore each other otherwise, only working together when absolutely necessary or to deal with mutual threats. The Seers, too, keep to themselves, more interested in their own plots and schemes than the spiritual descendants of their gods' ancient enemies. With lines drawn clearly in the sand and each Order its own isolated island, what are newly Awakened mages to do? How do they navigate the divided world they have Awakened in, the grand search for truth and meaning, and their own developing identity in a place where echo chambers and neotribalism are not only the reality but the expectation?​

          ⯎ THE CAST



          DERRIK THORPE (26) - HE/HIM - LAWYER
          (Played by Keni)​

          Derrik Thorpe has the look in his eye of someone who's seen a lot of things in his life and knows better than to talk about them. But don't let the dyed hair, piercings, and tattoos fool you; Derik is a softy, and likely more intimidated by strangers than they are by him. Originally from South Dakota, he and his mother moved to LA when he was a teenager and never looked back. Derrik graduated from UCLA with his JD but has yet to actually practice Law. The past two years have been… complicated, to say the least, with some very low lows and not much in the way of highs. But he's moved out of his mom's place, he's seeing a therapist, and his life seems to be getting back on track.

          Or it was until he received a letter in the mail containing two items. The first is an invitation to a funeral, the only information provided an address and a date written in embossed golden letters. The other is a polaroid of him and the other boys at a faith camp that he attended when he was fourteen back in South Dakota, an experience that he'd very much like to forget. And while that camp has long since shut down, the other boys' names forgotten, he can't help but feel that he knows exactly which one it is that died.

          Sure, Derrik hasn't really been around people in a long time, and he doesn't remember a polaroid being taken that day at camp, but the funeral seems like it might be fine. Who knows, he might even get the chance to ask the other boys some burning questions. What could go wrong?





          DAMIAN WHELAN (38) - HE/HIM - PRIVATE INVESTIGATOR​
          (Played by Xeno)​

          Damian's never met a bottle of hard liquor or a cigarette that he didn't like. He runs a private investigation business that he inherited from his dad, lives in a one-bedroom apartment in North Hollywood that reeks of cigarettes and booze, and his life partner is a guinea pig named Sam Spadey.

          Unlike our other protagonists, Damian's life is filled with work–even with a business degree from UCLA, it's not easy running a 3 person business, especially when it's community-focused and you're paying your Gen Z employees living wages. What that means for Damian is a lot of long hours, late nights, and uncompensated overtime. It's an often thankless job, but maybe the satisfaction of making a difference for someone, even in a small way, makes up for it. If not, a smoke and a couple glasses of whiskey are usually enough to take the edge off after a particularly grueling day.

          Damian doesn't have the luxury of getting to do whatever he likes. Money's tight, which means when he gets an odd case, he's inclined to take it. And he's dealt with his fair share of… strange cases over the years. But this one's certainly new. Normally his clients call or ask to meet, but this one slipped an envelope under the door to the office, accompanied by an email from a throwaway account. $5000 to investigate the case of a guy who's died, some leads to follow up on, and a summary of the police report. The first half has already been deposited in his account. The second will be wired to him upon completion.

          There's a lot of secrecy on the part of his mystery client, and the funeral's rapidly approaching, but the money's already sitting comfortably in his account and the client just wants more information, not definitive answers. He'd be lying if he said he wasn't curious about what's going on. It should be an open and shut case.





          COLLIN WHITMAN (30) - HE/HIM - ANGEL INVESTOR​
          (Played by Screwy)​

          Collin has always been a little bit eccentric. He has certain habits and tendencies and needs things to be done a particular way or he freaks out. Luckily for him, Dave is there to take care of all the things he can't on his own. He and Dave met playing Dungeons and Dragons, and it wasn't long before Colin hired him to be his "personal concierge." They naturally spend a lot of time together, and aside from the weekly D&D sessions he plays, Dave is Collin's only contact with the outside world.

          Because of his peculiarities, Collin lives relatively off the grid. After his parents died, he got rich off the initial cryptocurrency boom and has not had to work since. Instead, he pursued an online degree in cryptography at UCLA and invested some of his shocking net worth into his tech bro D&D buddies' startups, which all took off. Using their technologies, Dave's connections, and a sickening amount of money, Collin has established a life for himself of near total anonymity. He doesn't believe in lavish living or extravagance, preferring to eat and dress simply. His greatest expenditure is on security. He has a number of properties across the country, his presence has been scrubbed from the Internet, and nobody can find him unless he wants to be found.

          Or so he thought until a letter arrived for him 6 months ago. Though he was freaked out at first, Collin immediately recognized that it contained a cipher and decoded it, uncovering an address. When he mailed his response, a new ciphertext letter came from a different PO box in LA. This became a pattern, a game of coded messages and new addresses. Then his "pen-pal" went silent. A few days ago, Collin received a funeral invitation and realized what must have happened. It's been a while since he's been in public, but he's dying to know how his "friend" found him and what became of them.​

              ⯎ 0 | WAKING WORLD DREAM
              ⯌ 0.0 | The Funeral
              "Your story begins and ends at a funeral. But it doesn't have to." - Anonymous


              ⯌ ⯎ ⯌




              There's nothing quite as terrifying and exciting as a first session, especially after a long period of not running games. Pros and amateurs alike struggle with getting things started. Players, too, feel a little bit of awkwardness and anxiety (in my experience) wondering how everything is going to fit together. Tons have been written about how to run an effective start to a campaign and whether opening around a table at a tavern is the greatest sin known to man or not. I like to do things differently, though. For me, the most exciting and interesting first sessions have always been the ones that started in unexpected places. And since the first session naturally sets the tone for the rest of the game, I figured it was important to set the stage right. So naturally, I figured the best way to kick off this wacky chronicle of mine would be to throw them into the deep end.


              Our first chapter starts in medias res as our three protagonists round a corner in the dark, lungs burning, hearts racing, running like their lives depend on it as men's screams ring out in the background—one of them in pain, the other in growing horror. They're in some kind of dark passageway that smells of rust, dust, and mildew, with only the faint illumination of penlights they're carrying with them to guide the way. Damian, at the front of the pack, notices a familiar stale, cloying scent accompanied by the sound of steady dripping. Approaching the nearest wall, he catches sight of a purplish-black, tar-like substance. Whatever it is, one look at it has him bolting at twice the speed.

              Behind him, Collin catches sight of the dust and grime coating every surface and instinctively protects his face with his forearms, skin crawling. Which is fortuitous, because at that precise moment something barbed slices into the area where his face would have been, instead tearing through his sleeves and drawing blood. As he blinks through the pain, silvery motes seem to dance across the periphery of his vision.

              Derrik, at the back, looks over his shoulder to see a fourth and fifth figure running behind him. But the fifth doesn't quite round the bend as smoothly as the others did and is forced to brace himself to avoid slamming into it. As he looks ahead and meets Derrik, an unreadable expression on his face, something yanks him back into the shadows. The fourth figure, still a ways behind Derrik, shouts to the others to keep running and not turn back. Derrik is only too happy to oblige as he hears the familiar sound of electricity buzzing nearby.

              As Derrik overtakes Collin, he dances out of the way of a sparking cable that suddenly drops from the ceiling, shouting at the other man to run faster before he overtakes him, rapidly putting distance between them. Collin, noticing the sparking electrical box nearby, disregards the advice completely and stops to examine it for a switch or lever, figuring that getting some more illumination is his biggest priority at the moment. He fails to get the lights working (and when Screwy opts to take a Dramatic Failure) his hand connects with something causing a blinding flash of light to flood the area, bringing the scene to a close for him.

              Meanwhile at the front, Damian rapidly approaches a fork in the path as the corridor splits off in two directions. But just before the split is a 6 foot long pool of the substance he saw before. Rather than slow down, he takes a running leap and manages to just clear the edge on the other side. Both tunnels are crowned by dilapidated signs: one shows a downward arrow and the other a chain-like symbol. He decides to go with the former and the passageway, true to the sign, begins to slope downward, gently at first and then increasingly sharp. But the sound of rushing liquid behind him leaves no room for relief. Spotting what looks to be a service ladder leading to a hatch in the ceiling, he makes a leap and hangs off of it, trying his best to curl his body and make himself as small as possible as he presses himself against the hatch in the ceiling. A veritable torrent of the purplish-black substance rushes past where he was standing but moments ago. Seemingly in defiance of physics, the surface of this liquid rises higher and higher, threatening to reach him, but Damian is able to fumble with the lever and swing the latch open. He's blinded by light on the outside, just like Collin, crossing the threshold and leaving the scope of this scene.

              And then there was one. Derrik, unlike the others, is mainly focused on the ceiling, counting wires and deftly dodging side to side as sparking cables arc downward toward him one after another. In the distance, he sees the corridor end in a bend, a bright light shining from around the corner. Not seeing any other exits and fearing the rising crackle of electricity behind him, he dashes around the corner and joins the others in the light.

              And there we have the first scene of the chronicle! This scene had our first and only use of music for the first session, a tense and heart-pounding song that immediately ramped up the tension and put them in fight-or-flight mode. In retrospect I feel like I could have pushed it further, but for the first scene of the chronicle I think I did a pretty good job. The players certainly seemed to enjoy it.

              As the light clears, Collin finds himself disembodied in a void, the only sound he can hear a voice that echoes, "Guard the keys." Soon, another joins in, then another, until they become a garbled cacophony. He shouts at them to stop and finds himself reliving a memory from several years back. His mom tells him that they'll talk when she gets back, looking apologetic. She leaves the handkerchief she painstakingly made for his father when they first started dating on the table in the entryway before leaving. The grandfather clock tolls five times and the dream begins to dissolve.

              It is night time now, and the monstrously large moon casts its silver glow on the clock, which grows larger and taller by the second until it becomes a towering lighthouse. As he tries to approach, looking for a door, Collin finds that oddly enough, he can't seem to make it any closer. But along its base he spots bushes of black roses and notes that all along its surface are silver barbs, as if the structure was designed to prevent anyone from scaling it. In the background, he can just barely make out the silhouettes of four other towering structures, but they're too far away to discern any details. All of a sudden, he hears the sound of shattering glass, an apology, and then sees his handkerchief, a red stain spreading across its surface. As he reaches down to pick it up, the dream ends.

              Collin jolts awake in the back seat of a rental car, clutching the pristine handkerchief in his hands as the driver, his personal assistant and confidant Dave, peers at him in the mirror. Collin is understandably frazzled and tries, failing, to explain what he's seen. Asking Dave whether he's ever had any strange dreams, the man regales him with a story from college in which he had a stress dream about an elementary school teacher showing up in his common room and getting increasingly frantic when he couldn't find a first aid kit for her. Dave lets Collin know they've arrived at the location, and he looks out the window.

              Damian blinks awake on the couch, scotch glass still in hand and a noticeable stain on his dress shirt. Aside from a crick in his neck and burgeoning headache, he feels like his sleep was restful and dreamless. As he goes about his morning routine, though, he can't help but stumble around. Whether because of the hangover or something else, he keeps misjudging distances, stubbing toes on tables that should be further away and stopping a few feet short of where the door actually is. Despite these minor setbacks, he gets in the shower (Xeno notes that he is the type to brush and shower at the same time) and is ready to go with plenty of time to spare. On the way, he stops for two coffees and an egg sandwich and fields a call from Sarah, his secretary.

              "Oh my god. You'll never guess what."

              "...What?"

              "No, that's not how this works. You have to guess."

              Damian correctly guesses that it's about Tomas, her SpinCycle instructor, prompting her to note with awe that he is pretty good at his job. Apparently, Tomas had never had lasagna before, and she was angling to suggest bringing some for him next class when her eavesdropping archnemesis Linda showed up. According to Linda, her mother's lasagna is legendary, and she used the secret family recipe as an excuse to get Tomas's number in clear violation of Sarah's dibs. Sarah, frustrated that her six month "project" is being threatened, references the other case the office is working on, suggesting that Damian's other worker Ollie might have one more missing dog to look for if Linda doesn't back off. With all the NPCs at the church, there were a ton of avenues of investigation open to the characters. But because they only talked to Abigail and Mrs. Carver, his room was the only obvious choice. If they’d asked around or learned some more, this investigation board would likely make a little more sense than it does, but the drama of them finding the board and realizing that Jacob might be a stalker was certainly delicious. Even better, it’s all contextless since they haven’t really picked up many clues about Jacob thus far. I can't wait to see what sort of conclusions they end up jumping to next session as they analyze the information he’s gathered.

                                              "...What? What? What!?!?! So you're not arresting us?"

                                              "No."

                                              "Oh. Are you actually FBI?"

                                              "Yeah."

                                              "Oh..."

                                              Derrik bends over double and begins to heave as the tension pours out of his body. Before long, he's back to his normal prickly self. "What the fuck was that? Actually, no. No, I don't wanna know. Don't care." Sam just shrugs. "Cool, that kinda makes things easier for me. So... I've kinda got some places to go... but I'll catch you around."

                                              When Derrik asks how to contact him, he says that Derrik's got his number, gives him a little wave, and then walks around the corner that Jericho mysteriously backed himself into and vanished from earlier. Certain it's a dead end, Derrik peers around the corner to find it empty yet again. He hurriedly assures himself that he does not care and does not want to know what any of that is about. Turning back to speak to Brian, he finds the hallway empty and hears the sound of a door slamming in the distance. It would seem that everyone's vanishing on him today.


                                              In the alleyway outside UCLA, Damian is hit with the musky, pungent odor of a wild animal. It's an acrid, sour smell that seems to radiate from the beast. Looking down at its body, it looks like that of a muscular girl, but as he watches, her musculature begins to stretch and expand, swelling and tearing apart until the girl he was chasing is a 6 foot 5 muscled minotaur. Damian does the only logical thing in the situation and whips out his phone, taking as many pictures as he can of the monstrosity in front of him. Then he takes off running. Unfortunately, the girl was faster than him back when she was human. Now that she's a giant predator with a stride that's easily twice his, he's got no chance. The last thing he remembers is being tackled from behind with the force of a truck, his body hitting the grimy metal of the walls of the alleyway before everything fades to black.

                                              Back in Jacob's room, Collin receives an email with the brute force program and calls his friend back to ask how it works. Steve tells him that he just has to load it onto a USB, plug it in, and it'll do all the heavy lifting for him. How long it takes will depend on the strength of the password, but it should be at least a couple minutes. Collin confirms that Steve's “on for next Thursday” and Steve warns him to stay out of trouble before Collin ends the call.

                                              It takes 20 minutes to break in, but Steve's program does the job. As Collin logs in, Steam, Discord, and Google Chrome all pop up. He begins to examine the various apps that Jacob used, looking for the most recent modified files. In his documents he sees titles for recent school projects, mostly involving folklore, mythology, mirages, and sleepwalking. In terms of his search history, his most recent activity seems to have been a series of comments on Reddit. Collin notes that his avatar is the default, his username the same randomly generated string that you get when you create an account. He's never made any posts of his own, only comments, and they're exclusively on the subreddit WeirdButTrueLA. According to the description, it's a sub for documenting weird or unexplainable happenings in the city. It seems to mostly be collections of screenshots or short stories describing personal experiences.

                                              From Jacob's comment history, it seems that he's been reaching out to people and asking to DM them for more information. His chat history shows that he chatted with a couple of them, introducing himself as a grad student looking to set up interviews. His earliest comment is about a year old and the most recent is in December of 2024. Oddly, the user, remakingK, has no comment or post history and seems to have contacted Jacob rather than the other way around. The message just has a Discord handle, the same as his reddit username. When Collin goes to Discord to check, he notes that Jacob has no servers and exactly one friend: remakingK. Their chat history has only one message in it from this mysterious redditor: "The person you're looking for can be found here."

                                              Attached to the message is a link to the Google Maps page for Collin's home address.

                                              This, notably, freaks him out. He begins going through various programs on the laptop trying to figure out if there's any hacking software installed.

                                              At this point, Collin is still working under the assumption that Jacob is some kind of master hacker who has cut through all the layers of his security, broken into his computer, and taken a picture of him using his webcam for his murder wall. But if we look at the evidence, this message history seems to imply that Jacob didn't even know where he lived or who he was until about 8 months ago. He definitely has stalker energy, and it does seem like he was looking for Collin in some form, but this remakingK individual was the one to point him in the right direction. This, combined with Mrs. Carver specifically telling Collin that she hoped he wouldn't let what happened get in the way of D&D, suggests a far more likely explanation for how he got that webcam picture, don't you think?

                                              Collin decides to change Jacob's laptop password to prevent anyone else from getting in. But as he's typing in the new password, he sees strange symbols like those on the edge of the medallion appearing instead of letters. The keys on the keyboard appear to be shifting into that shape as well, and when he looks at a book on the shelf, its silver title mutates before his eyes. It turns out that that is the limit of weirdness Collin can tolerate and he leaves, but not before snapping a couple pictures of the investigation board (which he has lovingly called the murder wall). It is at this point, as he's locking the door behind him, that he hears the ringing of 5 bells to signal that it is now 5 o'clock. This is definitely the second time he's heard those bells now, which only serves to freak him out even more.

                                              Just then, Ray and Derrik return from the third floor to receive a word vomited, context-less string of events from Collin of what just transpired. What's worse, Collin will not under any circumstances go back into the room. He's freaked out and keeps saying that it's not a good place.

                                              "Are you okay?" - Ray

                                              "No!" - Collin

                                              He explains that he broke into Jacob's laptop and found a lot of weird stuff, which Derrik thinks is crossing the line. "He had a picture of me on this murder wall!" Collin replies defensively. This, of course, doesn’t make any sense to Derrik, and he turns to Ray.

                                              "Is it just me or does he just say things all the time that he expects us to understand?"

                                              "I kind of think all of you do that. But it might just be because I joined late."

                                              He's right. Derrik gets on Collin's case, but both of them say the craziest things sometime with no context. Like, "Hey, I'm in the middle of being arrested by Sam (who you don't know). Oh, he was at the funeral. He's FBI," followed by a distinct lack of FBI or signs of arrest when you meet back up.

                                              As they make their way into the room, they find Damian lying unconscious in Jacob's bed, tucked in by some kind soul. Collin, still outside, emphatically points out that he was not in there when he left.


                                              Damian is floating in the void, but at the edge of his consciousness he can hear the sound of a lock being jiggled and the telltale sounds of someone trying to open a door. There's a grunt of frustration, maybe because of a stuck key, before a familiar masculine voice says, "Finally." He smells Trout Piccata, tasting capers briefly on his tongue before waking up.

                                              Both Keni and Xeno began screaming in disgust at the concept of tasting capers. Which is completely fair, but I'm just going off of what was written in the backstory! Later I learned that Xeno had forgotten about a particular experience that he'd written, leading to his surprise and confusion at this bit. When he realized what it was, he was like, "Oh.... Oh." Because it was definitely something big. We'll get there eventually.

                                              He's feeling queasy and has a pounding headache, which isn't helped by the fact that his vision is swimming. Derrik has a kind of blue aura radiating around him, as does Collin, though his is yellow. Strangely, Ray does not seem to have any particular aura.

                                              Xeno: "It's because he's not chosen. Maybe he's not real?"
                                              Well, he’s certainly not Ray-el.

                                              Damian refuses all attempts to check his vitals or make sure he's okay. Strangely, though he feels awful, there are no bruises anywhere on his body. He rationalizes his recent experiences as a weird hallucination and figures that the girl must have hit him or something. At this point, Ray starts looking at the other three with worry. It seems like they've all developed strange behaviors in a very short span of time.

                                              I mean, imagine. Supposed FBI arrests that didn't go anywhere. Hearing things that don't make sense. Claiming that the unconscious person in bed was not there when you left the room 30 seconds ago and the window is locked. Insisting you were chasing someone and then woke up lying in bed with no recollection of how you got there. It’s all concerning behavior.

                                              While his attention is turned away from Damian, Derrik notices the "murder wall" and notices a picture of a heavy iron key, the head crafted to look like a skeleton with blue gemstones glittering in its eye sockets. Derrik snaps a picture of it. That's when he sees the photos of himself and freezes. Despite everything, he's oddly calm about the situation.

                                              Damian, meanwhile, is looking for alcohol and drugs in the apartment since Abigail mentioned him "going hard" at the party and the toxicology report had said that he likely overdosed. But there are no bottles or drug paraphernalia around, and the minifridge is completely empty. It does not seem like the room of someone who was into the party scene. He joins Derrik at the murder wall and immediately notices a map of Union Station. Amtrak Baggage Services is circled in red and there's a receipt pinned to it dated July 31st, the day before Jacob's birthday (and the day he died). It appears that he had stored something there.

                                              "I'm glad you're not on here." - Derrik, to Ray

                                              "I wouldn't expect to be. I mean, I barely knew the family." - Ray, spoken like an NPC out of his depth.

                                              Collin in Derrik get into a heated argument about him coming inside, which culminates in Derrik telling him to either come in or leave and not come back.

                                              "I am doing this under duress." - Collin, taking a single step inside as Derrik locks the door.

                                              The PCs take some time to review the murder wall and spin theories about what exactly is going on. Derrik says he feels like cars have been following him for long periods of his life and that this makes him feel vindicated. He admits that he knew Jacob when they were kids but barely remembered him, and didn't even know his name until the funeral. He wants to know the name of Damian's employer because he feels like they might be out to try and kidnap him or something. Unfortunately, Damian has no idea who it is that employed him to look into Jacob. His top suspects were the Carver family and Sam, who he'd spotted scoping out the crowd during the funeral.

                                              Eventually, Collin shows the medallion to the others. Damian picks out that it's relatively new but effort was put into making it appear like an antique. From what he can tell, the medallion is likely some kind of badge or signifier of some sort. Perhaps a collector's item? He reasons that it likely has some kind of significance within some small, esoteric group, though he has no idea what that group might be.

                                              Collin checks his tracking app and notices Dave is on the highway heading back in the direction of the church. He gives him a ring, but gets little out of him other than a quick, "Hey boss" before loud sirens in the background drown him out. He asks what Dave's up to, but his voice is glitchy on the other end and the call ends up dropping. Anxious, Collin checks the news and finds out that there was a 2 car collision on the freeway just now, which must have been the source of the sirens. He sends Dave a couple texts, but they go undelivered.

                                              At about this time, Ray waves his phone at everyone. A quick search for Elias pulled up Elias Ren, a Teaching Assistant in the Anthropology Department at UCLA. The picture he shows everyone is of a skinny Asian man with circular glasses, hair in a small ponytail with an undercut, and preppy clothes. He's got a dark academia vibe and looks identical to the man Derrik, Collin, and Damian saw on the screensaver of Jacob's laptop. Having found their next lead, the four of them head out to the Anthropology building, deciding to split up. Damian and Collin go to check out the library while Derrik and Ray look for Elias's office.

                                              The first thing Damian and Collin notice is that there are 2 security guards conspicuously posted outside the library. They remember that Brian Williams had said something about security and the library right before the chase. But neither guard seems particularly interested in them as they enter. The Anthropology Department's library is on the smaller side. Perhaps because of summer session, there's only one person out in the stacks and one librarian behind the counter, a short woman with a bob and glasses. Damian introduces himself, and the second he says the word "investigation," she lights up and says that he must be here about the missing book. She tells him that she's already explained all she knows to the administration, but that she's willing to answer any questions that he might have. As far as she's aware, there's never been a case of theft from this library before, and it was such an obscure book that she can't understand why someone would steal it.

                                              Damian, who was originally going to ask about Jacob, switches gears immediately and begins to inquire about the book. She informs him that the book itself had no title on the spine or cover, but it was old and bore signs of extensive use. It came from a collection of books donated by former alumni and people with ties to the university, and it seemed to be written in some kind of "very strange hieroglyphics" or something.

                                              "Jacob and I spent so much time pondering what it could mean." - Librarian

                                              Damian approximates the size of the missing book from Jacob's room with his fingers and the librarian says that's the right size. When Collin shows her the medallion, she confirms that the strange hieroglyphs looked just like those runes.

                                              "Perhaps you should talk to Elias. He was the one who pointed it out to me in the first place." - Librarian

                                              Before they leave, they ask her about any other books Jacob might have been interested in. The librarian, who clearly does not know Jacob's fate, says there might have been a project last year for one of Elias's class to do with urban legends, but that was a long time ago. From what she remembers, he had checked out books on dreams and sleepwalking, as well as one about mirages. She mentions that she hasn't seen him in weeks, and the two PCs decide it's better to let someone else break the news to her.


                                              Meanwhile, Derrik and Ray make their way through the maze of hallways in the Anthropology department, finally arriving at a hallway deep in the heart of the building. At the far end is a small, nondescript door with the name Elias Ren written in chalk. There's a poster with two clocks in it, labelled Time Out and Time In. Based on the hands of the clock, it looks like Elias left 5 or so minutes ago and plans to be back in 25 minutes. But Derrik notices there's light on under the door and he can't make out any sounds from inside the room. Ray says it can't hurt to take a peek, so the two of them make their way inside.

                                              It's a small, cozy office space that is conspicuously clean. Elias is clearly something of a neat freak. There's a desk and three chairs, a mobile blackboard covered in papers, maps, and writing. He's got neat stacks of to-be graded papers on the desk, a laptop, and a satchel hanging from behind his chair. A candle is burning on his desk and it smells faintly of lavender.

                                              Derrik and Ray perform a quick search and almost immediately hit the payload.

                                              Derrik finds a book called "The City of Angels" and a few others on funerary practices and burial rites in the satchel. He takes a few pictures, but then notices the chalkboard has a map of the US with large red dots at particular spots across the country. On top of it is a map of missing persons cases, each one represented by dots. For some reason, the missing persons cases form clusters that match the areas of interest on the first map. More concerning for Derrik is the fact that Elias or someone else has drawn biblically accurate angels on the board. As Ray leans on it, it flips over and reveals something else. There are a series of symbols that look just like the ones on the medallion Collin found in Jacob's room. Under them is a quatrain.

                                              Gather the Keys
                                              Guard them and Wait
                                              Only their Union
                                              Will Open The Gate

                                              Derrik immediately pulls out his phone and starts capturing evidence. He tells Ray that he should probably flip it back around and he goes to do just that. It is at that moment that the boys hear the sound of a throat clearing and turn to see none other than Elias Ren standing with his arms crossed at the door. He hasn't shaved in days, bears noticeable dark circles under his eyes, and his right arm is bandaged from his elbow to his fingertips. His face is wet, as if he just splashed water on it.

                                              "...And what exactly are you doing in here?"

                                              Elias has a British accent, but at times it's rather faint or imperceptible.

                                              "Uh.... Coming to meet you?" - Derrik

                                              Elias raises an eyebrow at that. "I see," he says as he steps inside the office, closes the door behind him, and turns the lock.



                                              And that's the end of Chapter 3! Honestly, there's nothing quite like listening to the players scream in anxiety after I end on a cliffhanger. It's almost enough to stave off the sadness of having to wait an entire week for more. 3.5 hours just isn't enough.

                                              Overall, I'd say that I'm pretty satisfied with how this chapter went. The struggle of the Storyteller is wondering how things look from the perspective of the players and hoping that they don't feel too lost or out of their depth. But I've got great players who roll with the punches and never fail to throw curveballs my way. This session they really started using the group whiteboard to take notes, which fills me with joy and pride. I'm the kind of player that puts together a 100 page theory document when I'm a player in a mystery game, so it's fun to see the kinds of connections they make and the things they focus on. Now, the players don't know that they're in a Mage: the Awakening game, but you all do. So I'm wondering what people think so far.

                                              What's up with Jacob Carver? What do the strange things they're experiencing mean? How about that ominous poem on the whiteboard? Points and a NPC named after you for anyone who can correctly guess what's going on or what I have planned.
