        Some music composers make made-up pseudo-languages to put in their music exactly because of this effect. There is also an interesting psychological theory, that for everyone, their "non-native language" seems more expressive than the language they talk and conveys more emotion and feeling; this is attributed to the fact that due to you being unable to process the "text" of the speech, your perception focuses on more universal aspects of talking, which are supposed to surpass languages. Thus, for a person not speaking Japanese, listening to japanese anime with original audio and [their language] subtitles will feel more "expressive", so to speak.

                                    I always assumed that High Speech was a language that, unlike others, had an intrinsic relationship between the symbol (words) and the meaning. So, in English the word is "tree", but it could have been anything else, while in HS tree is tree because it couldn't be anything else. But reading this topic looks like I'm way off mark?
                                    Second edition is a bit different in the flavour text.

                                    As a mage I think of the supernal symbol of trees, getting that pure meaning into my head and then I just make a sound. Perhaps "Tagrus" for the sake of having an example. Another mage hearing me has their magical senses flash and although they hear the sound "Tagrus" inside their head the supernal concept of trees just pops into their head magically. Doesn't matter if they speak a completely different language than me, they know what I meant. They're also not going to get confused like you can with a normal language, even if they're a mathematician they're not going to think that I mean logic trees because that's not the symbol I invoked even though in English the word "Tree" could refer to both meanings.

                                    There's no guarantee that tomorrow when I say the word tree again in High Speech that it'll be "Tagrus" again. Maybe tomorrow the word "Saffam" pops into my head instead when I think of the supernal symbol. If you try to say tree in High Speech maybe "Volvandrigast" seems right to you instead. It doesn't matter though because the word is conveyed directly and magically. It's why Sleepers can never understand High Speech, they can't memorize the words because there are no words.

                                                    It works also with 'Accented High Speech' version from 1E Tome of Mysteries ( page 92 ), as character uses all the time the same original language for High Speech words.

                                                    Accented: The Accented form is superimposed on normal speech. The mage speaks a chosen language with particular inﬂections so that it acts as an occult “carrier wave” for the hidden “code” of High Speech. Mages using this form appear to be speaking normal words in a strange accent. The cadence, tone and associated body language convey the magical import of the language. This makes it seem strangely accented (and often archaic) to Sleepers. Sleepers can repeat these speech patterns to repeat the “secret codes,” and other mages and beings who look for High Speech (such as ancient Artifacts, spirits and tomb guardians) will understand.

                                                    The speech in the carrier language does not have the same meaning as that conveyed through High Speech accent, but Awakened witnesses can easily discern the secrets so conveyed. Mages who come from cultures that especially honor a living language prefer this form. It is sometimes called the Awakened version of a language (“Awakened Japanese,” for instance). Users often believe that it is the true form of High Speech.
