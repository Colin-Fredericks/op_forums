                    Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post
                    A noble/self-justifying Seer basically believes in the medieval ethos of the https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_chain_of_being&quot;]&quot;Great Chain of Being&quot;[/URL]. The people at the bottom need to be kept at the bottom and the top at the top because reality itself depends on it. There's a reason that the usurpation of the rightful king in Shakespearean plays results in nature itself being thrown into turmoil.

                    Lovecraft (in his cultural-scale social darwinistic racism) dealt with similar themes in &quot;At the Mountain of Madness&quot; and how the rising of the lower (the Shoggoth) cause the degredation that destroys Elder Things civilization.


                    Of course for anyone who actually believes in the professed ideals of liberal democracy, it's hard to not see this ideology as inherently villainous, but through much of history it's been taken for granted as a given truth (including by racist misogynists who claimed to believe in liberal democracy).
                    What is interesting is the Diamond uses the same logic in why they fight the Seers: the Exarchs are not the rightful rulers of the Supernal, as beings who took the Heavens by force, and therefore reality itself isn't what it should be. Hence the Lie and Paradox.


                    As for the need for Villians, the best groups are often the Pentacle. The Orders have enough greatness that they support outright black, who often are those who embraced their missions (or at least part of their Order's ethos) too much. Here are some examples:
                    - An arrow who creates conflict to gain challenges, or creates oaths that drives him to darkness.
                    - A Silver Ladder who "just can't get people to see the way" and thus forces them to.
                    - A Guardian who no longer knows who they once were as they cycle through Masques, or who no longer trusts any Mage as he has seen them all fail to many times.
                    -A free council Mage who is now a terrorist, not a freedom fighter, or who harms sleepers because "he doesn't believe anything should be kept from them, even if it may be painful" and thus exposes them to the shard of the abyss by actively using magic in front of them.
                    -A Mysterium Mage who claims that the only way to protect magic is to purge sleepers, or who willingly sacrificed sleepers and mages so as to be taught magical secrets by one of the bound which he fears may otherwise be lost for all of time.

                    Another group is the Scelesti. Serving the Anti-reality has actually less of an obvious evil, even if it is truly worse than a Seer. An example from 1e is a Aswadim (an Archmage sworn to the Abyss) who seeks to destroy all of the Shadow. Why? Because he wants to replace it with a version he created and raised from the Abyss that would create a Shadow that is not predatory amongst its denizens, but instead a positive community, and have this false Shadow in turn act to make humanity better. It sounds truly noble, and he turned to such a solution as he saw no other way to fix the Shadow with the the vast, God-like powers he had as an Archmage. He is also friendly and overall a decent guy(God?), even towards those who hinder him (to a point... He isn't a pushover.) It doesn't mean he should actually be supported though, but he can make players question themselves.

                    As for the Seers themselves, the "best" morally gray ones are the "believers" and the "fearful."

                    -Having those who truly believe that the Exarchs are Divine and thus serve from belief makes their actions somewhat harder to call out. I mean, can you truly fault someone who not only believes they are doing the work of the gods, but can actually point to their servants (Ochema), their influence and active control, and their issuance of commands for choosing to serve such beings? It may mean they do horrible things, but they truly believe that they are doing an actual God's will.
                    -The "fearful" Seer plays into this a bit to, and can overlap as well. A true believer would also fear their God's wrath, which unlike modern religion, they can actively see the Exarchs moving in the world and know that they will punish those (either via Ochema or other Seers) those who displease them, or worse, actively work against them. Even someone who doesn't see them as gods could rightly argue that the Seers are powerful, and that they would rather live in luxury and not fear some cosmic beings displeasure, even if that means serving a tyrant, than face the risk of such beings noting them and directing their wrath upon them.

                    Mixing the fearful and faithful seers in can make them somewhat more gray; both are selfish in a way but one that can make someone at least understand their motives. Just don't forget the ones drawn to the Seers cause they like power and want to be on the "winning team" either, or that even those who are faithful or fearful seers are going to engage in dark stuff in service of their lords.

                                                    As much as I'm liking the new edition of mage, I'm also having trouble with this. For all it's idealism the pentacle is flawed, you can find some real nasty bastards in their ranks and I love it, but the seers? They're just selfish assholes who have sold themselves. They know it, don't care about it, and even rub it in your face. I was really hoping that they wouldn't make it so obvious in the new edition.
                                                    One thing that you could do, and it's what I choose when I run a game, is this: The abyss is the result of a massive magical catasthrope due to the abuse of magick. The world had to be severed from the supernal to trap the abyss between both and that way prevent reality being swallowed by the abyss. Now, who did cause the catasthrope and who saved the world? That's where both factions disagree. The pentacle says that the exarcs created the abyss to rule a non magical humanity as absolute tyrants, the oracles are the heroic rebels trying to give humanity a fighting chance. The seers say that exarcs are the ones working tirelesly to keep both worlds safe from the abyss while they try to fix it. The pentacle is nothing but a bunch of ignorant anarchists working for a group of power hungry terrorists that don't care about destroying reality as long as they can get to the top. Who's right and who's wrong? Who knows? Perhaps they are even both right, or both wrong, you decide.
                                                    It's just a matter of making the setting your own.

                                                        Another awesome Seer motivation comes from the flavor quote from my favorite Legacy, The Quiescent:

                                                        "It's not a Lie. It's a governing principle of the world, and we should just get used to it."
                                                        To the Seers, the Pentacle is fighting a war that literally ended before the beginning of time. The Exarchs have sat on their thrones for the entirety of this reality's existence and nothing is likely to ever dislodge them; the Supernal has likewise been parted from the Fallen by the Abyss, and the Lie is as much a part of being human as any other possible descriptor. Dreams of mass Awakening or a return to Atlantis are pleasant fantasies, but utterly unlikely of ever coming true. No one has struck down even the least of the Exarchs over the span of human history, and the only grow more powerful every single day.

                                                        Why not serve the winning team and enjoy the ride? It's not a matter of being a selfish asshole or a divinely-inspired zealot, it's a matter of facing the facts and not throwing your life away chasing deluded idealism.

                                                        Remi. she/her. game designer.
