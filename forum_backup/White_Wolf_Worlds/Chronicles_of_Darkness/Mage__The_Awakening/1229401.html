    Storyteller Plot Idea

    06-22-2018, 02:00 PM
    So I've done some Storytelling over the last few years and am building a Mage campaign for some friends. I had an idea for a major plot villain and wanted some feedback on it.

    The Hierophant has been found. Or at least, that's what the Guardians are claiming. They are being secretive about it, and saying he will be arriving in Edmonton shortly. When the day comes, and he does arrive, he is able to demonstrate mastery over 5 different Arcana, specifically Forces, Matter, Spirit, Death, and Mind. In addition to this, his theoretical understanding of Arcana is unrivaled, he seemingly does not have ANY Inferior Aracana (as demonstrated by his ability to related all of the Arcana to each other without the gaps in understanding caused by having an Inferior).

    As icing on the cake, his very presence in the city appears to mitigate Paradox. Mechanically, all mages in the city will receive and additional reach they can use on all spell casting before risking Paradox.

    Seems pretty sweet right? Maybe a little too good to be true?

    The twist: An attentive mind might catch that the 5 Arcana that he demonstrates mastery of are infact the 5 Inferior Arcana for the 5 watchtowers. He is not the Hierophant; if anything, he is the Anti-Hierophant. He isn't mitigating Paradox, he's redirecting it. Every time a Mage makes use of the free reach his presence provides, that reach DOES risk paradox, just not at the location the spell is cast. Somewhere in the city, a ball of concentrated Paradox is forming, and each spell cast with the benefits provided makes it a little bit bigger. Once he accumulates enough Paradox, the Anti-Hierophant will unleash it all at once plunging the city/region/country/Fallen World (depending on desired scope of the campaign) into he abyss. If he succeeds, he'll destroy everything. Can the players figure out the problem before it's too late?

      It's workable, but you're going to want to figure out how they're redirecting Paradox so if the players do figure it out they can come up with plans. You're also going to want to figure out what the Anti-Heirophant is so that players can do research and come up with plans. You don't need to provide solutions to the problems, but there should be hidden information available for players to work with when coming up with their own solutions.

                      Isn't the belief in the Heiromagus a secret the Guardians keep from all but their most trustworthy members ? Even when there is a potential candidate, they would actively try to sabotage him, because if he is the real deal, then he will have the Wisdom to overcome trials most mages wouldn't. So one option is that the Guardians use some leverage on the party to make them investigate him, observe him, test him and so on. The basic idea is that they would use blackmail to make sure the player cabal remains discrete. The they hand out missions and give nice perks for completion, like imbued items, a safehouse, a few favors, things to sweeten the deal. So why doesn't the Order handle it themselves ? Because an influential masters feels there is already enough evidence to support his claim and wants to set him on the road to success (this master is a Scelestus). However other, more sceptic masters do not agree and are ultimately who setup the party's missions (behind layers of indirection, as the Guardians like to do).

                      You can also throw in an outsider to plant seeds of suspicion. For example, an Aegis Kai Doru who is conducting a parallel investigation and is willing to help the party, or throw in a surprisingly sane Banisher who only targets the AH (he Awoke when his town and by extension his mage wife were consumed by the AH testing his plan on a smaller scale ) or even the old "crackpot" Nameless mage that keeps warning people, fearful that his visions come to pass. You can even have the Seers begin attacking in an almost suicidal manner, because either they wipe out this threat ASAP or the Exarchs will send an ochemata down to take care of it, Unmaking everything nearby (flesh, minds, souls, spirits, everything) as a "safety zone when removing this cancer".

                      You can also have the Paradox buildup cause a massive stir in the Shadow, with the original inhabitants of the area fleeing in a panic, even high ranked spirits resorting to possession, others searching for asylum or even new haunts. All that can point them in the right direction.

                      I think it would be cool if they earned the info of what the Hieromagus even is and in the process see how such a person appearing rattles even the shadowy Guardians.

                                      An interesting idea.

                                      As others have pointed out, the whole hieromagus idea isn't really widespread outside the Guardians, but I can see a group of them going public with it if they think the messiah is here.
                                      As fun as the most oblivious use of said group is, as deadly magic-ninja bodyguards, if I recall correctly, part of the hieromagus’s “duties” was passing judgment on the Guardians for their sins. Maybe there are some who don't like the whole judged bit and try to discredit the false Messiah. Pontential allies for the PC-s abound.

                                      Other than that:

                                      Originally posted by TenguGrib View Post
                                      Once he accumulates enough Paradox, the Anti-Hierophant will unleash it all at once plunging the city/region/country/Fallen World (depending on desired scope of the campaign) into he abyss. If he succeeds, he'll destroy everything. Can the players figure out the problem before it's too late?
                                      It’s just my opinion, and you can do whatever you want on your table, bit I have a personal bias against the whole mass-destruction for whatever petty reason. Of course there may be a reason that makes sense, and the character may as well be insane(and seeing that He is dicking around with the Abyss he probably is.) but its probably not the way I would spin it.

                                      As I try to keep my antagonists both sympathetic and understandable, here is something:

                                      The collected paradox isn’t just a bomb(at least the A-H thinks it isn’t). It is part of a summoning ritual, bringing one of the Annunaki the stillborn universes and cast of timelines that live in the abyss. Should the PC-s fail the Anunnaki’s alternate history will overwrite the fallen world. The A-H thinks that said history is objectively better that the current one and tries to use it to bring a new golden age for humanity. (Perhaps he’s even right.)

                                      In the event of failure the PC-s somehow survive unchanged(Perhaps inside an archmage’ golden road or an emnation realm) and are stranded in the new world.
                                      They must gather those who similarly unchanged, and restore the world to its proper state.

                                      Incidentally, if you go with a golden road as the way of survival, most of the survivors will probably be seers, as they actively include archmages in their hierarchies and thus the most likely to be in a golden road when the paradox hits. But then you are involving archmages, so an alternative may be better.

                                      As they investigate the new timeline (whatever you decide it may be) they discover that their timeline got displaced into the abyss and now lives ass one of the Annunaki.
                                      They must replicate the A-H’s ritual to bring it back.(That it may be the original time-line and theirs is the invader probably best implied but not confirmed.)

                                      Granted it only really works in a global plot, but probably can be scaled back(Not an annunaki but an abyssal watchtower that gets Summoned for example.)

                                                  Some fantastic ideas, and yeah sorry for not verifying the term, Hieromagus is the correct word. I've just been reading Practical Guide to Evil a lot and Hierophant is one of the characters. (I highly recommend that online book for WOD and Exalted fans as the Named in it remind me of Exalted levels of power)

                                                  I love the idea of going the Annunaki route as I was a big fan of the Prince of a Thousand Leaves in the Boston book and used it in my first attempt at a Mage Chronicle.

                                                  I should mention that the Seers were recently kicked out of the city due to the Hierach activating an Artifact his Cabal dug up. Part of the campaign would be focused around realizing he did that by way of human sacrifice and drawing on the Abyss. He really does have everyones best interests at heart, and is willing to sacrifice his soul for what he believes in. The first part of the campaign was going to be focused on cleaning up all the messes left when all the Seers were suddenly and very abruptly evicted from the city, leaving their various experiments and Sanctums unattended. I even have a short story I wrote about one of the Seers in the city, taking place just before the eviction. Partly as a writing exercise for myself, and partly to help me flesh out his character. I planned on having him taking advantage of the situation to undermine his competitors among the Seers and elevate himself higher int he Tetrachy (spelling?). Seeing as he can't get into the city himself, using the players as pawns to weaken his opponents would be his approach, offering them tidbits of minor artifacts and worthless arcane lore for their help. In addition, he'd help them hunt down and capture the Selestus that's infiltrated their ranks (after all, the Abyss is as bad for the Seers as it is for the Pentacle).

                                                  I also am going to have at least one Selestus hiding among the Pentacle mages.

                                                  As for this New World which could end up replacing the Fallen World should the players fail to stop the AH, I love the idea of it being actually better. Like, objectively better. But I'd lean towards having it that their own loved ones are not around. Maybe vastly reduced presence of things that go bump in the night? An abundance of positive spirits of things like Love, Joy, Laughter, etc. I know all spirits can be a problem in the wrong situation, but maybe not in this New World. Hell, maybe even have it that Sleepers don't increase the risk of Paradox and Mages can practice out in the open. But again, none of the people they know and love are here. The AH was right, this world is better, but it isn't THEIR world.

                                                  As to getting them into that world intact and unchanged, yeah there's a number of ways that could be accomplished. Maybe a Golden Road, or a pocket dimension, or even being close to the AH when it goes off (after all he'd want to keep his knowledge of what he'd done and why so maybe he has a field setup to isolate himself).

                                                  Thanks to everyone who's posted so far, I'm getting a lot of wonderful inspiration from reading these replies.
