    Don't know if someone said this already, but I'll just say this as a last word and in case you missed it through the wall of posts (because I know I did miss quite a few points, cause I didn't have enough mental energy to invest myself fully in a debate like this) :

    It's easy to think murder, mind-control, and all those other immoral acts are AoH because they're immoral or unethical in the eyes of some arbitrary universal code of conduct.

    They are not. They just happen to cross with mortal concepts of morality and ethics.

    They're considered Hubris because they mean that the mage has gotten so low that he's willing to do stuff that he knows that it will have irreversible repercussions with unforeseen side-effects, when he is, you know, a wizard. As in, someone who can legitimately call himself a Master of the Universe, given a few years of training.

    It's the equivalent of a politician (someone in a position of power, much like a mage) murdering his rival, when he could have either best him with a better campaign, better policy-making, better lobbying, or literally anything else.

    The world itself asks you, "Did you have to do that? You have magic, for god's sake. Couldn't you have done better?"
    The irreversibility of it as pertains a sustained pattern is definitely a thing.

        Originally posted by Johnny Awesome View Post
        The Abyss isn't "evil", so I don't see any reason why immoral acts should invoke it more readily.
        Lets put aside Wisdom for a moment, and I kind of want to address this. I'm not trying to say anything about morals or not of Wisdom. But I think that this is kind of a misconception about the Abyss should be addressed.

        The Abyss isn't "evil" in the same way that Cthulhu isn't "evil." In that its an ancient being beyond the ken of mortals, while evil is a paltry concept devised by mortals to reflect harmful things to themselves and society. The Abyss/Cthulhu just is, has "existed" before reality and will "exist" after reality is swallowed up by entropy and the heat death of the universe. There is no Creator God to devise laws to rebel against that holds sway over this eternal phenomeon. The universe is large and uncaring, and doesn't care for human definitions of good and evil.

        That said, that's just Lovecraft's definition of evil. Or, perhaps, a Christian one. By many real world standards, however, the Abyss and all its inhabitants pretty much are evil. The Abyss is hungry and doesn't care who it hurts. It wants to feed on and replace entire swaths of reality. Dealing with the Abyss inevitably leads to your brain cracking. This isn't just a stillborn reality that you can pick fluffy bunnies that were never born out of. Its pure destruction and chaos that wants to return everything to chaos. Bringing anything Abyssal into reality undermines reality itself and leads to destruction and upsetting anything resembling natural balance.

        But even that doesn't address why immoral acts invoke it. For that, I'm going to start pulling information out of Intruders - Encounters with the Abyss and the Abyss chapter of Summoners. Its broken - not only are the things inside the Abyss twisted and harmful, but you invoke them by doing horrific things to people and places so they become broken as well. Seriously - repeatedly mind controlling someone allows abyssal mind spiders to take physical root inside their brain. You can summon an abyssal succubus that exists purely to indulge brutal sex crimes through acts of human depravity and cruelty; well, that's how you summon pretty much every Acamoth. When you break something, even metaphorically, it allows the Abyss to seep in. Broken promises, meaningless deaths, destroying the very concept of space, and more are all used to call down the abyss. Moral acts tend to not break things, while immoral acts tend to break things.

        There's a very good reason why mages call the Supernal and the Abyss with names that are synonymous with Heaven and Hell. To mages, the Abyss pretty much is evil incarnate. You commit attrocities to pull it closer. The Abyss represents the closest thing to demon summoners in Mage (and not just goetic thought projections), and is fully presented with all the warnings and badness we expect out of real world demons, including their mere presence being dangerous to life itself. So, yes.

        In conclusion, the Abyss is evil, and immoral acts allow it greater purchase in the world than it would have otherwise. Its always been that way since the very beginning of 1e, and has not changed in 2e.
