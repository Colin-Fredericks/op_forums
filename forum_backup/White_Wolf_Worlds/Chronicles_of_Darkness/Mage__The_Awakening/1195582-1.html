(and just for the record, the True Fae in 1e do not have Contracts with rating of more than 5 dots. In 2e, they don't have dot rating at all. You practically say "True Fae are Greater Beings" when they don't qualify, and force the ST to make up things on their own if they want to use them. Again, that is not a good design, as you practically have no antagonists in your game other than Archmasters, but spend wordcount on saying that they exist, and you need to make them up on your own.

Plus, Luna is, like, a really important character for Forsaken- the way you present it feels like you are sticking a finger in the eye of Werewolf. You practically present the death of one of the most powerful beings in the world as nothing more than a tool to talk about hubristic archmasters, and that it had no consequences whatsoever. Again, it is not a good design.

    In short, IMO, if you talk about Greater Beings as something more than a narrative tool, they should have actual mechanics to support them (which they currently don't. Even high Ranked spirits don't have it in the new edition- but they are at least possible to be translated and don't need to be made up out of scratch- and again, you shouldn't force the game design of one game on others and expect them to hold, especially when you contradict it by listing the Gentry, who don't answer the qualifications, as someone who do) for it is way too much work for a ST to do on their own- and you should be very careful with dealing with references to important characters to other gamelines, for those games have their own fans, and they may not take it very well.

                    In an incident where someone is essentially showing off, there is slightly more lax consequences than, say, truly attempting to erase one of your peers from existence. Remember that the act of killing, in the perspective of Archmages/gods/major Spirits etc, doesn't necessarily mean final. "Final death" is much more serious, as it is the human equivalent of dead.
                    That said, offing Luna just to show you can is disrespectful to Luna and her cohorts. It's like slapping someone in the face, and wasting their time and resources. In this particular scenario, I'd imagine that the Archmage who killed Luna would be forced to make amends, AND suffer social stigma for hundreds of years (plus always living with that skeleton in your closet). And in a social environment like Archmage, it's crucial to realize that social standing is *everything.* Your world as an Archmage expands, but your social world shrinks dramatically. What your fellow Archmages think of you can make or break you.

                    Of course, it takes more than direct combat in any random location to truly kill a Spirit as powerful as Luna. Common knowledge would indicate that the Archmage who killed her wasn't attempting a to really end her existence for good. Think less "whip out a handgun and shoot you in the head" and more "walk up to you and punch your arm to start a fight, then fistfight you until knocking you out." But it's still something that would garner you a bad reputation. Besides, Luna herself wouldn't necessarily *know* the Archmage wasn't trying to truly kill her; in her perspective, she may have been fighting for her life! Scaring someone that badly is bad form, too.
                    Also consider that meeting Luna is a big deal. She's not just casually strolling around. What this Archmage did was very intentional -- which makes it all the more gratuitous.

                    Then there are those Archmages who truly step over the line. Timelines get reversed and destroyed, histories rewritten, fates respun, new bodies created, people caught in eternally-looping rooms and bottomless pits... bad new bears. Best not violate the Pax too severely.

                    And remember: the Pax Arcanum can be applied to beings that are not Archmages if they are aware of it, even if they don't willingly follow it.

                    Maybe what the archmage killed was an Ochema equivalent of the Queen of Shadows, who reigns over only our planet’s moon, and the ‘true’ Luna resurrected ‘Earthly’ Luna?
                    That's the problem with gods: once they start replicating themselves, they're hard to pin down.

                    I'd imagine, by the way, that the creation of too many Ochema would be a violation of the Pax. Then again, making 10 is less severe than 50, so that's a good metric to which illustrates the subjective "sliding scale" of severity of breaking the Pax.

                    This Archmage fought "Luna." Whether that means one of Luna's Ochemata fought the "real" Archmage, or one of the Archmage's Ochemata fought the "real" Luna, etc... is for a more detailed story. It could even mean the Archmage and Luna gathered all their Ochema and had a full-on war, less cage match, more World War 3.

                            Casual conversation typically doesn't delve too deeply into nuance; that's what backstory and knowledge of the setting provide. What a casual conversation provides is a glimpse into what ARCHMAGES are thinking. A lack of nuance says something about the perspective of the one saying it.

                            Ochema are up-to-and-including perfect clones. At their most powerful, they are exact copies of the Archmaster, who share everything with him: soul, quintessences, mana, abilities, etc. As such, making one with free will and the same level of magical proficiency is paramount to giving birth to a new human (and a new Archmage!) - such an undertaking is not a light decision.

                            Much more common is an Ochema with either reduced freedom but full power, or reduced power but full freedom. Archmasters commonly employ Ochema as tools, like a remotely controlled vehicle-clone. Obviously, the practical applications of Ochema are extensive.

                            The creation of one, however, is a significant undertaking. As an Imperial Spell, it potentially takes a lot of effort - and besides, no two instances of Imperial Spells are exactly alike, even if you're casting the "same" Ochema spell.

                            Avatars are, in my view, what could be considered the Ochema of Ascended Beings. As they have no physical form, Avatars serve that purpose. I could be missing something here, though,

                            So, any rules for Ochema/Avatars, etc?
                            Well I'll throw this out there right away: the death of an Ochema is it's own punishment, as they are each an investment. Also, depending on whether it was free-willed, putting one in danger may risk wisdom decline. The original creator may suffer the same effect as physically dying in The Lie (i.e. Soul Shocked) but this is unconfirmed. Rumor has it that the Archmage must be sharing the Ochema's senses at the time of its death for this to happen - giving the Archmage some incentive to create Ochema which can function on their own and make their own decisions. Archmages known to be cunning and treacherous often make Ochema without free will however, as they fear their own creations will attempt to usurp them. As they saying goes, "evil always turns on itself."
