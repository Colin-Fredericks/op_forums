        Originally posted by Satchel View Post
        I really don't care about the particulars of how difficult you think learning a given Rote is — I'm saying you learned it from somewhere and therefore inhabit a system where you are likely expected to do more than putter around being forever-young. If your character's in an Order they very likely have a mentor who asks them to do things for them in exchange for their instruction, and even without the context of a mentor you aren't picking up magical resources for free. You have better things to do with your spell control slots in that environment than devote them to holding back the longest-term threat to your existence as a young Awakened mystic.
        Dropping the spell for a bit when you need the slots isn't a big deal either, especially if you're young. But this isn't really about Gnosis 1 babies, this is about people who are seriously considering becoming liches. The fact that it's available at character creation is mostly relevant in that it shows how much easier it is to achieve than every other form of lichdom.

        Originally posted by Satchel View Post
        "Spending the rest on being able to regain resources" costs you every free Reach that you wouldn't be spending on Advanced Duration with a rote. Rotes in turn do nothing to benefit your dicepool besides provide a Yantra — a Gnosis 2 Time 2 starting character is going to be leaning on the Mudra bonus and whatever other Yantra they choose to keep their dicepool above a coin-flip.
        4 dice is already a 75% chance of success. Assuming a modest Mudra skill rating of 2, the use of a Mantra (+2) and taking a -2 penalty to bump the Duration up lands you at 6 dice and ~90% chance of success. Increasing the dice pool through extended ritual casting, higher Gnosis/Time/Skill and more yantras (if available) trivializes the roll completely. It's true you can't safely cast the full version at a moment's notice, but casting an incomplete version (that doesn't let you regain Mana, say) is quite enough until you can get to safety and do a proper ritual casting.

        Originally posted by Satchel View Post
        Veil of Moments 1) does not un-ring the bell of "I have five days left until I hit my expiration date and every second out of this spell is one I'm not getting back" and 2) is a two-dot effect and therefore will disintegrate with relatively small applications of Dissonance or dispellation.
        I agree with you here. It's not a fantastic solution when you're already at death's door - though still better than any of those Indefinite spells, because 5 days you can dip into in an emergency is much better than no days.

        I hadn't actually considered Dissonance, but it doesn't seem like that big of a deal - it only applies if a mortal realizes you are unnaturally ageless, and any still-human lich will need to take steps to prevent that from happening.

        Originally posted by Satchel View Post
        The existence of Veil of Moments does not imply the widespread quality of Rote forms of Veil of Moments or the universality of Mastery of Time and in the meanwhile the world has plenty of forces running around that can age a human years in a minute or cast down magic with a glare, including some Supernal beings.
        Nor is there anything that implies it's rarer than a Rote of any other 2-dot spell.

        Originally posted by Satchel View Post

        Oh, I misread the wiki. I thought it said they were Reapers to sustain their lichdom, but it's the other way around.
        That is not my assumption, though. My assumption is that most mages who are preoccupied with living forever would think to check if maybe there exist any simple Time spells that halt aging indefinitely.

        Originally posted by Satchel View Post
        Power. Efficiencies. Better fringe benefits. A more robust source of agelessness. An actual honest-to-god Mystery to probe. Most routes to immortality are not as fragile as Veil of Moments. Most Reapers are not just messing with souls for a way to live longer.
        I still contend that it is more robust than many source of immortality that are harder to achieve, which is my whole beef. It's obviously safer than a Spell Of Not Dying, but recasting a spell every now and then is also a hell of a lot more expedient than stealing souls with about the same frequency - and a hell of a lot less likely to bring the Pentacle hammer crashing down on your head. Even stealing a new body every ~30 years is arguably a bigger hassle, depending on your Gnosis, how badly you need that spell slot and how likely you are to get your spell removed, but Veil of Moments is still hugely more ethical.

        Transformative lichdom is obviously a different story, and while it sidesteps a lot of the issues other liches have, you do have to be willing to give up your humanity.

        Which is ultimately why I don't like Veil of Moments. Llichdom is described as both difficult and either dangerous to the point of idiocy (Spell Of Not Dying), deeply unethical (Tremere, bodysnatching) or requiring you to completely sacrifice your humanity (ghost-liches, spirit-liches etc.). Veil of Moments bypasses every single one of these points, providing an easy, safe, morally uncomplicated and wholly untransformative means of near-indefinite longevity for anyone who wants it. Apart from a few edge cases it completely removes the archetype of the decent human being driven to extremes by the sympathetic motivation of not wanting to die (because that person would just use Veil of Moments rather than resort to any of the other methods) and leaves you only with the dick-pistons who kill for convenience and the weirdos who just really want to be Goetia for whatever reason.


        This is the last I will say on the matter, because I think we've derailed this thread badly enough as it is. If you still don't see my point of view, I think we'll have to agree to disagree.

                                        I think the point that Satchel is trying to make, is that though the Veil of Moment's does pause the clock of your lifespan for it's duration, it isn't so much immortality as it is prolonging life - as whenever the spell is not active, your clock is still ticking.

                                        In my opinion, at that particular level, it's a moot point until you reach the point where your walking around for thousands of years.However - this is still not a good form of immortality compared to some of the others. A better alternative than say, the tremere's lichdom, but as was pointed out - if you're a tremere for immortality, you are doing it for the wrong reasons (Tremere are Reapers first and foremost - immortality is just a bonus). That said - with the Veil of Moments, you are sacrificing -something- in exchange for those precious saved seconds.

                                        You are sacrificing growth.

                                        Now - mechanically of course, it's easy enough to drop the spell, spend "X" experience, and then put the spell back up. But that's a purely mechanical expression of a significant amount of effort - increasing traits, growing, and moving forward is far beyond the course of simply 'expending experience points'. The narrative is far more in depth when you think about it - your knowledge doesn't increase from an initiates to a disciples over the course of a few seconds - expending that increase represents training, study, and precious effort put into actually trying to grow, things which take time - things that, while the spell is active, are all frozen. No matter how much studying you do - nothing seems to stay. No matter how much exercise you do, you don't grow more healthy or less healthy. You simply 'exist'. For a person, this kind of lichdom might work - simply 'existing', because you are so afraid of death you are willing to be frozen in place. But for a mage, this kind of lichdom is an anathema far worse than altering yourself into a different kind of immortal being, using death mastery to take efforts not to die, or even stealing other people's bodies or years. At least the Lich's who become ghosts or steal bodies have a certain amount of respect and fear from Awakened Society - the mage who chooses not to grow in exchange for life would be viewed with little more than pity and contempt.

                                        ... At least, that's my view on it anyways, and what the limitation of not being able to spend experiences seems like it's supposed to represent.

                                        ... Though that kind of explanation does, admittedly, fall flat when a player tries to spend experiences towards raising their contacts or a trait that is not 'internal' and shouldn't be effected by the spell.
