            You could always use the less orthodox reading of the Cumulative Paradox rule. Basically, rule isn't very clear if it talks about adding +1 Paradox Dice per Paradox Risked in the scene to All Spells, or to spells that risk Paradox "to begin with". I like the unpopular ruling, mostly because it allows risk generation even in slow scenes (scenes can be long) instead of making pretty much "intense action scenes only". It also introduces a soft limit on a number of times you can try something - if you risked Paradox at least once in a scene, you can't really fish for Praxis in order to mana regen because each fishing attempt will bloat the paradox risk.

            EDIT
            For reference, page 115 of core rulebook. Dice Modifiers table, third entry.

                                                        Originally posted by WHW View Post
                                                        Currenlty airing anime Little Witch Academia shows a lot how Dramatic Failures on Spellcasting and Paradox can make Mage game fun while also progressing character arcs and not necessarily just act as story derailing punishments. Do Paradox in that way and players will be tempted to Dramatic Failure that Failure and will be more willing to not do everything they can to overpower Paradoxes.
                                                        Pretty much this. I find Paradox to be much more interesting and to come up a lot more when it isn't used as a punishment stick. While Paradox can and should end disastrously every now and then, to do so every time (or even most of the time, really) makes it way too predictable. Yes, Paraadox warps and twists a spell, changing it from how the casting mage originally designed it, but who's to say that those changes can't be beneficial? Increased Potency, targeting even more of a mages enemies than said mage was able to do without it, etc. IMO Paradox shouldn't be a punishment, it should be another temptation. Scelesti exist for a reason. The Abyss is another, albeit dangerous, path to power and showing that Paradox can help you as much as it screws you over goes a long way into proving that.

                                                        And even when you do use it to screw players over, do your best to make it interesting. Make them look forward to seeing what happens next, not dread how they know they're about to get screwed again. It'll make your players a lot more willing to let Paradox fly rather than stifling it every time, and with it usually being just as useful as it is not, when it comes time to hit them with a really nasty one then it'll be one to remember rather than just the norm.
