                    I allways thought of hunters as mortals that evolved and became resistant to the lies of the supernatural, be it the Masquerade, Lunacy, Qiescense, etc... I consider them no sleepwalkers, nor servants of the Lie.

                    My thought is that Hunters are humans that have become resistant or inmune to a sickness. For me, the key word is evolution, or if you want, their minds or souls are able to accept and digest the concept that "there are monsters out there" without stripping their sanity to pieces. It keeps the theme of hunter centered on the sense of humanity.

                    Of course they have fear of a Werewolf (oh my, of a Werewolf and of a natural grizzly bear running to them while roaring fiercely and disposed to chomp the hunter's head), but that fear is caused no more by Lunacy (well, this is the way I treat hunters).

                    This moment of clear awareness may come from a traumatic experience, a natural curiosity, a fanciful mind, a breaking point or born already with it. The fragment of the abyss is there, but it affects the mortal no more. Is useless. The Lie cannot manipulate the Hunter no more, no for blinding him nor for using him. Nor this fragment, nor another one. He is inmune.

                    What prevents them from awakening? I like to theorize that the Vigil makes them more aware of their humanity. It reinforces their sense of being human, and that sense of belonging is what prevents them from awakening. This sense of belonging can take any form: racial superiority, racial survival, destroy any menace to human race, serve and protect, academic curiosity, scientific coldness, and all the grey tones in between you can think about.

                                I have, in fact, used that very premise in a long-running Hunters in Rome game. The antagonist was one of the forebears of the Seers of the Throne, and he wanted to thrust Rome into chaos because a Fallen Rome was easier to keep Asleep than an Enlightened Empire. He had a number of mystery cults and political allies in several provinces as well as connections in the Parthian Empire, and the vast majority of the game was taking down his allies. The catch, of course, was that he was actually keeping them alive and helping them because he honestly thought their attempts to stop his plans were just creating even more chaos, a la 'I win either way.'

                                A Mage makes a really great Big Bad or Man Behind The Man in a Hunter game. But that's like, one Mage, not a Cabal or, god forbid, an Order.

                                            I've been toying with the idea of letting mortal player characters (not even hunters) go up against a mage if I ever get around to ST:ing a campaign of my own that I've been thinking about for a while. But despite knowing that two of my potential players read this forum I'm going to describe how I'd do it:
                                            First of all, it would be an unaffiliated mage with neither a cabal or order to fall back on.
                                            Secondly, I'd carefully select Arcana dots that don't have an obvious use that straight up incapacitates one or several of the characters with a single spell. I'm not saying which Arcana because that'd be too much of a spoiler if I get around to using this idea.
                                            Lastly, I would not portray the most competent of mages. They would definitely not be stupid, but lack of training and experience can give the players an edge if it comes to a straight up fight. It's not like the mage can take five minutes to peruse the book to find the perfect spell to cast in actual combat. For example, a Matter mage would not necessarily realize they could end the fight instantly by solidifying the air around all enemies.

                                            And yes, I realize this mage would be severely limited compared to any player controlled mage ever, and that 99% of all mages will wipe the floor with a group of mortals, regardless if they're experienced hunters.
