        Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post

        I do wonder though, when - in linear time - the first Awakeings were.

        One idea I had for PALEOlithic Mage, so as not to immediately turn Mages into gods of the setting with the mere ability to shape matter into shapes they want in a setting where basic tool use is cutting edge, is that the first linear time Awakened could only access the Subtle Arcana. The Gross Arcana came with the Agricultural Revolution.
        I don't think a paleolithic Awakened would be considered a god in-setting. Definitely a mythic hero, though, tasked by the gods to fulfill a grand destiny in the benefits of his tribe or clan. Remember that, back then, the physical and spiritual world where much more interrelated; the Pangaeans where probably the first gods and caretakers of early Homo sapiens. Awakened would not have been the big fish in the pond, but the first casualty when the gods and humans were in strong disagreement.

        To be able to forge tool is a potential skill for the Awakened, yes, but I'd argue at that time their skills would have been put to much more pressing matters no other tribe member could accomplish, like dealing with spirits, appeasing pangaeans, looking for fresh water, weather casting, safe travel, etc. Many of these task would need for the Awakened to be far from their tribes and doing things noone else can do.

        Also, an important consideration is population numbers. Nomadic tribes rarely number more than a few hundreds, and even considering a much higher occurrence of Awakenings, that still makes the Awakened a thing of myth and legend, appearing only in some generations, and signaling a time of profound change for their communities, either for good or ill.

                                        And it depends. Dissonance kicks in when mortals realize something is obviously magical. It isn't based on belief so much as a recognition of a magical aspect. So even if mortals beleive in a mage's ability to make a weapon that is resistant to harm and stronger than any stone blade (i.e. a steel sword) their recognition of it as magical is itself damaging to the magical Patterning and can destroy it.

                                        Versus the modern era, where a steel sword is not obviously of magical origin (unless the sleeper knows a mage created it with magic) as steel blades can be made mundanely. It isn't like Ascension, where tech is magic now accepted by the consensus, but rather that the abyssal spark in sleepers can't pick up on Supernal magic now without the sleeper being aware of obvious magic (i.e. a breaking point). Thus, things that would have been fantastic in the past but are now mundane wouldn't trigger a sleeper unless something gives away it's magical nature.
