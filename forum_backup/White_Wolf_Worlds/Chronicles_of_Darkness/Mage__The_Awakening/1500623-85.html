    This is purely in the realm of homebrew, but I think that if you go with the interpretation that secondary casters don't factor in the primary actor's Yantra bonuses and spell factor penalties (which is my preferred interpretation as it would play better), then I would love to let the secondary actors use Yantras of their own, with the regular non-stacking caveat (i.e. any Yantra used by the primary actor or by another secondary actor can't be reused).
    That way, you can have the circle of mages each holding a tool of their own, and it actually does something.
    It would also mean that a cabal with high Shadow Name investment can get a lot of mileage out of teamworking spells that fit shared themes (as the Persona is potentially the best Yantra you could possibly have on a non-Rote spell, and it's naturally stackable since each is tied to a different identity (unless something really funky is going on)). And I think a cabal with such compatible magical identities should be incentivized to work their magic together.

        Originally posted by Satchel View Post
        Why, in this hypothetical use of a system where you want the secondary actors to roll more successes, are you assuming that the point is to maximize spell factors through penalties, much less that the primary actor is working with what can rightly be called the bare minimum reasonable odds of success to cast a spell that their less-talented secondary actor is able to cast normally?

        Just because the spellcasting rules lean hard into the "only one success is needed to succeed" part of ChroD's base system doesn't mean the other reason you want more dice is gone, particularly when the leader still benefits from having the spell as a Praxis or Rote.

        The two core Merits that affect teamwork spellcasting remove the "unskilled penalty" for secondary actors (and increase the bonus they contribute on success by one) and grant the primary actor 8-Again, respectively. Setting aside the options that provide extra steps or Reach to the primary spell factor, an exceptional success can bypass Withstand, generate Mana, or create Conditions that grant Arcane Beats, all of which are sufficiently major for a mage to tap their fellows for assistance, particularly if they don't have the Gnosis, Yantras, or stamina necessary to build a more conventional Imago on their own in a reasonable timeframe.

        I am also curious why the assumption is that the casters will roll for and release Paradox in this situation. By definition, any teamwork casting is working with one more Reach than would be available to a mage casting in an action scene, and containing a Paradox does not penalize the spellcasting roll. The most likely situation where Paradox is an issue, outside of hypothetical wildly inadvisable constructions, is that the primary caster's Imago uses more Reach than one or more secondary casters can safely incorporate, whether because they're an Adept upscaling an Apprentice-level spell or because they're using a Rote the rest of their team haven't learned.

        Like, the point of teamwork casting is very much not to keep your net dicepool as low as possible and flip a coin on whether the firework explodes as planned, it is to compound the advantages of ritual casting to allow uncommon breakthroughs or cut down on the overall number of ritual intervals needed to achieve a desired result.
        I don't assume anyone is going to release a paradox, I assume the more people you include in the ritual, the more likely someone who doesn't like you, or has a political agenda that wants to see you fail, gets included (never mind a secret scelesti or seer spy). And they choose to release the paradox intentionally.

        Getting an exceptional success does become easy, and unexceptional, but it is rarely useful.

        A condition and a beat? Pretty sure that are many ways to otherwise get a beat and magic can directly create conditions.

        Getting an extra step in the primary factor? Trust me, taking a -2 dice pool modifier is a far more effective (and reliable) way of achieving that than giving yourself a good chance of hitting 3/5 successes.

        Getting an extra reach in the primary factor? I guess it depends on how available mana is, but [gnosis/2] (rounded up) is enough to mitigate one extra reach.

        And generating mana? Again there are more reliable ways to generate mana. The only spell which actually costs enough mana that you would really want a refund is Create Truth, and if you *can* cast it, you now have a Hallow so mana shouldn't really be an issue...

        You seem to be questioning why the magic system is built around a risks reward mechanic where you minimise your dice pool (risking the spell failing) against maximising your outcome (additional spell factors) - ignoring the second decision of risk of paradox ba additional reach - core to the decision making of every spell is how you go about getting enough out of it and what risks you are willing to take.

        So unless you are planning actually push yourself, why are you bothering with a ritual at all? (Except when you need to break an unbeatable withstand, and fish for an exceptional success... Though making a system for making exceptional successes mundane and easy to achieve doesn't seem that interesting from a narrative perspective, imho).

        As for the Merits you mentioned, the Free Council one requires everyone to have the same focus, so less than 1 in 5 mages can help, and the Mysterium on is one is arguably the best order specific merit in the game... At least the first dot. Especially from a narrative point of view of having the Mysterium include new members in their rituals where they don't have the Arcanum requirements... It gives good vibes.

        But again, everyone needs it, so if the Hierarch asks for all of the Moros to get together and cast some really big Death spell, neither order merit is actually going to be usable (or all the Acanthus in the Consilium to cast a prophecy for the coming year to guide the Consilium...).

        You are always guaranteed that every member of a given path can participate, because they have to have at least 1 dot in their ruling arcana. So Path based rituals make some more sense... (Where Order based things feel like they are more obscure, private affairs).
