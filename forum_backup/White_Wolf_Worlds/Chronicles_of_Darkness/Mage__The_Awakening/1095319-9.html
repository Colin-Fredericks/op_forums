        Originally posted by Johnny Awesome View Post
        Is there a reason why the Gateway would require a living host like Balam's brother?
        First idea that comes to mind: The Prince embodies human cannibalism - a violation done by a person, one flesh subsuming another. Even a mighty Spirit is not a full person and doesn't even have flesh. It needs to Claim a human to contain all the necessary symbolism to refine Resonance with the Prince.

        Alternatively, Abyss is empowered by Wisdom and Integrity loss. To enhance the connection, the Intruder needs a soul and mind to grind town into delirium and incoherence. Since the brother is an important NPC, it could be neat to figure out if the process makes use of his personality, or discards it like trash. The former might tie the encounter to his and Baron's relationship. The latter explicitly makes it all unfair - Abyss not caring who suffers for it.

        In fact you could determine is there was a reason that the brother found the Soul Stone, or if it's all just an awful coincidence.

                        The way I read it (and it is ambiguous), the Temple "landed" in the Shadow. The aforementioned Iris is located near the shore and leads from the Material into the Shadow, which is how Cultists gain access. A cannon assault would require the NPCs to move their ship into the Shadow, which is difficult, but possible.

                        I you already planned the Temple to be in Twilight, you need to decide which frequency it's in. Death, Spirit, Mind or other. The relevant Arcanum can open Irises to that frequency, and cannonballs can be blasted through. Alternatively, the NPCs can cast Correspondence (Space 1) to learn the Key to the Cultists' Verge, open it from afar and aim really well (Compelling of Space should be enough to deal with the dice penalties).

                        If you need to design encounters, at least some of them may take place in the Shadow that has been twisted by the Temple's presence.

                                When Boston Unveiled was published Abyssal Verges weren't a thing. I assume if it was written today, the Temple would be made into a Verge. However, whenever the Abyss is involved, consistent metaphysics can be discarded, especially if it's in favor of players having fun. You could also assume that the Temple doesn't "come" from the Abyss - it's merely the Shadow beginning to reflect the Prince instead of the Fallen World. The Cultists would be trying to pull the Prince even closer, so that the Material becomes overwritten as well.

                                I believe the following has priority: Your PCs and NPCs already made a plan based on information they learned. You don't want to retroactively lie to them. Did you lead your players to believe that the Temple is in the Material (or Twilight)? Hold on to that, and reshape the metaphysics to fit. Did they wrongly assume that the Temple will be easy to reach? In that case you can surprise them, make the plan with the ship pointless, and force your NPCs to improvise a new plan during the assault.

                                        I have two players that have chosen workable concepts: Obrimos Perfected Adept & Moros Necromancer Investigator.

He's in the Adamantine Arrow and wanted his own Sanctum & Hallow (2 dots) which is a Sacred Tree in the secluded area of a city park.

                                        I know it's off the wall, but this player really wants all this eclectic stuff and I really believe in letting people play the characters they want to play.

                                        He's never played a WoD game before and I'd really like to figure out how to make this character work. Here are my questions:

                                        1. Can a Raven Greed spirit make any sense?

                                        2. Can a Sanctum based around a Sacred Tree in the middle of Boston Commons park make any sense? How can this be a Safe Place, for instance?

                                        3. Is there an Acanthus Legacy with Matter as a 3rd Arcana make any sense?

                                        I'd really appreciate assistance making this jumble of stuff all fit together somehow, because let's face it: The idea of an Acanthus Arrow Junk-artist Matter mage tasked with protecting a Sacred Tree with his Raven Spirit could be very cool.

                                        With Fate, you can use shielding/ruling to Prevent people from ever being fated to enter or something similar. This could make a sufficiently secluded area secure even without traditional security measures. People would just never find it or choose to go there under normal circumstances. Creating a Safe Place with dots equal to potency seems like a decent effect for such a spell. Representing it with just pure Merit dots would just be a way to not bother with the bookkeeping of replacing the spell with a higher version when you spend XP to increase it or to deal with the particulars of spending WP dots and regaining them. There are likely to be consequences to "walling off" what once might have been freely accessible to other supernatural entities though.

                                        I dont personally know of such a Legacy, but canonically Mages create personal legacies all the time. Joining an old storied Legacy is cool, but realistically I think most Mages are rocking personalized Legacies. I also think most of them dont get past One or two attainments. Anyway, you can probably do something with the idea of "One man's trash...etc". At least, thats what comes to mind for me. Off hand though, everyone starts with at least 3 different arcana at chargen by default(just the way the rules work out). You don't really need a Legacy right away just because they started with some Matter dots. Only Arcana they aren't allowed to start with is Forces as an Acanthus.

                                        Raven greed spirit makes total sense to me and others already said good things about it.

                                                    Hey, I'm already invested. I'm happy to see any updates you're willing to share.

                                                    As for the Ban spell, here's a quote from the book:
                                                    "Magic that manipulates space, like a teleportation power or the ability to step from one world to another, provokes a Clash of Wills to allow ingress or egress."

                                                    If the character has supernatural power to move through the Gauntlet, or into some half-formed Abyssal otherworld, that might be enough to escape (with Clash of Wills). Powers that let you escape bindings and entrapment should count as well.

                                                    If your Ban is combined with a different Arcanum to only affect some phenomena, like adding Life to only trap human beings, the subject might transform themselves into something else to escape. Perhaps using Veiling could be enough (with Clash of Wills).

Everyone received Steadfast, Inspired and +3 on a skill for 3 uses.

                                                        They set out on the Hawk of Guangdong (acquired by the PCs and gifted to the Dead Wrens in Beast of Burden).
