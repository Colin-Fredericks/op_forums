    Tell me about your Guardian character

    04-01-2017, 12:56 AM
    I've never played a Guardian. I've never been able to "get into" them. Their themes seem to be religious fanaticism (hieromagus), paranoia, betrayal, and weird fanatical beliefs about vulgar magic and paradox that don't really jive with the game mechanics. They seem like worthy antagonists on par with Seers rather than player characters, or at best NPC "men in black" who show up if the PCs get too crazy with paradox. I don't really understand why in role play terms a mixed cabal would allow a Guardian as a member. Mages of any sense will assume that the Guardian is just a spy whose real loyalties lie with his order rather than with the cabal. Guardians recruitment and initiation procedures guarantee it, and they have the reputation to match.

    I'd like to get some insight from players who have played Guardian characters about how they made it work in a mixed cabal. What was the character background? Why did the character become a Guardian? How did the character interact with the rest of the cabal? Why did the cabal trust the Guardian character? How did the character deal with the almost universal mistrust and dislike from mages of other orders? Did the character end up betraying the cabal somehow for the sake of their order or their fanatical beliefs? How much tut-tutting did you need to RP whenever the other PCs used vulgar magic or experienced paradox?

    EDIT: I guess the bottom line is, why would anyone want the RP a member of the Mage Stasi, and how would you even make that work with a group of other players who don't want to play the same.

      I just started to play a character that is a Guardian of the Veil. She is a Fate Mage and a member of the Thread Cutters. She is from Greece and when she arrived in London stayed to herself, she keeps in touch with the Local Guardians but not openly. She befriended an older Mage that belongs to the local Arrow, the friendship grow and she came to confide in him. This grew into a Mentor/Tutor relationship. The other Arrows of London have never seen her outside of his company and most know that he teaches her so most of them believe her to be Arrow, or atleast don't believe her to be Guardian. She is still relatively new to the City, Forgot to mention it is 1859.

      In the Chronicles the London Concilium leadership has an uneasy truce with the Seers of the Throne and openly hostilities have abated. I believe that the ST stated that the City is controlled by 3 Diamond order and 3 Seers of the Throne acting as the Concilium. The Arrow opposed this and alot of them were attacked and killed. The Arrow hate that the Seers have so much power in the Concilium but are to weak to oppose it at the moment.

      This brings me to the Cabal, there is a Cabal of Nameless anarchists that has been poking both sides of the fence Seers and Order. They have become enough of a nuisance that the Seers and the Concilium are looking into dealing with them. Their last attack was on a Pylon of Seers and this ruffled some feathers and got notice of the Arrow. The Cabal is an untrusting sort and they have been betrayed in the past by others. The ST to bring my character into the group sent her to the Cabal with a letter of introduction from the Leader of the Arrows, offering her to help them in their struggles with the Seers. She represented that help and the letter asked to allow her to join them in their struggles. She was instructed by her Mentor to do whatever it takes to join the group and aid in their attacks on the Seers and to try and dissuade anymore attacks on the Diamond Orders as a whole. The Cabal knowing the General and that he is Arrow and introducing her as a Loyal Servant assume that she is Arrow and she has not dissuaded this thought. But the only way that they would take her in is she cast a Sworn Oath to not betray them. She said she would take the Oath is they also took it, and after some intense negotiations everyone agreed to take the Oath. She cast the oath with a conditional duration that it would last until all parties involved felt that it was no longer needed, and trust was established. The group is taking on another new member and he is an Arrow, and he has not known her as anything but an Arrow. The oath went as follows:

      "By the hand of Fate we are bound by this Oath to do no intentional harm to each other, I swear that I shall do no intentional harm to either of those in my presence, to break this Oath shall sever my connection to the Supernal and strip me of my Magic, until a time that I can Atone for my actions and pay restitution. I further swear that I shall not Alter not Cancel this Oath without the mutual Approval of all involved."

      So now a Guardian is firmly entrenched in the Arrow. She has no intent on betraying the cabal or the Arrow, but she is still a Guardian and a Thread Cutter. We will see how this turns out.

              From the guy who plays Cowl for Dave's The Man Comes Around:
              Well, I have found that the key to playing a successful Guardian PC is to remember rule one: thou shalt not dick over thy cabal mates. And also to remember the corollary to rule one: unless it is really important or really unimportant (and ideally amusing - for them, not for you).

              It is expected that you will have secrets, and use them. It is expected that you will draw on your connections for benefit. It is expected that from time to time you will manipulate the other players (but please, please never try to do this using the game rules and a Something+Manipulation roll). What you must always do, though, is be a part of the party. Not a lone wolf tolerated for the sake of the game, or someone on the periphery who is kept around because they have useful data. If you are going to get away with doing 'Guardianish' things (secrets, lies, the occasional sneaky or blatant murder, &tc) without putting unacceptable strain on the party both IC and OC, you need to be a solid member of that party. Helpful, useful, likeable (even if with an Eyeroll at times, or a love-hate relationship).

              Despite the way the Guardians are often used as an antagonist force within the Mage setting by many GMs, they are explicitly not like the mirrorshade-wearing New World Order of the old days (whom, I admit, I still have a fondness for, as my fellow players will attest). The super-spy or even FBI agent archetypes of the old days have given way to the manipulative mob boss, the hard-bitten enforcer, Supernatural-style hunter, or the religious jihadist, and because so many spy/agent PCs tend to be played as boilerplate avatars of their badge or of James Bond rather than as rounded characters with their own personalities and motivations, I think this is all to the good. The idea that playing a role corrodes your own essential identity is explicitly (and interestingly) explored within the theme of the GoV, it is true, but there is so much more to the Masques than James Bond or Fox Mulder or Agent Smith.

              Whether you embrace or reject the religious aspect (a conflict occuring within the Order in-setting as well as just when generating a character) you are able to explore the morality of magic in a way that other Orders are not - in that you are bound by duty to do something about it. However, falling into the trap of being the 'guy that whines about vulgar magic' in a PC group is another pitfall to a bad experience playing a Guardian: after all, the majority except the most fanatical of the GoV will use it occasionally. Instead, try to channel, focus, encourage, barter, coax: you are not just an agent enforcing ideals, you should be an advocate of them, (a ... prophet, perhaps?).

              While the other Orders have themes and methodologies and beliefs that bind them together, nowhere else (except possibly some elements of the Adamantine Arrow) are the ties as strong. Factions within the Guardians have their differing methods (Id Ops, for example as extremists) but by and large all are dedicated towards a tangible shared goal (as opposed to the other Orders' intangible ones). There is a strong sense of unity through shared adversity, at a level much more prevalent that in the other Orders. Like any gang - survive the initiation, and you have a family for life - indeed, until death. Others of your Order outside your cabal will often help not hinder - not a given in the Fallen World.

              Of courses then you have the 'pariah order' issue - many other mages are quite ambivalent (to say the least) about the self-appointed Mage police who have no scruples about being murderous if the situation is severe enough (and it has to be very severe to warrant losing a precious awakened individual, not to mention the wisdom issues - many have an overly trigger-happy impression of the GoV), and outright hostility is not uncommon. This isn't something which has overtly come up in TMCA, apart from Knave's warnings to Phil about Cowl, partly because the under-siege feeling of the Diamond Orders in Las Vegas blurs the emnity towards the spies-and-enforcers-and-intelligencegathering Order and lowers tolerances for flashy but also due to the fact of the GoV pact with the corrupt and overbearing Hierarch to give them free reign in the City.

              It's a bit like playing as an intelligent Bud White out of LA Confidential - the Orders are the Mob, the GoV the corrupt police department, the Seers the city's equally corrupt officials and politicians and high society on the other side: the Guardians are the thin line that keeps the mob in line just enough to avoid a crackdown... while still taking payoffs.

              So, I find that playing a Guardian can be richly rewarding - and you even get to slip on the mirrorshades from time, too.
              I've found that to be a pretty useful outlook, and I really like playing Guardians.

                  Everything Guardians of the Veil do is intended to protect Magic (and by extension Mages) from corruption. Ideally, their philosophies and methods should facilitate that. If you're not familiar with it, the Trolley Problem implies some of their core philosophy. Let's say a powerful God of Disease is about to wake and bring about a national pandemic. All other means of stopping it failed, and the only option left is to perform a human sacrifice on an innocent. At this point the math seems clear - it's one life against many. However, even with such high stakes, killing an innocent isn't something most people are capable of doing. If someone goes through with the deed, they will likely struggle with guilt, or accept their actions as right. The former means a decent person is now living in misery. The latter is actually worse, since that's what Wisdom degeneration is like. Mages tend to fixate on things and in some cases, the killer might want to apply this math outside of grave circumstances. The third option - heroic self-sacrifice, isn't acceptable either, because it means a genuinely good Mage is dead. This is where Guardians come in. The responsibility of the Order is to take over, because when it comes to "Sins for a Good Cause" they're mentally prepared and they know what they're doing. This way they can both protect the world and keep other Mages from getting messed up.

                  Of course, their work is not limited to that. A properly functioning Order constantly gathers intelligence to know if such a Disease God exists. If it does, they keep it secret so Supernal and Fallen meddlers don't poke at it. If they can't, they employ trade-craft and infiltration to keep an eye out on factors that could wake it. In case that fails, they maintain a roster of acceptable sacrifices. Better to use someone in coma than a person living their life to the fullest. They also teach other Mages the ethical use of Magic. "Did you hear about that fiasco in Madrid? Some Mage summoned Lord of the Flies without their Order's supervision. What a dumb-ass, am I right?" "Do you want to learn the art of Prophecy from Disease Spirits? Sure, you need a quarantined area stocked with antibiotics, a collection of Banes and a Thyrsus on staff. Now that I mention it, did you know Pythia from the Silver Ladder teaches both healing and divination Rotes to those who help with disaster relief? I could give you her number."

                  More than that, Guardians are constantly working for the benefit of the community. They direct fresh Awakenings to the other Orders (only the other Orders). They keep an eye out for Seers and other threats, ready to ring a warning when it matters. They're always willing to help out with magical disasters, since it not only maintains the Veil, but also nets them goodwill they desperately need. They're not just spies and assassins, but also trouble-shooters, preachers, psychologists and parole officers. More than that, to prevent a sense of entitlement, Guardian training drills into them that yes, they are horrible people, and they have to work hard to be the right kind of horrible. Having a well-trained Guardian in your Cabal is having someone who wants you to succeed in life and be the best person you can possibly be.

                              Those are great Guardian concepts.

                              It's interesting, the flexibility offered by the Order.

                              You can be the voice of reason and humanity kindly warning mages away from a dangerous path.

                                  The third one is actually a problem the Guardians have recognized, hence their rule of not dicking over your cabal. Guardians are expected to employ spy-craft on the regular, but not against cabal members. Basically, the level of loyalty prioritizes cabal over Order, and even in cases where Guardians stepping in might be entirely warranted the Guardian member of the cabal isn't expected to act against his cabal personally. They can alert the Order that something needs investigating, but they're not expected to give details or aid to the Guardians involved in the investigation unless things get really grimdark.

                                  The same code also kicks in for the fourth example really.

                                  Guardians consider this policy, and the fact that they keep an eye out for Awakenings and helpfully direct new Mages to other Orders that they'll be comfortable in, to be the best way to avoid becoming so hated that they're wiped out.
