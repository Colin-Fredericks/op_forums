                Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post

                I'm going to point out that the rules state that the severity of a Paradox Condition is supposed to be based on the number of successes left over after the Wisdom roll, so I think that if a person is left with almost four times as many successes as what qualifies as exceptional and the Storyteller doesn't make that something that they feel heavily, that Storyteller really kind of went out of their way to softball the whole thing for them.

                I feel comfortable saying that the rules as presented are the kind of thing where rolling a Paradox dice pool of over fifty would be effectively disincentivized anyway.
                Ok,
                The severity of the Paradox Condition is based on the number of remaining successes gained on the Paradox roll.
                This is mechanically represented by the paradox conditions, and mostly involves attracting Abyssal entities and generating more paradox if you cast more spells...

                I can't remember if I used an Abyssal Imago or Abyssal backlash, but neither have any reference to exceptional successes.

                For Imago, with 19 successes you can't resolve it "the good way" as casting a spell with 19 additional reach and not risking a paradox would be impossible.

                So you are forced to risk (a lot of) paradox if you cast anything or wait until it lapsed.

                For backlash, an extra 19 dive to the paradox pool (if you risk one) is likewise rather risky, but you can cast spells so long as you avoid any paradox risk.

                If you are just ignoring paradox and happen to he playing a low wisdom character (such that containing can only do a small number of bashing damage each time) then you may still be limited in combat scenes, and that is the main drawback RAW.

                If I had a player actually try to push the limits i would get more creative with my paradox conditions, and have "attracting the attention of an Annunkai" eventually lead to things like the creation of an Abyssal verge, and Abyssal Nimbus with open conditions leading to possession by various Abyssal entities.

                But since my players avoided all paradox all the time, and this one simply avoiding casting anything for the duration of the paradox condition, I found that satisfactory.

                Basically from what I can tell, paradox only gets bad when you create paradox anomolies. Creating an Abyssal Environment tilt, or summoning an Abyssal entity of rank [Reach -3] is pretty serious, and having a -19 to your dice pool for the spell casting almost guarentees that the spell will fail, so in a last ditch effort to cast a spell without being able to contain it, you are guarenteed to fail.

                That makes the after always incentivised to contain paradoxes, even when it kills the character (unless they are explicitly trying to create a paradox anomoly...)

                So unfortunately paradox RAW lacks teeth IMO. Especially for low wisdom characters (who are unlikely to take much damage from attempting to contain a paradox).

                Infact, if I wanted to encourage players to release paradox, I would likely reverse the dice penalty on spellcasting and turn it into a dice bonus.

                Allowing the player extra power (but also messing with the outcome of the spell).

That's intentional and encourages characters who don't have the Wisdom to fully contain a Paradox to instead release it and burn Mana on noise-reduction.

                                    Originally posted by Horodrigo View Post
                                    If a member of the cult awakened, it would need to be a Seer or it would have the free will to choose another Order?
                                    Satchel has answered the point on whether the cult named "Chosen of Mammon" from the blue book has anything at all to do with Seers of the Throne, but even for those cases where one does Awaken while a member of a Seer Mystery Cult, they have no less free will than any other mage.

                                    That said, they're realistically going to be likely to be noticed first by Seers of the Throne (who may very well throw off their attempt to Awaken in the first place, if seen early enough and deemed unworthy), and as a group the Seers don't offer a choice to any Awakened who encounter them first.

                                    (An idea people have often seemed to strangely overlook when seeking an answer to the question of "why would people join the Seers". For most of them, being a Seer sucks. It's paranoia and uncertainty, being beholden to commands that might encompass every level of your life, and having little real opportunity to advance in the ranks, not to mention likely having to do things that seem messed up to you. There are material benefits, but those are less of a salve after a while. But the Order has plenty of ways to sweep up the newly Awakened before anybody in the Pentacle can get to them, and they're hardly offering an opportunity to join the other side, nor likely to give an entirely honest image of what they're about.)

                                                            Originally posted by Horodrigo View Post
                                                            Does Shadow Names can appear radically different from a human on the Astral?
                                                            If one has the Shadow Name Merit, yes. The core book describes how a person like that can appear radically different in their dream form, although possibly still within certain boundaries of humanity.

                                                            Originally posted by Horodrigo
                                                            If yes, is it possible for a mage, using legacies, Life Arcana or other means, to permanently change her physical appearance to something more mystical, like something closely resembling her Shadow Name?

                                                            Use the Shadow Name "Medusa, the Snake Haired Maiden" as an example. It would be too difficult to actually create a functional living snake hair?
                                                            Some Legacies apparently do still come with some physical alterations. The Daksha, for instance, with their third eyes in the back of their heads. But otherwise, the Life Arcanum is still going to be subject to the usual standards of limitations of time and what can be witnessed by Sleepers, and something like growing snakes from one's head is going to be part of that. Under normal circumstances, the Shadow Name is not sacrosanct under that.

                                                            However, I personally like the premise introduced in Signs of Sorcery that there can occasionally be yantras that provide benefits other than the usual dice bonuses, and could accommodate the idea that a mage might uncover something rare and obscure that can make the likes of certain Life magic Lasting in a way that it wouldn't otherwise be.

                                                            Plus, there's always the option of Mysteries that might enhance one in a way a bit beyond the scope of usual spellcasting. It could be something that enhances the capacity to cast like the runewalking of London, or a place with its own capacity for transformation. An Emanation Realm in particular could possess the properties that one requires; go in pursuit of some place embodying Metamorphosis or the like.
