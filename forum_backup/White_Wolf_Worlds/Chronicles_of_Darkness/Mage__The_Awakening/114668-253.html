    Originally posted by Unahim View Post
    Why not simply make a reverse version of Warding gesture, then?
    Because that is, in practice, just casting Warding Gesture on the area in the Frog Room interpretation.

    Neither of those things make any difference for the current discussion.
    Yes. It was all I could find. I shared those because I could not find anything else and wondered if there had been some confusion of those statements, as often happens in discussions of this sort.

    Most of the rest is you just saying "But, if it works like this then [thing I like to play out in Y way] does not actually [play out in Y way]." Yes... ok? That's not an argument for how it is, only for how you want it to be. There's house rules for that.
    That there are house rules for that does not change the fact that I'm pretty sure most people aren't going to think Area Scale works like the reverse of Ghost Gate's Reach effect for every spell without being told that's how it works. Awakened magic as presented sticks to its subjects in a way that connects back to the mage through their Nimbus, and Area Scale automatically applies to the default Range factor of a spell cast on anything you're not directly touching — it's far from unintuitive to conceive of the effects of such a spell as being more like shrapnel or a paint balloon than like a gas grenade or an umbrella given all of that.

    Why? It'll only become more Mysterious, because you know that's not how it is supposed to work. The mystery! The intrigue! The excitement!
    The needless complication of a normal baseline quality of spellcasting! This is a basic facet of gameplay that does not benefit from lacking a clear reason for it to work the way it does.

    A spell landing in a discrete location and affecting a less limited but still finite number of targets for a set length of time is more generally applicable for spell effects than a spell landing in a discrete location and affecting a notionally unlimited number of targets for as long as they stay in that discrete location; targeting a room that holds a maximum of eighty-five people should not let you affect more than eighty-five people with a spell made to put people to sleep just because you changed one spell factor — that sounds more in line with a Reach effect or a different spell.

    It makes Area Scale both less expedient and less dangerous, which does not seem to jive with "the possibility of hitting people you didn't intend to by using a less strictly limited number of subjects is a feature, not a bug" outside of a very weird application that doesn't suggest long-term Area spells are ever used outside of traps or Sanctums by any but the supremely unWise.

    Or it could just be "some mage cast an area spell I guess", but I don't see that as a particularly involved story hook, so why mourn the fact that you actually have to add a bit of mystery and strangeness in there?
    Because the consequence is both louder and shorter-term than would normally be implied by "so hey we altered the nature of an entire location to have an always-on application of mystical pressure throughout its area" — pretty much nothing else in the setting has area-based powers that work as constant exertions rather than spaces in which the user has options they can exercise, which at the very least suggests that it shouldn't be the default function of area-based powers in Mage converted from non-area-based powers.

                                                        I think the intuitive bit depends on where a person comes at it from. Vancian magic has a completely different outlook than the magic system in Mage. If a person familiar with a specific spell-centric magic system deals with variable spell parameters then they keep the basic spell and the parameters' affects change based on the spell. The Mage system is centered on an effect and all the spell parameters/rules are absolute. Keeping the correct mindset for the system helps.

                                                        It is intuitive to consider a "turn into a frog" spell to be based on subject rather than area. However, aimed spells are always area, so then you can't have a ranged frog spell, it would be a zone of frog. A zone of frog is already counter-intuitive, so switch to something that is intuitively going to work with an area and back translate.

                                                        A spell that sets the temperature of a room would intuitively have an area scale. If a thermometer was in the area for the spell's duration and removed, the thermometer would adjust to the ambient temperature. At least, that would be the intuitive result. A thermometer brought into the area would quick as it could read the temperature of the room set by the spell. The spell effect is not lasting so intuitively this is how it works.

                                                        If we then switch this to a spell that turns people into frogs within the area, a person substitutes for a thermometer in the above example. Inside they are a frog, outside they are not. Lasting effects are permanent. A spell duration is only relevant as long as the magic is in effect and the magic is on the area rather than spread like some contagion onto things within the area.

                                                        Without familiarity with free-form effect-based power systems, this may not be intuitive, but that is a lack of understanding of how the system works. Mechanically, you can't take a spell that turns a person into a frog and have it affect an area, a person is not an area. Instead you make an area where people turn into frogs. If the area is what is affected then it doesn't matter if it is temperature control, complete silence, or people turning into frogs, you will always be limited to the magic only works within the area.

                                                        There are limits to the magic system. Attainments and archmastery are necessary to overcome the limits. The system doesn't support tossing a spell into a room that turns everyone into a frog for a month whether they stay in the room or not in the basic form of the system. This is good because free-form systems are complicated enough.

                                                        Honestly, I found it incredibly difficult to follow the supposedly intuitive way of looking at the magic because it didn't seem to fit the free-form power model. Going from subject to area means losing subjects as a focus.
