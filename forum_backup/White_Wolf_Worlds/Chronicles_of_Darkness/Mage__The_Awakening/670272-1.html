    Other TV pearl I found today...

    TV - Witches of East End ( 2013-2014 )

    Story about New England family Beauchamp cabal of four witches, each with her own take on magic, bound by curses of immortality. Character represent four Orders stances - mother Joanna is Mastigos Guardian, protecting her daughters and Mysteries. Aunt Wendy is Thyrsus Silver Ladder, wanting to free the magic, but also cultivate the traditions. Daughter Ingrid is Obrimos Mysterium, wanting to learn all she can about Mysteries. And Daughter Freya is Mastigos of Free Council, rebellious of family, going on her ways about Art.

                ERASED / Boku dake ga Inai Machi (2012-2016 manga, 2016 anime)

                Satoru, almost-30ty manga artist, is a man gifted with a weird ability. When something horrible happens in his vicinity, he is kicked back in time to the nearest point from which he could have intervened and stop it; he repeats the loop as long as needed for him to save the day. Satoru is also a man plagued with guilt; as a child, just like many others, he ignored ostracized girl in his classroom. This girl, 15 years ago, fell victim to a serial kidnapper and was murdered, just like 2 other kids from Satoru's school.
                While this looks at first like a prelude to a story about solving impossible situations, it's not really about that.

                After spooking a kidnapper by noticing his failed attempt at kidnapping a small girl, Satoru's mother start thinking about the case from 15 years ago. Then, she figures it out! But before she can share it with anyone, Satoru finds her murdered in his home. Desperate to save his mother, he begs for his power to kick in. And it does...


                ...transporting him 15 years back in time.

                While there is pretty much 0 supernatural or magic in the series, other than the central idea of main character being transported back in time, I feel this is a perfect CofD, and more importantly Mage, inspiration. It's pretty dark, touches upon uncomfortable subjects (while still having tact), and it's centered about very creepy mystery. Visuals are good, characters are good, music is good, and writing is good. Manga was fantastic, and while anime has to cut out some of the character scenes due to room limitations (anime is 12 episodes, manga was something about 50 chapters long), it does a superb work so far. I recommend it for everyone, even people not found of typical anime aesthetics and themes. Give it a try! It's a fantastic thriller, even if the central mystery is pretty easy to solve on your own :P.

                            Kristen Britain's Green Rider has a number of alternate Renaissance era scenes that could be adapted (particularly for the Mysterium and Adamantite Arrow). But the scene with the D'yer Wall strikes me as being a quintessential description of the magic of the Tamer of Stones Legacy.

                            Some quick background - generations ago, an evil mage twisted the neighboring forest into a place of Lovecraftian nightmares. So the D'yer family sponsored a bunch of stone mages to wall it off.

                            Jump ahead a thousand years of so. A new dark mage decides he's going to break the wall, and claim mastery over those dark horrors. So he casually sidles up to the D'yer Wall - unguarded for generations - and tries to blast it with his own magic. After all, he's the student of the Evil Overlord himself! Breaking a wall will be nothing for him!

                            Some time later, the dark mage comes to, sprawled out on the grass. And all he can do is stare in shock that humans could make such a thing. Abandoned for centuries, completely untended, and yet he can still hear them. The grunts of the workers, the ringing of hammers, the thud of the stones . . . all woven together into the spells and bound into every inch of the wall. Its been a thousand years, and he can still make out each and every voice, raised together in song. Raising a barrier that no evil could fly over, crawl under, or punch through. A barrier to stand until the end of time, to keep their families safe.

                                        The parts in Psychonauts where the protagonist Raz uses his psychic powers to enter the mindscape of some very eccentric people are excellent inspiration for stories focused on exploring Oneiroi. Also, one of the nicknames for the Mastigos in 2ED is Psychonauts, fittingly enough.

                                        Don't Rest Your Head has lots of inspiration for Paradox effects, Abyssals and the parts of the Astral where surreal and psychological horror reigns.

                                        The entirety of Eraserhead can be seen as the Mystery Play of a Mastigos. Oppressive industrial landscapes, nightmarish imagery fused with the mundane and an eerie and very sparse soundtrack all come together to give a sense of wanting to escape, to transgress. The fate of the deformed baby at the end can be seen as Spencer signing his name on the Watchtower of the Iron Gauntlet in a very gruesome way.
