            According to THIS DEVBLOG about the Orders:

            Originally posted by Dave Brookshaw
            Mage has always strained at the very early nWoD’s design principle of limiting the action to a single city (it makes no sense when 1/10th of starting characters can teleport.) and the Orders are global networks while simultaneously being local hierarchies that don’t answer to a central authority, except for the Seers. On the other side, the structures of the Orders described in the Order books describe factions with a lot of members – so many that if you sit down and try to figure out how many mages there should be in a single city according to all six books put together, you rapidly go over a hundred. So, as we’ve spoiled elsewhere, a single Order Caucus (for example a Guardian Epopt, all her Cultors, and the Labyrinths they’re running) now covers more than one Consilium. Even then, the “Guardians of the Veil” is made up of every one of those Caucuses, who are all independent of one another, and have the backing of ex-Guardian archmasters. Rather than explain that the Orders are Tier 2-3 subsets of Tier 3-4 networks, we’re not worrying about it. Which is why the word “Tier” does not appear in second edition. Mages visiting foreign Caucuses are like police officers out of jurisdiction – they aren’t part of the local politics, and the culture will have differences to what they’re used to, but they know the job.

                            I don't find it accurate to describe Orders as global conspiracies, because to me that conjures the image of a super secret board meeting of white men who meet occasionally in some underground bunker or executive room or similar to vaguely allude to their agenda and make sinister plans for every part of the globe. The Diamond Orders are structured more like world wide religions, in which various "local" hierarchies (quotes because, given what they need to make caucuses functional, they'll collect over whatever area they need to in order to have enough mages) that maintain communication with each other and occasionally hold international conferences to discuss collective issues. As I recall, the Free Council is being played up as more focused on the Assembly than any collective organisation, although many will take advantage of those conferences in order to gather information and network and occasionally coordinate big maneuvers. The Seers of the Throne try to take that global conspiracy form (at least donning the trappings of it) but the command hierarchies are convoluted, distributed, and multi-layered (like how basically any Seer might receive direct orders from the Exarchs, or at least think they have). The Ministers are almost a deconstruction of the typical Illuminati council, given that they're literally incoherent and their attendents need to try and interpret their pronouncements and translate them into working policies by feel. In short, the Orders exist globally, but you're not playing the ground level soldier in some distant scheme and the actual issues in your locality and Mysteries of your residence are at the forefront of most minds.

If they are prominent in a region, they could even have power structures different from Consilia and Assemblies.

                                        EDIT: Because of this, if you don't want to change the setting, but still give Nameless Orders a prominent place in your chronicle, you should think of reasons for these Nameless Orders to exist separately from both the Diamond Orders and the Council of Free Assemblies. If it would be possible to align their interests, becoming part of on of the global Orders has many benefits, and the Pentacle would certainly have tried to either recruit, reform or even destroy these Nameless in the past, depending on their philosophies. As Nameless Orders are relatively less powerful, the reason and way they resisted assimilation will be an important part of your setting, and possibly also a source of plot hooks.
