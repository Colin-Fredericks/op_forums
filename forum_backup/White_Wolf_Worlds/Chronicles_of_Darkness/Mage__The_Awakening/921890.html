    Dresden Files as MtAw game

    06-27-2016, 04:12 AM
    In new corebook, Dresden Files series is mentioned ( next to Constatine ) as main mages related inspiration. So let’s dissect stories and character for ideas on Mage game and CoD on general. Just let’s stick outside of outright spoilers – describing elements from particular book is okay – saying what happened to characters is not.

    I know that Dresden’s magic is based on emotions, not secret truths like one of Awakened – but similarities are more profound when you look over details. Here are some of them.
    • High Speech and Dresden’s ‘Babble Incantations’ – Even when used from different reasons ( Dresden do this to separates him from power, in Mage it’s ‘blueprint of universe’ ), end effect is the same – magic use is babbling to Sleepers ear. Awakened can even go with Dresden’s way of incanting random words from foreign languages. 8-)
    • Yantras/Instruments and Dresden’s Props – They really sound as the same. Just see Wikia description of Props in Dresdenverse:
    ‘Props
    Most magical practitioners use various props (physical objects) in the casting of their spells. It isn't actually necessary to do so. Any spell can be cast as an entirely mental construct, but the more complex a spell is, the more the practitioner has to divide his or her concentration.
    Using props reduces the amount of mental effort needed to cast a spell by providing a visible object which encapsulates the meaning that the practitioner assigns to it. In other words, a prop is a symbolic placeholder for a part of the spell in the practitioner's mind.‘

    Sounds a lot like Awakening’s Yantras, don’t it?
    • Consilium and White Council – On local level, they seem the same, i.e. Chicago’s White Council looks just like Consilium system – even Wardens works just as Sentinels in Mage. Difference is on the world wide level – but I could argue that World Wide Council looks like Global Convocations that Silver Ladder tries to enact from forever.
    • Quiescence – This effect is not called in Dresdenverse, but comic’s Welcome to the Jungle panel looks like best definition of phenomena for Mage:

      Now to the main characters from Storm Front in Dresden Files as they would be in Mage:
      • Harry Dresden – Obrimos Adamantine Arrow private investigator with chivalric bent. His Long Term Nimbus is corrupting electronics.
      • ‘Bob’ – Harry’s Familiar, anchored to scull in his Sanctum. He is with formidable power, so he is rather powerful ( as Rank 3 Familiar being ). In books he is Air Spirit with Influences in Knowledge. In TV series, he is fellow Ghost Mage.
      • Donald Morgan – Chicago’s Sentinel of local Consilium. Using his position as punishing tool sounds like Lex Magica Merit of Silver Ladder, even if character looks more as Adamantine Arrow. Still I cannot guess his Path…
      • Karrin Murphy – Mortal hunter that leads police ‘Special Investigations’ Devision. Interesting is that most of her subordinates do not acknowledge supernatural, so they are not hunters, only normal Sleepers. Case can be made that Murphy would be Sleepwalker.
      • Susan Rodrigez – Second mortal hunter, this time coming from journalist career, working for new age tabloid. Clearly Sleepwalker.

                      Originally posted by branford View Post
                      For those who are familiar with the Dresden Files, and I'm certain Dave and the Mage crew are fans since Harry Dresden is often an example of various Mage archetypes and issues (e.g., Harry is the quintessential low-Wisdom, yet "good," wizard), do you see the increased parallels with the DF universe now that sleepers and all manner of sleepwalkers and minor talents are tied to the inherent social fabric of the Mage setting?

                      In the Dresden Files, there are numerous supernaturally talented and informed human individuals and groups (and ample other supernatural bengs), but the White Council of Wizards are the veritable top of the magic food chain, who are responsible for keeping a lid on all the big magical cr@p in the setting, but are also terrifying to those whom they counsel and protect (usually because they have a habit of executing anyone alleged to broken the Rules and Magic, and due process and mercy are not priorities). In Mage 2e, particularly with their large and growing number of major and minor templates and various antagonists, I see the supernal mages of the Pentacle and Seers as the type of scary powerful people whom the nWOD mediums, psychokinetics, clairvoyants, dabblers in static magic and others, go to for help and protection or seeking power and knowledge, but otherwise know to stay the hell out of their way for their own safety, and make damn sure no true mage finds them in any way "mysterious."
                      Originally posted by Dave Brookshaw View Post
                      Yeah - as Harry is to the inhabitants of Mac's pub, an Adamantine Arrow mage is to assorted occultists.
                      What I exceptionally​ strike for Harry as Arrow is that Arrows can take Oaths as their Obsessions - basicly the missions to fullfil that grants Arcane Beats. Dresden is chivalry wizard that practition magic by helping others with they supernatural problems - if this is not Arrow archetype, I do not know what Arrows are then.

                      And let's not forget of his magic - Dresden Wikia on Harry's subject:

                      As one of the most powerful younger wizards of the White Council, Harry has a large repertoire of magical powers. His skills at evocation, or ‘quick and dirty’ magic, aka "kaboom magic", aka "battle magic", lean toward large blasts of fire and force, sometimes using wind, and also has some skill with earth (usually manipulating gravity) and lightning magic. He has considerable magical brute strength, though his control is initially somewhat lacking - for example, while he could easily shatter a pair of handcuffs, he may also break the wrists of the person wearing them. For this reason, Harry is ofttimes referred to as a magical 'thug'. Later in the series, Harry is also shown to have tremendous power in ritual form battle magic, when he uses the energy of a ley line to concentrate gravity for several miles around into a small zone, and flattens hundreds of enemy combatants at once.

                      And his Dedicated Magial Tool - Staff — A long staff used for channeling magic. Also works well as a bludgeoning weapon. In TV series, it changed to hockey stick, that looks for me as even more Arrow move.

                      His other favorite magical item - Shield bracelet — A bracelet with tiny medieval shield charms on it. Used for creating force fields. The bracelet began as a simple silver construction, but has evolved to feature a chain of multiple metals woven together and shields of different metals.

                      Yeah, Harry is Arrow mage - Maybe less 'warrior' but more 'detective', but still Arrow in the heart.

                      Add-On: There is also one thought that DaveB pointed in Open Development phase on Harry...

                      Originally posted by Dave Brookshaw View Post
                      Unless you drop off the end completely and become a Mad One (and how I dislike the name for them, for the wrong connotations it gives. Still, it's better than "Maurauder") I don't see low Wisdom as a punishment, more a reflection of how you're playing your character. If the character imagos first and thinks second, no matter how good his intentions, he should be low Wisdom.

                      Which is where my partially-quoted "Harry Dresden is Wisdom 3" comes from. Not a statement of fact - that'd be ludicrous, as he's a character from a different world who has his own roleplaying game - but a mission statement for myself and David Hill, who wrote Wisdom fresh from Humanity and Harmony. The corollory, of course, is that you can be an utter asshole and have Wisdom 10. Precise Evil is still evil, but your relative nice-ness is not something I want Wisdom to model.
                      Harry is example of 'Good Intentions, Low Wisdom' by design.

                                      Yes there is stuff later on that would push him towards fitting with the Arrows, but I still agree with your assessment. He's not interested in conflict or challenge at all. In fact he hates it.

                                      Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                      Harry is so Arrow it hurts.
                                      I don't agree. Harry's driving passions that fuel his fire magic are both his belief that the strong should never harm the weak, and his love for magic as a creative art form. Harry explains at one point how he most enjoys sitting next to his fireplace reading a good book with his pets, and doing magical experiments in his lab. He says that if that were his whole life, he would be content and satisfied. However, he nevertheless accepts the call to adventure because of his desire to safeguard the weak. Ultimately, Harry strives for peace. He may seek out conflict in the end, but he doesn't do it for conflict's sake.

                                      He doesn't believe in conflict as a virtue to be exalted, but as an unfortunate necessity that in a perfect world would be better-off eliminated. You can see this most in his views on how apprentices should be trained in shielding magic. He was trained by having baseballs thrown at him by his mentor. He rejects that idea as cruel and immoral and refuses to repeat the practice. Arrow philosophy would disagree entirely with this decision.

                                      You can see how he hates conflict when he withholds information from those around him to keep them from getting involved. An Arrow would be supportive of others who want to seek out conflict and become stronger. Harry is the opposite. Remember how he says "Chivalry is not dead"? He sees weakness and naivety as a virtue to be protected, not a flaw to be outgrown. He hopes that by taking the whole conflict on himself, he can prevent others from having to suffer as well. He would rather others remain weak and peaceful, than to allow them to suffer the conflict he hates so much - even if it means his undoing.

                                      He might fit in enough to be a member, but he'd promote heretical views. Ultimately he rejects the Arrows' core tenet that conflict is magic.

                                                      Now, mind you, and muddling the affair, this is assuming he's not actually a Goetic equivalent.

                                                      The classical element of air is associated with two major properties: "Moistness" and "Warmth" (think breath, not wind). Moistness represents formlessness; the ability to adapt and conform due to lack of strict shape and rigid structure. Warmth is the power of separation, which breaks apart mixed things and causes them to attract others of their kind, so that they become sorted by type (as with an alembic or an                                                       oil distillation tower). When you combine the two, you get that air is the element of formless intangible things, and has the ability to sort them into their component parts...or in other words, air is the element of analysis. Breaking intangible ideas and concepts down to understand them. Hence why Bob is described interchangeably as a spirit of air and a spirit of intellect.
