            The problem is, anyone can be a physicist or understand atoms. The game generally tends to be pretty bad about emphasizing that only mages can understand or interact with the Supernal, and even though there are other magical domains they can still ultimately understand them.

            And the problem if you rule "they're completely separate" is that it's somewhat inelegant. If there's no connection between the Arcadias, then that's a bit awkward. If you talk up how mages know all of this about the building blocks of reality and so on, and then say that "no, the other Magic is completely different", that's kind of an inelegant solution.

            The reason I like the "symbols" view is that it allows the Supernal to be connected to other forms of magic without subsuming them.

            And yes, you can make the argument that the most fundamental building block of something is the most important part of it.

            And about Emanations, you can describe that the same way you can rationalize summoning: 2e emphasizes that magic manipulates truths, so you can see bringing "Supernal" beings or phenomena into the world as using magic to add truth to symbols.

            To use another specific example, a Supernal entity that resembles a Fae isn't an actual Fae, but it isn't actually a being from Other Better Arcadia: it's the Mage using the ability of their magic to create "truth" to give substance to the ideas of narrative, capriciousness, and fate, which connects those with related thematic symbols and forms them into a shape that resembles other supernatural beings also informed by those ideals.

                                                    To put it another way: mages have insight into a particular way in which the world is built, and knowledge of that structure that allows them to rearrange it in certain ways, which we call spells and sorcery. This is the same for changelings, demons, and mummies. Changelings know how to broker deals with the voices of living phenomena; demons perceive occult complexities in ways they can exploit for reality-bending effects; mummies have an intimate understanding of the partitioned makeup of the human soul and how to draw on it for strength. Mages don't harness their own Pillars, or pledge Goblin Oaths, or use Embedded reality tricks with reflexive ease.

                                                    Because of the way their particular perspective privileges understanding and picking things apart, mages may be the best equipped out of the various templates to go afield and start trying to pick apart indirect ways of studying what separates the Ab from the Ba, or how to communicate with whatever it is that signs the Contract of Hours, or what it takes for a human being to exploit Unchained physics. But they don't just have a spell that gets them from zero to understanding. They just have good research tools. Other templates can do research, too.

                                                    All of it is part of a deep and complicated whole. All of it is relevant, somehow. Blind men crowd around an elephant.
