                                Originally posted by HardcoreHannes View Post
                                So how do you handle it if e.g. 3 turns into combat somebody casts honing the form on a character to bolster his past self with +3 strength. Do you roll all the combat rolls again?
                                Combat in our Mage games is extremely rare, and has only lasted longer than a single turn once ever. If that came up (it hasn't), the ST would handle it narratively. We don't reroll dice unless the scene itself gets rewound in its entirety. Generally speaking, for that kind of thing (seeking an advantage in an earlier situation) we just go with Shifting Sands.

                                In our group, Temporal Sympathy is mostly used for advancing the narrative, working around obstacles creatively, information gathering, etc. It enables a lot of really fun developments that are impossible without it.

                                At the climax of our last story arc, we saved the life of an NPC that was important to one of our allies by using a teamwork, ritually cast Temporal Stutter to throw them into the present from moments before they died - which was years ago. Cue trying to actually explain to the person we just saved what the hell just happened.

                                Like Charlaquin, I suppose I understand why it was changed, as I guess it would be problematic for other groups, but it was really, really amazing as-written for ours.

                                    Originally posted by MercSet View Post
                                    If you really wanted a spell casted back in time. Shifting sands time travel and cast it. It's actually fine, the errate just added an extra step, a limiter, and barrier for newbie mages to cross. Combined ShiftingSands+spell you want. This move creates less of a headache. no more throwing spells hundreds of years into the past. no more Killing your rival before they're born nonsense. At two you get basic pre/post-con, and three is the real fireworks show.
                                    The problem I have with the erattaed version isn't that you can no longer cast spells back in time. The problem is, all it does now is allow you to cast Momentary Flux on subjects in the past, cast Postcognition on subjects with weaker than Unchanged Temporal Sympathy, and Web-Weaver to bolster Sympathetic Links to things the subject has touched more than one turn ago.

                                    Every other spell that Temporal Sympathy can explicitly affect is a Time •• or higher spell, so there will never be a time when you are able to cast them and not be able to use Temporal Sympathy. There would functionally be no difference at all if they didn't require Temporal Sympathy and just made the additions a normal part of those spells. It'd shave a few words off the wordcount too, which is a bonus. Web-Weaver could still have that "Add Time ••" effect even if Time's Lesser Utility Attainment was something else, and again there would be no functional change to the spell. So in effect, the only thing post-errata Temporal Sympathy actually does is locks some of the functionality of Momentary Flux and Postcognition behind a paywall of "buy Time •• if you want the full effect." Honestly, I'd be fine with them just bumping those two spells up to Apprentice level if it meant having a Lesser Utility Attainment that actually did something.
