      If you can Imbue an item you can cast Word of Command, allowing you to max out the spell factors without actually affecting your dice pool.

      You can make the Imbued item trigger something that would happen as part of another action: e.g. imbue a weapon so that it casts a deleterious spell on whatever it strikes. Yes, this costs mana and won't always work (you still have to roll for the spell) but it is the only way to hit someone with a weapon and blast the with magic.

      The spell is more difficult to trace to you, even if you're the one that made the item.

      The "always on" mode can be useful for defensive effects.

                      A spell in an Imbued Item takes its usual casting time according to the factors the creator used when they made it, whether the spell is Contingent or not. The only way you can cast a spell reflexively through an Imbued Item is if the creator of the item used Reach to imbue one of the handful of spells that can be made reflexive via Reach (like Exceptional Luck or Suppress Life). A trigger still requires a user to trigger it, and the user must direct the item to cast, meaning they must take an action in accordance with the spell's casting time as though they were themselves casting the spell.

                      The benefits of an Imbued Item are: anyone can use it, even a Sleeper; it can act as a Mana battery, allowing you to use the item's Mana instead of your own to cast the spell or (if you're a mage) spend it to mitigate Paradox; with an exceptional success on the casting roll, you can relinquish the spell without a Willpower point/dot; if you use an Imbued Item that you didn't create, the spell you cast with it doesn't count toward your own spell control limit; casting with an Imbued Item means you don't leave your Nimbus around for others to find; and you can use Imbued Items to cast spells you wouldn't otherwise have the Arcana dots to cast, or cast them sympathetically even if you don't actually have the Attainments to do that, if the creator made them that way. Also, since the spell remains under the creator's control and when it's cast they can alter the spell factors and such, you could make one, give it to someone else, have them activate the spell while you're elsewhere, but still control how it's cast (provided you're in touch with that person or scrying on them or whatever so you know what you're doing). And if your item falls into the wrong hands, you can just cancel the spell or destroy the imbuement with Mana, whether you're present or not.

                      There's an expanded explanation of Imbued Items on p. 67 of Signs of Sorcery, if you want more detail.

                      Meghan Fitzgerald | Onyx Path freelance writer & developer
                      Changeling: The Lost developer
                      Mage: The Awakening developer

                                      It's the item that casts the spell (using the character's Gnosis, if any), not the character. The item begins casting the spell immediately after its trigger condition occurs to completion. It doesn't cast its spell simultaneously with the occurrence of its trigger condition unless the spell itself is reflexive via an established Reach option. It can't delay casting its spell after it's triggered (unless I suppose Flexible Reach allows the creator of the spell to shift from Instant to Ritual casting time).

                                      A main benefit of imbued items is enjoying persistent spell effects of Indefinite duration that didn't require a Willpower dot to relinquish (exception success) and don't consume a spell control slot. Imagine a Ring of Wards and Signs -- whoever wears it gets the benefit of that protection forever unless it's dispelled, and even then it can be reactivated by a Prime 1 spell. It's really good.

                                                      What you describe is reasonable if that's how you want to play but I don't think it's the most straightforward reading of the rules. Some snippets from core and SoS:

                                                      contingent (triggered by a word, gesture, or condition)
                                                      Contingent Items are triggered by a word, gesture, or condition
                                                      Any character that knows the trigger method may activate an Imbued Item, causing it to cast.
                                                      ​
                                                      I think the third quote there is the point of ambiguity. Does it mean, "Any character that knows the trigger method may [satisfy it to] activate an Imbued Item, causing it to cast." or does it mean "Any character that knows the trigger method may [satisfy it and then] activate an Imbued Item, causing it to cast."

                                                      For me, the use of the word "trigger" in the first two quotes implies that the satisfying the contingent condition/word/gesture/whatever is the trigger of the "gun" rather than its safety. Imagine a Sleepwalker using an imbued item. She satisfies the contingent condition and then has to do... something else to make the item cast? What would a Sleepwalker even be able to do in this arena beyond satisfying the trigger condition? Focus hard? Make a wish? They're magically impotent.

                                                      EDIT: To clarify, I see the user's "direction" of the imbued item's casting, like the contribution of her Gnosis to the dice pool, as involuntary. Once user triggers the item, she has no choice but to immediately decide spell factors and select a target if appropriate. I would rather have had all the details of casting, including the dice pool, pinned down as part of the creation of the item. Like I would want a Stick of Shooting Lightning to function exactly the same way upon every activation, regardless of who is activating it, and have the subject of the spell determined by an Aimed Spell roll. That's just how the Magical Item trope works in my mind.
