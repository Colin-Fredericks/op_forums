    2 questions

    1. Why is it that in mage society, the Wise are expected to join legacies? Awakening is a very personal experience, and mages are walking symbols of truth from what I understand, who all seek mysteries of their own and individualistically so (especially in regards to Ascention). And in a culture that encourages individualism, why would they be expected to join a group that explitly involves the process of (voluntarily) reforging their souls and seceding to a theory regarding where Truth can be found? While I can understand why it is not uncommon mages could find cliques they feel resonance with and that they agree with in an academic sense, it does surprise me it would be so common as to be an expectation because of how varied obsessions and the fallen world is

    2. Is Obsession linked to gnosis, being mystical in of itself? Or is the fact that you gain more Obsessions via higher gnosis simply an abstraction representing how the deeper insight into a mage's path leads them to more easily see the dragons tail and seek it out in multiple things?

            Because developing a new Legacy is more difficult than joining an existing one, each dot of Gnosis requires another breakthrough, and there's little point in reinventing the wheel just for the sake of complete personalization of your magical toolbox. Driven seekers after Truth though they are, most mages are not up to inventing a new magical philosophy at the stage in their spiritual development where they are expected to have aligned themselves with such a philosophy.

            Forging a Legacy is a task that requires you to actually undergo the process that forms the basis for initiation — we're talking about tasks and ordeals not unlike what changelings need to go through to found a Court or attain a kith, or what werewolves need to do to earn a couple dots of Renown. Pinning your Legacy's theory down in practice requires experiential support for its Mysteries, which requires finding Mysteries that mesh with that perspective.

            Also, pretenses of unworldliness notwithstanding, Legacy mentors have an incentive to get other mages to read their books and join their monthly meetings. The Diamond's jackdaw approach to magical praxis does not prevent its mentors from giving its students a nudge toward vaguely-compatible Legacies that have something to offer those mentors for the new recruits. Irreversible though the process typically is, a mage who joins a Legacy and disagrees with the orthodoxy always has the option to develop novel Attainments as they move forward.

                    Entirely my own interpretation, not RAW, but…
                    Based on the supernal being objective Truth, the constraints of the Fallen World being a major theme, and that many of the published legacies (especially 1E) have weird side effects and draw backs [epoch wraiths comes to mind most strongly], my head cannon is that not only is forming a legacy quite difficult, but it also forces the prospect to accept a Truth that can be deeply unsettling, inconvenient, or undermines some of their intentions (but not the entire philosophy).
                    Supernal truth, like natural law, is not necessarily moral - even without the Exarchs’ corruption - and making a legacy is not only about deciding what magic matters to you and developing a philosophy, but also discovering a primordial truth and become a living anchor for it.

                    For example: Tamers of the River are about mastering the nurturing and destructive powers of water - healing, flooding, and rain for crops, especially free from the Lie, are remarkable powers to channel in any agrarian society; anyone might want that. But it’s core Truth is impermanence, and it demands its adherence sacrifice their memories and attachments - which is sure something to balk at.
                    You can easily imagine the founder of this Legacy seeking out the nourishing powers of water to save their people, and seriously consider giving up when they realize they might have to sacrifice the very relationships they’re trying to protect.
                    Joining the legacy does not necessarily let you sidestep those drawbacks - but you can learn early on what they are, and they’ve since been fully integrated into the philosophies and trials and recruitment pitch. But the Founder? We can imagine these costs might have undermined their (secondary) goals, and totally blindsided them, after years of obsessive work.

                    So I’ve strongly emphasized that interpretation of how hard it is to found a legacy in my games - and I include several NPCs who tried to codify their own, but were not willing to accept the Truths they needed, and gave up instead - perhaps leaving notes for someone more willing to walk that path for them.

                    For example, my Player wanted to found a Legacy that was all about teaching, networking with other mages, and providing the information they needed to (safely) pursue their obsessions.
                    The Truth that had to accept was that learning happened when the student was ready; you can’t force enlightenment on another. As a consequence, they can’t just info-dump everything they *think* someone else should hear, or academically bully people into changing opinions. They have to act as an advisor and mentor, providing the information their student is ready and searching for, grimacing at the unasked questions and the “in one ear and out the other” moments when someone doesn’t think a particular fact is important.
                    It still lets them show up as a SME, provide key information to solve a puzzle, talk people off of a ledge, and have those “sweet summer child, you’ve never dealt with werewolves” moments that they were seeking, but curtailed enough to not fundamentally alter the motives (or hubris) of the mages they consulted, or attempt an Awakening gambit.

                    As a foil, I have a geomancer NPC that sought out the secrets of Neithian Architecture (Mummy) and Occult Matrixes (Demon) - but would have had to accept a Seer-like attitude of sewing suffering and exploiting Sleepers - so abandoned the nascent Legacy and destroyed its notes.

                                Originally posted by Primordial newcomer View Post
                                Those Orders find like-minded mages, yes, but other than the seers of the throne, they don't stick mages into tiers of subservience (well, to the extent of the throne) or reforge souls as legacies do. And the free council is a democracy of MANY differing opinions and individuals.
                                Literally the only form of imposed hierarchy in a Legacy is the need for initiation and the necessary imbalance of knowledge therein, and there is a world of difference between "mages tend to be individualistic and their societal structures are mostly about limiting the collateral damage of powerful people fucking with each other" and "Awakened culture encourages individualism."

                                You are reading more into the process of joining a Legacy than is necessarily implied at base. It's the postgrad specialization to Order training's undergrad education, and as a process that involves dealing with the world (most easily the parts of it that are conversational), you will typically need to navigate long-term human interaction to get that training and the damage substandard training could do to your mentor's reputation means they will usually be somewhat invested in ensuring you are trained properly if they agreed to train you.

                                The only subservience involved is that people will typically want compensation for taking that kind of risk, and service and favors tend to be an evergreen currency on that front — you're forging a sympathetic link as strong as that shared between the average parent and child, and as the one agreeing to the responsibility of imparting secret knowledge the mentor is the party who would be at a disadvantage if this was done for free. "Do as I advise while I'm teaching you so you don't embarrass me or worse" is an ask most mages who are not going full Megalomaniacal can agree to without considering their individuality compromised by the act of deference.

                                                            Originally posted by Aeneas View Post
                                                            Is it true that Prime cannot affect anything material?                                                             I find it a bit of an ambiguously phrased question, but if it's asking if it can only affect spells and mana, the answer is no.

                                                            For a start, you can cast Prime spells on people that expand their sensory range. Prime's purview of Truth is able to engage with physical subjects, in terms of being able to recognise that something is not innately what it appears to be (it can recognise things as mundane as dyed hair). It can affect suitable objects by converting them into grimoires or stores of mana, as well as being able to make them into something like imbued items on a limited basis. It can alter things suitable to function as yantras so spells cast using them get 9- or 8-again. It can render objects capable of interacting with things in Twilight. And yes, its attack spells can strike physical things.
