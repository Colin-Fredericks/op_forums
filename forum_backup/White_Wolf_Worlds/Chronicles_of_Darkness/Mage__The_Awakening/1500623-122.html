                        How the money will go away is entirely on the ST hands, but overall it's seen as a dick move towards those who buy Resources with dots to let a player get away with indefinite wealth for a large period of time.
                        I'm inclined to approach it in terms of the typical difference between having a lot of money because you have a sustainable income stream and having it because of some one-off circumstance.

                        People who suddenly receive several million dollars have a tendency to blow through it very quickly, because in the best case scenario there's something of high value that they're willing to indulge in without concern for the money in the long term, and in the worst (and most typical) they don't really have a good perspective on how far that money can go and suddenly escalate their spending in a manner that soaks it up at speed.

                        Otherwise you're trying to spend it sustainably over a lifetime, and like... spending a few million at a rate where it will last the rest of your life will maintain a comfortable middle class lifestyle (for the people who don't have a few million because they're being compensated for damages that entail costly medical care).

                        It's different sure if a player proposes that their character is using illicit means to rip off several million a month, but buying the Merit is probably how you represent them undergoing the process of making that money look legit, rather than being something that brings intense scrutiny on them even if it isn't judged to be illegal.

                        Otherwise, it's like a lot of illegal enterprises; sometimes you end up with a lot of money that just sits around on pallets waiting to be laundered or spent. Pablo Escobar lost about 10% of his money a year to rats, mould, and water damage.

                        (A person might do something like use their magic to bullshit their way into being a professional gambler who always wins at high stakes and is maybe able to distribute those winnings through multiple identities so that nobody refuses to deal them in or starts becoming suspicious that they cheat, but that in itself entails so much extra effort that even if that isn't folded into it being the Merit, it probably just isn't practical as something for most mages to care to deal with, and certainly more than should take up the time of a PC.)

                                        In terms of engagement with the Availability system, I think it's also simple enough to jury rig having money without Resources. It would just be something like having no option to buy anything above a certain rating, certain one-off purchases will essentially wipe out your spending power (you might have actual money left over, but it can be assumed to be dedicated towards more regular needs), and low rating purchases still ultimately have a limit on them.

                                        None of this need be broken down to specific numbers, since some of it is probably assuming that money is still also being spent on some basic regularities. Resources gives you recourse to buy things beyond your character's expected means by virtue of credit, debt, and equity, a bit of money you pulled out of a one-off action does not.

                                                I think one important distinction to make here is that one off vs consistent earnings (big win vs having Resources) is not a distinction of legality.

                                                A Mafia Boss, or a Seer running embezzlement schemes, are not one-offing their way to riches, they effectively have Resources.

                                                The point isn't just having a salary, but having a system in place, whomever put it there, making the flow of money somewhat reliable. That's what organized crime do, what companies do, etc.

                                                But such systems also have costs, which is why the one-off rarely is sustainable. This is something most players gloss over.

                                                Let's say you got your big win and now want to use it to open a storefront, so you make it into stable funds. You go to NYC to open a grocery store, mid-tier nothing too fancy, where you can also discreetly hide some magical works.

                                                Going for Lower Manhattan or SoHo, staying the hell away of Fifth Avenue, your opening cost alone can hit US $ 400.000 between rent, utilities, renovations, staff, etc, take 9 months to open and still this covers the first 6 months only. You'll need extra capital for emergencies and to stay open during the build up of traffic, which means more rent (100k to 200k every 6 months), maintenance, wages, taxes, utilities, insurance... And it can take a couple years for a successful business to start to really sustain itself and generate revenue. Be prepared for 2 solid years feeling that you're the poorest member of staff.

                                                That'll easily lock away 1 Million dollars even if some of this money remains at the "end" of the process, and even then things can go wrong in several ways that you can't control. Wanna just live off interest, instead? Contrary to movies, a well maintained investment won't reliably give you more than 10% a year above inflation. Any bigger wins are due to volatility, not acumen, and are offset by equally uncontrollable risks of loss.

                                                Want to personally control things to guarantee success in game? Then be prepared to spend a lot of time in-character making money rather than engaging the actual game.

                                                In the end, the point is that having things for an extended period of time is a completely 'nother beast than buying something once. A house or vehicle will have maintenance, stable revenue is a long-term, high investment process, and if you can't delegate this it'll also take your time.

                                                You know, as in there's a reason why the great real financial professionals in the market work for wages and fees, not from investments. The ones who do were rich to begin with.

                                                A character that one-off a big sum isn't fated to loose it only because they don't know how to make it last. They'll loose it because making it last is complex and rarely within their interests. It takes time and requires you to give up the big sum right away just to start reaping benefits sometime in the future.

                                                        Originally posted by quelsec View Post
                                                        So whats the point of investing in resourcess again? A spell once or twice a sesion and you have all the resourcess you need.
                                                        You don't need to cast the spell, you have consistent access to the benefits of that Merit, and you know where the money is coming from. Shifting the Odds being Duration-primary means you have to take penalties to the casting roll to get more than one use of the Merit at a rating of more than one dot.
                                                        Also if its lasting why does shifting the odds have a primary factor of duration?
                                                        It isn't. Shifting the Odds can give you temporary access to Social Merits, which go away after you've used them [Potency] times unless you spend Experiences to purchase the Merit.

                                                        Also, it has Duration as its primary factor because the base effect (which doesn't scale off of Potency at all) brings you to what you're looking for within 24 hours as long as the spell is active, which requires it to last for more than one scene in most contexts. That doesn't mean the corrupt cop you find by Shifting the Odds doesn't remember you when you next meet them after the spell's Duration ends, because the Lasting effects of Shifting the Odds are that you met the person from the category you were trying to meet or made use of the Social Merit you had temporary access to.
