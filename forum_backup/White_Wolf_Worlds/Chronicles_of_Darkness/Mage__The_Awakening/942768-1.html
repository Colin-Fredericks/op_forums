                        I disagree completely with this statement. The beat system is one of the best things to come out of the GMC. I can't think of any other mechanic more relevant to set the tone for a CoD game than beats. Sure, you may ignore them if you want, and in some circumstances I do so myself. However, whenever I do include them, the game feels more fun and focused, players stray less from the plot and when they do, they do so in dramatically interesting directions. It is hands down one of the best Experience systems I've seen (which isn't strange at all, since most XP systems are slowly moving in this direction, compare Dungeon World which is my seminal example for a lot of things).

                        By the way, the Beats system is not really about character advancement. It kinda is, but that's not its main use. Using it just for that is a waste of its potential. Is it the perfect system? Nah, it just beats (pun intended) any other system that I've seen so far. Feel free to pose a counter-example, I would like to learn if anything better exists, honestly.

                            The same community that largely supports dropping dramatic tension to exploit a loophole in the rules to grant a character in game resources also largely rejects the exploitation of a similar loophole to grant a character experience points (a meta game resource).

                            In short most people want their characters topped off at the expense of dramatic tension, but no one wants to see characters freely advance in power without participating in the story.
                            I must admit, that has not been my impression of those threads. While I did get the feel of most people being disinclined to change the rules on the praxis-acquisition of willpower and mana, I did notice that most, if not all, were against this being exploited as a loophole, i.e. against a Mage spamming pretty lights to get mana, as it were.
                            Rather the system was not seen as problematic ( not everyone agrees, I know XD ) due to insistence on in-character behavior that goes on with the plot and story and mood/theme rather than the proposed "Gimme a few minutes, gotta drain some luck into Mana" notion, meaning the system is fine as long as the exceptional successes are allowed to happen organically, from casting your praxis with at the very least a reasonable excuse. Outside of actual combat, you can get Mana in other reasonably convenient ways, so it's not too much of a drop in dramatic tension.

                            As far as beats go, it is so far my favourite XP mechanic because it not only allows player agency in XP-acquisition in a narrative way, it also rewards story progression. All of the beat gains are things that are character development moments, if you choose to take them as such (which I do, ymmv) - a horrible failure that can stay with you for a while (dramatic failure), taking yet another step towards the megalomaniacal mage personality (Wisdom risk), experiencing the high of excellent spellcasting (exceptional success), these are all moments that can build a character.
                            Doing one of those things over and over in the same way is not unlike someone developing an addiction to a feeling of heightened reality, what can be described as "really being alive" or "feeling something," if the character is in a bad way.

                            But I digress too much, for I have a Beat Engine to propose! (before I get into my convoluted one, a simple one is the condition sequence of Addicted -> Deprived, then resolving Deprived)

                            What Doesn't Break You Makes You Stronger
                            (and sometimes uses puns)

                            In a never-talked-about area of the Adamantine Arrow compound there is a room with a non-descript door, within it lie two boxes, each an on altar, on opposite sides of the room.
                            The room has no other entrances or exits, no windows. The boxes are illuminated with candles, providing the only light in the room, their surfaces large enough to place a hand on with fingers spread out.
                            The box on the left side appears to be made of steel and inlaid with gold decorations and script, bearing depictions of double-edged swords, and the word BREAK.
                            The box on the right apperas to be made of stone, inlaid with copper decorations and script, bearing depictions of axes, and the word RENEW.
                            The walls are decorated with tapestries depicting Mages triumphant in a bloodied state.

                            The room sees no use in times of conflict, when the Arrows are tempered through battle. But when times of peace come, the Adamantine Arrow knows the need to stay ready.To train, yes, but a keen awareness exists that sparring matches, or indeed anything, cannot fully prepare one for true battle.
                            Nothing can do it fully, but you can get closer. In times of prolonged peace, Arrows who have never experienced battle, but will be expected to take up arms when the need arises - never quite newcomers, but those with some measure of growth shown - are ushered into the room with the non-descript door in the place nobody talks about.
                            They are instructed to find strength in overcoming pain.
                            The screams and shouting from the room are never commented upon by those standing guard outside, and those exiting do so with greater understanding.


                            Mechanics!
                            Each of the boxes is an imbued item. They are both triggered by placing one's hand on the surface of the box. The left box contains a Forces effect that inflicts the Disabled Condition by breaking both of the target's legs. With all the pain that involves.
                            The initiate then has to make their way across the room (by using their hands for movement) to trigger the second box.
                            The right box contains a Life effect that heals some damage and cures the Disabled Condition by unbreaking the legs, as it were.
                            There we go, Condition, resolved, take a Beat.


                            Originally posted by Servanous View Post
                            As a final note the best experience engine is to attempt a roll with a poorly know skill or spell once per scene and volunteer a critical failure. That’s the “beat engine” that I would actually use.
                            That's a great one.

                            Of course, if you really want into optimisation, and I don't see why not as long as you also make it interesting in-character behavior that's fun for the group, make use of eeeeverything that gives beats.
                            In every scene, get and resolve a condition (or 'take a beat from exceptional success' if the ST rules that way), dramatically fail on a roll in a dramatic way (that will get you a reputation ), risk Wisdom and portray the subsequent introspection from that, and try to make some progress on an Obsession whenever remotely possible.
