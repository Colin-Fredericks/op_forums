    [Homebrew brainstorming help] Magic Styles in 2e

    02-04-2020, 01:18 PM
    In my free time from working on my thesis recently I've been thinking about Mage, both for my forever-in-planning quest on SV and for when I can run it again for my tabletop group.

    The whole idea for this comes from the fact that for me the guide to Magic Styles given in the Chroniclers Guide is my favorite way to inject character and, well, style, into Awakening's spellcasting. Indeed it's what I used in my NY Chronicle combined with the Rote Factors from Tome of the Mysteries (and also the variants for High Speech in that book), to give extra worth and character to rote spells, making them worth the exp investment IMO. On the other hand, while I found the magic styles incredibly flavorful I wasn't to be truthful completely satisfied with the mechanics offered for their implementation in the Chronicler's Guide.

    Enter Awakening 2nd edition and it's Yantras[1]. "Equipment bonuses for spellcasting". Quite apart from the fact that I find it much easier to use mechanically, it allows for more variety in casting style compared to the more simple one mage-one style solution of the Chronicler's guide, which says that while picking up more than one style is technically possible it's not expected or assumed as the norm and it's rather hard. The mix and match of occult styles and trappings fits more in my mind with Awakening's magic.

    However, and I admit this may be a misjudgment, I feel that the Yantras are still kind of flavorless.

    Now, I admit I say this without having yet played or run a game using 2nd edition's Spellcasting system, but knowing my players and how they used to cast spells before I introduced magic styles, and having watched some episodes of the ongoing Mage AP streamed on Onyx Path's Twitch, as written it seems to me that the common occurrence is a rather dry "yeah I use High Speech for a +2 and another whatever Yantra for a +1" with no further elaboration beyond the minimum required to keep the Yantra relevant to the spell.

    Having said all of this I'm not that good at homebrew (terrible actually), and would really welcome any help fleshing out the half-formed thoughts I have about this.

    So in no particular order, some of my random thoughts on bringing Style in 2nd edition:
    • Each Style should be a short descriptor (Autohypnosis, Angel Channeler, Mystical fashion stylist) which should guide the types of Yantras used in spellcasting.
    • Not sure if Styles should have a dot rating or simply be a "tag" for magic, and if they do have a rating should it be up to 3 or 5? My thought is 3 but I waver on it.
    • How many Styles should a starting character have? My guess is 3, one inspired by Path, one by Order and one by the character's mentor/background/concept/whatever. Sometimes I think it's too many though, and 1 is better as a starting point.
    • Praxis spells when learned are associated with one of the Styles the character knows. If you want to get the exceptional success advantage you have to cast your spell in-style.
    • Rotes also have a "Style tag" indicating the association of Fallen symbolism used by the rote creator. If you cast the spell "in-style" beyond adding the skill to the dice pool you also get another benefit, but I'm unsure if it should be an extra +1 Reach or a +1 to the primary factor, more similar to the TotM rote factors.
    • Related to that, a though the perhaps a Style should have associated Skills that make sense as rote skills to guide the creation, but honestly, rote creation is something that's is only a concern for Arcana Masters and thus not really important for the majority of the life of a player character, unless the player just beelines for it I guess.

    Those are just some musing I wanted to get out of my head recently, I may have more coming soon.

    Is it a terrible idea? A decent one? An absolutely awful one?

    [1] I tend to call them simply Foci in my head and notes because I prefer it as a term but for clarity's sake, I'm going to use only the core term here.

          Its not a bad idea, but I feel what you are looking for is already there. In first edition, you had the Magical Traditions book focusing on that. While it was cool flavor wise, mechanically it wasn't incentivized.

          In Ascension, a Tradition's style was a ton of fun, but it also demanded a lot of work to fit what you wanted to do into you character's interpretation of the paradigm. Yantras allow for more field improvisation.

          So lets say a case where the character wants to make a portal, they choose to use "High Speech and a whatever +1 yantra". Sure, mechanically it works, but they didn't do anything interesting with the yantras. So they could take out a key, it half vanishes in midair, the caster twists it and you see the portal open, like a door no one had noticed was there.

          Another mage could take out a knife and quite literally cut the portal into midair. Yet another will run an algorithm on his smartphone, making spatial calculations leap out of the screen and open the portal. Another uses a nearby lake, they visualize the destination within the water's surface, a drop floats up from the lake and grows into the portal.

          When combat or subtlety are needed, these descriptions may be less used to focus on the problem at hand. But a character's style is the collection of clothes, items and resources they carry to suit their preferences. Some perhaps carved of rare materials, from trophies, experiments, etc.

          Lets look at the proposed changes in order:

          * The style guided yantras sound like Legacy yantras, which reward you by focusing on your character's specialty

          * Styles having a dot rating is implemented by the Shadow Name merit. By sticking to that style's symbolism, they gain a powerful spellcasting bonus. If they have a powerful one, the Shadow Self merit gives additional bonuses.

          * Path styles are reflected by Path specific tools, Order styles are reflected by Order tools yantras and concept style is covered by Legacies (if they make their own, it is their character's concept made into a style)

          * Lets say you are a chef, you can cook from a humble burger to levre à la royale. A praxis is a dish you have cooked so often that you can do it perfectly on autopilot. Its an emergent property of the character's style.

          * A Rote's style is the skill mudra encoded into it. So when you make your own, you are personalizing it to your style. Its why you get the best results (Rote property) faster than when you cast it from a step by step manual (ritual casting from a grimmoire).

          * +1 to the primary factor can already be attained by using the dice the Rote's skill mudra. If you add the +1 Reach, it can really raise the power curve of your campaign. For example, before you could instantly attack a foe with a Making of Death had to use Reach for instant cast, the caster had to get in close range, then roll for touch attack. Otherwise, they would have to use aimed spells for ranged specialists. Reaching for anything more risked Paradox. Now they can do it at sensory range. Which means players and NPCs would gravitate towards them and create a heavy XP sink.

          I think the players you described were handwaving a bit the need to add symbolism to their spells. But to me yantras always felt like a mechanical incentive to make your spells flavorful.

          New experiences are the font of creativity, when seeking inspiration, break your routine.

          The Szary Strażnik, an Obrimos Legacy whose invisible hands guide through the Glyphs of Fate (Fate/Prime)

                              So here are more thoughts on Magical Styles for 2e:
                              • I have to thank again Satchel and Kaiser for reminding me of the Shadow name merit, I have yet to internalize a lot of 2e Awakening like I did 1e so the reminder saved me from going in circles. Thank you.
                              • On that note, I think I will use the Shadow Name merit as a base for Style, since as Satchel mentioned it's where a lot of the idea of "spellcasting style" is in 2e. I think that its privacy benefits (for lack of a better term) will go to a re-written Occultation.
                              • I personally feel that Path tools and Order tools aren't any more a "magical style" by themselves then they were in 1e, which is why all the Order books had occult symbols and correspondences that were common to each and the whole of the Tome of the Watchtowers had the same for Paths.
                              • Indeed I think I'm gonna re-read those books and use them as sources for Style-bound Yantras, but I'm getting ahead of myself.
                              • Legacies are tricky because I honestly haven't yet made up my mind of how much of their mechanics I like and how much I don't. The 5 attainments was something that I already had done at my table during 1e because it allowed both for more flavourful abilities as well as more power and utility, because a lot of 1e Legacies except a few standouts had either boring or weak attainments, at least to my reckoning. Still I think (and the Chronicler's guide does as well) that Legacies are indeed an expression of style, but different than a regular Mage's Occult Praxis (which is the term I want to use for the combination of a Mage's Styles) since it's built around a strong belief in a certain worldview or goal, which the Mage shapes their very soul around.
                              • Saying that a Rote's style is just the encoded skill is as undescriptive now as it was in 1e, because I personally think that Skills (and skills+attributes in 1e) in CofD cover such a great variety of things each that saying that a rote for say uses the Occult skill doesn't actually say what kind of mystical trapping out of hundreds it uses. This is why I also really liked about TotM Rote factors.

                              I think I'm kind of starting to get a feel for what I want to do exactly, I think that I can start to make some kind of 1st draft once I've re-read the relevant 1e books and I'm done with writing my thesis.
                              I know that this personal hack may not be your cup of tea, but still thanks for engaging with me on this Satchel and Kaiser.
