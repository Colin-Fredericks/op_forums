    It's not cartoonish. The book itself says that a character acquires a praxis in-universe by casting it over and over again. It should be perfectly natural for a mage to continue doing this on a regular basis. Furthermore, mages absolutely CAN interact with exceptional successes. Exceptional successes are an aspect of Fate. Any mage with Fate can observe, study, and manipulate them. Even without Fate, a mage can tell that they've gained mana from casting the spell, and connect the dots. Therefore, there's no reason why a mage shouldn't be able to study exceptional successes in-depth and determine that they're an untapped source of mana.

    If you were talking about a Storyteller saying "No you can't just blow up a city block for no reason. Come up with a better character motivation", then I'd absolutely agree with you. However, in this case, praxis spam constitutes a completely valid mystery to study. The problem that it breaks canon and ruins the mana economy is the fault of the system, not of the player who found a perfectly valid in-character goal. That's why I say it should be houseruled.

                                            As a ST, I would use the situation to define what differentiates an exceptional success from a regular success.

                                            Much like the way one of those other systems use the concept of Taking 10 or Taking 20, if there is no possibility of negative consequences, then it is just a normal success.

                                            Casting a spell over and over, in a multitude of situations and conditions allows a mage to master the spell, and thus allows it to become a praxis. This makes is easier for the mage to gain an exceptional success when casting this spell.

                                            From a mechanical point, limiting exceptional successes to actions that pose the potential for bad things to happen to you if you fail allows for a consistent rule that players can follow while also keeping its value for bonuses when they count. If failure doesn't count in the practice room where you are practicing, then exceptional successes don't count either.

                                            From a narrative or thematic point, limiting exceptional successes to spells cast "In Play" (I don't count the practice spam room as in play) maintains the tension between risk and reward.

                                            I do not agree that an exceptional success is a thing within the game universe, or that it is something that can be monitored, managed, and replicated using Fate. Fate in my mind can be used to increase your odds to such a degree that it is more likely that your mage will achieve spectacular results. But unless your mage is actually rolling dice that truly determine the outcome of his actions, the concept of an exceptional success is purely mechanical and removed from the view and manipulation of the character.

                                            So this means praxis spaming will not be a thing in my games. Any attempts at such will meet with Mr. Paradox (Cousin to Mr. Murphy) in some way, shape, form, or fashion.

                                            For anyone asking, or even demanding, an in-universe explanation, I would answer that Fate is fickle, unpredictable, and inscrutable. Even for mages. At least those below 8 or so dots in the Fate arcana.

                                                            It still feels like you're overstating the "in-universe" knowledge being presented. Fate Sight lets you see people in the middle of extremely poor, or extremely good, moments, because those things exist in-universe. It just uses the existing dramatic failure/exceptional success mechanics to say what's significant enough to count. If your character can single out extremely injured people, and the system says to note this as people that have damage in their last 3 Health boxes, it doesn't mean they know Health boxes are a thing, or that damage is done in discrete chunks. It just a way to define in the system what's significantly injured enough to ping as, "that person is seriously injured."

                                                            Fate doesn't prove that exceptional successes are a real thing in-universe. It proves what we all already know: that doing really well on something is a thing in-universe. Fate just uses the out-of-universe exceptional success metric as a mechanical signpost for when doing really well is significant enough to register as notable to Fate Sight.
