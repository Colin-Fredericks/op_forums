                            What sort of challenge do you want the Acamoth/Gulmoth to pose?
                            • A physical threat, possibly to be faced in the final battle?
                            • A puzzle that needs to be solved, that otherwise keeps some options closed from the players?
                            • A social interaction, that needs to be convinced or manipulated to give way to the PCs?
                            • A moral conundrum? It could be an unsolvable one, designed just to make players squirm.

                            How did the Intruder get there, and what is its relationship with the Cult?
                            • Do you want it to be a discrete entity to be confronted, or more of a force of unnature that needs more creativity to be overcome?
                            • Was it purposefully summoned by the Cult, or was it unexpected, possibly a wildcard unaligned with any of the sides?​
                            • Is it an inhabitant of the Prince, or a secondary side-effect of reality deteriorating?

                            Most importantly: what would your players enjoy in the encounter? What abilities would they like to flex and what aspects of their characters do they want to express?

                                                    About Debra Henley and the Fate spell. It's best to choose if you want to serve your players with a shocking plot twist or escalating tension. If you choose tension, assume that the new Fate is already tainted by the Abyss and out of Davy Jones' control. He would likely warn the PC's about this, which plays into your hands. A malfunctioning spell may have all kinds of dangerous effects, up to summoning Debra's Abyssal Imprint (from the Intruders: Encounters with the Abyss sourcebook).

                                                    On the other hand, you can discard the idea of the Spell being tainted. Make it known that Fate is helping characters along towards the Temple, until they learn that Baron is about to meet his doom. It could be rather rude to erase your player's PC like that so be sure to either give him a good chance of saving himself, or prepare a campaign set within the Prince of 100.000 Leaves. The erased characters may very well still be there.

                                                    Alternatively, do tell them the Spell went wrong, but that it also helps them along towards the Temple. They will face a dilemma that way.

                                                    As for other questions:
                                                    1. Tactics are not my comfort zone. Still, perhaps you could run a session based on drafting a plan and have Ferrum lead that meeting. That way the players aren't forced to think of everything, but can still offer their ideas for you to use. Even if they decide that all the important stuff is better left to other Mages, pivotal events may fall upon them through happenstance. Factors that can pull PCs into action are: Baron's Fate spell, Balam's connection to his brother, Individuals within the Cult following their own agendas (cannibal twins out for revenge, Sanctifier of the Meek's attempts at conversion, Wendigo wanting to reclaim the Pendant).

                                                    2. The obvious solution is destroying all the Leaves and erasing them from living memory, or at the very least making sure they don't occupy the same place (or mind). The challenges with that are many, first of them being that the Temple will be around for far longer. If the outcome of the story is that the Consillium is rebuilt around keeping the Temple quarantined, then that is an interesting outcome. The more obvious solution is explosives. Lots of them. Even if you can't shunt an Intrusion back into the Abyss, you may just try to destroy it.
                                                    If you let Rhukavira manifest, you may go several routes. The first is that Sleepers do see and react to it with either willful ignorance or conspirational fervor. Strange things do happen in the world of CofD, and this will be a prominent one. The second is that more of Rhukavira manifests, but either in an out of the way location, or Sleepers are convinced it was always there. This is whole-sale reality editing, not mere translocation. The Veil will be maintained, but the Prince still gains a greater foothold near Boston.

                                                    3. Ask your players (especially Balam's) how they feel about that plot thread, and what they think would be most poetically appropriate. Give them that, but not in the way they expect. That's the best I can offer.​​

                                                            Happy to help.

                                                            Claim is a Manifestation Condition, and those can be outright ended with Banishment (Spirit 4). The possessing Spirit may also choose to disentangle from the host, but I don't remember how long that process takes. Lesser Spells may help in facilitating that, but again, I don't know how they interact with Claimed specifically - casting Command Spirit (Spirit 2) with an order to "Leave this man!" might work, or it might not. Ultimately, even if the exorcism succeeds, the brother is still a Temple Hound, and likely suffering aftereffects from his flesh being reconfigured back and forth.

                                                            You've asked for advice about this confrontation before, and I'm pretty proud of the response I shared back then. The only thing I'm able to add is "What kind of entity would want to possess a Temple Hound?". A Spirit Noble of Temple Hounds seeking to broaden the hunt? A Missionary who botched its arrival and got trapped? A refugee (or multiple refugees) from the Prince desperate to leave that realm and latching on to whoever it can? A Gatekeeper (Zuul!) that stabilizes the connection between the Prince and reality? Something from the unformed Abyss that stumbled in, corrosive to both reality and the Prince?
