                    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                    The distant stars are where the Idigam come from. Like, the Werewolf antagonist so iconic that the Chronicle was going to be named after them.
                    Mysteries about their ultimate origin notwithstanding, for all practical purposes, the idigam come from Earth or the Moon, as evident from "idigam" being the First Tongue rendering of their epithet of "Moon-Banished" and the other category being "Earth-Bound." If they came from distant stars the distinction between them and the more conventional void-spirits getting past Luna's orbit would be entirely arbitrary and their resurgence following the Moon landings would be a drop in the bucket.

                    The Shadow of outer space is largely as empty as its material counterpart and that shows itself in the types of spirits that come from outer space being hungry and desperate and ill-suited to what-passes-for-life on the Shadow of the Earth when they aren't particularly spiteful reflections of Helios's baleful radiance in the form of planetary angels. Idigam with the right Dread Power can call void spirits down from beyond the lunar sphere, but the root quality of their initial Formlessness and ability to Coalesce speaks more to some sort of primordial process of the Shadow than anything to do with the likes of Alpha Centauri and such.

                    The Whorl allows you to travel astronomic distances in the part of the cosmology that corresponds to the interiority of the universe separate from human culture and individual human minds, which themselves are each theoretically several times the scope of the material world that forms the basis for their creators' perspectives. Even accounting for entropic processes like the loss of a Temenos realm's connections winnowing the actual accessible space, that's dramatically bigger in practice than any realistic amount of spatial distortion the Shadow brings into play because the Shadow is a singular reflection of the world, no matter how distorted it sometimes gets — it's shaped by the perspectives of the living world on the other side of the Gauntlet, the less human activity in a location to thicken the Gauntlet the less distorted the Shadow tends to be on average, and the fact that everything's perspectives are feeding into the same space means there's only so much "this space feels bigger because it's inhabited by fingernail-sized bugs" can skew the scale of anywhere that hasn't been carved out by a god or subject to a similar level of plot device.

                    It's a different branch of the metaphysics, but the material on the limits of Supernal Verges is salient here as a storytelling tool: the Shadow is defined primarily by reflecting the world as it is, with things like entire horrible alternate-universe cities or phantom mountains where the Firstborn dwell being legendary outlier locations. Even with the assumption of frugal set-dressing in Oneiric memory-realms, a popular tree in a national park is reflected in the Astral on three different layers, one of those layers has copies of it for every individual person who remembers it, and another layer may have multiple thematically distinct archetypes of it for different categories of people who know about it.

                    All of this is setting aside that I don't think "the Astral is as big as the sky" was the point of the line being quoted and that all of the above makes it plain that at bare minimum the Shadow needs to lean heavily into outliers to approach the scales that the Astral easily surpasses due to not being confined to connections to real geography.

                        It's hard to say to which degree any mind is really able to create a representation of everything they experience, much less to give such representations adequate proportions.

                        Both the Earth and the universe at large are too good at doing things in unimaginable scales in the literal sense: not just big, but big enough that while we can mathematically comprehend their size, we have no parameter to picture it without heavily simplifying distortions.

                        So... Is any Astral representation proportional to the actual phenomena or places? I highly doubt. Very few people are capable of forming a proportional mental picture of their city, and the actual populated area is so small that even if you sum the "mental world" of everyone it will not necessarily cover an area comparable to their proportional share of uninhabited area on Earth. And that's talking about surface, only.

                        As a reality that sums up mental experiences and representations, the Astral is unbound by spatial limits, true. But as a reality that sums up the reflections and interpretations of all space and matter in the universe, the Shadow is unbound by limits on what can be or has been experienced.

Most of the problems that the Shadow causes for werewolves and other concerned parties stem from conceptual borders and human involvement.

                            The Shadow is important because it's close to material reality and has broad concrete influence over its phenomena. It can do that without trying to equivocate on whether its spatial dimensions can outpace the entirety of significant subjective experience in the realm of souls.

And I don't think anyone here questions how easy it is to make anything about the Astral relevant to a game, while exploring space in the Shadow would be a rather particular kind of game.

                                Your descriptions also fully ignore the point I made: however big you think the entirety of significant subjective experience in the realm of souls is, the comparison is with the entirety of objective reality outside the need for souls or subjective experience, which is by definition an unknowable quantity. As is anything we say about the Shadow beyond the Moon since we only know it through spirits already within our influence sphere and as such compromise as data: we don't know if entering the area change them to what we see.

                                I fully understand the underlying philosophical concept you're starting from, the capacity of subjective reality to multiply instantiations of the world and of impossible worlds would be inherently "bigger" than reality as it encompasses and then expands.

                                What I'm doing is reject this as fact. Because it is philosophically objectionable. It assumes a lot about both human experience and reality beyond this experience. Objectively it faces a lot of real challenges as several experiments can demonstrate the innate limitations of what subjective experience can really encompass, giving us insight on where it fails.

                                Not that it proves the opposite, but those other perspectives demonstrate the assumption is fragile, a possibility open to debate at best. It has no bearing on the fictional setting, either, but the setting accepts and benefits from the lack of a definitive answer. The Astral improves upon conscious experience, making it factually bigger than objective experience by orders of magnitude. The Shadow also improves upon physical reality, though, and this reality is itself bigger in a fiction where untold supernatural phenomena may exist beyond our knowledge and perception.

                                The only true answer for those questions in the game if taken that seriously is that all those realms are incomprehensibly large and of isn't possible to meaningfully measure them. What we can do at best is make some educated guesses and be honest about assumptions and their limitations when doing so.
