            Originally posted by Diggs View Post
            If we then switch this to a spell that turns people into frogs within the area, a person substitutes for a thermometer in the above example. Inside they are a frog, outside they are not. Lasting effects are permanent. A spell duration is only relevant as long as the magic is in effect and the magic is on the area rather than spread like some contagion onto things within the area.
            That's the thing — my reading was that the spell goes off in the area and sticks to anyone that was within that area when it went off, but did not otherwise continue to propagate in the case of effects that do not specifically mess with a location, because sticking to people is what a person-affecting spell effect cast at every other Range except Aimed does. It does not follow intuitively that a spell to affect an individual target can only not affect an individual target at one specific point on the range axis — as-written, an Aimed spell that hits a person affects them only as long as they remain within arm's reach of the space they occupied when you hit them unless there's another hidden exception that lets Aimed spells without any extra Scale factors ignore the stipulation that Aimed spells must use Area Scale.

            There are spells for which people obviously substitute for thermometers in the above analogy, but they're spells whose effects are already things like "gravity works differently in this space" or "there's a wall of fire here" or "this place is actually another place." I can conceive of plenty of visual metaphors for personally-targeted effects that pick up and cease when a person enters and leaves a given location, but the lack of anything like one of them in the book — the lack of some sort of "here's how spells work in the fiction as extensions of a mage's will in the world of three spatial dimensions and linear time" — means none of them come to mind as readily as "making this spell into a projectile affected by Defense and cover means boosting its number of potential targets works by making it splash outward on impact."

            The fact that you have to find the disjunction and reverse-engineer an entirely different paradigm of effect-placement when the most obvious thing to indicate the splash-based reading isn't how it works is the bit where it's dramatically easier to use Area Scale than Number of Subjects to affect large numbers of people is, I think, self-explanatory as a demonstration that this could have been made clearer than it was.

            There are limits to the magic system. Attainments and archmastery are necessary to overcome the limits. The system doesn't support tossing a spell into a room that turns everyone into a frog for a month whether they stay in the room or not in the basic form of the system. This is good because free-form systems are complicated enough.
            There are limits to the magic system, yes. One of the established, specifically-stated limits that I had based my opposition to "the spell can pick up new targets and drop old ones" on was the fact that that's a lower-key version of the qualities particular to Dynamics spells. A spell that can theoretically apply a normally targeted effect to an entire neighborhood full of people (but no more than that over the course of its Duration) seems substantially more precedented than a spell that can theoretically target many times the population of an entire neighborhood full of people over the course of its Duration before the level of Arcanum competence at which you can make viral memetic spells.

            It saves bookkeeping on figuring who's still affected, gives another clear reason to bother with number-based Scale, and adds another tick to the box of Excuses For Mages To Work In Isolated Locales, but the questions it raises in turn are also not answered or alluded to by the book — Can the area be moved, like a lantern or a sunshade or a shroud, to allow characters to maintain an effect while moving from place to place? Do characters subjected to the spell within the area gain the same sympathy to the mage that a direct target would, or is this type of Scale a viable means of targeting a subject without making them a mobile link back to you or subjecting them to your Long-Term Nimbus? Does any other normal spell modification have this same slipperiness that allows a subject to shed the effect without ending the spell or reducing its effective Potency to nil, or is Area Scale unique in this regard? Does any partial-effect weirdness happen at the thresholds of the area, or does the spell only affect a target that's fully inside the target area? Can dynamic spell control shape holes into the area of a spell's effect that either bisect the space or have no connection to an exterior edge, or does the blanketed area have to be a single contiguous filled region? — and the fact that the system defaults to "a spell affects the thing it works on until it ends" rather than "a spell affects viable subjects in the space it occupies at the start of its Duration, which may be mobile and fragmentary if that space is one or more discrete objects, and if a viable subject is in that space it is under the effects of the spell until it leaves or the spell ends" is one of those vital framing mechanisms akin to "all spells default to ritual casting time unless effort and insight are leveraged to make it take effect sooner" for the purposes of understanding How Magic Works.

            I'm glad to get clarification, but I'd prefer if that clarification came with actionable adjustments to my understanding of how it all hangs together.

                                                Originally posted by Diggs View Post
                                                If new people are affecting then what duration do they have? There should only be the one duration. That would make it the same as Without a Trace cast on anyone inside the room for full duration and the remaining duration for anyone entering later based on the time one enters.                                                 The thing is, again, "spells cannot increase their factors without being re-cast" is one of those things that seems directly contradicted by allowing the spell to affect more people than were present when it was cast — to a casual reading, the effect of Pocket Dimension is "there is a Pocket Dimension of this scale," not "anyone who enters the area covered by this scale is in a Pocket Dimension," and likewise for similar spells that normally affect an area, and the top billing and less stringent requirements of Target Scale combined with the language of a spell "hitting" a region bias the natural reading towards "spells that normally affect targets act the same without explicit changes represented by Reach effects et al."

                                                Also, "there should only be the one duration" as a reading relies on the predicate that the spell's effect must remain in the area to be arrived at as a conclusion — when "the spell remains in the area and anyone who enters it later gains the effect as well" is, as far as I've seen, a view seriously advanced by nobody in this discussion.

                                                The sticky-effect reading assumes that there is one Duration, which applies to anyone who the spell hits when it initially goes off and nobody else, which is how spells that apply to people normally operate. Again, the only significant clues that this isn't how it works are a notional redundancy that can still be justified by preparation and safety being important to the Wise and the fact that naturally Area-based spells that affect locations still affect people in those locations. Otherwise you're fighting the instinct that giving a spell Area Scale — a thing that takes no special resources on its own and must be applied on any spell cast outside of Touch Range without Sensory Range — fundamentally changes the description of what the spell does in a fashion whose reverse is literally a Reach effect for some published core spells, lets a long-Duration spell affect dramatically more subjects over time than just the ones that can fit inside that space, and removes an aspect of target-based spells that does not obviously owe itself to the spell being target-based by letting the effect slide off subjects who leave the area and still regain it later.

                                                There's room to get to the spell-shadow interpretation — the above observation re: Pocket Dimension, the fact that a ball of fire burns people inside of it and not outside of it, the large number of media examples of sudden bolts of magic being vectors for damage or immobilization over more involved effects — but it would be a lot easier to arrive at that conclusion if "spells last for their Duration and affect anyone who lingers in their area of effect" was earlier in the description of how spellcraft worked rather than sandwiched in between "spells affect a chosen target and last for their Duration" and the rest of the system.
