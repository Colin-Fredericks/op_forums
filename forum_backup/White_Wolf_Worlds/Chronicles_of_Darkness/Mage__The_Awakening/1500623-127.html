            Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
            Is that stated or inferred somewhere? Because I got more of a "the game is silent about this so the ST decides" than a straight "stop using physics to work outside the bounded setting".
            A mage needs to be able to recognize the symbolism of a Yantra to be able to leverage that symbolism, sympathy is sufficiently particular that temporal sympathy requires a correspondence to the time that you're trying to view/evoke/travel to, and spatial sympathy's descending order of metaphysical closeness is easy to compare to the notion that you can't use the fact that someone was born in or lives in a country to teleport to that country while they're outside of it or scry on its president. "Starlight comes from stars" does not outpace the fact that most starlight is barely a material piece of a specific star at all in the same way that a random passerby on the street about whom you know nothing at all is not even close enough to be considered a Weak connection to you even if they happen to live on that street.

            Sympathetic connections are significant enough that the spell for turning the faint semblance of one from physical contact into a mechanically definite one requires that contact to last for at least the system's smallest unit of narrative time (still loosely measured in seconds) and 2e lacks 1e's mechanical categories of sympathetic connection below casual acquaintance. Tome of the Pentacle has a whole section dedicated to explaining why would-be archaeomancers typically visit the locations they're investigating instead of relying on secondhand connections for reasons that can be summarized as "sympathy is particular," itself further refining on the corebook's stipulation that a material sympathy Yantra represents the subject as it is now.

            Awakened magic specifically doesn't occupy the framework where ground-level PCs can play with abstractions as though they were concrete phenomena like some powers available to monsters do — see the whole observed deal with the Mask of Eyes not working on the known principles of sympathy, or Darklings from Changeling being able to merge with immaterial phenomena like smoke and sunlight to become temporarily intangible, or Unchained demons being able to do things like conceptually redirect or move injuries. 2e defines the Supernal World mages see as where the concrete and the abstract mix, and explicitly states that without archmastery it's impossible to Veil something against abstract concepts or forces.

            On the other end of comparison, Mage shares a setting with Promethean, where the repeatable means of making one of the Created involve recombination of discernible parts of a human body (which tears itself apart if it fails) and the main successful peripheral methods of using Azoth to create life from parts of other life are all forms of cloning or mutating an existing specimen — an Extempore can get away with being a human form spun together from desert sands or an archetypal idea because their whole struggle is being practically non-repeatable phenomena even by Created standards and needing to figure out how to reproduce their condition to end it, but most Prometheans whose creation rituals work regularly are ones made from human parts big enough that the likes of the Residual Memory Merit make sense in the same vein as urban legends about organ transplants changing people's personalities.

            The inference, in short, is that nothing about the game does support using extremely small, very fast, functionally interchangeable particles as "pieces of" an extremely distant subject, but the game does take pains to emphasize the limits of sympathy in multiple places.

                        I cannot stop talking about light as a material phenomenon in the discussion of its use as a required component of a type of casting that requires a material component, specifically compared with subatomic particles by the immediate following inquiry about neutrinos.

                        Likewise, the fact that you are talking about stars as light generators is immaterial to the fact that the question that prompted this discussion was "can you use sunlight to teleport someone to the corona of the sun?"

                        The unit of relevance being symbolism is a limiting factor on what a mage can use as a Yantra because spellcasting is an act of will and imagination. The light of a lamp is not a symbolic referent to the lamp for the purposes of surpassing vast distances either — you can't use a distant spotlight to sympathetically teleport over the horizon, either, because while you could teleport to the location within range of your senses that corresponds to the part of the beam you can see and even use Forces and Space to bend your perceptions to better see an endpoint that doesn't require you to emerge in midair, the light does not have material sympathy with the lamp any more than you have sympathy with the air you exhaled five minutes ago. Material sympathy Yantras use concrete objects of macroscopic scale for a reason and they are the foundation of this conversation.

                        The Chronicles of Darkness are a setting where multiple frames of reality are actively deceiving the people that inhabit them. The Chronicles of Darkness are a place where matter routinely goes missing without explanation and causality allows for things like the Key to an Avernian Gate always reappearing in the same abandoned house or a mummy emerging from its tomb minutes after it was destroyed. Physics exists and mostly works as we know it to in the real world, but magic does not consistently adhere to physical laws and there is only so far "characters with moderate to major knowledge of the Space Arcanum don't teleport subjects to outer space using sunlight/moonlight/starlight because they don't think it's worth it" can explain why the topic has never come up despite the established curiosity and knowledge bases of the Awakened in the twenty years the line has existed in published form.

                        Awakened sympathetic magic requires a Sympathy Yantra, which is a type of Tool Yantra, which the system defines as an item in the absence of mechanics allowing other things to be treated as Tool Yantras. Light is not an item and any attempts to treat it as such require engagement with the fact that the typical Awakened toolkit does not have means of turning light into an item with sympathies that the mage can borrow for Space magic — air is a similarly insubstantial vector for connection to a subject, and that's much closer to being material. By direct comparison, a lacuna prevents Time magic from being used to examine the history of a rubber tree that was used to make a rubber item being scrutinized; a change in fundamental form has consequences on an object's magical utility even if it's made of the same material, and ubiquitous or diffuse phenomena are not objects.

                        I could see a case to be made for splitting hairs over how small a quantity of sand from a beach or desert could serve as a connection to that location, but phenomena that are almost entirely sensory experiences like light or smells are insubstantial enough that while they might serve as vectors for targeting spells via Sensory range or function as Environmental Yantras, they do not have the weight of concrete presence to serve as Tools for sympathetic magic, else you open the door to weird questions like "why do mages need to take solid or liquid samples from things they want to use sympathy on when they could just use little jars full of air or magically preserve the heat from exposure/contact?"
