    First Mage

    04-13-2020, 06:02 PM
    Hello forum! Tomorrow will be session 0 of my first ever Mage: The Awakening game! I've been wanting to play any of the Chronicles of Darkness games for years, so I'm thrilled to finally have the opportunity. I'm hoping for some assistance in building my first character! I have a few ideas I'm interested in trying out.

    When I was first invited to join this game, I was under the false impression we would be playing Ascension and immediately fell in love with the Akashayana. I was disappointed to say the least when my misunderstanding was corrected, but perhaps I could still make this a reality. In the character creation section there is a sidebar about Nameless Orders, so maybe I could have my Mage be a member of a small cell of Akashayana that have faded into obscurity (intentionally?) while the new Orders rose. Are Ascension and Awakening unified or separate? Worst case scenario the Adamantine Arrow seems like the next best thing, but it's a lot more broad in scope and much more militant in philosophy.

    The concept of the character is to be a new Mage struggling with personal demons who finds peace and purpose through martial discipline. He would be a martial arts instructor by day and a guardian of his cabal and sanctum by night. To serve this end, I really like the Sphere of Forces, particularly the defensive capabilities. I would think his primary spell would be Velocity Control to slow bullets. I want him to be able to walk into a room and feel perfectly secure from physical threats, and be a threat himself. Supplementary Spheres would be Life for healing and physical enhancement, and perhaps Prime for protecting against magical effects. This makes it seem like Obrimos would be the perfect Path, right?

    When it comes to Paths, I really like the attitudes and experiences of the Mastigos. I especially like the Stygian odyssey* of facing one's fears and moral failings to purge the soul (I'm an edgelord, I know ). That said, I don't especially like one of the Spheres they are tied to. Mind is excellent, and leans into the moral introspection angle of the Path when grappling with the ethical implications of its use. Space, on the other hand, I don't enjoy so much. I get why it works, sympathy and all that, I just don't find its abilities as compelling or well suited to the character.

    ​* "A mage who walks the Path of Scourging through the nightmarish labyrinth of the realm of Pandemonium, at the center of which is the Watchtower of the Iron Gauntlet. Pandemonium is also called the Realm of Nightmares, for its echoes appear to Sleepers most often in their most terrible and dreaded dreams, where they fall from endless heights, never to hit the ground, or run for what seems like hours but never make any ground. Their worst fears or repressed emotions are brought forth in places touched by Pandemonium, to be examined and judged by strangers, who mock and condemn them. Through such a gauntlet of humiliation and submission, a soul is scourged of its sins and is thus purified to reunite, cleansed and free, with the divine."

    Unfortunately, I find the attitudes of the Obrimos to be even worse. Being a shining beacon of moral authority is sort of the opposite of what I'm looking for. Then again, I could do something interesting here. Instead of having the typical exalted experience through the Aether**, his journey was excruciating. Every step was like walking on burning coals, the wind scourged him like barbed whips, and by the end he was barely conscious when he carved his name into the Watchtower wall. Get all the edginess of the Mastigos with the bombastic Spheres of the Obrimos. It's a bit of a stretch though, and I would frankly prefer him just be a Mastigos with a different Sphere specialization. I'm not sure how strict you're expected to adhere to the lore in that regard.

    ** "A mage who walks the Path of the Mighty, gliding on celestial winds through the realm of the Aether and the firmament of the stars to reach the Watchtower of the Golden Key. Only the elect can enter here, guarded by the Hosts with their swords of fire. Lightning strikes any who fly with false wings, like Icarus downed by his hubris. He who would wield the Flame Supernal must not flinch in the face of adversity, and cleave to one of the many visions of the divine."

    So, current rough draft for this character is: Hector, Mastigos Mage of a Nameless Order (Akashayana). He runs a martial arts gym that is itself a Sanctum that he has been assigned guardianship over by his mentor. He trains to master his physical and magical abilities to protect himself and the world from the horrors of the Chronicles of Darkness, and keeps a close eye on his students for potential Awakenings. He is primarily a Forces Mage, supplemented by Mind to seek diplomatic solutions and Life to heal up after negotiations fail.

    My other character is much less difficult to work. Loosely based on a classmate of mine, this character would be named Apollo, an Obrimos Mage of the Free Council. He's a street performer, a fire spinner/breather with a troupe of Sleeper/walker apprentices and a regular fanbase that he is slowly attempting to Awaken through subtle use of Forces and Prime to enhance his performance. The real life person I'm basing this on is a physical masterpiece, dude is unbelievably shredded, so I'm giving him Life as well to reflect his good health. This concept is pretty much ready to go, but I'm still playing with the prior one. Any tips or suggestions would be much appreciated!

      Glad to hear you are psyched. Ascension and Awakening are very different games, both are pretty awesome for different reasons.

      Magic in Ascension is the result of consensual reality and you focus on exploring, defining and spreading your version of it as you grow.

      In Awakening there is one Supernal Truth, but its a weird, multi layered and complex. Mages have been outside Plato's Cave for the merest hint of a moment. They became able to call upon and manipulate the Forms to alter reality. They expand their power by witnessing first hand the supernatural, exploring it and internalizing their insights.

      Philosophically speaking, you could replicate the Akashayana with a Perfected Adept:

      https://whitewolf.fandom.com/wiki/Perfected_Adept

      You can find a 2ed conversion here:

      http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...d-to-2e/page24

      The focus on action, honor and self improvement is at the core of the Adamantine Arrow. The return to simplicity, mindfulness and martial arts is a focus of the Legacy.

      However, they focus on Forces and Life. But the Astral is open to all mages, so the insights of the mind would still be available.

      Regarding Apollo, have you considered making him a member of the Pygmalian Society ? He seems to be working with a similar train of thought:

      https://whitewolf.fandom.com/wiki/Pygmalian

      You can find a 2ed conversion on the first page of the same thread as the Adepts.

      Welcome to Mage:The Awakening, hope you have a blast !

          Ascension and Awakening are different games with different histories and metaphysics. "Spheres" are an Ascension thing; the term in Awakening is Arcana. Also, you seem to be mixing editions, given that Velocity Control is a 2e spell and you're referencing the 1e Path writeups as the basis of your pursuits beyond "what Arcana do I want to mess with?"

          "The lore" with regards to the Paths is essentially that each one starts from a different basic perspective of What Magic Is and how different important bits filter into their view of the Arcana.

          The Obrimos are primed* to examine magic through the lens of Power and Command, and look at the thing itself as a complex system — moral authority is orthogonal to the Path of the Mighty's yen for defining, structuring, and otherwise struggling with the fact that their Awakening relies on a certain amount of divine inspiration (read: leaps of logic that you will not be able to explain to people in language). They deal with the magic of Forms, and the danger of their focal pillar of the soul is handled as a "moral authority" thing pretty much entirely for the reasons that their symbolic correspondences in the Tarot include Strength: power bound by discipline and insight. They're a little less clear-cut than some of the other Paths as far as What Their Magic's For, but if I had to try and put a word to it, "self-expression" would be the one.

          The Mastigos, by contrast, are all about self-mastery; they wrestle their inner demons through the lens of Transgression and Confrontation to force them to work for them, and managing the collection of passions and internal conflicts in themselves and other conscious beings is a reflective process. Perception, placement, and personality make up the core of their fifth of the soul, and the ongoing process of dealing with the Devil is more about identifying and managing urges than putting a name on your dirty laundry. There's a kind of a Ring of Gyges thing with the Path of Scourging, in that Mind and Space are really easy Arcana to exploit but they're also really useful in general — "diplomacy" is not the first thing that comes to mind as the use for power over The Mind Itself absent the rather restrained exercise of "make myself into a different person who can handle this discussion well."

          Running through the other three in briefer terms since they aren't the main dichotomy you're going over: Acanthus pursue self-determination, Moros pursue self-perfection, and the Thyrsus (the other less-clear one along with the Obrimos) angle for self-transcendence. They look at magic through lenses of choice-and-consquence, permanence-and-transition, and boundaries-and-intercession, respectively, and the stereotypical view of them, at least, is not quite so high-strung as the Obrimos and the Mastigos, as you could probably gather from 1e giving them a free dot in Composure instead of Resolve. The Acanthus trend a smidgen manipulative, the Moros tend to be pretty chill about things that most people are culturally taught to keep their distance from, and the Thyrsus tend to take the social approach to other worlds even when they aren't the Shadow.

          I'd recommend against having the gym be the Sanctum unless it's specifically for mages and Sleepwalkers or you're having him work a lot of subtle magic there; your Sanctum is a ritual space for you to practice magic in relative privacy, as an extension of the mundane uses of a Safe Place. Is the Sanctum in a hidden portion of the gym? (Could give you something to do with the affinity for Space, at least, if you want to stick with Mastigos.)

          If the magical abilities are the main points of priority for the choice of Path it may be worth prioritizing one angle over the rest and taking rotes and Praxes in the other Arcana — the free improvisation in two Arcana is the biggest mechanical impact your Path has, but if you're after an Arcanum for just one or two primary tricks then you can take the time to get good at those specific things without needing the whole tenth of All Awakened Magic to come naturally to you.

              If you're doing 2e, I'd recommend against pursuing specific Arcana for "defensive capabilities". In Awakening 2e, there are things called Arcanum Attainments and you get Mage Armor at 2 dots in any Arcanum. This is basically because any Arcanum is useful for basic things like hurting people and preventing you from getting hurt; they just differ in how they do it. Arcanum Attainments are discussed starting on p192 of the corebook. If you want to defend against bullets, for instance, you could use Fate to just be conveniently lucky that you happened to move in a pattern that dodged them all (think how Domino was portrayed in Deadpool 2). Or use Matter to thicken the air or transmute them into harmless vapors. Or use Death to "kill" their impact. That's basically narrative flavor. Make sure you have 2 dots in something other than Prime or Spirit and you'll be fine.

              With "being a threat himself", virtually every starting Mage is that: with three dots in any Arcanum, you can cause direct damage with magic. You can also get Very Creative, but you'll have to read up on Creative Thaumaturgy and talk with your ST on anything you try. There's literally too much to cover. There was a recent thread on how to use Prime for assassination, for instance.

              If you're really bothered by the Mastigos write-up, I'd suggest reading through this thread:
              http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...more-palatable I'm sure people would be up to doing a Why The Obrimos Aren't So Bad thread, too, if what Satchel said doesn't satisfy you.

                  Akashic Brothers as a small order should work, but you probably would enjoy the character concept just as well as an Adamantine Arrow or Free Council mage.

                  AA are martial, but they aren't all bully boys or bruisers. Their philosophy is one of personal betterment and service.
                  The Free Council, unlike most mages, believe that Sleepers have valuable insights into the great mysteries, and study their developments for new truths. This means that they would fully support learning from Sleeper masters of an art as a reasonable means of improving one's own Gnosis.

                  (Either or both would likely attend classes is your character has the skills to make that reasonable, regardless of affiliation.)

                  The Perfected Adepts were created with them in mind. (Pretty sure their 1e write-up even called them Akashi somewhere) That said, if you go with a focus on self-perfection and combat focus, you could be from a number of legacies. (e.g. The Uncrowned Kings are literally attempting to set up their own alchemical crucible to purify their mind, body, and soul.)

                  There's a bit of a dearth of martial arts as legacies, so a custom legacy may be more appealing. That's more work but it can be fun.


                  Go take a peek at the section "attainments" after the spells listing. Look up Space. That's great fun for a martial artist of any soft style, at least.

                  Outward and Inward Eye lets you see everything around you, granting amazing spatial awareness.
                  Ground Eater let's you run really fast, which is always nice to have in your back pocket.
                  Break Boundary allows you to bypass locks and chains. Also nice to have.Fits a legendary ninja.
                  The martial uses of Ban and CoLocation are innumerable and not all obvious.
                  Perfect Sympathy fits a martial character who learns about a foe and uses that insight to defeat them.
                  Alter Direction allows for all sorts of silly wuxia stunts. It's great armor, too.

                  So, my suggestions would be:
                  Don't write off the Space arcanum. Knowing where to strike is an important part of martial skill. Not being where an attack is going to be is another.
                  Make sure your gym has a locked office or private practice room. That would be an appropriate ritual space.
                  Consider membership in Adamantine Arrow or Free Council..If not, chat with your ST and see about representing your group with the Cult Merit. While at it, you might consider Martial Arts as a Professional Skill Merit, too.

                  Grump, grouse, and/or gripe.

                                      Here's a few links to some pages you might be interested in:

                                      Some mechanical optimization: http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...er-development

                                      Cabal creation: http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...eation-process

                                      Some good advice on learning the game in general: http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...learning-curve

                                      There are other pages here on the forums that can help as well, but they might take some digging on your part. One thing I would keep in your mind is to keep a stronger focus on who the person is and what makes them tic over what they are capable of. Interesting games are made of interesting people, less so interesting skills. Of course don't be afraid to leave some of the canvas blank so you can explore it after the game has started, but you'll probably want to answer a few questions:

                                      What is the character's personality?
                                      What is one of the best memories the character has?
                                      What are two habits the character has (probably emphasis on mental tics over good vs bad)?
                                      What personal trauma has the character gone through or experienced? Is it still relevant to who they are today?
                                      What obsession did they have that lead them to awakening? You will probably want to thematically tie this in to the path they awaken to. Some example questions that can help answer that would be:
                                      Acanthus: What duty is caging you to your predictable routine?
                                      Mastigos: What kind of personal passion do you have: obsession, destructive thought pattern, or spiritual wound? Describe it.
                                      Moros: What heavy responsibility is weighing you down, leading you towards an undesirable change?
                                      Obrimos: Where in your life do you have an ordered, hierarchical system? What does that look like?
                                      Thyrsus: Where do you go to be most alive?

                                      Last question: Has your character experienced something supernatural before they Awakened? If so, what was it and how did it affect them?

                                      Finally, consider leaning heavily into and on your aspirations and obsessions. These will inform your ST what kinds of things you want in the game.

                                      Oh, and if your ST forgets or doesn't think about it, you should ask them to do a Lines and Veils thing (from Changeling the Lost, but it's a great idea anyways). It basically goes like this:
                                      "A venerable old safety technique, Lines and Veils allow players to pick and choose what they want to addressin the chronicle. Before the game, the Storyteller prepares two sheets of paper. Label one “Lines” and the other “Veils.” Lines are things the chronicle will absolutely not touch on, not even in passing. Veils are things that can happen, but will not be played out, instead addressed with a “fade to black.” The Storyteller asks players what they’d like added to the lists, and notes that the lists can be edited at any time. Veils can be moved to Lines, Lines can be moved to Veils, new Veils or Lines can be added, or Veils or Lines can be taken away (with the consensus of the other players). Veils and Lines cannot be used to cut out antagonists (i.e. “I don’t want the True Fae to be a part of this chronicle at all, not even mentioned in passing”) but can be used to restrict antagonists’ actions that might be uncomfortable for some players (i.e. “I do not want the True Fae in this chronicle to use sexual violence”)."

                                      It's less important in a game where trauma isn't part of the main focus of the game, but it's still a very valuable thing to include. Just replace True Fae and other references to Changeling the Lost with appropriate things from Mage and other parts of the horror genre that are appropriate.

                                              The benefit of a Praxis is essentially a crit bonus, right? So I want this on spells that benefit from critical successes. I honestly have no idea what spells would benefit from this.
                                              The benefit of Praxes is that, as spells you are especially practiced at, they succeed exceptionally more easily, which means they're more likely to gain the benefits of exceptional success (for which see the spellcasting section's roll results).

                                              Some factor-intensive spells might be said to benefit "more" from exceptional success, since the list of options includes a free step or a free Reach in the primary spell factor or completely ignoring a target's Withstand rating, but every spell benefits from exceptional success, because the rest of the options on that list are "apply a Condition to yourself or the subject that gives Arcane Beats when it's resolved" or "get Mana equal to what you spent on the spell, plus one more point," and regardless of which option you take from the list you are also getting a point of Willpower out the result.

                                              Basically, Praxes serve vaguely the same purpose as your Vice; they're a fallback recovery mechanism as much as they are "signature spells" for folks who can't make their own rotes yet.
