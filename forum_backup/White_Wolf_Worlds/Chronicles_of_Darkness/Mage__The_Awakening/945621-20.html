                                            Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
                                            Here's a question then, in the spirit of elevating this thread: how do you rectify the Supernal significance of Death with the desire to live forever? Death means more than just the cessation of life, but is instead wrapped up in the ends of things and entropy along with the wider Moros fascination on the transformation of things. Is lichdom an act of hubris because it is one of stasis, choosing to defy the natural flux of Death imposed over time - and would pursuing a transformative escape from mortality be less of a sin against Wisdom than simply prolonging one's current state?
                                            Absolutely. It probably got lost in the relatively long posts I've written, but in a nutshell I would say that for things to properly define themselves, they must interact with other things and that interaction requires change. But things are defined as much by when they are static as when they change. If everything changed all the time, universe would be in utter chaos, and there are certain constants present in this reality.

                                            The key would be how to prevent specifically, and I quote my previous post, "the cessation of continuation of a specific sequence of perceptions that define a single human mind, happening by the virtue of degradation of brain cells and organism as a whole, with similar processes not even happening in some existing animals". This is a deliberately mouthful term to show just how narrow kind of change we are talking about. The goal would be to make static only and only this particular process, and let everything else change (personality, memories, muscle mass if you exercise, etc...).

                                            Frankly, I'm a transhumanist and *I* wouldn't accept anything less. Rather than becoming a static thing unable to be shaped by outside stimuli, I would really prefer death. Transhumanism isn't about overcoming humanity, it's about overcoming physical limitations so that you can be more human, not less.

                                                    Originally posted by Joker View Post
                                                    I am pretty sure you are confusing Liches and Reapers here, which happens pretty often probably thanks to Tremere being both Liches and Reapers and the best known example of both.
                                                    Nothing about being a Lich requires you to fuck with people's souls. In fact, extending your life without harming anyone else is extremely easy in Mage. The description of Liches even acknowledges that, but handwaves it away as those tactics being dispellable (which completely ignores the existence of Legacy attainments, but maybe the rules for Legacies were written after the part about Liches).
                                                    Since this is more or less an issue for the ST, simply ruling that Legacies can't accomplish this without serious drawbacks as part of the bargain can maintain the theme of immortality coming at great cost to one's sense of humanity. Or not, depending on the style of game.

                                                    I think Legacies are perhaps a bit too easy to start thinking of in purely mechanical terms, especially since the mechanics for constructing their powers are as precise as they are (this is not a bad thing mind). But I've found they work best for me as a ST if there's a greater focus on an underlying assumption that, as an in-setting thing, a mage *can't* just define whatever powers they might want in the process of creating one, but that they arise organically from that Mage's development of Gnosis as they come into contact with Supernal truth. Legacy attainments to provide drawback free immortality by duplicating long duration life and death spells would have to come out of a narrative that made sense for those powers, and if the campaign setting has innate themes that say doing this makes you a monster, becoming a monster would have to arise in the legacy itself.
