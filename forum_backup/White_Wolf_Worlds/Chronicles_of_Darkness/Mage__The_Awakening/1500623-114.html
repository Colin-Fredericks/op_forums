        The general situation would be that, per the "Effect" note at the start of the section, tool Yantras provide a +1 bonus unless a different bonus supersedes it as described in the individual Yantra.

        You would need to be leveraging different symbolism to use a single tool as multiple different types of Tool Yantra, so you couldn't double up on either of Path Tools or Order Tools with the same tool, but if you're an Arrow and you have a Path Tool that's a weapon you can use it as both of those things in their different interpretations of What Weapons Mean.

        Soul stones count as Dedicated Tools for their creators, and part of that is that they double as Path or Order Tools, which normally provide a +1 bonus. A sanity-check interpretation (unless you want soul stones to be even more especially useful as magical aids if aligned with overlapping symbolism) is to interpret the "or" as exclusive and thus only allow the benefit of casting with someone else's soul stone to apply to the first use of it as a tool, with any additional applications using the more typical bonus.

                Order Tools are functionally mechanically identical to Path Tools in almost every way, i.e. they provide a +1 bonus for each different tool used to make the casting appropriate to the archetype the group represents.

                The Arrow exalts challenge and martial prowess, so the things that their magic incorporates include means of inflicting harm on others and means of protecting someone from harm and their Order Merit allows them to treat actions related to Fighting as Order Tools.

                The Free Council puts particular stock in the idea that human culture shapes the Supernal, so the common ground between the objects their magic may incorporate is that they're complex systems and their Order Merit allows them to treat less material examples of such systems in action as Order Tools and double up on doing so with Sleepers engaging in it.

                The Seers believe magic is a reward from the rulers of the Supernal for their servitude, so their Order Tools are designed to invoke the Exarch they serve, with lay members doing so through their Ministry connection and Prelates gaining the ability to further invoke them as more direct Patrons.

                Faction members further expand the material definition of what counts as an Order Tool based on their philosophy just as they add to the list of Skills they are encouraged to practice to benefit their Rote casting.

                Magic is sufficiently open-ended that an exhaustive definition of what spells can and cannot benefit from the less-personal-than-Persona-Yantras Flavor Tools would be prohibitively verbose to no real benefit. It's a vibe check.

                                Originally posted by quelsec View Post
                                Can you explain legacy attainments to me? The wording in the book is confusing and i cant get why they get less duration than potency.
                                They don't. They get free ranks equal to its highest prerequisite Arcanum minus one in Potency or Duration as determined by the primary factor of the spell the Attainment is based on, plus additional spell factors that would penalize casting the Attainment as a spell based on its lowest Arcanum.

                                For example, a Fourth Attainment based on Stealing Fire used by a practitioner with Prime 4 can make a Sleeper into a Sleepwalker for up to five turns (or a month, if one of the Attainment's two fixed Reach are put toward Advanced Duration) because that's three free steps up the Duration chart for a Duration-primary spell cast by someone with four dots in the Arcanum.

                                Without assigning Reach or other factors, it takes a scene to use and they can only affect a single willing target, because Potency starts at 1, Withstand reduces effective Potency, and Resolve doesn't go below 1 in normal circumstances, but since the Legacy member definitionally has enough Prime dots to offset four dice worth of spell factor penalties they could assign two spell factors between increasing Potency on a one-for-one basis (allowing them to affect subjects who haven't consciously allowed it with Resolve ratings up to 1 and 2, respectively) or doubling the number of subjects they can affect in one sitting (first two, then four).

                                In this case, assigning one Reach to Advanced Duration is easily the highest priority from that budget, since Stealing Fire is not terribly useful in extremely short-term situations, but the remaining Reach can be assigned to either making the Attainment usable as an instant action with no prep time, changing the primary factor from Duration to Potency (making the effect only last a scene without assigning other factors but allowing it to affect targets up to Resolve 3 without their conscious allowance), multiplying the number of subjects it can be used on in one sitting by five (up to five subjects with no Scale factors assigned, up to ten with one assigned, or up to twenty with two assigned), or (with 1 Mana and both additional factors assigned to Duration) making the Attainment's effects last indefinitely. It's technically also possible to spend the Reach on giving it Advanced Potency, but since Legacy Attainments can't be dispelled there's not much point to that.

                                Between the fixed Reach assignment and the limited ability to increase other spell factors, Legacy Attainments are less flexible than the spells they're based on, but they have consistent and reliable effects that can't be dispelled, and their power and flexibility still increase with knowledge of the Arcanum they're based on.
