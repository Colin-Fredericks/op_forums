                I've never really understood the argument that Mage is somehow more superheroes with trenchcoats, either for Awakening or back in my Ascension days than other lines. A fundamental aspect of the Mage settings, to me, sets a rather terrifying table as is. While the nature of Mages and the opening up of the reality of the world(s) they inhabit certainly provides the Mage the tools, in potentia anyway, to deal with the avenues now open to them. It doesn't really change that these things are horrific. A predatory Shadow world of spirits and less definable things, literal inner demons, horrific entities some which by their very nature defy explanation or definition,Symbols of tyranny,etc. Awakening is not lacking for varying levels of horror in external threats.

                But that's not all the horror in Mage. Dealing with other people, some of whom have made themselves something more(less?) than human, and what they can do with magic is itself horrifying. To say nothing of having those capabilities yourself. Knowing you COULD rip into someone's mind and rewire essentially who they are, to say nothing of the million and one other things that is within the grasp of a Mage. That's going to be an ever present temptation for mages, it's sets a table for Wisdom and the tensions in Mage societies. Real world people deal all the time with thoughts of fancy about what they might do to someone with even just a tiny fraction of the capabilities Mages have available to them. And that's just talking your everday mostly decent people.

                I think to ignore or dismiss the horror inherent to Mage requires an ignorance or intentional oversight of the the very evils of the real world and the motivations behind some of the most horrific acts in human history. Human nature can be very terrifying. That, to a certain extent to me, sets a contrast against those creatures in the WoD where some of the horror is inherent to the nature of being(vampire,werewolf,etc) before you start diving into how urges and motivations and ideologies start to rear their ugly head in the other games. Human evil, in the sense of real world evil runs throughout all the lines and Mage is no exception.

                                                Can't remember if I posted this before, but one way you can dial up some problematic implications of Mage is:

                                                All magic (Mage magic, at least) comes from the Abyss. Remember how in Seer/Diamond lore, how the Abyss was formed (or brought into relevance) by the Fall? Invert that. The Abyss wasn't created, the Supernal was. In the Time Before, there was only the World That Is, and the Abyss of all that Is Not. Atlantis/Lemuria/the Exarchs/etc. created the Supernal with the Watchtowers (known and unknown) which filter and "stabilize" the surrounding Abyssal chaos into their own Supernal Realms. This created safe* magic, at the cost of the Exarchs being able to use them to more directly impose their will over the world. The Exarchs don't want people to Awaken, because the more there are, the less stable the system becomes (see: Paradox). All spells are imposing Abyssal Unreality upon the Real World, with the only difference being some are sanctioned and rendered less chaotic.

                                                This is inspired by, and similar to, the Secrets of Shangri-la in the Mage Chronicler's Guide.

                                                Another idea is to play with Mage Sight and Gnosis. Make Mage Sight basically always on with Gnosis as it's Opacity. Effort can be spent to Focus on it (like currently with Active Mage Sight), but as Gnosis climbs, it intrudes more and more into a Mage's normal perceptions. At Gnosis 5, its overlapping a Mage's senses at 50%, making separating the two annoying, but hardly impossible. But at Gnosis 9-10? They might not even be able to perceive reality as anything other than collections of symbols. That would, I think, be alienating from humanity.

                                                Malkydel: "And the Machine dictated; let there be adequate illumination."
                                                Yossarian: "And lo, it was optimal."
