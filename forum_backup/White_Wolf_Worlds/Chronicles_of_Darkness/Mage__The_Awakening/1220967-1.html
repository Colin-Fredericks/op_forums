            Look at it this way. You’ve got magic, yeah? You touched infinity and saw the world the way it should be. And now you’ve got to figure out a way to live with it without breaking the minds of everyone around you and/or summoning up an abomination from the deep places.

            You could try to make yourself a finer man. To hone your sword-arm and use your magic to be yourself, but more. And if you were to run into some Byzantine cavalryman with a tattoo of arrows hidden on his arm, you’d recognize him as a brother.

            You could witness something called up that cannot be put down, a shrieking thing of bones and skin. It opens your eyes; you need to be careful, and you need to keep your gift from hurting others (and to stop things like that from doing the same). And if you were to run into some Briton who speaks of labyrinths, you would understand his meaning.

            You could go out and gather the secret and hidden things of the world, and follow wherever they point you, and when you come across a monastery with a second library, one opened by a word in no earthly tongue, you would embrace the Christian who keeps the mysteries safe.

            Or you could seek to use your power to make men wiser, to make yourself a jarl, to point men towards the enlightenment you’ve seen. Maybe you think of it as climbing the world tree, but the symbol of the climb- that is universal.

            One improvement I’ve seen in Mage 2e moving forward is the idea that the Diamond Orders aren’t Proto-Atlantean-High-Secrets but, rather, groups of likeminded people that coalesce around the same symbols and ideals, and eventually retrofit the idea of Atlantis to explain these very human drives when faced with the gift of enlightenment: self-improvement, secrecy, study, and leadership. And when you meet someone from a foreign sect, those similarities will be more important than the regional differences.

            Also them Vikings were all over the Mediterranean, you know what I mean?

                                                            Well, a lot of those Darshana/Nameless cults joined the Orders because, by that point, the Orders have resources. They're by definition a group of people from other cultures who have united and share their Artifacts, Grimoires, Masters, Mysteries, cults, and knowledge. They would be a lot of resistance, so any assimilation happening would still take place over generations.
