    The House Rule Thread

    06-10-2017, 08:11 AM
    I wanted to start a thread where we can discuss house rules, since I feel like Mage tends to lend itself to these more than most other gamelines. A couple of things I wanted to get out there before we start, though:

    This is mostly for actual house rules, not rules interpretations - allowing Death to heal probably isn't a house rule so much as an interpretation. A house rule in this context would be, for example, placing Gnosis caps. Additionally, this isn't the place to argue about someone else's house rule.

    I'll start:

    At our table, we don't allow any bonuses that aren't Yantras or Ritual bonuses to factor into the spell when determining maximum spell factors. You can stack spell factors all you want until your total dice pool after accounting for Gnosis + Arcana + Ritual Bonus + Yantras - Spell Factors is -5, but no further.

    After that's done, any additional bonuses (From Fate spells, Conditions, Merits, etc) is applied.

    This tends to curb any kind of ridiculous bonus stacking.

      I have a house rule regarding Shifting Sands and kind of a house-rule for Hallows:
      Shifting Sands requires 1 point of Mana with each use and travelling back a full scene requires 1 additional point of mana. The flow of mana at a Hallow is also constant regardless of Time Manipulation, thus you cannot gain mana from a Hallow more than once using Shifting Sands.

      This keeps people with the initial Time 3 from just saying, "Alright guys with my 1 Dot Hallow I will get full mana."

      "Teamwork makes the dream work!"

          One minor hack is to cap magical healing - you can "patch over" damage, but you can only magically heal up to your Health total (these caps fall off at the same rate natural healing happens).

          Proximus hacks:
          • Proximi can become closer to full Mages through incremental improvement (see the Homebrew Mage Merits thread for details).
            • Dynastic Heir and Arcane Prodigy can increase effect Gnosis and Arcana, respectively.
            • Wise Sleepwalker lets Proximi try to contain their Paradoxes.
            • Spell Control and Mystic Tools let you sustain more spells, and use Yantras, respectively.
            • Attaining Insight unlocks the Arcana based Attainments for Proximi (at least, for their Blessing Arcana).
          • Allow Proximi to pick up out-of-Dynasty Blessings at a surcharge (+1 XP for in-Arcana, +2 XP for out-of-Arcana). Always costs Mana, and granting any Free Reach is optional.
          • Proximi can incur Paradox normally (the Cancel-Blessing-and-Aggravate-Curse is an ST option).
          • Proximi don't risk Paradox (closer to Legacy Attainments), but exceeding 1 Reach causes Strain - Reduce Willpower by 1 per, recovering at 1 per day.
          • Link Effective Gnosis to Integrity on the same scale as Nephilim and Primum - Glitches correspond to Nimbus Conditions, culminating in Mad-like status (like Second-hand Secrecy).
          • Give each Proximus Dynasty some Boon - either a passive bonus to enforce its concept, some trick like bonus Potency/Duration or extra Reach for using their Blessings in theme, or another flavor-enhancing perk.


          Another one for cross-over is intended to limit Mage interaction with others and make Sleepwalkers more valuable and special: No Free Sleepwalking - Unless the being is 1) A Mage, Proximus, or possesses Wisdom 2) an Ephemeral Entity 3) Never human or 4) Actually has the Sleepwalker or Fitful Slumber Merit, they count as a Sleeper. Vampire? Sleeper. Changeling? Sleeper. Mummy? Sleeper. Sin-Eater? Sleeper (the Geist isn't, but the human is, which can lead to interesting conversations). Demons, Werewolves, and Prometheans are tricky. This serves to isolate Mages more, and make someone who doesn't cause Dissonance significant.

                                          The only real house rule I have in my game is for identifying spells as they're cast. The others are just updates of 1e rules (especially Astral mechanics).

                                          I made this rule quite a while ago, back when I was first learning mage. Now that I think about how improvised spells and rotes work, these rules may not actually make sense. Identifying spells usually requires decoding it as a Mystery.

                                          Identifying Spells
                                          You must have active mage sight of the correct Arcanum (or Prime sight). Roll Gnosis + Arcanum to identify the spell as a reflexive action for Instant spells, or an extended action for ritual spells (time of each roll = your ritual interval).
                                          • The number of successes required for identifying an instant spell is equal to the dots of the highest Arcanum of the spell.
                                          • The number of successes required to identify a ritual spell is equal to the dots of the highest Arcanum used in the spell multiplied by the number of ritual intervals used by the caster(s).
                                          Dice pool modifiers:
                                          • Add +1 for Prime sight.
                                          • Add +1 for Mastery of the highest arcanum involved.
                                          • Add +1 for Archmastery of the highest arcanum involved (stacks with Mastery bonus).
                                          • Add +1 if your Gnosis is above 5.
