        I find myself thinking that, given Marduk's position in the setting, the main determining factor of what he would have been shouldn't be in trying to derive something from all the minor details provided but what makes a compelling outcome to any modern day characters investigating the Mystery of him.

        That does allow for the possibility of uncovering evidence of a Bronze Age society governed by a spirit that had managed to start running along some unconventional lines or a Supernal entity who was fixed without being Bound.

        Still, I'm the type who's generally inclined to lean Mysteries in the direction of "more between Heaven and Earth than dreamt of in your philosophies" more often than not. My own priority for the outcome of investigating the Mystery of how an ancient society was governed by a supernatural entity would be to fold that into a larger Mystery of how and why so many types of supernatural entity, including ones difficult to categorise, could govern openly in a different era of the world, and what changed. The outcome of the question of "what was Marduk" becomes "why is Marduk not anymore".

        (Even if on a practical level the tone and scope of a lot of Chronicles games is likely to render the initial conclusions about Marduk as an unsettling hanging question that never gets resolved.)

                                I think it's pretty safe to say from practical, tactical, and gameline-historical perspectives that the threshold for "open supernatural rule" is just "this place is run by a supernatural being of some kind and this is a matter of unhidden public knowledge" — Marduk possessing the kings of Babylon in the earlier part of his rule doesn't remove the strategic/social pressures that led the Kindred of Transylvania to spread word of the banes of the Strix that bedeviled them, and Irem is right there as an example where even their military supremacy wasn't a matter of having zombie armies or wings of fire so much as knowing how to use troop formations and proper metallurgy to train and arm their mortal soldiers (with relics being a subtler and more portable/less vital force multiplier to be deployed where necessary).

                                To borrow from Vampire's usual line of thought that derives into the Masquerade centuries later, it's one thing for your subjects to know you have magic powers and a whole different thing for them to know you're a specific subtype of monster with particular weaknesses and limitations that can be exploited. The pretenses of omniscience and immortality alone go a long way when attached to state power.
