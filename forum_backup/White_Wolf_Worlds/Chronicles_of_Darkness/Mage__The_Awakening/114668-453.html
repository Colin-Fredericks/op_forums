    Originally posted by saibot View Post
    Usually when a spell fails because of Withstand in my games, it is because the player actually forgot to even consider Withstand when preparing the spell. Otherwise they usually raise it more than high enough to get past any Withstand. What empe999 says about Withstand on debuffs is true though. The system does not lend itself very well to debuffs because they are relatively ineffectual. In many cases buffing your own is going to translate to roughly the same impact as debuffing the enemy, but the former is much easier. Of course not all Arcana offer buffs for every situation, while debuffs are more widespread over the Arcana.
    When you buff a character, it means only they become a bigger threat with their attacks that round. But debuff the giant spirit and it will have the same effect as buffing the entire cabal and their allies, should they focus attacks on it, all while requiring lower scale on the cast. I imagine that Withstand combined with the spell stacking rules are there to discourage debuffing as a first order strategy, especially since its not a particularly inventive one.

                        Originally posted by galivet View Post
                        Suppose your players want to found a new Nameless Order. What would you make them go through to define Order Tools that function as yantras?
                        I would make it into a 3 dot merit called Nameless Order (X):

                        1 dot: Membership grants a free dot in Occult
                        2 dots: Gain a specialty in an Order skill (like Sculpting for crafts)
                        3 dots: Gain a 2 dot merit

                        Beyond that would require them to become a full Diamond Order. In other words, "4 dots" means Order sized organization and "5 dots" means there is probably someone in the archmage weight class backing the organization.

                        Should they wish to progress to a full global conspiracy type of Order, they need to define what they think magic is and then explore its Mysteries until they link to its symbolism. The 3 main methods that come to mind are:

                        1- Reclamation: Find Supernal proof of your thesis, likely by looking into a lost ruin, Verge, Emanation, etc.

                        2- Illumination: Have a mighty patron (archmage, Kerberos, True Fae, etc) give it weight with their involvement. Beating/tricking a True Fae, having an archmage perfect the idea (this is how the Mysterium was created)

                        3- Corroboration: Make their thesis into Truth by an event of enough magnitude to make the Supernal accept it, like The Great Refusal (aka create a Crowning Moment of Awesome)

                        At the end of the day, Orders have a lot of adherents and history powering their symbolism, making something with as much metaphysical weight should probably be a challenge.

                                                            Originally posted by galivet View Post
                                                            So for an extended action, when aiming for "apply the exceptional success result of the action when you complete your goal," once a player has met the required number of successes but still has rolls remaining, can the player simply spend more time rolling to accrue an exceptional success so long as she doesn't exceed the maximum number of rolls?
                                                            No, because once you've crossed the threshold of required successes the extended action is over, and also because an exceptional success is a result on a singular roll. You can no more get an exceptional success by rolling five successes over two or more rolls than you can damage a Durability 3 object barehanded by rolling two successes on two separate Brawl rolls.

                                                            Or does "apply the exceptional success result of the action when you complete your goal" require an exceptional success on the final roll where the player achieved the required number of successes?
                                                            The latter.

                                                            If the latter is true: Suppose the player requires two more successes for an extended action. On her next roll, the player achieves 5 success. Is that an exceptional success? Or did she need 7 successes for an exceptional success?
                                                            Historically, it's varied, because this is a particular exceptional success result rather than a general rule for extended actions. In the context of the 2e ruleset, the latter would be necessary for an action that specified you needed to exceed the required success count, but getting five successes on the last roll would still produce the normal results of an exceptional success on an extended action (speedier completion/lowered threshold/Condtion).
