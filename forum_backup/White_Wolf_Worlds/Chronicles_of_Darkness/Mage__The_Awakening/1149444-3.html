    I feel like Mage is easily the NWoD/ChroD system I've played with the most moving parts. I've probably played nearly a decade of the Storyteller system, playing almost every other gameline and running more than my fair share of mortals, changeling, hunter and demon games. And I have been playing Mage 2E for about a year now bi-weekly and I still feel completely overwhelmed by the system. My wife is also completely incapable of engaging with the mechanics, despite us greatly enjoying the game that we're playing.

    My personal issues with learning the system can probably come down to a few points:
    - There's too much jargon that's not mentally easy to differentiate. Why are there so many sub-classes of Yantra? None of us can figure out the various magical tool requirements. Why both praxis and rote? Why use the term rote when rote is already a core NWoD system diceroll mechanic? "Lasting" vs "Persistent"? I can't keep my head around it. And I have a chemistry PhD, which was years of dealing with jargon absolutely everywhere.
    - Too many subsystems and they probably could use a severe pruning. Why does the nimbus system exist? Mage sight is my best example here - did it really need three levels? Did it need more mechanics for adding extra sight arcana? Did it need to exist as subsystem compared to Unveiling arcana?
    - I never can remember if I need mana or not, I never understand the process of regaining mana outside of pattern scouring which seems straightforward enough, plus I never seemed to understand if you could actually draw mana out of stuff without significant investment in prime, but, that seems to require mana too because not-primary arcana?
    - I feel the example spells have made the process of learning the improvised spell system more confusing rather than helpful, because of a whole bunch that do things like "well, this particular spell needs a mana" or "this particular effect seems to need a reach", it feels like it's added a bunch of complexity to places where the system could have just gone "+1 reach for extra conditional/fine control on any spell" rather than having to reference this one effect and one sub-effect that specifically needs reach.
    - There's just a lot to keep track of in general. It's hard not to get caught in decision paralysis.
    - The expectation of ritual casting is stifling, it makes it feel like it's hard to react to things as they happen in game and that you have to prepare everything hours beforehand. It can arrest the progress of the game I suppose? We've been having trouble with pacing.

    My wife's had troubles with the system in these sorts of ways:
    - Jargon is absolutely killer for her, the linguistic complexity of the terms is extreme even compared to most of the other NWOD.
    - The improvisation process for spellcasting is completely lost on her so much so that our ST has basically gone to "work out what you want to try and do and I'll work out the spell for you", but it results in her never using any improvised spells, basically.
    - Her concept is a crafter/magic itemsmith and she still doesn't understand how she's actually supposed to do this...and the rest of the table haven't really worked it out either.

    The table as a whole has been just having problems too:
    - No one ever can remember how paradox works or at what points we're supposed to be invoking it and how, even with the quickcasting sheets.
    - The system's complexity means that we're basically completely ignoring conditions because there's too much to keep track of. Which of course results in us not getting more than 2-3 beats a session total.
    - Generic issue with aspirations/obsessions that unless they're focused very specifically on a group activity of the session we pre-agree to most of us only resolve maybe 1 a session at most, because any personal character aspirations aren't particularly viable unless the player gets a solo spot for a scene or two.

    But those last two aren't really Mage's fault, just a general difficulty with 2E in general.

    I've personally been reading the book, my wife never reads game books because she doesn't learn from them well but it has been a huge struggle compared to all the other WoDs to even get that feeling of being baseline competent with the system and using it to actually accomplish anything. It feels like the complexity of the system is oppressive - I get that once mastery of the system is obtained the system is likely stupidly powerful, but at a Gnosis 4/Arcana 4 mage I feel less powerful and capable as a character than I ever did as a starting Changeling or Demon or Vampire or whatever simply due to how opaque the whole thing feels at times.

                                        Yantras are divided into categories because there's a limit to how many dice you can get from a single one. You can exceed this by using multiple categories, which you're gonna need to do for really big spells.

                                        Also the easiest way to cast a permanent spell is to make the duration the primary factor. Some have that by default and others you need to spend a reach. This means you bump up the duration by arcarnum-1, which if you're arcarnum 4 takes care of 6 dice of those penalties. Now, you can probably manage to cast at 4 dice but a few yantras aren't gonna hurt here, especially if you want to increase other factors.

                                        If you're casting ritually you can also extend the ritual by it's base time (in the case of gnosis 4, an hour) for an extra die up to 5 times. So in this case a 6 hour ritual would yield the maximum bonus.
