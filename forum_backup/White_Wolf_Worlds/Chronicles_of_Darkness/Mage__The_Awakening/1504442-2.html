                                Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
                                What stops a concerted effort from Mages to use Space and destroy Vampires in a city?
                                My answer would be: "Not much, but mages as a whole and each individual Mage usually have better things to do."

                                First of all, Awakened are not single nation yet. In fact, there is vast schism between those who think that serving Supernal tyrant-gods is abhorrent, those who think it's a pretty sweet deal, and those who busy exploiting magic in all fun and horrible ways while first two groups duke it out. In most cases, when Pentacle mages band together in numbers to solve problem, that problem is usually somehow tied to other mages - Seers, Banishers, Scelesti, Tremere, list goes on... - or other sublime powers who are actually a large enough problem for Mages to solve collectively. If Pentacle and Seers are able to agree in any given geographical area that vampires need to go and stay go - yeah, I imagine vampire population would take a nosedive there. But most of the time, they won't be able to agree, and will actually support vampires fighting the other side, waging a proxy war. So let's drop "all the Mages" and look at single cabal or mage.

                                Second of all, Awakened are usually not interested in waging war on vampires. Most of them have their Obsessions; said Obsessions take most of their time and attention, not leaving much of them to wage genocidal interspecies wars. Of course, there are mages whose Obsessions include vampires, usually as objects of research and maybe dissection, but those mages are not interested in wiping them all out; on the contrary, I would except them to take measure so there is a lot of vampires they can abduct and study. There is a possibility of mage wanting to kill vampire on principle - as act of vengeance, for example. But it's more likely that said mage won't find a lot of support from other Awakened; at best, he would need to wage his crusade on his own; in the worst case, Awakened themselves will take notice and take act to avoid unnecessary conflict. It's not fun spending time doing Sanctum repairs after a band of bloodthirsty vampires tried to destroy it because some other warlock somewhere have taken his tantrum a bit too far.

                                Third, while Awakened are very powerful and become much more powerful with a little imagination and effort (who needs a sympathetic casting, when you can combine Outward and Inward Eye with Zoom In to just sit in your room and fry vampires with sensory-range spells while sitting in your Sanctum?), they are not all-powerful. Other posters have brought up various ways in which vampires can respond, but it's very safe to say - no matter how powerful single mage is, going against whole organizations of supernatural beings is a quest doomed to failure, sooner or later. This doesn't mean there are no such mages, and that does not mean they can't kill a lot of vampires before going down in flames... but sooner or later they will.

                                    But the point of the thread is precisely why they have that opinion in the first place?
                                    It probably pays to not reverse cause and effect. This thread didn't emerge from reading The Silver Ladder and Free Council supplements and then ask the question as a logical extension of what those books said about vampires, this thread emerged because Ib strongly dislikes vampires and beasts and expected the two orders with the most superficial interest in Sleepers to share in that opinion and to act on it, and happened to have text that supported that take.

                                    I realize that sounds like nitpicking, but It's important to see how Lysander's comment is highly relevant, because the leaders of the Awakened community aren't taking their stances out of a gut moral-emotional feeling, but out of a philosophical and ideological stance-and if there's been a beat across a lot of the posts on this thread, it's been the reminder than the Pentacle is not as good as actually acting on their high-minded ideals and ambitions as they want you to think they are. Wiping out vampires is about on par with actually convening a Grand Convocation-great idea on paper, kind of too hard to actually feasibly do when there are a million other things these mages could be doing. Sure, there's gonna be a cabal now and then who really gets into the idea and does something about it, but likely their doing so is more of a major disruption that is more likely to get smoothed out rather than fully supported.

                                    Mages are generally intruding bastards whose presence does a lot to destabilize the local supernatural ecosystem, and it's important to highlight just how often that disruption is more of a passive consequence than a concerted effort, the product of the ignorance and arrogance of people who are both powerful and pretentious about their relationship to the world. The positions of the Ladder and the Council on vampries is probably best read in that light rather than trying to wrestle with obtuse angles of reading.
                                    ​
                                    Originally posted by Michael View Post
                                    But this returns us to the original issue of the thread. It's a handwave; using narrative to paper over mechanics. (To be clear that's not a criticism, I think it's just the natural result of the way the game is constructed.)
                                    Having just properly ingested this, I have to say I find it weird to posit that we should take the supplements at their word for their stance on vampires but then posit that their explanation for why they don't actually act on that stance is a handwave. It's one thing for someone (me, I mean me) to posit that the games have softly retconned that bit of text (and yes, the onus is on me to prove that, and yes, there's no direct text for support, so uphill battle for me, yay), it's another to say "read this bit seriously, but not that bit seriously". If we're going to deal with the text as the text, if we're going to take The Silver Ladder and the Free Council at their word in their books, then shouldn't we be taking them at their word?

                                    Narrative and mechanics are not unrelated-in fact, narrative is often effectively just the softest layer of implementation. THe mechanical reason why the Ladder and Council don't go to war with the Kindred is because it's not really feasible or useful to try. If we take them at their word, we have to work backwards from that stance to justify it. If we don't, then we might as well just admit we're in a bit of canon that needs modern citation.

                                    Like, yes, there are things that aren't problems per se so much as a consequence of construction, but if we must use the whole apple, then the whole apple we must use. There's been a reason why I tend to center conversation on Awakening more centrally on Second Edition and the books that directly lead to it (Imperial Mysteries and Left Hand Path, as defined by Brookshaw), and it's not just because I can pull up those books instead of relying on my memory-the whole apple is no longer the most coherent fruit.

                                            I have a preference for interpreting what it means for a mage to be able to sense a subject for the purposes of casting at sensory range a bit strictly, so in a lot of cases casting an area spell over a very large range isn't going to cut it. It can tell a person where a subject is, but they'll have to move in closer to get a good shot.

                                            The Space Arcanum is referenced in the first post, but it's not particularly relevant; you don't have sympathetic connections to strangers, and you need to get within sensory range to cast the spell that creates them wholecloth.

                                            I'm also inclined to play up the extent to which mages are averse to using the Art for murder. That even when fighting one another, there's a preference to use spells to hone in on a target's location and then employ weapons. Saves one the experience of forming an imago precisely calibrated to end a life.

                                            Although on that note, you'd be talking about an Adept to practically do it in the first place, and it's not exactly as though those are supposed to be common. If you're using Fraying spells, that's either a crazy amount of Potency to make sure you get it on the first go or constantly battering them with spells to do bashing damage, which has to be even more disturbing and traumatic to anybody not a sociopath or really battle hardened to get done (and if you're a battle hardened Pentacle mage you're probably somebody well acquainted with how slowly and brutally beating a target to death with your soul is something that sullies you).

                                            Oh, also, constantly hitting a vampire with bashing damage is kind of useless, because even if they don't have the Resilience Discipline to reduce the amount of damage they take, it's not particularly difficult for them to spend Vitae to regenerate that kind of damage, and if you drive them into a corner there's a good chance they'll either wilfully go around draining whatever humans are close to hand or be driven into Frenzy to do it and do you see why Acts of Hubris are predicated on the idea of mages not thinking things through?

                                            Also, do none of these vampires have Obfuscate? I would say Cloak of Night ought to be sufficient to force Clash of Wills against spells to detect the presence of something.

                                            It's admittedly a bit of dealer's choice on whether or not a vampire who has retreated into an Unmarked Grave can be targeted in their own right or if a mage attacker is forced to play by the rules of damaging the material that they've merged into, but if one does assume the latter then it kind of limits the mage's options if they're doing it purely through spells from afar.

                                            For the vampire who knows they've come under attack and is has made a decent go of appropriate countermeasures, a bit of Auspex might be useful for finding things out about who did it and where they are, and make some headway on finding their vulnerabilities to strike back. I would even say it can be reasonable for the one-dot power of that Discipline to alert a vampire that they're about to come under some form of distant supernatural attack, which can afford an opportunity to ready defences if they have them.

                                            That's before considering Devotions. There are a lot of directions a vampire's power can go in with Devotions, to increase their sturdiness, their stealth, their capacity to hone in on things targeting them with extrasensory means. Sure, not every vampire will have the appropriate powers, but if you don't know then it's the kind of thing which is why a mage's mentors will typically impress upon them the value of knowing what you're going after before flinging deadly magic around.

                                            Hey, if you're a mid-level mage, how much Reach is all of this costing you? Enough to be risking some Paradox? Is this kind of thing worth the level of injury you might be bestowing upon yourself, the Conditions you might incur? Oh what's that, you didn't contain these Paradoxes? Yeah, doing that sort of thing at a distance or over an area is a paddlin'.

                                            I would think that these are some assumptions to mean that going to war with other supernatural beings is not done lightly, for the cabal that might have an axe to grind. And it is most likely a cabal. Mage society is very atomized, Order caucuses are not generally depicted as organized to the extent that lots of them would be thoroughly organised on a project that is even relevant to their core ethos, let alone something as tangential as this.

                                            And it all emerges from an assumption that mages are very prejudicial. I wouldn't say that most of them know very much about vampires at all, but I'd say there's enough there that your peers will view you as very quaint for not having learned that they're basically just people. Viewing the relationship between vampires and humans as literally parasitic is fairly crude, because a tick is not somebody who can take its host out for fun nights on the town, or have been a family member prior to becoming a tick, and having your blood sucked by those creatures doesn't cause a deep sense of release. Vampires aren't parasites, they're dysfunctional relationships, and if a mage is going to go around killing everybody who is doing shit like that they're going to start making observable gaps in the population of a city.

                                            Sure, some vampires go way worse, but again, that's why Awakened society thinks you're better off learning more of the finer points of things you want to go to war with. Like, if the existence of certain vampires who are doing some really dark and grim stuff is so offensive to you, that's your business, but casting large scale spells all over with the intent of finding and killing all of them and letting God sort them out is worse than morally dubious, it's sloppy, and the Awakened look askance at their fellows when they're sloppy because sloppy Supernal magic shits where they eat.
