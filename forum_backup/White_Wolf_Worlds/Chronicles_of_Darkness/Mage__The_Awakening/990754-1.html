    See, the problem with that is that is makes the Supernal into too much of a defined place for me, and it also steps on the toes of the assorted other lines. The Shadow is where predator symbols eat prey symbols, it's the Shadow's hat. The Underworld is where the dead go. To give another example, if Supernal Arcadia is literally just a dimension with elves and stuff, that makes Phenomenal Arcadia kind of redundant, plus the unfortunate "the Supernal world is the Fallen world but better and purer" idea, which I really don't think 2e is supporting much.

    To go more into the narrative purpose argument, and away from Watsonianism, the problem with the Supernal being a place where "predator symbols eat prey symbols" from a worldbuilding and storytelling standpoint is that the game already has a perfectly fine and fleshed out place where embodiments of predation eat embodiments of prey, and it has the bonus of being a place that mages can travel too fairly easily that you can easily work into campaigns and adventures.

    The Watsonian argument against it would what I mentioned earlier, about how the Supernal is a formless sea of potential without the Phenomenal to shape it.

                        So, after ruminating on the question for a few days and reading everyone's answers, my own response has coalesced.

                        My take on Supernal entities has to be that they're not really entities at all, in the typical sense of the word. They are more akin to demesnes or Verges or Emanation Realms; manifestations of Supernal law, in an exceptionally narrow and confined manner.

                        The Supernal is not a separate universe or other worlds or another plane of being - at least, not in the classical sense of being able to imagine it as a place filled with objects and places you can walk about in. It is the structure of reality; it is the aggregated symbols that give the universe form and meaning. Symbols are things for which any action is an expression of itself. That is, a symbol is a Platonic essence; The Thing Itself. It cannot be meaningfully subdivided.

                        Symbols, thus, do what their nature commands; interacting with all other symbols to produce reality. The Exarchs send dreams and visions and trinkets because it is what an Exarch does; the Tyrant is not a Tyrant if it does not command its lessers. Storms manifest around destruction, for it is what storms do. Cats are graceful and everyone senses the presence of feline grace even in entirely different species, because the very idea of a Cat is bound into grace intimately.

                        So when summoning a Supernal entity, one is not pulling out a symbol or creating an entity or what have you; one is simply emphasizing and enhancing a Supernal symbol's influence over a segment of reality; just as one might with a spell, save with exceptional clarity and purity. This singular symbol is given control over a localized region of reality and it does what symbols do when given control: It changes the world around it. The way it does so is by shaping the reality it's been given influence over. When it does so, this just inevitably means it takes on forms and shapes sufficient to appear as an entity.

                        But it's not the symbol. It's just the symbol given more influence.

                            Originally posted by Axelgear View Post
                            Another way to frame the question: What is a Supernal being doing when it's not summoned into the Fallen World?
                            My take would be: something we can't understand.

                            For me, summoning is not coalescing symbols as much as cutting parts of greater symbols and mixing them up. But the "greater" symbol stays active. A mage needs to do it that way because he can't understand the full scope of a true Supernal symbol.

                            For instance, when you call upon a supernal being in the form of three ladies with a single eye, you do not take "death" and "fate" and make them into people. You take one tiny part of Fate, one tiny part of Death and mix them into something adequate for your mind to grasp without your brain melting through your ears. (And that explains why, for instance, you can have Fate as an arcanum and Fate as it is understood by the changelings)

                            So the summons are created by the mage's mind to be able to interact with the symbol power. But in the supernal, the symbols act according to their true nature which is far beyond anything your street level mage can understand. And for me the division between symbols is, in part, arbitrary: "death" and "life" aren't opposite symbols. They are opposite "poles" on a spectrum that comprises a greater symbol. Think of it as your average country: inside the country, several lobbies will try to stir the country in some way. Businessmen will try to lower the taxes while public servant will try to have more money for their work. So they might appear, to someone who look at a street level, as opposed to each other as it is posible. But if you look at the level of a chief of state, a coherence and a dynamic appear. So for me, in the Supernal, symbols are acting in ways human can barely understand and in accordance to their nature, while respecting a sort of equilibrium.
