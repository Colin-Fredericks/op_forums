                        Originally posted by lnodiv View Post
                        I think it would be cool to see those Astral mechanic updates, too.
                        This is what I have for the Astral Realms. It probably needs some polishing. My players don't really ever use them, because they're too lazy to read my wiki! I basically went through the Astral Realms book, made a list of what mages can do in the Oneiros, and then attempted to 2e-ify it.

                        The Oneiros:
                        • Paradox: Spells cast in a mage's own Oneiros never cause Paradox, as long as the spell doesn't leave the Oneiros. Spells cast in a Sleeper's Oneiros always have a base of one Paradox Die. Mind spells benefit from -2 to Paradox in the Astral (2e core rules).
                        • Mental Practice: Mages can work on picking up mental merits and skills by running through scenes in their Oneiros. Reading a book on Survival puts it all in the mage's subconscious, and then the mage can practice it in their Oneiros. Performing these rituals gives the Mental Practices condition.
                        • Subconscious Investigation: Memory-based scenes can be used to solve puzzles and re-examine clues that a mage might have missed the first time. Using the Oneiros as the basis for an extended Investigation, Occult, or research roll grants the Astral Investigation condition. A common use of this is to peruse information on a topic, and then delve deep into it in the Astral to gain the research bonus.
                        • Becoming Wiser: Mages can delve into their Oneiros to meditate upon Wisdom, either to help regain it or help prevent further degeneration. If the mage has the Obsession "Becoming wiser," they can use the Oneiros as a basis for working towards regaining wisdom (core book p. 88) by replaying through scenes in their head where they were Unwise and recognizing their faults.
                        • Wisdom Meditation: By focusing on past hubris and rationalizing the reasons for doing it, a mage can meditate within the Oneiros to gain the Wisdom Meditation condition, strengthening their resolve against further Wisdom degeneration.
                        • Enlightenment: Self-enlightenment is a common reason for mages to wander their own Oneiroi. They wander the realms of daydreams, Virtue, Vice, and Nightmare looking for shortcuts within their soul that move them closer to their Supernal Realm. Journeys like these grant 1 Beat or Arcane Beat, depending on the nature of the journey. Journeys of magical enlightenment grant Arcane Beats, and journeys to overcome personal flaws or challenges grant regular Beats.
                        • Removing Mental Conditions: Entering a Derangement Realm and overcoming it allow mages to remove an ongoing mental condition and gain a Beat for doing so. Such realms are dangerous, as anyone with the condition is helpless to resist it while there. Elder mages advise less-experienced mages to stay away from these realms. Note that true insanity and delusions reach into the Madness Realms of the Temenos, so curing these conditions requires venturing beyond the Oneiros.
                        • Magical Alteration: Mages can use Supernal magic within Oneiroi (their own or others') to alter personalities, memories, and emotions. This is similar to the spell "Psychic Reprogramming," but works on different principles. For example, using Matter to destroy a strong bridge that represents the victim's confidence makes the victim timid and nervous. This usually imposes mental conditions upon the victim, which resolve normally. These effects last as long as the spells do, unless the conditions are resolved earlier. The magical alteration can be detected, but only if investigating the Oneiros.
                        • Deep Alteration: Mages can look into Oneiroic Vaults for memories and defining personality traits, and even manipulate them through the use of Oneiroic symbolism. Memories a person remembers strongly that are tied tight to their unconscious mind are there in the open for travelers to find and experience. Otherwise, the traveler must enter the Vault. Mages can dig up memories to learn things about the person, or manipulate them to grant the Resurfaced Memories, Suppressed Memories, or Locked Memories conditions to the person whose Oneiros they are in.
                        The Temenos:
                        • Meeting Places: Some mages use realms of the Temenos as safe meeting places. Hiding in the hundreds of thousands of realms in the Temenos is an effective way to communicate safely, especially if it's within a realm of one's own creation. Mages that enter the Temenos together can all fulfill the condition of a Temenos realm simultaneously and end up there together.
                        • Creating Realms: The Temenos is at heart about narrative, and so every new narrative that is shared by two or more people exists somewhere in the Temenos. These narratives can be novels, plays, movies, songs, paintings, urban legends or, even TV shows. If a work is sufficiently powerful and original, once others see or hear it, this new narrative spontaneously generates a realm in the Temenos. If the audience is exceedingly small, the realm has few connections with the rest of the Temenos and may become an isolated realm. Mages create realms in the Temenos for myriad purposes. A simple example is a private space for cabals to meet without fear of being spied upon. [some text paraphrased from 1e Astral Realms book]
                        • Altering the Fallen World: Altering the Temenos can have effects in the Fallen. Want people to stop watching a TV show? Enter the Temenos and kill the main characters. On some level, people will think the show dead and ratings will drastically decrease. The roll to manipulate the Temenos is extended, usually paired with appropriate traits. A single manipulation can never affect more than 10 million people, and it increases in difficult the greater the scale is. See below for more information.
                          • These rules are basically a straight port from the 1e Astral Realms book, so no point in listing all the modifiers and required successes here.
                        • Finding Information: The Temenos is home to ideas long-lost to humanity. If a mage wants to learn how to make Greek Fire, they can probably find out in the Temenos. If a mage needs to find the one nugget of truth in an insane conspiracy theory, they can enter a Madness Realm.
                        • Curing Insanity: The insane build connections to Madness Realms and then absorb parts of them into their own Oneiros. By entering the madness realm as it's being absorbed into a person's Oneiros, and then building connections from the Oneiros to more grounded concepts in the Temenos, a mage can cure insanity or similar conditions.
                        Astral Conditions: These Conditions are granted by performing actions in the Oneiros (and sometimes the Temenos). They cover the special rules required for the various actions a mage can do in the Astral Realms.

                        Mental Practices
                        You are actively on a journey within your Oneiros to learn a new mental skill or merit they meet the prerequisites for. The journey is symbolic of the skill or merit in question, and you must overcome challenges related to it in order to succeed. This condition temporarily grants the minimum dots in the skill or merit if you don't already have it. Otherwise, it grants the next step up. If you journey out of the scene before overcoming the challenges, this condition and the skill or merit it grants are immediately lost. The temporary dots can only be used within the context of the scene: for example, the mage cannot take this condition to learn the Investigation skill and then replay memories in her head to reexamine a crime scene in her past (though she could certainly do so after buying a dot in Investigation).
                        • Beat: When you attempt to use the skill or merit, but fail.
                        • Resolution: The challenges are fully overcome.
                        Astral Investigation
                        You are actively on a journey within your or another's Oneiros to reexamine past evidence, gain insights you might have missed, or otherwise re-investigate something. The scene that takes place is the memory itself, replaying out as the mage first experienced it. If the mages notices different things, or takes a different path, they might notice something they missed the first time. This condition grants a +1 to +3 Equipment bonus to Investigate or Occult rolls. It requires that the mage have viewed or experienced the situation they wish to (re-)investigate. If in another's Oneiros, that person must be present with the mage to assist the investigation. The mage may run through these scenes as many times as they want, but resolving the condition only grants Beats if a new insight is gained and on the final time when the mage realizes there are no more insights left.
                        • Resolution: When the astral investigation is complete and a new insight is gained, or the mage realizes there are no more insights to be found.
                        Wisdom Meditation
                        By examining past experiences of Wisdom degeneration and meditating on their rationalization at the time, and then realizing how it might not have been the strongest of rationalizations, or even realizing that it was a very strong rationalization, the mage protects themselves from further wisdom degeneration. While this condition is active, it grants a +1 die bonus to the mage's next Wisdom degeneration roll. The condition lapses after one Chapter, or after the roll is made, whichever comes first.
                        • Resolution: When you fail your next wisdom degeneration roll.
                        Resurfaced Memories
                        Symbolic manipulations to the Oneiros have forced a memory to come back to the forefront. It works best with memories already accessible to the consciousness and generally makes the process of remembering and thinking just a little bit less stressful. While this condition is active, it grants a +1 die bonus on rolls relating to remembering things. It lapses after one Chapter.
                        • Resolution: Succeeding on a related memory roll while this condition is active.
                        Suppressed Memories
                        This condition is the opposite of the Resurfaced Memories condition. Oneiroic manipulation has made it harder to remember general things. It imposes a -1 die penalty on all rolls relating to memory. The condition lapses after one month.
                        • Resolution: Failing a memory roll while this condition is active.
                        Locked Memories
                        Some memories have been fully locked away via symbolic moving of the memories into a safe place in the Oneiros (a vault, chest, or something similar). While this condition is active, the character simply doesn't remember them. Attempts to read the mind of the character do not register the memories as there. The effects are also not magical, so spells that detect mental alterations will not register anything. This condition may also impose other conditions if the memories locked away are significant enough to alter personality. The condition lapses after one month.
                        • Resolution: When the memories are unlocked, or the condition lapses.
                        • Beat: When a forgotten memory causes a problem for the character.
                        Conflicted
                        The character has let loose inner demons long since locked away, and an ideology or morality that conflicts with the character's normal beliefs has taken root inside the character's soul. Past decisions are revisited, old addictions have returned, and wisdom of life choices is questioned. The character is torn between the path they've chosen and what could have been (or what could be). The character is prone to take actions they wouldn't normally, as they try to figure out what they truly want in life. This Condition does not arise from a person reconsidering their positions on an issue after argumentation or experience; rather, this drastic alteration in ideology comes from supernatural manipulation of the Oneiros.
                        • Resolution: You accept the conflicting stance, changing your ideology, or you manage to shake the fixation (which can require a journey into a Derangement realm).
                        • Beat: You take an action in line with the conflicting ideology.

                            I tossed some house rules into the Sleeper chapter because I wasn't satisfied with how that was handled. I preferred a less restrictive take on the "Sleepwalker" concept; rather than people with the Sleepwalker merit or a Supernatural Merit representing a power, I call any mortal a sleepwalker whose character concept is defined by connection to genuine supernatural forces.

                            This is a narrative /playability consideration rather than an in-world thing, but the in-setting excuse is that anyone who crosses that strange threshold into the supernatural underground is no longer susceptible to the Sleeping Curse, or Lunacy, etc. This has no effect on active powers (Mind magic, Concealment infrastructure, etc), just the passive "justifies a recognizably modern world of secret horrors" excuse stuff. More than just an internal power can rouse abate the quiescence.

                            This means...

                            Hunters on the Vigil, but not one-off survivors of a supernatural encounter.
                            Cultists of a bona fide Mystery Cult, but not just J. Random Branch Davidian.
                            A ritual magician with actual working thaumaturgical practices, but not someone who picked up a New Age spell book at Barnes & Noble.
                            Slashers on an Undertaking, not your friendly neighborhood mundane serial killer.
                            Anyone with a Supernatural Merit or Template of basically any kind; including wielders of mystical relics as long as they've somehow mastered or understood their operation.
                            Miscellaneous other weirdnesses; sometimes those one-off survivors of supernatural exposure use the XP to buy the Sleepwalker merit, etc.

                            This is mainly for playability and narrative purposes. I find it absurd to have werewolf hunters who forget they saw a werewolf, or witch-hunters who can't remember magic. I prefer to keep the number of big deal supernaturals fairly low and populate my games with "supporting cast" type people; it becomes irritating to have to contrive some excuse for why they're able to participate in the occult underground every time.

                            The idea is mostly that the shadow side of the World of Darkness has fuzzy boundaries with the mundane, and it's not that hard to step in to the occult world. It's hard to step back out, though; once seen, cannot be unseen, etc. Enough exposure, and you're in.

                                            I have been thinking more about the Sleeping Curse, and I think instead I'd prefer to handle it akin to the Lunacy rules in Werewolf 2nd. So it's an Integrity roll for the Sleeper observer, as usual, when they glimpse magic, with a Success, like Lunacy, meaning they "likely forget" but not necessarily forcing amnesia, though a failure does so - the amnesia being situational, but Dissonance and the like still proceed as usual.
