                                                        Originally posted by 21C Hermit View Post
                                                        What is this Parametric Language the Secret Order of the Gate is said to use? The word "parametric," at a glance, seems to have many different meanings, and I can't figure out which one the Order is supposed to be referring to.
                                                        Tessie's answer was what I remember from that section as well, in case you haven't had a chance to look into the matter more. Basic premise is that the language will say what something is not, instead of what it is. It doesn't sound like a very easy langauge to work with, so I imagine it has some sort of inconceivable trick that allows it to actually function, something a human mind could not cook up, but something a superintelligence like the lonely exarch could.

                                                        If you're familiar with the greek philosopher Parmenides, think of the parametic language as Parmenides' worst nightmare. Parmenides said something to the effect of "you can't describe something by what it is not" and the Lonely Exarch is all "hold my beer" and made a language that is purely that.

                                                        I guess a working example, if I'm not mistaken, would be like... somewhere on the forum someone said something like a "bird that isn't a bird knocking on your door", so the parametic language might work by defining the bird-not-bird by saying, well, you're not a bird, you're not a raptor, you're not a mammal, and you're not a spirit, and entrapping and commanding the abyssal by using the definitions of what it isn't as a sort of lattice to trap or compel it? Especially if used as a yantra?

                                                        Not a whole lot to go off of as the parametic language was literally only described in a paragraph, but, its a fun thought experiment irregardless to have a language that works in the complete opposite fashion of all other languages.

                                                            Originally posted by aceDiamond View Post
                                                            Fair, but it would be slightly easier to codify if "engage in your Order's hubris" was stuck somewhere into the Acts of Hubris section.
                                                            I guess it would also make character creation harder and complicate the metaphysics of using the Inuring mechanic. Hubris is about downfalls though, so the Acts of Hubris are so significant that they limit the net-capacity of a Mage to be wise with their magic, and the Order's hubris is lesser, more sociological down-falls instead of existential. However, another way to look at it is that the hubris of an Order is a clear path to Acts of Hubris, so perhaps its actually redundant to classify them mechanically when the Order's hubris is an in-story way for you to reach an act of hubris that follows from the faults of the Order you've joined.

                                                            I think it is important to separate the two because the Order's varying perspectives allow them to cloud Acts of Hubris. A Silver Ladder mage does not consider mind-controlling or commanding spirits to be an Act of Hubris--the power to do this is humanity's birthright, in their view. Never the less, it is an Act of Hubris to do this.

                                                            If the Acts of Hubris are what will mechanically cause Wisdom to lower? Then the Hubris section in the Orders write-ups are the road-maps the players and ST can follow to get to those Acts of Hubris. Which is handy! It helps the ST make the npc's interesting, and it helps players have excellent and believable ways to cause logical but compelling drama, and get that sweet sweet arcane xp.
