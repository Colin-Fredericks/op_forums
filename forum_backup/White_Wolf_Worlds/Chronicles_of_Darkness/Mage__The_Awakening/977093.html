    Which spells are best as Legacy Attainments?

    09-27-2016, 06:41 AM
    I am designing legacies for my characters and quite often have to stop and think about whether a certain spell should be an attainment, or if that spell is better left as a spell.

    So what are your thoughts about what spells are best as Attainments? Because of the way Attainments work rules-wise - no dispellation and no spell slot required - I see spells that boost and buff the mage, especially long-term, as the better ones. Summoning spells MIGHT count as such as well, but probably not all the time since Attainments have lackluster Reach. So, mainly spells that use the Shielding, Weaving and Patterning Practice. Spells that use the Practices of Unveiling, Knowing, Compelling and Ruling seem better left as spells as they really do not benefit from the advantages that Attainments have over spells.

    But that may be just me, right? Do any of you have contrasting experiences of the possibilities and limitations of Legacy Attainments?

                                          I have to agree that direct damage spells seem fairly anemic as Attainments. Guaranteed success with X potency is certainly nice, but the very earliest you can get it is as a third Attainment, requiring Gnosis 4 and Arcanum 3, for <Arcanum> automatic bashing damage; if you were instead casting it as a spell, you'd be looking at a base dice pool of 7; a Potency 3 Fraying spell would have you rolling 3 dice even before adding Yantras and such. And, well, Bashing damage is not exactly going to bring the house down. A four-dot Attainment can get you Lethal damage, but at that point you're Gnosis 6 and you can definitely get something better with your regular spellcasting. And the errata looks set to make a hypothetical direct damage Attainment even weaker, taking the primary spell factor from <primary arcanum> to <primary arcanum minus one>. The one potential advantage I see of direct-damage Legacy Attainments is the ability to tack on extra Scale as a secondary spell factor, but it doesn't seem very strong to me.

                                          Spells that allow the target to Withstand them (curses, mind-reading, mental influence, and such) are also problematic, on account of the fixed Potency (and will get even more so with the errata). Even against a resistance stat of 2, you need 3 dots in the relevant Arcanum to do anything at all, and anything that increases the relevant stat will quickly push you out of range.

                                          So what you want, for a potent Attainment, is a spell that a) you'll want to have active often, to take advantage of the free Spell Control, b) that isn't Withstood, c) that doesn't need a lot of Reach to be good. Which seems to me to mostly mean, as you say, self-buffs of one sort or another; long-term ones let you have them up constantly without spell control difficulties, and short-term ones of the form "get +Potency to <roll>" can be nice to have guaranteed success of decent Potency on.

                                          Another possibility is warding effects, like Ward, Ban, Shield of Chronos, and the like. The fixed Potency is of little concern, because they cannot be dispelled. They just stick around until the duration expires. And you can just throw them around like confetti, because spell control? What spell control? You do need to allocate Reach to Advanced Duration if you want them to be around long enough to be relevant, but hey, can't have everything. Besides, even if you only have one Reach available for the Attainment and therefore have to make it a ritual, an hour or so to set up an un-dispellable ward that doesn't count against spell control seems like a pretty good deal to me.

                                          Originally posted by Vent0 View Post
                                          Well, I'd say figure out the "story" or concept of your legacy, then pick spells core to that. Unless optimizing is part of the concept, it seems backwards to pick just spells that game the system best.
                                          IMO, at least, some level of optimisation ought to be part of the concept for anything being developed by mages. Especially something as serious as a Legacy. You're talking about shaping your very being; is any mage going to do that for something that they could do better with normal magic? It seems implausible to me. A Legacy Attainment that doesn't offer some kind of reasonable advantage over casting a spell is unlikely to ever be developed, and if developed, is unlikely to find anyone interested in learning it.

                                                      Good:
                                                      - Any long duration buffing spells (saves you spell control)
                                                      - Any spell that generally risks wisdom -> Mind Control spell, soul stealing, damage spells
                                                      - Any spell you really don't want counterspelled or dispelled -> anything that keeps you alive or can be used as an emergency escape spell

                                                      Great example of something that fits all three is an advanced duration damage spell. Without having to spend spell control or risking Widsom you deal your arcanum in damage every hour for up to a week, with no way of dispelling it or getting rid of it.

                                                      Bad:
                                                      - Any instant cast spell cast spell that doesn't risk wisdom and that enemies have no reason to try and counterspell -> Know Spirit and similar spells
                                                      - Any spell that needs both a good duration and a good potency, or lots of Reach.

                                                      My custom legacy (2e)- The Disciples of Rathma - Life/Death focused Moros/Thyrsus Legacy, comments appreciated
