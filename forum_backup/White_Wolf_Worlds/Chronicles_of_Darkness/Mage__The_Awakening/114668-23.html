    Originally posted by WHW View Post
    I'm trying to rob a house in 12 hours. No Shield of Chronos. Owner of the house casts a spell to check if his house is robbed in 12 hours, he sees me, he reacts. Future will change if his actions interact with mine. Or not.

    I'm trying to rob a house in 12 hours. I'm planning on casting Shield of Chronos when I'm inside. Owner of the house casts a spell to check if his house is robbed in 12 hours. Still uncast Shield of Chronos fizzles his spell. He may act upon this knowledge to interfere with the future, for example by being at home when I planned to attack, and thus making me not cast Shield of Chronos at all.

    From game perspective, this might seem like creating retroactive changes, because player's choice of using Shield of Chronos now might affect divinations that happened before player declared that action. So if your player didn't declare that he intends on using that spell beforehand, but remembers about it and casts it "when the time comes", you may have to retroactively change events that assumed his action was divined properly.
    This is circular. It seems like his reaction is the diviner's reaction is the same whether or not the Shield of Chronos was cast.From a storyteller's or players perspective, you've got to roll with the punches. The NPC divines the crime if there is no Shield of Chronos, or the NPC can't divine the crime because of the Shield. There's what happens in the story and what happens out of the story. It's a good idea to keep them separate. If time being divined can't be seen the storyteller can know that whether or not the player is Shielding or divining, and can therefore plan accordingly. Does that make sense?

    It's getting all Schroedinger's Cat if you start mixing up in-fiction and out-of-fiction things. Either the door's locked or it's not -- whether or not someone plans on robbing you doesn't matter.

            I'm going to rob a house in 12 hours. The house owner casts divination, sees me robbing their house, and recognises me, so they come and find me in six hours and crack their knuckles menacingly. I decide not to rob the house.

            My future action of robbing the house has made the house owner take an action that then prevents the future action happening. Exactly the same thing - all precognition abilities are weird like that.

            (My two options for how to run it are "what do you intend to do?" and have NPCs act as appropriate, or - and this one is weirder and requires the players to be OK with it - actually running the original scene to see what would happen, then saying "but that doesn't actually happen, because..." and going into an earlier scene where the opposition intervene. There's a subtler option where the opposition turn out to have prepared for it in advance, like finding out the stuff in the safe was replaced with fakes because they knew you were going to rob it.

                I'm sorry but I can't quite parse what you are saying. It might help if you re-read and correct your post. Still, I will try to help to the best of my ability.

                Without a Shield of Chronos, the Diviner knows exactly what will happen and can take exact precautions. He might even contact the burglar to discourage them. If a Shield of Chronos is in place, the Diviner doesn't know what will happen, only that someone will cast this spell in his Sanctum. This is enough to become paranoid, investigate further and improve all of ones defences. In either case, the burglar could be affected by he investigation before he even decides to break in, but that's beside the point.

                The truth is that if you introduce Divination into the game you must learn to think four-dimensionally. You must predict what would have happened had nobody used a Divination spell. Then, if somebody uses a Divination spell, you give them relevant information and change the future accordingly. Also, you must do it each and every time someone casts a Divination spell. It requires you to ask your players about their future plans and about their reactions to most likely outcomes (What do you do if you succeed? What if someone is home? What if you're found out?). Also, whatever the characters do, you must keep in mind that interested parties might have already predicted it and let it go that far. That's a lot of work.

                There is a saving grace in the fact that Divinations stop being accurate the moment they're made. The minuscule changes in timing and the Butterfly Effect can land the characters in situations nobody predicted. If, after casting their spell, the Diviner accidentally inspires you to break into their Sanctum, you have time until their next Divination. Of course, you should have your Shield on.

                        Take Time magic out of the equation. Insert any other kind of magic and apply it to the problem. As a storyteller, what I do is approach every problem from the perspective of the players and I have an answer, clue, or plot hook for all the way the problem can be solved using magic. "If they use Death, this is what happens..." "If they use Time, this is what they learn..." etc. When Time 2 or greater is introduced in the game, the storyteller has to be prepared to answer those questions and deal with those issues. A murder mystery needs to be able to deal with Post Cognition and pre-cognition magic. Be prepared for that. If you're not prepared for that or you're not flexible, then it seems like a railroad and the players aren't going to have fun and the storyteller isn't either when the players get off the track.

                        This question and this issue is not exclusive to the Shield of Chronos.

                                            I don't think much will be said about the Aeons in 2e. They are one of those things that are cool but not crucial for a core book with a limited page count. Also, I don't think there are Aeons in the supernal. Supernal "inhabitants" are each a kind of Truth, but it is implied in Imperial Mysteries that their shape depends on the interpretation of the observer. Rather than there being a single "Arcana God" it is possible that each Arcana is simply made by a nearly infinite cluster of interconnected Truths.

                                            Matter 4 can fully create organic matter, because organic chemistry is fully under its purview as long as the end result or the components are not "alive". There is a distinction between something being organic in origin and being alive. For example, a piece of coal can be organic matter (created from burning wood) but certainly not alive.

                                            If you turn a person into stone, they are stone. Stone can't think. Perhaps with Mind you could keep someone conscious in this state. Perhaps as little as Mind 2 if you use that Arcana to "shield" their consciousness from being lost during the transformation? If not, probably Mind 4 to allow the stone to think, or something like that. However, perhaps your could only turn them *partially* to stone? I can't say if the transmutation would go back to normal at the end, because I think it hasn't been revealed how those spells work in general for 2e.

                                                        Whenever you use spell factors you take dice penalties. There isn't a damage factor though, that's just Potency.
