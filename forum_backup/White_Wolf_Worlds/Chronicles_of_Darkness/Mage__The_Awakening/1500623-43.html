                            Here's the deal:

                            Continuous effect spells cast on a number of subjects will affect those subjects until the spell ends, no matter where they are.
                            Continuous effect spells cast on an area will affect all valid subjects within the area and all valid subjects who enters that area until they leave or the spell ends.
                            Instantaneous effect spells cast on a number of subjects will periodically take effect on those subjects until the spell ends, no matter where they are.
                            Instantaneous effect spells cast on an area will periodically take effect on all valid subjects who are within that area at the time, until the spell ends.

                                Originally posted by Cauthon View Post
                                cast on a wide area doesn't add new subjects who enter the area after the casting, although subjects who were within the area when it was initially cast continue to be affected if they leave.
                                I think the big problem with this interpretation is that it renders Subjects as the Range factor for a spell basically pointless.

                                How many people can you fit into a supermarket? A couple hundred? If you can do that with a -4 penalty, why would you ever bother casting a spell to affect 160 subjects with a -10 penalty?

                                I'm sure a person could say that they'd want to cast on a large number of people but not everybody within the area, but I think the circumstances in which one is being so discriminating are just so uncommon as to not justify the distinction. I have to imagine that in the majority of cases, if you're casting a spell to affect a large group of people, you intend to cast it on all of them together.

                                So then having a Range factor based in numbers and based in Area that do the same thing ends up favouring the latter to the point that the former becomes irrelevant.

                                (Plus the fact that there is Fate magic to already selectively exclude subjects from Area spells.)

                                So Area works on everything valid that walks in, and stops working on whatever walks out. It's the targeting factor for when you want to bestow a quality on a location. If you want the spell to travel with the targets, it needs to go by numbers, with all the penalties that go with that.

                                It makes it a steeper penalty to cast a spell on the same number of people as an area that gets to go with them, but conversely the largest area you can cast on could affect hundreds or thousands of people for the cost of it staying in place.

                                Originally posted by Cauthon
                                Which is a enough of a distinction that I'd personally disallow a Rote/Praxis of Oaths Fulfilled to be used to create this "zone of awareness". Still works fine for tagging a bunch of people, which potentially serves the same purpose if there isn't much traffic into or out of the designated area.
                                I think the problem with that approach is that it's trying to make range into a function of spell effect, when that isn't how any other magic in the game works. What a spell affects is very thoroughly consolidated under the Range spell factor; there's no such thing as a spell that creates a zone, there is a spell whose targeting has been calibrated to the scale of a zone. Effect is what you make happen within the zone.
