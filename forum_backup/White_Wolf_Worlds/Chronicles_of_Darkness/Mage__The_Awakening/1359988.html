    Forces vs Matter house rules

    01-09-2020, 06:42 PM
    Real thing that happened in my game: Mr. Moros is played by an engineer with an excellent knowledge of physics. He used a creative thaumaturgy Matter Weaving spell to lower the specific heat of some very hot concrete such that it dumped all the heat it could no longer store into the air above it and burned a guy standing on it (mostly his feet). In my games I do not ever again want to listen to physics math to ballpark what Mr. Moros just did with creative thaumaturgy, and I also don't like how Mr. Moros does things with Matter involving heat, fire, and electricity that Mr. Obrimos only wishes he could do with Forces. So its time to come up with a house rule, and I would love feedback on my idea below or other ideas.

    I did some searching on this forum and found a few occasions where people have discussed the Forces/Matter overlap in the past, but didn't see much in the way of ideas to better separate them. Basically the conceptual/thematic issue evolves from many players' understanding of basic physics and natural inclination to apply those ideas to the magic system, which (unfortunately, IMO) also shows up in some of the example rotes in the core rulebook (e.g. Alter Conductivity) and so the game seems to tacitly encourage.

    For example, Fire is under the purview of Forces, but fire is also a chemical reaction and chemical reactions are under the purview of Matter, so in many circumstances Matter ends up being about as good as working with fire as Forces. It takes a Forces master to Make fire, but a disciple of Matter can grant a material the property of being pyrophoric using a Weaving spell so that it ignites spontaneously in the presence of air. Similarly, Electricity is under the purview of Forces, but electricity only matters in the game insofar as it interacts with objects, so Matter's control over physical properties like resistance, capacitance, and charge give it de facto dominion over electricity too for many practical applications. Matter Weaving can make an object phosphorescent, thus creating light. I think you get the idea. "Many Roads" addresses using different Arcana to produce the same result, but my problem here is about using different Arcana to manipulate the same facets of reality -- there should not be such overlap the purviews of the different arcana. It's a problem that primarily comes up between Matter and Forces because the concept of a "force" is just energy interacting with matter; they aren't cleanly separable. Several phenomenon under the purview of Forces belong to categories of things that are under the purview of Matter and it's just too much overlap for my tastes.

    The best I can come up with is to emphasize that magic does not align with sleeper science, and adopt some house rules to keep Matter from stepping on Force's toes so much. Here's my idea:
    • Most Matter spells (exceptions further down) cannot influence the interactions of matter with the specific items listed as under the purview of Forces (electricity, fire, heat, light, etc...). Some physical properties Matter cannot change or influence about an object (not exhausting although I sure tried):
      • Flammability
      • Specific Heat
      • Conductivity/Resistance (good bye, Alter Conductivity, you were always too sciencey for this game)
      • Electrical charge
      • Capacitance
      • Luminescence
      • Radioactivity
      • Halving-thickness (ability to absorb radiation, like lead)
      • Acoustic resonance
    • Mass (i.e. gravity, although good luck impacting it much), magnetic field strength, and magnetic polarity are still on the table for Matter.
    • If Matter Patterning is used to transform one natural material into another natural material, the resulting matter has the typical properties for that type of matter. For example, Patterning cloth into lead does make it absorb radiation as normal for lead, because then it is lead.
    • Matter Making can make matter not found in nature that interacts with Forces phenomenon as the caster sees fit.
    • Matter Mage Armor does not defend against the items under the purview of Forces -- no water vapor dynamically and intelligently condensing to quench a fireball. I'm iffy on this one despite my distaste for how the Matter mage armor description sounds like imperial magic.

          The solution to this is to stop thinking about how things would work according to mundane physics and think how they would work according to Supernal magic. For something like the spell to change how concrete holds/transmits heat, I'd compare what a spell of that level would be capable of to the existing spell 'Alter Conductivity'. It would increase/decrease the amount of heat said section of concrete could hold/transmit by 2 per point of potency, limited by whatever direct form of heat you, the ST, decide it is directly connected to, advanced math not needed. If you decide that from a magic standpoint this random section of concrete that has been in the sun all day is only holding 1L worth of heat (possibly judging things based on Forces Transform energy table) then it only holds 1L of damage, mundane physics be damned.

                  You can manipulate the fire directly, or you can manipulate the flammability and caloric content of what it's burning. You can call lightning, or you can give something high positive charge and the lightning (or electric current from whatever its touching) will find it. Forces always act on something; if you configure the something correctly then the laws of the fallen world will handle the forces part for you -- sometimes more effectively than using Forces magic.

                  But I'm not really bothered by which one is more effective; I'm bothered by Matter having so much to do with manipulating the same things as Forces. It's a degree of redundancy and overlap that doesn't exist between any other two paths. It robs Forces of too much of its own uniqueness and flavor. Sometimes what you want to do with Fire takes more dots with Matter than Forces, sometimes less, I don't care. I care that Matter and Forces both manipulate the chemical reaction we call fire even though fire is called out as belonging to Forces. It's not about balance, it's about having two Arcana that are very distinct rather than heavily overlapping.

                  EDIT: Is there any other case where the rulebook gives a rote for one arcana that manipulates a phenomenon explicitly listed as belonging to another arcana without requiring conjunctional casting? They couldn't come up with stuff for Matter to do without (haha) stealing Force's thunder?

                                                          I tend to take an opposite approach here to a lot of posters.

                                                          Science is not a skill rolled for spellcasting. Even Matter spells are rolled with Gnosis - how well you see past the illusion of the material world, not how well you embrace it. Thus, your - player or character - knowledge of science is not a factor in the effectiveness of your magic. Magic, after all, is about transcending the mundane, not embracing it. I might use the heat capacity of concrete to explain why the Matter spell caused an explosion when it paradoxed - the lie and the abyss tainting the intention of the spell - but I wouldn't allow it to be the intention of the spell.

                                                          Matter has a purview of alchemy, gases, solids, liquids, shaping, crafting, transmutation, stasis. Burning people with hot concrete just... doesn't really fit with that. I'm not terribly interested in arguments by players about why it scientifically follows from other capabilities - the Supernal is not fundamentally scientific. An imago to burn a guy standing on hot concrete is an imago about manipulating heat, not about manipulating concrete.


                                                          On a more game level, I tend to frown on players trying to stretch an arcanum's purview. It centralizes the game around the most creative player, makes it harder for people the other players to feel like they can really contribute. I've been on the 'winning' side of that frequently enough, and while it was fun for me, it was to the detriment of the group.

                                                          So yes, I agree this is a table problem. But I don't necessarily think it's the Obrimos' problem - feeling resentful that someone else is reducing the specialness of your character by edging in on their deal (without spending any additional XP) is a genuine problem. Maybe the solution is to talk with the Obrimos, if you don't feel it's reasonable. Maybe it's to talk with the Moros, to tell them not to steal other people's thunder.

                                                          But either way, chastisizing an ST for looking out for the quieter, less creative players at their table leaves me with a big frown. Social dynamics is important, and in Mage, I find that restricting arcana - forcing people to rely on the rest of their group and the rest of world - is one of the stronger tools at my disposal to spread the spotlight around a bit.
