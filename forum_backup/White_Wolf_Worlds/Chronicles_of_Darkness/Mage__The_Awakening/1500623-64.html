                I would note, I think you can change your self-image, in the same way your dream form is liable to change if you develop a new shadow name.

                You can't simply imagine it is different, or use it to disguise yourself.

                The dream form may look nothing like the sorcerer, because this is her self-image as influenced by contact from the Supernal, and divorced from the limitations of flesh. Characters with the Shadow Name Merit often appear radically different in age, race, sex, or fitness, as their dream forms conform to their magical selves.
                The rules for Archmages don't specify that their physical bodies are actually dream forms, but I would happily allow them to appear in bodies of the age, race, sex and fitness that conform to their magical selves...

                But spend 90 years in the material realm and it will change your self-image significantly.

                I just think, in the same sense mages can take on a Shadow Name, Archmages should be able to alter their self-image by investing significant time and effort.

                    Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
                    Do mages know all the new accessible Practices when achieving a new rank in an Arcana? i.e. if I go from Arcana 1 to Arcana 2, do I know the Ruling, Veiling and Perfecting things that this Arcana can do instinctively?
                    I think that's looking at it too much in terms of gameplay being how something is experienced in character.

                    In character, developing a new Arcanum dot is not a matter of spending the currency to move you up a level and then it being a question of whether or not you even know what to do with that level.

                    It's a matter of how becoming more powerful in an Arcanum is a function of the learning and discovery and introspection that allows you to comprehend further ways those symbols can be assembled into imagos with greater sophistication (as well as greater depth of understanding of how to effectively assemble already known symbols, which is represented by greater Reach and primary spell factors).

                    The game positions Ruling, Veiling, and Shielding as applications of the Arcana that all organically reside at about the same level of understanding, which I do think holds up.

                    In short, it's not that becoming an Apprentice means you might then be able to realise that you're capable of Ruling spells, it's that the process of coming to understand how to cast Ruling spells (as an advancement of Compelling spells) is what becoming an Apprentice even is.
