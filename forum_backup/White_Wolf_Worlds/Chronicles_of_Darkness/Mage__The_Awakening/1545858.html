    Having a hard time with this awakening I'm plotting out

    11-14-2024, 01:16 PM
    So last time I ran mage we ended with a NPC beginning his mystery play, as the watchtower drew in the Mastigos. The NPC is magically compelled to obsess over a magical item, but the Mastigos hid the memory with Mind veiling. This did not stop the magical compulsion to obsess, but without anything to focus on it's been driving him crazy. Instead of breaking though Stygia called out to him, and he'll have to attempt to complete his mystery play and discover this memory that was taken, or sacrifice.

    The Mastigos is taking on the role within the Mystery Play as the "Occulter" the shade that hides the Truth from others, but honestly I'm not really sure what to actually have him do that isn't just watching an NPC so their thing. Any ideas?

    I'm not worried about the rest of the cabal, I have something in mind for those outside the awakening that will keep them involved and busy, it's just this Mastigos issue.

      For the moment we'll set aside the fact that as a Mastigos being in a Stygian Mystery Play is an odd situation to be in (joining an Adyton requires a mage to be of the appropriate Path and exceptionally succeed at Revealing the Mystery of the Lucid), since that sounds like it's either a done deal or else would necessitate changing the Prospect's Watchtower before the next session; unless he gains and tries to use Mana the only practical difference is in knowing how the NPC is seeing the world.

      In the context of being part of the Mystery Play, worry less about "having the player do things" and more about directing the Adyton's narrative toward him in ways appropriate to the Prospect's tests, likely in the context of what he's done to their memory — if they pass the trials, both the memory modification and the compulsion will be cleared away, and the trials continue until they either spend the Experiences to get their first dot of Gnosis or conclusively fail one of the tests (tests which have no obligation to keep intruders safe).

      The Prospect could try to bargain for their memory back, or try to extract an explanation for why the Mastigos hid it from them, and the magical item in question is almost certainly going to be important regardless. Basically just run the chapter as normal with the added weight of mythic symbolism on the Mastigos and the NPC's perspectives.

          As Satchel said, usually only other Moros can be pulled into a Moros Awakening. However, your Mastigos was already heavily involved in that NPC's occult development, so he could be "incorporated" as a result of that. It's a unique Mystery the PC can boast about later on, especially among Mages that study Awakenings (Alethians).

          Now, the most common lesson of Stygia is learning to let go, and the NPC has a number of things that count:
          • The Obsession towards the magic item.
          • Interest in the supernatural, in general.
          • The Veling spell that protects him.
          • Having the Mastigos as a protector.
          • Whatever would anchor him to his mundane life.

          Your player's actions may steer that NPCs choices. Depending on what happens, the NPC may:
          • Fully pass the trial and Awaken.
          • Receive a Supernal right to claim the magic item.
          • Be forever Supernally separated from the item.
          • Be forever Supernally separated from the Mastigos.
          • Lose a major personal attachment from his Sleeper life.
          • Let go of the supernatural, but become a Sleepwalker.
          • Let go of the supernatural, but become a Banisher.

          Just about the only way to fail that trial is to try and keep things as they are.

                                          Because this doesn't exist in the books and you are asking a question about how to handle something in a game that has books on the forums for that game. The closest thing in the books to what you are describing is entering an Adyton, which can explicitly happen by accident and casts the intruder in the Mystery Play while they're within range of the Prospect's senses.

                                          There is no real next-closest thing described in the books to "the Watchtower picking an outside character to be a participant in the Adyton," because the Watchtowers are historically specific contrasts to the Exarchs in their degree of direct meddling in the world — the full spectrum of technical possibility behind undetailed things like the powers of Sacred Artifacts or weird slants on Imperial spells to directly Awaken people nevertheless runs into the fact that the Watchtowers themselves are not under the control of anybody and do not evidence any clear agenda besides offering the Awakening to Prospects who meet the necessary criteria.

                                          We have systems for joining in on an Awakening and it was more expedient to state up front that this is unusual when those are the only context we have for doing so and then offer advice as it would pertain to the normal situation. You voiced doubts about there being anything limiting the Path of a mage subjected to a process that is not described anywhere in the books when given that up-front statement and were then given a citation of the text describing how the closest thing to what you described is limited by Path.

                                          I began my actual advice with the note that the Adyton is a filter centered on the Lucid mage-to-be because the systems we have for being involved with an Adyton at the level where you can see the symbolism the Prospect sees has potential consequences and rewards beyond simply knowing they're in the middle of an Awakening. Pushing back and drawing an arbitrary line between the established systems for joining an Adyton and this situation makes it unclear if any of those consequences are actually on the table anymore or if this is just being used as a more involved way of watching an Awakening from the outside with no added risk of metamorphosis, opportunity to harvest Pneuma, or risk to Wisdom.

                                          I cannot devil's-proof what you've described as impossible any more than I can prove my real-life house isn't haunted by real-life imperceptible spirits. The narrative fait accompli of a Mastigos being in a Stygian Mystery Play in your game has been established regardless, which means the most I can do for your game absent the willingness and/or ability to rewrite events into a more plausible form is to emphasize that the situation you have created is a weird one that is breaking with known rules and established precedents, possibly due to additional tampering with the Watchtower by a third party, which potentially brings Supernal attention and Aponoia into play.

                                          You mentioned having things to keep the rest of the cabal busy while the Mastigos deals with this entanglement. Are they elsewhere? Also, given the discussion it's probably worth clarifying: is this Mystery Play a Waking World Dream or has the Mastigos been pulled along on a Supernal Journey in a further break from conventional understanding?
