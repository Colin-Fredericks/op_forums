            A thing to remember about systems of oppression: They work because of differential gradients of power. Tyrants rule because they control, and money is a form of control. Sleepers covet it - and are denied it - because wealth IS power, however much that wealth is a shadow of true Supernal power.

            Fate spells are vulnerable things, their effects temporary and often coming with strings attached. A Mage who wishes for money may outright have Fate waft a winning ticket in front of them. Wealth spirits are going to act commensurate to their power and react unkindly to those who try to force their hand. They also likely are known by the other Awakened of the area, who may have bargains with the spirit and take offense to someone else mucking about with their meal ticket. Woe betide any Thyrsus who lets their guard down...

            The point here is that money is not a non-thing for sorcerers. If it were, the Silver Ladder and Seers would lose half their temptation.

                Originally posted by Axelgear View Post

                I think you vastly underestimate the power of money. There is a reason that an entire Seer Ministry is dedicated to the flow of wealth and one of the Exarchial Seals (that is becoming a greater seal) is named Mammon.

                For a start, while money has its uses, this isn't just about a Mage's life. A Mage is, yes, personally quite powerful, although I think many overestimate how quickly and readily that power transfers into direct material comfort (at least without using Wisdom-violation-worthy forms of it). An Acanthus making tricks of Fate occur to live a comfortable hobo existence or a Thyrsus living under an overpass are getting by but hardly living easily. You could use your magic to steal from ATMs, but any appreciable amount stolen is going to cause criminal inquiries, complicating your life and making it hard to spend that money on anything legitimate.

                On top of that, money is in some ways better than magic. Both are a form of power which can be used to bend and reshape the world to your will but money requires no concentration, no special learning, and is, indeed, transferable. It's also - and I say this under a very narrow set of moral constraints - generally ethical to use, as all voluntary monetary transactions are done of both parties' free will. That makes it consensual with Sleepers in a way that magic can never really be.

                This means a Mage can use money as a kind of magical shortcut. They can build and maintain far larger networks of connections that don't rely on them having to constantly cast spells and perform miracles than they could otherwise do. Money is how you pay your Sleepwalker assistants; keep the lights on in your Sanctum; ensure the local Athenaeum isn't going to be repossessed (or investigated) by the State; minimize the maintenance needed for your Cryptopoly or Ministry (meaning you have more time to build up other things or, more likely, chase your own Mysteries), and so on.

                Money doesn't have power, it is power; broken down and put into units that fit in your pocket or onto a digital chip.

                For that reason, the Seers are absolutely going to be watching lotteries because all the money and resources poured into a lottery that don't get directed as they see fit are money and resources working against the Exarchs. If Pentacle agents win, it doesn't matter if they are making money off the backs of poor and desperate Sleepers because the Seers get that either way. It does, however, mean that the Pentacle gets a huge chunk of resources it can use to finance its organization, freeing up other resources for expansion, opening new possibilities, and generally making life easier and better for a lot of people.

                That is absolutely something the Seers cannot allow.
                I agree. If you're a brutal tyrant are you going to allow someone to pick up a potent weapon to wield against you? Not likely. After school programs to help the inner cities, non-gentrified urban renewal projects, lifting entire families or communities out of poverty, to provide scholarships for greater learning and a bunch of other stuff that raises the eyes of the downtrodden to hope for more out of life than just grinding out an existence. Money is the best type of tool against the oppression of the poor, and to be fair most of the world is really poor.

                For this exact reason I imagine some power Mammon Seer Pylons checking out the diamonds coming through Antwerp, and yet others checking out sizable gold deposits.Mages sloppy enough to leave connections to those materials are prime recruiting or elimination candidates.

                The power to give money away or use it to actually help people is devastating to the Seer's cause.

                                I think the idea is not necessarily that the Seers (or Guardians) monitor it, so much as they could be monitoring it. After all, "winning the lottery" is probably one of the first ideas that crosses the mind of any acanthus raised in a culture with a lottery system. (And/or any mage with knowledge of Fate and/or Time.)

                                The depth and frequency of such investigations likely depends on the resources available to the organization. A resident caucus that's short-handed might only check the big winners -- and even then, only a cursory scrutiny of the recipient and their immediate family. On the flip side, a more regimented and populous institution might be able to afford a team that specializes in digging into these sorts of things.

                                Importantly, these orders aren't monolithic (hegemonic?) cultures; they splinter and have different goals/focii. Thyrsus Seers who follow The Raptor maybe don't give a butt about money. Religious-esque seers might be more focused on interpreting prophecies or summoning an ochemata.

                                In fact, I feel like Guardians are even more likely to monitor lotteries -- maybe there's even a cabal dedicated to doing such across the US or something -- than Seers, as mages of any affiliation are likely to pull such a stunt. Seers learning that Seers have rigged the lottery might feel like the time investigating was wasted. But that kind of info is always of interest to GoV. :P But, it's not as if the above points don't apply to the GoV.

                                            Originally posted by Axelgear View Post
                                            Pretty much every major lottery in the developed world requires that winners allow their personal details to be published (largely so that the lottery corporations or government institutions running them can demonstrate that they are not fraudulent). Anyone who wins is going to be scrutinized by every Pentacle member, Seer, Banisher, and any Left-Handed Mages in the area, before we even get to the other groups who might think less than kindly of the Awakened (Hunters, anyone?) that can make a Mage's life hell.

                                            Naturally, the smart Awakened takes the obvious next step of "Well, just have someone else do it", but then you run into the issue that they need to transfer that money from you and, in those aforementioned nations, governments really don't like it when someone just suddenly decides to withdraw a few million dollars in cash. They tend to assume not-nice things are going to be done with it (and, generally, they're right). When the mortal authorities have their radar pinged, you can bet everyone else's will be as well, all but confirming the suspicions of many a priori.

                                            And on we go. The more layers of security you add, the more obvious it becomes what you're up to.
                                            Actually, the subject of collecting from a lottery anonymously is something I've thought about a lot (not even in the context of Mage), because if I ever won the lottery in real life I simply wouldn't want anyone to know. The summary is, if you win the lottery, contact your lawyer and have them set up a blind trust to collect the money on your behalf. It is legal in most states and happens more often than you'd think.

                                                            Well, that's what I meant by taking it away, making it a part of the story in a way that limits access to it. E.g. if you win the lottery, you attract a lot of negative attention, your assets get frozen because of Guardian/Seer involvement. Or If you decided to make gold coins then local vendors and law enforcement get suspicious and you have to go to greater and greater lengths to liquidate them (which actually happened in a 1E game of mine that was a fun story hook). As far as putting restrictions on spells like that, I mean, the suggestion was straight from a 2-dot spell in the canon book that gives resource dots by affecting probabilities. It's probably the most direct analog to this actual situation in the book.

                                                            In retrospect the best way to handle this is by having the lottery winnings count as a 5-dot "Cash" equipment rather than Resources, so that it dwindles with expenditures and does not interfere with the Sanctity of Merits.

                                                            Back to the topic on hand... the weird thing is, it's rare to split a lottery jackpot. If it were easy to accomplish magically then presumably several clever wizards would be splitting the jackpots every week, but for some reason this doesn't happen. What is actively preventing this? It's weird but it might be a good Mystery in and of itself. You try to go back in time to win the lottery but it doesn't work, and you have to figure out why (this becomes your Obsession). Because the Lottery has such heavy metaphysical weight maybe there is some Lottery spirit of great rank that actively seeks to keep this from happening. In fact, a lot of spirits probably have vested interests in the sanctity of the lottery (spirits of hope, spirits of greed, spirits of fortune, spirits of fairness, etc).

                                                            There's also always the possibility of other supernatural interference. The book Night Terrors talks about an Arcadian creature, the Metathron, that seeks out and punishes mages who abuse Fate magic. Something similar could exist for mages who abuse Time.

                                                            There are a number of ways to resolve this (including many more creative ways discussed in this thread), but I stand by my comment that the boon from such usage of spells should be temporary unless they spend the merit dots. Let the player enjoy her spoils, but be thinking about ways it's going to backfire (preferably in a way that creates good story hooks). Even if they spend the merit dots they should be subject to something in the story for being so audacious with magic (with the difference being that they get to hold onto the assets).

                                                            My ideas are bad and I should feel bad.
