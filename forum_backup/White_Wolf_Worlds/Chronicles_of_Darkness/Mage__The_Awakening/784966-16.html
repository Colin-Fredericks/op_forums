    I think it's best to build upon facts you've already established. Ask yourself why the Seers came to use blood of a Scelestus and if they knew about their captive's nature. Who is the Scelestus? Is he related to the Prince of 100.000 Leaves? Is it Blood of the Lamb? Perhaps Deep Purple's properties are an indirect result of an Investment, and the corruption begun with blood.

    A source of inspiration

    Unprocessed blood might have dangerous properties of its own, perhaps even intended by the Investment:
    • Puddles of blood are deeper than possible, so objects and people might fall in.
    • Contact can cause hallucinations that deal psychosomatic damage.
    • A degree of body control. Not too much, though - players hate losing agency.
    If the Scelestus keeps on bleeding beyond what he should be able to, it would be a good reason to outright disintegrate him.

    Beyond the blood moving by its own volition, the Scelestus' Space Arcanum may cause it to:
    • Spill towards walls, forcing your players to dodge.
    • Pool on the floor, walls or the ceiling.
    • Float as if in absence of gravity.
    • Slowly flow towards the characters, forcing them to play "The Floor is Lava", while everything goes haywire.
    All of which makes it     difficult to navigate the room.

    As for making the Scelestus look like a Boss Battle, transforming the enemy into a monster is a classic:
    • Another source of inspiration. Horrendous noise optional. I'd lean away from making him look a more functional monster, but towards showing that he's suffering as well.
    • Have the Scelestus peel his skin open to reveal a different place. A rotting forest, a field of furnaces, a mound of frozen corpses. You can literally connect him to an Annunaki of your choice. Bonus points if that vista is the source of the blood.
    • Something I learned from Earthbound (the Nintendo game). An opponent wasting their turn to do something unnerving shows how little contact with reality they have. Make sure to make up for it somehow, so the players don't win too fast.

    You can also transform the environment. The Space Arcanum is enough to do that.
    • Yet another source of inspiration.
    • You can reverse gravity to make equipment fall on players. Bonus points if the Scelestus gets hurt too - hanging from his catheters. That would underscore that nobody has control over the situation.
    • Portal players to a storage box sunken in the ocean and filled with bones of other victims. Boston is a port city, after all. Only do this to players you know will survive it. Note that players might want to investigate the remains.

    On top of that, a barely lucid Scelestus will probably cause and release Paradox like nobody's business. This fact makes the battlefield your playground. You have every excuse to throw in creepy effects, dangerous phenomena or go all-out and summon Gulmoth...s. Gulmoi? Summon Abyssal Intruders. The longer the fight goes, the bigger the Paradox pool, so this gives your players a sort of countdown.

    As you players approach the opponent you have a chance to build atmosphere. I'm thinking of blinking panels on the wall, stating "Silent alarm activated. The asset is awake. Initiate Indigo evacuation procedure" - or something in that vein. When things get tough, you can activate the emergency speaker system, and when they escalate further, you can make it give twisted messages, explode, bleed or melt.

    A final thought: If the Seers knew what they were dealing with, the players may decide to tattle to their superiors or competitors. Which seems pretty reasonable, all things considered.

                So things went a little differently than I had planned, but not necessarily in a bad way.

                The group immediately decided on defensive measures(all group spells): Honing the Form (+3 Stamina), Ward (+3 on the Clash for 6 dice), Wards & Signs (Potency 4)

                Clint (the Mastigos Seer) attempted 4 times to open a Scrying window on them and failed due to Clashing with the Ward and/or not having enough Potency to overcome Wards & Signs. After spending 4 Mana he gave up, much to Dr. Kether's dismay.

                In the mean time, the group managed to get to her lab and use Telekinesis to open the locked door, only to find the two Seers arguing.

                Dr. Kether was sizing them up and cast Mental Scan, but did not overcome the Wards & Signs again.

                So both groups were in a situation where they were worried about the power level of their potential opponent. A true stand off.

                The PCs informed her about the Sanctifier of the Meek (Acamoth) to her surprise and she acquiesced that "the Abyss concerns us all", but that she needed to know who they were "What Ministry do you serve?" to establish some trust.

                The Mastigos leader of the group rolled no successes on a roll to know anything about Seer hierarchy so instead of trying to fake it he told her that the group were Apostates, but he rolled so poorly she just laughed and accused them of being Pentacle mages.

                The Thyrsus Silver Ladder decided that if the jig was up he would just throw the Guardians under the bus, since they aren't very well represented in Boston and worrying about the Abyss was in their wheelhouse. He rolled ok, but she rolled an exceptional success to detect his ruse. She played along just to diffuse the situation.

                She agreed that they would temporarily halt the distribution of Deep Purple at least long enough for the Acamoth to be dealt with.

                Campus security came knocking and the group headed back to their Sanctum after being escorted off campus.

                After a good night sleep they got up, harvested Mana from their Hallow and decided that they would leave the Seers alone for now and deal with the cannibal splinter group, under the control of the Acamoth, that has taken control of the gang distributing Deep Purple.

                                                    All listed Mages are only Gnosis 2. They're at the beginning of their journeys and it's reasonable for them to not have joined a Legacy yet. Also, I'm not sure but I don't think they could induct new members either way, at least until they gain higher Attainments.

                                                    Either way, I'm assuming that these Mages would join Legacies that match their approach to Magic and their Arcana spreads. That does not have to be the case. They can join unfitting Legacies through political pressure, social benefits, or carelessness.

                                                    Looking at Rabid Annie's and Archer's Arcana spread, there are no official Legacies that would fit their inclinations. You would need to either assign them new Arcana dots, or let them go through formative experiences that send them on a new trajectory through life. Preferably in-character.

                                                    Cathubodva is easy, because her Arcana fit two official Legacies. Her friendship with Spirits would make her into a great Keeper of the Covenant, and her affinity for Celts would guide her towards Sodality of Tor.

                                                    Agdos doesn't have the necessary Arcana ratings yet, but she seems to be bound towards an alchemical Legacy like Uncrowned Kings, or towards other material-based direction like Forge Masters.

                                                    Renard seems to take the Awakened condition lightly enough that I would make it a point that she didn't join a Legacy at all. Sisterhood of the Blessed might have something she'd like, but they would require her to show some effort, and that's a tough call.

                                                    I don't know who to choose as the Cryptologos, because I can't check all the writeups at the moment. This matter is a bit complicated, because this Legacy was based on Mind in 1st edition, but on Prime in 2nd edition, and only the former has had its Attainments published. If I had to make a pick I'd choose Ahriman, because he's said to know languages unsuited for humanity, he knows a lot of Prime and a little bit of Mind. It would also be a surprise for the players, because his career was mostly focused on Death and forbidden lore. Him having such a respectable Legacy would be a curveball.

                                                    There are no Acanthus-specific Spirit Legacies, but you are aware that Mages of any Path can join any Legacy? Keepers of the Covenant​ are based on Fate and Spirit already, and you can just grant that player Spirit as an extra Ruling Arcanum. Outside of that, Scions of God and Gaolers of Ialdabaoth are not aligned with any Path.
