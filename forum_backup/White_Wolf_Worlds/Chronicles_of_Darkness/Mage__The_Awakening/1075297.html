    Ash Dies at the End - an Actual Play

    04-08-2017, 07:43 PM
    Back in 2013, Axelgear, myself, and two other players began a game of Mage: the Awakening. Our first. Initially, there was nothing that stood out about it. Most of us were floundering because we hadn’t really grasped the nature of the setting, or the themes inherent within, and we all made the rookie mistake of not coordinating on what our characters wanted or where the game was going. Stories were a bit disconnected, characters didn’t play well off each other, and at one point an entire plotline was scrapped and resolved off-camera.

    But something happened. Near the middle-to-end point, we began to get a better appreciation for the setting, and for what our characters stood for. Things became more complex. Characters became more nuanced. We began to comprehend just what the setting was going for. Unfortunately, due to both time schedules and a complete disconnect by players in terms of theme, the unified game died in a bunch of fire (not entirely a metaphor). From that, three different solo games began, two of which continue into today.

    I’m sharing this now, years out, because I feel they can present some insight into approaching the setting, dos and don’ts. I also do it because these games ended up being some of the most interesting and most crazy experiences I’ve had. Sometimes they were unbearably human, and sometimes entertainingly over-the-top. I'm also doing this because Axelgear won't stop encouraging me to.

    This AP will be separated into three chronicles, each detailing a specific era in both the games and their styles, and all of them following from the perspective of an Obrimos by the shadow name of Ash. Part of this will be transcribed from memory—mostly the earliest examples—and part will be done from logs.

    With that said, on to the characters of note:

      CHRONICLE I: THE THREE-HEADED DRAGON
      The first game, and the most tumultuous. We didn’t ‘get’ Mage too well at first. Ash essentially wanted to advocate as if science-magic was still a thing that existed from Ascension, Kismet’s player wasn’t into much of it, and Midas’s player was more into exploring old ruins than dealing with human costs and hubris. Most of this is reconstructed from memory, and a lot of it will be retconned over the course of the telling so that it’ll make sense. I’ll note what we did wrong and when I’ve taken liberties for sake of cohesion.

      Our first chronicle begins in Oregon with the Cabal of the Three-Headed Dragon, a name they wouldn’t adopt for some time. Three recently-Awakened, each with atypical skill in magic, and in whom several significant mages see promise—more than just potential, more an omen…

      The Players
      Argentum Everto/Æ/Ash
      An Obrimos in his mid-twenties, Ash has a master’s in theoretical physics, is incredibly intelligent, and has an uncanny skill with firearms. Unfortunately, social he is not. His Awakening took the form of a mystery play in which he ignored any number of fantastical creatures to finish some divine mathematical formula and then burn his house down. At the start of the game, he’s optimistic to step into a world more meaningful than the one he grew up in. Despite having sensibilities more suited to the Free Council, he joins the Silver Ladder for their ideals.

      Midas
      David was a chemistry teacher. A Moros, he Awoke in a car crash, and after some Paradox-related gaffes met his mentor, Adelaide. Prior to the start of the game he’d had his own set of adventures, in which he found a fondness for alchemy and earned the patronage of a Supernal being by the name of Gur Aryeh. In many ways he embodies the ‘gentleman explorer’ aesthetic. He originally hails from Chicago. He’s charming and emotionally-intelligent, with a preference for swords if violence becomes necessary. He’s a recent member of the Mysterium.

      Kismet
      Kismet was a police sketch artist who Awoke as an Acanthus during a frantic moment where she felt her life was spiraling out of her control. Unfortunately, suddenly stepping into a secret world of mysteries and horrors is unlikely to help that. Perhaps even more risky is her choice to join the Guardians of the Veil.


      The Supporting Cast
      The Mentors
      Goch – A dark-skinned man who somehow knew exactly where to find Ash shortly after his Awakening. His approach to teaching Ash is hands-off—though that may simply be because he has no idea how to do it.

      Adelaide – Midas’s mentor. A woman just past middle age, she’s a member of the Sodality of Tor. Not long after their introduction, she also comes to fulfill the role for Kismet, as well as be a teacher of her Legacy.

      The Good Father – Kismet’s first mentor. Secretly a Clavicularus, he skipped town when Kismet found out what he was and they realized their arrangement wouldn’t work with her convictions as a Guardian.


      The Consilium
      NOTE: The state of the Oregon Consilium is the perfect example of the setting issues we experienced as our first game. It was atypically large, lacked a hierarch, and had representatives from caucuses instead of singular cabals. None of us, ST included, realized this was odd until long after the fact. It was composed of:

      Orcus – In his nineties, Orcus has been a Mage and a member of the Mysterium for a very long time. At least a third-degree master, he’s the unspoken power behind the Oregon Consilium. His face is almost always obscured by a hood.

      Marx – Young, vibrant, a member of the Free Council, and a bit of an anarchist, Marx is the rebel-in-office. She wears a pair of large gauntlets, and has extensive drug connections. As many mages do.

      Tartarus – A Guardian just past middle-age. He’s seen some shit, and has the scars and temperament to prove it.

      Seshat – A businesswoman and member of the Silver Ladder. Largely the source of any resources the Consilium required.

      Dagda – Dagda didna do shit


      Other Characters
      Pathos – A stage magician at a nightclub, Pathos is a member of the Silver Ladder and an Obrimos. She quickly becomes the most important person to Ash, and plays many roles in his life—confidante, friend, ally, nursemaid, host. Enabler.

      Gavril – A fortune-teller from Siberia, divining the future and past from a strange stone found near the Stony Tunguska River.

      Argos – A spirit hound from a Chicago spirit court, Argos sniffs out Abyssal corruption. He serves as Midas’s ally, guide, and familiar.

      Malthus – Malthus is a powerful Moros. In the 80s he showed immense talent and potential alongside arrogant and radical streaks. At some point this led him down darker avenues of power. The extent of his acts and depravities are only known to him, but he eventually turned Tremere, and earned a spear through his heart from Tartarus for it. Somehow, that hasn't stopped him, as he's returned, has amassed a small cult, and is building a Ladder from stolen souls.

                  Chapter 1: Blood and Spiders
                  Part 1

                  It's evening, and Kismet, Ash, and Midas all find themselves invited (more like called) to a gathering in the woods, hosted by the eldest and most powerful member of the Consilium, a Mystagogue by the name of Orcus. They're told that the other counselors would be in attendance, and to present themselves accordingly. Somehow word of this found its way to Midas's mentor, Adelaide, and somehow a number of other Mages found their way to the spot, and are making an event of it. Someone has made a soulstone, and a number of mages--mostly the younger ones--are busy showing off and generally making idiots of themselves.

                  Ash looks around at everyone, a little confused, but without judgement. Midas is no stranger to these things. Kismet is keeping to herself, otherwise uninvolved with all that's going on. The members of the council are off discussing things amongst themselves--well, all but Orcus is. Orcus is busy speaking with Adelaide, and while the hood makes discerning his expression impossible, Adelaide's age-lined face is clearly excited.

                  Roughly an hour after all the intended guests have arrived, the council pulls Ash, Midas, and Kismet aside. As tends to happen when the heads of the Consilium uniformly draw their attention to you, suddenly the three mages are now the center of everyone's attention. The faces of the council all wear different expressions, and from the way Orcus takes the lead in talking, it's fairly clear whatever this is, it belongs to him and they're simply cooperating.

                  Orcus speaks, and the three mages listen. He outlines what he wants from them: all three have been solo acts up until this point, and from what he knows he thinks that the three of them would work well together. Midas is already experienced, and Ash and Kismet's individual talents might benefit from that guidance. Midas catches Adelaide from the corner of his eye; she's smiling in a way that's more unsettling than warm. He writes it off.

                  Orcus continues: there's a task he needs done, something he's been intending to take care of for some time but hasn't managed to do so owing to the fact that he's 93 and breathing is getting harder. He doesn't say as much in those words, but a coughing fit which is stopped by a magical pat on the back by the Silver Ladder councilor--Seshat--says it for him. He feels that, owing to the simplicity of it, it'd be a good way for the mages to get an idea of each other, and hopefully see how well they work together.

                  Ash looks over at the other two. One's dressed like he stepped out a book on victorian-era fashion. The other looks far too casual to be what she is. And here he is, wearing a suit a size too small he'd borrowed from Goch, eyes sunken from a few weeks of increasingly-troubled sleep. Still, he smiles and waves at them, and they return the gesture. The three of them agree to lend Orcus a hand.

                  Somehow, all three of them resist the urge to make a joke about accepting a quest.

                  Marx--a councilor from the Free Council, a title and affiliation that makes addressing her difficult--steps forward, and the heavy gauntlets she wears dig into the air. A moment later she wrenches open a hole in space, on the other side of which the newly-formed cabal and see an aged mesoamerican temple with its ziggurat-like tiers. Marx looks smug at their surprised reactions, Ash and Kismet especially (their jaws have been hanging open), and shoos them through it.

                  As the cabal passes through the portal, it hits them: the sudden change in humidity and temperature that makes Ash and Midas wish they'd dressed differently. They approach the Temple, and Midas takes point--he's had the most experience with these sorts of things, having delved into a ruin or two already which may hold any number of magical items or creatures inside. As they climb the steps and reach the top, this decision is validated. They find a hole descending into darkness, stone having crumbled away, and through it Midas senses something atypical through it, having invested some study into the Spirit Arcanum. The three look at each other, and descend into darkness.

                  As the cabal reaches the bottom, they all sense that things have changed, and Midas confirms it: they've passed through a verge. They're in the Shadow now, something which makes Ash especially nervous. This is well outside his frames of reference, and he clutches his pistol tightly.

                  The cabal passes through a carved cavern, the walls covered in murals and reliefs definitely not in-line with any native american iconography any of them are familiar with. Unfortunately, they're so busy gawking at the inconsistent markings that they don't notice that they've begun walking over one, and that there's an odd patterning to it. Too late, Kismet's foot steps on a trigger. The ceiling begins to rush down at them.

                  Ash freezes for a moment, trying to make sense of this. It's like something out of Indiana Jones, and there's something severely ridiculous about it all--why would the makers of this place include this? How would they have set up this mechanism? Why would they do it here? It's both too high-tech and low-tech all at once to make sense. He's pulled out of his confused contemplations by the sound of one of the others yelling, and reflexively he raises his hand, telekinetically stopping and holding the ceiling in place with Forces.

                  After clearing the ceiling trap, the cabal finds itself much more cautious, actually looking at where they're going. They manage to dodge another floor switch, and proceed further into the hidden temple. As they go further, the halls narrow. Cobwebs begin to cover the walls and ceiling, and eventually they find themselves entering into a larger chamber. It's lit a soft blue, and it's so thick with dust and gossamer webs that it almost look like they've stepped into a cloudscape.

                  It's then that Midas notices something moving in the soft light, and draws all their attention to it. It emerges from the fluffy clouds of blue-white, and whatever peace this place might've had is shattered. The cabal finds itself face-to-face with a horse-sized spider, and from the way its rushing towards them it is not happy that they've intruded.

                  Ash reacts first, with the primal terror of an arachnophobe, and draws his gun. He weaves an imago, and a gout of flame fires from its barrel, scorching and burning away a good chunk of the webs. It strikes the spider, melting part of its carapace and enraging it further. Ash has given it a priority target, and the thing leaps at him, sinking fangs into his arm. The obrimos screams and falls to his knees as he feels it injecting something into him--this monster is venomous.

                  A second later, Midas's sword cuts through the spider's body, finishing the job and cleaving the monster in two. Kismet immediately jumps to attending Ash, having invested in the Life Arcanum. She heals his wound and, when he states he feels wrong, she attempts to purify the poison. This doesn't work as well as either of them like.

                  Blackness begins to pour from Ash's eyes, nose, ears, and mouth, and Midas and Kismet realize from how that horrid fountain wriggles and squirms that it's not ichor--it's more spiders! The monster spirit-spider's venom was even more spiders. It takes a moment for it to end and for the dregs to skitter away into the darkness, leaving Ash pale and shivering from the disgusting and unexpected horror he'd just experienced. Midas and Kismet help him to his feet, saying nothing as he shivers in their hold, and help guide him further into the temple.



                  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

                  This was the first session and the one we got the most wrong. There was no rhyme or reason to the gathering or the venture beyond the fact that they were all mages, they were part of a secret society, and clearly this is what secret societies do. A pointless party was held in the woods and the council hands off a quest to the newbies. There are other issues, which you may have noticed already, and I'll be elaborating on those in the next installment.

                  I adjusted and this was adjusted canonically as we went forward, but there's only so much you can do for damage control on a gaffe this large. The gathering was now incidental, there were behind-the-scenes rumblings involving certain individuals which will be elaborated on later, and there's something of a reasoning behind it.

                      It's weird how "Vomiting spiders from his eyes" is nostalgic for me these days. It's one of the few things not regrettable about this bit.

                      This whole arc and the breakdowns that followed were one big lesson in "Learn what your players want and don't just expect the characters to mesh together". It should've been a big warning sign from the get-go that all the players were really, really after different things, given how they reacted.

                      Also, a big reason for why setting guides that help you think about how a given society runs are a good idea.

                      Worth adding to this that the entire temple was meant to be the skeletal remains of an enormous snake god. Don't ask me why. Another good thing: Planning symbolism before spectacle.

                          Chapter 1: Blood and Spiders
                          Part 2

                          Eventually, Ash gets his bearings and manages to walk on his own again, They journey takes them deeper, past smoother rocks which look organically-carved, and finally they exit into a large chamber. There's a large cylinder before them, and in front of that is an altar. It's flanked on each side by stone jaguars, and from the grooves in it and the way they drain into a pattern around the cylinder, it's not hard to tell just what sort of chamber this is: the ritual sacrifice, drain-the-blood kind. There are no other exits out of the chamber than the way they entered, and this is the only path they took which hadn't ended in an empty room, so the cabal concludes that this place must have whatever it was Orcus was looking for--that, or he wanted the giant spider, and the ship had already sailed on that one.

                          They approach the altar, and it's not hard for them to figure things out, even with mage sight: the stone cylinder is tied to the blood-draining mechanisms, as the carvings across its surface depicts a man in a mask holding aloft what is likely a heart above an altar, as others before it bow. From the way the stone jaguars shimmer in their sights, though, it's easy to tell it won't be as simple as a little bloodletting. Since Kismet knows enough of Life to heal, they elect to cut themselves and cheat the ritual. Ash is given the option to abstain, given he just dealt with spider-poison, but as they perform the act it becomes clear they're going to risk exhausting Kismet's mana or bleeding themselves to dangerous points, so Ash eventually chips in.

                          Of course, the stone jaguars don't appreciate the ritual being cheated like this, and very quickly become not-stone jaguars. Just as quickly, however, the three mages put them down, leaving them as shattered remnants on the floor.

                          The three of them stand there for a while, wrists open over the grooves in the stonework, until finally they hear the sound of stone scraping against stone. Kismet runs her fingers across their wounds, and their cuts close. The cylinder rotates and the front of it falls away, revealing its contents. There are some weapons on racks, vaguely resembling but not placeable as belonging to several mesoamerican civilizations of the past, none of them registering as magical in their eyes, but the clear prize is sitting on a pedestal in the center of it all. It's a golden mask, and from its shape it's obviously the one depicted on the stone carvings.

                          Having dealt with stone jaguars, a giant spider, and ritual bloodletting, they carefully take the mask from its resting place and hightail it out of the temple as fast as they can. It feels strange in Ash's grip, like it's made of something which neither absorbs or radiates heat. Outside, as they wait for Marx to open a portal back home, they finally get the chance to socialize. Kismet's reserved but friendly, Midas is all smiles and easy to converse with. Standing out is Ash, who almost seems traumatized by the experience; he's quiet, staring off into nothing, and when he's finally drawn out of that state all they can really get out of him is that this wasn't what he expected at all going into all of this.

                          The portal opens, and the now-proven mages are shuffled back in front of the councilors. Midas passes the mask to Orcus, and even though the old mystagogue's eyes are hidden by his hood it's easy to tell he's happy--not at getting the mask, but that it was this team that did it, as if it proved some small thing to him. To the side, Adelaide watches, still smiling. Orcus praises them, and it comes time for them to speak. Kismet goes first, and her words are a short expression of thanks. Midas is much the same, though more eloquent and with overtures on being pleased to add to the Mysterium's storehouse.

                          Then it's Ash's turn.

                          "What the fuck did you have us do?"

                          The obrimos isn't happy with what's occurred. They treaded haphazardly through an untouched site, smashing stone statues that were possibly thousands of year old, disturbing mechanism worth studying, and killing a- okay, he can't be too upset that the spider died, but maybe killing it wasn't the best option. And for what? A stupid mask? They should've sent a team, they should've sent an archaeologist, they should've sent anyone but three newbies with no experience in any relevant fields.

                          Some of the councilors look unhappy with this turn of events--they clearly weren't on board with this venture, but it's another to be challenged so openly and brazenly. Seshat, especially, looks upset. Adelaide to the side looks horrified. Goch looks somewhere between mortified and furious, mouth hanging open and eyes blazing at the audacity of his mentee.

                          Orcus, for his part, takes it in stride, likely having expected it. He assures Ash that sometimes there's reason and meaning behind events like this, that it's worth trusting in the words and judgment of your wisers.

                          "You move in mysterious ways?" Ash prods. That earns a sharp frown from Orcus. Marx, meanwhile, is beaming at the whole display, having managed to catch herself in the middle of the beginning of a clap before realizing that probably wouldn't help matters.

                          Things conclude quickly after that. Their meeting adjourns and everyone begins to leave. Orcus is disappointed but not entirely crestfallen, and before he goes he pats Ash on the shoulder in a conciliatory gesture, vowing that they'll talk later. Goch stares at Ash with fury, and with all the grace of a husband disappointed by their drunken spouse or a parent angry at the very public tantrum their child just threw, said he'd be pulling the car around and waiting.

                          Ash stands on the edge of the clearing, watching everyone mingle as the impromptu festivities start to end. Midas and Kismet are clustered near Adelaide. They're wearing smiles, exchanging pleasant words. Midas cranes his neck to look for him, and when he finally does he gestures for Ash to join them. Ash raises a hand in refusal, but meets it with a forced smile--something about it would be wrong, with the way Midas is glowing from all the positive attention. Guests file past, thanking Adelaide, and it's easy to tell just who let the word out. Marx is halfway through a portal to god-knows-where when he catches her gaze, and surprisingly she offers him a small wink--a subtle mark of attention that leaves him a little startled.

                          Ash's tearducts still tingle, and he still tastes foul hairs on his tongue. There's a black streak leftover from the adventure down the white dress shirt he's wearing. A breeze picks up, chilling the moist stain. He turns to follow Goch, sparing a final look at Adelaide and what he supposes is now his cabal. Kismet and Midas's backs are to him, but Adelaide is now crouching, stroking the back of a fox that even from here Ash can tell is more than what it appears--probably a familiar, like the spirit hound Midas had told him about. Her eyes briefly meet his, and there's no anger or disappointment in them; they're focused, searching for something in the way he looks back.

                          He vanishes into the woods.

                                          Interlude I: Heroine

                                          When Ash stepped into this wider world, he was confident that it’d be filled with people of high ideals and meaning, but since then he’s seen that just as many of his new peers are capable of short-sighted stupidity, selfishness, and blind arrogance. Goch certainly hasn’t improved those perceptions, with the way he’s been avoiding speaking with Ash like an upset parent. The heady sense of optimism—hell, the feeling of being rescued—has begun to tarnish. Everything is different, but it’s looking the same as it ever was. He lives like a ghost, without a job, without friends, and without a former life worth clinging to.

                                          The next time Ash takes a walk through Portland, Marx is waiting for him. She’s right around a corner, like she’d expected him. Like she’d been waiting for him. It doesn’t take much to sway him, a few nice words, a few remarks on how impressed she and her friends are, and suddenly Ash finds himself with her arm over his shoulders and him being guided into a nearby bar. He’s never had much attention, especially from the opposite sex, and he’s caught too off-guard to even consider not following her lead.

                                          Having been dry his entire life, it’s not in Ash’s character to ask for a drink. Having never been surrounded by people proud of and praising him either, and that makes it impossible to resist the frothing mug that’s pushed in front of his fact. It tastes terrible on his tongue, but their looks are expectant and his need to push something, anything right now spurs him on. He’s not sure what it is, but it hits him fast, making his face warm and his joints lose.

                                          More importantly, it’s easier to not think about where he is, and why he is, and what it all means. For the moment, it just is, and there’s a heavyset man clapping him on the back, while Marx glows like a saint, backlit by some neon jukebox straight out of the 1960s. When she smiles, he can’t help but do the same.

                                          It’s not infatuation, he’s positive. He’s felt that before. This runs deeper, something more meaningful. Gratitude, he fails to think the word. A drowning man being thrown a lifejacket he hadn’t even known he’d needed, and right now he owes that to her. Emboldened, they feed him more drinks and he keeps downing them. The relief is so great he nearly cries. The openness and invincibility is so atypical he worries that there may be something wrong, but it’s hard to think that for long, not when he’s suddenly dragged into a game of quarters. It’s like they knew he’s use his magic to cheat.

                                          Time vanishes. Most of the group has cleared out, and Ash realizes he never asked their names—that he didn’t even want to know. Things are dying down, and he’s struggling to remember when things started up enough for that to begin happening. They move to the run down apartment of one of the group, a guy he overhears Marx talking to enough to catch the name “Charlie.”

                                          Charlie is turning water into cocaine when Ash realizes there’s a drug lab in the corner. Marx is smoking something, and when she catches him staring she holds it out.

                                          “Try this,” she commands.

                                          He can’t find it in himself to deny it. Not on this night, not with the things he’s already done, not from the woman who gave him this. He takes it, and (after clumsily being walked through how to inhale) his lungs take a blow and suddenly that pleasant numbness he’s been walking with metastasizes into a floating euphoria. He laughs. She laughs. He hugs her, and she returns it after several seconds.

                                          It’s the early morning when Marx tears a portal back to the outskirts of Goch’s mansion and leads him through, a back full of… whatever it is he’d smoked stuffed into a pants pocket and a phone number written on a piece of paper covered In chemical formulas in his hand.

                                          “If you want more, call this,” she tells him. “If things get too weird, we’ll make it stop, too,” she offers. He doesn’t want it to stop. And if things were to go south, he couldn’t imagine asking them to fix things—not when he could call on Kismet to clean his blood or brain instead.

                                          A few days later, hanging with Charlie and a few other non-faces, Ash allows himself to try a few pills, one’s he told are safe to mix. Two weeks later, he tries a bit of white powder they’re in the middle of snorting. A month goes by, and he lets them show him how to tie off a vein. Marx’s smile is wide and the fist-bumps he receives from the others are hard enough to bruise, once he does it right himself. By the second month he’s no longer stumbling into situations and being offered advice, he’s asking them directly if they have anything new.

                                          Ash knows it’s dangerous, and risky, and wrong. But that ship’s sailed, hasn’t it? Everything he already knew is wrong, everything that he’d hoped for is wrong, so why wouldn’t this be right? Why can’t it be? It helps him think, it helps him sleep, it makes him feel ways he’s never felt before, and, after all, magic keeps it from having any lasting effects, so they’re being responsible.

                                          Years later, when he stops to think about it, he’ll realize Marx was trying to poach him, probably in hopes that influence over him might extend into influence over Orcus. He’ll also realize she succeeded—he wouldn’t betray his Order, but any position she took he would’ve echoed, and anything she wanted to do he would've supported. He’ll also realize he didn’t mind it.

                                              Interlude II: Pathos

                                              The sun is going down through the window of Ash's spartan guestroom, and he is abruptly startled awake from a nap. He's been sleeping better since developing his vices, and he's been left more tired than usual with his late-night activities with Marx and her buddies. Despite this, the sleep he has been getting has only become more restless, plagued by strange dreams which feel almost as vivid as reality but still leave him struggling to hold onto their meanings once he's awake again. When they're gone, they leave behind a sense of intense, uncertain urgency which drives him crazy. So is the case this time.

                                              Ash leans over to his nightstand and hurriedly pulls out what's in it: a copy of the christian bible and a phonebook. The dreams have left a passage behind that he remembers, and he hurriedly finds the verse. Compulsively, he flips to a page in the yellow pages which corresponds to the verse, and is met with a full-page ad for a nightclub he'd driven past once or twice. For a second, he freezes, wondering what the hell he's doing, thinking about how this makes no sense.

                                              "Nothing better to do," he affirms to himself. He pushes the books away and heads out the door.

                                              By the time Ash has found a parking spot and walked to the club, it's dark. The place is a small-scale operation, as evidenced by it mostly being one room of modest size with a bar on one end, a stage on the other, and seating throughout the rest. A woman dressed up as a stereotypical stage magician is performing on stage at the moment, and Ash takes a seat at the bar to watch. Boredly, he decides to read the auras of the guests, and notices that the magician is the only one who lights up so bright.

                                              She's one of the Awakened. He's feeling less skeptical about those strange dreams now, even as he's trying to convince himself that this is still more than likely a coincidence.

                                              Ash sticks around and watches the show, only having two shots of... honestly he didn't ask the bartender. No sense in getting sloshed right now, he wants to stay clearheaded for this. About ten minutes after the magician's set, she shows up at the bar in plainclothes, asking the bartender for a drink. Ash determines that he should talk to her. Not because of any mysterious dreams, of course, just, y'know, to make conversation. He's only doing it because he wants to, of course. He looks over, and attempts to subtly start a conversation about their shared nature.

                                              "So, what Order?" Ash asks, not even looking at her. Swing and a miss.

                                              The woman turns to look at Ash, and gives him a long, cautious look. He's done something similar enough to know she's examining him like he'd done earlier. Finally, she replies. "Not here," she says, and Ash finds himself being pulled into the club's small unisex bathroom. "Spill," she orders.

                                              Ash holds up his hands defensively. "Argentum Everto, Silver Ladder. I was stopping in on a walk for some drinks and there you were." It's not really a lie, and it was far less suspect than 'my dreams led me to you.' Which they certainly didn't. "I saw you and thought it'd be worth saying hello."

                                              The woman scrutinizes him for a moment more, then extends her hand. "Pathos. Silver Ladder," she says as Ash shakes her hand.

                                              Their conversation is brief--after all, they are having it in a public place in a room usually intended for only one person, but it's amicable. She's trying to inspire some wonder and curiosity through stage magic--not the most original trick, but it's hers and she's good at it. Ash shares his feelings of being the odd-man-out and is entirely honest when he says he'd like to work with her--it's a thing to do, finally, and there's no baggage associated with her like there is Goch or his cabal or the consilium.

                                              "Remember, if anyone asks, I was the best you'd ever had," he jokes as she moves to open the door. He hadn't even realized he'd been making the joke until it was out of his mouth; it's uncharacteristically daring.

                                              Pathos gives him a funny look, but smiles. "Oh yeah, you rocked my world," she returns, turning the door handle.

                                              ~ ~ ~

                                              When Ash finally leaves the club, more tipsy than he'd entered it, he's feeling optimistic again. Sure, the first few months have been a series of disappointments, but he's finding himself capable of things he'd never thought of before, doing things he's never considered. Plus he'll be performing as Dr. Science in a few weeks, once he figures out a direction for the set--he's thinking prop-comedy interspersed with educational punchlines, but it's still a work in progress.

                                              Maybe just because he's been handed more of the same problems doesn't mean he can't make something of them. Maybe he has the right tools, the right friends to make a meaning for himself this time around.

                                              He'll feel a bit silly the day he eventually goes up on stage in a frizzy white wig and waves around a plasma pistol which would comically and mysteriously misfire, but afterwards, when he and Pathos discuss their acts and how to improve them with each other, he can't feel it is anything but the best decision he's made in a while.

                                                  Interlude III: Goch

                                                  "You don't know how?" Pathos asks one night over dinner. Their sets are done, and the place has mostly cleared out. They're splitting a bag of stale chips and some unfinished complementary bread as the staff begins to turn over chairs and set them on tables.

                                                  "I'm sitting there in what's left of the frame of my home, and he just drives up in a limo," Ash affirms. "I thought you- I thought we had a spell or something that tells us."

                                                  "No, Ash," Pathos says. "Unless... he's not an Acanthus, right?"

                                                  Ash shakes his head.

                                                  "Not as likely, then." Pathos takes a bite out of her bread, and chews it thoughtfully. "You need to ask. Or find out. It's not good with us for someone else to know more about what's going on with you than yourself."

                                                  Ash grunts, taking another slice.

                                                  ~ ~ ~

                                                  It's the next morning when Ash approaches Goch at the table. He's sitting across from a mountain of a man named Volund, someone else that near as Ash can tell lives there too--they'd never been formally introduced, and had only caught his name on occasions when Goch had been speaking with the man. They both look expectantly at him; this is the first time they've seen him in the mornings in the past two months, and from the way his eyes are sunken Ash is certain they can tell he hasn't slept. His eyes are also bloodshot and his clothes wrinkled, so he's positive they can read the hangover.

                                                  Silently, Ash takes a seat and a piece of grapefruit. After eating it, he asks: "How did you find me?"

                                                  Volund freezes in the middle of raising a spoon of cereal to his mouth. Goch stiffens, and slowly puts down the paper he's been reading. He's smiling, and it's the opposite of reassuring.

                                                  "I was keeping track of you," Goch says, eyes much wider than they'd been a moment ago.

                                                  Ash sits there for a moment, waiting for Goch to elaborate on that creepy statement. When Goch doesn't, Ash asks "why?" It comes out lower and drier than he wants.

                                                  "My family has," Goch elaborates. "We've been following lots of people. For a while." He leans forward, and Ash instinctively leans back. "You're part of a long, proud line, Argentum."

                                                  Ash momentarily thinks back to his childhood, to dad's rough fists, to mom's backyard target practice on painted and overturned garbage cans, to mom being dragged away to police and ambulance sirens as some nameless officer places a blanket over his shoulders and sits with him.

                                                  "... are you sure about that?" Ash asks.

                                                  Goch nods. "We've been taking care of you. I'm taking care of you." His breathing seems heavier. "My father looked after your family."

                                                  Ash is squirming his way to his feet. "I don't think-"

                                                  "Emerich," Goch says.

                                                  Ash freezes, hand on the back of his seat. Uncle Emerich. Slowly, he eases himself back into his seat, though he keeps himself pressed into the back of it.

                                                  "We've been looking after you for generations. And here you are," Goch says. From the way Goch licks his lips, Ash expects a 'finally' has gone unspoken. "You owe yourself to the Thule Society."

                                                  It takes a moment for Ash to process what he's just heard, and he tries to remember where he's heard that name bef- oh. Oh shit. He goes rigid, and his dark-rimmed eyes go wide. The skin on his face tightens, and he puts on long-lipped look which would be at home on a befuddled frog.

                                                  Goch seems oblivious to Ash's sudden shock, and immediately jumps to his feet. "Here, let me show you something," he says, urging Ash to follow. Ash, numb inside in a way even Charlie's drugs couldn't replicate, stands and follows.

                                                  They walk through the long halls of the mansion, up a few flights of stairs, past a row of portraits which Ash suddenly finds himself seeing in a new light. "Honestly, I don't know why people treat eugenics like it's such a dirty word," Goch says. "Sure, it's hard some formative issues and has been exploited, but it's a legitimate science." They stop before the door to the attic loft. He produces a key, and unlocks the door. "I mean, it's not like we're Nazis." Goch opens the door.

                                                  Ash is treated to the sight of filing cabinets, bins filled with organized and tagged files, and a dozen angry red swastika banners hanging from the walls.

                                                  Goch is silent for a moment. "I thought we took those down," he declares.

                                                  The bottom falls out of Ash's stomach. He's had spiders crawl out of his ears and eyes, but somehow this is the most insane thing he's seen and heard since Awakening. The worst part is, it makes an element of sense--his mother's psychoses, his father's bitter resignation, uncle Emerich's strong kicks and german utterances. It's ridiculous. It's horrifying. This is part of the world he's a part of now? These people claimed membership and allegiance to the society of mages he belongs to now?

                                                  Ash's jaw hangs open, and he looks at Goch. Dark-skinned Goch, who doesn't have a seperate bedroom set up for Volund. Ash tries to say something, but all that comes out of his mouth are giggles and laughs. There's too much monstrous absurdity right now to open that can of worms.

                                                  Goch grins back nervously, and leads Ash into the room. He sits him down on a metal folding chair before a table of file-bins. "Everything here, invoices, lineages, medical records, everything, back four to six generations." He's eager, and slides one bin in front of Ash. He recognizes the family name on it. The giggles have faded, leaving him numb save for an unsettling tickle in his chest. He pulls a file and pages through it, noticing names and dates, noting some people have lightning bolts marked next to their names.

                                                  There's a welling urge to set it all on fire, to put an end to this, to- to- to erase this bit of his history that makes him sick and delirious with disgust. All it'd take is one image to set it all going, to leave this abominable racist legacy in ash.

                                                  "Everything's here, and now you're here," Goch says over Ash's shoulder. And just like that, Ash hears that tone of desperation in Goch's voice. Ash turns his head to look at his mentor and host, at the wide smile on his features. Ash can see the man's childhood, can see the sorts of things Emerich undoubtably did to his emotions, to hear the sorts of things that were undoubtably said. But he can't comprehend the depths of the sort of damage Emerich had inflicted on his own son to make him the dutiful steward of everything against who he was. And he feels that, empathizes with it, sees lines and parallels between the two of them, fighting so hard to hold onto them through the simmering hatred he feels for everything this stands for and for those people who were stupid and monstrous and decadent enough to hold onto it over the decades.

                                                  And like that, everything Ash is feeling drains away.

                                                  "Okay," Ash says, and it feels like vomiting. Calmly, he turns his attention back to his family's file, and blindly reaches for another bin, not caring who he pulls information on.

                                                  "So... please don't embarrass us again," Goch says at the doorframe.

He and Goch never had a chance.

                                                  Ash's chest quakes one in a single chuckle. He keeps reading.

                                                          Orcus sighs. “Yes, they will,” he says, and while Ash can’t see the mystagogue’s eyes under his hood, he can still feel the old man’s pleading gaze.

                                                          “Noted,” Ash says crisply.

                                                          As the council disperses and the cabal leaves the conference room, Ash and Kismet find their shoulders clapped in Tartarus’s iron grip. His gaze is stern. “I know what you’re made of,” he says to Kismet, “so I don’t need to make a case for you. But you-“ he turns his gaze to Ash “-probably need some emphasis.

                                                          “Two of my associates are tracking and mopping up after some hellbeast we call the Ripper. It’s been making gory messes across the state the past few months. There's something I desperately-" Tartarus emphasizes the word "-need to pursue, so I need some sets of able hands to help them take care of this one. I know a third of you is trustworthy, a third of you is competent, and a third of you-“ Tartarus’s gaze settles on Ash. There’s a moment of quiet. “-If he cares enough about people enough to put them in danger for their own benefit, probably cares enough to try and save them if given the chance.”

                                                          The grip on Ash’s shoulder tightens, and then lets go. Ash squirms away violently.

                                                          “Yes sir,” Kismet answers for them, stiffly. Ash shoots a sidelong glare at her, and then another to Tartarus. He’s heard the rundown on their order from Goch, and he hasn’t liked much of it.

                                                          “Whatever, death-squad buddies,” Ash says, disappointed at the lack of rise it gets out of Tartarus. “Just tell us where to go.”
