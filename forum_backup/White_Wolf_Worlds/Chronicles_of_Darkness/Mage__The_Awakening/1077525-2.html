            Spell Factors for Attainments, 101:
            Primary Factor = Arcana Rating of highest Arcana used in Attainment
            Secondary Factors = Half of Arcana Rating of lowest Arcana used in Attainment (round up/down, according to ST preference).

                        The main vehicle for flavoring legacy attainments is taking advantage of the "atypical" allowance to make the attainment slightly more powerful but substantially more niche than its corresponding spell.

                        The rank 4 11Q attainment is clearly "Like Prophecy, but it only works for this one niche kind of thing, but also somehow you get to use Sympathetic magic without including Space."

                        Since all 2E legacies are homebrew except for 11Q, ST approval is important for the particulars of legacy attainments. I'm just trying to discuss guidelines for mechanics that should not be at all controversial based on RAW.

                                It's a four step process:
                                1. Think to yourself, "This would be a cool attainment if only it could also manage X, but the rules don't permit that."
                                2. Go ahead and make the attainment able to do X so it can be worth using
                                3. Realize that your ST will see the attainment as "unbalanced"
                                4. Throw in a few thematically-appropriate circumstantial restrictions so that the attainment really only useful in situations that the ST builds into the story to let it shine
                                Example
                                1. This attainment to predict the future crimes of a suspect would be awesome... except that if the suspect is around for to use this on him,, then that probably means I already caught the guy. Pointless. I need to be able to use it against a subject who's still a fugitive. Arggh, but Space isn't part of this legacy. Even if Space was part of the legacy, the skimpy number of Potency I get to work with won't let me overcome the Withstand for the sympathetic range casting. The rules just aren't supporting me here.
                                2. Fuck it, I'm saying it works on fugitives too. I have no idea what else I could do for a rank 4 attainment for this legacy; this has got to be it, rules-be-damned.
                                3. Well... Gary probably isn't going to let this fly as-is. I gotta throw him some bones somehow.
                                4. Uh, so it only works if I have the information on the guy to build a profile. And it takes a long time to use; I'll blow the reach on sensory range for no good reason. And it takes a sympathy yantra. And I'll blow the Optional Matter effect on something that doesn't really expand the utility of the attainment. Yeah, so now I'll only get to use this if Gary tees it up for me. We'll both be happy.

                                        There are a ton of player capabilities in Mage that are only useful if the ST builds opportunities into the story for them to shine. Spells related to curing/resisting poisons or diseases, spells dealing with Extreme Envionments, the entire Spirit arcanum, etc... Atypical legacy attainments aren't unique in that regard..

                                        My opinion is that only the ST gets to decide when the players can break the rules of the game. So if a player has a legacy attainment that breaks the rules of the game, then the ST decides when the player gets to use it. If you're the ST, you can approve anything you like.

                                        If you don't want to be in the position of judging such attainments, the guidelines in the OP result in attainments that are useful and that follow the rules strictly. Does it result in cookie-cutter legacy attainments? Sure... but when you consider the thematic variations enabled by following the template for each of the ten Arcana, that's still a hell of a lot more variety than is available to e.g. a vampire picking between Cruac and Lancea Sanctum.
