    Mage Magia/Mages and organized crime

    10-24-2016, 04:50 PM
    What are some connections/hooks that would work to connect Mages with organized crime? I've always personally liked the Free Council as something of a mafia-like organization, because I like the idea that they use organized crime the way the Diamond uses mystery cults and fraternal organizations: to give themselves a covert foothold in Sleeper society. Making the mafia influence primary would work best if you're going for a grittier setting, especially one with a lower number of mages, but there's bound to be plenty of involvement with organized crime even in the most optimistic and heroic chronicle, since criminal organizations are basically the closest real-world model we have for the supernatural shadow societies of ChroD.

    Also, damn it, autocorrect put a typo in the title again. Could a mod edit that to "mafia"? Thanks.

      To be honest, this is something that comes up a fair amount in my thinking about the Awakened.

      I think the first thing to say is that every Order has its fingers in organized crime in some shape or form. The Mysterium, for example, is so deep in bed with the black market trade in antiquities that it's downright indecent. The Adamantine Arrow runs guns like nobody's business. The Guardians infiltrate the police and other authorities to get access to their information networks. The Silver Ladder outright has its stock and trade in corrupting Sleeper institutions in ways that vary from merely ethically grey to outright illegal and potentially treasonous.

      As far as connecting the Awakened with Sleepers in the organized crime world, it has to be remembered that most organized crime is a kind of modern feudalism; with street level members paying forth a cut of their income to a lieutenant of a larger crime family, who passes on their own tithe to their superiors, and so on. It's fairly easy for the Awakened to get a reasonably significant foothold in the world of organized crime by subverting any link in the chain, giving them influence over everyone below.

      A thing to ask, though, is why a sorcerer would want to bother. Considerable time and risk have to be placed into controlling street gangs or the mob. What's the reward? Money? The power to inflict violence? To redirect social ills from your neighbourhood?

                              There is a thought form that goes around that has any group that can influence having their fingers in as many pies as possible.

                              That said, while it's situational, considering the wide variety of character options those situations can bump into them. Entire cabals can be devoted to or borne from criminal organizations.

                              An obvious one is that a member of some criminal organization Awakens. They keep in contact, even help, for personal reasons.

                              For more 'professional' reasons, criminal organizations offer catspaws and distractions. The Mysterium protects their libraries but also fills them, and a criminal organization has people who can be tasked to get the objects and grimoires for them. There's also the rumors and gossip that come along. Like any social organization, word travels among them, but as a criminal organization they might be privy to things that most people won't start rumors about, 'We were trying to put the pressure on this shopkeeper and he whistles and a giant made of clay came out.'

                              Plus since many activities mages might find themselves in are illegal or at least sketchy, having an illicit means to get people or objects in and out of places can be a great assist. Groups like the Silver Ladder might appreciate their political or personal power, I believe 'Enforcers' was high on their ladder of desirables. For the Adamantine Arrow, who appreciate conflict, both sides can offer staging grounds for larger tactical strategies, as well as people who may be more willing to join in martial training. While the Free Council might not like the mafia, there are plenty of terrorist and revolutionary cells that might offer the same kind of support as more traditional criminal organizations, and would certainly be useful in detecting Followers of the Lie and attempting to bring Democracy to places that might not have them.

                                              There are three things that mages as a whole tend to be involved in, be they Diamond / FC, Seer, or one of the lesser Banisher, Lich, Reaper, Scelesti or Mad problem children.

                                              ~ Addiction to Mysteries
                                              ~ Dealing with Conspiracies
                                              ~ Hubris

                                              All mages will want some kind of access to underground relic trade. Or any other kind of magic that might come from criminal trades. Reapers and scelesti might, but that's a case-by-case basis, not a trend as a whole. The Silver Ladder want their cults, but that's a form of humans-as-mysteries to study and experiment with, not really using the cults for influence.

                                              Where influence might come into play is the Conspiracy angle. The Diamond want to study and keep mysteries in their natural habitat. The Seers want their gods' mysteries to be the only ones anyone listens to, either converting or destroying them. The FC want to put the mysteries on the 'net for all to see. Okay, a gross oversimplification, but the point is that we have roughly a three way Ascension War going on here, fighting for control of the world's Mysteries. Criminal influence may come into play as a kind of weapon to use against the opposing conspiracies.

                                              Hubris and social influence have little to do with each other, despite what would normally sound like a natural fit. Hubris, in the case of mages, tends to reflect using magic in inappropate ways. Binding living things aginst their will and killing is an issue. Social influence doesn't really register that much.


                                              So, there you go. If you're going to have the Orders associate with the criminal element, you should have one of the above reasons for it. Preparing to fight against the Gods of the world or seeking underground relics are the big two reasons, I find.

                                                  I actually played a F.C. character who started pulling strings in organized crime for two reasons:

                                                  1. Seers were consolidating power using a corrupt government and organized crime.
                                                  2. He needed foot soldiers to combat the Seer crime factions, as he could only be in two places at once, meaningfully. (He generally stashed a "him" or two somewhere safe, though, to get out of dodge as needed.)

                                                  Using unwitting but evil followers of the Lie as troops made him a lot less hesitant to do things that would risk their lives in a war with the intentional Followers of the Lie.

                                                  Mind magic, cleverness, and callousness cut a really frightening figure.

                                                  This character was an honestly kind person who actively maintained Wisdom 7---Was working on 8. He came to the conclusion that, to actually make any headway against the Lie, he needed to become a weapon, so he did.

                                                  That's the creepiest part about that story: He planned precision bombing, assassination, and triggering a gang-war not despite being a kind-hearted person but directly because of it. Compassion for his fellow man wouldn't allow him to do less. This is one reason the F.C. are every bit as scary any other Order.
                                                  ( He was spending considerable thought and planning on containing the collateral damage, obviously, but the fact that it was coming was certain. )


                                                  Hooks are varied:

                                                  Senator Baconpants is pushing one wacky bill. Given the bill entirely hurts him and his interests, it's obviouys someone has hooks in. Do the mages simply dislike the change or will they free him?

                                                  They say mages have no trouble finding money, and that is, for the most part, true. Doing so legally isn't nearly so convenient.

                                                  Want to disappear? Need new paperwork? Creating a new identity is a lot of work. The best identities require illegal aspects.

                                                  You know what's a great way to get access to people? Give 'em what they want. A Mastigos can easily figure out what people want and give it to them. With a little more legwork, he can adjust it on a large scale, too.

                                                  Criminal magi can come to the attentions of law enforcement and criminals. Both might attack or recruit them.

                                                  So far, I've left it ambiguous whether these criminals are aware of magic. They can be. If they are, they would seek leverage on as many magi as they could get or destroy any rivals to those they consider loyal.
