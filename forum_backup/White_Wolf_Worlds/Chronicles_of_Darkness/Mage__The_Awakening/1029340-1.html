        WHW - It sounds like what you're after will need to evolve with the game you're in, but I can at least give it a shot.

        Fragments of Eternity (●●●●● ●●●●●)

        Death is the road to awe. When one witnesses the precipice between life and its end, there one may stand at the edge of a great cliff and gaze into the undiscovered country of Stygia. It is often in such moments that Sleepers come face to face with the Lie, and those who struggle through the Mystery Play find their way to Awakening.

        Sometimes, they bring things with them for the journey; things so symbolically bound to them that these items become Artifacts in the process; the physical embodiment of a Supernal thought.

        Fragments of Eternity are Stygian Artifacts created when someone suffers a violent death that sees pieces of the source of their demise thrust into their body and left there. This may be metal or glass shards from a car crash, a broken piece of a blade, or a bullet. These fragments are never something forgettable; they're violently obvious, and anyone bearing such fragments feels them with every move and breath, suffering a constant reminder of their demise.

        (Those with a Fragment of Eternity embedded in them suffer a -1 to all Physical rolls from the pain of a foreign object grinding around in their insides.)

        The first and most obvious feature of a fragment is its connection to death. Whenever a Fragment is within (bearer's Gnosis x 10) yards of a person who has died in the past day or who will die within the next 24 hours, it begins to hurt. How this pain manifests is usually tied to its initial sensation of entry; a bullet burns, glass shards cause a sharp shock, etc. This increases the penalty the object inflicts to -2 as the object tugs at their insides and points them towards the doomed individual.

        (This is a Death/Time 1 Knowing/Unveiling effect.)

        In most cases, this is as far as the story goes. Most Moros prefer to have the foreign object removed than continue to endure its presence. Of those who do not, though, there are... Stories.

        Fragments of Eternity were meant to claim their bearer's life. This sensation is never lost on the bearer; they feel it there, always; a reminder that they should be dead. Some describe feeling the object move, as if it's still trying to kill them; squirming in their guts. They're not wrong.

        If allowed to fester within the Mage, the fragment begins to take root. Individual fragments begin to grow; extruding wires of metal or glass like veins and constituting themselves into a network of threads and nodes. Allowed to continue doing so, they will keep extending through the host's body until they reach vital organs and begin to wrap around them. The fragments slowly infiltrate these tissues and transform them into inhuman structures. Autopsies on Moros who endured the presence of their fragments may find crystalline stomachs, brass leaf intestines, or literal iron lungs. These corpses might be confused for Sariras if not for the fact that they still rot.

        After a period of around six months, the fragments are now permanently a part of the Mage's Pattern; removing them (without sufficiently swift application of regenerative Life magic) is lethal.

        At this stage, the character no longer suffers damage like a living human; their organs are hard and their blood is often replaced with strange substances like motor oil or gasoline. They take only Bashing damage from any attack that does not score an exceptional success and do not risk unconsciousness from having their last health box filled with Bashing. If their final health box is filled with lethal damage, the character appears to die but their soul remains trapped within the matrix of wires within their body. Matter magic to "repair" the character can restore them to whatever mockery of life they now exhibit, so long as it's applied before their body rots away.

        In addition to the above, the character gains 3/3 armour.

        (This is a Death 3 effect.)

        At this stage, though, the character's new bodily additions become hungry. The Artifact, rather than producing its own Mana, drains Mana. This Mana comes from the pool of the Mage who carries it at a rate of one per day. If the Mage cannot pay the due, the Artifact forces Scouring upon them and takes all the Mana from doing so. The character cannot heal the associated damage until they regain at least three Mana.

        During this time, the character feels increasingly morbid and obsessed with death, most often their own. The character is frequently haunted by dreams of an afterlife they have been denied; of an end or rest that is forbidden to them. There is a persistent sensation that they have missed their chance, though for what is hard to say.

        Any time the basic power of the Artifact is triggered, the character gains the Obsessed Condition towards witnessing (or bringing about) the death they know is coming.

        For the few who carry on with such Artifacts, this is often where it ends. Obsessed with their nightmares, many take their own lives or, just as often, are killed by their peers who covet the Artifact within. Many are unwilling to fight back.

        The ones who struggle on despite this, of course, inevitably fail at some point to feed their manavoric organs. When this happens, the character may enter a third and more horrific phase still.

        Hungry for the death it was denied, the Artifact will blossom further. It pushes its way through the bearer's skin; extruding nodules from their arms and hands. These nodules, when commanded, erupt into strange weapons, usually symbolic of how the bearer died - bone spurs for one mauled by animals, glass spears for those impaled on broken glass, bludgeons for a car crash, or even spewing bullets or flames for firearms and explosions. These weapons never count as improvised for the wielder and have a Durability of 4 and Structure of 2.

        In addition to the natural benefit of these weapons, any time they strike, the drain off the dying energies of the victim to feed the Artifact. This causes the weapons to deal (4 - Stamina) extra levels of lethal damage on a hit, with the extra levels dealt transferred to the wielder as Mana. These wounds are never natural in appearance, often putrefying as if inflicted on a long-dead corpse, though these marks tend to fade on healing.

        On top of these benefits, the character's armour rises to 4/4 and they no longer suffer the penalties associated with the Artifact's presence.

        The drawbacks, though, are severe. The greater expenditure has greater costs; the Artifact now drains three Mana a day from the host (and has similarly dire demands if not fed; inflicting three Resistant lethal levels or draining three attribute dots if not fed). Likewise, the host must roll Resolve + Composure whenever below half their maximum Mana to avoid gaining the Obsessed Condition towards feeding.

        (This is a Death 4 effect, mimicking the effects of Devouring the Slain.)

        ---

        There you go. A horrible artifact for you.

            This is extremely amazing and definitely hitting the themes, feel and mood I crave in the game. This is the brand of personal horror I really lacked in our main Mage game; I love the whole grotesque, body horror feel, and I love how you weaved mana sustenance into it. I wonder how you would tackle my Obrimos idea (in this case, "target" being the bioelectric field of your body, including electricity in the brain and neural system; "supernal, Forces-Prime infection of your electric self") .

            I feel that Fragments of Eternity would work best as a set of evolving Conditions, reflecting deeper and deeper stages of the infection. This looks, smells and works perfectly as a Persistent Condition of some sort. When I'm done with being sick (goddamn it), I'll try turning these into Conditions and posting here, if you are OK with hijacking of your thread for that. Actually, I really dig the idea that Paradox poisoning of the Pattern could so similar things, too...
            Yes, I really like idea of Magic slowly turning it's users into inhuman monsters, including their physical and spiritual selves, can't help it.
