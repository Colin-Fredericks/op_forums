        The basic difference between a Sleeper and a Sleepwalker is that the Sleeper has a piece of the Abyss in their soul, the Sleepwalker doesn't.
        ​A Sleeper may not recognize the difference between just plain old supernatural and the Supernal but the Abyss does, it recognizes Supernal magic when it sees it and reacts regardless of the beliefs of the Sleeper.
        ​That Abyssal shard is, however, dependent upon the senses of the Sleeper so if the Sleeper's senses don't see the Supernal at work neither does the Abyss and therefore there is no Dissonance.
        ​This means that if the Sleeper lives in a culture where non Supernal magic is common and he doesn't see the actual casting of the spell, both he and that Abyssal shard can easily believe that the fruit of that spell came from something else and there is no Dissonance.
        ​So long as a Mage in such a culture hides his actual Supernal casting from the senses of the Sleepers around him everything is good. He doesn't have to worry about the two stones he just stacked atop each other with magic falling down the first time his Sleeper neighbors see them.

        ​In the Time Before, if such a time ever actually existed, there was no Abyss, no Abyssal shards and thus, no Sleepers. Everyone was by default then a Sleepwalker with the Awakened being those who had taken the step of creating a sympathetic link to the Supernal.

                Originally posted by 2ptTakrill View Post
                The basic difference between a Sleeper and a Sleepwalker is that the Sleeper has a piece of the Abyss in their soul, the Sleepwalker doesn't.
                ​A Sleeper may not recognize the difference between just plain old supernatural and the Supernal but the Abyss does, it recognizes Supernal magic when it sees it and reacts regardless of the beliefs of the Sleeper.
                ​That Abyssal shard is, however, dependent upon the senses of the Sleeper so if the Sleeper's senses don't see the Supernal at work neither does the Abyss and therefore there is no Dissonance...
                This is the way I understand it. It's not important what the Sleeper believes, but what the Abyss perceives. However, I would clarify that a Sleeper doesn't have to recognize it as Supernal themselves to trigger paradox. It's not "that must be Supernal magic, as opposed to regular magic, which I believe in" that's required.

                The way I run it, it's specifically the "this can't be happening!" reaction that draws the Abyss' attention, even if the phenomenon fits a Sleeper's worldview or could otherwise be rationalized later. Thus, even if a given Sleeper believes in aliens, if a supernal UFO suddenly lands on their front lawn, all it takes is that moment of the Sleeper disbelieving their senses for the abyss to take notice and slip through. (By contrast, a person accustomed to personal visits from aliens wouldn't immediately trigger dissonance if a mage were to counterfeit one with Supernal magic.)

                To give a less extreme example, it's perfectly possible by modern science (if improbable) to spontaneously loose feeling in both legs and vision in one eye, but most people are still going to experience a moment of disbelief, and bam, dissonance (yes, I have a player who has Exceptional Luck as a rote, why do you ask?). To wit: it's "Sleepers witnessing an obvious manifestation of the Supernal" (pg. 298) not "Sleepers witnessing an obviously Supernal manifestation".

                In short, at least in my games, you can't cheese your way out of paradox/dissonance when using "vulgar" magic just because it could be explained after the fact. It might be harsher than the way some people run it, but I found nothing beats ruining a character's power trip than rolling a critical success on those sweet, sweet paradox dice . (Let's just say that Acanthus will think twice before he hexes an entire room full of Sleepers again.)

                Edit: felt like I would more directly address the OP. From my position, a society that believes in magic would certainly let a mage get away with more, but unless the people are actually experiencing the supernatural on a regular basis, most obvious supernal magic will still suffer dissonance because it's not "normal". I would also keep in mind that it's pretty common for "magic users" to be pushed to the edges of society (not necessarily shunned, but certainly kept "apart") even in cultures that have them as part of them belief system. Even if you belief in it, magic is still "the weird stuff we don't understand", and watching the village wizard summon time-demons from Arcadia will still break your brain. Again, it's less about belief and more about Cthuloid horrors trying to break into reality.

                        Originally posted by 21C Hermit View Post

                        I read that part as Quiescence being shaped by perception, not belief. After all, we aren't talking about Ascension here, where Reality Zones and Paradigms and subjective truths are a thing. .
                        There are definitely not subjective truths (mage related ones anyway), but there definitely are subjective lies. One of the few (ambiguously) positive elements mentioned in Seers of the Throne is the writeup of the Corrupt Medic; that a legitimate form of Seer character is a super doctor, who tricks people into thinking they've been healed with radical medical practices when in reality she's using healing magic. They aren't above inflicting diseases that they later cure to reinforce that Doctor Knows Best of course. There is definitely a strong element of belief there. As a side note, the medical industry fits the feel of the Seers even more than it did the Technocracy; medical professionals often intentionally or unintentionally cultivate an air of mysterious, unquestioned authority and hidden lore with their weird symbols and their latin.

                        Another archetypal Seer character mentioned is the Investigator of Miracles; an apparent priest who goes around investigating and respectfully explaining away miracles, taking over the rare, genuinely miraculous sites; and, if they are not miracles from the Seers, they will be sanitized into mundanity. This, too, definitely involves belief.

                        Originally posted by 21C Hermit View Post
                        The Diamond and the Free Council aren't working on different Paradigms that make their magic work differently.
                        The Free Council most definitely does engage in (little p) paradigm engineering with sleepers. They do indeed monkey around with what people believe is possible and impossible. The Free Council, like the Guardians of the Veil, do sometimes find themselves becoming so obsessed that they act in ironic common cause with Seers and even nudging people away from Awakening on purpose. There are Libertines who try to push the boundaries of real scientific research, Libertines who make enhanced tech gadgets and "fake tech" (using spells to do as tech did but better, like making "better robots" and "better flamethrowers" and so forth), and Libertines that try to make sleepers just plain accept a wider range of possibilities. Libertines don't suddenly start thinking they're just gifted boffins a la some confused owod technomancer, but different Libertines definitely vary on what the borders of acceptability, possibility and probability are. In terms of owod analogies, some techy Libertines are more like technomancers with pet scientific theories and all, and some are almost totally ignorant regarding science and just use technology as special effects props.

                        Paradigms are inward and outward focused in MtAsc, but while inward facing (lower case) paradigms have little effect in Awakening, mages most certainly do present themselves as boffins, miracle workers, wise women, and extreme martial artists to give sleepers different ways to relate to their magic (if they're not totally secretive at all times), and boffins and extreme martial artists do indeed work slightly differently even though they are (hopefully) totally aware of what they're doing. The Guardians, on the other hand, definitely trick themselves almost as much as they trick anyone else.

                        One of the big charms of Awakening for me (someone who grew up with Ascension) is that instead of it being mostly subjectivity (with some objective elements that mages usually aggressively ignore), its two objective realities (the Supernal and Phenomenal Worlds) sandwiching an anti-reality (the Abyss) with the vagaries of mages, sleepwalkers, sleepers and the Lie, and different levels of reality-ness and fallen-ness (verges, demenses, a mage in his private dream world vs a sleeper in his with mage guests, etc). What is particularly curious is that in the most "subjective reality" possible, his own personal dream world, a mage suffers no paradox at all, while even though while you're asleep you would logically not be able to even be able to begin to understand the borders of natural and supernatural, a sleeper's Oneiros is automatically cognizant of all magic transpiring inside as traumatizing, impossible, and supernatural. Apropos of Inception, I suppose.

                        But if you don't think the level of cognizance of the supernatural and the impossible fits Awakening's themes pertaining to whether sleepers are affected by the Curse, the dictionary definition of cognizance includes: "awareness, realization, or knowledge; notice; perception." There is a distinct "eureka!" moment associated with the Quiescence, as it is described as a botched awakening. The Lie isn't there to keep people from believing in weird stuff, its there to keep people from awakening, and its there to keep the awakened and Supernal beings on their toes, with some weird stuff being prohibited as a side effect.

                        Finally, its worth pointing out that 1e->2e radically changed these elements: stuff is just plain vulgar or covert in 1e with no appeals process allowed (except for the worse), while in 2e sleeper reactions are almost an afterthought and the main cause of problems is reaching.

                                    Neolithic mages with fully believing sleeper friends have to do the overtly supernatural elements of their craft in private (ie. magicking up furrows for an irrigation canal that would have taken all the men in the village busy for months), but they can display the finished product in public afterwards, and they can do the yantras with the complete knowledge and intent of the town that, yes, he's doing magic and that, yes, the ditches were made by magic. Yes, the tattoo I gave you while you were sedated gives you extra health and luck.

                                    Although sleepwalkers are not a particularly important part of the sample Neolithic setting and not particularly prevalent (if the Wise are thought of as "fire bearers" then the Wise think of sleepwalkers as "ember bearers," and there's not really any further attention given to them), even sleepers make use of extensive rituals and ceremonies to protect themselves from spirits, to placate and sacrifice to spirits, and so forth. You can actually, knowingly, make sacrificial pots for mages to use as yantras every day, in a village whose building you know was magicked into existence (while noone was watching) by a mage, help a mage perform his yantras, and then say goodbye to the mage who you helped with yantras who tells you "Ok, I'm going to go into the sacred hut to finish my obvious magic, bye" and not be a sleepwalker. It seems to be less belief and more partial magic use.

                                    Its ironic that mages derive fabulous benefits from sleepwalker assistance (a mage in a village comprised of 100 sleepwalkers he's friends with could potentially get 100 extra spells) but they have a hard time directly making them directly, while other monsters sometimes make them in bulk.

                                    Also, I can't find any evidence that monsters are themselves sleepwalkers -- it seems to be "integrity + supernatural merit reflecting innate capability/minor template/sleepwalker merit/etc." Meaning, I don't think they can carry spells like a sleepwalker can.
