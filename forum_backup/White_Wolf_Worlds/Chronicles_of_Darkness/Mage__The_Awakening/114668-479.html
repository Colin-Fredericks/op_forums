        One thing that I think can be worthwhile for designing Legacies is to consider that not all of the burden of what the Legacy is about needs to rest on the Attainments.

        I think there are other examples, but a prominent one is the Echo Walkers. Their Attainments are about using Life and Prime to enhance themselves into an image of angelic proto-humans. But where they get that image from is using a Death + Prime spell that messes around with people's souls and provides a glimpse inside of them, which they regard as providing insight into a more primal soul.

        You could have other characters where they use spells to modify drugs to imbibe as a procedure for using Attainments that do other things.

        Although now, as far as powers go, I'm imagining a Legacy that imbibes substances for the purpose of forming a deeply intuitive sense of them and kind of sympathy, internal processing to facilitate external transformation.

        I'm thinking Thyrsus who extend the Path's usual image of interconnected natural world to the substance things are composed of (in their own right rather than by way of Spirit), with shaping and transmutation through a lens of living properties in seemingly non-living things. It starts off with modification to substances that can interact in a biology (maybe not just drugs, also salt and soil) and builds up to the creation of objects that can grow and heal akin to living things.

                                Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post

                                New question: Is there a official timeline of the Nameless War (I love that name), and the Great Refusal?


                                I expect that or something close to it would be in the history section of the Free Council book (at this time the last remaining absence from my collection).

                                Mind, I would find it logical that the Nameless War didn't exactly lend itself to a straightforward timeline. Not when it was distributed across so many fronts of numerous columns who had common cause but loose organisation. I expect that when it comes to compiling information about the thing, many Awakened scholars would find it easier to make timelines in the history of particular Assemblies, or some of the most famous columns and Strategos, rather than a singular cohesive timeline covering the whole effort.

                                And even then I expect that there are a lot of secrets that were never jotted down and that the interests of cohesion in the Pentacle would prefer to let lie. Cruel actions taking place in the shadows that could still spark outrage if brought up.

                                Still, I'll be interested to see what the book makes of it.

                                (As far as I'm concerned, Kosciej the Deathless is close enough to canon as one of the Free Council's Founders, although not exactly in a position to create a new Celestial Ladder.)

                                Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
                                To Ecstatics, they are not simple chemicals, they are tools to expand their minds and turn their perceptions inside-out.
                                I just have trouble associating Time with altered perceptions. The only spells or attainments that would work would seem to be unveiling, and neither of times two example spells fit well.
                                Well, let me have a look...

                                Time 1: Divination and Postcognition can qualify after a fashion, but Perfect Timing is probably the closest to a sense of altered perceptions focused in the here and now (which might be more of an Ecstatic thing).
                                Time 2: Choose the Thread has that element of glimpsing to improve the efficiency of actions, and the experience of Tipping the Hourglass to speed up and slow down the experience of time has a perceptual element. One might opt to portray Constant Presence as a kind of firm awareness of self that can't be infringed upon.
                                Time 3: Acceleration could be regarded as an extreme increase in perceptual speed. A thematically modified version of Temporal Summoning could make the reclaimed past self less of a physical thing and more evoking a prior state of mind for purposes of reclaiming lost perspectives or exploring alternative ways they might have developed.
                                Time 4: Present as Past has a pretty direct perceptual element, I think, a heightened state of awareness for events that are about to happen, and an openly stated element for social interaction either in becoming harder to persuade from anticipating all approaches or seeing paths directly to another's heart. Prophecy might be closer to a perceptual thing than actual Divination is, since it's based not on analysing what will be but meditating on possible outcomes for hypothetical actions or methods that might be used to achieve a desired result. Rewrite History could build off of that prior rendition of Temporal Summoning, a mage being able to immerse themselves in the memory of learning skills and other experiences with such a heightened state of mind that they can effectively construct how those efforts might have gone into different paths in life.
                                Time 5: Blink of an Eye or Temporal Pocket are basically Philip Fry on 100 cups of coffee, your mind racing to such a degree that everything else seems to be standing still in comparison.

                                In addition, while there are no published spells to affect Temporal Sympathy, it is stated how the experience of that has a perceptual element; a stronger sympathy creates a sense of more "closeness" to the time in question, making the memory of it easier and more vivid and compelling, and a destroyed sympathy is said to cause memory of a history to fade away. One could include elements for a mage to manipulate their own sympathy along those lines, perhaps in a bit of reverse order whereby what they can recall or obscure being the part that alters the actual substance of the thread.

                                At a moment like this, I think the descriptions of Mage Sight in Signs of Sorcery could be useful, the descriptions of what each Arcanum looks like to each Path. Time as a Cult of Ecstasy might regard it could be more fitting to Mastigos than Acanthus, and its internally focused purposes could be something that aligns with common Mastigos aspirations of self-awareness and mastery.

                                                Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
                                                New question: Is there a official timeline of the Nameless War (I love that name), and the Great Refusal?
                                                So it turns out that the original Free Council book opted to be pretty bare bones about the nature and history of the Nameless War. There's context for why there were lots of revolutionary Nameless opposed to the Diamond through the 19th century, and that they were intertwined with Sleeper revolutions, but not much about events.

                                                The Great Refusal does receive a bit of elaboration, though, in terms of more nuanced and messy history. Its stated that Seer emissaries made contact with Nameless groups as early as 1895, that there were ongoing rejections based in ideology that prompted violent reprisal from Seers, and then some prominent figures promoting and coordinating a plan to string Seers along until they were vulnerable, complicated by columns who actually wanted to join up, leading to the game being given away early and efforts divided between hitting Seers and purging those among their own who were looking to join.

                                                New Year's Eve 1899 is maintained as the official date of the Assembly that brought them together as a fully realised Order, while the Silver Ladder book briefly described the Great Convocation that resolved to invite them into an alliance.

                                                Overall, I'd maintain the idea that the Nameless War took a form that defies compiling a straightforward timeline as opposed to describing a century of continuous hostility.

                                                            Originally posted by Horodrigo View Post
                                                            Is it possible for mages to protect other supernatural beings against their bans and banes? For example, veiling a Vampire from the sun with Force or a Werewolf/Changeling from silver/iron with Matter?
                                                            Not in the sense of removing a Bane or Ban from the supernatural being, but mages can definitely do a lot to prevent interactions. Forces Shielding can prevent sunlight and fire from hitting the vampire. Matter can make a werewolf physically repel silver, but should something be able to force silver through the spell's effect to make contact, the werewolf will react as usual. Same thing for changelings and iron, but even the presence of iron can do a lot to mess with changelings, and that's difficult to do anything about without straight up removing the iron completely from the location.
                                                            Bans can be a lot more difficult to circumvent depending on their nature, but Fate can go a long way to avoid situations where a Ban would come into effect.
