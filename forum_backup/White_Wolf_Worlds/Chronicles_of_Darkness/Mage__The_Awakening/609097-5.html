        Approximately 3 out of 5 Mages don't have the same worries about being trans (or hell, any sort of other self-identity issue, really.)

        An Obrimos can use Prime to make it intuitively obvious what gender they are, regardless of how much their fleshy shells might seem to disagree. And this would pretty much cover it for a lot of transgender people, I would think (it would obviously be insufficient for transsexuals.)

        Thyrsus are really obvious.

        And Mastigos? Dear lord, Mastigos. I don't think it would take Space 5 to change your True Name, it would just involve being willing to maintain a proper Veiling (heck, one of the spoiled spells let you wipe all your sympathetic connections, so it isn't like they are sacrosanct.) As for Mind...

        Any Mage who is sufficiently good at Mind can wake up in the morning and decide to try out being gay that day. Or straight. Or a bigot, Or a member of this or that social group. Heck, it's a path to personal growth, if you think about it - walk a thousand miles in someone else's shoes, and all that.

        Their gender doesn't match their fleshy shell, and they don't have the Life to change it? Just change your gender. As far as you are concerned, the problem is solved. Prime might also be able to pull this off... maybe.

        Acanthus and Moros are kinda out of luck though (I dunno - maybe Fate could streamline the whole transitioning process with amazing luck. But I can't see a way to use Death or Matter in an appropriate way). But otherwise...

        Unlike the proverbial physician, Mages can heal themselves. So I'd assume that any Mage that didn't address the issue has a good reason for doing so.

        Now, the Hubris of doing so...

        I like those spins. Never thought of using Prime or Mind like that. (I'd favour using forces to set people on fire if they're clocking me but APPARENTLY that's 'hubristic' and 'excessive' and 'a breach of the veil')
        I mean the Space thing is just explicitly against RAW/RAI, but -shrug- that's been hashed over. To death. And back.

        mind though, 'a good reason for doing so' is a very big category. There's all the same reasons that a Sleeper who can pass as cis might choose not to go stealth - identity, community, solidarity - as well as magical reasons; it's something someone would have to upkeep, on a practical level, and for most people it's a strong part of who you are - so embracing it might well be a path to Gnosis.

                        (crossposting to Wyrd's name thread)

                        I am considering changing it to "sympathetic name," for the same reason. "True" names (apart from their use in literally *two* places in the whole gameline to designate the Archmastery Excision of Space) run counter to Awakening's magical thesis, where a mage's deliberately-constructed magical identity (their "Shadow" name) can be said to be more real than their Sleeper identity. There's room in Mage's cosmology for mages who argue that one's Fallen identity is inescapable, and it's the Seers of the Throne. Compare the Daksha (creepy as they are for other reasons) with the Chrysalides: the Pentacle Legacy is accepting of transformation, the Seer Legacy is based on dismorphia.

                        I'm currently seeing if there's room for a bit more explanation of the topic in the corebook, as institutionally the Pentacle are ahead of Sleeper society when it comes to accepting transgendered, agendered, or similar. Every single mage has a deadname-equivalent, and referring to another mage by her sympathetic identity unless you're the most intimate of friends is incredibly rude at best and a punishable offence in most Silver Ladder, Guardian, and Free Council Caucuses. When your sex and gender are only three dots of Life and Mind away from being changed magically, when you appear in the Astral as your soul's self-image no matter what your physical body looks like, when mage society is founded on the notion of escaping from the oppressive systems of the Lie and defining yourself as a magical being, a woman insisting that she's a woman despite Sleepers' limited society saying otherwise is no big deal.

                                        Okay, so.

                                        I snuck a sidebar onto the end of the 2e core's Shadow Name section. I've tweaked its wording a few times in discussion with several trans friends:

                                        Shadow Names
                                        A Shadow Name is an expression of who the mage thinks she is in the Supernal World, without the Fallen, mundane person inside it. Most are only used when performing magic or meeting other mages, like a persona that can be adopted at will. Some mages change their voice or body language so much that at first glance they’re unrecognizable when out of character.
                                        Mages take their Shadow Names very seriously. They’re symbols, and therefore Supernal, influencing their bearers’ lives. A mage naming himself after a god, for example, would be able to use items relating to his namesake as magical tools, but might find his magical career influenced to force him into that identity, events eerily paralleling the myth-symbols he made his magical identity from. Not every mage is reenacting her namesake’s legend — not even most of them — but a Shadow Name is not something to be chosen lightly.
                                        A Shadow Name serves two functions. First, it helps conceal the mage’s sympathetic name from enemy mages who might use that knowledge to cast spells on her sympathetically (see p. XX). More importantly, though, it prevents the mage’s Nimbus from affecting those who only know her by her Sleeping identity. So long as the mage practices magic within her Shadow Name, only those things close to her as a mage (her cabal, sanctum, apprentices, dedicated tools, etc.) are affected by her Nimbus.
                                        A Shadow Name’s protection works best, however, if a mage avoids using magic in the presence of the loved ones of his Sleeper life. If he allows too much of a connection to form between his Shadow Name and his un-Awakened family and friends they may yet fall under the influence of his magic.
                                        Using a mage’s sympathetic name to address or refer to him except in the most intimate of friendships is considered offensively rude by the overwhelming majority of magical societies. In some Orders, such as the Free Council, Silver Ladder, and Guardians of the Veil, it’s a punishable crime.
                                        [sidebar]

                                        Magical Identity Politics
                                        As a culture based around the rejection of mortal oppression who almost all construct new identities for magical purposes, the Awakened have always been more enlightened than Sleeping society with regard to acceptance of different genders and sexualities, even if they remain as sadly prone to other prejudices as Sleepers. Many mages appear as different sexes when in the Astral Realms, or use their spells and Legacies to become who they are, without the Lie’s constraints. Many Mastigos and Thyrsus deliberately change gender, sex, and orientation temporarily as a means of self-examination and growth.
                                        Historically, Sleeping “magical” practices were a haven for transgender and non-binary people in many cultures; modern mages know that these roles are sometimes just as limiting as outright persecution, but some older Legacies were formed around them and must contend with how their inheritance appears to the modern Orders.

                                        Dave Brookshaw

                                            Looks good.

                                            I just have a couple of very minor points. First, a mage need not necessarily use his Sympathetic / Real Name as part of his "Sleeping Identity." Just as mages can easily change sex and gender, their magic permits them to change minds and physical and electronic records, if for no other reason that to conceal their Sympathetic Name. I hope the text makes clear that mages have multiple identities / names, including their Shadow Name, Sympathetic Name, and one or more Sleeping Names / identities, that may or may not be the same as or related to their Sympathetic name. Something as simple as entries in the Lexicon section of the book might provide some clarity.

                                            Although I have no issue with a permanent and indelible Sympathetic Name, the sidebar doesn't really solve the concern some have expressed that, absent the intervention of an archmaster, a character has no control over their Sympathetic Name, as it may not reflect or even be opposed to their Fallen identity. My reading of the relevant forum threads was that people fully understood and have no objection to the nature and impact of a Shadow Name, but didn't like the implications of a Real Name that they couldn't change because of real world transgender discrimination issues.

                                            Lastly, can a mage change his Shadow Name, and is the magical tool use relating to the Shadow Name automatic or does it require a separate merit?
