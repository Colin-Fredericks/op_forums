    Alternative Duel Arcane

    04-07-2017, 09:45 PM
    So I recently got to try out the new Duel Arcane rules. I like the idea behind them, but ultimately in practice I found the system to be kind of boring. To be fair though, the original 1e duel arcane didn't jive with me either - I've always been a fan of the alternative forms more because most generic duels boil down to one extended, resisted, and contested action. So, I'm currently looking for two things:

    1) House Rules to make the 2e Original Duel Arcane more coherent and enjoyable. I liked that the Doors were only knocked off one at a time, but I'm not sure I liked that the duels were only feasible at high arcana levels, and that if you defended it basically became trying to negotiate. For that matter, I didn't like negotiation in it's current form - my current thought process is to invert it to make it so that you offer a condition your opponent agrees to in order to remove one of your doors, rather than offering to do something for them so they will lower their door.

    2) And ideas on what kinds of magical duels might be considered alternative. I'm currently in the process of creating a territorial duel where mages create avatars and territory and try to destroy the other mage's landscape to win, but that's a work in progress before I post it here. What ideas might you suggest? How would you pull up things like Shadow Chess and other nifty duels?

              Personally, I use some sort of Astral Battle as Arcane Duel.
              The normal rules for dream form apply, them the combatants get dispelled from previous spells as a form of remove cheating. (I permit clever cheats, if the players come with some clever ones)
              Them, they fight until someone is soul shocked. But instead of Soul Shocked Condition, they get the Defeated Condition.

              There are some simple rules:
              - No Unmaking Spells. (If you are a Master, you have many ways to deal with someone without having to use this)
              - You can't use spells to affect your opponent real body. (Enforced by a Ban spell)
              - You can offer deals the same way the normal Arcane Duel suggest but they don't give you any bonuses, just an incentive for your oponent to forfeit and not be totally humilliated byt the loss.
              - The first turn happens at the same time, to remove some "I Win" spells from the table. After this, we follow the Initiative order. (Clever mages use this to protect themselves on the first turn)

                  That does sound interesting.

                  One form of duel arcane that I have currently thought up involves utilizing an avatar and creating a virtual landscape in a theoretical supernal plane - so, effectively an astral battle (just not in the astral world). This landscape would be decided by both parties and provide a variety of tools that would feasibly be in such a landscape (for instance, rivers have water and a volcano has lava - you might not find it everywhere, but if you have the volcano you can utilize heat, fire, lava, etc because it's there to target and you don't necessarily have to transmute it. Animals might be in a forest, etc etc.

                  The goal is that you can either attack as you would normally in a duel arcane, using a sword - which is a manifestation of the raw supernal rather than any attack and when you fail cannot be used with the arcana you used - or you can have your avatar attack their avatar. Succeeding at a sword attack would remove a door. Destroying their avatar would destroy a door as well, and force them to take their next turn reforming a new avatar (meaning there is a turn they could not defend or attack). At the moment I'm just working on the full mechanics of such a duel, but the end idea is that even when you run out of arcana to 'attack' with, you sitll have a means to win, and it's more dynamic than just 'attack, defend'. It lets masters use unmaking spells against a weaker opponent but doesn't make it autowin for the master as there is still a (remote) chance of a high willed non-master managing to get sword attacks in before their avatar is unmade

                  Main issue is length-of-battle atm.

                      Okay, so this is the first draft so far of said alternative duel style. There are some elements I'd like to add to it right now but I'm not sure how to while keeping it balanced (the element I want to add is gathering mystical resources with the subtle arcana, but I need something unique and strategic for gross arcana so I'll need to work on it. It's basically melding the new arcane rules with Astral Elements now that I look on it - not that I mind, but still. Anyways, thoughts?

                      Alternative Duel - Battle for Domain (Astral Duel Arcane?)

                      Step 1: Determine the Stakes

                      Step 2: Define the Avatar

                      Each player takes the time to define their Avatar. The Avatar is effectively the dream form of the mage, and uses all the same mechanics, including generating the Amnion and the ability to call forth path tools.

                      Step 3: Determine the Territories and Doors

                      Each player takes the time to define their Domain - effectively the Territories of the Supernal Battlefield in which the two Avatars will duel. The Domain can be largely narrative, but often times it defines the tools and circumstances that the mages have at their disposal. If none of the Domains in the battle include a sky, for instance, no one will be calling down lightning bolts from it without the practice of making. If none of the Domains have plants or wildlife, one will not be affecting them. Each character defines one aspect of their Domain per Door they have, and when they lose a door the opponent describes the destruction of one of their territories. Each character gets a number of Doors equal to their half their willpower rounded up, plus their Gnosis.

                      Step 4: Draw Swords

                      In this form of duel, there are a number of different actions that a mage might take. The Avatars self as the symbolic representation of the mage, and are capable of doing anything that they are capable of doing in the other world. This can include physical combat, or magically afflicting the target. As long as the description of the Domain includes things which may be used as Yantras, the Avatar may use such to cast. Since casting is done in the supernal world and merely given as a reflection of possibility, spells are all given an unlimited amount of reach in which to operate. If an Avatar is destroyed, it must be recorporated - this takes one turn. If an Avatar is destroyed, the mage loses one door automatically.

                      The Sword Attack: Instead of attacking or using magic against the other opponents avatar, mages in the duel have the capability of committing a sword attack against the other mage. Whereas most actions involving the Avatar must utilize the mage's arcana rating, the effects of the Sword Attack are not about the mage's current knowledge, but the mage's potential. The mage rolls attribute + arcana, and upon a success opens a door against their target. The attacks only open one door, and excess successes are added to a character's dice pool for their next action. If the mage fails to open a door with a sword attack, they lose the capability to utilize that Arcana as a Sword. The mage must have an Avatar to make a Sword Attack.

                      The Shield Defense: A mage may opt to defend against a Sword Attack with a Shield. This operates very similarly to the Sword Attack, utilizing an Attribute + Arcana appropriate to the defensive response of the sword. Each success negates one success on a sword attack. If the shield defense exceeds the sword attack, excess successes are added to the next action the defender makes. The mage must have an Avatar to make a Shield Defense.

                      Negotiation: In addition to sword and shield, a mage may take an action in order to negotiate and parlay with their opponent. Their are two types of negotiation, Olive Branch and Terms of Surrender. If a mage does not have an Avatar, they may make a reflexive negotiation while reforming their avatar.

                      Olive Branch: A mage may negotiate for the capture of one of the characters Domains, in return for the removal of one of the tools in which they are utilizing against their opponent. A mage might negotiate not to use a particular Sword, for instance - or even not to utilize an Arcana at all in the duel, even with their Avatar or Shield. A mage who breaks their word on this loses the duel from a social standpoint (though if they win, the loser still gains the defeated condition and loses all willpower). It is possible that a mage may also offer a promise outside of the duel.

                      Terms of Surrender: A mage who sees a losing battle may offer terms in order to mitigate the damage of a loss. The mage adds an additional term for their opponent to accept or deny - for instance "No harm will come to my friends as a result of this" - if their opponent accepts this term, the mage loses one door automatically.

                          Supernal Chess

                          Alright, so Shadow Chess - Right? Was always in love with it - didn't get to use it much because well, frankly, most mages didn't have both Prime and Spirit in the games I ran. At least not at those levels. However - I have made an attempt at creating a similar type of Duel Arcane and the best part is, it can be used with the same Arcana that the normal one can (Prime). Variants however exist which can allow mages without Prime to create a similar circle, making it so that this duel can be done without the need for a Disciple of Prime around. The initial form assumes that the mage is making supernal representations of supernal entities - though the various alternatives allow you to do spirits, demons, etc. Tried to figure out how Death would work, buuuuut I'm not sure that was the best example for how a Moros might make a unique ghostly style chess game (honestly, it probably would involve a haunted house or cemetary rather than what I went with. BEHOLD MY ZOMBIE PAWNS)

                          For the most part, Supernal Chess assumes that you and your opponent are the King. The goal of Supernal Chess is to put the King into Checkmate - that is, sap all of his Willpower. (I went with Willpower, but may switch to Doors later). Once a mage is sapped of Willpower, they are defeated and unable to continue the match. Some matches created with mastery level arcana actually can be lethal, however.

                          This is sort of a first draft made in the middle of the night however - and hopefully I'll be able to achieve some good tips on how this might be improved (or at least better clarified - if anything's confusing please lemme know.)


                          Create Pawn: In order to create a pawn, the mage must roll Gnosis + Arcana. Upon a success, the mage creates a rank 1 entity which comes into the scene with a power, finesse, and resistance of 1. Each additional success can be applied to the spirit to bolster it's strength, making it a more formidable piece. The entity appears based upon the path of the mage as well as the flavor of the Arcana used to create it. It begins play with 1 dot of influence in the Arcana used to create it, as well as a single numina - which may be from the typical lists (so long as it's appropriate to the arcana) or may be a spell that the mage themselves could cast from the Arcana used to create the pawn - though the spell rank cannot exceed the Entities' Rank.

                          (A variant, Shadow Chess - which was actually the original version of this duel before those unattuned with the Ars Spiritus became interested - utilizes only spirits. These variations base themselves upon the Arcana used to create the spirit and the local spirits and takes place in the Shadow. Another variant, Astral Chess, takes place within a mixed onieros of the two duelists and the daemons tend to take the form of the opponent's daemons. The Death Arcana is rarely used for this, but it has been known to happen - called "River Chess", two mages would enter into the underworld and create the circle - the circle would feature a 'shadow river' in which the kings sat in boats. The goal was to knock the other king into the river - which, in the underworld, tends to be a bad thing.)

                          Bolster Piece: If the mage has a pawn in place - or if the mage creating a pawn has more than a single success - the mage may opt to bolster their Pawn. The mage makes 'purchases' into the strength of the entity which he created. A single success can buy a dot of power, finesse, or resistance. The mage may also give a spirit an essence or heal one corpus for the cost of a success. For two successes, the mage may grant the entity an influence appropriate to the Arcana used to Bolster it. For three successes, the mage may purchase a numini for the spirit. For five successes, the mage may increase the entities' rank - changing a pawn into a knight, or a knight into a bishop, a bishop to a rook, or a rook into a queen.

                          Direct Pieces: If the mage does not opt to create or bolster his pieces, he may instead make a move. Typically most Chess games begin with the king roughly (20?) yards away - meaning it takes a full turn for the pieces to make their move towards the King unless the piece is exceptionally powerful. Attacks are made as usual, but against the King they instead inflict damage to the King's Willpower, rather than his health. A king hit is considered 'in check' - he may opt to take the damage, or divert the damage to one of his pieces in order to spare himself. If this diverted damage exceeds the spirits resistance, it discorporates and the mage takes the excess damage.

                          Checkmate: Checkmate is achieved when one mage has run out of Willpower. Simple, easy.

                          (Note: I also considered doing it that, rather than whenever one mage ran out of willpower they forfeited - making it whenever the mage ran out of pieces and could not summon more. If I were to go with this option, however, I would make creating a piece cost willpower - meaning you would only have as many pieces up as you had willpower dots in most matches.)
