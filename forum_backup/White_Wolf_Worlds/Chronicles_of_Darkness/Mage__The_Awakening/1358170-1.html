    I agree with the Shadow Name idea. Mages have nothing fundamentally driving them away from humanity, nor do they posses natures that they need to balance against it.

    Wisdom represents how careful a mage is, how precise their with their spells and mindful of the consequences. Its not related to how nice they are is or even their emotional state. When they don't exert this control regularly, then the small warps in reality they subconsciously generate spread farther.

    So what they can do is separate their mage and mundane personas. Barring emergencies, plus the occasional Knowing or Unveiling spells, they should avoid casting whenever they use their Sympathetic Name. Those that further cultivate this separation gain merits like Shadow Name. But that is entirely up to the mage.

    But that is exactly the challenge, all of those stem from the mage. Wisdom is a personal restraint they enforce by consistently being more precise, optimal, knowing when not to cast or when to back away before going too far chasing an Obsession. Meaningful interaction with a Touchstone cannot teach any of this to a mage.

    Their mundane Touchstones can help ground them in the Fallen world (although not exclusively, the ghost of a departed person they cared about could do so as well), it can help them decompress, foster character growth and this can certainly be a source of normal beats. But its not related to instilling the wish or skillset needed to be Wise, its more likely that there is an Order or Consilia appointed specialist for that (probably a Guardian or Arrow for the Consilia, rarely a Ladder).

    So what you can do is make these meaninful people or objects/memories provide them with a chance to end (or temporarily suppress, in case of the permanent versions) the Rampant or Melalomaniac conditions via significant interaction, stopping the destructive spiral. The Wisdom specialist could let them earn the beats needed to buy back said Wisdom via lessons. That way you could throw a mundane lifeline to allow a hubristic character to find their balance again, which I believe is what you are looking for.

                        Giving Beats for hanging out with your non-plot related friends and family is an incredibly bad design idea.

                        Wisdom is the only thing you can't get if you don't have Arcane Beats. You can't get as many Praxis spells, but you can get up to 10 with normal Beats, and Legacy Attainments only need Arcane Beats if you don't have a tutor.

                        So you have just incentivized the players to not participate in the plot. They can sit around hanging out with their Touchstones, do a bit of magical practice as a group on the weekends, and farm Beats until they're the masters of the universe in terms of power level.

                        This is one of the differences between writing fiction, where a neat little romantic scene can work easily as a break in the action, and an RPG, where you want to keep things moving because that sort of scene eats into time not really playing the game.

                        Less hyperbolic, this idea has the inverse effect of the stated desire of having Touchstones contrast Obsessions and help Wisdom. Normal Beats can't help Wisdom. It's one of the key dramatic tensions in Mage. Learning to be Wise means putting your Wisdom on the line. The best way to learn how to act properly to is go out and risk hubris so you can learn it first hand. A mage that spends all their time delving into the magical can generate Arcane Beats and buy Wisdom, even if they risk losing some Wisdom too. A mage that sits around with their normal-Beat-generating-Touchstones can't raise their Wisdom, as they learn nothing about actually balancing their lives.

                        Because an important thing? Empathy has nothing to do with Wisdom. A character with very poor empathy for Sleepers (like a lot of the Silver Ladder) can still act Wise, because they are motivated by the abstract consequences of their actions rather than actually caring about the feelings of normal folks that would get hurt along the way.

                        As well, Mage has lots of mechanical incentives to keep mages coming back to their Fallen lives. They're just opt in instead of automatic.

Having advisors in the form of friends and family outside of mage circles helps keep her grounded and remind her of her higher duty, both magically and in mundane life: service to the needs of her people. She gains a Wisdom benefit from maintaining those connections (+1 on degeneration rolls), provided she spends a scene interacting with such advisors during the story. In essence, they help her check herself before she wrecks herself and her nation both. She balances the Arcane Beat loss of time taken with Sleeping counselors when she could be pursuing the Mysteries with a Common Beat gain for engaging in this interaction and maintaining those connections. Wisdom is benefited in the equation without any decrease in the number of times she risks degeneration.

                                        Been a minute, just going to chime in with, Wisdom doesn't care about empathy, or require you to consider things from anyone else's perspective. Wisdom is about being in control of yourself and your actions through not engaging in self-deception, and being aware of the consequences of your actions. You can do terrible things that will make all the Sleepers recoil in horror and still be Wise. Interacting with Sleepers, or anyone else for that matter is not a guarantor of maintaining Wisdom.
