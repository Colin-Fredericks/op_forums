            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
            It's not about distance, it's about levels of resolution.
            A spell that targets a planet or star without better defining a place within it doesn't need better resolution than seeing the entire star as a spec itself. The example that set this tangent was to simply locate a meteor.

            Some cases are absurd by themselves. You can't affect an star planet with an area spell just because it looks like a spec for you, and you can't pinpoint a target you can't perceive just because you can see the planet or the area in space your target is in. In such instances, you're just arguing things no one said.

            But if a spell can target a place without specifying a location in that place, then it logically works as normal. Locating where in your visible field a specific spec of light will appear isn't that much of a stretch, and the fact the spec itself is astronomically far and big is meaningless for the task.

            Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
            Indeed. Opening portals to stars millions of light years away is a bit of silliness it's better to put the kibosh on so that everybody can stay focused rather than trying to stretch things to clever edge cases. Avoid more conversations like conjuring up Hemingway books.
            While that's a valid approach, I don't think it is the intent of the system, at least not in this manner. Keeping the game from ever dealing with space because you think it is silly seems a little too far, it's an overreaction to overblown expectations on what players will do with such things.

            Mage is about people that focus on their own Obsessions all the time, you're simply pre-determining one such obsession as off-limits, because?

            I didn't got the point.

            But that has no relation to the question. It just means you're on your right to define it, or anything else, as an exclusion case.

            No book defined space or the sky as an exclusion case (or said it isn't), and your argument was that targeting space seemed like lack of clarity on the rules. It isn't lack of clarity, it enables working anything in the game if the group wants to. If the ST don't want to, then you just define it as an exclusion case.

            While I usually agree with you, and also dealt with silly questions from people in the forum wanting to explore material space in the game, the notion that the game should write off the possibility, or that it is somehow implied, just isn't in it.

                                    Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
                                    Can there be such a thing as a spirit of a specific linage? (Perhaps what is commonly referred to as a ancestor spirit?) (I understand there is no such thing as a human spirit, but apparently spirits of nations exist?)
                                    Assuming they do exist, would there be anything special about those spirits who resonate with Proximi?

                                    Even if there were, I suspect they'll go magath pretty quick. And to begin with, I'd say the lineage in question will need some 'public weight' for spirits to form around them, as it seems to be the case for cities and nations. So a super important and powerful lineage that also happens to be super secret likely won't have a spirit representing them. You'll have better luck looking for Goetia, or spirits of certain aspects that the lineage involves themselves with. (A lineage of assassins may be long-term allies with a Murder spirit, for instance.)

                                    By anything special, would you be thinking about Influences? "Magic" is one of those things that are too diverse and broad to be represented as a single purview, I think. It'll be like having a spirit of Emotions. So Proximus-lineage-spirits shouldn't have something like "Magic" as an Influence, not even "Acanthus Magic" or "Curse Magic", for example. But say, a spirit aligned with the Sisters of the Mountain for instance should have Influences over Hills, Herbs, Healing, etc.

                                    Oh, but then again, IIRC Dark Eras had a case of allowing Countermagic as a Numen, so spirits who had extensive relationships with mages could develop Numina that roughly interact with Awakened magic.

                                    Dunno about ghost/Death-focused Thyrsus. Seems like an unexplored space.

                                            I think you could put spirits in for ancestral lineages, but they'd be an uncommon thing that probably mostly get involved with families that already have particular ties to the Shadow.

                                            Although I think like... the Werewolf: the Forsaken ones were kind of supposed to be something that Uratha actually turned into? It looks to me like an artefact of a time when Forsaken was going a bit more for retaining the idea of consolidated and cultivated family lines of werewolves, which I find Second Edition to have all but dropped completely (there's still a hereditary aspect, it just doesn't seem to be given any wider impact beyond "sometimes your kids or grandkids might also be werewolves" and made small placed alongside additional reasons why somebody might undergo the First Change).

                                            And interaction with Proximi I think is the kind of thing that needs to engage with them not exactly being normal families above and beyond having hereditary magical powers; the notion that a lot of those lines trace back to Imperial spells, refugees from Emanations, possibly ancestry with some kind of actual god.

                                            Mind, in Mage terms I can't help but feel like it becomes a bit of a square peg for a round hole when the Temenos is there. A personification for a family lineage feels like a much more organic fit as a Goetia under the circumstances, and family identities with greater or lesser prominence are definitely part of that realm. It's less accessible than the Shadow, but that might be for the best, and one could possibly work in that they're a type with a little bit more leeway than most of them get.
