                            Divergent Blessing (• to •••)
                            Prerequisites: Proximus
                            Effect: The power in your blood manifests differently than most of your kin. You can add a Blessing in one of your Dynasty's Arcana, with a dot rating equal to the dots in this merit, to your list of available Blessings.
                            Drawback: This replaces a number of dots of Blessings equal to the dots in this merit - you are effectively swapping some spells for another one. Sanctity of Merits still applies if you are swapping out Blessings you already possess.
                            [Comments: This gives a little bit more flexibility as far as spells go with existing Proximus Dynasties. If there is one Proximus Dynasty you mostly like, but just wish it had a few spells different, now you can adjust it.]

                                Merit: Adamant Blade (●●)
                                Prerequisite: Adamant Hand ●●, Adamantine Arrow Status ●
                                Effect: You have studied the advanced principles of the Adamant Way, further melding your magical skills with your martial arts. When you cast a damaging spell, you may choose to focus its Imago through the lens of your martial training, resulting in the following effects:
                                • The spell manifests in the approximate shape of a melee weapon of your choice. This effect is obviously supernatural and reflects the Arcanum used - Death creates a dagger of writhing shadow, Forces generates a spear of crackling lightning, Matter collects shards of masonry and earth from the environment and forms makeshift brass knuckles, etc.
                                • These "spell-weapons" do not have weapon modifiers or apply Initiative penalties, but you may make All-Out Attacks, attempt called shots, and apply the effects of any applicable Fighting Styles you know. (This includes the use of Brawl-based styles if you create a brass-knuckles equivalent. If you know Two-Weapon Fighting, you conjure a second weapon in your other hand.)
                                • Attacking with a spell-weapon uses Strength + Weaponry (substitute Brawl if you conjure brass knuckles or equivalents; substitute Dexterity if you have Fighting Finesse.) Damage is equivalent to the spell's Potency. Do not add successes on your attack roll as additional damage. On an Exceptional Success, regain a point of Willpower and a point of Mana, as your martial skill echoes the Supernal truth of the Adamant Way.
                                • The spell automatically gains Advanced Duration, lasting for one scene. You roll for Paradox once, at the time the spell is initially cast. If Sleepers witness your casting, they impact the Paradox roll as normal; if they stumble into the fight mid-scene, their Quiescence slowly degrades your spell-weapon's Potency. If you Reach to deal aggravated damage, you must spend 1 Mana per strike, but you may choose not to pay this cost and deal lethal damage at any time during the spell's duration.
                                • If you are disarmed of a spell-weapon, it automatically re-forms or returns to your hands on your next turn. (If the disarming maneuver uses bare hands, the opponent may take damage from touching the spell-weapon at Storyteller discretion.) The spell-weapon may be dispelled or cancelled as normal.
                                • If you already know the spell as a rote, you may substitute the combat skill being used (Brawl or Weaponry) for the usual rote skill, and this rote skill receives the +1 Order affinity bonus.
                                • Mundane armor applies to spell-weapons on an Arcanum-by-Arcanum basis. Common sense and Storyteller discretion apply; as a general rule, if the same spell could be used to attack an object, then mundane armor applies (but if a single attack deals more damage than the armor's general rating, apply the Pierced Armor tilt.) If the spell could not be used to attack objects, mundane armor does not apply. (Developer's breakdown: Forces, Matter, Prime, Space, Spirit, and Time apply to mundane armor; Death, Life, and Mind bypass it.)
                                • Arcanum limitations on targets apply as normal. (E.g. Death blades won't harm constructs that never lived; Life blades won't harm the undead; Mind blades won't harm mindless zombies.)

                                        Inherent Magic (• to •••••)
                                        Prerequisites: Sleepwalker
                                        Effect: You have some inherent trick of magic as part of your nature. Build out this ability as a Legacy Attainment (Mage: the Awakening 2E pg 198) with a dot rating equal to the dots in this merit. Activating this ability costs at least 1 Willpower - convert any additional Mana costs into additional Willpower that must be spent to use the ability. This ability never provokes Breaking Points for the character to use, though it confers no such immunity to other supernatural events doing so. All major decisions, including Spell Factors, are fixed when this merit is selected.
                                        Drawback: Such powers are never free. Work with the Storyteller to determine some circumstance that is required to use the ability, or some Condition that it inflicts as a consequence of its use or possession.
                                        Note: This merit may only be selected at character creation or after a significantly transformative event.
                                        [Comments: An easy way to give characters a range of flexible supernatural abilities, outside of Supernatural Merits. It's modeled off Legacy Attainments to eliminate much of the Supernal Magic considerations, like Paradox, Dissonance, etc. I might need to add in a line for what to do when you want a conjunctive effect (like "add Arcana X to" expanded effects).]

                                                Merit: Way of the Shadow's Edge (●)
                                                Prerequisite: Death ●●●, Adamant Hand ●●, "Shadow Crafting" rote
                                                Effect: Unique among the Subtle Arcana, Death allows for the manipulation of a Fallen phenomenon - shadows - into tangible shapes, with stolen substance made real enough to sever a limb or deflect a bullet. For Necromancers of all Orders, knowing that a blade or a trusty shield is only a dark corner away has been a source of chill comfort since time immemorial.

                                                For some Arrows, however, this sort of shadow-crafting is but the first step. Exploiting the fluidity of shadows opens new doors to the seasoned warrior: the shadow in one's hands can shift its shape and size, flickering out like a lash or narrowing to an impossibly-fine point. When wielding weapons born of "Shadow Crafting," the mage may apply a number of equipment tags (see Hurt Locker, pg. 141) equal to his dots in Death from the following list: Accurate, Bleed, Brawl, Concealed, Grapple, Guard, Knockdown, Piercing, Reach, Stun, Thrown. The mage may change them each turn reflexively; a thrown shadow-blade slithers back to the bearer's hand in the process. Finally, the mage gains additional Reach options for use with the rote:

                                                +1 Reach, add Matter ●●●: The mage imbues the shadows with Matter's solidity, drawing from the other half of the Stygian spectrum. Shadow-crafted weapons, armor, and equipment now have a rating equal to Potency, to a maximum of the mage's Matter dots.
                                                +1 Reach, Death ●●●●: Shadow-crafted weapons deal aggravated damage at 1 Mana per strike.

                                                        You got me thinking.

                                                        Shadow Mysteries: 3 & 5 dot Merit (Shadow Initiate and Adept)
                                                        The merit represents an alignment to the forces of shadow. There needs to be some cause for this to develop, but the character (and possibly player) needn't have any idea what it was.
                                                        This connection to shadow manifests as an improved ability to metaphysically connect to shadow. This has a few effects.
                                                        • The character improves their ability to blend into shadows. The drawback is that this applies to penalties, as well.
                                                          • At 3 dots, this improves any existing bonus to stealth or concealment from shadows by one. Increase any penalty from excessive lighting by -1.
                                                          • At 5 dots, double any such bonus or penalty from lighting.
                                                        • The character improves their ability to copy or follow someone.
                                                          • At 3 dots, this grants the 9-Again property or 8-Again, if already 9-Again.
                                                          • At 5 dots, this grants 8- and 9-again and the Rote quality to the rolls.
                                                          • The drawback is that the character using this ability cannot exceed the overall success of the person that they are shadowing. If a roll is extended, this limit only applies to the ultimate result, but excessive successes might, if appropriate to the story, provide Conditions.
                                                        • Finally, the connection provides an intimate understanding of the relationship of shadows to the Supernal.
                                                          • ​Mimicry of another's movements and speech throughout casting and for at least a turn (it cannot be reflexive) qualifies as a Mirror or Shadow Path or Personal Tool Yantra (+1). This can also be used as a Representational Sympathetic Yantra of the subject. (These applications are separate Yantra though they involve only a single physical activity.)
                                                          • ​At 5 dots, all Yantra involving shadow, including mimicry, improve by +1.
                                                            Also at 5 dots, the mage can mimic a subject to lock a spell onto their pattern more certainly. This can only be applied to an effect with duration greater than one turn that specifically and directly targets that subject or both that subject and the caster. This grants a +3 (bonus from this merit included) to casting, but the effect's duration ends instantly when the mage ceases copying the subject's physical actions for any reason.​
                                                        (At Death 5, a Reach 3 effect can be applied to shadow spells (e.g. Deepen Shadow, or Shadow Sculpting) to grant this Merit for the effect's duration. Some develop it spontaneously and permanently.)
                                                        This is overly convoluted, still, I think, but not a bad start.

                                                            #255
                                                            07-07-2017, 04:10 PM
                                                            Originally posted by Mrmdubois View Post
                                                            Are you aware that you could just use a Death Perfecting spell to increase a shadow's equipment rating, and that Shadow Sculpting would cover weapons that change shape and size and can return to you, and that it's actually desirable to use more than one spell because it increases the chances of a Mage running up against or exceeding his limits?

                                                            Three separate points here; taking them individually.

                                                            RE: use of Perfecting, I'd suggest that there's a good reason why the RAW "Shadow Crafting" just gives the equipment rating as 2 as opposed to Potency: because Matter is the supreme "Hephaestus at his forge" Arcanum, and if you can create & buff items to whatever level you want sans Matter, why take Matter in the first place? To paraphrase this same topic over in the Creative Thaumaturgy thread: stepping on another Arcanum's toes is fine, but making one of its core strengths obsolete isn't. (Put another way: if you want shadows with the strength of titanium, you have to study titanium first.)

                                                            RE: "Shadow Sculpting," that's another instant action (at best) to cast, plus more instant actions to reshape each turn, which doesn't capture the moment-to-moment reflexive fluidity of martial arts that I was going for.

                                                            RE: "it's actually desirable to use more than one spell because it increases the chances of a Mage running up against or exceeding his limits," your mileage may vary. Yes, "reach exceeding grasp" and the horrors of unbounded pride are core themes of Mage, but every table is going to differ on how often and how tightly those themes constrain characters' abilities. If you want to say that any capability not already explicitly granted RAW requires a Reach or combined spells, hey, more power to you. Given how tight spell control & combined-spell limits are already, esp. for low-Gnosis mages, I prefer to lean more towards the dark-superhero side of the spectrum and cut them some slack.
