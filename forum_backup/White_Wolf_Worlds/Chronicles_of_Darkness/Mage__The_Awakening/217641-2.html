    But there’s no reason for the vampire not to be a Kindred, either: hunters should be able to deal with Kindred, Uratha, Awakened, etc.

    Don’t get me wrong, though: I’m not asking you to “waste space” on the fact that Hunter overlooked Disbelief; as you say, that’s Hunter’s problem, which is why I suggested that a future Hunter book should deal with it. But that’s a different thing from arguing that hunters should never do crossovers, and should only deal with the rough approximations of the other gamelines that are presented in the various Hunter books.

    In fact, to my way of looking at things, the “rough approximations” that Hunter features are examples of the new World of Darkness having “more things under the sun than are dreamed of in your philosophy, Horatio”, to quote the Bard — where “Horatio” is a Kindred, or an Uratha, or an Awakened: the witches in Witch Finders can be used to approximate Mage’s Awakened and Second Sight’s thaumaturges; but they can also be used to approximate magicians who are neither of the above. The vampires of Night Stalkers don’t just approximate the Kindred; they approximate the various vampires in Wicked Dead, and many other kinds of vampires that aren’t addressed anywhere else in the new World of Darkness. Want to really mess with one of the Awakened? Confront him with one of these “approximations”: someone who wields magic but who isn’t any type of magic user that the mage is aware of. Hunter’s monsters have uses outside of Hunter.

                                            First off, I’ve been wondering since the start of this thread why it isn’t in the Hunter forum rather than the Mage forum, as the majority of the issues being broight up here are the sorts of things that Hunter ought to deal with, not Mage.

                                            The Sleepwalker Merit has been proposed a couple of times as a cure-all for this issue. And yes, if the Storyteller feels comfortable giving it to every player character in a Hunter game, that would indeed bypass the problem.

                                            However, I’m assuming that Sleepwalker is a Supernatural Merit; and with reason: its effects involve granting a (minor) supernatural benefit (namely, exempting the person from a supernatural penalty that other have to contend with); and while the rules don’t outright say so, the general nature of Supernatural Merits implies that they’re not something that just anyone can pick up just by spending Experience on them — which holds true of Sleepwalker. If it isn’t Supernatural, I’d like to know the reasoning behind why not. As such, the above solution doesn’t work for Storytellers who are wanting to emphasize the “ordinary people are capable of more than anyone gives them credit for,” as Sleepwalkers aren’t ordinary people anymore. It’s true that Hunter isn’t exclusively focused on this theme; Endowments, in particular, are a massive violation of it. But Hunter sequesters Endowments to Tier Three play for a reason: the themes in play at Tier Three are different from those in play at the lower Tiers. So there’s a need for hunters to have a means of at least taking the edge off of the Sleeping Curse without flat-out negating it.

                                                    To answer the question of why I put it in the Mage forum, my theory that was the impetus of this thread kind of relies on two assumptions:

                                                    1. The Lie exists
                                                    2. Hunters are it's unwitting pawns

                                                    1 is a default assumption of Mage and is not a default assumption of Hunter, and 2 doesn't work well in a game where the Hunters are the protagonists. So the two key components of my theory don't play nicely with Hunter, but could be used to make them interesting Mage antagonists, as an example of how Sleepers can actually pose a threat to Mages if they are dependent enough on The Lie to fight for it. Therefore I felt it was more appropriate to put it in the forum for the template that would be the protagonists of such a story.

                                                    That's also why I've been resistant to things like "just use Banishers" or "just give them the Sleepwalker Merit." Sure, that's a thing I could do, but it would tell a very different story than the one I was initially proposing. The whole "don't underestimate sleepers" moral kind of falls flat if the Hunters are Sleepwalkers.
