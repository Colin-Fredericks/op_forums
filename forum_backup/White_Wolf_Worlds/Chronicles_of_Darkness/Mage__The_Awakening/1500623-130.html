                    Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
                    In the most common scenarios, how many masters has an order in a city? and how common is a 3rd degree master?

                    (I am aware that chronicle specific situations might make this "whatever the Storyteller says" but I am asking as part of the known or speculated world building for second edition).
                    Setting aside chronicle specific situations, the population of mages in a city at all is variable, the size of the Orders is not uniform globally nor locally, and the Orders divide their Caucuses into culturally similar regional groups rather than singular cities like a Consilium is made to manage.

                    And while 2e has made the chart less granular a mage still needs to be at least Gnosis 6 to have any kind of multiple Mastery. This is to say nothing of the fact that the easiest way to master three Arcana is to have a Legacy that grants a third Ruling Arcanum, which not all of them do, with the alternative being tutelage in the dots above your natural limit by an existing Master, which comes with its own power dynamics and social circumstances. Every dot of Gnosis a mage gains comes with a breakthrough significant enough to potentially change the character's Anchors in line with their Obsessions and Aspirations, and this narrative requirement for a breakthrough is the main limiting factor against mages generating Arcane Experiences casually ratcheting up their Gnosis from grinding their teeth against singular Mysteries.

                    Convocations, which are more events than political bodies, are run by committees of Magisters who can be any Pentacle mage that can prove mastery of an Arcanum, but while the Silver Ladder tries to have Convocations regularly in areas that overlap the territories of all five Orders, that doesn't indicate anything about the population of Masters of any degree in a specific city operating locally.

                    It can probably safely be assumed that any city with enough of an established Awakened presence to have a Consilium there are likely to be at least enough Masters living or formerly active to maintain a healthy currency of rotecraft either directly or through grimoires and to act as mentors to at least one newer generation of mages. The tendency of the Pentacle Orders to all be meritocratic to some degree means Masters are likely to occupy positions of importance in a political structure if they are present.

                    Masters are rare enough to be notable figures, but not so extraordinary that it's not weirder to have a good-sized Consilium without Masters than for there to be one with any at all, and every mage not laboring under unusual circumstances has the capacity for second-degree Mastery without special training. Fifth-degree Masters of the sort who could verify that it takes more than a conjunctional Making spell of all the Subtle Arcana are described with terms like "vanishingly rare" and "luminaries," so third- and fourth-degree Masters presumably fit into the middle ground between those two extremes, with the note that mages seldom pour all their advancement resources into just mastering singular Arcana.

                        Simplest answer is: there's no Normal distribution for this. It's less that the ST can do whatever and more that statistical variance is way too large.

                        Even if we assume that Awakenings follow an even statistical spread, once it happens the result is an unusually resourceful, and as such mobile, individual. Both the social dynamics of Order and Concilia and the nature of Mysteries add good reason for this mobile individual to move, to relocate to where their interests are.

                        You should expect most Mages in a city to not be from there at all, and the overall population to depend from what the area offers to those Mages rather than be a reflection of population size.

Even setting aside those cases where a Master is jealously guarding their niche, both of the Orders that maintain stores of knowledge that aren't an individual person require compensation.

                                    There's already mild social gates implied around teaching certain rotes and ratings of the Mastigos Arcana and it's not hard to imagine it could take some convincing for someone to believe teaching you the Lore of Disintegrating People and the methods to create and propagate methods of making any spell you have the ability to cast more effective is not going to come back to bite them, especially since the most expedient way for them to have attained Mastery was from hard-earned Arcane Experiences.

                                    Joining a Legacy gives your mentor direct leverage and an incentive to get along and generally provides less inherently dangerous knowledge than the Practice of Unmaking. Getting a Master to teach you involves approaching someone of a different Path with its own perspective, or a Legacy whose knowledge of that Arcanum may come from Mysteries that they want to keep stewardship of, and the only thing they have over you as insurance or assurance that the knowledge won't be misused is whatever they get out of it.

                                    Lest we forget, mages in Awakening are not assumed to be altruists and there are many reasons they keep secrets, including the root powerset whose modern name literally means "secret."

                                                    Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
                                                    Can you be both member of an Order and (by using the Mystery Cult Initiation) a Nameless Order? Do you get the same status restrictions as when using Order Status?
                                                    The Mystery Cult Initiation Merit explicitly reifies the "Nameless" sidebar's statement that being Nameless is mutually exclusive with belonging to one of the six major Orders.

                                                    How many people you need for a Mystery Cult Initiation? I was seeing the one in Night Horros, and it seems to be at least 4 people (Tanris, Pictas, Rowan, Sylvene) minimum for formation.
                                                    The characters named in the Hand of Destiny's description are the inner circle of the Mystery Cult in addition to being a cabal of mages, and the same paragraph states that "Most Hand of Destiny mages outside of the inner circle" don't know about the hope-eating gulmoth Tamris is running a pyramid scheme to feed. There are way more than four people in the Hand by design.

                                                    There are no hard numbers given for the threshold for what constitutes a Mystery Cult with an Initiation Merit, but the type of thing they are means it's definitionally unlikely for anything that you wouldn't describe as at least a secret society to qualify.

                                                    Nameless Orders can be as small in scope as the local level and potentially being just a few cabals who joined forces, with the section immediately after that description in the corebook defining the cabal a mage might join having between two and thirty of their peers (i.e. around three to thirty-one mages per cabal), so you may reasonably assume the membership numbers for a given cabal to be in the same range as you would get for the total number of monsters of a given type in a given faction in a given city, which I've generally shorthanded to "enough to fill a typical high school class" — anywhere between fifteen and forty, depending on the setting.

                                                    Can you have an Awakened only Mystery Cult that offers Awakened only merits, WITHOUT being a Nameless Order?
                                                    The definition of a Nameless Order is a small organization of mages. They're Nameless because they don't have the huge metaphysical backing of the Sects. It's purely a matter of nomenclature and at best you'd get characters who'd have words to say about being called Nameless — if you have a mage-based Mystery Cult and you're not a Nameless Order, you're a subgroup within the Orders and don't get to use the Merit to get Rote Specialties like a Nameless Order gets to do as a workaround for not getting them from basic Order Status.
