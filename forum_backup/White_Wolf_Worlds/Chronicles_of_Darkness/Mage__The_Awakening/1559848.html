    A comprehensive guide to build abyssal entities

    05-08-2026, 11:59 PM
    After reading a lot of the core books and browsing a good deal in this forum I find that there isn't a clear way to create abyssal entities. More so, as written abyssal or aren't defined or just use the template of ephemeral entities that makes them lackluster if a mage decide to confront them. Abyssals should be scary and weird, but in a way that doesn't just make them just like horrors or normal sci fi antagonists. For that I propose some rules, mostly stolen from other people ideas, that makes sense to me and maked them easier to build and use in the story.

    First is the nature of the abyss. It has already being said that that is the place that isn't. For that I like to think it like a lower dimesion that the fallen world, like if the fallen word is in the third dimension and the supernal realms in a 4th dimension the abyss should be the two dimensional cracks between them. So, in this line I thought that abyssal entities could be composed by the lack of arcanas, and when they appear in the fallen world they subert the rules of those arcana, trascending it. Mechanically, for each rank a abyssal entity has, it suberts an arcana, in this case I called it nullities.

    Examples of nullities:
    Time: it exists continuously in all possible times. Can exist inside an ucronia caused or corrupted by it
    Fate: it exists outside the realm of possibility. Causality doesn’t affect it and it existence causes paradox
    Space: can appear anywhere/exist everywhere at once
    Mind: can exist as just an abstract and antieuclidian idea, deforms goetia
    Matter: can destroy any substance, seems to be from an illogical substance
    Death: is indestructible. Deform or bind souls.
    Prime: deforms magic and creates impossible truths.
    Forces: deforms or nulls the laws of physics.
    Life: deforms and corrupts life, going outside of what is possible.
    Spirit: deforms or controls spirits. Can create spirits out of impossible concepts.

    For this I'm working on effects that each nullities grants to the entities, a sort of Numina, with the idea that for each rank the abyssal has it grants it a free "numina" related to that expression.

    This relates to general rules for all abyssal entities making them stronger and more of a challenge for mages:

    General abyssal entity rules

    Resistant
    Entity resist Supernal Magic much more efficiently than other of her kind – she gets automatic Withstand source equal to its Rank against Awakened spells.
    That-Which-Must-Not-Be-Named
    The ephemeral being can’t be seen by specific mage sights related to ephemeral beings, being registered just as a “void” in their senses. However the entity is able to interact with any beings in Twilight and can be affected by abilities that affect beings in Twilight. It still does not count as any 'type' of ephemeral being for purposes of targeting effects, other than being an Abyssal Ephemeral Entity. For purposes of Sympathy casters are considered to not know its name.
    Affront to Possibility
    Sleepers that suffer Breaking Points due to its presence suffer a penalty equal to rank on their roll; on a dramatic fail it can cause a specific abyssal condition and the entity gains essence equals to half the sleeper’s integrity
    Abyssal nullity
    Each abyssal entity represents the lack of a dimension, expressed by the arcana. As such, when manifested in the material realm, they bypass the laws of said arcana. All supernal magic from said arcana results fails if targets directly or indirectly the entity.

    ​Finally I like to discuss the distinction between Gulmoth and Acamoths. So far there is a crude idea making the Gulmoths like Spirits and Acamoths like Goetia. I'm using Gulmoths as Abyssal Entities that when manifest have a twillight form or definable expression, while Acamoths like corruptions or infections that exist within fallen beings and only have a concrete manifestation if taken to the Astral Planes.

    I have more to say on this but I would like to hear opinions on this ideas​

      A lot of the language you are using around this idea presents a particular sticking point.

      There is a part of the Mage setting that is metaphorically buried deeper than the Fallen World, as distant from our world as ours is from the Supernal Realms, broken into many different realms that each lack some element or property that makes up reality. The things from these places sometimes emerge into the Fallen World, where they are inimical by nature because their distinct lack functions like a vacuum, sucking away that property from the world around them.

      It's not the Abyss. It's the Lower Depths. (And, to be really specific, the things that the Lower Depths lack don't map to an entire Arcanum of phenomena, nor fit neatly within a single Arcanum. Examples of things Lower Depths might lack include location, identity, and form.)

      If you think of the Lower Depths in terms of having less of what is real or meaningful, the Abyss is defined as having incompatible reality and meaning. Rather than the empty set, the Abyss is the sum of impossibilities: things that not only don't exist but can't exist, contradictory to the makeup of the world that is. When a piece of the Abyss touches the Fallen World, the result is called a Paradox. Mathematics become inconsistent and hostile. Effects precede causes. Diseases gain consciousness. Memories invert. Things happen that are bizarre and generally forsake and undermine the things the world is built on.

      Your actual ideas, as written, don't necessarily contradict this if you alter the wording. Several of your "nullities" don't actually describe nullities but contradictions. Though again, I think tying it to entire Arcana is overbroad.

              I feel as though "This isn't something new but a way to implement something that already exists" is directly incompatible with things like redefining the difference between acamoth and gulmoth, making Abyssal entities specifically more resistant to Supernal magic as an intrinsic quality, and rewriting their relation to Quiescence and Dissonance, to say nothing of making a topological comparison better suited to its main competition/mutual antagonistic force as Stupid Loserman pointed out above. The 2e corebook compares acamoth and gulmoth to Goetia and spirits as a space-saving measure, but the thematics of the Abyss overall are fairly well-defined this far into Awakening as a gameline.

              The Abyss destroys reason, hope, and being. Its individual manifestations are understandable in a way the infinite Void cannot be, and the dangerous specimens that make their way to the Phenomenal World are sent there to render some facet of ordered reality meaningless by their interface with their surroundings. The Dur-Abzu are the clearest rendering of this, being Abyssal experiences meant to directly negate the messaging of the Supernal Realms, but the cosmological emphases on mockery, ignorance, nonsense, impossibility, degradation and destruction make its place in the Lie clear, at least in its placement as a source of antagonists.

              2e's emphases are slightly different than 1e, but the classic division between gulmoth and acamoth being their provenance still translates into the current system by the fact that the acamoth's interactions with the world around them were through dreams by which they endeavored to make people's souls more like the Abyss, which has been simplified somewhat from "there's a hibernating thing in the deep Shadow that projects its fever dreams around it and can be convinced to make a deal" to "when the Abyss twists the Astral (which it might do through the proximity of a weird thing in the deep Shadow) it forms acamoth in people's souls." Not for nothing does Nameless and Accursed's material on the Nasnasi's modified summoning mechanics treat gulmoth as manifest entities and acamoth as recondite.

              Classically the degree of malicious intent and action of an Abyssal being has scaled with Rank, and their contagious unreality is currently represented by the use of Influences to corrupt the phenomena they exist/do-not-exist in mockery of, Abyssal Environmental Tilts spawned by Paradox or other ill fortune, and unique Numina relating to their concept. They're monkey-paw magical potential writ in flesh and effluvia, and while that's infamously got some dire big narrative pops from when things go especially off-course it does require some degree of insidiousness to allow them as a whole to creep into the cracks. They're Mysteries that lead investigators down dead-ends into endless mazes and rabbit holes that terminate in cruel laughter or hollow acceptance. Meddling with Abyssal beings is poking at the force that makes sufficiently overt magic come with a catch, so rather than flatly making magic less effective against them it may be more interesting to tap into Paradox Reach effects and the like — perhaps an acamoth squatting in one of the ruins of the Spire Perilous responds to spells that use the Mantra Yantra by imposing the Mute Condition on the caster or saps the Finesse of any mage who casts a spell using Runes, or maybe the effects of an Abyssal Verge begin to spread wherever a Gulmoth manifests. (There's also the case to be made that treating gulmoth like spirits leaves the door open for Abyssal fetishes, which in turn serves as an additional avenue for unsettling strangeness.)

              The OP feels like it's describing less that Abyssal beings are particularly difficult to mechanically represent and moreso that the mechanics for Influences and Numina overall are very flexible and pagecount is at a premium for providing examples or intensive suggestions for designing tools to suit a specific concept. These aren't Pangaeans or Idigam or even angels; they don't particularly need extra-strength special mechanics as a consistent feature to be A Problem between having Weird tools in the standard kit and universally treating Paradox, Dissonance, and Quiescence (and Sleepers undergoing breaking points, when Sleepers always suffer breaking points from exposure to Abyssal phenomena) as sources of Essence and healing. An Abyssal entity can primarily be built as a conceptually appropriate Mystery whose core actor is a spirit in name only, breaking out aspects comparable to the powers of Coalesced Idigam from Werewolf for particularly egregious specimens
