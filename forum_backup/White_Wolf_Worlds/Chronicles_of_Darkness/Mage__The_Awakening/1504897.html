    [Necro] How can I showcase the evil of the Seers Of Throne without going overboard?

    03-08-2023, 11:23 PM
    In summary, given what I know about real life atrocities committed by powerful people throughout history, it seems to me that portraying the Seers of the Throne in a strictly realistic fashion would require me to narrate scenes involving topics that many players would find excessively upsetting, like child soldiers, forced prostitution, religious persecution, slavery in cellphone factories, and so forth. What options do I have, besides putting all that horror in the background and/or seeking players with different levels of tolerance for such extreme topics?

    My guess is that I can lean closer to the idea of Seers as James Bond-esque villains who "already won" and ran out of ideas, so they just rehash what works for maintaining power ad infinitum. The kind of people who may or may not hate their jobs, but are not allowed to leave lest they become a liability in terms of operational security. But once they come home, they have to spend an hour in subservient prayer to their respective Exarchs, in front of a pyramid-shaped alter, and on their hands and knees. For examples of how Seers want to be seen VS who they truly are, I'm considering using the videos below as references, respectively. I don't get the memes in the second video, I just get Exarchal vibes from it, to be clear. What do you all think?

          Originally posted by GibberingEloquence View Post
          I don't get the memes in the second video, I just get Exarchal vibes from it, to be clear.
          Woof, that's some real cryptofash stuff there. The representative of the "globalist bankers" controlling the world is pointedly named as "Mr. Rothschild," Wojaks, the channel's other thumbnails have "you will eat the bugs" and "you'll own nothing and you'll be happy," "the clown timeline." These memes exist at a point of intersection between channer culture and Infowars. I'm not blaming you for it, but I don't like having been shown this video.

          You don't have to go hard on the extremes of the banality of evil, nor do you have to showcase it directly. You don't need to personally view the suffering and poverty inflicted to know that a company is using sweatshop labor and that that is bad and hurting people; you don't need to go straight to child soldiers and human trafficking. The corebook has an example Seer of the Throne monologuing internally about how he is funding gentrification projects that will attract commercial business to an area and thereby raise the cost of living and reduce the availability of affordable housing.

          Now I would imagine in the average game, the process of fighting that Seer isn't going to look like a community-building solidarity movement so much as genre action horror where you build sympathy through catspaws to one another building to a confrontation in the Astral Realms while he dons the Profane Urim to weaponize innocent human lives against you, because this is a hobby for entertainment as much as it is an artistic pursuit. If you need moments to make it personal and visceral, you might be better off showcasing the things the Seers are capable of doing individually, to protect themselves and acquire resources for their projects, rather than showcasing the actual long-term projects that subjugate the Fallen World.

          Remember also that the Seers of the Throne don't make up the sum total or even the majority of the Exarchs' thrall over the world. A lot of banal evil goes on without their involvement, and they don't have a constant hotline to the will of their masters so much as revelatory fever-visions pointing them in sinister directions. Ultimately, the Seers of the Throne serve themselves before any others, with abasement to the hidden masters as merely a means to sate their selfish ambitions. They are in it because it is an easy way to translate the gift of Awakening into executive credit lines and friends in high places without making dangerous enemies. They prefer to rule in Hell than serve in Heaven. In the absence of clear attention and direction from their Exarch master, pylons are likely to pursue projects that they can justify as pleasing to their Ministry, but which coincidentally harness the Mysteries to garner personal power.

              You have the option of putting some horrible stuff in the background, and/or giving the players (and characters) the option to investigate.

                  There are two additional methods you can use:

                  1) Scale down the evil. You can depict: injustice in civil courts, effects of gentrification, toxic work environments, cronyism in academia... Look for Reddit threads about worst things that happened to average people, and find something your players can handle.

                  2) Scale up the fantasy. Making sure the evil is supernatural in nature allows for greater emotional detachment. replace child soldiers with Myrmidons, prostitution with Hollow ones programmed to enjoy it, slavery with zombies, make Seers tamper with ephemeral realms etc.

                                                      I also think that something inherent to the pyramidal structure of the Seers is that the majority of them are engaged in work that is primarily about building their capital. Acts of infiltration, subversion, deal making. The busywork of making large numbers of influential people beholden, which then gets transmitted from the person who made the deal up to the level of a superior who employs that leverage on the strategic level, coordinated with numerous other operations, in order to apply the pressure that cultivates and nurtures the oppressive systems that the Seers are concerned with.

                                                      The Seer that you're most likely to meet if you're not part of a cabal that is concerned with dedicated opposition to them isn't kicking puppies, they're doing things like divining which member of an advertising firm has vulnerabilities, whether those be in things such as greed, being beholden to others, or a hurt that they want relieved, and applying powers to the purpose of covertly addressing those things after having established a quid pro quo with the person, that one day (and that day will definitely come) the Seer will come to them seeking a favour. That advertising executive will be directed as part of an operation coordinated with the actions of several other people, and now the incidence of people consuming large quantities of soft drinks has gone up, creating knock on effects in public healthcare and the pharmaceutical industry and a lot of public commentators hemming and hawing over obesity epidemics and greater incidences of people being derided over their physical appearance.

                                                      The thing is, most Seers don't really see the big picture. If you're a soldier of the Iron Pyramid, you get told to go somewhere and carry out some objective without ever really knowing what it's meant to accomplish. If you're the head of your Pylon, you might have the smarts to know what your general objective for building influence or applying pressure might be in service of, and bringing other Pylons under your umbrella to exert greater and greater control over implementing these procedures is part of how you rise in status... but often you're only slightly less in the dark.

                                                      In either case, on your off hours you live like somebody who has at least a six figure annual salary without putting in quite that amount of work or career building, as well as the general thrill of exerting power over the lives of other people, so you're satisfied.

                                                      The Pentacle cabal dedicated in warfare probably needs to look past these peons. Killing a decent number of them off might slow down the flow of power to the top of the tetrarchy, but it's easily noticed by some very powerful mages, who are liable to close ranks, observe their attackers, and apply a lot of their banked power to destroy them. It's ultimately more effective to spend your time tracing the sequences of reporting to superiors, mapping out the local pyramid, identifying the people at the higher levels. Kill off somebody in the middle, and you create some chaos for a while as everybody below them scrambles to fill the vacancy. Find a person at the top (and determine that they aren't an archmage), and if you can get to them you can do some real damage when the lower ranks stop receiving their orders and all of that power held in reserve becomes inaccessible, the equivalent of a bank account that nobody has the access code to anymore.
