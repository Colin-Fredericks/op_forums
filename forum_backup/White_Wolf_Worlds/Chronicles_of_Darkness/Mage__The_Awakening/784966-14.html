                Deep Purple

                In an effort to create soul-less hive mind drone servitors for Unity, Dr. Amanda Keller of Psychometric Operations has synthesized an intravenous drug that slowly subverts the Oneiros and destroys the Daimon of the user.                     There are forces in New England lands. Secret whispers between the sights of wind. The sounds of running river are the same as rhythms of Supernal incantations. All they want to be study it. And to get the power from it.

                    • Spirit of the Land – Mysterious being most Awakened sought to know in New England is a powerful Shadows spirit of this Lands. It’s Rank 7 being, on par of Wolf gods the werewolf worship or just a bit below ephemeral beings like Sun or Moon. Many wonder how spirit of surrounding can be this powerful and wonder if Atlantean civilizations that left ruins in New England woods have something to do with it. ( See Boston Unveiled, Chapter Four „Off the Map” )
                    • Bound unbound – Other theory states that this being is Supernal god, thrown into our world when Exarchs took their Thrones. Awakened are then ideal meal.
                    • Prisoner from Abyss – Most disturbing theory is that „Spirit” is powerful Acamoth, abyssal being living in Astral Space. Most seek mages to use their Astral link with Abyss to go over their home they were separated eons ago. But the New England being had somehow evolved and can travel between strata of Astral on its own – it lies in Anima Mundi most often, leaving imprint on the land and beings in region. Still, it likes to play with Awakened, offering lore and power. For the price.

                                You can have the Acamoth represent a belief held within the Prince of 100,000 Leaves. Treating an atrocity as a holy act is the most obvious example. For instance, Boston's Abyssal counterpart has cannibalism as a religious sacrament. This Acamoth would encourage those with authority to eat the weak, and the weak to accept it as a way of things. Notice that it's not about hunger - Boston's cannibal spirits have it covered, and they resent the Abyssal Intruders. It's about holy duty. As a result, the name is likely to sound like an euphemism - Bringer of Blessings, The Great Kindness, Sanctifier of the Meek. A benefit of such approach is that players will dread what the euphemism means, instead of learning from an obvious name.

                                Any target of Acamoth's Influence would suffer cognitive dissonance between what they always believed to be right and what they're led to believe. Imagine a social worker getting intrusive thoughts that maybe the best charity they could give to a struggling family is to eat one of them, and that very family starting to yearn for that blessing - to their own distress. That assumes they're decent people. People in more pathological circumstances would slide down that slope much faster - likely using the same excuses they used for their ordinary misdeeds (sense of privilege and shifting the blame).

                                If you want to expand your options, you can search existing religions for unique beliefs and then corrupt them. For instance, a twisted belief in reincarnation can give raise to a caste of torturers. If causing pain nets you negative karma, but suffering nets you positive karma, it's reasonable to create Hell on Earth to have humanity reach Nirvana faster. It demands some to accept the stains on their Souls to bring anguish to others, for the good of all. Of course, for their sacrifice they deserve special privileges in life. On the other hand, a twisted belief in an afterlife can lead to creation to human farms, where children are conceived and promptly aborted to fill the Heavens with innocent Souls, for the glory of God.

                                Acamoth would promote such beliefs in the Fallen World. Worryingly, the can be savvy enough to be subtle.

                                        If you're going for something more visceral, you could focus on the new behavior of the addicts. I don't know if you decided what it's going to be. If not, you could consider using the Hunger affliction. It was first described in the Antagonists supplement, and mentioned in Boston Unveiled. It's a condition that causes human cannibals to degenerate into monsters. I think some Red Word cultists keep their afflicted grandfather as a pet. It has the benefit of transforming multiple subjects, as opposed to just the Claimed one.

                                        As for the Acamoth, you can keep the motif of corrupt sanctity. Start as if you're making a Spirit of Faith: a priest in rich robes, a beam of holy light and music, an Angel, a praying supplicant. Then, add a splash of something horrible: the priest has multiple hungry holes in place of a face, the light is accompanied by screams of agony, the Angel is designed by H. R. Giger, the ecstatic supplicant has their skin chewed off. An Actual Play called "Skyscaper Graveyard" used lyrics from Pink Floyd's Sheep as an Abyssal prayer, so you can borrow that. Choose an innocuous name and make sure to describe it all as sacred, because it is. Within the Prince, at least.

                                        Do you only need a description of the Acamoth, or do you want ideas on its behavior as well?

                                                The Acamoth's plans depend heavily on two external factors, namely the Red Word Cult and Dr. Amanda Keller.

                                                If you want to keep things simple, the Cult doesn't factor into the story at all. It doesn't exist, or the Acamoth works alone, either through ignorance or on purpose. Alternatively, you can allow for minor interaction between the two sides. Leave phone records, bus tickets or addressed packages for your players to find - just enough to point towards Howard's Rock. A step further is to create a close cooperation, but at this point things are getting intricate. The last option is to, if the PCs fail at their mission, have remnants of the addict group be creators of the Red Word Cult.

                                                Dr. Amanda Keller, ruthless as she is, is as likely to abhor the Abyss as any other sane Mage. If you want to keep things simple make her ignorant of Deep Purple's effects. The Acamoth is subtle, or the affected addicts were dropped from the project, and she didn't care to check. Alternately, she knows and is doing damage control. Her reputation is at stake, so the test subjects are being killed off, whether they're compromised or not. A final option is that not only doesn't she know - she is being manipulated. See below.

                                                The Acamoth might come up with one or more of the following plans:
                                                1. It tries to redeem its wards by replacing drug addiction with hunger for flesh. By its reckoning, being transformed by the Hunger is an obvious improvement of their lot. That makes it a pretty straightforward antagonist. It doesn't need to be subtle - it uses their desperation, persuasion and coercion to get them to eat human flesh, until someone becomes afflicted.
                                                2. It sees potential in at least some of its protégés, and attempts to turn them into proper worshipers. Methods vary, but include: cult tactics, editing the Oneiros, punishment and reward, being a confidante, Investments (fittingly, "Break an Addiction" is one of them) and strategically used Numina (like Rapture).
                                                3. It knows about Deep Purple and wants it to find wider use. The first tactic is to recreate the recipe for its own use. The other is to give Dr. Keller the most successful drug trial she has ever seen. If it's particularly ambitious, it might gradually push her research onto a darker path. If other Seers accuse her of Scelesti practices (deservedly or not), it will offer a helping hand, and the Cult will find it's first Awakened member.

                                                Involving the Abyss in the game is a balancing act. You want to make it alien and horrifying, but at the same time to give your players some point of reference. Normally these points are contradictory, but the Prince of 100,000 Leaves is unusually hospitable for an Abyssal Realm. You could even live there (not that you'd want to). That makes its Acamoth relatively understandable, which means you can imbue them with personality.
                                                1. A judgmental Acamoth will raise righteous fury against those who oppose it. It speaks like a hell-fire preacher, accompanied by holy light and sound of horns. Angry and not interested in negotiation, it will accept only other worshipers and sacrifices.
                                                2. A compassionate Acamoth is a genuinely kind person... thing. It will empathize with you and try to solve your problems. Some of its advice is actually very good. Other will involve cannibalism, and it will call invoke happiness, dignity and living for a purpose as arguments for it. If one of its Cultists is killed, it will plead for your players to eat their body - for dignity's sake. When refused it will break down crying, unable to understand.
                                                3. An intellectual Acamoth is aching to understand the real world. Somewhat distracted, it has strange speech patterns, such as repetition, meandering sentences, not using statements or referring to concepts as if to people. It actually admires Jesus Christ, as the Holy Lamb for the Slaughter, but asks for clarification on multiple points. For instance, why do people use bread as substitute?
