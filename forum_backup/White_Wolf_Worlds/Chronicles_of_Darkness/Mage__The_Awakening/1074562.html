    New mage storyteller

    04-07-2017, 05:16 AM
    Hello there ! So here's the deal, I want to run a mage chronicle for my regular player group. Now i have some experience storytelling Vampire the requiem, and even though i'm more familiar with mage the ascension, i was digging the second edition of requiem and the chronicle of darkness so much that i bought Mtaw 2nd edition.

    And WOW, this game is confusing, especially the cosmology. I'm digging the gnostic feeling of the game and the occult mysteries but well, i find it difficult to warp my head around the mage cosmology, The supernal realms are just diffirent vision of the same place but you can summon different entities from it ? Do they have self-awereness ? Do they interact with each orther ? What about bounds ? are they just concepts made flesh ? Is the supernal realms another world all together or just an idea ? and what about the abyss ? The abyss seems like a very real (bad) place fulll of nasty thing and even nastier realities. So yeah, as you can see i have a loooooot of questions, the universe is huge and i love it but i can't quite comprehend it just yet.

    So any help/advice would be appreciated. There is no plot for the chronicle just yet, heck i'm still considering going Ascenscion over Awakening.

    (I apologize in advance for my poor english, it's not my primary language)

    .

      It's kind of supposed to be confusing, every question you raise can become a potential plot hook or setting detail that you can customize to your personal preferences.

      The Supernal Realms are more like a layer of reality than a place, Mages just describe them in their most abstract form as places because it's easier to wrap your head around than the actual 11th dimension approach. It's indeed multifaceted on top of that, and the five "realms" that Mages draw from aren't even the whole of it. Just as the Abyss has everything not possible or untrue in it so to does the Supernal have everything that is, and there's a lot more of it than can be captured in the five Paths.

      Do they interact with each other? Not really, because the Path is a filter for viewing the Supernal, and usually (Without Prime 4 Apocalypse) no one is using two different filters at the same time. What happens when you are though? Plot.

              Gonna take my own stab at answering it.

              Originally posted by Emerick View Post
              The supernal realms are just diffirent vision of the same place but you can summon different entities from it ?
              As Mrm said, the Supernal is not a "place", but more like an aspect of reality. It represents and underpins reality as a whole, but it's not a place you can walk to. The Realms themselves may or may not be "real" in a strict sense; different cultural traditions hold different ideas. The Tremere believe the divisions exist but are forced and arbitrary; produced by the mythic Dragons of Atlantis, along with the Watchtowers. The Mages of Mutapa seem to see it otherwise; each Path being a lineage from a divine ancestor, but all Awakened drawing from the same unified source.

              At the end of the day, the Paths are best thought of as perspectives, though, rather than as a union with a specific place that other Mages can't access. Because, again, not a "place" in a physical sense.

              Supernal entities may be creatures, or they might actually just be what happens when you give a concept influence locally over reality. No different from how putting enough heat into wood starts a fire.

              Originally posted by Emerick View Post
              Do they have self-awereness ?
              Most appear to. They can quite often talk and communicate in a very human manner.

              Originally posted by Emerick View Post
              Do they interact with each orther ?
              Unknown and up for debate. Certainly, they seem to, on some level. They're aware of each other, at the very least. If asked about the Supernal, these entities will have knowledge of, and mention, the Exarchs, even if totally unrelated or outright opposed to them.

              Originally posted by Emerick View Post
              What about bounds ? are they just concepts made flesh ?
              The Bound are a Mystery. Whatever else they may be, they're manavoric entities which use the Arcanum; feasting on souls and worship. The Atlantean paradigm sees the Bound as the Old Gods; the formerly Supernal entities, cast out beyond their former station when the Exarchs sat the Thrones and claimed the Supernal as their own. Whether that's the case or not is up to you, but there's an entire set of Archmasters whose big goal is to restore one of the Bound to the Supernal and ride its coat tails up into the cosmos.

              Man, that's a lot of capital letters.

              Originally posted by Emerick View Post
              Is the supernal realms another world all together or just an idea ?
              This isn't explicitly answered, but it definitely isn't a place you can walk to. Archmasters can, briefly, force their perspective on the Supernal to make places within it, but the Supernal itself is... Something else. Does the law of gravity reside anywhere? Does the meaning found in art have a physical location?

              (And, no, don't say in serotonin and dopamine levels. Smartass.)

              The Supernal is a part of reality. It gives reality its form and function. Importantly, it also imparts reality its meaning and order.

              Beyond that, it's up to Mages to figure out.

              Originally posted by Emerick View Post
              and what about the abyss ? The abyss seems like a very real (bad) place fulll of nasty thing and even nastier realities.
              The Abyss is to the Supernal as a shadow is to light; it is the Supernal's antithesis. It isn't malefic evil, as it was largely first conceived to be in 1e, but it is definitely inimical to all sane, sensible, and reasonable minds. It's every impossibility gathered in one non-place; an infinite array of still-born godlings that are universes unto themselves, all trying to force their way into reality and remake it in their own image. The Abyss is corrosive and destructive and ultimately rejected by the Fallen World, sometimes violently, as a body rejects an invading virus.

              The Abyss is what stands between the mundane and the Truth; eternally blocking clear sighted vision with impossible possibilities.

                  The way I think of the relationship between the Supernal World and the Fallen (or Phenomenal) World is that the Fallen is reality and the Phenomenal is the truth that defines it. For a rough analogy from popular media, the Supernal is to the Phenomenal as the code is to the Matrix. The various Supernal Realms are like categories of those truths. Pandemonium, for example, is the totality of the symbols that define Mind and Space within the Phenomenal World. Some of these symbols do indeed appear to display autonomy and self-awareness.

                  Originally posted by Emerick View Post
                  and what about the abyss ? The abyss seems like a very real (bad) place fulll of nasty thing and even nastier realities.
                  If the Supernal is truth, then the Abyss is untruth, and in the same way that the Supernal defines the Phenomenal, the Abyss defines Paradox - the unreality to the Abyss's Untruth. In the Matrix analogy, the Abyss would be like corrupted code. It is no more a place than the Supernal Realms are, but its corruption can bring unreal things into Phenomenal reality.

                      Some of your issues with Mage 2E are caused by the tight restrictions the developers had on word-count. You can infer some of what the others have said from the Core, but a lot of the people speaking above have read 1E books that clarified a lot of the same questions we would have been asking if we started on 2E before 1E too. So no biggie.

                      They've answered your questions pretty well, so I'll add something else that may not be immediately apparent. Ascension has the setting established pretty thoroughly from what I am guessing----but Chronicles of Darkness practically begs for you to fill in the gaps yourself, and so, it deliberately leaves gaps for you, the ST, to fill.

                      These games drop all kinds of stuff everywhere between 1E and 2E books that make things seem more interconnected than they initially display themselves, but not to give ST's hints of some greater meta-plot for you to figure out and adhere to, but more that it wants to give you more legos to build your own setting out of. Is Arcadia the same as the Changelings? How come Strix from Requiem can mess with the God-Machine? This is all for you to figure out.

                      You can even just flat-out choose to ignore aspects of the setting(s) if it will make your story better for your players, as long as they sorta know in advance that what you are doing will deviate from the books so they don't get upset. For example, Mage establishes the Lower Depths as definitely separate from the Abyss, but I prefer to make them a lot more inter-related than the books say.


                      The Mage cosmology is definitely the harder of the cosmologies, half because it doesn't play nice with the other game cosmologies, and because when you ignore all the power-gaming you can do in Mage, the game is really about philosophy and perspective. Instead of a huge wealth of paradigms, though, the Mage 2E game assumes that most people naturally come to 5 "paradigms" as a matter of course--the Paths are like archetypes that Awakened Mages just naturally fall into. They stop having those Paths per se when they reach Archmastery and strike their own path, which could be very loosely analogous to Ascensions paradigms, which would I guess sorta make sense if I'm not fully off the mark, given that archmasters participate in practically their own game---the ascension war.

                      Either way, we have a space arcanum and a mind arcanum that already says that space and mind are illusions of our perspective, so the Supernal Realms wouldn't be the Supernal Realms unless they embodied that too. It is less important to be accurate over what the books say something definitely is concerning psychoactive places like the Hedge or the Supernal Realms, and focus more on eliciting an interesting response from your players--play with their perspectives, emphasis theme over detail, make them feel like they've learned something while also having more questions so that they keep coming back for more.

                      Since Mage is a game about being addicted to mysteries, your job as ST will be to get your players addicted to the mysteries. Which is great---it means you do not need to answer all of your own questions right away either!

                                                      The Ruin is about nihilism; making people think they can't pursue opportunities in front of them, out of cynicism borne of too many failures and suffering.
