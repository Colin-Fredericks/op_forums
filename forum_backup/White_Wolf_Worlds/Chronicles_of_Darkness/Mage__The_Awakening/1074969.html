    How do you typically choose to spend experience?

    04-07-2017, 11:33 PM
    What's your typical "order of operations" for spending experience with a new character in a long-running chronicle?

    I understand that this depends to a significant degree on your RP objectives for the character and the nature of the chronicle. I'm looking for rules of thumb that an experience player would often apply to make a long-lived character more effective. I've seen some strong opinions on this forum about how to allocate dots during character creation so I believe people can also provide guidance on experience allocation.

    For example "Get Gnosis 3 asap" or "Buy four or five rotes before you bother increasing any Arcana ratings" or "Get your highest-rated Arcana to 5 before you increase any of your lower-rated Arcana" or "Get at least Prime 2 early on". Or even "horde all your experience until you hit a specific challenge that requires you spend it."

    Thanks in advance for any advice.

      Define "effective"?

      As a rule, I'd advise against compulsively chasing the Arcana unless that's what your character is actually doing in-game. If they're desperately trying to unlock the secrets of the Supernal in as direct a manner as possible - and there are definitely Mages who do this - then pick whatever Arcanum is their favourite and focus on that. Buy up Gnosis and the Arcanum, potentially to the exclusion of all others. Buy skills and specialties related to that.

      Most Mages aren't like this, though. Most are dilettantes, who will acquire other Arcana, rotes, praxes, etc.

      Really, though, above all, remember it's the journey, not the destination. This is about having fun. Buy stuff that fits your character, not some "optimal" attempt at building. Becoming a master in an Arcanum rapidly lets you play with magic a bit more, but it can deprive your ST of fun challenges and the requisite knowledge to gain the fifth dot is meant to be significant. A master is revered even among the Awakened. They have deep and powerful insights into their area of specialty. These are the people other Mages speak to when they have trouble with the subject.

      If your character hasn't had time to build that knowledge, build the connections, build that depth, all that will feel... Hollow. It stops being a sign of deep, metaphysical insight and instead just becomes dots on a sheet. I've seen it happen and it isn't fun.

      Take it slow. Enjoy the growth.

              Example of "effective":

              Characters with too few praxes can have trouble regenerating Willpower, and Willpower regeneration is generally really important, so it's advisable to invest in a small set of praxes ASAP.

              Characters with low Gnosis can't have very many spells active so they struggle with maintaining important defenses or buffs while still being able to cast in-the-moment. Consequently it's important to invest in Gnosis early.

              Effective basically means having invested experience wisely so as to be versatile and prepared to tackle a variety of different challenges.

                  Yeah, when playing Mage, you might as well not bother with filling the mundane part of your character sheet. 9 times out of 10 answer to a problem is "Magic", so "more Magic" is usually the best thing - and a thing that will give you most options to roleplay and solve problems with - to buy. So Gnosis 3 --> 1st Attainment of a Legacy to get 3rd ruling and never touch it forever --> favorite Arcanum to the limit--> Gnosis higher--> Arcanum to the limit--> Gnosis higher--> Arcanum to the limit.
                  Other than that, only thing really worth investing in is Status. Getting good Status: Your Order and Status: Consilium will birth you many dots of "Merit equivalents" via requisitions.

                  EDIT
                  When we don't want that trend to happen in our games, we make it so you can only spend Arcane XP on Arcana and Gnosis, and hard lock Gnosis at certain thresholds ("no Gnosis higher than 3 until it's lifted later in the Chronicle", for example).

                          If you are using a Praxis purely as between scenes willpower recovery, then you really only need one Praxis.

                          Re: Variety of challenges. Thats actually one of the worst attitudes to take in an RPG. As an ST, I would take a Forces Master of Kung Fu super specialized McCombatpants then a true Mage dilente. With a specialized character I know exactly how to shine the spotlight on them and I know exactly how to subvert expectations. It's the old addage that Superman isnt the issue of the Justice League, Martian Manhunter is.

                          In many games being The Bard comes with enough draebacks. In Mage you can actually be amazing at everything.

                          Character Creation
                          First I decide on general concept, theme, and mood I eant to play. It helps if I think of actions I want the character to be doing and an emotional response I want to be evoking. I try to decide on a Path, Order, and the character's relationship with Arcana (do they focus on one? Many? Etc). Things are flexible at this point.

                          From there, I decide if my concept requires any 4's or 1's in attributes. The rest get set to 2 and 3 as appropriate. I make a note to return here if I need to qualify for merits.

                          For Skills, I decide if I need any skills at 4 or 5 for the concept and put those in first. I limit myself to one or two of those, max. Then I make sure every skill I need to execute my concept has between 2 and 3 dots as feels appropriate. I try to have all Order skills at at least 2 dots unless I am playing someone notably bad in the area or someone new to the order. Then I fill out fluffy 1's or 2's as I have dots leftover. Note that I return to skills later if I need to qualify for merits or have better Rotes.

                          Note that post creation, I probably will not purchase Attributes or Skills, so I try to get them where I want them at creation. This also helps me focus my concept.

                          I get 10 merit dots, not Gnosis, unless there is starting exp. Order, Consilium Status, and Mentor tend to be my first decisions. Second I buy in to any group merits (safe place. Hollow, sanctum, themed cabal) that the rest of the group wants. The rest I use for fluff, with a slight nod that Shadow Name, Destiny, and Familiar are slightly above curve, but not enough that I would give up something flavorful for them.

                          For Arcana, I decide what Arcana, if any, I need at 3. If I needed one at 3, then I pick what Arcana I'm okay with having at 1. Otherwise I tend to favor 3/2/1, 3/1/1/1, 2/2/2 or 2/2/1/1. I try to get Prime 1+ if I can.

                          Spending EXP
                          Gnosis, Arcana, Merits and Rotes as it makes sense for the story in about that order of priority. Gnosis 3 is the big threshold--after that I tend to try to flesh out my Arcana. I favor reflecting on the past story and thinking about what areas my character would logically improve, though sometimes I do ask the ST to include beung able to purchase a thing as part of a story. Merits are interspliced throughout.

                                              I won't lie, I like having a solid base of Merits. Don't overlook the usefulness of having a Sanctum or Library, for example. I mix this in with buying into Arcana that suit my desires for the character, with plans on going towards Gnosis more slowly. Admittedly, I've not played in any games that lasted quite a while yet, but I'm in one that feels like it'll go the distance and I've already got some solid plans on where I want to take things.

                                              Since we started out pre-Awakening, I used some experience towards starting out with Potent Nimbus (since it just fit the character too well) and putting a point into a Safe Place so the cabal has a base to start working towards making it into a Sanctum. I guess what I'm saying is, merits provide you with a lot of base advantages that magic might not be able to provide to you as quickly as you might need it. Then you can start rounding out your Arcana and moving up your Gnosis. I'm going to be a BIT contrary here and point out that your rote skills are worth putting points into as well.

                                              Lastly, definitely you want to buy rotes and praxes (especially those like Attainments for a Legacy you're going towards... it's an investment at that point that'll pay off) for those spells you're going to want a lot of skill in casting OR will be using all the time. I advise buying them when you find a spell just keeps coming up in game (or with a Praxis, you come up with a super cool signature spell you want to make your own).
