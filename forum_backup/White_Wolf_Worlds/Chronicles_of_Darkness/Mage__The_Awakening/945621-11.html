        Originally posted by Gammelbraxen View Post
        TL, DR: The Exarchs made immortality impossible through supernal magic. Seek out other sources of power or reach Archmastery and shake of the influence of the Exarchs.

        Also, yeah ... easy immortality is boring. In a Horror game? If you do accomplish it, there should be a Price.
        I like to think there doesn't have to be.
        What originally drew me to Mage is that generally there's no capital-P Price for magic.

        I mean, there's always a price for anything really, if you choose to look at it that way, but unlike for lesser magics, true (Supernal) Magic is deep understanding and immense power with price that a devoted athlete (or devoted anything that takes effort) would laugh at. And the Horror, to me, is in that. Who are you when you have unadulturated power?

        Personally, if I ever do manage to gather a table to play (I am scheming XD ) I will allow the Death 5 Steal Lifespan spell in 2E. Which is a great example both for this topic and for why I don't like 'But it has a Price' motiff for Mage.
        You can steal someone's life, with no consequence to yourself, if you scheme it well. And the horror comes from the result of this.
        To be clear, not that you have more years to your life. As a DM/GM/Storyteller, that elves can live 700 years and dwarves 500 and humans 100 is rather inconsequential to my campaign. Same for Mages. Lifespan of 80 years or 100? 100 or 200? You'll live as long as it's convenient for the game.
        The result is who you are afterwards. You're a person who stole someone's life.
        The two key versions of the aftermath of the Wisdom degeneration roll (for I would insist on this even at Wisdom 3, maybe not 2). You will either succeed on your roll and fell bad about it, properly guilty and troubled (Guilty Condition or not, you did some deep shit) or you will fail and become more of a monster. A person (when can that term stop applying, eh?) who's fine with stealing someone's life. What other acts are inconsequential in your pursuit of Mysteries, of power, of whatever Obsession drags you into the depts of Gnosis.

                                                    The 1e Astral Realms book, iirc, had a pretty straightforward description of the Morphean experience, which is that it is absolutely bonkers. Specifically, the most famous kind of Morphean involves flipping the basic relationship of one's soul to the universe inside-out, so that one no longer requires a body or non-Astral existence to maintain the Oneiros. The major second-order effect of this is that one's goetia are completely cut off from the outside world; rather than outside influence providing the material with which your subjective experience is constructed by your unconscious, the unconscious provides motivation and support for your conscious mind without any fuel. The goetia grow bizarre and twisted in isolation, while no experience the Morphean has can change their unconscious drives, because the basic relationship of experience/memory >>> unconscious >>> decisions (which is present in the Mastigos conception of the mind) has had its first two steps inverted: unconscious >>> filters all experience >>> conscious decisions. It's a fascinating concept for a character! Also quite mad, and only more so as time goes by. Morpheans are also noted to steal objects and memories from other people's Astral selves because then they can insert those as experience, learning and changing as people through suturing bit of other souls onto the outside of their hermetically-sealed self.
                                                    Morpheans are great, and I would say considerably more inhuman than any other kind of lich I'm aware of.
