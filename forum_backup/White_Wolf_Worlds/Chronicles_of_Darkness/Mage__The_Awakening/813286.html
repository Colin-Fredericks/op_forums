    [2ED] Lie, Dissonance and Quiescence

    01-21-2016, 07:52 AM
    Topic for three core terms in Mage setting, that are also often confused. Discussion to hammer and showing concepts in 2ED universe of it. First, short sum up.

    Lie – collection of false facts about reality that Sleepers believe in. All of “magic does not exists”, “god/goddess are not literal beings”, “there is only this material world”. Contrast to the Truth mages uncover in their lives after Awakening.

    Dissonance – effect of making magic more unstable by Abyssal Sympathy ( “Shard” ) all Sleepers have in their souls. In 1ED known as Disbelieve, but do not connected to the convocation of Sleepers. Even Sleepers firmly believing in magic make Dissonance.

    Quiescence – also known as Sleeping Curse. Effect of Sleepers instinctual rejecting Supernal Magic, and forgetting about spell events. Quiescence is also harming Sleepers soul, lowering their Integrity trait ( i.e. making them a bit less emotional stable ).

    And I have probably the best definition of Quiescence and it’s confrontation with general population Integrity in comic media. Straight from Dresden Files “Welcome to the Jungle” story – just after Harry Dresden acknowledges that the he used magic to the Sleeper's face…


    Is it correct? If not, where I make mistake in understanding?

          Basically. The Lie, the Masquerade, the Veil, G-M hiding? All the supernatural in the CofD tend to rely on a generalized "Doubting Thomas" effect. The general populous don't acknowledge the supernatural even if they live side by side with it. This effect is dialed up in Mage. Quintessence is actually a supernatural compulsion forcing people to ignore Awakened magic. Its still a Doubting Thomas effect, but its a DT effect enforced by Abyssal magic. In an ideal world, people wouldn't doubt they've seen anything supernatural. But, in the Fallen World, people are magically broken/castrated and blinded.

          Dissonance is when that doubting thomas effect triggers. It causes people to disbelieve they saw anything as well as rip into the magic they witnessed.

          I've seen The Lie used interchangably with describing the state of the Fallen World as well as another name for the Quintessence curse. I tend to think that the words are used interchangably - Lie as the common name, Quintessence as the formal/scientific name. Its like the difference between "dog" and "canis lupis famiiaris."

                  One thing I like about the design of Requiem and Awakening (and probably Forsaken, but I am not familiar with it) so far, especially for 2e, is that there are mechanical effects that enforce or hint at the creation of in-game cultural restrictions such as the Veil and Masquerade. It allows characters within the setting to have good reasons to enforce their secrecy, and at the same time if a member of that culture doesn't enforce it, the world itself will cover some of their tracks and impose some penalty. In this manner, even a newly Awakened or Embraced realizes by themselves that they need to hide, even without a mentor. Of course, without a mentor, the process is going to be painful and might get you killed or worse.

                      I agree with Wyrdhampster's interpretation, from what I've seen in the development blogs. I was never able to get into Mage in 1e, I was one of those people that got turned off by the core book and never dug enough into later material to learn how much more interesting the setting actually is than the core book made it look. So with that in mind, here is my understanding from what I've read about 2e.

                      The Lie is the feeling of powerlessness that we all feel at some point in our lives. It's the notion that humanity is small and insignificant in the face of a vast and uncaring universe. It's the little voice in our heads that tells us to keep our heads down, fall in line, don't make waves, we're not special and we're not important. Depending on how Mage-centric your crossover swings, this may be the cause of that Doubting Thomas Effect that we see in other lines, or it may just be another expression of that same phenomenon. Either way, it is a large part of what keeps people from Awakening, and it has a strong connection to the Supernal symbols of oppression that the Awakened refer to as the Exarchs. The Atlantis myth casts them as Ascended Mages from the time before that wove themselves into the Supernal and created the symbols of the Lie to keep humanity under control and prevent them from Awakening and eventually Ascending.

                      Dissonance is the result of the symbols of unreality (which the Awakened refer to as The Abyss) woven into the soul of every human being affected by The Lie (whom the Awakened refer to as Sleepers). When a Sleeper comes into contact with Supernal magic, the Abyssal symbols in her soul reacts with the Supernal symbols that the Mage uses to cast his spell in a manner similar to how anti-matter interacts with normal matter. The Sleeper's soul and the Mage's spell are both damaged by this conflict, and unreality may seep into reality in a phenomenon the Awakened call Paradox. For reasons that should be fairly obvious but suffer from "why doesn't it work like it did in cWoD" syndrome, this interaction occurs regardless of the Sleeper's awareness of or belief in magic. However, some humans are able to shed the shard of Abyss in their soul without actually Awakening, with one of the easiest ways to do this being gaining magical or supernatural abilities.

                      Quiessence, also known as the Sleeping Curse, is the soul's self-defense mechanism against the harm caused to it by Dissonance. the Sleeper subconsciously attempts to rationalize what they've experienced in a way that complies with The Lie. Failing that, they will outright suppress the memory. Again, depending on how Mage-centric your crossover swings, this may be the cause of other, similar memory-altering phenomena like Lunacy, or it may be unrelated.

                              For a vampire, the primary reason to keep the Maquerade is a social one. This is fitting for a social-political game. Mage is a game about exploration, Mysteries, learning, growing, and creating society. In other words, mental exploration. Naturally, mage is going to have their Must Hide equivalent rooted in those mental areas. The threat of the Lie is also higher for mages for a simple reason - Doubting Thomas is their weakness. Vampires have sunlight issues in this game in no small part because its a social challenge - daylight is when majority of humans do things. Mages struggle against fear and ignorance, the magical equivalents of science denial.

                              In short, despite being Hide!! equivalents, they serve vastly different purposes in the narrative. Its much like Wisdom versus Humanity. Sure, they are both karma meters, but that is all they have in common.

                                              Originally posted by GottaGoFeast View Post
                                              I like that idea! Do you have any house rules, or is just a matter of emphasis?
                                              I do have some houserules for 2e (I've shied away from running or playing Mage, and only lately have come around to the idea of doing so). Right now, my tentative houserules is to start every single spell with baseline Paradox (so instead of a free Reach, you always risk Paradox no matter how casual the spell is). In 1e, I'd get rid of the Vulgar/Covert system and run all spells as automatically risking Paradox at all times. For Quiescence, I'd run it closer to Lunacy, with the idea that mortals with high Willpower (or that succeed at their Integrity roll) can actually remember what happened, and are likely to become Hunters. I might steal the 2e Lunacy rules and have dramatic failures in the Integrity roll bestow the Proximi minor template.

                                              EDIT: I also might tweak the Disbelief rules to make sure that mages can't feasibly cast anything that would have any long-term permanent consequences on humanity, like mind-controlling authority figures or any such shenanigans. I'll probably adjust Disbelief so that it can reasonably keep magic in check.

                                              In terms of fluff, my baseline assumption is that mages are completely deluded about the nature of reality. Everything in the Mage books is true, when observed by a Mage. For everyone else, the Supernal Realms are nightmarish hell planes of soul-murder, and the Abyss is the stalwart guardian of humanity against the incursions of the aforementioned planes. Since humanity has a sympathetic connection to the Abyss, I reasoned that the Abyss is probably a very good thing for humanity. I also have Abyssal entities consider regular humans to be kin, to drive this point home. Where Mages see their souls as having "awakened into the truth", everyone else sees their souls as missing a vital component, which was replaced by a shard of the hellish realms. Paradox, then, is the Abyss trying to protect humanity from the Supernal soul-murder.

                                              Of course, Exarchs are still the bad guys (because they use the Supernal to actively oppress humanity) and Mad Ones are still terribly dangerous, but even Pentacle mages are hubristic and misguided. Banishers are the closest thing to "heroic mages", from humanity's perspective, and Scelesti are... complicated.

                                              This viewpoint emphasises an aspect of hubris that I thought wasn't terribly well explored in either edition of mage (though I'd have to read 2e properly to say for sure): the idea that hubris blinds. That mages are convinced that their delusions about reality are true, and that their hubris blinds them to the idea that they might be wrong, that their actions may not, in fact, be justified. That maybe humanity, in its powerlessness and sympathy for the Abyssal, might be right. That way, the personal horror for Mage is less "the price of power" and more "holy shit what if I am the monster???"
