    Do Proximi seem incredibly weak to anyone else?

    09-24-2016, 01:27 PM
    It seems like with Willpower as a spellcasting pool, no Arcanum rating to add to a spell's Primary Factor, and Yantras to gain bonus dice, Proximi would be extremely ineffective at casting spells with beyond the basic factors. And with no free Reach and Paradox success completely canceling the spell, they can't compensate by Reaching into Advanced factors without having to burn a ton of Mana, and their pool maxes out at 5. I get that not having Gnosis or Wisdom has a lot of knock-on effects that just make sense (no free Reach, no free Primary Factor, fixed spellcasting dice pool, no Attainments, etc.), but some of the other restrictions seem unduly harsh to layer on top of those limitations. Granted, they should by all rights be ineffective spellcasters compared to Awakened, but having to spend 5 hours and half of your mana pool to cast a Potency 1 spell that only affects a single target at sensory range for a scene and still has a 1/10 chance of doing nothing seems like too much to me.

    I'm considering allowing them to use a single Yantra, and maybe adjusting the way Paradox affects their spells so that it warps them normally instead of cancelling them. Would such a change be game-breaking or lore-breaking in any way I'm not seeing?

    Going by Willow now, or Wil for short. She/Her/Hers.

                              I find Proximi, as written, extremely lackluster. To the point that I honestly struggle to see how one is supposed to use them in game.

                              As allies they are niche at best, assuming a fairly standard spread of 3-5 players one of the Cabal will almost certainly be better at doing anything a Proximi could. As Antagonists they are unimpressive, even the most potent possible Proximi (15+ Blessings, Willpower 10) is going to be outclassed in their familial field by a starting mage who happens to focus on those Arcana (which again, with an expected 3-5 player spread is almost guaranteed), and they aren't common enough to be a threat in numbers. I guess one could use them as a mystery jumping point, chasing the legacy of an Archmage, but without a means to study supernal symbols or leap into Astral Realms (right you are Mrmdubois) Proximi themselves cannot offer a whole lot on that front.

                              They don't even share the themes of the awakened, they aren't Addicted to Mysteries, they don't struggle with Hubris, they can't study the hidden symbols that rule society. It seems to me that they fill basically the same niche as mortals with supernatural merits, which is a pity because 'cursed sorcerers, who inherit supernal power' really is splendid narrative territory.

                              Edit: Okay, so as not to be a total kvetch, there are two themes which I find Proximi do explore quite well: Choice & Entitlement.

                              Choice - Mages, all Mages, choose to Awaken. Proximi do not, they have power and a curse thrust upon them exclusively because of who their parents (or great, great, great, etc, grandparents) are. Proximi can make the point, one most Mages would balk at, that not everyone wants to awaken. Some people were quite happy living an ignorant, sleepy life.

                              Entitlement - Proximi (can) also have something Mages do not: Pedigree. Agatha, a Sister of the Mountain, isn't some uppity city chick who hated her job so much she went magic-crazy (<-Proximi POV) and now wants to save the trees. She's the heir to a long line of guardians going back generations. She's got history, upbringing, and a curse that keeps her in line. In other words, she has the right to do what she does. She pays the price, she keeps the tradition. What Mage can honestly claim to do the same? And what give them the right to intervene?

                              These two themes are excellent, and pose questions the Awakened may otherwise never consider. Unfortunately, when push come to shove Proximi don't have the bite to backup their bark, and end up having to run to other Mages for help. Which kinda ruins it if you ask me.
