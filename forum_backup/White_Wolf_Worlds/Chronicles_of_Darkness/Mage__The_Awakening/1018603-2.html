        Originally posted by MCN View Post
        Anyways, the point is that tyranny or causing suffering is closer to a Yantra than anything else. A tool, not a goal.
        I want to address your whole post but this feels like a good summary of it so I'm gonna save space on quoting.

        This assessment doesn't sit with me. The precise why is a hard thing to put into words, because it's not wrong, per se, but it doesn't feel right.

        As best as I can summarize it, magic doesn't have a separation between an action and the thing itself. Symbols of Tyranny are Tyranny itself. When a violent servant of a dictatorial regime bludgeons a protestor with a cudgel, that is the General. When someone doesn't dare speak up in favour of liberty and equality for fear of what the neighbours might think, that is the Unity. There is no distinction between the Exarchs and the kind of tyranny they endorse. They are the thing which is itself.

        For that reason, tyranny is a goal, because the Exarchs seek tyranny. Whether that tyranny makes them more powerful or not is anyone's guess but tyranny isn't a means for them; it is a goal unto itself. That they rule and maintain hegemony is a part of the very idea that they are: Tyrants reign. If they did not reign, they would not be the Exarchs.

        (This is, incidentally, a part of why the Free Council believes hierarchy fosters the Lie: Hierarchies must enforce themselves on the unwilling, and the use of force to enforce one's will on another for the sake of controlling them is Exarchial in the extreme.)

        The Seers themselves, meanwhile, are Mages. As you say, that means they're addicted to the Mysteries; chasing their Obsessions. The Seers of the Throne offer that but, and this is critical, so does every Order. If all you want is a pile of occult lore, the Mysterium has your number. The difference that the Seers offer is that you get what they offer cheap. They don't require a year of your life spent researching a Mystery before you get your first initiation. The moment you prove you're a cold bastard by ruining someone's business or bribing a politician into killing a bill that helps people - the moment you can no longer hold onto any hope that you're not really hurting anyone - is the moment you get access to their shiny things. The funny thing about being a cold bastard is: It's easy. Tearing things down, hurting people, ruining lives; these things take seconds. Building is always harder than destroying.

        Likewise, the Seers offer material rewards. While the more powerful Seers shift their gaze off of the mundane - mostly because they're so personally potent and involved in the Mysteries and jaded by excess and human misery at that point, that wealth holds no more promises or secrets - and into the esoteric, keep in mind that the lure of material wealth is a powerful one. A Mage who is promised a fat bank account is no more or less susceptible to the offer than anyone else, but it's more than just that. The Seers don't just give you wealth; they give you privilege. Money is great and all, but the Seers help you get to actually use it. It's not just "Can you buy a fancy house?", it's "Can you buy a fancy house next door to a crime lord who invites you over for a party, whereupon you shoot illegal firearms and explosives off the back of his deck as a celebration, before the police arrive, join the party, and place bets on his equally illegal mutant animal pit fighting ring?". The Seers don't just offer you freedom from want; they offer you the grandest heights the world has to offer. They are outright Satan standing with Christ on the Quarantania and offering all the kingdoms of the world.

        All of that comes from denying other people the same opportunities; from creating rules that apply to all and then exempting yourself from them. That is the very essence of tyranny.

        All the tools, all the Mysteries that Seers get access to, exist in service of those goals. These Mysteries are not idle things, preserved "because". They exist as tools to enslave the world.

        Tyranny is the yantra and the spell. It is the means and the end; the purpose and the method. The two cannot be separated anymore than the Exarchs can from the actions of which they are the symbol.

                        But Exarchs are Supernal symbols. Living ones, in fact. For whom action and intent are one and the same, which is a big theme of Supernal magic in general.

They are one and the same for that Exarch. To say that the former reason is different from the latter is to say they aren't living concepts, but just a reflection of that concept. We already have Rank 6+ spirits for that.

                        And sorry to say this, MCN, but saying that the Exarchs are actually one or more of the cosmic god-forces in other gamelines seem less like a creative interpretation of the game, and more a reversal of that "All gamelines are shadows of the Mage cosmology" thing we had before that some Mage players insisted on. This particular "hard line", where Exarchs are Supernal gods of Tyranny, is mor of a setting statement needed to pin the game down with consistency. And I don't think even the Crossover Chronicles we're all anticipating will trample one gameline's setting in favor of another - afterall, we had Mage developers clearly saying that Supernal Arcadia and Changeling Arcadia are not one and the same? If we go. "Oh, the God-Machine is actually the Father" or "Oh, the Psychopomp manifests as a Rank 9 kerberos", then we can say "Ah-hah, so the Gentry are Supernal Entities of Arcadia! Pssh, changelings aren't so important in the grand scheme after all!" or "Ah-hah, Pangaeans really are earth-bound Superna Entities of the Primal Wild!" That is one thing I dread the most in any sort of Crossover material.

                        Oh, and about the Dark Mother and the Raptor. We know that some mages claim that the Begotten must be of the Raptor, because the former are primordial fear-creatures and the latter is the symbol of control through fear of the wilderness. This is cannot be the same as saying that the Dark Mother and the Raptor are one and the same. It's just that the Dark Mother and her Begotten are patterns of the Fallen World that resonate strongly with the Supernal symbol that is the Raptor. The Primordial Dream resonates with many other symbols other than the Raptor, and the Raptor resonates with many other patterns than the Primordial Dream.

                        About "strengthening". Like I said, Supernal gods do not act. They exist. Whether this continued act of existence counts as 'strengthening' themselves is a semantics problem. Do we strengthen ourselves by breathing, eating and drinking? Normally, we don't. But the nutrients we consume build our muscles and facilitate our hormone and nervous systems, so does it count as 'strengthening' our bodies? If you put it that way, well yes.
