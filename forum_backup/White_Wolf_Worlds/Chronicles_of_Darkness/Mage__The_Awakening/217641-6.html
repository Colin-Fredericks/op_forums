                                                    One thing to remember is that the Lie hides magic, not mages. A sleeper doesn't have to see the spells to start getting suspicious and a hunter sure as hell doesn't need to seem them to. Why do hunters need to accurately remember supernal magic to hunt supernal mages? Simply believing them to be unnatural as fuck is enough to set off most. That guy who seems to always bet on the right horse, in spite of not having a way to rig the race, he may be in league with a spirit or he may be Awake. That girl who everyone seems to adore but no one can remember more than 'that one with the hair and glasses right? Yeah she's awesome!,' could be a vampire or a mage. The lingering effects of magic, the broken minds, raped souls, and otherwise unnatural effects, are more than enough to get the wrong attention.

                                                    If you need more than that to work with though, look at the witchunts of old and the writeup in Mage Core, people fear what they don't understand, they fear that kind of power. The Quiscience only hides the magic, the power and unholy insight you gain from it is what gets people wanting you dead. What's a more interesting game, one where the hunter is after you because he saw a fireball, or the one where he's after you because he's afraid of you, because he hates you for being so damn different? I'm assuming the mage's point of view here, being in the mage forum, but the point still stands; there are more complex reasons to provoke mage hunters than 'I saw them cast a spell.'

                                                    That said, I could be totally wrong here and underestimating the lie.

                                                            Oh, I largely agree, but the issue (for me is)

                                                            -The Lie as it stands is a bit too perfect; the Hunter wouldn't remember something vaguely unnatural and scary happened, he wouldn't remember any supernatural element to the scene at all.

                                                            -Amnesiac witch/werewolf/anything hunters just strikes me as extremely dumb.

                                                            -I have an aesthetic preference for a wide array of failure modes for built-in supernatural defenses; weaknesses, cures, vulnerabilities, etc. "Most of the time, Luna clouds the minds of men for us...sometimes, this fails...we don't know why or how.." I also have a preference for hunters having a high degree of utility as dangerous antagonists.

                                                            Hence, the Vigil grants that little extra subconscious push that unclouds the memories. Maybe not well, maybe not even immediately ,but there's always been a caveat with Lunacy or Dominate and what-have-you about the memories being repressed, how they might come out in dreams or hypnosis, that sort of thing. It's basically just the plot cover to have the patient at the insane asylum whose drawings represent what they did as a vampire slave that they otherwise can't remember, and so on.

                                                            In actual practice, if a hunter is after a mage, he's probably not after him because he witnessed a spell. Who goes around casting random spells in front of those rare few people who take up the Vigil? It's more, if a hunter fights a mage, I want him to remember the battle.
