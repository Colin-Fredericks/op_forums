    I don't think it's Wise (hah) to manipulate these dice bonuses without the context. I feel that these +5 dark yantras would throw a spanner into design of spellcasting, as it apparently is balanced about +3 being hard to get, with +1 being the bread and butter. If you really wanted to make it attractive, how about allowing to use mana gained in the sacrifice "in one go", bypassing mana-per-turn rules to boost the spell? This way it serves as a shortcut to power, which is usually what black magic is about.

    EDIT
    Basically, imagine you have 10 mana and human sacrifice worth 5 mana. You do the ritual, do the sacrifice as the last yantra; it should give you 5 mana, but you already are full; instead of being wasted, it goes towards your spell. Then you apply 10 of your own mana to make a powerful spell that uses a lot of Reach.
    I guess you could do this anyway by bleeding mana into the ritual per turn and regaining it by doing sacrificies as yantras (so you empty out your mana tank, but refill it during yantras). No need for extra dice, it seems practical enough. You don't get to cast "theoretically more powerfull spells" than anyone else, as your maximum is the same, but you get to cast at your peak easier and you can replenish your resources easier, so you get to cast at your peak easier even more. With Dark Magic, you can afford using the juice. If you increase yantra bonus per sacrifice to +5 (which is possibly what is in the book anyway, because I remember someone somewhere mumbling that yantra bonus for sacrifice might be equal to mana gained), you will bloat dark spells numerically by a lot; imagine that it gives you 5 dot yantra and that you are sacrificing 3 people, so you get +15 to your spell roll (though you need to spend 10 on spell factors). That's oppressively too much. And before you go "but a willpower adds +3!", well...maybe the system was balanced while calculating that in?

            You spend Mana to reduce paradox. You get paradox by Reaching to make your spell more powerful. If you can spend more mana at once, you can cast more powerful spells without them blowing in your face.
            Problem with making Left Handed path objectively more powerful is that it will make it objectively more powerful, which isn't really the default stance the game takes on it. Kinda like Dark Side - Left Handed Path is supposed to be quicker, easier, more dangerous, but not more powerful. And, from practical stand point, if you make Left Handed practices too good, you will invite an arms race where you either use left handed means or are left behind in dust. It's healthier for the game to not make non-desirable (and left handed practices are such; after all, default protagonists are not supposed to follow left handed path) options too good, because it's a no win situation - PCs are punished if they follow the spirit of the game and don't use them, and they are punished if they do use them.
            Actually, I think that the basic problem here is that game doesn't really want you to consider going Left-Handed, while you want game to want you to go Left-Handed. Kinda like many Star Wars games don't encourage going Dark Side in default rules, and open that up only in "antagonist sourcebooks".

            As for mana problems - I think that having basically unlimited mana as long as you can sacrifice something, someone, is a great perk to have. It won't make you more powerful in *a single instance*, but it's a broad power boost. Dark Side doesn't need nukes, not being bound by constraints and limits is good enough on it's own (and doesn't make non left handers losers).

            As for Gnosis 5 - people really consider that mid tier gnosis? It always seemed like end-game Gnosis for us, with "middle of the road" being 3 at most...

            Also, you missed something -even if you can spend 5 mana per turn, if you are doing a ritual, you will only pour 10 mana into it (or whatever is your mana limit); doing a human sacrifice as a part of ritual will restore ~5 mana points instantly, so you "cheated in" another 5 mana points into the ritual, exceeding your basic limit. That's 5 paradox dice removed from the roll, increasing your paradox reduction by half (10 --> 15). That's a reach or two. This, curiously, works better if your gnosis is lower, because you get less paradox per reach, while mana remains constant.

                After some extra thinking, I really like potential relationship between Gnosis and Sacrifices. Early on, when every Reach gives you small Paradox pool, that unholy burst of Mana is enough to get you high and more; one sacrifice is all you need, and you are swimming in energy. But as your Gnosis rises, Paradox per Reach gets bigger, and your usual sacrifices start being less and less efficient; this, coupled with the lifestyle you estabilished and standards you adopted, this becomes very annoying, so you try to make it right. And best way to do it is by sacrificing more people.
                This is, by the way, a very basic technique that pyramid schemes and cults (and Seers of the Throne, probably) use to strengthen their grip on their victims - they introduce them to desirable lifestyle (money, partners, food, free stuff, social respect), let them adopt it as their default state, and then they pose a danger to it or take it away - most of the time, people won't feel good about downgrading their lifestyle, so they will work extra hard to get it back.
                Kind of like you could comfortably live without a cell phone today, or with your old model; but after you switch to new one, and it breaks after you got accustomed to it, it doesn't feel *right* to use the old one, you feel pressure to buy one with all the gadgets the broken one had, right?
                Well, human sacrifices do that to you :V.
