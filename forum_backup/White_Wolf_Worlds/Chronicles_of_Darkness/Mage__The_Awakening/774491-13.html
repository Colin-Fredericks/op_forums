I'd assume that Hunters wouldn't want to just bust in at first sight with no real preparation for what they're going after. And as someone else mentioned, push to talk is what I was assuming going in, not transmitting everything automatically, just having the people in the field report back pretty much everything they see.

                            So at this point, the coordinator sitting back at base would have had absolutely zero exposure to any sort of Supernal magic. No visuals, no audio, no touch, taste, or smell. All they'd be working with are sheets of mundane paper, written on by mundane ink. And even if the events described would be unbelievable to your average joe, the guy keeping track of these things is still a Hunter. He knows there's shit out there, and without ever feeling the touch of Quiescence there's nothing to really force him to think differently. So he'd take his in-scene reports seriously, and prepare countermeasures to the things depicted within.

                            So at this point, when the field agents go in, even if they don't consciously know what they're going to be dealing with, they'll still have all they need to be able to appropriately react to any previously gained information. Now whether they could adapt to the situation quickly enough is simply going to be a measure of the Hunters skill. They don't know what they're going into, so even though they're equipped with everything (relatively) that they'd need, if they lack the appropriate training necessary to keep cool under pressure and adapt they're pretty much dead regardless. That said, that's pretty much always been the case, so I figure if they're any good at being a Hunter at all this shouldn't be that much of an issue against mages specifically as it's already a base job prerequisite.

                            That and the people doing recon are still going to suffer a lot of breaking point checks regardless. Larger organizations have the benefit of being able to rotate out their recon specialist to give them time to get their shit together. Small cells will obviously struggle, but that's just the usual.

                                        I just keep seeing any mage with at least Forces 2 and the slighest tactical sense cutting-off all communication with the coordinator (and among the attacking Hunters) with the barest of effort if and when the mana hits the fan. Plans usually fall apart upon first contact with the enemy (although if the Hunters survive, they'll likely have no memory of any plan).

                                        Complicated? Not really. Time consuming? Not really? These are things that Hunters should be doing regardless assuming they possess the manpower. Doing recon and having someone to coordinate everyone and keep track of details is a basic step to becoming organized, and taking notes is the first step to gathering information about anything. It should become pretty obvious pretty quickly that the minds/memories of those who witness the event directly become compromised and that notes and other reminders don't really work very well. And a hunter cell that hasn't learned the value of recon is a dead hunter cell. Regardless of whether they unknowingly bust into a vampires 'haven' unaware that that vampire is the Prince, and his 'haven' is Elysium or trying to chase down someone they believed to be a mage only to find themselves lost in the Hedge as the changeling they were gunning for jukes them in the thorns, not doing your research gets people killed.

                                        As for how they'd know to do so, that's easy. The next logical step of finding out memories are being messed with is to test these things, to see if the notes and other recordings affect those who were not directly exposed. This could fairly easily be tested even on non-hunters (and assuming you want a thorough test you'll probably have to test it on non-hunters at some point regardless). Results would show that perceiving the event itself would mess with memories, but as long as direct perception isn't involved you're good. And then the Hunters spread the information around to other cells, and then even hunters who haven't specifically done these tests will know.

                                        Honestly, with as ever present an effect it is, this should be as common knowledge as fire and vampires. How would hunters know to use fire against vampires? Testing. Sure, some fictional sources list fire as a weakness, but there are just as many that don't. Someone had to test all the myths at some point, be it fire, sunlight, garlic, wooden stakes, spilled rice, whatever. Same thing here. People will do testing, and once they're pretty sure of their findings, word will spread. Hell, it was probably one of the Tier 3 organizations that figured this crap out first, but information trickles down.
