          A mage may have one dedicated tool at one time. A mage may use a maximum number of yantras according to their gnosis in each spell. A yantra is symbolism of an item, every item contains symbolism but a mage can only utilize so many symbols at once.

          Your rote yantras as determined by your order. Each order gives three. So the suggested skill for spells is mostly just saying which of those skill the developers think should fit but are not the only ones that can fit. I'd say that when you learn a rote you learn it with a set rote yantra (skill) that can apply to it but I am not sure if that is how the game is intended.

                          You can dedicate only one tool, but you may use multiple dedicated tools. Some abilities allow you to count something as a "dedicated tool". Egregore does that, Techne does that, turning something into a Soul Stone does that, Adamant Hand does that, and so on. In these cases, Dedicated Tool benefit does stack.

                              Egregor (****) and a mage using their own soul stone as a Yantra are considered using their respective items as dedicated magical tools, pp. 98, 101.

                              However, Adamant Hand and Techne do not. Both merits permit their respective purviews to become Order tools (+1 bonus to spellcasting), not dedicated magical tools.

                              It is also unclear whether the Paradox mitigation benefit provided by a mage's one officially permitted dedicated magical tool stacks with the Egregore or soul stone Yantra benefit or if the maximum Paradox mitigation for use of dedicated magical tools is -2.

                                      Yantras seen with Prime should be able to determine their relative value to casting. In other words, yes, anything can be a yantra, but Prime will tell you that the plain wooden cup is a better Yantra for a healing spell than all those gold ones.

                                      The Rote skills are just typical skills that are used for encoding Rotes. A Master only encodes one skill into his Rote, so two Mages might know an identical Rote but use completely different skills to cast it. Use it to individuate your Master NPCs a bit. They're just suggestions, so feel free to figure out how any skill could have a metaphysical reason to enforce the Imago of a given spell in the forging of a Rote.
