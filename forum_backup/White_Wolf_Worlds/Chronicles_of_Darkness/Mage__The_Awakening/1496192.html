    Shadow Name too close to your real name

    11-04-2022, 10:10 AM
    Greetings,

    I would like to know the implications of my shadow name sounding too close to my real name. My character Edward (Acanthus, Free Council), has been looking into the symbolism of Air, Water and not following the current... So he wants to be Eddy, problem is that is too close to his real name or to the diminutive Eddie.

    What are the implications of having shadow and real names too close? Sympathetic effects and resonances included.

    Thanks,

      I think he would only run into issues if he lets his mortal friends call him Eddy, which would lead to him being "that guy" who INSISTS on being called Ed or Edward and corrects people every time they said Eddy. I'd also have him try to keep other mages/monsters from knowing his real name.

      Any sleeper who has accepted Eddy as a name for Edward wouldn't be protected from the nimbus, etc. Likewise, if too many mages are easily able to think of Eddy as just short for Edward, the whole thing COULD be in danger. But as long as Eddy makes sure to uphold the symbolism of his name, it shouldn't be a problem.

      Of course, if he went plural, Eddies, it is probably different enough to make it work.

          Physical approximation doesn't matter, symbolic one does. Their mundane name can be Edward and they behave one way.

          But when they become Eddy, they are a Moros embodying the role of Edward Elric. Their wear a red jacked with an Oroboros symbol, they have a metal gauntlet augmenting an arm (a multitool or weapon), they use alchemical symbolism, are more confident, have different body language and mannerisms. So symbolically the Tapestry sees them as two different people, so no problem.

          But if they behave similarly, dress similarly, have a similar name and so on, then the Shadow Name won't do its job of grounding their Nimbus to only affect their mage self. Mechanically, I would say this would happen if both are similar and the mage doesn't have a dot in Shadow Name. Mysteries, adversaries and other phenomena will be drawn to them in circumstances where its harder to practice discretion.

          If they choose a different Shadow Name and practice basic separation of their personas, this effect lessens to standard levels. If they purchase a dot in Shadow Name (whether its similar or not) by doing the work to establish the magical persona, then the merit's benefits make things easier.

                  I believe it will not, since James has no significant symbolism. He stopped or paused his Sleeper life to pursue his mage life, one transitioned into the other, which means his contacts will be under the effects of his Nimbus.

                  In terms of Arcadian logic, actor E was playing role A, then he moved to role B. Arcadia needs to move the story of A forward, so it merges it with B, since E was playing the role before. Now E is playing B taking over the story of A, rather than switching between the roles of A and B.

The supernatural is also actively dangerous, so being constantly exposed to insane spirits, Lovecraftian horrors, terrors from beyond the veil of death and much more will severely affect their overall wellness. So it wouldn't be surprising if someone in their Order sat them down for a talk, if not for concern then because they don't want to deal with a Rapt.

                      I think the information on Shadow Names really conveys a lot of useful insight here.

                      For a start, there's a clearly established standard for what a person's sympathetic name is given at the start of the description of the Space Arcanum; it's the name people (specifically people with Strong sympathetic links to you) called you by the first time you started forming sympathetic links to other people during infancy (said to be the time a child begins forming a sense of self). If he was called James (Hall) at the time, then that is the permanent name for sympathetic magic purposes, barring the intervention of magic. If he was called by a nickname from the outset, then that is the sympathetic name, even if his technical legal name is James.

                      Sympathetic names are specific, so a modified form will not suffice if it's all the mage knows.

                      I'd say that using a Shadow Name that is identical to your sympathetic name for the purposes of stopping sympathetic magic is basically useless, even if it's meant as a homonym. Like, if a mage knows your name is Eddy, they're not going to confuse themselves into thinking they're visualising the meaning in terms of air flow when they're casting a spell.

                      On the other side, Shadow Name for the purposes of preventing your Nimbus from leaking along your sympathetic connections goes a bit deeper than just having a different moniker when encountering other mages. Even if you don't go deep enough for it to be represented by the Merit, the assumption is still that a mage adopts certain differences in persona that give the Shadow Name some substance. Some of this description of how the mage lives does sound like it gives it that backing, although frankly it also sounds like the mage is also detaching himself from most of what a Shadow Name would protect anyway.

                      Of course, there's more to a Shadow Name than the functionally practical, there are deep rooted cultural reasons to why mages primarily use them among one another, and don't exactly treat them casually either.

                      Hmm... I think somebody needs to know that something is your sympathetic name before they could use it in a spell, so a person who has been called Eddy their whole life could still present that in a context where mages wouldn't immediately peg it as a personal name and be safe. And I think the surname does also need to be known.
