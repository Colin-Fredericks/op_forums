                                Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post

                                So, you know I'm firmly a member of the "mages are monsters" camp. But I think that, for some people, the issue is that the ways in which Mages are monsters is ignorable. A noir detective game doesn't give you the tools to play a superhero. Promethean's humanist themes are inseparably integrated into the mechanics. Mage presents mages as monsters in the fiction, and it gives you the tools to play a monstrous mage. But it doesn't do anything to discourage you from ignoring those tools. People read that Mages are supposed to be monsters, then they play the game and don't experience any compulsion towards monstrosity. They are given the option of playing the game the way it is presented in the fiction, but not prevented from playing it in a different way, so they question the fiction. It's an issue of ludonarrative dissonance. This is, unfortunately, a natural consequence of the carrots over sticks design philosophy.

                                I agree a lot with this. In the end, I don't think that I've had a single player character in our group act "like a Monster" - there is no push, no need, no motivation to do so. Lot of problems can be solved in non-monsterous ways, and both setting and mechanics actually seem to punish monstrous behavior - Wisdom is a thing losing which sucks and can be a "game over" for you if you lose enough of it, and apparently there are mechanisms of the peer pressure and social regulation that actively punish and restrict monstrous behaviors (abusing sleepers, "dangerous magic", even stuff like unsafely relinquising spells are apparently either straight out illegal, or leading to social ostracism). "Evil" magics - like sacrifices or soul stealing and other stuff - currently seem pointless, because...why bother? You can get same or better results using "legit" means. And you won't have to rebuy Wisdom or explain yourself to your peers!

                                I can take a genuinely ok or even good person, and then transform her into a Vampire, and the game will naturally put her into a horror position of becoming a monster, and having to be a monster in order to continue her nightly existence. The very idea of turning a well meaning, "equality for everyone" girl into a Ventrue with major dots in Dominate makes me smile about the struggles to uphold her humanity. The same character in Mage?
                                Wisdom 7 all the day, without very much to challenge that.Turning that into a horror game requires about as much effort on my part as turning a DND adventure into one, with the note that I don't feel like either game gives me proper tools for that at all, so I'm mostly doing it myself from the scratch.

                                    Angels are decidedly monstrous, but much of culture understands them to be benevolent. Granted, they're emissaries of the God Machine - but they're perhaps more monstrous because of their capabilities. I believe they're more monstrous than regular human cultists, to some degree.

                                    It could be argued that it doesn't matter if you're the most benevolent, or neutral, or evil mage. Your designation as mage makes you terrifying to behold because for humans you're an anomaly. From the perspective of those within the Lie, presumably all of us in the real world, mages are exceedingly strange and mysterious. They're monstrous simply by virtue of being, not necessarily acting. They become more monstrous if they're actively making life hell for humans (or whatever is perceiving them.)

                                    I'm sure, to vampires and werewolves, mages are bizarre and creepy even if they don't mean to be.

                                    You can be as nice as you want to be, but I know more or less how much your kind can do, and how can I trust you to be benevolent when your kin twist and manipulate things all the time? How do we (humans/vampires/werewolves/etc.) know you're not just part of some greater up-fuckery? How do we know you're not just a more organized version of all the horrors that exist in the world of darkness?

                                        Originally posted by WHW View Post
                                        I agree a lot with this. In the end, I don't think that I've had a single player character in our group act "like a Monster" - there is no push, no need, no motivation to do so.
                                        It is explicitly the job of the Storyteller to put pressure on the player characters and encourage choices that result in dramatic outcomes that lean on their flaws. People being hesitant to make the push that the game calls for them to make is a different problem from that push not existing in the game.

                                        "The same character in Mage" as a well-meaning Ventrue that specced as far into Dominate as a starting character can manage is a character with three dots in Mind and all their starting Rotes and Praxes devoted to mind control and memory alteration and probably an Obsession relating to social hierarchy in some fashion; the fastest way for her to branch out from a casual ability to violate the sanctity of another character's mind with a thought and a gesture is to gamble with her Wisdom in service to her Virtue, suffer intense failures in spellcasting, take Conditions on exceptional spells, and contain Paradoxes to garner Arcane Beats with which to broaden the scope of her specialties. She can certainly coast her way to that point for most things that aren't Wisdom dots or extra Praxes, but her more driven contemporaries will outpace her quite rapidly if she rests on her laurels in a notionally meritocratic society like the average Caucus or Consilium.

                                        The world exists outside the characters, and the nature of Awakening inclines characters to want to keep up with at least some parts of it; the mechanics alone cannot provide all of that drive without turning it from a drive to an inarguable and objective need, at which point the game would be more about being A Kind Of Monster than it would be about being a Mage with a view of the cutthroat world of the enthralling supernatural.

                                                            He did that for a group of bandits and even established them as the defense force of the town - set up a method of getting them training and employment via the mines they recently cleared of goblins.

                                                            The bandits agreed to it and so my party went off to clear another area two days from town.

                                                            When we came back a bunch of villagers were dead because the bandits couldn't shake their old ways and didn't particularly care to work when they could just steal.


                                                            This kind of shit happened numerous times during the adventure and ultimately my character retired an apathetic dragon-slayer who became leader of a mercenary group.


                                                            I thought I was making good and right choices. I thought I was being heroic. The world played out differently and those choices did not resonate as heroic.


                                                            That isn't to say I couldn't have made it as a heroic archer! But it would have been pretty difficult to maintain that energy given the unforgiving world. Basically, making the whole "heroic archer" thing all the more heroic for those that can manage it.
