                            Originally posted by Dark Archon View Post
                            As for Mage - this could lead to many interesting stories (unless he is Acanthus; then he can just Postcognition himself).
                            I don't know, sufficient disconnect from one's own past might sever the temporal sympathy to it entirely.

                            Besides that, I don't think scrutinising an unknown past in detail is exactly a trivial thing. First of all you've got to select a target time period, which might leave one casting about randomly until hitting upon a time that is useful. Second, observing one's past in detail can be Reach heavy; you're obligated to Reach for sensory range to cast through temporal sympathy, and you probably want Advanced Duration to get anything more than brief snippets.... I guess when you already need to have Time 2 to cast that, you get enough Reach, but you might want to push further to be able to fast forward around inconsequential parts unless one wishes to spend actual hours of real time looking over the stuff. And it's costing Mana every time you cast.

                            Oh wait, I forgot about Reaching for instant casting, unless one wants to spend three hours in ritual per hour to Unveil.

                            Also, if we're talking about a fresh mage, that's facing the prospect of the spell failing outright; like, we're talking about a starting pool of three dice, and getting that Potency high enough to overcome the presumably Weak temporal sympathy requires taking a -4 penalty. Functionally speaking, you only get one Yantra to take you above the chance die, because one of your other slots gets taken up by the sympathetic Yantra to cast the spell in the first place.

                            Account for all of that and it becomes a question of how much a character wants to spend dedicated solely to rebuilding their own timeline (accounting for the fact that they can only cast so many times before tapping out of Mana).

                            I'm thinking a story of the newly Awakened amnesia Acanthus is not one where being an Apprentice of Time sweeps the matter away entirely but rather the kind of narrative where a person goes around searching for clues that provide brief snatches of memory, several of which need to be assembled to provide one another with context. Just where it's not actual memory but the character taking short glimpses into key points of the past and gradually stringing them together.

                            Either that or the character working towards becoming powerful enough to make reliable spells with decent power and duration that do not risk Paradox to go after larger chunks at a time.

                                    Originally posted by KaiserAfini View Post
                                    They can bypass the penalty by using High Speech and spending Willpower.
                                    Spending Willpower is certainly helpful to turn the spell from very likely to fail into having a fighting chance when it's critical, but I should think the prospect of spending it repeatedly on spells in sequence is hardly trivial.

                                    Originally posted by KaiserAfini
                                    Since they are Acanthus, they can probably use a spell mixing elements of a Knowing of Time with Lucky Number to guess the most relevant moment and duration for the Postcognition to have the maximum benefit.
                                    Lucky Number is not an information granting spell though, it's a Ruling to help guide a randomised action into a form that could be useful to the subject.

                                    I wouldn't take using a Ruling for similar processes is totally off the table, but that it has to operate on a scope of random selection more narrow than just every possible point of your life. That a subject has to devise a reasonable number of suggested times to scry back to which could be chosen at random, and then Rule the randomness towards an outcome that is useful. And even then I would think the result would be messier than choosing a phone number; that's something objective and set in stone, whereas a useful choice between multiple options can be a more subjective assessment.

                                    For a Knowing to get a sense of which times are useful to look at... I'm thinking that's also something which needs to be cast backward upon the times to be assessed. Useful to get a quick answer on whether or not to spend hours looking at a period that could be a waste of time, but still something that entails repeated casting (with strained dice pools), spending Mana and having an idea of where to start looking.

                                    It'll be easier when you can cast Prophecy and get information on what selections will advance towards a goal or assess the value of a hypothetical course of action through the future rather than the past, but that goes beyond being the option of every given Acanthus.

                                    Originally posted by KaiserAfini

                                                            If they kill every Seer on principle, they might have a lot of problems with Wisdom unless they have an advanced Masque.

                                                            Keep in mind, a mage that does not look into things would not know that sometimes a new mage is coerced into servitude for noble reasons, such as obtaining a cure for a loved one. The Seers make sure they never pay off the debt.

                                                            Some had to convert to survive. A mirror freedom fighter found that using the patron of a Seer as a yantra was very effective against Ministers and a nice piece of poetic justice. Even when the Iron Door showed up in their Oneiros, they keep pushing it. Its close to opening and the Exarch has marked them as a potential chosen. So now they have been blackmailed into being a Seer, all the while other Seers are setting them up so the future Prelate never takes the mantle and the Pentacle probably see a deserter (the Arrows and Guardians may have them act as a double agent while being mindful of the potential liability). Now they balance their duties while searching for a way to excise the door from their soul.

                                                            There are some Seers that are like Mother Talzin, The Crones from Witcher 3 or the Wyrd Sisters from Gargoyles. Apparently neutral, agents of fate and destiny that don't get involved directly. They keep to their promises, but make it so deals twist on the person. They potentialize destinies, but set targets on a path that ultimately benefits their agenda and leaves the subject lost. They are civil, open to negotiations, but sagacious and far too convenient to get rid of. Ministers of the Prophet, Ruin or Chancellor might fit that mold.

                                                            So not all Seers are worth terminating immediately, the domino effect might cause other Pylons to target them or leak information about rivals in the Iron Pyramid. In some cases, negotiating neutrality or use of unique resources might pan out better. A skilled enough diplomat might even make some defect.

                                                            All of those might pose interesting dilemmas to challenge the character's approach. When added to the fact the police might see the effects of such an approach as the actions of a serial killer and that might mobilize Sleeper police to get involved (a Paradox hazard waiting to happen), things might get pretty hectic. The Seer was a key part of keeping a Bound sealed under the city ? Their vampire allies see this as an attack on their operations? The city father was sponsoring them to keep Abyssal spirits at bay ? Now things just got much worse.
