      I wish I could be in a large group that used it regularly, but its probably best for very small groups, or one-on-one sessions. I've played with a few people before, but they've all only played one-on-one so far, I've never gotten to play in an actual group.

      With that said... Astral has come up a lot, and has been very interesting. My player has always enjoyed his excursions into the Astral. Made such a big impact that when his character, who was working on a Life-based legacy or trying to find a way to join the orphans of proteus, met a Dreamspeaker? Decided to pursue the Dreamspeaker legacy so that his character could be in the astral at all times.

      But I have got to say. How can a game eventually not include a high-level-but-high-stakes visit to an Aeon? The only person I've played with who didn't love occassional visits to the Astral hated any other-world excursion--didn't like the Shadow, didn't like the Astral, didn't even like going into the Hedge or fleshing it out in Changeling.
      The others though very much enjoyed visiting the Aeons at least once, with one of my player-friends visiting the Aeons more than once (and accepting/suffering detrimental effects for doing so) just for the pleasure of being menaced by the likes of Dahhak, or the dangerous allure of Glorianna.

      Reasons to possibly include the astral might be--to learn a legacy through a soul stone, perhaps in a broken, falling apart onieros adventure; the setting has a strong astral presence like the 2E gamebooks sample setting in, what was it, Los Angeles or San-Fran? One of those.

      Or your setting might just hold its Consiliums in the Temenos because it is safer or easier in that specific town--probably advantageous if you include a strong presence of witch-finders in your story.

      And of course the Free-Councilers and Seers both looove tampering with the Astral so, story-hooks with that.

      But even as much as I love the Astral, if your games are safely anchored in conventional reality there's nothing wrong with that. It just meant your players had nothing distracting them I guess? If you have nothing major planned that uses the Astral then it would probably only come up for use in down-time, or when too many people can't make it but some people still want to play Mage for a little bit.

      I think the Astral sort of requires that using it gets a lot more personal with your players and their characters, but if that's something your troupe wants and can do, then the Astral is a great resource for getting into more personal details like character arcs and such. I definitely recommend it if it gets a good, worthy opportunity for usage!
