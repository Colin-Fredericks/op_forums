    10-14-2016, 05:05 PM

                      The mage is doing buffing anyway, and so is the changeling. The changeling's contract gives more dice for one action, but is harder to use. If the mage is going to be using her action for enhancing allies anyway, it makes sense to combine with the changeling to create an effect greater than either could achieve on their own, and it neatly deals with the problem of the mage having easier access to similar powers if they can make your powers easier. As for the composition of my group, my family is filled with RPG fans and I'm the one with the chronicles of darkness stuff, so I get to be the storyteller. My sister's boyfriend also likes RPGs and he hangs around the house often enough to be worth the effort of including, at least as an occasional group member.
                      Edit
                      One of the big things to consider here is that if you allow Mages to directly use the practice of Perfection on the powers of other supernaturals, are you going to be ok when they jump to the logical conclusion that the other practices (especially fraying, weakening them in an equal and opposite way of Perfecting) on them as well?
                      First of all, isn't there already a sidebar or something in the rulebook explicitly stating that the can use fraying as a counterspell for other supernatural powers? I seem to recall a note about dispelling werewolf gifts with a spirit 3 spell, but I could be wrong. second, the mage is being played by my mom, who has been playing RPGs since before I was born. I fully expect her (once she's had time to figure out the details of how arcana work) to do that, and I expect her to do it in such a way that it improves the game for everyone. She doesn't steal the spotlight from others constantly, which is why I felt confident letting her play the mage.

                          Given that answering "yes" implies that the same effects can be applied to Awakened spellcasting itself, take cues from the Yantra-boosters in Prime; don't add straight dice to power rolls outright, but feel free to give them 8- or 9-Again.                               I agree with Satchel, here. That's a good rule of thumb. Other sorts of relatively clean boosts could be things like "adding one to the number of powers allowed of a given sort" or, of course, straight attribute modifiers.

                              Directly enhancing the extant powers of others is far more of a power-creep risk than allowing them to interact and bolster individual's ability. 1e Contracts could be manipulated by Fate effects, so feel free to allow that, but I think that was limited to rewriting them or slipping their leash.

                              Grump, grouse, and/or gripe.
