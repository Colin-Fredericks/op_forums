                        Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
                        Only a few questions today, mostly opinions.
                        1. Would creating merits that require a legacy to gain be a good idea for a setting that places more emphasis on legacies?
                        1.5. Would alternate or secondary attainments be a better idea?
                        2. Are there any ways to get into contact with the lower depths in 2e, or summon something from a specific lower depth?
                        3. Would anyone like to see the singers in silence redone for 2e?
                        1: Merits can have such a wide range of effects that it’s really hard to answer this question. Sure, if you added a merit - something like Mystery Cult maybe - it could emphasize the prominence of legacies. Having an extraordinarily enticing unique merit for each legacy might be a little ambitious, though.
                        1.5: that can also be a way of emphasizing that legacies give you unique, badass powers.
                        1: ....But legacies have a whole lot of useful stuff already. It’s pretty well worth it for any character. In my experience, if a player doesn’t go for a legacy, it’s often because they’re still trying to figure out the system’s mechanic is general, and aren’t ready for another layer of complexity. That, or, legacies can feel like a super special personalization, and they may not feel like they know their character or the direction of the game well enough to commit to that.
                        Without knowing what your goals are for emphasizing legacies, I recommend focusing on the ‘fluff side’. Give every NPC a legacy, make them networks of mentors and allies, make each one a mystery. Have several legacies for each character that have a hook for their specialties. Emphasize that it’s the true way mages discover the Supernal, and that it’s their best way to push back against the Lie.

                        2: Most lower depths have *a* means of intrusion that can be used to summon or contact them, but these are entirely unique to each realm... because each realm is totally unique. There’s no formal or shared process, like summoning Supernals. The realms are not actually related to each other in any way; “lower depth” just means “we aren’t really sure, but it seems hungry.” Figuring out how to contact or summon from one is a Mystery - probably following up on an intrusion that already happened “naturally”.

                            Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
                            1. Would creating merits that require a legacy to gain be a good idea for a setting that places more emphasis on legacies?
                            I think it's reasonable for there to be some Merits that represent a bit of particular focus or insight provided by the Legacy that is in keeping with the structure of other Mage specific Merits. Not for every Legacy, but if it's fitting why not.

                            As far as "good idea" goes, I think such things would work best as benefits that only members of the Legacy would really want or benefit from. Not broadly applicable things that a player interested in optimization would be weighing and measuring.

                            Originally posted by TempleBuilder
                            1.5. Would alternate or secondary attainments be a better idea?
                            Here do you mean the powers called Legacy Attainments, or the Attainments that come from having Arcana levels?

                            I feel as though a magical thing a Legacy member would benefit from innately and permanently would be best modelled as one of their actual Attainment powers rather than anything else. It's perfectly fine for a character to develop the Legacy down an alternative path (although it still needs to be a variation on the core idea that they initiated into; an Eleventh Question mage might decide they want the Third Attainment to be something other than specialized Divination, but still focused on investigation to uncover mysteries).

                            Originally posted by TempleBuilder
                            2. Are there any ways to get into contact with the lower depths in 2e, or summon something from a specific lower depth?
                            Contacting the Lower Depths is described as being possible with the Irises called Scars, but the general rule is that the nature of those deprived realities breaks down all non-native entities who go into them. Either that or a human would be in drastic danger from the absence of something they need to remain alive.

                            Before anything else, I think that summoning things from a Lower Depth is going to encounter the limitation inherent to all such spells; if the targeted entity is not within sensory range, calling it must be cast sympathetically (and if not in the same realm of existence, might require additional Arcana to target that realm)*. How would you propose to form the level of association for even a weak sympathetic connection to a Lower Depths entity?

                            Lower Depths can be very different from one another, so some might have a form of reality cohesive enough to be targeted with certain Arcana and some do not. The dangers inherent to Scars and limits of analysing them probably means that most of the time when a mage encounters an Iris they conclude leads to a Lower Depth, it's hard to even know what's on the other side let alone figure out what Arcana, if any, are good for trying to do anything with it. If they do know that, they might be capable of issuing a general summons to something on the immediate other side of a Scar.

                            Even with a sympathetic connection and knowledge of compatible Arcana, I think it wouldn't be possible to summon a known entity that is currently residing in its home while you have no door to it. Summoning from the Shadow is different because it exists in a pervasive parallel to the material realm, separated "only" by the Gauntlet (of varying strength to Withstand the spell). A Lower Depth (and the Underworld, for that matter) might be seen as functionally having Gauntlets of infinite strength, impossible to cast spells through.

                            It's a bit easier in the case of things from Lower Depths that are active in the world already. If one regards the Strix as originating from a Lower Depth, then summoning them can be a matter of having Death magic that targets shadows or shadow beings which are Potent enough to overcome their Resistance. But again, doing that on anything other than a Strix within sensory range requires sympathetic magic. Although I think in their case they'd experience it less as a compelling call to another presence and more like the entity being forcefully dragged. I think commanding a strix would require a separate spell, and be a perilous proposition in any event. And the Strix might be among the more comprehensible Lower Depths entities.

                            Otherwise, there may be cases where a mage has learned of some Mystery, some procedure or ritual that keys into a quality of a Lower Depth or its inhabitants that would call upon them without use of the Arcana. If one learns of such methods, I'd say they are used at one's peril.

                            I would think that the overall attitude towards Lower Depths in the dedicated summoning lore of the Awakened is "it's a really bad idea to trying calling upon entities that you have a poor understanding of", and so generally discouraged.

                            * Summoning from the Astral differs from this, but that's a matter of the relationship it has with the rest of the world. The Anima Mundi is always inside of you.
