                What I believe about death in the real world has nothing to do with it. The game's stance on immortality is predicted on avoiding the worst parts of Ascension's setting and following through on Death being one of the foundations blocks of the setting's universe.

                If Death weren't an Arcanum, if we didn't have Moros in the game, I'd make "is immortal" a 1-dot Merit because it has no bearing on a game mechanical level. Hell, the Tremere get it as a side effect of what they do to themselves (the focus of their theme being what they did to themselves and their Hunger)

                I greatly enjoy games like Eclipse Phase where (as its strapline says) death is a disease that can be cured. But in Mage death is something the enlightened grow to accept.

                On that, the game happens to agree with my own real-world beliefs. It contradicts many others: In our world magic is not real, consciousness is nothing but a biochemical process and free will an illusion (most of our "decisions" are rationalised after the event by our brains.) While it's comforting to say "that's not him" in regard to people whose personalities change due to illness, it's an illusion. You are nothing more than a program running on a meat computer. When that meat breaks down, the program will end. You (anyone who reads this) are going to die. There is no afterlife, you will not "go" anywhere, you'll just stop. Get used to it.

                Awakening presents a world with Meaning behind everything. Our world has none. That's why it's compelling fiction.

Is a damaged copy of someone's personality enough of a person to care about their suffering? Asshole mages and some non-sin eater bound say no. The player character types say yes.

                            Joker Nice ninja post. Pretty much my thoughts, saved me a lot of time writing it. Thanks. As I mentioned earlier, what about mages using magic like Life or Fate and getting ahead of modern medicine by using the Arcana as, for example, gene editing tools, with Lasting effects? The changes would be purely biological, after all, and more of a matter of convenience in getting the technology a few years earlier than other people.

                            As for my take on immortality, to not be capable of dying means by definition to be a fundamental aspect of reality (pretty much equal to Ascending, there might be other similar forms of existence). Not aging is a wholly different matter, as I've also already mentioned.

                            For people like Arcane, who want to know my reasons for not wanting to die of old age: I may be an aspiring scientist (B.Sc in genetics so far) but in my heart I'm really a pioneer and an explorer. In this era, you can at most explore places like dark bottoms of oceans or maybe depths of rainforests, but that doesn't bring with it the thrill of finding a widespread advanced civilization or finding a whole different continent with different climate and exciting vistas. I'm sure you know what I mean. Humans could explore like this for most of our species' history. And we will be able to do this again, at least regarding the vistas and setting up new colonies, once we leave our planet, which will hopefully be in not too distant future.

                            So, here I am, someone who grew up with Jules Verne's books, encyclopaedias about the universe and sci-fi comics, living in the one era when I can't really fulfill my dreams. There has to be more to life than this. To want to extend life by supressing human aging was a completely natural conclusion of my dreams about the future to which I came at some point in my early teens. I considered the decision completely natural, an extension of all my hopes. Typical responses about "family and enjoying the life now" might be understandable, but after a while started to cause an allergic reaction, because those people who say this don't generally know much about human psychology to understand that what they enjoy may be uninteresting to others at best, or torturing at worst, due to drastically different personalities people tend to have. I'm happy they love their lives, but *I* could never enjoy that.

                            Hell, even the fear of death doesn't really play a role. Fearing death has no point, it cripples you. It's the hopes, and ambitions to achieve those hopes that drive me.

                            Now, in a mage game, consider how a character with at least a hint of my personality would react to an idea that a certain hope-crushing process is a "virtue" that is supposed to be "embraced", combined with certain information about cosmic tyrants wanting to rid humanity of all hope and dreams. There is no way they wouldn't connect the two concepts, especially as culling the herd before they get too many ideas about life would be an effective control tactic.

                                                    Originally posted by branford View Post
                                                    Not all changes are the same. The end of life transition is a fundamental supernal truth as expressed by Stygia. That's why fearing death is an expression of the oppression of the Exarchs.
                                                    Eh... fearing death is an expression of tyranny because it can be used to control people, or drive them to extremes. That has nothing to do with regular mages - when effective immortality is a simple spell, you can't even say that fear motivates it to any extent. Why NOT do it? Its not a fear of death, but simply having more time to study Mysteries and achieve goals in a manner that takes more time, and likely safer since you'll feel less rushed.

                                                    Fear of death... is more like the state of being a vampire or ghoul*. You are willing to do anything to be able to keep on living. You eat people. You murder people. Die so that I might live. And then, after time, you become a monster because you become bored. You start playing with people's lives because of you don't want to die, but you cam't feel alive either. You addict yourself to vitae, so that you can feel the rush and the power flowing through you, so you can feel. You're dead inside, but the body keeps on moving. That's what fear of death is like. For a mage? There's no need to go that far. You can very easily hold onto your morals, because there's nothing to compromise. There's no need to risk other's lives. There's no need to fear ennui, because mages are driven to uncover mysteries - that's their raison d'etre, and the world is full of mysteries to solve to the point a ten thousand year old magus would unlikely be bored. Granted, mages will still manipulate people, because that's how you gain mysteries and ascend and stuff, but that's no different than a non-immortal mage.

                                                    Also, ascending effectively makes you immortal, and that's a desired state. So, clearly, there's caveats to the whole immorality = bad thing.

                                                    Honestly, I'm left with the feeling that liches are trying to be set up as a potential antagonist, like ghost mages were in 1e, or that one really old mage that turned into a goetic from one of DaveB's actual plays. I mean, from a story perspective, they're a rich mine of plot hooks. Its a classic thing. "Super ancient mages tend to be trouble." That's actually pretty cool idea. I think the problem is more the presentation of these ancients alongside Banishers and liches and the Mad, rather than anything else. Its like people's problems with the initial presentation of Heroes, and now alchemists in Promethean. It makes sense that there would be a notable population of non-bad immortal mages, but these are an antagonist group to challenge the PCs, so they get portrated in an unfavorable light.
