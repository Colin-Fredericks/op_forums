                If anything, Mage is actually more interesting to me - because it can be as horror inducing as the situation calls for.

                It isn't trying to tell me what horror is. It lets me come to that conclusion on my own.

                I don't find Cthulhu particularly scary. Nor do I find vampires or werewolves to be scary.

                Scary, to me, is the stories that happen in-between. Something that resonates with me as disturbing or unsettling.


                I'm not scared when I run out of resources. I'm scared when I'm not sure I can handle something, or I've grossly misunderstood a situation. I'm scared when I won a moral battle and lost the moral war.


                I'm very particular about comedy, and hate to be told or convinced that something is funny and I should be laughing now. Comedy, like horror, should be organic.

                No amount of spooky skeletons and blood-sucking (or the threat of being annihilated by sunlight) can make me think vampire is scary.

                If anything, all that other stuff just makes me think of edgy-goth vibes.

                Jump-scares are cute and all, but real horror is the stuff you think about when you're alone. Real horror, to me, is the stuff that gets to you even when you're with other people.

                "You break your finger to cast a spell! There is blood and it hurts! Scary way to cast a spell, isn't it? Isn't it scary?! Are you unsettled? Have you been disturbed, or dare I say, frightened?!"

                vs.

                "Yeah, I checked him out. The spirit says it'll come out peacefully if we let it bang John Doe's wife one more time."
                "Sounds good to me."
                "Are you serious?"
                "I have shit to do, man. Nobody is getting hurt. Just let it fuck and fuck off."

                The second one is much more unsettling on several layers, to me.

                Adding an edgy or cryptic sacrifice to every spell would just make it super pretentious - like the game is saying magic is scary BECAUSE it is costly. When in reality, that isn't what I find scary about magic (or the supernatural) at all.

                                Originally posted by Professor Phobos View Post
                                It's not built or structured the way horror magic works.
                                Magic in Mage involves parceling a bit of the omnipresent symbolic cloud that is your power into discrete pieces and sticking them to places and things that then have reality contrive to rubber-band back to you. Some of the things you do with those agents endangers your ability to limit the scope of this rubber-banding unless they're one of the small number of spells that you've given more wiggle-room to (which themselves are more likely to go weird or wrong without additional preparation or oversight), and keeping each of those agents on-task in the long-term requires a little bit of your mystical focus or a big chunk of your personal will. Heightening your mystical insight gives you more specifically-supernatural alterations to your destiny that also serve as channels for better weakening your hold on your magic, which itself helps you heighten your mystical insight.

                                Mages are the only splat whose power structure is built around their actively using them all the time despite the long-term consequences of doing so, and the way Wisdom and Obsessions work means that that rapidly goes downhill if they cross the wrong lines on an impulse. "The rush of dragging down the heavens to do your bidding may blind you to the burning shell of your surroundings" is a horror best viewed in retrospect or from outside, yes, but "you got what you wanted to happen just then to happen and now everything else is dust and smoke" does not lose the dust and the smoke just because you got what you wanted to happen to happen.

                                Magic is an extension of a mage's identity — its inclination is to be, and without care, being precedes deliberation. The tension Mage deals with in its horror is between that all-important preparation that makes magic work most safely and effectively and the fact that there's very little to discourage you from trying over and over again in rapid succession at the start — carelessness is not only easy but encouraged in a lot of the mechanics that also incentivize stringent maintenance of your magical identity. The most basic use of Reach is saying "I need this to happen in the next thirty seconds, not an hour from now," and the most high-spec Yantra available to more than one spell is "this is something it makes sense for me to be able to do as Shadow-Name the Example-Mage." Mages stick bits of themselves everywhere they do magic, and they are themselves people to whom magic literally comes naturally — there's a reason the two archetypical ends of the scale are isolated hermitage and fast-track to self-destruction with extra collateral damage.

I invite you to do so.                                                     Nah, don't worry about that. It wouldn't be a mage argument on the internet if it didn't have someone being rude and condescending.

                                                    What made me realize I didn't care for Mage as part of the CofD (though it's not without its merits on its own, mind) is a passage in GURPS Horror, where Ken Hite describes how magic in horror stories is different from fantasy magic. I think it's a pretty good set of criteria that defines horror magic from fantasy magic. The principles, paraphrased, were as follows:

                                                    -Magic is serious. "Someone who trivially uses magic should be as scary as someone who trivially uses plutonium." Mage arguably has this already and in fact seems to be largely the response I've been getting. The rest of this bullet point is about the investment, cost, price of magical power. Mage is a bit light on this stuff for my tastes but, eh, it's a gameplay convenience. Point conceded.

                                                    -Magic is dangerous. This is definitely not enough I think; for powerful magic there should be a lot more risk of physical injury, or mutation, or madness, or being sucked into a vortex or possessed or whatever. Magic is pretty reliably safe in Mage unless you go looking for paradox. Yes, mages have adversaries that are quite dangerous, but that's an issue of relative power, not intrinsic horror.

                                                    -Magic is uncanny. "Even in the most overt full-magic campaign, mages shouldn't truly understand the powers they toy with." I've thought since Mage 1E we got far too much reliable information about the underlying metaphysics, cosmology, and secret history. I'd love to see more uncertainty about magic in the game; the same way Requiem 2nd introduced stuff like Revenants and additional lost clans, failed embraces, etc. Let's get broken watchtowers, lost paths, a fallen Order that exists only during certain celestial events, etc. Just go nuts. Mage: The Ascension was once described as reducing magic to something as interesting as VCR repair, which is a criticism I think could be levied at Awakening just as well. It's just too...explained. Ars Magica does a good job at having Hermetic Theory be really broad and powerful, but limited in ways that mysterious other magics were not. Stuff like that would be good for Awakening.

                                                    -Magic is scary. This is the big one. Magic in horror stories is Just Not Right; it has horrific spoor. Milk spoils. Candles flicker. Power surges. "If you aren't scared to use it to save your life, it isn't black enough for horror." Magic in Mage is arguably a desirable, empowering thing for humanity; heck, two of the factions (three, really) are all about that.

                                                    Mage magic feels like fantasy magic, not horror magic. Which is why I don't think it fits in a horror setting, because it's just not black enough magic to be That Old Black Magic.

                                                    Envision an alternative, darker Mage, where the obsessions are more consuming, the magic more perilous (say you cut the Supernal Realms and all magic is the engineered use of the Abyss to violate reality for your own goals). Does that lose the hubris and abuse of powers aspect? I don't think so.

                                                            Not addressing any specific point, but I think a lot of complaints re:horror grow out of players forgetting that Mages are the monsters. It's an easy thing to forget, since pound for pound they're still (to a bigger degree than any other splat except probably Hunter) human, but Mages aren't the guys who are (typically) menaced by the forces they wield, any more than a vampire is menaced by drinking blood. They menace others by doing things like accidentally wiping half of their family out of the timeline while insisting it was the right choice, or drive people insane by repeatedly exposing them to arcane powers to Awaken them while insisting it's for their own good, or who carelessly release spells that they forget to check on which make an entire city block now exist in a separate dimension five minutes in the future where the air makes you think every day is like Sunday.

                                                            Magic isn't usually scary to a mage in the way we usually think of scary when it comes to horror films and books. They usually see it as a holy, pure, important force. Even as everyone is screaming and their eyes are bleeding at the rock inscribed with the so-called "holy" symbol. That's it's horror: the things you are allowing to happen and the things you are becoming because of it.

                                                            Forcing Mages to use horrifying regents or to have them always risk turning into tentacled monsters (the first of which they can do if they want and the second they sort of already risk if they aren't careful with Paradox) is something you can do, if you feel it fits the sort of tone for the game you're looking to run, but as a "fix" to a perceived "problem" in the game it comes across as missing the point.
