        There is a pretty good argument that the Oneiros is not the same thing as the Soul. The Mummy perspective would place them both as parts of the Five-fold soul, so from what we know in Mage.

        1) replacing a Mage's soul with a sleeper's soul doesn't stop them doing magic or change their personality.

        2) removing the soul doesn't instantly remove their path/gnosis, so they are still connected to the Supernal.

        There remains a question about what would happen to anyone inside their Oneiros when the soul is removed.

        To me the most intuitive answer is that the Oneiros begins to shut down, its power source is gone, the driving force, but the personality doesn't change.

        Any native Goetia will stop gaining essence and slowly lose energy and eventually enter hibernation, unless a soul is restored - in a similar manner to the willpower/gnosis/wisdom draining away slowly.

        Any visitors to the Oneiros will be unharmed until it eventually collapses on itself, and they are either ejected, or soul-shocked as if their astral form had been destroyed (particularly in the case where the soul is removed and the character instantly gain the thrall condition, skipping the slow degredation - for example if the extra reach effects of Sever soul are used).

        The alternative is that the Oneiros just vanishes, but that doesn't seem as satisfying to roleplay.

A soul is a necessary component of that, whether it be because it takes a complete pattern to be so attuned, or its specific qualities are required to properly form and will imagos, or it's something like a lens to see through. But it is not, in and of itself, the sum total of one's Awakened being, such that its absence immediately prevents spellcasting, nor can its transfer carry Awakening, nor is it irreplaceable.

                        On the other hand, I think the soul does encompass the sum total of the Oneiros, such that the absence of it gives nothing to actually project into.

                                            Originally posted by orathaic View Post

                                            Oblations generating Mana is limited by mastery of the Legacy's Ruling Arcanum, and Mana capacity is limited by Gnosis, which goes down as an intermediate consequence of soul loss; in a strict sense there's nothing stopping Legacy Oblations from working while soulless.

                                                        Originally posted by orathaic View Post
                                                        I think an apt comparison can be made most clearly with Deviants and Changelings (and maybe vampires). Afaik, Deviants souls become shattered (though they are so varied this may be a simplification) and Changelings becomes twisted in a way which also alters their souls.
                                                        Shallow Graves describes breaking points as damaging and puncturing the metaphysical container for the human soul (putting holes in it that provide ways in for possessing entities and reduces the "pressure" that a soul can muster to drive out such beings through abjuration) in its subsection on Integrity in the larger section on souls in general; deviants lack the part of their spiritual anatomy that would correspond to a soul-container with Integrity in a way that likens trying to steal a transformed soul with grabbing a live wire. All deviants have at least a passable substitute for a soul, whose volatility is the reason they have a Stability track.

                                                        Changelings lose bits of their souls in the transition into and out of Arcadian servitude (crossing through a realm that exists on the boundary between conscious and unconscious experience) in a way that takes some of their memories with it in solidified metaphors and leaves them prone to evoking sudden bursts of emotion in those who perceive them, stitching those missing pieces back into their souls by taking them out of the Hedge. The Lost liches known as the Empty do not remove their souls — quite the opposite — but instead remove their hearts for safekeeping as part of their immortality, and Contracts (and the Wyrd more generally) work on sympathetic principles that heavily involve names and identity and reciprocity.

                                                        Mages develop Legacies by altering their Gnosis and magical identity, which in turn shapes the soul attached to it. Their Awakening already causes them to attract consistent magical phenomena to them and the things related to them (even if constrained to "just" things connected to their Shadow Name), and their magical identity affects the development of their Nimbus. Legacy Attainments are strictly metered in what they are capable of as non-Supernal manifestations of Supernal study. "This character tried to get more Legacy Attainments and it went sideways" is a category of Rapture because messing with the soul is tricky business.

                                                        Can you do some kind of soul shaping in a sleeper and make them something else? Form a line of proximi perhaps?
                                                        Depending on the specific instances, this could be the source of some Supernatural Merits, but broadly speaking Integrity is not quite as durable as Gnosis in this fashion — any given Sleeper of the right mindset might experience a lucky breaking point, fail the Watchtower's trials, and come out of the experience as a Sleepwalker, and some Proximus lineages are almost certainly the result of spontaneous phenomena rather than deliberate action by archmasters or Supernal beings, but things beyond the scope of the published material on the Subtle Arcana are deep lore about the soul that would be developed specifically for a given table's game.
