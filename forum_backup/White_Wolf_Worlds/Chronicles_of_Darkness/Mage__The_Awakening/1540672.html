    Fault Assimilation - A new system involving Rapt Faults and the Oneiros

    08-18-2024, 08:29 PM

    Fault Assimilation

    Mad Ramblings and Musings for Background

    The Rapt have always been my favorite antagonists in Mage the Awakening, likely all of Chronicles of Darkness. I've really been a fan of the fact that these guys aren't necessarily evil, instead they are broken, but in being broken they gain something more in the forms of faults, savants, and in different cases another ruling arcanum, allowing some Rapt to have four ruling arcana. Super cool concept that I love spending time thinking about.

    I've also been a big fan of the oneiros as a concept, filled with all the little parts that make a character who they are. I love thinking about what the oneiroses of different characters would be like, from environment to inhabitants. I even think about my own oneiros and what sorts of things make up the person that is me.

    Something that I thought was an interesting idea from very early on is the idea of stealing goetia from the minds of other mages and binding them in your own oneiros, effectively taking parts from others and sticking them into yourself. Eventually, of course, this evolved into the idea of taking the goetic faults of Rapt mages and drawing them into yourself. For me this is about a few things;
    • Firstly, the Rapt have touched the Supernal in a way that most mages never will, and it has altered their magic in a meaningful way, but the change also extends to their oneiros, their virtue is replaced by their new fault and that fault is said to defend the oneiros of the Rapt mage.
    • Secondly, I like to imagine that the state of Rapture allows those who have lost all their wisdom certain insights into the nature of magic that other mages can't access. Granted, this is likely only directed at their fault, the new thing that drives them, but the knowledge and insight gained must be on a level those non-Rapt mages obsessed with similar things must covet.
    • The Rapt are dangerous, they leak magic wherever they go, cannot control their spells, almost automatically release paradox that can cause problems for an entire year, are no longer connected to humanity’s soul, and are addicted to magic on a level that no normal mage will experience, and this speaks nothing of their greater Tulpa, and can be brought into the fallen world to further their fault.
    • The Rapt 's condition can never be cured, only managed. Because of this, linked with the danger they represent, it seems likely to me that both the Seers and the Pentacle end up destroying the majority of Rapt they come across. This is very sad to me, because all of the insights the state of Rapture affords will be lost with them. Even if their grimoires are recovered it would be like reading about someone else's experience with the divine, interesting, but not very useful in reproducing it or internalizing the lessons learned.

    All of this is what has led me to thinking about and developing this system. This is for those mages in the Mage the Awakening universe who agree with me, that when the Rapt dies something is lost, maybe forever. Originally I thought of this as a Mysterium endeavor, not wanting those parts of Corpus of Magic that have been granted to the Rapt lost forever seems unacceptable to the mystagogues who are willing to take in the goetic faults of their fellow mage. As I thought more on it though it slowly became something I see Talons of the Adamantine Arrow doing, taking on the most broken parts of someone else as a sort of struggle they must undergo on behalf of their now fallen awakened, creating conflict within their souls by assimilating the most broken parts of other mages into their oneiros. I've even thought about Guardians of the Veil who take the faults and change them into masques. Whatever the case, these mages understand that the Rapt are dangerous, but also understand that what they know and can do is valuable, and do not want to lose it.

    I see all the mages who partake in Fault Assimilation as Mastigos, after all this is certainly a gauntlet they must go through, in fact that they drop themselves into in purpose, in order to house the faults in their own personalities, but this is certainly biased on my part, and any mage of any path or order could find value and meaning in this process.

    The process is simple enough I believe; use a collection of Mind spells to establish a connection between your own oneiros and that of the Rapt and then summon their fault through that connection from its native world into yours, or, summon the goetia into the physical world and then into yourself. Whatever will get the fault into your oneiros, making it a part of you. While this process is simple, the end results need to be interesting enough that players will want to do it, dangerous enough so that the majority of mages don't do it, and powerful enough to make that danger worth it. I believe I have found a combination of these that works well, but it does make an assumption and requires a different system, Gnostic Attainments. Both are described next.

    The fault is arguably the most broken and magic addicted part of the Rapt’s oneiros and this system involves taking that part of someone else and putting it into yourself, similar to if a player had their character take out the parts of someone who was addicted to meth and then placed those parts into themself. It's very dangerous. However, (this is the part that I am assuming true to allow for this system to work, your storyteller might not allow it as this is not confirmed to be the case anywhere that I've seen in cannon) it is also the part of the mage that is most directly connected to the Supernal. I imagine it is similar to the black doors from Signs and Sorcery that can cause a mage to become a prelate, they are a direct connection to Supernal power.

    Gnostic Attainments

    I did not come up with the idea of Gnostic Attainments myself. Two other members of the Mage the Awakening fan discord developed ideas, Gnostic Attainments and Gnostic Merits, that I drew on when coming up with how I was gonna handle these sorts of things.

    "Akira is a homebrewer and amateur ttrpg dev on Magecord. He came up with the idea of including mechanics for the sort of weird esoteric abilities/powers Mages (and similar beings) could gain from the proper insight/changes. Think of them as similar Chaos Mutations, but they don't suck to have. If you want to model an ability/persistent magical condition that violates normal game mechanics (even spellcasting) and your chronicle is less grounded or more epic/cosmic than how DaveB's vision depicts the setting, Gnostic Attainments are your friend.

    Akira is interested in making a more comprehensive post about Gnostic Attainments here later, but his schedule is very busy rn”

    - Akira when I asked him how to describe his idea here.

    “Gnostic merits were created to encourage Mages to go out and have weird experiences, and create play-options that weren’t immediately inserted into the general soup of ‘anyone with the right Arcana could have this trick’.”

    - Elementa when asked how I should describe Gnostic Merits here.

    For me, I wanted to include a mechanism by which I could demonstrate the weird and arcane after effects of certain types of magic that mages could get up to, without having my players pay for results, so I ended up using something of a mix of these two ideas, drawing on the mechanical inspiration from Akira, and mixing it with the thematic ideas Elementa described. So Gnostic Attainments in my chronicles tend to be the result of some strange experience that a mage might have, where arcane experience doesn't feel like enough of a reward that results in the mage having something that is more like a power than spell. Fault Assimilation relies on this system.

    The System

    So you've done it. You discovered a Rapt mage, learned what their fault was, and decided that the mage needed to go but that the magical insights they had discovered were something that needed to remain after the death of the Rapt. You've absorbed the goetia and have undergone your first Fault Assimilation. Congratulations! You are now in the journey towards wisdom degeneration yourself, but at least you get some cool magic to go along with it!

    The first thing that happens when a mage engages in Fault Assimilation is they risk wisdom degeneration against Understanding. You have just taken the most broken part of another mage and put it into your oneiros, making it a part of you, this is similar to an act of self mutilation. Take the arcane beat that comes along with that and roll the dice to see if you learn anything about how to make good choices when it comes to magic.

    Upon completing Fault Assimilation your character now has a Gnostic Attainment that reflects what the Fault represents, however, the nature of this attainment is not intuitive to your mage. Similar to how a character who has the vice “Prideful,” and therefore a pride goetia yet still doesn't necessarily understand their own pride, your mage must investigate their new Fault in order to understand what magic they can tap into with it. This can be done in at least two ways. Firstly, the attainment is an opacity 3 mystery of whichever arcanum was associated with the Rapt’s Fault that can be revealed by scrutinizing the goetia of the Fault in order to understand the true nature of the Fault’s relationship with the Supernal, and as a side effect of that knowledge, your character understands how to use their new Gnostic Attainment. Another way would be to simply confront and defeat the goetia in a symbolically appropriate way. Just as the prideful mage may have to find a way to speak directly to the pride goetia in order to understand their pride, your mage can learn about the nature of the fault by speaking to the goetia, this is purely information based. If your mage has assimilated the Fault “Devour Souls,” the mage does not intuitively understand the mystical information that the Rapt had learned before Fault Assimilation happened, they must go and speak to the goetic Fault if they wish to understand what the Rapt knew.

    So, you've assimilated the Fault and you've taken the time to understand its Supernal connection and can now use your attainment, but what does that actually look like? To activate your new Gnostic Attainment simply spend a mana to tap into the Supernal connection the Fault has, this activates the attainment while also driving the goetia into a frenzy in which they attempt to seize control of your soul. At the end of the scene make a wisdom roll for every Gnostic Attainment you've gained from Fault Assimilation. A successful roll means your mage has managed to contain that Fault within themselves, subduing it for now. A failed roll means that the Fault has gained influence over you and you gain the “Rapt Emulation” arcana condition below.

    Rapt Emulation (Arcane)

    Your character has undergone the process of Fault Assimilation and then attempted to draw on the stolen power of the Rapt, leading to the goetic Fault to wrestle power away from your daimon and suppress your virtue(s). Your mage is now existing in a state that is similar, although not quite as broken, as the Rapt they stole from. Replace your virtue with the Fault, you now regain all your willpower when fulfilling this Fault. You gain arcane beats from making progress towards this fault as if you had made progress towards an obsession, including gaining a mana for doing so, but do not gain these effects from your obsession for as long as this condition exists.
    You can have multiple instances of this condition at once, but only one per assimilated Fault. Each one becomes your virtue and you can arcane beats and mana whenever making progress towards any of them.
    Resolution: Risk wisdom degeneration from an action taken in pursuit of the Fault.

    Thinking about the Gnostic Attainments

    Fault Assimilation is a very dangerous game to play, assimilating the most broken parts of a Rapt’s soul puts you at risk of falling towards Rapture yourself, but for the obsessed mage, the knowledge, insight, and power might be worth the risk. So what does this power look like exactly? Really the Gnostic Attainments gained from Fault Assimilation can work however the Storyteller and the player interested in Fault Assimilation decide they should, but I'll provide guidelines on how I'd do them in my game and then provide some examples below.

    The first thing is that all Gnostic Attainments should do is be linked to the mage's gnosis, scaling with it in some way. This is due to the fact that the attainment is powered by the Fault connection to the Supernal, so the stronger connection the mage has, the more use they can get out of their attainments. Be creative in this.

    The second thing is to make sure the attainment makes sense both in context of the game and the nature of the Rapt the Fault has been taken from. This can mean that the attainment grants an ability associated with the arcana, but not something that is a spell, or it can mean granting other sorts of bonuses associated with the theme of the Fault in other ways. Be creative in this.

    Thirdly, they should be appealing in some way to the player, they should be useful enough that it is worth risking gaining the Rapt Emulation condition. This of course does not mean they should be something the mage wants to use in every situation, but you want your player to weigh the risks against the benefits, so I encourage you to come up with fun and strange ideas, along with practical ideas. Remember, once a mage has assimilated more than one Fault, the use of the Gnostic Attainments associated with this system risks every Fault driving your character towards Rapture. A simple, practical power can be just as useful in terms of making your player have to think about this as a strange one. Be creative in this.

      Example Gnostic Attainments

      A note before diving into these, I haven’t used this system in game yet, so these are not a set of examples of what I’ve done, instead they are a list of examples that I think might work, if I ever get to use this in a game I’ll come back here and give an update on how it went and also share the examples of the Gnostic Attainments used. I didn't include these in the main post because, well, I don't know what they'll actually look like after playtesting and want them kept seperate for that reason.

      Falcon’s Fault | Gnostic Attainment

      Falcon was a member of the Adamantine Arrow obsessed with the use of long range firearms, serving as a marksman for his caucus before he fell to Rapture. Now his Fault has been assimilated by another mage, granting them the knowledge and accuracy that Falcon had honed.
      Spend a mana to activate this attainment. For the remainder of the scene your character no longer suffers the untrained penalty for firearms and adds half of their Gnosis (rounded up) to their firearms skill. This can take them above their normal limit. (You could also do this with any other skill as appropriate)

      Vice’s Fault | Gnostic Attainment

      Vice, a member of the Bene Ashmadai, failed to learn temperance when it came to her demons, and she fell into Rapture. Now her fault has been assimilated by another mage, allowing them to summon their own demons in a moment’s notice.
      Spend a mana to activate this attainment. Your character can summon a number of Goetia from their Oneiros equal to half their Gnosis (rounded up), regardless of if they could normally access the astral. These goetia are manifested and remain until the end of the scene. Should one be destroyed, it returns to the mage’s oneiros until it has reformed.

      Hemorrhage’s Fault | Gnosis Attainment

      Hemorrhage was a mage who studied the Kindred, but when his Wisdom broke he took on the life of one, instead of just someone who studied. Now the fault has been assimilated by another mage, granting them powers similar to the Kindred.
      Spend a mana to activate this attainment. For the remainder of the scene your mage gains the ability to drink a subject's blood and convert that blood into Vitae, for a total number of Vitae equal to half their Gnosis (rounded up). The Vitae has to be stored in the same boxes as your mana, and therefore the mana box must be empty. This vitae remains even after the scene ends, but without reactivating this ability the only way to remove the vitae is either by using the Death arcanum or by taking a point of lethal damage per point of vitae you wish to rid yourself of. You can use this vitae to form blood bonds with others.
      While this attainment is active, you may consume the vitae to gain the benefits of the following disciplines for one turn; Clerity, Resilience, Vigor, or to heal 2 points of lethal damage.
