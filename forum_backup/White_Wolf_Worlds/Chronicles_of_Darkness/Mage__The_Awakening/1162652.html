    Two New Arcana: Schism and Void

    11-26-2017, 03:00 AM
    I'm working on a homebrew setting with a heavy emphasis on insanity and corruption (Lovecraftian influences, naturally), and for various reasons I've decided on supplementing the existing ten Arcana with two additional ones. (The Qlippothic Spheres of Ascension's Nephandi were an inspiration here, as well as the fact that both Paradox and insanity are going to be playing big roles in the setting).

    EDIT: I've started a Google Doc where I've begun listing out ideas for effects, details, attainments, etc. for these new Arcana. (From the responses I've been getting, it's very clear that I was a bit premature in posting this, as the suitability of these additions depends heavily on the context of the setting.) Anywho, you can check out the details here: The Eye of God: Aberrant Spheres

    Schism, the Eleventh Arcana
    Purview: madness, contradiction, randomness, chaos, Paradox, Paradox spirits, Paradox realms, hexes
    • At its heart, Schism is the Arcana of madness---both mental madness, and the madness of reality itself. In addition to creating and propagating chaos, it also has some command over the very idea of contradiction itself, potentially finding it easier to make things happen the more impossible they are. Schism magic will be very counter-intuitive and odd, violating rules of sympathy and causality in unpredictable ways.
    • Fate is being refocused on its Supernal Ideal, in that its purview is to reduce randomness, to control fate, to improve---blessings and not hexes, in other words, the latter of which have moved to Schism.
    • Mind has lost the ability to cause undirected insanity with much the same justification. Mind can cause someone to hallucinate, but only something specific. Schism can cause someone to hallucinate, but only as a side effect from inflicting them with insanity.
    • In line with Call of Cthulhu's approach to magic, Paradox is going to be much more common in this setting (though balanced with less-severe effects). Schism will allow mages to affect Paradox directly, including shielding themselves from it in much the way Marauders protected themselves from Paradox in Ascension (i.e. when they caused Paradox, the backlash bounced off of them to hit other people in the vicinity)

    Void, the Twelfth Arcana
    Purview: the Abyss, Abyssal spirits, the Lie, Quiescence, nothingness, annihilation, dispelling, absence, endings
    • Void is the Arcana of absolute annihilation. Its effects are a hot knife through butter to the fabric of Reality, lancing out people, places, things and thoughts with brutal efficacy. Kill someone with Void, and you can't go back in time to save them: Their annihilation transcends time, as their soul, their past, their future, and their destiny are all excised from Reality. Void also has in its purview the Lie itself, giving it effects that can cause or manipulate Quiescence, as well as command over the Abyss and the denizens therein.
    • Death loses "strict annihilation"-style effects from its purview, though it retains destructive or decay-based effects.
    • Prime loses the ability to dispel magic to Void, but retains its ability to counterspell, its ability to temporarily suppress magic, and its ability to analyze effects (perhaps to find other ways around them).
    • Spirit and Mind both lose any ability they had to influence Gulmoth and Acamoth, respectively: Abyssal spirits are now the domain of Void.

    Any thoughts, ideas jump into your head after reading the above? I know adding two new Arcana is quite a marked change to the system, but I do think they manage to carve out unique niches for themselves that don't step on the toes of the other Arcana too severely.

    Let me know what you think, and thanks in advance for any insights you can offer!

      Sorry if this isn't as helpful as you might want:

      But my first reaction is that this is self defeating. It's not that you can't have 12 Arcana instead of 10. The number is exceedingly arbitrary. But this basically creates 10 "neutral" Arcana and two "evil" Arcana. People are only going to invest in Schism and Void if they're either already bad people, or because you've locked off effect into them and players have to buy into them for those effects rather than because the darker powers they offer are legit tempting to dip into.

      Instead of emphasizing things, this sort of effort frequently does the opposite in play because players will just avoid it. If Fate has boons, and Schism has hexes, a player that wants to play the "good guy," just avoids Schism. In the RAW, you can't buy the ability to bless people with boons without buying the ability to curse people with hexes at the same time. It's much easier to get people to use morally-neutral powers in bad ways than it is to get them to invest in morally-bad powers.

      There's also bits that just feel off. Like Awakening mages don't need to buy a power to deflect a Paradox off of themselves. They can all already choose to not contain the Paradox.

              Hmm, I suppose I'd need to go into a lot more depth in describing the setting to explain why I sought to create two "darker" Arcana in the first place, but that's a bit outside the scope... suffice it to say that there is a great deal in the purviews of those Arcana that will make them important and attractive despite their darkness (particularly the heavy emphasis on Paradox that the setting will entail, the proximity of the Abyss and the central role of madness). There are major changes to Paradox, too, such that containing/not containing is a choice with a great deal more teeth to it; mages in this setting will very much appreciate a method to rid themselves of, or protect themselves from, Paradox... and therein will lie the tension of how far to stray in these directions.

              I suppose, to put it short: The setting is designed to force the players to constantly judge the degree to which they're willing to corrupt themselves for the benefit of their higher ideals ("would you sell your soul to save the world?"-type stuff). These two Arcana are very much intended to be dark and dangerous, and you're absolutely right that in a conventional game of Mage they'd suffer from every one of the problems you outline.

              Once I have a better handle on the other aspects of the setting, I might return here and put these Arcana into a bit better context than I initially did.

                      I would also suggest that making insanity and paradox into Arcana does not highlight them as threats to the awakened, or really a matter of choosing to corrupt oneself for the greater good. The range and degree of power that Arcana offer instead give total power to the mages: with 2 dots, they can literally make people immune to those purviews. If you want to make something a threat, don't give players nigh limitless power over it.

                      For perspective, think about how Death mages deal with ghost stories. While there are mysteries and dangers and unforeseen consequences to acting like you're a Master of Death, a ghost will never be scary. And there's not really any corruption or downside to studying Death to deal with ghosts. If a player's goal is "I don't want ghosts to bother me", they can shield themselves, ward their flat, and become invisible to ghosts -
                      and can drop or reapply those spells when the plot demands it almost without consequence. Maybe that's the mood you're going for, but that's not what I think of as 'Lovecraftian'.

                      Instead, I suggest digging in to Left Handed Path and Imperial Mysteries (one of the factions wants to save the world with the Abyss). These books offered a lot of ways to play with these concepts, tempt players with some degree of control, but keeping it an unpredictable and dangerous force that could taint and consume them.

                          Originally posted by Seraph Kitty View Post

                          That's a good thing to keep in mind, thank you. I do intend to make Void and Schism dangerous and unpredictable---distinct in some ways to the standardized operations of the other Arcana (for example, a Schism Shielding spell against Paradox would not simply protect you from Paradox, but would bounce the Paradox onto random people in your vicinity---much like Marauders from Ascension). Schism will be unpredictable, Void will be overreaching and prone to backfiring.

                          Then there's the central mechanic/theme of the game: The player characters are the only mages in the world, and their powers have been granted to them by a mysterious object that claims to be the Eye of God. Gnosis is thus replaced with Communion, which is a measurement of how far the characters have allied themselves with the Eye's power---and, given its uncertain motives, this is not without its risks. So really the entire structure of the setting is being upended, which made me realize immediately my mistake in presenting these Arcana as if they could be slotted into a conventional Mage game, without providing the context of how different the setting I'm contemplating will be.

                                      Even with such distinction, maybe the new spheres are not the best mechanics for what you're doing.

                                      Taking Madness out of the other spheres, you're saying that Matter, Spirit or Life can't go Mad without adding a new Arcanum. An Arcanum not only adds an aspect to the world, it sustains such aspect by itself. Well, at least as Magic goes. What a Master in Madness can actually affect without so much on other Arcana?

                                      If you make them into something mechanically extraneous to the Arcana, it will be easier to work them into other magic. Maybe a Merit for each with stringent drawbacks to compensate the intense power (as you already thought to do), and limited in dots to, lets say, Gnosis -1. You could work all the power and concepts in those Merits, and either make them as something that encompass all Magic or keep with them as Arcana for all in-setting effects, but mechanically it would lessen the weight on the rules of other Arcana and make it easier for the characters to dabble in them without lacking on other powers.

                                      You can make those Merits also into requisites to make some effects with other Arcana, or it could be more effective to do with them.

                                                  They are conceptually Arcana, and that is all good. Their description went very well. Also I can comprehend the issues with the new mechanics. It can put too much power and in a very wrong way in the hands of other Arcana, specially Mind.

                                                  But mechanically, they aren't working very well as Arcana. First they start already with two distinct rules that reduce their power. Second they work mostly doing something other Arcana already do, with or without taking this effect from them. Schism is all about stepping in the toes of other Arcana just to make "paradoxal stuff". If it is too much under the control of the player, it becomes an all-powers Arcanum that can excuse itself on paradox or Paradox to make anything. If it isn't, then it is just a wild card that the Mage can't properly use. Void is the very concept of Unmaking practice, so it is hard to think of any use for it that isn't already a destructive effect of other spheres. I actually had far more the impression of Disciplines than Arcana reading them.

Schism could make the effects described and many others easier, maybe in the same vein, or just be a requisite for achieving such, though I would stick to the same mechanics for Void.
