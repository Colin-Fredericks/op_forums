    Limits of Supernal magic

    02-11-2026, 08:15 AM
    Someone was asking on Reddit about the limitations of Mages.

    Which got me thinking what a mage can't do - without Archmastery/Ascension and ignoring cross splatt limitations (like sure, you can't mess with a Vampire's Blood Potency) what can't a Mage do?

    There is the obvious, creating a soul with magic (you need to create a new human if you want a soul...), time travel back to before your own existence (birth?), entering the Supernal (safely) is creating a Lustrum.

    Bringing back the dead seems reasonable doable, so doesn't belong on this list (Death can do it with an indefinite spell, Time can rewrite the actual past.... Though going back to the moment of death and faking that death with Death magic then Temporal Stuttering the corpse into the present is a lot of work to minimise changes to the time stream.... Totally worth it for Lasting effects...).

    There is some questions on preventing Dissonance? Prime can make sleepers into sleepwalkers for a duration, but it is unclear whether this can prevent spell effects being eroded by dissonance or only delay it.

    Messing with Rank 6 entities and above is limited (autolosing CoW). So add killing gods to the list.

    And I am ignoring Imperial Practices, so sure as Mage can't create a self-altering spell. But they can cast multiple different spells over time which is Mage altering spells... or the Practice of Entities - sure you can't remove a Vampire's fear of sunlight (or is that altering the bane for all vampires?) but you can cast a shielding spell on one vampires, protecting them from sunlight (right?).

    Affecting an area larger than a campus/small neighbourhood is a hard limit, but you can just cast multiple spells.

    What am I forgetting? Small things like healing resistant damage (though you can increase the natural healing rate of the body, and I think Word of God allows this affect resistant damage)...

          Not traveling before your own birth is only a hard limit for Time alone, because you don't have a physical presence there. You could use conjunctional Mind to possess someone that lived at the time, make a body to possess with Life and so on. Its just a Bad Idea for mages. Archmages use an Imperial Practice, which is essentially a smart spell that alters how history puts itself back together to mostly preserve your original intent. But the Unmaking of Time mages use unravels the whole thing, adds your changes and then history puts itself back together by the path of least resistance, and the farther back you go, the more unintended consequences cascade.

          Some other hard limits are that you cannot physically enter the Astral, cannot destroy Artifacts, cannot create Eidoforms by yourself and cannot create rotes through pure spellcasting.

                                                  Time Magic is quite possibly the most dangerous of all the Arcana to use in any given situation. You aren't just changing one minor event in the past, you're also changing every event that comes after it. That's the premise of the butterfly effect, or chaos theory, where even the tiniest action can have far reaching consequences down the line.

                                                  Using Time to accelerate the healing process is certainly doable, though I would rule that doing so shortens your life span every time you use it.
