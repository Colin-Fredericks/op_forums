    Originally posted by KaiserAfini View Post
    Another puzzling example, what happens if a mage transforms into blood mist, to move unimpeded in gaseous form, and is then consumed by a vampire. Is the vitae nourishing ? What happens to their mana ? What happens after it is processed and incorporated into its pattern ? Do they become two wills in one body ? Can the vampire bleed him from his system ? Does the merge create an automatic restoration of certain advantages of the living so long as the last point of "Vitae" is not used up ? Can the mage become bigger if he reforms after stealing more vitae than he originally was ?
    I think all of those actually can be answered.
    • Vampire's don't get Vitae from blood; they get it through blood. Unless the mage designed the spell that the blood mist would be the mage's own blood, the vampire would gain no Vitae from drinking it. (And the vast majority of vampires wouldn't be able to drink it in the first place.)
    • If the vampire could drink the mage and process them into Vitae, the mage would continually take damage during the feeding process because dealing damage is just as important to feeding as the medium being blood. If fully consumed, the mage would definitely die.
    • I don't recall if it has been included in any supplements yet, but Dave Brookshaw has stated that mage Vitae should cause hallucinations (similar to Mage Sight) in vampires, and I think it's because of the Mana. Otherwise the Mana would just do what it usually does when not in a proper container; dissipate.
    • If the mage dies, their will isn't there to do anything. If the mage is ingested without harm, there's no meaningful interaction to merge their wills.
    • Vampires can bleed in two ways: Willingly expressing Vitae, and involuntarily bleeding according to natural processes when using Blush of Health. The vampire could definitely bleed the remains of the mage if processed into Vitae (though there are no general rules for "tagged" Vitae, afaik, so they might not be able to choose which Vitae to bleed). Ingested blood doesn't naturally appear at wounds (unless, say, the stomach was pierced).
    • There are no advantages gained from drinking blood from non-humans in second edition.
    • The mage can never become larger by the spell ending. Only the blood that the mage transformed into would change back. If Vitae was mixed up in the mist of blood, then that Vitae would remain where it was when the spell ends (which is quite possibly in the space the mage's old body now occupies.
    With a huge caveat for the fact that anything can happen if the ST thinks it would be more interesting and fun than what I just described.

                            Originally posted by Neos01 View Post

                            Unless you cast both with a combination spell

                            I don't think a Combined Spell will accomplish that. The Supernal Veil portion of the Combined Spell would have no subject, as the spell you want to Veil does not exist until the Combined Spell is cast. This is the reason that Hung Spell is itself not formulated as a Combined Spell.

                            EDIT: I think this comes down to how the ST resolves the ambiguity in how PMS works and multiple interpretations are valid. If the ST rules that the mage triggers PMS when casting a spell, Veiling the mage ahead of time prevents the PMS ping. If the ST rules that the spell triggers PMS when it's cast, then Supernal Veil doesn't help. If the ST rules that the subject triggers PMS when effected by a spell, then Veiling the subject prevents the PMS ping. I don't think the rules about PMS are specific enough to say which is the correct interpretation conclusively.

                            Like, here's the text from Signs of Sorcery:

                            As described on p. 90 of Mage, Peripheral Mage Sight automatically detects the presence of any active, unconcealed supernatural effects: the casting of a spell, activation of a vampire’s Discipline...
                            Since a "casting" or an "activation" are not things a Veiling spell can target, it's up to the ST exactly what needs to be Veiled to prevent a PMS ping. I personally rule that it's the magical effect itself since Supernal Veiling specifically mentions that it can target spells.

                            EDIT 2: Eh, maybe this bit of text lends credence to the idea that it's the spell itself that needs to be veiled:

                            Likewise, if a ghost in the same room uses a Numen, the mage knows that something supernatural happened, but without a spell or Attainment to let her see things in Twilight, the effect would seem to originate from empty space.

                            Substitute "Veiled mage casting a spell" for "ghost in Twilight using a Numen" and it seems like the intention is that the spell will still ping PMS even if the mage is Veiled.

                                        Originally posted by Newes View Post
                                        How does a materialized spirit or ghost appear if examined either physically or by Life magic. Does a materialized cat spirit that looks like a cat also feel like a cat and read as a cat to life magic, or does it feel like a bag of goo and read strangely / not register to Life magic? What about supernal creatures (who to my understanding are solid by default in the phenomenal world)?
                                        Spirits have stylised appearances related to their natures. A cat spirit will have a lot of cat traits, but will generally not be confused for an ordinary cat. How it feels, sounds, smells, etc, will also be related to its cat nature (with the exception of the fact that it can talk). Being materialised does not change how it appears to Life Sight. It still has the same body as it did when in a Twilight state as well as when in the Shadow.

                                        Originally posted by Newes View Post
                                        Do Supernal entities have numina, or is their power bound up in their influence arcana?
                                        According to the table on page 254 only ephemeral entities get Numina. So no. Also, they straight up have Arcana instead of Influences, rather than Influences that map to the Arcana.

                                                It helps to think about ephemera as a substance (which it happens to be in the setting). That's why all ephemeral beings share so many similarities, most notably being in Twilight when in the material world.
