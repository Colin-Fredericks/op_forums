                                            Originally posted by Signs of Sorcery, Introduction, p14-15, "Advanced Supernal Cosmology"
                                            Mages often speak of one Supernal World and five Supernal Realms defined by Path. Others of five Worlds and one Realm. In truth, there’s as many Worlds as mages and only one Realm, but both can be divided into five parts. The Supernal Realm is all the symbols of everything that can exist. Each of the five Paths reveals a large set of those symbols, which are then experienced by a mage looking into the Supernal World with her Sight and viewing phenomena they define. It’s easy to think of Pandemonium as separate to the Aether, Arcadia, Primal Wild, and Stygia based on the experiences of mages in the Fallen World, but there’s absolutely no evidence to suggest that the Supernal Realm is so subdivided, and lots of implication from archmasters and Exarchal sources that it isn’t. Those sources instead refer to individual symbolic landscapes classed by barely understood criteria: Dhatus, Exarchates, Cintamani, and other, rarer terms. The best known, thanks to the Corpus Mysteriorum using it, is Lustrum, defined in its pages as the translation of a single Supernal symbol into a phenomenon a Fallen being can interact with. Awakened theoreticians describe Lustrums as the reverse of more familiar things like Shadow Names and Yantras — while the Awakened spend great effort putting themselves and their work in Supernal terms, a Lustrum is the Supernal Realm putting on a mask of being a place, thing, or being. The only ones most mages will ever encounter are their Watchtowers, during their Awakenings. Outside of bizarre Mysteries involving deep Emanation Realms, mages only glimpse other Lustrums in Awakenings gone terribly wrong or in the actions of extremely powerful Supernal Entities.
                                            Lustrums first showed up under that name in Imperial Mysteries, as things archmasters needed to impose on the Supernal through an act of will to navigate it for any length of time and things that Supernal Gods could create as shelter for guests, should they feel the inclination; being in the Supernal without the protection of a Lustrum will cause it to annihilate and/or absorb you.

                                                The Lustrum is like a filter. In the Supernal, if you cannot find yourself amidst the symbols of everything, then you dissolve into everything. Think of it as a more extreme version of the Ecstatic Winds. Its also the reason that while Emanations can have direct entrances to the Supernal at their very depths, no one tries to use them, because regular mages have no clue how to survive that.

                                                Normally you require at least an archmage candidate who is starting to grok the idea of Imperial magic to create one. The Watchtowers have a Lustrum that acts as generic filter, allowing the Lucid to understand and connect to certain specific sets of symbols. The downside is that they are not as personalized as a proper archmage's Lustrum, so they provide consistent results rather than optimal ones for the person (which is why every Arcanum is Ruling for an archmage and they are far stronger in Clashes of Wills).

                                                        Originally posted by orathaic View Post
                                                        Is there a different name for the area around a Lustrum?
                                                        The Lustrum is the area. Every part of the directly-experienced Supernal that's not being interpreted as a Lustrum is just the Supernal.

                                                        I'm not sure what you mean. When someone awakens, do other mages who happen to be there see the Lustrum and interact with the space?
                                                        Focused Mage Sight sees the Supernal through the object being Scrutinized, in contrast to seeing the object in the context of the Supernal; a Lustrum is the Supernal interpreted as a scene. Mages can describe what they see in their Focused Sight to other mages, or convey it through their memory, but Opacity is tracked separately for every mage observing a Mystery and Mage Sight is a personal experience.

                                                        A Lustrum is the same place for everybody occupying it, whether it's the Cintamani of a collaborating archmaster (for which the characters must deliberately seek to align their experience, else they will merely be interpreting the same Supernal symbol in different ways), the foyer of a hospitable True God, or the Adyton of an Awakening being intruded upon by another mage who shares the Prospect's potential Path through very lucky uses of Focused Mage Sight (it requires an exceptional success on a Revelation roll for what counts as an Opacity 6 Mystery).

                                                            Originally posted by orathaic View Post

                                                            I think i am more confused now, since the Supernal in 2e is more an overlay on reality than actual physical realm
                                                            It is in a sense, but it's also still supposed to be distinct enough that Ascension matters.

                                                            I mean, in the very rare cases where it matters at all to mages who are not archmasters (Irises in the heart of Emanations are rare and difficult enough to find and approach as it is), the question of "where" you might end up, whether it be some totally disconnected distant place or something immediately corresponding to where you were a moment before, is going to be rendered a bit moot by the fact that there's nothing you can do to keep from being annihilated to the point that you might never have existed if you go through.

                                                            Still, I think it's a bit of an error to look at what Active Mage Sight reveals and think of the Supernal as being like the Shadow. When you look at that level upon something and see, say, a spiralling dance of fair folk, you're not seeing something that is literally happening in that exact spot on the other side of the Abyss, you're getting information about the essence of what is at work there conveyed through symbols.

                                                            I also think some ways in which the Supernal might be seen as overlaying the Phenomenal are complicated by something like what all five Realms say about Space, namely that distance is really more of a perceptual thing emerging out of a sense of intimacy. With that in mind, is a fairy circle seen in New York really something like 3000 Supernal miles away from the same symbol perceived in Los Angeles? Can you really say if it isn't the actual same Supernal process that is applying to two (or more) Phenomenal places simultaneously?

                                                            In any case, the Lustrum mostly matters for archmasters, who don't really travel to the Supernal as though it was a distinct physical place so much as they tune the frequency of their existence until it functions on the more abstract level of the Supernal (even if many of them experience this in terms of walking along their Golden Road, because the symbolism matters). It's just that once an archmaster does so, it becomes necessary to engage with one's reality by grasping for relevant concepts and willing them to assume a comprehensible symbolic form, and where engagement with the Supernal over a period of time tends to require creating a chain of Lustrums.

                                                            The very idea of an area immediately surrounding the Lustrum is complicated by the fact that experiencing space in terms of physical distinctions is really only something that takes place inside the Lustrum. Beyond the boundaries of the scene that the archmaster has created, there is everything and nothing all at once.
