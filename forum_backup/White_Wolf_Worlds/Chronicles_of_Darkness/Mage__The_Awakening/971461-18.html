                    Morality porn is Gecko's uncharitable shorthand for everything she dislikes about Promethean.                                 I'm not against humanism, I'm against specific aspects of Promethean and against fiction that boils down to "oh my god isn't immortality awful". That's what mortality porn means. Harry Potter and Highlander are other examples of it. Humanism is a real world philosophy that doesn't really go into immortality, but I primarily agree with what it does deal with. Promethean has very little to do with humanism as a philosophy, and mostly uses it as a shorthand for "if you don't want your character to become human you're PLAYING WRONG". But "mortality porn" specifically is a very valuable term to describe the bizarre aversion many scifi and fantasy authors have to the concept. Dinosaur Comics has a brilliant strip about this, where T-Rex, my personal hero, basically says that humans tell all these stories about how immortality would be awful because we aren't immortal, and we want to reassure ourselves that we aren't missing anything. I think that's very true in a philosophical and historical sense- humanity has developed a deep and enduring Stockholm syndrome with death and mortality, because it's been inescapable. That becomes a problem, however, now that we're finally reaching an era in which those two might be overcome.

                                I'd like to apologize for my comment about "maybe ChroD isn't for you", because it was unfair. Bigdamnhero's incredibly uncharitable misreading of my post as "everyone should be irredeemably evil" really kind of hit a nerve.

                                And that aside, I do think people should be able to play any way they want, but I think systems and settings are set up to facilitate certain kinds of playing, and people playing them should be aware of that. My issue with Mage is that it doesn't do as well capturing the sort of atmosphere and stories that I want out of ChroD.

                                Also, speaking of uncharitability: "people who agree with me have friends and social lives but people who disagree with me are shut-ins with no friends". If you have a gripe with the mechanical implications of my suggestions and their potential impact on the game's playability, by all means use the knowledge you have gained from all the time you've spent Playing With Actual People to tell me when I'm doing something that could make the game unplayable. Are you saying that I'm making the game too depressing and dark for people to enjoy? Plenty of people love Wraith, Changeling, Promethean, and Mummy, and those are all quite a bit darker than I'll probably ever make Mage. Are you saying that mechanics that emphasize the inhumanity of the protagonists hinder its ability to be played and enjoyed? Vampire, Changeling, and Unknown Armies all have important mechanics that measure how divided you are from humanity, and all of those mechanics seem to work quite well for those games. As an aside, if you're ever wondering what I'm going for, I'm asking myself "how do I make this more like Unknown Armies?". As much as I love playing Unknown Armies, I would still love to having something like that with ChroD's higher power level and and setting.

                                Okay, I admit it. I don't really get to play tabletop RPGs much due to numerous personal issues. My primary interest in tabletop RPGs is in setting and philosophy. I'm sorry about that. The primary point of this hack is philosophical and setting based. I'm sorry if you don't think that's a good idea, or that's not how you engage with tabletop games. I'm sorry I'm not approaching this from an "experiential" perspective, but a "I read the book and learned about the setting and I would like to change some parts of it" perspective. Satchel, I didn't know that was you. I'm sorry that I wasn't able to pull off the campaign. Can we...can we get over it? I'm sorry. I was new to things and I promised something I didn't end up delivering. But here's a fun idea! Let's not dredge up irrelevant personal things to argue that people we don't like are antisocial shutins! All of the shit I've been involved in, and I've never dredged up stuff people did from years ago to point at it and say "See! See! Look! This person is a stupid dumb nerd!" You're explicitly telling people that they shouldn't agree with me or support me because apparently I'm some sort of socially-broken, nonfunctional monster.

                                Charlaquin, clearcutting in this case would primarily mean removing existing elements that already emphasize the instability and monstrosity of Mage. Which would you consider those to be, and do you think my changes risk breaking them? Things like Obsession and Paradox fill those rolls, and I'm proposing emphasizing both of them and making them even nastier, so that's sort of like planting even more trees? Metaphors are hard.
