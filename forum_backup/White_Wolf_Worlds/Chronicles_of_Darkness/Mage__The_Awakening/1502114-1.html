            Sooooort of?

            Again*, you can't sit on the sidelines of an Order and expect to get Status, so if you're a low status member of an Order becasue you're off chasing your own Obsession butterflies and attain Mastery of an Arcanum, you're probably not going to gain esteem as a member of that order. On the other hand, if you're active and reliable as a member, then the attainment of mastery is likely to to more directly result in the rise of your esteem in the eyes of your peers.

            As a general rule, the game mostly assumes these two things are inter-related most of the time-one joins an Order to take advantage of the resources that makes it easier to expand your grasp of magic, the needs of the Order create opportunitiies for that expansion, fulfilling those needs raises your general appraisal with the order, your Obsessions become larger and grander the more powerful you get, that relative size turns into benefits and opportunities for others in the Order, and so on.

            But because they aren't synonymous with each other, the game has to understand how the two are viewed and feared and/or respected by others on their own, and in that regards, mastery of the Arcana is a more universal signifier of action and knowledge than the more politcally biased nature of Status. When you take those two with that in mind and reintroduce them to each other, you get the rough disparity we started with- they probably rise together when you work the two together, but only gain esteem as a Master if you don't-which is not nothing.

            Some Orders are going to have some flex and stretch on that-the Silver Ladder, as noted, and the Mysterium are more likely to lean towards giving Status with Mastery, the Arrow and the Guardians are less likely, and the Free Council is a general mess on the subject given how the democratic nature of the Sect makes status even more wibbly due to the fluid inter-relation between popularity and mastery of the Arcana can and can't be-But as a general rule, if you're active, it will, if you're not, it won't.

            *Barring, again, exceptional circumstances.

                    This a world with the Demon, Demon, Demon Problem, so two kinds of magisters are the norm.

                    Convocations are.....well, they're not an exception to the usual rules, but it is an exceptional occasion.

                    Anyways, in theory, the principle is that any Master can basically claim a right to take on the responsibility and activity of arranging, directing, and maintaining a Convocation, a task that is theoretically always welcome because Convocations are big, hard to arrange things that can always do with the power and resources of anyone whose put in the work to become a Master.

                    In practice, this can potentially be a political clusterfuck given the purpose of the convocation and the feelings of the masters involved. As a minor example, let's say the Arrow Caucuses of the American West have put up a call for a Convocation for getting a top down perspective on the last season or two's activities from enemies and generally coordinating what the forward action's going to be between them. As a rule, you want the Adamant Sages and Thunderbolt Guardians there-you probably do not want Johnny Teethkicker, a Talon in the arrow because he's too busy kicking in teeth for his own obscure purposes, to be part of the planning and scheduling, because he's pretty demonstrably always distracted by his practice of just randomly kicking in people's teeth. This can be a problem when he, as a Master because his Obsession with kicking teeth in has yielded results for him personally, exercises his right to be a magister for the Convocation. You can imagine the actual leaders of the Arrow Caucuses doing their level best to convince him that he doesn't want to arrange a Convocation because he'd be happier kicking in teeth, but if he insists, he has to be permitted a role in planning and arranging the Convcation and specific roles in keeping it going when it happens, which means, strictly speaking, the Convocation is just gonna have to risk possibly falling down as he eventually and invariably pursues his Mystery of choice that is wrapped up in the inexplicable art of kicking in teeth. Or maybe it goes fine, but that doesn't stop the political kerfuffle at the start.

                    As I mentioned in the post that I'm waiting to be re-allowed, there is a general understanding that for mages in the orders, Status and Mastery are very likely to inter-related to each other as a rule. The role of Magister and it's strict relationship to Mastery for Convocation usually relies on this, but allows narrative wrinkles and pecularities for Order and Sect-centered scenarios, but at the same time also allows for Convocations within Pentacle territories that account for and accomodate solitaires, cabals, Legacies and Nameless Orders outside of the system without much Order-based fuss.

The Repeating Nomenclature Problem may colloquially be known as the Demon, Demon, or Demon Problem, but it has many forms. Anyways, the order books are not my main area of verse for Mage, I can't speak for too much of the organizational nuances brought up in those.

I mentioned in the last post* that Status and Mastery are often inter-related because doing the things that raises one's esteem in an Order and likely awards authority to that esteem and the things that expand a mage's identity and ability to grasp magic are often intertwined enough to create the illusion of being the same. It's easier to become a Master by joining an Order and doing Order things than working outside of them, and it's easier to gain Status in an Order by doing Order things.

                            That said, they aren't the same thing this-this is neither the changeling Courts nor the Arisen Guilds-and the thing about Magister being a position of authority that is demarcated strictly and solely by Mastery of the Arcana is that it keeps the overall settings central conceit of the universal metric of esteem being that Mastery of the Arcana squarely where it is.

                            A Magister doesn't need be a rank 4/5 Order member, but rank 4/5 Order members are more likely to be Magisters than non-rank 4/5 Order members, because the Orders make the pursuit of Mastery easier the more you invest in them.

                                I found that the Order books tended to be consistent with the idea that the term "magister" tended to be applied to powerful mages who were immersed in the deeper esoterics of their Order's philosophy and mysticism, and the Silver Ladder book extended that concept with the point of "these people are assumed to have a sense of communion with the Oracles that makes them qualified to run a Convocation". It appeared to be a distinction from what the books described as the actual hierarchical ranks of a caucus that Status could be a prerequisite for/equivalent to, even if at times the concept overlapped (most apparent with the Hierophants of the Mysterium).

                                Honestly, I think it was one of those details that a Second Edition would come at as constituting an unnecessary and confusing extra layer of complexity that should be pared down. For the moment I think it suffices to say that Magister is a term for a mage (regardless of Order) whose mastery makes them eligible to be part of the managing body of a Convocation, that the top ranks of the Orders such as Deacon or Epopt absorb some of the qualities ascribed to magisters before, and that somebody most focused on more esoteric parts of the Mysteries can exist regardless of Status or Arcanum level.

                                I feel as though maintaining Status ought to be viewed as a nuanced thing that acknowledges some common foibles of human character and institutions; that once a person attains high rank it can be hard to shift them even if their dedication seems to waver simply through a sense of inertia in the lower ranks, a lacking sense of procedure of and right to do so along with lingering good will from the process that led to their rise in the first place. I think the circumstances of the Diamond Orders have a tendency to select for people who demonstrate their dedication as they rise up the ranks, that it's unlikely to get somebody who can become a Deacon or Adamant Sage without internalizing the motives for those offices along the way, but in cases where they are idle or perhaps their assumption of the title was a bad call in the first place a lot of people can stall in uncertainty about doing anything in response to it.

                                I think some of this would consider the extent to which Orders aren't exactly centralised organisations with a core body that makes the promotion decisions and holds those within them to account. I'd say that in most cases your caucus's Deacon was chosen for the role by a preceding Deacon. That simplifies some angles on it and complicates others.
