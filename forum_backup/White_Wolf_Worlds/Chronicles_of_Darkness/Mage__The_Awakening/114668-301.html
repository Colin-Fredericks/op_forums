    For space mage armor "if the character successfully dodges an attack the player can spend one point of mana to redirect the attack to another applicable target. The attack hits with successes equal to the mages space arcanum rating"

    1) Does dodging an attack require to take the dodge action IE forgoing ones turn?

    2) Can this be done for multiple attacks per turn?

    3) What is an applicable target? Someone whom the shooter could have shoot or someone whom the mage could potentially fire a weapon at?

    4) So Mr untouchable with a defense of 10 that applies against firearms would not even get a chance to resist when the mages ally shot the mage in order to allow the mage to redirect the shoot thus circumventing the defense?

    5) In the case of some fisticuffs someone tries to attack the mage are they a valid target for having their fist redirected back to themselves?

        1. Well, yes. How do you expect to dodge without dodging?

        2. Not unless you're capable of dodging multiple attacks each turn, with every redirected attack costing one Mana so you're also subject to your Mana per turn limits.

        3. The former, I'm pretty sure.

        4. That would require the mage also being able to dodge the attack, with the risk of failing and getting shot by their ally. I would not allow the use of this attainment on attacks without any successes (before comparing successes).

        5. I wouldn't say so, but others might run it differently. I can very much see the appeal of such a humorous incident.

            Tessie has a pretty good grasp on how I would rule it, but I thought I would lend my opinion on some of the questions you asked.

            #2. Yes, this should be possible since dodging is applicable against multiple attacks, but as Tessie pointed out you need to have the mana per turn limit to handle this.

            #3. I would say anyone within a Space Rating yard radius of you would be an applicable target. That is just how I would rule it anyway.

            #4. This one is a tricky one and I agree with Tessie's interpretation on this as well, but I think the intent would be use the Mage's Space Rating as a die pool to make an attack rather than it just being automatic successes since that seems way overpowered to begin with.

            #5. I agree with Tessie here as well, but you can also change the direction of a bullet from a gun to "straight to the attacker", so this would be possible in my opinion.

                                                            Hmm, it'd depend on the nature of the concealment, and perhaps what was being concealed. If it was pure physical invisibility, such that the camera simply couldn't record the phenomenon, I'd say no. If it has to purely physical concealment, you could have an Investigation roll to reveal something like "this looks photoshoped". But if the photo/recording itself is warded, or it's some kind of active effect keeping people from seeing it, that would definitely Clash. And if it's Abyssal in origin, make their Mage Sight go crazy.

                                                            Edit: hang on, just realized you were asking about peripheral sight specifically...hm normally concealment effects don't trigger peripheral, otherwise they wouldn't be very good at concealment. But if it wasn't really meant for concealment, and just so happens to work to keep people seeing it, that could trigger it. Again, it partially depends on how the concealment works.
