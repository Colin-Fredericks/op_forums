                                                            Very strange...
                                                            Oukronia the Master Enchanter is training a couple of Disciples and wards her Sanctum against historical revision to keep its contents in order, casting Constant Presence on the area before the exercise begins. Her students travel back in time a scene or two to play time-detective with each other's alterations to the past and see if they can track each other down by following their respective breadcrumb trails, potentially drastically changing the nature of the building. Once their spells' Duration runs out and/or they catch up to the present, history begins to settle and a Clash of Wills ensues; if Oukronia wins the Clash, her Sanctum reverts to the state it would have been in in the present where its history was not altered — it's effectively picked up and re-placed following the ripple effect of finalized time alteration.

                                                            Alternately, Unicorn the 5D Chess Champ walks into Oukronia's Sanctum knowing her students plan to ambush him in the past, and so he casts Constant Presence on himself to keep them from looping their way into a tactical coup once they've seen him establish his route into the main hall. One of the Disciples casts Shifting Sands and snipes him from a far window, mortally wounding him, and when they return to the present, if Unicorn wins the Clash he doesn't have a bullet in his head.

                                                            "Travelers are insulated against the alterations of history" is the quality Constant Presence is leveraging in its return; as long as the spell's Duration is running, the present version of the subject is the version of it that will exist when someone alters its history and then returns to the present. The talk about the tell-tale signs of disruption in the spell's initial description seems to be more about the fact that Time mages can spot the work of time travelers and like to have insurance against it — past versions of a character pulling themselves forward in response to meddling would seem to be the opposite of the spell's intent, in addition to functionally being a bootleg version of Temporal Stutter with free Duration.

                                                            There is nothing to indicate any sort of alternate timeline shenanigans are the intended reading of the mechanics; spells to read the future cast in the past show the "future" as it unfolded into the present the time-traveler came from, but unless you're looking at an event with Time Sight there's no sign that it's any different from what was going to happen other than any foreknowledge you already had, which is normally imperfect on account of the way magical precognition works in the present.
