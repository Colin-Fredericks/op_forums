        The problem is Perfect Recall only includes what you could perceive at the time. You remember you felt your peripheral sight ping, but you never used Time sight to see the fracture in the timeline. Therefore, it doesn't register anything if you use it on the memory.

        You would need Prime 1 to recall the supernatural details of the scene. Alternatively, just add Time 1. You get a perfect mental reference to the moment and use that perfect link to follow the Postcognition threat back to the appointed time. The advantage is that, unlike Camera Obscura, the scene doesn't degrade.

        A third option is to use Spirit 1 to find an echo of the events as perceived by the quasi sentient ephemera of the scene. It would be a blurry mess, probably with a penalty to Opacity, but it would still be a place to start.

        There are many ways to go about it, all you need is a lens that is good enough to provide a lead.

                2 - Is there any information on the... Origin... Of the Abyss? I saw someone claiming that it used to be "around" the whole Tapestry instead of "between" the Phenomenal and Supernal Worlds, and what the Gate did was to forcibly change its "placement", but I can't say if that is canonical, implied, or a fanciful bit from a personal chronicle that was presented as fact. I would guess the third option, but the question I worded in 1 makes me wonder if it isn't something that slipped by me as well.
                Historical looks in very early Dark Eras suggest 1e's forwarded mythology about the Exarchs creating the Abyss are just that.

                Mummy's perspective on the cosmology in Dreams of Avarice provides a very probable equivalent in the formless waters-that-are-not-waters of Nu and the stillborn children of Atum and Khepera who slid into those waters in the frenzy of creation that the great gods began to thwart the Devourer; in this model, the location predates material existence, but its denizens were made soon after reality kicked into gear.

                Changeling's resonant not-quite-equivalent in the Hedge, meanwhile, tends to suggest that the borderland was created after the Fae took Arcadia (in 2e) or only became significant after whatever change led the Fae to start taking human captives (in 1e). The lines are blurred here, as the dream-level view of the Aeon of the Abyss explicitly stands in opposition to the Fae, but the Huntsmen (who predate the Fae as residents of Arcadia) move with more speed and more of their own intent within the Hedge.

                Which is to say: There's theories to be found.
