    The Scarlet Letter

    Don't be mislead by the Scarlet's Letter deceivingly small size: that Supernal Spirit is one of the most potent and frightful Wraiths in all Pandemonium. It embodies the social power that the outcast detains over those who shun them. The weight of a prostitute's glance on the men clamoring their disdain for her, but secretly lusting after her; the shame and cowardice they display while reluctantly bargaining with their drug dealer; the repressed admiration they feel when a foreigner (or a woman) turns out to perform better than the majority ; the fear in the heart of the righteous believers crossing the rumored witch's path, nervously gesturing in their pockets to conjure the evil eye.

    Rank: 5
    Arcana: Mind and Fate

    Appearance
    Quite soberly, the Letter manifests as a doppelgänger of its Summoner, standing quietly in its Summoner circle. The resemblance is perfect on every fashion – except for two things. First, its defiant, somber glare (in which some pretend to have seen fire, and ruins, and many other wonders. Second, the pattern of swollen, translucent veins it arbors on its forehead, or on the back of its left hand: the bloodflow, visibly coursing through them, traces the shape of the Scarlet Letter at each heartbeat.

    The Letter's form per se varies from one onlooker to another. Mages almost always see a twisted version of their Inferior Arcanum's Rune, while Vampires often see it take the shape of the number 7 (whatever that may mean, it doesn't seem to exactly please them). Only the strange creatures calling themselves "Begotten" seem thrilled by the sight of the Letter, enthusiastically refering to it as "the Initial of the Dark Mother's Name". Mastigos know better than to inquire about that, but many shiver at the thought: if that is truly only the first letter, what in the hell (and for a Mastigos, Hell is meant quite literally) would answer to the full name?

    The Entity never try to communicate with its Summoner. When it answers to its Summoner's questions about his purviews, it's through telepathical imagery – often crude and traumatizing. The only occasions it’ll express itself verbally is when he receives the order to use its powers against foes, or at the beginning of a fight where he and its Summoner will be involved. A simple phrase in High Speech, that one could translate as such: “Let us be evil, then”.

    Trial:
    Summoning the Scarlet Letter is embracing the Outcast in oneself. It's becoming the villain, the monster extreme politicians paint in their hateful speeches, the Antichrist bigots yell about when they preach in the street, the opposite of what . The power of the Entity floods the body and soul of the Summoner, flailing him with all the bullying and exclusion he either suffered or inflicted, trying to tear apart the bastion of identity he built to be socially accepted: and the stronger that social stronghold is, the more painful that overthrowing shall be.

    Upon a successful evocation, the Summoner loses (Summoner's total Social Merit dots minus half their Gnosis) lethal damage. He can mitigate those injuries by accepting a -1 in any of his Resistance Attributes (Resolve, Stamina, Composure), on a 1-to-1 basis. It reflects the Entity's efforts to make him less thick-skinned, easier to alienate.

    Special Power: Fueled by Their Fears

    Apart from his unparalleled mastery of both Mind and Fate Arcanum, as well as its extensive knowledge of all prosecuted/exiled/witch-hunted groups or individuals, the Scarlet Letter grants one quite peculiar boon: the power to pull his opponent’s subconscious fears about the Other, and makes them true.

    The Summoner gains a pool of dices equal to his total Social Merits dots: in the unlikely case of a Summoner without any Social Merit dots, the pool contains (Gnosis minus the Summoner's lowest Social Attribute) dices. At any times while the Entity is less than 10 meters away from him, he may spend one dice, roll it and apply the effect specified next to its number (see below). He may spend 1 Mana to choose the entry directly above or below the one he rolled. If the pool is emptied, the Entity’s time in the Fallen World is considered over, even if it shouldn’t be over per usual Summoning rules.

        You might consider integrating more the dark temptations of academia; the things that produce a cycle of self-expansion. The need for funding in academia is so dire that a 2017 Nobel Prize winner (Jeffrey Hall, whose work contributed to identifying circadian regulation mechanisms in flies, which were also found in people; a whole new model for understanding our body's regulatory systems) received his prize after he'd been forced to close his lab due to lack of funds.

        This is a feature the world over. The financial crisis crushed academic research funds, and they're slow to resurge, and this is hurt by the fact that equipment is increasingly expensive. A million dollars in funding might've been split between ten labs a few decades ago, but now it goes to one or two, both because of the relative expenses and the intricacies of scientific research in the modern era.

        As such, the temptation to fudge data or falsify findings or break ethics codes are getting all the stronger. Publish, as they say, or perish. A spirit definitely would want its university to grow, and so...

                Beliar, the acceptance
                Rank 5 stygian entity. May or may not be a royal avatar of death itself... Shes very vauge about that, and enjoy implying contradictory things depending on the one she speaks to.

                More specifically shes the acceptance of death and loss. She is grief, and she is mourning. But she is also comfort in solace in knowing that death is an inevitable part of life for most mortals.
                For that reason shes a bitty enemy of those that cheat death. Cults, as well as cabals themed around her, are often taskes with hunting down those that trick death. Making her a potent antagonist for kindred, or others that did not die when they were "supposed to."

                She does like to appear as a human, preferring to be mistaken for an archmage... this ofcourse causes some confusion for those having an encounter with her, as she appears to be bound by the supernal rules of summoning. But from her perspective, it is a lot simpler to act as a source of comfort and understanding when you dont look like a supernal creature beyond mortal comprehension. So when you encounter her, she will usually appear as a youngish woman, whos a bit on the pale side.. Only real tell is her eyes and mouth being black voids.

                The trials she sets before someone usually involves hunting down someone who defies death, and thus have gotten themselves on the list of enemies. Alternatively giving people the power to think they can truly bring back the dead. This always ends in people learning a dear lecture about along the lines of: "things that are dead should not return."

                What she offers depend greatly on what the person seeks. She is compassionate, yes, and not unsympathetic to mortal tribulations. She will often grant boons involving power over the dead (Provided the user intents to put them to proper rest). Or boons relating to generally making other people able to aid others in accepting death... It really depends on what you need her to do, as she can be applied to the story as required by the Storyteller.

                Ban: Must hunt down and destroy anyone whos actively cheating death in her presence.
                Bane: Collected tears of someone mourning the loss of their child.


                She may sound like a great ally for most causes, but shes honestly more interesting to play out as an antagonist, or neutral party. Shes immensely powerful, but also immensely hard to get a hold on. In my campaign we have someone who lost a loved one, and is questing to bring him back. And in cases like that she works well as a understanding, but also sternly moralizing presence, that acts halfway as a mother figure trying to talk the PC to accept her grief. But also as a vast threat that promises to destroy the PC, if she ever reaches her goal.

                -... All in all, Im bending the rules a bit with this one (Mainly stuff about how she can interact with the mortal world regularly) But shes a ton of fun to pop in every so often!

                Also, she may or may not be inspired by "Death" from the Sandman comics by Niel Gaiman... But dont tell my players that!

                                    For most idigam, change is the bottom line. Even those that coalesce are constantly seeking new ways to change the world around them. A very few, though, have rejected change. They’ve come to recognize that change for the sake of change leads to chaos. Gamugur is one of the rare idigam that values stability, and it has found that stability in the faith of humans.

                                    Contrary to modern iconography, angels weren’t always described as humans with wings and halos. Always resistant to change, Gamugur has taken on the old-school appearance of an angel more in keeping with the descriptions found in the Old Testament or Dante’s Divine Comedy. Each of Gamugur’s three heads bears 33 never-blinking eyes, and its three mouths speak with one voice. Its body is sexless and has six arms and nine legs. Gamugur is borne aloft by blindingly white wings that are set in three rows on its back, and it is surrounded at all times by a nimbus of golden light.

                                    [For more information on this Idigam, see p. 139 of Night Horrors: Wolfsbane.]


                                    Gamugur, The Unholy Host
                                    Rank: 5
                                    Attributes: Power 12, Finesse 15, Resistance 10
                                    Influences: Faith 3, Stability 4
                                    Manifestations: Gauntlet Breach, Image, Materialize, Reaching, Shadow Gateway, Twilight Form
                                    Numina: Aggressive Meme, Emotional Aura, Fate Sense, Gauntlet Vision*, Implant Mission, Rapture, Resurrection**, Soul Snatch, Speed, Two Influence dots
                                    Essence Shaping: Essence Venom, Essence Void, Forge Servant (Claimed, Spawn***), One Million Eyes, Spirit Manipulation
                                    Dread Powers: Bane-Salve, Divine Clay, Mind of God, The Light

                                    Corpus: 16
                                    Essence: 100
                                    Willpower: 10
                                    Defense: 18
                                    Size: 6
                                    Speed: 20
                                    Initiative: 20

                                    Ban: Gamugur cannot directly harm religious officials.
                                    Bane: Communion wine blessed by the pastor of the congregation

                                    • Divine Clay: Gamugur may only reshape flesh to the form it had before an injury, due to its reluctance to alter even unhappy changes. Each success on a Power + Finesse roll downgrades injuries by one level, to a minimum of bashing damage.

                                    • Mind of God: After communing with the stars, communicating with the minds of humans is child’s play. Gamugur constantly hears the thoughts of humans as a kind of background murmur. By concentrating on a specific human, it can read that person’s mind, down to their deepest, darkest secrets. Characters who would like to resist this kind of mental probing must first have some idea that Gamugur is reading their minds. Since the idigam often answers question before they are asked, however, this is pretty easy to guess at after a character has personally encountered the idigam at least once. Once a character knows (or guesses) that Gamugur is digging around in her head, she may roll Resolve + Composure (plus any supernatural resistance trait) vs. the idigam’s Power + Finesse to resist.

                                    • The Light: While injury and disease represent a kind of change, reversing those changes through healing also alters a body. Instead of lowering itself to commit changes, Gamugur simply places disease and injury in a kind of stasis where they no longer hinder the inflicted. Using this power costs Gamugur three Essence. Wounds still heal at the normal rate, but diseases remain inactive for a number of days equal to successes in a Power + Finesse roll.

                                    * -- Gauntlet Vision is a combination of the 1E Numina, Material Vision and Spirit Vision
                                    ** -- Gamugur's Resurrection Numen does not suffer any penalties based on how much time has passed since the death. However, any and all revived individuals become Ascended.
                                    *** -- The Cherubs are the result of Gamugur's Forge Spawn power.

                                                            I've been working on this supernal entity for an upcoming story. Feedback appreciated.

                                                            "Oriel"
                                                            Seraph of Prime, Death, and Fate
                                                            Rank 5
                                                            Oriel is the Angel of Destiny, the entity which knows the ultimate fate of every soul. Such a central and powerful symbol often masks its importance, claiming - perhaps not falsely - that it is merely a royal avatar of greater powers: a guide who can reveal, but not author, the strings of destiny. Only the most powerful of summoning rituals can reach this entity, although rumor has it that it can choose to appear for any summoning from Aether, Stygia, or forces of Fate to impart a revelation it deems necessay.

                                                            The Ritual: first, and most obviously, the summoning must occur in an area rich with death resonance - on top of an Avernian Gate or Stygian verge is ideal. It may not, however, be performed in a personal sanctum: it must occur in a (theoretically) public place, open to any who seek answers about about the departed. Next, the ritual space must be prepared with appropriate symbols of fate, which demonstrate that the final destiny is shaped by the path one walks. Finally, to prove their understanding of death, the summoner must either kill a creature at the time and manner ordained by fate, or destroy one that has cheated death.
                                                            Seers belonging to the cult of the Psychopomp who manage to gain a dot in Prelacy sometimes recieve secret invocations that can bypass some of the ritual requirements.

                                                            The Trial: Any who seek Oriel's revelation must first confront truths about their own soul and the destinies which shape their past and future. This is a brief astral journey which most often takes the forms of death which have touched their life: those they've lost, or killed, or cannot bear to part with. Frequently, the vision includes choices the summoner has made which have already damned them, or shatters a hope they have been holding onto. For a rare few, it reveals the consequences of choices they have yet to make, particularly if it would cause widespread suffering. Many think of the trial as a boon in itself, but the angel imparts revelation designed to break the will and make them reconsider the hubris of their course of action.

                                                            Boon: The chief boon imparted by Oriel is the most obvious reason to summon such an entity: it can reveal, and sometimes hasten, the fate of another soul. Such answers somehow manage to be both straightforwardly practical and so enigmatic as to obscure any truth. For example, it may reveal that a soul has been torn asunder, passed eternally behind the stars, has been written into the supernal, or has been reborn into a specific person. Many are told a list of people and locations without any context and no perceiveable trace of the soul in question; some consider this to mean "no valid answer exists", while others believe that the soul is broken down and spread throughout the universe. If the soul is still in the Fallen World or any reachable realm, this is of course revealed - sometimes, even, with explanations of how it got there or how to reach it.
                                                            Alternatively, if the soul in question is incorporated into the ritual, the summoner may ask Oriel to take it to its ultimate destiny early. Some ask this as a particularly final death, or in hopes of averting a coming tragedy. However, the angel only readily accepts if the soul has cheated death; for those whose time has not yet come, it will place a ban on the summoner to fulfill their remaining destiny in the mortal world - paying with every impact that soul would have on every other person. Consider this a combined spell of Forge Purpose (Prime 5) and Sworn Oaths (Fate 3), which grants supernatural insight into what impact must be recreated; the curse for breaking this Oath (or removing it) is that their own soul to be thrown into oblivion. The natural consequences, however, are that the Abyss will seep into the gaps of destiny, and other agents of Fate are called to mend those gaps as best they can.
