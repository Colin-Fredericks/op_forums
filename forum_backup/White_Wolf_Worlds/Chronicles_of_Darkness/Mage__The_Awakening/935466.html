    Starting Mana after Timeskips

    07-22-2016, 02:27 AM
    What is the starting Mana value for a character supposed to be at the beginning of a new Story and how, if at all, do dots in Hallow play into it? The book only talks about the value at the start of the Chronicle, right after character generation.
    • reset to full?
    • reset to equal Wisdom?
    • no change, exactly as at the end of last Story?
    • 1d10 + Hallow?

    I was unable to find anything about this in the book. It seems like a curious oversight. I personally like the last two best because they are the most likely to put the characters in a bind, but maybe someone has a better idea or even heard from one of the writers or even Dave.

                                      You can assume that it's full mana or whatever. Mages and Vampires use Mana and Blood for different purposes and the "mana amanagement" and "blood management" minigames have different emphasis. For Vampires, blood is both tactical and strategical resource - everything revolves around blood, you need it to survive, and you will probably spend it every scene; getting to 0 has real consequences.

                                      For Mages, it's less strategic and much more tactical. They have so many means to get their mana back, and it's so not-needed outside of stressful situations, that long-term mana management isn't really a problem or that interesting. There are many, many ways to get mana, and often, you are not really competing with anyone for it.
                                      When you are managing mana, it's probably in context of "fuck, I need to save the world, make myself sexy for the date, and record my favorite tv show back in the 1999, and world ends in 40 minutes, goddamit stupid world" or something like that - which boils down to being limited by your CURRENT and MAXIMUM mana. You *could* easily refill to full, but it would take time, and pursuing mana would mean not pursuing your goals right now.
                                      Basically, you are expected to manage Mana tactically - in context of your current "encounters" and chapters - and while you don't need ANY mana for everyday, off screen business, you can go dry very, very quickly during tense situations. Imagine a scene where each turn, your character is exposed to a dangerous tilt or condition that needs to be Pattern Restored ASAP. Pattern Restoration takes 3 mana. It will drain you to 0 in *4 turns* from maximum.
                                      Honestly? If you want characters in a bind, reset their Mana to maximum, but present time sensitive, mana intensive challenges. Hallows real strength isn't recharging mana when you are safe and don't have pressing matters. It's the fact that you can harvest it and store for later, when you need MANA RIGHT HERE, RIGHT NOW.
