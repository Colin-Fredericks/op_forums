    Question about supernal realms vs other realms

    10-19-2016, 08:31 PM
    Apologies since this has been discussed before, but I'm trying to get a handle on this, since I've been rereading Changeling. So, 2e has done a lot to sort of cut through the confusion on the realms, and specifying that the Arcadias aren't the same. So, is the general, basic assumption that the supernal realms aren't actual places, they're sort of...clusters of symbols that manifest within the soul/mind of the Mage when they Awaken?

    To give another example, the Empyrean isn't so much connected to the God-Machine or the Principle as it is an abstract conception of the same symbols, images, and concepts that comprise them? And Mage Arcadia isn't the same as Changeling Arcadia, because it isn't actually a place, but they're similar in that they both relate to similar concepts? So the Wyrd and Arcadia are physical manifestations of the concepts of fate and narrative, while Mage Arcadia is also related to those concepts, but in the sense that their presence in the world causes them to form into a "realm" in the Supernal?

    Awakening is kind of super confusing and weird when you get into it, but I'm not saying that as a criticism.

                  I could talk about how the phenomenon of hierophany is broadly salient here, but the short and long of it is that the Supernal is a transcendent realm that manifests through symbols that happen to be used by other realms for similar reasons.

                  Just as Beasts derive Kinship Nightmares from the core of "what elements of fear are associated with this being I am tied to?" regardless of whether the Primordial Dream is the actual root of the supernatural, the Supernal is the collection of grains of occult power that the Awakened glimpse at the heart of magical phenomena.

                      As I understand it, there is a difference between the Supernal world and the Supernal realms. The Supernal world is what Mages see when they use Mage Sight, the symbolic truths that define and are given meaning by the Fallen world. The Supernal realms are the hypothetical places from whence these symbols originate, which may or may not actually exist, and if they do, probably aren't "places" in the traditional sense of the word where it's a physical space you can occupy, but are more like cosmic addresses that exist beyond the Fallen world.

                      EDIT: To explain it in terms of the allegory of the cave, the Fallen world is the wall and the shadows being cast on it. The Supernal world is the cut outs being used to cast the shadows. The Supernal Realms are outside the cave.

                          Eh, I kind of prefer them not really being a physical place, and definitely not something that takes precedence over everything else. The "symbols manifested within the mind of the Mage" approach is one I really like, and I'm glad 2e has a lot of support for it, because it doesn't privilege the Mage cosmology over any other one. For example, if Fae Arcadia and Mage Arcadia are both places, then you have "the real shiny true Arcadia and the shitty fake pathetic reflection of Arcadia for losers", whereas "Mage Arcadia is a symbolic representation of the ideals of time and fate that a Mage creates within themself to connect their soul to the Supernal, which resembles Mage Arcadia to a point because it both draw from similar pools of symbols". I can see the cave allegory being something some mages tell themselves, but out of universe I don't really think it works out, unless the Supernal is the only kind of magic in the your setting.

                          I guess I'm just a crossover person, but I have heard a lot about how Awakening is crossover-compatible, and how quite a few Mysteries aren't connected to the Supernal and relate to other supernatural phenomena. I've also heard that mages don't approach them from the perspective of "oh look at these silly little fake versions of the Supernal realms", and going deep into the symbols approach seems like a good way to do it, allowing mages to study and comprehend other supernatural phenomena and connect it to their own understanding of the world, without making either cosmology subservient to the other.

                                      Supernal Realms being places or not is ultimately academic, because it won't matter in almost any game that you're actually in. It's sort of like the true cosmological nature of Duat from Mummy, or debating where the denizens of the underworld who never lived come from - good for a debate, but ultimately besides the point, so I wouldn't get too wrapped up in that.

                                      The most relateable analogy I've heard so far is from DaveB a long while back about how Awakening relates to the Matrix: mages are redpills, in that they are aware that their reality is a simulation and have the power to bend or break the artificial rules. The big difference? The Supernal isn't the real world, the Supernal is the source code that Neo can see.
