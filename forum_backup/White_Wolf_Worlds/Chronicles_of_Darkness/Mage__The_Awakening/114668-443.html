                        You can - you can even, for example, increase your Scale factor to cast a spell on three people and use Sympathetic Range for only one of them, be viewing a second through a scrying window and be touching the third.

                        However, the Attainment says *a* subject. You have to waste a yantra slot on a sympathetic yantra, have a sympathetic link, and pay a Mana for each individual subject. The Withstand ratings follow the standard rules for Withstand stacking - the highest individual rating plus one for each additional rating that applies. (Remember that not knowing their name adds one *to that individual rating* when doing this math). Because of this, you'll find that you will only be confident of multiple-sympathy spells where the spells themselves aren't Withstood but have Potency as the primary factor. Having the spell as a Praxis helps, as you might gamble on an exceptional success cancelling Withstand.

                        So - yeah. But you only really see it in high-Gnosis, high-Arcana mages that have the dice pools to burn on the complexities involved.

                        Dave Brookshaw

                            Originally posted by Enmai View Post
                            If that is the case, what would be the Withstand rating of the spell?
                            Withstand rating is per subject, not per spell. Just pointing it out so you don't get into a mental pitfall regarding that part.
                            Each subject calculates their own Withstand rating which in this case would probably invoke the multiple Withstand rule: Use the highest Withstand rating plus one for each additional Withstand rating that applies. In your example it would be the higher of their relevant Resistance Attribute and their Sympathetic Withstand rating according to the strength of the Sympathetic Connection (plus potential increase for not knowing their Sympathetic Name), and then add one to that. If the spell is not normally Withstood, it's just their Sympathetic Withstand rating.

                                        #6655
                                        06-11-2020, 07:08 PM
                                        I don't think so.

                                        I think that N&A implies that Abyssal realities are defined by anti-symbols. While Supernal symbols express what is, Abyssal anti-symbols express what is not. That's why Abyssal realities are described as "empty" -- they lack affirmative truths. Anything not expressly disallowed by an Abyssal reality's anti-symbols exists within that reality. It's like if you described the rules of a game only by saying what things the players can't do, so that any wild-ass thing not disallowed by those rules is allowed. The players could go wild with an unbounded amount of nonsense and the game wouldn't be fun for long.

                                        Abyssal intruders desire affirmative truths and invade "our" reality to experience them.

                                        Scelesti are seduced by the infinite possibilities available in realities where only a finite number of things are impossible. The example Scelesti by Path from N&A describe mages who essentially apply a Boolean NOT operator to the fundaments of their Paths (e.g. Acanthus who hold that there is no destiny and choices do not determine consequences).

                                        "Every choice has its consequences" is the sort of thing a Supernal symbol would capture.

                                        "Outcomes cannot be predicted from actions" is the sort of thing an Abyssal anti-symbol would capture. Outcomes could depend on random chance, or the phase of the moon, or how many freckles John has on his nose, so long as they can't be predicted from someone's actions. Such realities are incomprehensible to humans and difficult to describe as a ST, so "mind-shattering unreality" is a good place to leave off.

                                                        Originally posted by proindrakenzol View Post
                                                        I would say "no," actually. The Abyss would be described by its own, Abyssal symbols, the filtering of the Supernal Symbols' Truth through the Abyssal Symbols' Anti-truth is what creates the Lie of the Fallen World.
                                                        My arguments:

                                                        1. The Gate is an Exarch, or at least near enough to one that her Secret Order can be a Legacy; further, one that has access to an Abyssally-tainted Emanation Realm, i.e. a place where an Arcanum (Space, if memory serves) is strongly manifested is used by a heretical Ministry to command Abyssal entities.

                                                        2. Mages can be drawn from Abyssal worlds (Awakened doppelgangers are one of the oldest described forms of Paradox in the game across both editions, as a core example, the basis for a Legacy, and the backstory of a signature character) and their magic is not significantly different from their truly-extant counterparts.

                                                        3. "Abyssal Symbols" have never, to my knowledge, been described in any terms firmer than "things can be found in the Abyss that present false or antithetical symbolism to those found in Supernal Mystery Plays"; the Annunaki are the closest concretely-described such phenomena, but they're more defined ludonarratively by their opposition to Form and/or their desire to subsume what exists than to their component entities being anything so visually indistinct as "not-a-cat" or "un-fire." Reality is out to lunch in the Abyss, but the vast stretch of corrupted imagination being visible there doesn't mean a person trapped within it stops being a person — we've seen people experience its contents within the fiction.

                                                        4. "The Abyss" and "Abyssal entities" are, as the Nemean firmly reminds us in The Unmurdered Man, not to be confused with each other; there are multiple mentions of things being consigned to the Abyss because they are impossible or because they so offended the gods and/or the Exarchs.

                                                        5. Abyssal entities are modeled with the systems that use Essence and Resonance rather than Mana and Arcana, and are arguably the best-adapted among invisible entities to the material world; when present in the world, they are firmly (sometimes upsettingly) on the "concrete" side of the Supernal World's point "where concrete and the abstract mix." We have basically zero evidence that the Arcana do not work on Abyssal entities small enough to interact with, and the Arcana are the descriptive lens through which the Awakened gather occult information.

                                                        6. Befouling a spell draws upon the Abyss and never happens by accident, but mages who simply Reach beyond their grasp aren't invoking anybody's Abyssal shard, and the general bent of phenomena presented by known Annunaki is not incompatible with their components being real things assembled in nonsensical fashions. "Things that can't exist" require reference to things that can exist to be engaged with in a descriptive context.
