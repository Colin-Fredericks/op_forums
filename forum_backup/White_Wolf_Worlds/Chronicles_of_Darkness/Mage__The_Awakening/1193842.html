    Werewolf ST looking for help with using Mages

    03-10-2018, 04:15 AM
    I'm running a Werewolf game and had some sort of evil Mage as an antagonist, leading a cult of Worm blood addicted people\creatures. I had planned him being just a passing villain for the players to deal with, but during the session crap hit the fan and the players chose to flee for now.

    I don't plan on Mages being in a large role, but if the players return to the scene of the crime, I wanted to be better prepared. I've read parts but Mage is hard for my brain to understand so rather than buy the core book and muddling through it, I'd love some basic ideas from you experts.

    1. Do Mages police themselves, do they frown upon Mages running a cult and getting people killed\causing a scene? I assume they have their own version of a Masquerade type of thing. How well do they find out about such incidents? I assume they can find out incidents not covered in the news.

    2. What would this police\investigative force look like? Roughly how many, what sort of equipment. Let's assume the evil Mage is fairly powerful and was wanted, perhaps they could also assume presence of powerful Spirits (plus several people have already died\been torn to shreds.

    3. How would they fight if attacked by werewolves? Can they teleport easily? What does casting spells look like from outside, is it like Doctor Strange or Dresden Files? Words, gestures, glyphs, sparks?

      1) Yes, but it varies between factions. The Pentacle, the standard protagonist faction, definitely do frown on that sort of thing. The Seers of the Throne (analogous to the Pure) also tend to keep hidden, but have no moral compunctions about killing in secret.

      The Veil (the Mage term for 'Masquerade') is kind of self-enforcing owing to an effect called "Dissonance", similar to Lunacy but more powerful. It forces mortals to rationalize the effects of magic. Blast them with fireball and they will be convinced they've been shot. The flip side is that mortal attention erodes Awakened magic, so they can't jut go around tossing out spells because they hide themselves.

      Mages, like most supernaturals, are pretty well-embedded in mortal society. They have contacts in the police force, mayor's office, pest control, all that good stuff that can get them a heads up. Of course they also have excellent information-gathering powers. One of the most basic powers a mage has is to be able to look at something under their purview and find information about it. Sufficiently powerful ones also have access to remote viewing, psychometry, ghost interrogation and even prophecy.

      2-3) The answer to these is related, because it's important to understand that each mage has their own power set. There's basically only three powers that every single mage has - healing themselves at a very steep mana:health ratio, detecting supernatural stuff (vaguely, like Unseen Sense) and projecting a magical shield that adds a few dots of defense and/or armor. Beyond that, they are entirely defined by their power dots. So some can teleport effortlessly, while others literally nail themselves to the floor. Some throw fireballs, others boost themselves into close-combat monsters.

      Related to that there's no one structure for what a magic SWAT team looks like, because it would vary depending on the local mage culture and who's available. The easy answer is probably "like a group of PCs", but it could be all sorts of stuff. A lone assassin, a hoplite phalanx wielding magical spears, an Ocean's Eleven-style collection of hand-picked specialists.

      In terms of fighting, there's again no definitive answers, though a smart mage probably wouldn't try to take a werewolf in physical combat. Generally though, you would expect a mage to fight indirectly, in a matter befitting their specialty. A necromancer throws ghosts and zombies at you, a Matter specialist creates a pit at your feet, a Mind specialist confuses you and makes you fight each other etc. (Some) mages can and do teleport around the room chucking fireballs, but it's often one of their weaker options.

      e: but again, there are no hard rules and some mages definitely do sink all their magical potential into hitting people real good

      As for what spells look like, it's again pretty varied. Technically all you need to do to cast a spell is think, but all that hand-waving word-chanting stuff helps power it up. So the weakest spell can be cast without twitching a muscle, but the most powerful require standing in a circle for hours, chanting in a supernatural language and waving a staff around while you burn a photo of the person you intend to curse. In heated situations like combat, you'll mostly expect to see gestures (for the mage's favored spells) or the brandishing of magical tools (wands, coins, weapons). When they have slightly more time, they'll speak incantations in an incomprehensible language or paint runes on what they hope to enchant. With a lot of preparation, especially when casting spells at a distance, they will use something representative of the target and/or destroy symbolically resonant objects.

              4. They do and don’t, the Guardians of the Veil have appointed themselves to be that, but they’re usually stretched thin, and are unpopular to boot. A Mage has to do something pretty severe to get noticed by them.

              5. Mages are territorial, they can and do cross huge distances, but if they do they send word ahead of time or check in with the locals after arriving to keep everyone calm. Unless the whole point is super secret espionage or something.

              6. Mages construct their spells to do precisely how much damage they want to do and then roll to get a single success. Typically every two dice they take out of their roll equates to 1 damage B/L orA.

              7. Mana comes from Hallows, it tends to accumulate at a steady but slightly plodding pace. There are other ways to get mana, but the yield is fairly “low.” Mages don’t really need it though, it just limits some of their options to not have it. For instance a Mage without Mana can’t cast on you Sympathetically from halfway across the world.

                  The villain your are describing would probably be best represented by one of the Mad, these are Mages who went too far too fast and recklessly, the end result being that something inside them broke. Now the Mad have three traits that would suit your villain;
                  1-They tend to be loners who hide from other Mages because, well, they are considered dangerous (they often behave like serial killers).
                  ​2-There is one magical effect, like a spell that tends to cast itself when they get frustrated. This maybe the origin of signs of a witch; milk souring, babies sicken, fires starting etc.. They also tend to obsess on something related to that spell (note; they can't cast that spell intentionally. They have no control over it.).
                  ​3-Their magic tends to hide them, whether they want it to or not. People, even other super natural beings, tend to forget them after a while, even recordings and written records fade until there is little evidence (beyond their victims) that they ever existed. This makes it easy for them to 'disappear' without being hunted down.

                  ​As far as spell casting goes I wouldn't worry too much about how Mage actually works, you don't need to check all of the boxes for your villain. Just pick a handful of signature effects you want him to use, such as summoning and controlling Spirits, teleporting and fireballs, make these things he is 'best at'. Give him a dice pool and run with it. If you decide in play that you want him to be capable of other effects than just add them.

                  ​As far as wands, hand waving and chanting, in Mage it's not really a question of how powerful a spell is but how complicated. Teleporting himself and throwing instant fireballs will just require a thought, teleporting a group or filling an entire room with fire will be best served with tools and each tool adds another round to the casting. For really big spells they can perform rituals, spells that take hours to cast and require things like pentagrams drawn on the floor.

                  ​Mages gain Mana, their magic fuel, in one of three ways normally;
                  ​1-They have a Hallow, these are like Spirit Loci only with magic instead. Once a day they can be harvested for a small amount of Mana.
                  ​2-They have a Legacy, don't worry to much what it is but it means once a day they can stand on their heads and recite mantras to generate a small amount of Mana.
                  ​3-Your favorite will of course be blood sacrifice. If the Mage kills something with the intent of harvesting it then they gain Mana equal to the size of their victim. Rats give one, people give five.

                      Ditto on everything the others have said. A few points to add if you want to really flavour the encounter

                      First, Paradox. Subtlety is extremely important to mages magic. We have mentioned dissonance but paradox is their bane. The more clearly not normal the spell is or more powerful the effect then the bigger the chance for the spell to go away. To keep things simple the spell backfires or its parameters change. Sometimes it could also summon an abyssal entity if they really do badly. Mages can contain these paradox's in their bodies and souls but suffer damage when they do. General ways to avoid this include using magic tools of some symbolic meaning and to pump the spell with mana. Non supernatural witnesses always risk this Paradox unless mana is spent.

                      Second, Mages tend to effect their environment based on their path, integrity stat wisdom, and power stat gnosis. Low wisdom mages (likely for what you are thinking effect their environment more widely while powerful ones have a more potent effect. This is usually subtle like a pervading feeling but when they cut loose on something it causes environmental tilts custom to the mage based on their magical "Nimbus".


                      If all this is to complicated just remember that mages need to prepare themselves to be most effective and risk everything going to hell if they push to far with their magic. Their theme is pride before the fall after all. Some would embrace this. Most avoid it at all cost. As a rule of thumb give them a day and enough resources and they can probably waste the werewolves if they know what they are up against. Catch them not fully prepared and the PC's could probably massacre the mage unless he get very reckless. Head on full fight the werewolf will win (usually). You can pretty much make a mage with any type of powers you want. They vary quite a bit immensely.

                      Have fun!

                          Originally posted by Cainite View Post
                          I'

                          1. Do Mages police themselves, do they frown upon Mages running a cult and getting people killed\causing a scene? I assume they have their own version of a Masquerade type of thing. How well do they find out about such incidents? I assume they can find out incidents not covered in the news.
                          They kind of do, but like with werewolves it's not a foolproof system. Like werewolves Mages fall into three major groups: The pentacle, the "good guys" and PCs, which is equivalent to the forsaken. Then there are seers of the throne, the "evil but want to rule the world not destroy it" group which resembles the pure (e.g. like the pure they have inverted the creation myth of the pentacle). Finally there are the scelesti, the "let's consort with powers bent on the destruction of the world" group, which resembles the bale hounds.

                          The pentacle and seers are locked in a cold war that occasionally erupts into open conflict. While the pentacle would love to destroy their opponents they cannot do so without heavy losses (if at all) so they cannot effectively police them, and generally have to oppose/derail their schemes indirectly, so some level of control is there.

                          Both the pentacle and the seers kill any scelesti on sight (just like killing bale hounds is one of the few thing pure and forsaken can agree on), but of course scelesti don't put their allegiance on business cards. If however one is discovered the mages will get rid of him asap.

                          This leaves actions of mages of the pentacle, which generally are policed more than amongst forsaken. While the forsaken society is mostly based on the pack and higher levels of organisation such protectorates or tribes generally don't have a lot of power, mages are political creatures and have more powerful institutions. In a given area the mages of the pentacle are ruled by a consilium which does keep an eye on its territory and enforces the laws. The orders themselves are also quite powerful and can censure mages if they defy the order. Of course because mages are political such investigations can rapidly turn away from the material facts towards who has more and more powerful allies ...

                          So as the first thing I would determine what fraction your evil mage belongs to. Running a cult and get people addicted is certainly something that seer could do, as they want to keep humanity docile, under their control and unaware of the supernatural (and prevent them becoming mages). In this case you could use the "police" as a deus ex machina to give the players a hand. The good mage might explain that the reason shit hit the fan was that the cult leader used his magic to foresee the attack of the werewolves. Unfortunately the good mage cannot directly intervene because this would lead to open war with the seers, however he is willing to cast some spells on the pack that disrupt the precognition of their enemy, allowing another attack with better chances, taking care of this problem for him
                          If I understood you correctly this would fit the story you would like to run as it would keep the mage elements to a minimum

                          A scelesti would also have reasons to run such a cult, e.g. as a source of sacrifices. In this case the "police" would be more prepared to intervene right away and use violence, but probably also less inclined to have werewolves running around, as the powers a scelesti deals with are quite contagious. In such a case the players would probably have to evade the both mages while trying to protect their territory.

                          Finally, it is also possible that the evil mage is a member of the pentacle. While they do claim to act in humanities best interest, mages of the pentacle are not above doing pretty bad things "for the greater good", nor are the immune to the corruptive influence of their enemies. In this case the players would be faced with a political problem as the have to convince the local mages that one of their own has betrayed them. Unfortunately, mages tend to believe their own more than outsiders.

                          2. What would this police\investigative force look like? Roughly how many, what sort of equipment. Let's assume the evil Mage is fairly powerful and was wanted, perhaps they could also assume presence of powerful Spirits (plus several people have already died\been torn to shreds.
                          Assuming that the mages are aware of the evil mage, the first line would be the guardians of the veil (and order of the pentacle corresponding to a forsaken tribe). They are the secret police/intelligence service of the mages. They excel at tracking down people and are not to bad at killing them themselves. If however, he is too strong they would call for the admantine arrow, another order, which is effectively the military of the pentacle. Of course if the evil mage isn't hiding the arrow might be called from the start. If there is no time for planning, the the mages would just consist out of whoever the local consilium can dispatch.

                          Now as to what equipment they would have, that would depend on what they know of the evil mage. In contrast to werewolves the powers of mages are more variable. While every werewolf can transform into the same five shapes, different mages will have access to widely different spells. These spells are based on the 10 Acrana. For example the spirit acrana allows a mage to control/manipulate/attack spirits, while the death acrana allows him to deal with ghosts. A mage without those acrana is not much better equipped to deal with spirits and ghosts than a normal human, even if the mage in question is quite powerful in other ways, and can e.g. create matter from nothing.

                          So if the other mages are aware that their enemy has ghost and spirit allies they would try to send in mages specialised in these areas. On the other hand if they find out about this too late that would be a good reason for them to accept the help from werewolves (in general mages have quite a superiority complex re other supernaturals). Let's say the "police" sent is a mage from the admantine arrow specialised in controlling fire and lightning, then he would need help to deal with the spirits.

                          3. How would they fight if attacked by werewolves? Can they teleport easily? What does casting spells look like from outside, is it like Doctor Strange or Dresden Files? Words, gestures, glyphs, sparks?
                          They would most likely use mundane weapons, because there are only so many spells which deal direct damage. If they can they would try to call on the help of spirits/ghosts/goetia. Most mages won't be able to deal a lot of damage using their magic, so they would mostly use their magic to help them indirectly. e.g. they might slow down time around the werewolf, manipulate probability so the werewolf slips, weaken the ceiling above him and so on.
                          To provide an example of how much magic is focused on non-damage, a spell that deals bashing damage (not of much use against a werewolf) has the same requirements as a spell that can potentially reduce his speed to zero allowing the mage to escape.

                          Mages can teleport, but they need to know a specific acranum (space) to do so. Most mages won't have the required skill. Even if they have there are limits, e.g. they must have some connection to the place they teleport to - have been there, have something (e.g. a stone) from there and so on.

                          Casting a spell is essentially a purely mental act, so it is possible to do it without any signs. However, doing so generally helps the mage to cast more powerful magic. A mage can speak in high speech (the mage equivalent of the first tongue), he may perform certain gestures, he use a tool, or sacrifice something or he could draw runes. Please note that the tool in itself doesn't have to be magical. A mage trying to mess with fate could use a perfectly mundane coin or dice for help.

Doesn't seem hard to even make it as large and deep as a swimming pool, good luck getting out quickly after you are under 6 feet of stone/earth.

                                                          Does the matter have to be uniform, like you have to declare that all stone, or all steel etc. or could you just say "I turn the wall into liquid" "I turn the ground to gas"? Could you even turn all guns (steel) in an area to liquid, making them break instantly? This spell seems to me to be insanely useful/powerful.
                                                          It is a rather strong spell (and a good example how "normal" spells are often more harmful than "attack" spells) but there are some limits on it:

                                                          1. There is no indication that the density of the material would change. Since concrete or stone are denser than people they would swim on top of it. I don't know if you ever played around with mercury at school (since it is rather toxic) but if you throw pieces of light concrete on it the will swim on top. Likewise a werewolf would not sink to the ground of the liquid stone and be only partially submerged so he could break out of it thanks to his superior strength (a normal human on the other hand would be stuck). It would still be inconvenient and trap them until the succeed at a strength roll but wouldn't be outright lethal. Transforming the matter into gas would do the trick but that requires a reach which brings us to the second point:

                                                          2. The mage needs several reaches to use it in a combat situation:
                                                          - one for instant casting.
                                                          - one casting at sensory range (otherwise the ground the mage touches is also affected and he is trapped together with his victims).
                                                          - one reach to transform solids into gases.

                                                          Unless the mage has five dots in matter this will risk a paradox. If it is the first spell being cast the paradox has only two dices, but each paradox increases the dice pool for the next spell by one, so if the combat goes on it rapidly becomes lethally dangerous to cast a spell (as I/my character had to learn the hard way).
                                                          A mage with five dots could cast a spell like that without paradox, but then someone with five dots is supposed to be powerful.

                                                          3. The spell is withstood by the durability of the transformed material. This means that if the durability of the material is equal or larger than the potency of the spell it won't be transformed. Durability of stone/concrete is generally at 3.
                                                          If the mage has only three dots in matter then the spell has a potency of three which means that he cannot affect stuff with a durability of three. The mage can increase the potency by one but this reduces his dice pool by two dices. (At four dots in matter the base potency is 4 solving this problem - but again a character with four dots is meant to be strong, and a starting character will never have 4 dots.)

                                                          4. To affect a whole room, the mage needs to increase the area size, which levies another -2 to his dice pool (or use a reach which aggravates the praradox).

                                                          5. Thus casting the spell will likely have a -4 penalty. The spell casting roll is Gnosis+Acranum. A starting character has usually Gnosis 1 and at most Matter 3, giving a dice pool of 4 - which is eaten up by the penalties, leaving him with a chance die. He can improve his chances by including "Yantras" but doing so prolongs the casting giving his opponents time to react - and likely boost his dice pool only to 2-3 which still leaves a substantial chance of failing.

                                                          So yes the spell is a very powerful tool, but casting it in such a way is very difficult for a starting character and risks paradox.

                                                          Are there actually any other way for a Mage to detect people, lets say werewolves, if they enter a larger area? A ward of some kind for like a mile around?

                                                          Yes, but again there are limitations in practice.

                                                          1. The mage must expand the area of effect either through a reach (covering a house) or through dice penalties (-6 to cover a floor or -8 for a house) for it to be useful - if a werewolf is standing in the same room as you, you probably don't need magic to find them. To get a larger area the mage would need a reach and a dice penalty (to cover a house and garden probably reach and -4 penalty).
                                                          For starting characters the dice penalties to cover the area alone will make casting difficult.

                                                          2. The mage needs to either have a suspicion and use another reach for instant casting, e.g. before entering an abandoned building, detecting things for a few turns. Alternatively he could use a reach to extend the duration to at least one hour or longer and using the spell as a permanent safety feature against people sneaking into his house.

                                                          3. The spell will not extend into the shadow (although it probably could be made to do so by adding the spirit acranum), so if the werewolves use that to their advantage the can surprise the mage. Likewise since the spell does not register the spirit/flesh hybrid nature of the werewolf it won't recognise the urhan/urshul forms if set to detecting humanoids.

                                                          4. If the werewolf is using a supernatural ability to hide itself (e.g. the "Feet of mist" facet of the gift of evasion), the mage would have to roll gnosis+life acranum against primal urge+cunning renown, if the werewolf gets more successes the spell would not detect him. So an Irraka would have good chances to ambush a mage this way, especially since he would get the rote quality on his roll.
