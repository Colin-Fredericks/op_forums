    Different supernaturals just have different methods of ‘transcendence,’ so to speak. For mages it involves literally rewriting reality. For werewolves it involves hitting the limit and just keep on going. For Beasts, it involves incarnating your own mythos. For vampires, it involves waking up even after everything you once loved die. And so on.

    I’d argue that the following entities are on the tier that allow them to Clash Wills normally with archmages: Rank 6+ ephemerals, elder vampires from TYoN (those old enough to get their advanced Clan Curses), werewolves who became “boddhisattvas of the hunt” (Primal Urge 10 and beyond), Redeemed Prometheans, True Fae, Bound who reached their ‘endgames’ as teased in the Open Development blogs, mummies who reached Apotheosis, demons who fully unlocked their Ciphers, Beasts who attained Inheritance (not just Incarnation), and etc.

    Yes, some if not all of the above are pathetic in terms of power level. But everyone is when compared to archmages anyway, so I went for the ‘existential’ tiers.

                    Originally posted by falco1029 View Post

                    I tried to keep them at about the same level as 1e, I wasn't reinventing the wheel, as mentioned, it's a patch.

                    Regardless, Methuselah as we currently have them are weak and underexplored, but
                    1) I'd still call them just barely powerful enough to be able to, at least, Clash with an Archmage. The archmage is still likely much more powerful than them in the majority of ways. As Satchel pointed out, they DO have some minor 'break the template rules' abilities and they're said to be something on another level, so I think that they can Clash, even if i'd never call them (as they currently are) on the same power tier.
                    Edit: To put it simpler, Mages are far more powerful than Vampires, and Archmages are far more powerful than Methuselahs, but you'd still let a Vamp clash a mage and still let the Methuselah Clash the Archmage.
                    2) I tend to treat MEthuselahs as gods myself, and that tends to influence my homebrew 'fluff', I'll admit. I give them a new template, some inherent abilities, and free access to custom powers that further twist their template. I'm currently running a crossover game over skype/discord and the city's Methuselah is a force of nature that's just starting to wake up again as part of their current plot. Fun times.


                    On a separate note, I'm glad to see people making use of that document!
                    First off, I want to thank you for the effort you put into the Archmage Patch. It's a document that I deeply appreciate. Even if I don't see precisely eye-to-eye with you - overall it's groovy, and something I'll probably put to use.

                    As far as Methuselahs are concerned, I'll sum up my position as succinctly as I can. There are two major obstacles to calling Methuselah's a "Greater Being" -- one is mechanical, and one is thematic:

                    A) Mechanical: They have no access to Disciplines 6+
                    B) Thematic: Requiem specifically and intentionally avoids transcendent Vampires which are "godlike beings." (In my estimation, "godlike" = "greater being")

                    In every case of a "lesser being" becoming a "greater being" they experience a fundamental, relational shift to their reality. They abide by a new template, new rulesets, new relationships with themselves, with their Orders/Covenants/Political Groups, even with the Supernal (or equivalent) itself. Their change is dramatic, the scope of their influence hugely enlarged.

                    Methuselahs simply don't have a fundamental shift. They actually lose something, albeit what they lose is a weakness. This is in harmony with the theme of what the Vampires ascended form actually is: being Human once again. Their "Gondcala" (forgive the spelling mistake), their second Embrace, is the embrace of Humanity mixed with Vampire: a being that doesn't have the innate weaknesses of Vampire, but also retains their strengths. Methuselah is one step on the journey of becoming more like a Human once again, as they lose the weakness of memory loss / memory disruption, yet retain all their former abilities.

                    Mechanically, this isn't adding powers; it's taking away weakness. To climb the ladder, they don't add to the "good" so much as shed away the "bad." And it aligns perfectly with the theme of what it means for a Vampire, specifically a Vampire, to ascend.

                    Note that, again, the idea of a Cain-like supervampire, an "Archvampire" if you will, was present in Vampire: The Masquerade but noticably, intentionally absent in Requiem. This was fully intentional. What it MEANS to be "transcendent" to a vampire is fundamentally different to a Vampire than it is to a Mage.

                    I don't see why there is an arms race between Archmage and Methuselahs anyway. They can't, and don't need to be, equals. Nothing is truly equal; there is only more power, and less power. Vampires indisputably start with far more impressive powers and resilience than a 1 gnosis, 2 arcana mage. Mages do, however, outpace them in growth. It's a different game line with different themes and different powers.

                    If anything, I'd say that Vampires grow into something Other when they fall further and further into darkness; these beings are far stranger than Methuselahs, who in general wear the trappings of humanity. These Other beings are what the bloodlines are *becoming* - monsters that are as different from Vampires as Vampires are from humans. Gangrel seem to create the most of these strange beings, but all bloodlines have the capability. I'd argue that Mr. Three Bodies, the second example of Methuselah that Satchel mentioned, isn't a Methuselah at all -- unlike Zagreus, who is much like an Elder but without memory loss, Mr. Three Bodies is actually far stranger than a normal Vampire: he is what happens when a bloodline advanced past the known borders.
                    THIS is probably an example of a "Greater Being" as far as Vampires are concerned, except it's not greater in any sense but raw power. It's actually far more hopeless than a normal Vampire.
                    He even, if I remember correctly, referred to his former Vampiric state as being "once a man" -- as in, in comparison to what he is NOW, being a Vampire is being a Human. He's fallen that far.
                    Obviously having three bodies gives him a new template, so that's my version of a "Greater Being." Note that he is no longer a Vampire, or a Methuselah. He became Other.

                    Now, strictly from a mechanical point of view, this Other is probably going to cap out in power at about an Archmage at 6 dots. They are stronger than normal, and classify as a Greater Being, but they don't have nearly the access to the growth potential that an Archmage has, nor the reality-bending powers. They would have a wide degree of Disciplines they have mastered, plus some new template-exclusive powers similar to Legacy Attainments, varying depending on what bloodline they are. Disciplines 6+ do not add new abilities, but do strengthen the dice pool for skills 1-5 of each Discipline line.

                    One should always keep in mind that Vampires are a gameline focused on "Personal Horror and Visceral Drama." Greater Beings are overwhelmingly "tier 4" cosmic events in scope, and deal with lofty, philosophical, time-and-space adventuring drama. To create a Vampire that attains the same power-level as an Archmage or Rank 6+ spirit bucks against the very essence of what makes Vampire Vampire.

                    So while we disagree on the "level of being" that Methuselahs are (just to clarify, I still place Methuselah as firmly a lesser being, as they are still Vampires and not an advanced subtype) I can agree that even a theoretical Greater Being Vampire wouldn't hold a stick to a 7-10 dot Archmage. Not only would making that kind of Vampire be impossible with Disciplines 6+, but it would also destroy the theme of Vampire itself.

                    But it's your game. Do what thou will. But as far as canon goes, Disciplines 6+ don't exist, and Methuselahs seem to be, according to Rose, purely in the realm of "Elder Vampire" and not "Ascended, New Template Vampire."

                                    Since that is the case, we only have TYoN to go off, and therefore Methuselahs may not even exist. There are just Elders.

                                    What kinda baffles me is that many people look at Mage and envy their growth potential, but ignore the fact that Mages start out by far the weakest.
                                    Put a 1 gnosis, 2 life Mage vs a 1 harmony, 2 [whatever] werewolf in a cage, and see who wins. 99/100 times, it's going to be the werewolf. How is that balanced? It's not. Werewolves, just by virtue of being Werewolves, start off with hugely powerful, linear shapeshifting forms, and a slew of reflexive powers. They have so much raw power that they literally can tear a beginning mage or vapire to pieces. The tables turn when you dumb 500xp into each template, however. Mages start off the weakest, but end up the strongest.

                                    Where does Vampire fit in? They are the happy medium. While they don't start with the raw potency of Werewolf, they are nowhere near as frail as a Mage. Their growth is sharply linear, scaling well with experience. But they don't "cap off" as high as a Mage; they got part of their cake right from character creation, having innate Vampiric powers that put starting Mages to shame.

                                    Werewolf / Vampire // Mage

                                    Mage / Vampire // Werewolf

                                    The tables turn, slowly - and Vampires have the middle ground at all levels of play.

                                    If you want the growth potential of Mage, you should be prepared to give up the innate advantages of Vampire.

                                    But I'm digressing -- this is not World of Warcraft, where the primary engine and much of the fun comes from combat. Mage, Werewolf, and Vampire are games of roleplaying, not necessarily combat, and the fun is found in many avenues. All game lines are thematic and have unique situations. It happens that Mages are students of Supernal Magic, and Supernal Magic has virtually no limit; the limitation is found within the Mage herself. As the Mage grows in competency, her magic becomes strong and better. The growth potential of a Mage outstrips Werewolf and Vampire by definition; even as Werewolves and Vampires by definition have innate advantages that serve to empower them at all stages of the game, but especially early game. Giving them an advanced template is unnecessary, anti-thematic, and frankly doesn't fit with the overall schematic: Mages start off with nothing but magic, and end with all things magic, and that magic is essentially unlimited power -- Vampires and Werewolves can reach the limits of their respective power sources, but those power sources aren't as deep or potent as Supernal Magic. They are top-heavy, The Blood and Essence, giving you a nice fat slice of cake right off the bat, but their cake eventually runs out, whereas Mages get a thin slice to begin but are at an "all you can eat" buffet.

                                                Originally posted by 21C Hermit View Post
                                                As someone who doesn’t have the Changeling previews from the Kickstarter, how does the Hedge work in 2E? Manipulating its thorns and Trods would be an Entities spell, yes?
                                                Hedgespinning operates by performing mundane actions thematically related to your desired outcome at a penalty and spending Glamour to allocate excess successes among effects; the Hedge also shapes itself in response to stimuli, and "the Thorns" is essentially the common name for "the regions of the Hedge which are especially dangerous." Getting anywhere without following a trod calls for navigation rolls with a goal in mind, which is handled using a variant of the Chase mechanics, in which the Hedge itself is always a participant.
