    Reach as an alternative to dice penalties when casting?

    08-25-2022, 04:07 PM
    I'm considering this as a mechanics change in my Chronicle. Context: My players are extremely cautious. In the sessions we've had over the last several months, only one of them has risked Paradox during a single casting. That's how rarely they exceed free Reach. To mix things up, I'm considering letting them Reach for things like additional potency/duration rather than incurring penalties to their dice pool. So effectively, yes your chances of pulling off the spell aren't hindered, but your chances of Reaching beyond your safe range goes up in exchange for the extra oomph.

    Thoughts?

      Reach already does this? A Reach shifts a spell factor from basic to advanced, which is the difference between roughly one turn per point of duration or a near-exponential increase with a minimum of one scene or hour that reaches 24 hours at a single added point of duration.

      I'm not sure that players who are cautious about risking Paradox is a problem in the first place, but if you want to add a little more tension around the issue, I would just suggest running them up against rival sorcerers who have no quandaries about their reach exceeding their grasp. Put the squeeze on them, make them feel like they are making a sacrifice being so cautious with their spellcraft.

              You can change the primary factor from duration to Potency, although I tend to houserule that you can change choose either since being stuck with the one you originally chose sounds odd when using such a flexible system.

              There are tons of ways to increase Potency. A simple example is casting a spell on oneself. Get a canteen of water, use a swiss army knife to drop the subject's blood into their reflection (+2 persona yantra), chant in High Speech (+2 mantra), spend ritual time purifying the caster with the water (+2 cleansing yantra) and draw a rune using a temporary tattoo marker (+2 rune yantra). If they have time for it, they can get a +1 bonus that does not count as a yantra with each extra interval taken, up to a +5. All those use widely available mundane components.

              Beyond that they need to either make items that work as specialized yantras (using Prime and Fate to enhance their bonus), utilize Willpower (or increase the bonus with a Perfecting of Mind), Fate boons, create perfected metal yantras, develop a Shadow Name or Cabal Theme, have a ephemeral being with an Influence compatible with the spell use an Enhance Influence when casting it to provide a dice bonus, overcome the Trials of Supernal Summons to gain a specialized tool (tattoo, a tool of its magical symbolism/tradition, etc) or quest beyond the material for them, and many more.

              Mages are at their best when they have time to prepare, the same goes for their tools. Sure, you can probably wear down an investigation site's Opacity, but its much easier if you use your lucky hag stone inscribed with runes that the Acanthus prepared or the magnifying sheets with Prime enhancements the Obrimos made, perhaps it adds just enough for them to benefit from an exceptional success. Even if they don't want to invest Willpower dots, they can make unsafely relinquished ones with enough duration to not go out of control, or they can trade favors with a crafter, requisition from their Order, etc. The more they invest in this, the more well defined each character's signature style will become.

                                  Originally posted by KaiserAfini View Post
                                  In most cases that makes sense, but some spells, such as Impostor and Sleep of the Just, are duration primary. Which means that, by default, they cannot affect anyone and will likely be withstood unless the caster takes several turns to build it up via yantras, making them not the most practical options when first unlocked.
                                  The alternative being that you can make somebody stay awake or asleep or be perceived as somebody else for the whopping Duration of one turn without Reaching or adding factors, and in the event that you do bump those up, they do not have the insulation of having their Duration be the last factor to be reduced by Dissonance.

                                  Duration-primary spells are spells whose effects need to last more than they need to be able to overcome Withstand or provide a greater output. Potency-primary spells are typically the opposite. There is basically no spell that can be designed where one of those two things is not more important to the basic effect than its ability to affect a large number of people or a large area. Needing to take multiple turns to use Yantras is not an argument against this, because the default state of spellcasting takes at least ten minutes for the most powerful mages in the system.

                                          Also the mechanics are about choosing to risk small dice pool large spell factor spells (which either succeed with one success or fail completely) or less impressive spells which are more likely to succeed.

                                          Reach also gives you a trade off between safe free reach and paradox risk. But the paradox risk is fairly small given a) you can pay it down with mana, b) paradox success can be absorbed as bashing damage (15 minutes to heal, which is considerably less than the ritual casting time you might be using without the reach), c) paradox conditions aren't actually bad - they mostly make it more likely to gain more paradox, which is a paradox spiral, but you can just keep containing the paradox until your character sheet is filled with bashing and you collapse from the pain (you get to roll to avoid collapsing, but this looks great from a narrative point of view, low wisdom mages can essentially contain and ignore paradox conditions all day long...)
