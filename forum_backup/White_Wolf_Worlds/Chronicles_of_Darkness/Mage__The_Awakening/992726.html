    Hack: Going all out on Mage-centrism

    10-24-2016, 11:20 PM
    I've spent a lot of time trying to force Mage into crossover compatibility and balance, and while I still think that's worthy of doing, I'm beginning to think it would be at least a fun experiment to take things the other way, and posit a world where everything is Mage-centric.

    In this world:

    - The Supernal is the source of all magic, and all supernatural phenomena can ultimately be understood in Supernal terms.
    - The specific tone is focused less on archetypes of monstrosity and more on dark mystery, espionage/intrigue, and adventure.
    - All supernatural creatures are connected to the Supernal in some way. For example, the True Fae are there, but Arcadia is either definitely an Emanation realm, the same as Mage Arcadia, or something else.
    - Some of the other splats exist as minor templates that mages can possess, or as legacies. For example, vampires and changelings both exist, but they are much less powerful, as they lack Disciplines or Contracts. They can, however, Awaken as mages with some inherent powers and a tendency towards certain types of magic.

    Making mages even more flexible would be nice here as well, because if they're the only splat, then there's no problem of overlap, and they're free to cover all the ground they want.

    I admit it does have a fair bit of influence from Genius and Ascension. For example, Genius allowed you to be an Inspired Mane, and in Ascension you could be an artificial being. The problem with letting homunculi and androids awaken in Awakening, of course, is that you start getting thematic overlap with Promethean, and if you have mages with horns and green skin because they're actually physical embodiments of old superstitions you're treading on changeling's turf. But if we put it all in The World of Mana, we don't have to worry about that.

      Making Mage more flexible might be pretty hard actually. There's fairly little a Mage, given the right arcana, can't accomplish. The only thing I can think of is to make it so that instead of getting dots in arcana, you instead buy Practices.                   About the adjusting splats to minor templates, I'd be willing to put in the work to turn them into Merits or Legacies or something like that. Genius handles Manes as a Merit, so I'd go with something like that.

                  Just giving mages more power isn't what I'm looking for. I'm looking for a way to incorporate other supernatural beings into a game that is assumed to be entirely Mage-centric by default. And I fully admit that I'm just doing it because I think it would be cool to have mages that are ogres or goblins or androids.

                  For example, while the ultimate outcome is up to debate, I like the idea of doing away with Prometheans and instead having a wider category of magically-created beings. The Unbidden has an artificial human created by a Mage, so I'm talking about replacing Prometheans with various Mage-made artificial beings.

                  And yeah, this is aimed at Mage fans. With Mage, there's the urge to use it to explain all the other splats under that umbrella, but fans of those splats often take issue to that. I'm torn between the two: I think it's cool to make a whole coherent setting based entirely on the Supernal mythology, but I also love the other settings and don't want to overshadow them. This is meant to be a way to handle that, to create a codified Supernal-only setting to be Mage's playground. And while a lot of mages might see everything as Supernal, you kind of end up with the dilemma of saying either "no, they're arrogant dicks who're trying to force everything under their umbrella" or "yes, they're right, everything else is part of the Supernal". This is a way to employ the second possibility without having to overshadow the other lines.

                  And I don't think tone is that difficult to indicate. Mage already indicates a particular sort of tone, although there's plenty of room for change. All this would do is emphasize that, and emphasize how it differs from, say, Blue Book or any of the other lines, and how bringing everything under the Supernal umbrella changes things.

                  About "versatility", I was talking more about more options for atypical paths and mad science.

                      Originally posted by Gullinbursti View Post
                      I think most mages (and a number of Mage players ) already see the setting in these terms.
                      Most mages in the base game recognize that the vast majority of magical phenomena are several degrees removed from their slice of occult experience; most magic is Phenomenal in origin by virtue of the simple fact that it doesn't have to travel as far, metaphysically speaking.

                      I think the main thing to do if you're aiming to present the notion of the Supernal and/or the Abyss as the root of all things magical is to play up Verges, mess with the consequences of summoned Supernal entities staying past their deadline, and generally make ample use of visible buffers linking Supernal phenomena to Phenomenal specimens — there need not be straight-up vampire-Legacies for there to be connective tissue between Specters and vampirism when you can introduce proxy-vectors akin to the Strix.

                      Play up the expansiveness of the cosmology the Awakened are made aware of and the lengths to which pursuit of a Mystery can take them. Make use of the game's established threshold-beings to increase the proliferation of mages who've been subverted by some alien god or another, whether it be from poking around the wrong corners of the Anima Mundi or eating the wrong spirit-meat during an esoteric ritual in the Shadow.

                      Extend logically the implications of the tale of the Annunaki — Supernal magic is unclean power on its best behavior, putting on a pleasant face to keep the average observer from cottoning to how easily its destructive heritage comes to the fore, and there's so much that seems like it could be just outside the soap-bubble of coherent reality…

                                  Well, this is assuming a universe where mages are right, and all supernatural phenomena ultimately come from the Supernal or related parts of Mage cosmology. That doesn't mean that mages can't face reality-bending weirdness that defies explanation: with the Abyss and the Lower Depths, you have plenty of Weird Shit, and you can adapt other entities to fit the Mage cosmology while still leaving them massively dangerous and mysterious: the Gentry, the Idigam, the Machine, and so on could all be incorporated. And you could always say "this thing comes from another reality where the rules are completely different". There's plenty of room for things impossible to categorize and so on.

                                  There's also no longer a Mage supremacy issue or a crossover issue, because it's a self contained universe where Mage supremacy is the point, and other supernatural beings are less powerful but can learn Supernal magic.

                                  Does anyone have any ideas for how to incorporate major splats?

                                  Here's what I have so far:

                                  - Vampires don't have clans or Disciplines, but they have some basic powers and weaknesses and can Awaken.
                                  - Werewolves are a Legacy of Thyrsus mages who patrol the spirit world.
                                  - Prometheans are reinterpreted as the many kinds of artificial life a Mage could potentially create. These artificial beings can potentially Awaken.
                                  - Changelings are people kidnapped by the Gentry, who are explicitly Supernal entities similar in power and role to archmasters (and archmasters could theoretically abduct someone and turn them into a changeling). Changelings only get seeming and kith blessings (some of which are changed to provide bonuses to different types of magic), no contracts.
                                  - Sin-Eaters are a Legacy of Moros who returned from the verge of death by Awakening.
                                  - Mummies are a Legacy of liches, maybe? They use Supernal magic instead of their canon powers.
                                  - Deviants are handled via a Mythic Origin that represents the Mage being the result of some sort experiment that permanently altered them.
                                  - Beasts are, I don't know, Abyssal or Lower Depthsy?
                                  - The only Angels or Demons are Supernally-derived or lower depths entities.
