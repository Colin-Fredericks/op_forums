                        Playing the monkey paw/adversarial DM aside, its not an absolute.                                     I forgot to mention the choice isn't between killing or not killing the enemy. It is about KILLING the enemy, AND LOSING your family. Monsters can be unyielding and brutal.

                                    Either you let the enemy kill you and hopefully they honor their promise, or you kill them - knowing your family probably just died.

                                    It doesn't even have to be about your personal choices. It could be your reaction to the stupid shit people do around you.

                                    Another example? A person you've been pursuing for morally reprehensible crimes agrees to help you with something by giving you extremely pertinent information or helping you with a task that would let you deal with another more pressing matter - in exchange, they want you to vouch for them when their punishment is being decided so they don't suffer as badly. Let's say they can help you remove a deadly condition from someone you care about.

                                    Problem? One of your NPCs, or even one of your players, has suffered loss at the hands of this person you just made a deal with. Somehow, they end up killing your only lead before you get anything out of it.

                                    How do you respond? Is your character just going to eat this loss without any difficulty?

                                                    Originally posted by Malus View Post
                                                    If the Ghost Anchor is the very mistress you're proper screwed.
                                                    I think you misunderstand "destroy" Anchor. Anchor is a Condition on a noun (hypothetically on the mistress in this case) and the Condition can be destroyed without harming the mistress at all. That's 2nd Ed for ya.

                                                    I was thinking about my suggestion about making each Mana spent on Spell cause automatic successes for Paradox on top of normal Paradox rules for fostering Lovecraftian motifs (Unpredictable dangerous magic/"science" wielded by maniacs arrogant enough to meddle with forces beyond human fathoming), it also fosters Path specialization because all improvised magic outside of your Ruling Arcana are now sources of guaranteed Paradox, making Path and Legacy even more significant in defining. It also acts as a relative buff to Legacies that grant a third Ruling Arcanum (Relative because the third Ruling Arcanum provides some relief from the Hack). It also would mean that the relative mastery between having a Ruling Arcanum and not is way more significant than merely resource savings, it means having safe predictable spells only within your sphere unless you collect Rotes like crazy, which isn't encouraged enough in a setting that seems like it should be lore hungry. It also make Grimoires very important.

                                                    I like role specialization, more important Grimoires, meaningful commitments to who the character fundamentally is at creation, and people needing to lean upon each other to cover each other's weaknesses, and it all comes with the bonus of making 2/3 or more of Magic less sane and safe for most Mages. Also 1/5 to almost 1/3 of all Magic if you have a Legacy is not a bad slice of power potential at the drop of a hat and still makes them at least on part with other Major Templates. Also since particularly potent effects frequently require Mana it makes their use both tempting and frightening is a way that makes Monkey Paw a part of the mechanics not just a Storytelling style.

                                                    Also since this would be applied to all mages it doesn't affect the balance between mages except to make Scelesti terrifying.
