    Geckopirateship, I just use for my Gifted to work as Mage Template with my Instrumentalist Condition - it works really good. If you want to diverse more Gifted from Mages, just make them Pathless, add specific Instruments ( Yantras ) and let them be easier to pass their Wonders/Inventions under Dissonance. All that you need, really.

    Instrumentalist ( Persistent, Awakened only )
    You character, even if technically mage, does not see that her miracles are coming from her. Whatever if she is die hard scientists, seeing her magic as advanced technology, or dedicated priest, believing they are granted by her patrons – she cannot 'cast' any spell without utilizing appropriate Yantras. She cannot force herself to acknowledge that Supernal power comes from her and by this, is slave to tools and ceremonies. When character tries to cast without at least one Yantra in accordance with its worldview, spell is automatic Dramatic Failure – resolve it’s effects on character and environment in accordance.
    It’s very popular affliction in Nameless mages or some parts of Free Council. What’s even more disturbing, large parts of Banishers are under it.

    Possible Sources: Specific background. Wisdom loss.

    Resolution: Confrontation with reality of being mage. Dramatic Failure on spellcasting roll ( choose player and ST combined )

    Beat: Character gives a 'tribute' for higher force or analyze the 'scientific basis' of his miracles (Arcane). Character undermines the Truth about the origins of magic in other sorcerers.

        Another slightly more complicated one that might not quite match the math of the final game (I started my chronicle by adapting an early version of the rules because we were so eager to playtest it): The choice isn't to contain or release Paradox. Rather, a mage can either Contain or Control it.

        Containing deals one lethal damage for each Paradox die. Each success on the Wisdom roll downgrades one level of lethal to bashing or reduces bashing damage by one. This makes it much more difficult to contain more than a couple Paradox. (worth noting that we stripped out the increase to Paradox due to higher Gnosis, so like I said, the math might not work here).

        Controlling means pitting your spellcasting successes against the successes on the Paradox roll. If you score more successes than the Paradox dice, the spell works perfectly, as you have successfully maintained control over it. A large number of Paradox successes (5+) can still cause Anomalies and even Manifestations, but your spell is fine. If you score successes equal to the Paradox successes, the spell still happens as you envisioned, but it is considered relinquished unsafely (what mages in my world call a Lesser Havoc). If the Paradox successes exceed your spellcasting roll, usually nothing happens (spell factors get dropped to 0), but sometimes you end up with a Major Havoc - basically the "spell gets warped by the Abyss" effect we see in the Core.

        (Again, I'm not confident on the math, as our houseruled version doesn't increase Paradox based on Gnosis, the Paradox roll for Control is two dice per Reach beyond the mage's safe Grasp, and our spellcasting pools tend to be larger because spell factors don't reduce the spellcasting dice pool.)

        Also, Anomalies come in two flavors - Personal and Environmental. Environmental Anomalies are what you see in the Core. Personal Anomalies seek out a nearby "innocent bystander" (a Sleeper witness, if available, or a Sleepwalker or other minor template if there are no Sleeper witnesses). That victim suffers in some way related to the Arcanum of the spell - turned to stone, given a Destiny that turns her into a serial killer, etc. In the case of big Anomalies, the transformation is permanent or fatal - or else affects multiple victims.

        (We ditched Sleepers worsening Paradox - which I don't recommend unless you want to rewrite the setting, which relies pretty heavily on Quiescence being a thing - and this hack makes it so it's still a bad idea to cast magic in the presence of Sleeper witnesses in our game, but it's much more of a moral hazard, as killing an innocent person with a Personal Anomaly is a severe Wisdom sin).

        Our goal was to make it so that Containing a Paradox was safe for the environment but probably guaranteed to suck for you, while Controlling a Paradox was probably perfectly safe ("my spellcasting pool is much bigger than the Paradox pool") but the sheer number of times a mage makes spellcasting rolls means that statistically improbable events will eventually happen. The idea is to encourage the player to participate in the hubris of his character - run the calculations and decide whether he's willing to risk a Greater Havoc. Granted, we added a lot of new Reach effects to the spell list, so the other side of it is to make it totally worth an extra Reach or two to do something awesome that you should not be doing at your dot rating.
