                                Originally posted by solidork View Post
                                Why dont Time mages just win the lottery? Seers?

                                Suppose the cabal is confronted by a mystery. Is there (and should there) be anything preventing time mages from looking into the future where they have already solved the mystery and just short circuit the investigation process? I guess "Who will I discover the murderer to be?" could potentially finger an innocent person.
                                Time magic definitely makes things interesting. The lottery one definitely is a tricky one, because it is totally doable within the rules. That said, as you point out, it is also a way to out yourself. As someone who one a sizable ticket once, you need to show multiple ID's, fill out lots of paperwork, etc. (and I only won about $1k). Nevermind that large wins garner media attention, and staying under the radar just makes you more of a focus for the media. I always felt that doing something like this was a good story hook, as all those red tape and public spotlight things make the player stand out (and I bet both diamond and pentacle watch for such manipulations). It is likely the same for things like stock market, if not more so, since not only does exceptional lock in the market make you stand out as exceptional (and evidence shows luck does play a good bit in the market), you are messing with the economy... Which no order would want.

                                And the second one is easy: Paradox! As you pointed out, if you solve it by looking to the future to solve it, how did you first solve it? All glimpses of the future will change the present, but peeking to solve a mystery risks a huge conundrum: if current you is looking ahead to see the investigation and learn the answer, then no one would investigate... Which leads to the question of how you found out... In theory, if the police solve the case and you simply act on that future release of info, and never fill the cops in in the present, that could avoid this issue, but it means anything purely supernatural investigated solely by the canal will have some headaches.

                                Other reasons I have used is that the future is a nebulous mess that is trickier to pin down the more you look at it and the more troublesome of a question. Lotteries are going to be drawn at a set time and day, but the course of an investigation can lead to numerous turns and outcomes. For example, a player looking ahead could lead them to see the entire cabal dead, Mystery unsolved, which leads to both story hooks (how did they die, what can they do to avoid it) as well as keep the main story hidden. Or how far ahead do the mages know to look? "When I solve the case" isn't a time period you can know until you solve it.

                                Another option of it is a magic based mystery is Shields of Cronos and other magic meant to hide the future. It sounds cheap, but remember: the NPCs might be able to use magic too, and if a PC can think of a solution, so clike an NPC, and that means they would seem means to counter said solutions.

                                But your questions raise a good point, and the best answer whenever you have this is to:
                                -if a storyteller look for his to make such tricks lead to more questions and interesting story rather than shut it down. You should try to both create initial plots that aren't simple to solve (political, magical and catspaws all help) with a spell, and fun alternates if someone finds such a gap. Avoid railroading or shutting down creativity, but instead lead to each wrinkle a player creates lead to a new branch of the plot that lets you throw more into the story (like a Paradox Gulmoth summoned by manipulation of games of chance with Time or Fate magic...)
                                -if a player, not feel like the storyteller is shutting you down but trying to make a more interesting story. It is cool you can create an instant answer, but remember that as a group, that means the game is over if you remove all the challenge, and a ST who makes things not work out perfectly is just trying to create a good story (hopefully... Feel free to call them out if they are railroading all the fun out of it).

                                I hope that is somewhat helpful.

                                                            I'm trying to rob a house in 12 hours. No Shield of Chronos. Owner of the house casts a spell to check if his house is robbed in 12 hours, he sees me, he reacts. Future will change if his actions interact with mine. Or not.

                                                            I'm trying to rob a house in 12 hours. I'm planning on casting Shield of Chronos when I'm inside. Owner of the house casts a spell to check if his house is robbed in 12 hours. Still uncast Shield of Chronos fizzles his spell. He may act upon this knowledge to interfere with the future, for example by being at home when I planned to attack, and thus making me not cast Shield of Chronos at all.

                                                            From game perspective, this might seem like creating retroactive changes, because player's choice of using Shield of Chronos now might affect divinations that happened before player declared that action. So if your player didn't declare that he intends on using that spell beforehand, but remembers about it and casts it "when the time comes", you may have to retroactively change events that assumed his action was divined properly.
