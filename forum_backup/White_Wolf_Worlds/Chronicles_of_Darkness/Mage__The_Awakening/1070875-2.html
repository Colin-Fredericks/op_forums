                                It's not exactly what you are proposing but it might have ideas you can mine. Fantasy Flight Games put out a setting called Midnight that was a degree of post apocalypse caused when all souls were cut off from the afterlife, meaning zombie apocalypse and now we burn (or seal away) the dead.
                                ​There were two things that were specifically aimed at limiting mages in the setting.
                                ​The first, and I never actually bought the books so I can't remember what they were called, was a sort of hunting spirit that any use of magic (in your case probably Paradox) risked attracting. This spirit wasn't really that powerful but it couldn't be attacked or in any way effected directly. At the same time the only way it could effect the world was by possessing a weak willed host. The example I remember had a mage cast a spell in the wilderness, only to have that hawk lazily circling above suddenly come diving claws extended. He kills the hawk, only to have a chittering squirrel suddenly come leaping down from above, clawing at his face and eyes. He kills the squirrel, only to have a dear grazing in the next meadow come charging with antlers lowered. In the end the only thing he could do was kill its current host, then run and hope by the time it found another it had lost his trail. It could only see him through the eyes of host.

                                ​The second, thinking in terms of The Awakening, was that in the war the Seers and their Exarch patrons won. They then set up a theocracy modeled after the inquisition that hunted and executed anyone suspected of using magic. The Sleepers don't have to believe in magic, they just believe that they will be rewarded for turning in their neighbor (or strangers) while living in fear of the same being done to them. For Mages, if you accept being bound by the Iron Seals you can join the inquisition and live like royalty, but you do it knowing that at any time one of your rivals might succeed in turning that same inquisition on you (membership doesn't guarantee safety, not when politics are involved). If you don't join, then it is kill on sight and the rewards for helping are high.

                                        Now then, I think I've talked about the little flaws long enough, so lets get down to actually making this setting work. First and foremost you might want to decide exactly how mages fucked up so hard to destroy the world and to more importantly to what extent and why? How thorough was the destruction? Was it world wide? Were any sections of the globe spared to where modern conveniences still thrive? And what/who were the mages fighting against to cause such widespread destruction, because to be apocalyptic in nature this had to be some high tier shit.

                                        Was it mostly the fault of the Exarchs? Did they feel that they were being pushed back too much and decided to hit the reset button on humanities progress seeing that if they can't blind them with opulence they'd blind them with deprivation? Or maybe the Scelesti all finally got their shit together and started working on some sort of Celestial Ladder to the Abyss or something and the fight was to stop them from completing it? It would explain the prevalence of all the Abyssal stuff roaming the wasteland now.

                                        As for the actual hazards, a lot of your proposed solutions to making the dangers of the wastelands hard to deal with seemed to be by making them Abyssal in nature, of which I agree with. I would suggest that whatever you choose to be the exact cause of all of this to be able to justify the sheer amount of Abyssal stuff here. Anyways, lets start with corrupted Abyssal beasts roaming the wastes. The Abyss represents everything that isn't and can't be, which encompasses a vast amount of stuff. I feel that that should be embraced, and that the beasts that monstrosities that roam the countryside should have such a large amount of varying abilities between them that you're never sure what one is going to be capable of if you run across one. They should probably be composed of mostly animals (maybe buildings or even elements) corrupted and warped by the Abyss, with the occasional pure Abyssal creature thrown in to shake things up.

                                        Now lets talk about this Abyss tainted mana. I'm thinking it should function like a lapsed Paradox Condition, causing all of their spells to have a base Paradox chance of 1 die. I'd also allow mages to scour that mana away with like they scour other things. Which brings me to another aspect of corrupted Abyssal creatures I'd suggest, and that is that they rarely, if ever, actively attack those with traces of the Abyss inside them. Which means that most Sleepers would be relatively safe from them unless they actively attempt to provoke them. The same applies to mages with tainted mana in their systems. This leads to mages wanting to have at least 1 point of corrupted mana in them in order to avoid detection by the monsters, even though it causes all of their spells to cause Paradox.

                                        Another thing I was tossing around is to let the Abyssal creatures have low innate Sympathy through Sleepers. Their Abyssal nature resonating with the shard in a Sleepers soul, making it to where Sleepers have a strange lack of fear/aversion to the monstrosities around them. I'm leaning towards them still seeing them as they are, but just not being able to find anything wrong about their appearance. I'd go as far as to say that Sleepers even take the smaller more manageable ones as pets. There should also be some sort of unseen negative effect, because the Abyss is pretty much never benign to anyone but I haven't quite thought of what yet. I've got a few more ideas but I'll get at those sometime later.
