                    Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post
                    See, the problem with that is that is makes the Supernal into too much of a defined place for me, and it also steps on the toes of the assorted other lines. The Shadow is where predator symbols eat prey symbols, it's the Shadow's hat. The Underworld is where the dead go. To give another example, if Supernal Arcadia is literally just a dimension with elves and stuff, that makes Phenomenal Arcadia kind of redundant, plus the unfortunate "the Supernal world is the Fallen world but better and purer" idea, which I really don't think 2e is supporting much.

                    To go more into the narrative purpose argument, and away from Watsonianism, the problem with the Supernal being a place where "predator symbols eat prey symbols" from a worldbuilding and storytelling standpoint is that the game already has a perfectly fine and fleshed out place where embodiments of predation eat embodiments of prey, and it has the bonus of being a place that mages can travel too fairly easily that you can easily work into campaigns and adventures.

                    The Watsonian argument against it would what I mentioned earlier, about how the Supernal is a formless sea of potential without the Phenomenal to shape it.
                    The Shadow is a dim reflection of the truth of the Primal Wild. The Underworld is a dim reflection of Stygia. Fallen Arcadia is a dim reflection of Arcadia.

                    The storytelling purpose of it is perfect - the Shadow isn't a virtuous cycle. The Underworld is a shithole. Fallen Arcadia is a monstrous trap.

                    All of these things are indicative of the way the world is broken, something that's fundamental to the Gnostic theme of Mage. If you feel like this puts the Splat on a higher level of primacy than the others - knda tough? You're allowed to rewrite it of course, but that's on you.

                    We don't allow mages to cast spells, since this is the most unbalancing rule of all.

                                                The idealized Shadow is not the Primal Wild; it is a more virtuous Shadow, free from the cold dog-eat-dog violence of the Raptor and the blind catastrophe of the Nemesis; a virtuous, self-sustaining, ever-improving cycle but ultimately still the Shadow, not the Primal Wild.

                                                The Primal Wild contains these ideals, these possibilities... But it also contains the Raptor and Nemesis. It is also not a place. It is not the place Awakened should aspire to and seek to escape to or bring here; it is the sum of the symbols that make reality and tell the Awakened what is possible.

                                                In other words, the Supernal is not an idealized version of the Fallen World; it merely contains the symbolic possibility of a world that is not Fallen.
