                Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post

                Putting aside how needlessly dismissive "cheap wank-off fantasy", you can get immortality in ChroD for the cost of a single willpower dot if you can find a vampire willing to make the trade. In Ascension, immortality was a two-dot merit. I get that Awakening isn't either of those, but some of the points people have made are two-way streets. Immortality is unlikely to have a gameplay effect and few mages live to die of old age anyway. Those things given, I'm not why immortality shouldn't be cheap. And this isn't even about it not being cheap- it's about how Dave has explicitly said that he's opposed to the idea and the game is structured to oppose it.

                Dave has explicitly said in other places that he's against anyone being able to become immortal and that Mage is anti-transhumanist. I would obviously ignore that if I were to play it, but I'm sure you can see how disheartening it is for me to know that one of the leading designers holds those beliefs.
                I can't speak for Ascension. Ascension had different gaming principles to Awakening. The 90's were a weird time.

                In regards to how cheap immortality is, I can do you one cheaper and point at Mummy, where you not only live forever but also never really have to run the risk of dying due to destruction.

                I'm sure Vampire and Mummy are exactly what people fighting over in this thread, a prime example of Immortality without costs that make you more of a monster. Cutting the sarcasm and going for an aside here, both of those don't just give you immortality just for the sake of having and going "Woo hoo" with it, it makes us reveal things about ourselves with it.

                But yeah, no, the larger context is not "I just want immortality", it's "I want immortality with no strings attached", and that's honestly just flat escapism, and that's boring to me. I play games to have conflicts, not to just exercise power all willy-nilly. I am interested in prices paid and people changed. Having Immortality as an Obsession and trying to fulfill it without falling into the pitfalls of making light of a thing I desire? That sounds way cooler to me.

                Putting Dave aside for a second here, my point is that Mage is a game of the human will and the consequences of it's conquest against reality as it is. Immortality, on this stage,is more interesting and fun as a fight, a thing to achieve. The game still has options for immortality that are, at the very least, morally/ethically neutral. Ascension is the grandest immortality of all, and while there's mixed feelings on that one, it's not, say, the Tremere. Hell, Ascension is still mostly treated as desirable in Awakening. And that's just one thing. You might disagree with Dave's attitude and how that has structured Mage, but all the same, there is a fun conflict available. Immortality can still be sought. It can theoretically be obtained barring ST fiat. And thus, the quest for a long held dream of humanity is a good stage for a game. It's fun, it's conflict.

                Now, I'm not going to speak for Dave or about Dave. That said, I know I am pretty widely regarded as, what's the word, a deathist? Yeah, one of them. Now, by and large my context on that has always been "Personal dislike of it aside*, if I could get immortality, then I have to assume other people can too, and that just sounds like a great way to break society"-by and large, I just don't think humanity as a species is mature enough to handle having immortality. We don't even have equal pay between genders or the ability to go a presidency with a black guy without calling him a nigger, yeah, society could only get more fucked up at this point if we had the ability to make people immortal. Once the human family grows up some? Sure, we can talk about it, but for now? Nah, not worth the shit. Cyberpunk, worth remembering.

                And, personally, I see that Mage works in accordance to that idea. Hell, even what it says about immortality as a negative fits into that principle-while immortality itself might be a problem, the larger problem is the lack of wisdom and maturity in the people who seek it, a lack of wisdom and maturity that the species as a whole is prone to. Mage's main "moral thrust" is that a person owns themselves, that their Power is firmly kept in the hand of their Will rather than relying on more ephemeral supports provided by Lies we tell ourselves(and man oh man is Mage a game about Lying to our Selves) or accept from beyond us.

                Dave has sharpened the anti-transhumanist points in Mage(They were always there). I get that, I respect his opinion, and I actually share them to some degree. But I think there's very clearly room in Mage to fight that notion if someone so desired. So, fuck any of us who disagrees. Play a game of Mage where you obtain immortality, and you do not make of it a light thing, but instead show with power and maturity it's worth. Pay for it with conflict and drama and growing understanding and bright defiance.

                It's not like Promethean, where you will gut out far more of the game trying to play against intentions. Mage may not be thrilled about it, but it has the room for that story. It's there for you to tell it. It is there for you to Will it, if you are Wise enough to do so.

                This, by the way, has been why I've mostly ignored this thread. I don't see the conflict. I can tell that story. It's not barred to me. But maybe that's just me.

                *I'm not willing to argue about this point, but suffice it to say, one of the more important things I've heard is "There is always such a thing as too much living" and I have been around too many people who I feel have proven the point for me to greatly personally desire endless life. Fight to extend the lifespan, sure, some people don't live enough, but endless life? Nah. I can feel that there will be that time when I have lived all I need to.

                        Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post

                        Very helpful.

                        With all due respect, you're basically complaining that Awakening does not positively portray or allow transhuman or similar values, and the developer admits as much. The problem is that no one, whether it be OPP, Dave, or anyone else, ever promised or suggested this modern horror game would give you what you want.

                        We get that you want a modern wizard game that includes elements like easy immortality. Your are entitled to your tastes and preferences, but no rpg game suits everyone. The canon setting and themes of Mage are apparently not what you want. You can either change them at your table or not play at all. Heck, if you believe the market demands a transhuman modern horror magic game, you are free to write it yourself. However, repeatedly complaining that a game setting than a great many very much enjoy is somehow bad or deficient or must officially change to suit your own tastes is presumptuous and unproductive.

                        Simply, what you want or expect from Mage in many ways appears incompatible with the setting as fundamentally designed and intended. Rather than complain about what you don't like (and attacking the developer for not giving it to you), start threads requesting and discussing alternative settings or hacks to achieve what you like. Threads such as this are always popping-up in all the particular game forums, and results in far more amicable and constructive dialogue.
