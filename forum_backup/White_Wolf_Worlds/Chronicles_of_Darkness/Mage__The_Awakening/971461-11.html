        Comming back to the original question and ignoring, that there has been a history of planting these questions and ignoring the productive advice for it I would like to add the following:

        Gecko you should be more specific on what you actually want to change. Mostly if you want mechanical changes or philosophical changes.

        The mechanics of Mage:tA are in my opinion well suited for what you want. If you like, you can probably tweek them here and there, like you suggested for stronger paradox, but I think these are minor problems and should probably be discussed in separat thread.

        For the philosophical differences about if Mage is actually a horror game or not, I strongly suggest to read "The Night Watch" books from Sergei Lukyanenko. While also intresting in the depiction of magic and overlapping different worlds, I think the greatest contribution of the books is the constant struggle between having near ultimate power, but still beeing utterly unable to change the world for something good with it. Mages too are not almighty superheroes. They are constraint by morals and by politics and if you don't submit at least partially to those, you aren't free, you are most likely dead. Anyway, read the books, they are far better, than anything I could argue here.

                    Originally posted by HardcoreHannes View Post
                    They are constraint by morals and by politics and if you don't submit at least partially to those, you aren't free, you are most likely dead.
                    I think I agree with this. I personally prioritize the introspective horror of mage in my games--"what does it say about me that I so freely x, y, z," and so on. But I think my disconnect with Gecko's claims is that I see plenty in the book I could use for more extrospective and/or overt horror. I'm not saying the following is your issue, Gecko, but I think a lot of people (myself when I started out) think they have to use everything in the book. Obviously, in any game (including DnD), you can do whatever the fuck you want, but in CofD, you're encouraged to. If you don't think the Seers are scary, I agree with the posters saying to scrap them and create a new enemy or use Banishers.

                    Fun fact, I don't think I've even told Dave this--I have never, ever run a Mage game where the Seers were the main villains. Usually, my main villains are other Pentacle mages with contrasting goals, and it's a game of sabotage with ultimate power.
