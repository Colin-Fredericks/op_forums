    Proximi Dynasty: Teioku (庭憶, "The Garden of Remembrance")

    10-07-2026, 02:48 PM
    Nickname: Shions (紫苑, the amaranth flower, meaning "I won't forget you" in the language of flowers).

    Description: Memories are precious treasures that remain as part of us when we are separated from those we love. In honoring those bonds lies the calling of the Teioku—tenders of recollection and messengers of reminiscence. For countless generations, this lineage has comforted the living and the dead, ensuring those who have passed are not forgotten.

    Heirs to a tradition of capturing cherished experiences in art, they are sent forth into the world to help ghosts find closure. Each milestone of their journey celebrates the memories they forge, even as they harbor a desperate longing to be remembered themselves.

    Curse: The shions are bound to share the fear of being forgotten as much as those they are charged with remembering, clinging desperately to an anchor of their identity. Without it, they begin to fade from the world, and it is only acts of remembrance that can bring them to walk amongst the living once more.

    Persistent: The shions are unable to leave permanent records of their lives. Digital databases throw fatal errors when their names are typed, and security camera recordings capture them only as a blurry, out-of-focus smudge. If someone writes their name down, the ink fades away or dries up into dust within minutes.

    Only a single, ritually dedicated item from the Material Realm is capable of recording their identity at a time, serving as their anchor. Should a shion travel further than a kilometer away from their anchor, they lose Willpower at a rate of one point per scene. If the anchor is destroyed, the shion can dedicate another material object via a scene-long ritual, which involves placing a physical record of their existence upon it, and should it already have one, it now becomes clear.

    Beat: Whenever a shion is inconvenienced or gets into trouble due to having no personally identifiable information, they take a Beat.

    Severe Curse: If they run out of Willpower out of range of their anchor or if they suffer a Paradox, they fade away and become ghostly, trapped in Death Twilight. Should anyone say their name or perform a ritual of remembrance within sensory range of their anchor, the shion may roll Resolve + Presence in a contested action against that person's Stamina + Resolve + Supernal Tolerance. Success allows the shion to drain a single point of Willpower from the target, regaining their physical body. Should the shion find themselves trapped in this ghostly form without an anchor, they are violently pulled down towards the Great Below.

    Appearance: In the eyes of the outside world, the shions are a ghost story. They have no birth certificates, no bank accounts, no deeds to an ancestral home, not even a family registry, existing in the spaces between society. Within their family, memory is sacred, and elders pass down their family history through oral tradition and written allegory, keeping the embers of remembrance burning bright despite the curse.

    Younger members tend to behave boldly and dress in vivid colors, striving to make themselves unforgettable.

    The anchor of a shion is chosen with great care and holds sentimental value. It normally has instructions detailing who should inherit it, and often the location of a stash of keepsakes, so their loved ones will have something to remember them by. Shions normally have agreed delivery addresses for each other, so that they never lose contact with one another.

    Path: Moros

    Blessings of Death: Forensic Gaze, Ghost Gate, Speak with the Dead, Suppress Aura, Touch of the Grave, Without a Trace

    Blessings of Matter: Craftsman's Eye, Find the Balance, Hidden Hoard, Machine Invisibility, Nigredo and Albedo

    Blessings of Space: Break Boundary, Co-Location, Correspondence, Locate Object, Scrying

    Oblations: Creating keepsakes (scrapbooks, journals, memory boxes, handwritten letters, etc), making a eulogy, tending a memory garden, reminiscing with others, sending a care package.

    What were you thinking design wise ?

    The main theme of the Teioku is “cherishing memories”. The Curse serves two purposes. The first is pushing them to want to make good memories with the people they care about, making sure that they make experiences that will be remembered and retold even after their lifetime. The second goal is to make them empathize with ghosts that feel melancholy about being forgotten, driving them to have personal, emotional stakes in making sure these ghosts and their loved ones get closure.

    The anchor itself as a nexus of reminiscence draws a parallel to the ghosts themselves, and similar to them, they cannot venture too far from it before starting to feel drained. Having only one anchor makes receiving one or being trusted with one by a shion feel like a strong moment of trust or a particularly meaningful item to remember them by.

    When they start to forget themselves or when they suffer a Paradox, they become like ghosts and depend on being remembered in order to persist. Should the person who says their name or performs the ritual of remembrance not know about how they restore themselves, either because they do not know the shion or because they were not trusted with the knowledge, then they do not know to fail the contested action, mechanically forcing the shion to struggle as they try to be remembered. The roll is based on the mechanics of the Drain Numen and the ritual itself is inspired by how Hyōsube Ichibe'e restores himself in Bleach. Should they be without Anchors, they are dragged to the Underworld like a ghost, and someone who remembers them must take an item to be made into a new anchor and navigate the underworld to find them before it's too late, showing care and remembrance. A Beat is awarded should they play to the challenges of their Curse.

    The appearances section is meant to portray a family that deeply cares about their members, history and others, reflecting it in their traditions. The younger members tend to do everything they can to fight off the Curse, whereas more experienced ones focus more on those they care about. It also highlights the downside of being a ghost in the system, which can lead to interesting answers as one ponders how they deal with it and what challenges they face.

    The Blessings reflect their ability to wield their anonymity, as well as gaining the understanding and finding the connections needed to provide closure to the dead. Once they have done so, it helps them find and navigate safely to any keepsakes related to said ghost. The crafting based spells aid them in restoring and completing these items for those they were meant for.

    Lastly, the oblations are meant to bring the comfort of reminiscence to the living, of both the people in their lives and those who passed, ensuring they have plenty of memories to treasure.

    That concludes the design notes, hope you had as much fun reading them as I had writing them.
