                                I don't think it's possible to use Temporal Sympathy that way. It's a connection between a subject and its own past that measures how much the two has changed. Borrowing it doesn't make sense because either you borrow both ends, you'll just end up with a connection to your past self. If that past self doesn't exist (because you didn't exist yet) you suddenly have a connection to nothing and that doesn't seem usable. Or you're borrowing only one end, in which case you have a connection to someone else's past and I don't see how you can resolve that in a satisfactory way. Could you use that to affect someone else's past to cast spells on yourself? If you cast Corridors of Time on yourself, would you end up in the body of someone else? Not only does that seem like something you shouldn't be able to do with just Time, it also breaks the explicit limitation of not being able to be sent back in time before your own lifetime.

                                A much simpler way to create a grandfather paradox that follows the rules is to just send your grandfather back in time using Corridors of Time to mess up that initial meeting with your grandmother. If he's not prepared to be flung many decades into the past, he's bound to have a major freak out and do something very different than he did in the original timeline. (Just make sure to cast Constant Presence on yourself before you do it unless you want an unnecessarily convoluted and retroactive suicide.)

                                The way to prevent the ST from being swamped down with rewriting the timeline of the entire campaign is the simple social contract of don't do unnecessary stuff to create a shit ton of work for your co-players. That and a ton of mages (and higher beings) having a vested interest in not allowing that sort of behaviour.
