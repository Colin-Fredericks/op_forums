    Two Questions About the Spirit Arcanum

    11-20-2017, 11:19 AM
    Two (hopefully quick!) requests for clarification on MtAw 2nd Edition Spirit rules:

    1) Can the Spirit Arcanum directly affect Resonance Keywords?

    "Resonance" doesn't appear in the list of purviews for the Spirit Arcanum [p.179], and the only listed Spirit spells relating to Resonance target Manifestation Conditions in spite of Resonance, rather than affecting Resonance Keywords directly [see, e.g., "Opener of the Way" and "Erode Resonance", p. 181]. Resonance is explicitly within the purview of Prime [p. 165], but the only Prime spells that influence Resonance Keywords are restricted to Nodes and Hallows [see, e.g., "Geomancy", p. 169]. This suggests to me that Prime's purview deals with magically-sourced Resonance only, and not the broader definition of Resonance that governs the Influences of ephemeral entities.

    Muddying the waters further is the write-up for the Resonant Condition [p. 259], which states that "the Spirit and Mind Arcana can instill this Condition by manipulating Resonance, emotions, and thoughts." Given that the Resonant Condition means nothing more than "the subject of this Condition is within the sphere of Influence of a spirit or Goetia", this implies that Spirit magic can directly influence Resonance Keywords (with Mind manipulating the "emotions and thoughts" part). And the lack of mention of Prime is further evidence that the Resonance in Prime's purview is treated somehow differently than the Resonance on which the Resonant Manifestation Condition depends.

    So, would it be possible for a Spirit mage to directly impose a "Life" Resonance Keyword on a person/place/thing, thereby attracting spirits of that type and benefiting from the impact of their Influences?

    2) What is the range of the Spirit 3 spell "Spirit Summons"?

    I find the wording of the spell to be confusing: "The mage sends out a call to the nearest spirit within her sensory range. Conversely she can summon spirits she knows personally. She may send a general call and the nearest spirit will answer, or she can specify the type of spirit by Resonance." [p. 182].

    First, it clearly dictates "sensory range" as the range of the spell. But the next sentence begins with "conversely", suggesting to me that it describes an alternative to the first sentence, rather than a continuation. Then the third sentence repeats the idea of "nearest spirit", but again seems to offer an alternative (i.e. specifying a type of spirit by Resonance).

    So, is "sensory range" the range in all cases, or are there exceptions for personal knowledge or specifying Resonance?

    If it's always sensory range, then doesn't this make Spirit Summons rather limiting? After all, if the spirit is already in sensory range... why do I have to summon it? Can't I just cast Command Spirit on it right away---a lower-level spell that, in addition to letting me summon the spirit (with a command), also lets me give it a bunch of additional commands of greater complexity than those permitted by spending Reach on Spirit Summons?

    Thanks in advance for your help!

    Amateur dinosaur hunter and extreme weather enthusiast, whose interests include spoken mime, armchair parkour, conspicuous ninjutsu and Schröedinger's pentameter—of which this sentence may or may not be an example.

      What a mess.

      I think you are correct that Prime should affect Supernal resonance and Spirit should affect Spirit-themed resonance.

      As far as the range of Spirit Summons: I think it should be Sensory range regardless of what you are summoning. You either use it to summon:

      a) The nearest spirit
      b) The nearest spirit with "X" Resonance
      c) A spirit you know

      Sensory range is fairly far and it's possible to extend the range with Space.

      I think the limitation encourages Shamans to have knowledge of their local Spirit ecology to be effective. It keeps Spirit from being a catch-all tool that just lets you summon whatever you want wherever you want. It's still super powerful, but requires more care and study.

      I generally allow Spirit mages to make a case for why a particular spirit should be in the area and as long as they make a decent case I usually allow it.

          There is no difference between Prime and Spirit Resonance. Prime manipulates Resonance directly, but other arcana manipulate it via reverse engineering. Spirit for instance can slap a spirit into an area where it wouldn’t normally be able to comfortably exist, it can then exert its Influences to make the environment more amenable to it.

                          It should probably have stayed on the other side, unless other factors play into it. Spirits are perfectly capable of feeding from resonance from the shadow, escaping into the real world gives them more agency to create suitable resonances (and respite from being hunten by other spirits), so a spirit who has allready found a stable ecological nieche is less likely to feel pressured to cross over. Generally I treat Shadows crossing over as reasonably rare, as doing so poses a significant risk (namely essence bleed and upset werewolves) which can ultimately lead to its destruction. Main motivations for crossing over are unsated hunger, dangerous pursuit and other stranger occurences.

                          tl;dr

                          It makes sense to me if the Gaffling is infringing upon that delicious pain resonance from the real world because there's a jaggling there hugging the spot in the shadow that would eat it if it tried to infringe upon its territory otherwise.
