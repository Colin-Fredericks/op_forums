    Originally posted by Dark Archon View Post
    I do think there are canonical examples of that going, but even if not - this makes sense.
    It makes sense, up until you encounter a piece of setting writing that is incompatible with your assumptions, at which point the setting is wrong and your assumptions are correct?

    Originally posted by Dark Archon
    I don't forget this, but for me it's simple - the benefit of having permanent enchantments on yourself and Sanctums far, far outweighs any inconveniences you have to go through to achieve that. So you use Sacrificial Relinquishment (or worse analogues, if you have the stomach to do it), or you relinquish unsafely with as little Reach as practical and periodically clean up the spells which start to show damage to recast them, or you suck it up and burn some EXP.
    That's a bit contrary to design intentions, but even if your own priorities would find the spells preferable, a mage only has so many Willpower points to give up (or other things to sacrifice). And constantly returning to spells is a bit time inefficient.

    As far as Sleepwalkers go, that helps, but creates a bit of a liability in somebody that might be vulnerable to being targeted to disrupt the spell.

    House rules are probably not something that setting writers could account for.

    Originally posted by Dark Archon
    Where there is will, there is a way. Willworkers do not lack will, by definition.
    Then why not apply this to the will of the Tremere to go underground, particularly since that might be a much more proactive intention than preventing the Tremere from obscuring the manner in which they went underground?
