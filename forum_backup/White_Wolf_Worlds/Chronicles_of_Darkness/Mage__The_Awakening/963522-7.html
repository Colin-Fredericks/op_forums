        First, as a general matter, I'm certainly not claiming lawyers or the practice of law is somehow inherently virtuous. There are good lawyers and bad, travesties of justice and outcomes that most would consider both procedurally and substantially fair. Every lawyer and case should be judged on their own merits. I simply take issue with attorneys as the "go to" examples of greed or corruption. Without singling you out, as you obviously meant no offense, it's a lazy stereotype that belittles the hard work, dedication, professionalism, and competence of an entire profession that includes people who hold the entire spectrum of political, social, economic and other views.

        However, as another poster observed with my original comment, the examples you offered about the some well publicized aspects of the practice of law, such as criminal defense attorneys or expensive corporate counsel, are also not inherently corrupt, greedy, unethical, immoral, etc. As you now observe, however, it certainly does raise issues of competing ethics and morality such as individual outcomes versus availability and fairness of process (i.e., the adversarial system)..
