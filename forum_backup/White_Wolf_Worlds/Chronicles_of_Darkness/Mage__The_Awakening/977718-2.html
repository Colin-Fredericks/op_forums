    Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post

    So, you consider the horror in vampire and werewolf to be "forced"? Then there's a quandary here, since I love both of those games, and how their mechanics and setting emphasize the monstrosity of the protagonists. That's what I think Mage should have done, and that's what I'm trying to do. I actually think Mage needs more of that than any of the other games, because it has farther to go to make the characters undeniably monstrous. Vampires, werewolves, etc are instantly recognizable as monsters, but mages don't have that immediate association with monstrosity. They're the most human splat, too, and I always disliked how the most human splat was the most powerful. To be honest, I think of the player base who wants to play Mage "as written" and pushed back against my suggestions the same way I think of the people who play vampions-style. There's nothing wrong with it per se, it's just not what I look for in ChroD. And to be honest, people who look at Mage and go "this shouldn't have mad scientists in it" are not people I have much interest in playing with or discussing things with, because we have very different perceptions of horror fiction and want very different things out of the game. I personally think that people who don't want mad science in Mage are wrong as objectively as it's possible to be wrong about something like this, and if I can persuade them, that's fine, but if I can't, well, they're welcome to it.

    Again, all of this is off topic from actual build advice.

    Again again, I still have no idea how substantial my hack is going to be, because half of you are saying what I want is already there and half of you are saying that I'm going for a radical departure.
    Let me put it like this. I feel that pretty much any bad thing a vampire or werewolf does under the influence of such mechanics to be less monstrous than the same or similar acts done while not under their influence. Werewolves and vampires don't have a choice, and aren't completely responsible for their actions in those states. It's like being forced to take mind altering drugs by an outside source. You didn't choose to take the drugs, and aren't in control of the actions you commit while under their influence. Murdering someone in that state is, imo, less horrific than cold calculated murder maliciously planned out over some perceived grievance committed by whoever it is you're targeting.

    As for views on what does and doesn't fit in Mage, to be honest, officially, what you think doesn't really mean a thing. You are not, and have little to no influence over, the people who write and develop the game and decide what it is. So again, thinking they're wrong about something doesn't really mean squat. Now don't feel too bad about that because most people are in the exact same boat. Now at your table specifically, you have liberty to change things as much as you want, but even then you have to worry about your players willingness to accept such changes.

    And no, I really don't think anyone is really disagreeing that most (not going to say all without re-reviewing everything you've said first) of what you want is already there. It's what you want to do to force it that most people seem to be disagreeing with. It is currently there, but it isn't forced, which is how it is meant to be. No, there's nothing forcing it, but as you've been told many times, that's the point. If you want to emphasize these already present aspects without changing anything mechanically, or really even setting-wise, you need to do that on an ST level. Push those themes more to the forefront with how you present whatever story it is that you attempt to tell. Make sure your players are ready and willing to cater as heavily to the specific themes you want them to, and then just play the game.

        See, we disagree on the nature of "forced". I don't think that having mechanics that emphasize the monstrosity of the protagonists. Unknown Armies has, in addition to the most detailed sanity meter of any tabletop game, restrictions and costs for each school of magic that pretty much force any practicioner of it to be, at the very least, weird and somewhat distanced from othera, and a barely functional raving madman at higher levels of power. I don't consider this bad game design or forcing. The themes of the game explicitly spell out that it's about broken people trying to change the world, and that magic will destroy you. Having mechanics that support these themes is not railroading or bad game design. It's the same as an action movie based rpg that rewards players for doing over the top stunts- it uses game mechanics to support its theme. Generic systems like GURPs are there if you don't want that, but ChroD is a system that's designed to tell a specific type of story, and each game system is meant to communicate the idea of monstrosity in some way. If I wanted a game about people with superpowers who could potentially do horrible things with them, I'd play any number of other RPGs. I play ChroD because I like exploring horror archetypes, not just having magic powers.

        Another thing: it's very easy to argue the opposite of what you're saying. Many people find stories in which the protagonists must fight to overcome their own flaws and condition to be very compelling. To turn your statement around, murdering someone under the influence of a supernatural power is less evil than planning it out...but resisting the urge to do evil even when there are forces pressing for you to give into it is more impressive than not doing evil when you have no real incentive for doing it. I prefer that ChroD protagonists be, on some level, broken people and monsters. Vampire wouldn't be anywhere near as interesting if there was no Beast and no need to drink blood, because then you'd just be a superhero. Someone on the forum actually has a signature that even says something like "is it better to be born good or to overcome one's evil nature?" or something like that. My preferences regarding Mage are things that I think the game should have done in the first place, for the reasons I've explained. If you like Mage completely as it is, I'm sorry, but I guess we just aren't going to see eye to eye.

        As I've said, I dislike the "they're monsters because they could do bad things with their powers" trope. You can disagree, but no matter how many times you repeat it it won't win me over.

                Honestly, I feel that Mage handles temptation to do wrong better than any other line in ChroD for a few very simple reasons. One, how human Mages are. They aren't like Demons, whose thought processes, morality, and everything else that makes them them is so completely different than your average joe. That makes them so much more relatable. You can intimately understand their wants, needs, and the temptations that they face. Another is that, as I've mentioned, they're actually tempted. They aren't forced to do one thing or another because the mechanics say they're mind controlled into having to do so. There's no, you black out and do all the bad things I tried to tempt you into doing.
