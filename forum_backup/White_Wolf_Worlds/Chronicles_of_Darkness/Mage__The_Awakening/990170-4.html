        Hello.

        If I may go back a little (sorry, I was gone for a while because of work), I would like to rekindle the discussion about mages manipulating the building blocs of reality and that making them stronger than anyone else, according to some people.

        My understanding of the problem is more akin to the difference between physics and ingeniering. Theoretical physicists may manipulate something more fundamental but it takes time, efforts and money to turn their work into something useful. Ingenier may work with some grosser aspect of reality but it is easier to turn it into something which can have an "effect" in day to day lives, they'll just attain a lesser level of understanding of the finest points of how it works. In the end, they manipulate the same laws but not in the same way and if each one can manage to produce the results of the other it will take an insane amount of time and efforts for it to work out. Mages explaining that because they manipulate it unfiltered and at a more fundamental level, they are better and more powerful than, say, the werewolves is like Sheldon Cooper telling Howard Wollowitz that, even though he's MIT-trained and has been an astronaut, he's inferior to him. And that is fitting to a game of hubris.

                                Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post
                                Apologies since this has been discussed before, but I'm trying to get a handle on this, since I've been rereading Changeling. So, 2e has done a lot to sort of cut through the confusion on the realms, and specifying that the Arcadias aren't the same.
                                I actually play with this quite a lot, despite it being explicitly disavowed now.
                                In order to heighten certain aspects of drama and storytelling, I've had Arcadian Awakenings encounter changelings and vice versa. Once I had a mage come back with a partial Mien because they made deals to survive there. There's still a demarcation between the Supernal and the Fallen here, but it's not a clear one, and that ambiguity is a Mystery in and of itself.

                                So the short version is that the Supernal Realms both are and are not places. Imperial Mysteries hits on this a bit - the only way a lower-order being can perceive the Supernal is through a Lustrum, which is to say their soul struggles to perceive the symbols it's being bombarded with into a relatable format.

                                You might say that the Supernal therefore is a place, but its geography isn't based on what we would consider geography, it's a relational process of symbolism, similar to but far more permanent and real than dreams. The Arcana of Space, Time, Matter, Life, and Forces all still exist in each realm, even if one is heightened over another, and there is nothing less real about a rock encountered in the Supernal and a rock encountered in the Fallen World (indeed, that rock is far more real for being what it is, a reflection of symbolic interactions.) Two people can perceive the same rock on different Lustrums, and it is still the same rock, but their perception of it will differ. Its physical and temporal relation to its environment will change, too, and so will the environment.

                                Presumably the Ascended all have a much purer view of it.

                                The Supernal still influences things on the Fallen World, because everything on the Fallen World is a dim reflection of the truer concepts of the Supernal Realms. Before the World was Fallen, it presumably had more of that majesty, with the Subtle Arcana more fully expressed and visible and with Supernal Beings able to jaunt on down/up.

                                Part of the reason why this is so confusing and weird is that you are a Fallen Being, more or less. Presumably you don't have access to divine magic! You have no capacity to directly perceive the universe in the way that an Ascended Being does

                                The reason for the primacy of Mage (and something I feel nWoD does so much better than oWoD) is that mages reflect divine first causes. All other forms of magic are fundamentally "natural", in a sense. They're consequences of Fallen laws, even if some of those are occult laws that are beyond the sight of mortal beings (and even if those Fallen causes have Supernal antecedents - it's mentioned quite clearly that Werewolves and Vampires and other major templates all have Supernal backing at some level.) What makes a Mage special is their ability to draw down that Supernal, divine radiance to change the world, a true Alchemy in the sense of Hermes Trismegristis, who (or at least, the author claiming to be him) stated in the Emerald Tablet "As Above, So Below." He was essentially referring to the idea that a true magician makes a change in the divine realms that is then reflected here on the physical, and, arguably, vice versa (this especially is the argument of the Free Council, who posit that the Supernal is changed in response to Fallen action as well as vice versa.

                                We don't allow mages to cast spells, since this is the most unbalancing rule of all.

                                        Originally posted by Dave Brookshaw View Post
                                        There's some things in this thread that are... Incorrect. Putting it charitably, and I just know Gecko's going to ignore what the books say in favour of what someone on a thread once told her, even if they didn't know what they were talking about, so, in no particular order:

                                        Beasts have a much easier time navigating the astral than mages do, strictly by mechanics. Where the Primordial Dream is is not in any way mysterious: It's the area around the Omphalos at the far end of Temenos.

                                        Only mages and beings with Integrity have an Onerios. Only those and Beasts have a personal connection to the Temenos. This is because those realms are the inside of a human soul, and nothing else has one. Other supenatural beings like demons can get powers to let them enter someone *else's* soul, or have a beast pull them into the temenos with its powers.

                                        The world isn't Fallen because it's not the Supernal - there was a world before the Fall - but because it's damaged by the Abyss.

                                        The Supernal Realm is not a physical place, and destroys anything that tries to treat it like one.

                                        Other supernatural beings have Supernal symbols over them when viewed in Mage Sight, but then so does *everything*. Mages don't believe vampires are linked to Stygia just because they show up in Death Sight.
                                        Ok, so the Supernal Realms aren't a physical place. Sure, I can get that a realm made up of the symbols that order reality isn't a place. But what the heck does it mean, then, for summoned beings to exist? Or for someone to ascend into the supernal realms and still be alive? Or for the Exarchs to exist as beings with will? I guess what I'm asking is how the heck can you have a living symbol? I can't really wrap my mind around that one.
