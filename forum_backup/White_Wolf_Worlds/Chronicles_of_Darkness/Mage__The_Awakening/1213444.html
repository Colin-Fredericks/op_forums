    Arcanist's Mage Homebrew Thread

    04-30-2018, 11:44 AM
    So I have been doing a lot of thinking about Mage, how it functions, and ways that it could better evoke cultural and folkloric ideas of magic and those who practice it. There's also a couple of things about how certain subsystems, like Summoning Supernal Beings, Mage Sight, and making Imbued Items, function that just kind of rub me the wrong way. I kind of like how, for the most part, 2E has moved away from making the number of successes on a roll not dramatically affect most actions outside of Extended Actions or things like Chases or Combat. So I've been tweaking and poking at the rules to see how, if at all, they could be made a little more elegant.

    Summoning Supernal Beings is one of the things that kind of bugged me the most, if only because it didn't feel right. Ritual actions in Mage 2E, especially for Attainments, tend to be rather precise and calculated. Summonings of this style in folklore and various occult traditions in the real world are procedures undertaken with a lot of thought and a lot of research beforehand. They depend on preparation and correct action instead of mostly random chance. I've altered the rules ever so slightly (and tweaked Supernal Beings a little to match) to better fit that feeling.

    Truth Manifest- Supernal Beings

    Normally, the Supernal World doesn’t interfere with the Fallen one; it simply rests in the background, a Tapestry of symbols backlit by light casting shadows on the walls of Plato’s cave. In this way, it acts a lot like a phase of Twilight, one that the Awakened gain unique insight to with their Sight. Normally, those symbols are inert, but Mages are creatures of symbolism and semiotics at heart, and they can wake them into action. Supernal Beings exist naturally in Aedes and Emanations, where the symbolism of the Supernal World is manifest, or as the Bound, fallen god-monsters imprisoned, trapped, or conscripted to guard Ruins of the Time Before, but can be summoned (some might argue created) by Disciples of the Arcana in accordance with the Gnosis of their Path.
    • Attributes: Supernal Beings follow the normal rules for Ephemeral Beings
    • Manifest Truth: Summoned Supernal Beings do not possess Manifestations, and are normally Materialized, but may spend a point of Mana to conceal themselves within the Supernal World (where they are visible to Focused Mage Sight alone) or use spells to attune themselves to phases of Twilight. They can simply cancel the spell or spend another point of Mana to Materialize again.
    • Arcana: Instead of Influences and Numina, Supernal Beings possess two Arcana with dots equal to Rank, behaving otherwise as Mages when it comes to free Spell Factors and Attainments, but rolling Power + Finesse for spellcasting, and using Rank instead of Gnosis for things like Combined Spells and Yantras. Supernal Beings have unlimited Paradox-free Reach to use on spells and can activate Reach abilities at will so long as the spell remains under their control, but must spend Mana to use spells accordingly if they use an Arcanum that is not one of the Ruling Arcana of the Path they represent, or spells that break the limits of the Lie.
    • Boons: Every Supernal Being possesses a Boon, a unique ability related to either their Primary or Secondary Arcanum. They gain an additional Boon per dot of Rank over three (IE. A Rank 5 Angel has three Boons) and can sacrifice dots of their Arcana for more.
    • Thaumavore: Supernal Beings can only normally replenish Mana using spells, but they can also devour the Mana in an object or being’s Pattern, gaining Mana equal to the Size of an animal, all Mana stored in an object, or Health x3 in the case of a human (what a Mage would get Scouring them to death). Awakened are tempting targets for the Bound because they also gain any Mana in the Mage’s Pattern as well as another point of Mana per dot of Gnosis. They spend a point of Mana per day to remain active in the Fallen World and regain Mana equal to their Rank per day in an Emanation or Aedes. They also bleed a point of Mana per scene outside an area Resonant with their symbolism.
    • Other Traits: Supernal Beings possess a Virtue and Vice, but they need not be anything a human would recognize as such and they are fundamentally coloured by the Realm the Supernal Being is attuned to. Additionally, the Supernal Being possesses a Ban and a Bane. Common Bans for Supernal Beings are Trials that render them metaphysically incapable of assisting those who haven’t passed them, or obligating the Supernal Being to aid those who pass the Trial.
    Modified Attainment- Targeted Summoning

    Supernal Beings are magic made manifest, and even the most pitiful of them knows more about magic than most Awakened could ever hope to learn. Summoning their aid, then, is something to be desired, but it is dangerous and risky, something reserved only for those who truly dedicate themselves to the Mysteries of their Path. The Summoning is an Attainment that can only use free Reach and without free Spell Factors. The Summoning takes a scene, and the Scale Factor helps determine the radius of the summoning circle, the “safe zone” for the Supernal Being, while Potency is used against the Withstand of the Supernal Being's Rank. It costs a point of Mana to use the Attainment, and an additional point of Mana to specify the second Arcanum of the Supernal Being (provided you have another Arcanum at three dots). Summoning Mages may use any suitable Yantras and can benefit from the aid of other Awakened, but suffer a penalty equal to the Gnosis of the highest-Gnosis Mage of another Path present or participating in the ritual.

    Note- Home Address: A Mage summoning a Supernal Being known to dwell in a particular Aedes or Emanation gains a +4 Environmental Yantra bonus to the summoning if the Summoning is performed in the area of the Aedes or an Emanation's Iris. If they have completely understood the Aedes’ Mystery, they gain an additional point of Reach for the summoning.

    Note- Abyssal Intrusion: In a contested roll against the Attainment is a base dice pool of the participants’ Gnosis-derived Paradox pools, with additional dice based on the Abyssal taint and Paradox Conditions that all participating Mages are suffering from. Add a +1 if any Sleepers witness the Summoning, with the 9-again quality for a small group and the 8-again quality for a crowd. If the Paradox roll succeeds against the Summoning roll, then an Abyssal Being of equivalent Rank is summoned instead. Mana can be spent by participating Mages to reduce the Paradox pool.

    Note- Fallen and Bound: While Supernal Beings are powerful, they are vulnerable in the Fallen World. Within the summoning circle they are safe, but outside of the circle they suffer a point of Resistant Lethal Damage per scene, or reduce an Attribute by one as if it had been Scoured. The same thing occurs if a Mage suffers a Paradox in their vicinity one-to-one for Paradox successes, and the same for Dissonance caused by Sleepers. When the Duration of the Attainment runs out, if the Supernal Being isn’t within the circle then they treat the entire Fallen World as a Bane until it escapes or dies. If the Supernal Being is killed outside of the summoning circle, or is destroyed by deliberate magical attack, then they fall to the Abyss, destroyed in an obvious fashion. When killed within the circle, it returns to the Supernal World or its home Emanation. If the Reach and Mana has been dedicated to make the Attainment Indefinite, then the Supernal Being becomes one of the Bound. If one of the Bound is killed within the confines of the Scale Factor of the summoning, it simply reforms in the area, waking when exposed to the presence of Mana.

              That is correct, though I seem to have botched the writing on it. Obviously Scale and Duration are the two most important Spell Factors when using a summoning. I don't think it makes the Bound exceptionally more common, just gives them more places to come from apart from the Time Before. I like the idea of collapsing pocket dimensions, the crumbling Sancta of Masters, and the forgotten strongholds of the Orders being haunted by ornery, and more importantly, hungry, Supernal god-monsters. As it stands, the Attainment doesn't receive any free Spell Factors, and gets only three Reach maximum without Imperial magic, so making a Supernal Being one of the Bound still requires a -10 penalty to the roll, two Reach, and at least two Mana.

              HerbertIsBestBert As it stands, Summoning still isn't a spell, but rather a spell-like Attainment. A Demense is still a Yantra. Resonant tools are still Yantras. Ultimately, it's still the same, it just reduces the rolling to a minimum. Personally, without free Spell Factors I feel that it doesn't become any easier. Let's say that you have a bare minimum Mage looking to summon a Supernal Being. Without Yantras, you're rolling only four dice. If you're using the two best Yantras available, you've found an Aedes and an extremely rare otherworldly Sacrament to use as an offering (and bear in mind you can only use a given Sacrament once because it is used up in the roll), so now you're up to eleven dice. If you push yourself to the absolute limit, you have maybe five Spell Factors and a single Reach to use. To make the Summons even remotely useful, that Reach will have to be spent on Advanced Duration. If you don't extend Duration, or expand Scale, (and if you don't, then you've only got a Scene and the Supernal Being is standing in a circle that's only an arm's reach wide) then you, theoretically, could summon a Rank 5 Supernal Being.

              Again, you would need significant access to mystical resources to do so, at least a scene of prep, and only a scene of time with the Supernal Being. This also doesn't account for the Supernal Being's Ban, which is quite likely its Trial. If you go into it without prep, then you will, at best, have completely wasted your resources. At worst? You may have summoned an extraordinarily powerful Abyssal Being, and possibly destroyed a manifestation of the Supernal, which will be hell on your Wisdom and your reputation with the Awakened community.

                          Anywhere, here's my Imbued Item stuff. It's rough, thrown together in about half an hour, but for now, I like it.

                          Imbued Item (• to •••••)
                          Effect: An Imbued Item is an object enchanted by an Adept of Prime. The spell costs whatever Mana it normally would, and at least one Mana even if it normally would be costless, and calculates Reach as though the spell was cast at the minimum Arcana rating. The Imbued Item can store Mana equal to its dot rating, which it replenishes either through other spells, or at a rate of one point per scene when stored in the presence of a Hallow Resonant with the spell. Imbued Items roll their dot rating as the spellcasting dice pool. Awakened users can spend the item’s Mana instead of their own to mitigate Paradox, but only up to the limit imposed by their Gnosis. Paradoxes from an Imbued Item cannot be contained, instead automatically releasing. If an Imbued Item includes an Arcanum Attainment, increase its dot rating by one. Imbued Items can include multiple spells so long as it doesn’t exceed five dots. Mages may use suitable Yantras up to their Gnosis-derived limits.

                          Modified Attainment- Imbue Item
                          The mage can infuse an item with magic, creating an Imbued Item. The mage can imbue an item with any spell she is capable of casting. Imbued items retain their magic indefinitely; a spell cast using an imbued item might run out, but the imbued item itself does not. Attainments may only be included if they modify a spell; for example, mages can imbue items with spells using Sympathetic Range, but may not imbue an item with Mage Armor or the ability to create Rotes. The spell imbued into an item remains in the item’s creator’s spell control, even if the item only casts that spell when activated. If an imbued item casts its spell multiple times, however, it still only takes up one spell control “slot.” Most mages relinquish spells after they have imbued them, but if the mage retains control over an item’s spell, she may alter its spell factors when the item casts it, and even cancel it as though she cast it herself. By spending Mana equal to the item’s rating, a mage can destroy an imbued item she retains spell control over, no matter where it is.

                          System
                          Cost: Mana equal to the Imbued Item’s dot rating Dice Pool: Gnosis + Prime - dot rating Action: Special; as Equipment Building

                          Dramatic Failure: The item is imbued with magical energy, but it is cursed. The object has a different effect, and a different trigger, than the caster was intending. Perhaps worst of all, the item also possesses the enchanter’s Signature Nimbus.
                          Failure: The Mage successfully imbues the item, but the magic is unstable. The item gains the Fragile or Volatile Condition.
                          Success: The Mage imbues the item and it functions as intended. The enchanter decides if the item has a persistent spell on it, or if it is activated
                          Exceptional Success: The item receives an unexpected benefit (such as the 9-again quality to the spellcasting roll, or the benefits of being a Praxis). Alternatively, the enchanting Mage may relinquish the Imbued Item from their spell control without a point or dot of Willpower.

                          EDIT: It just occurs to me that I have no idea what relinquishing an Imbued Item unsafely even does. Would the Imbued Item's enchantment eventually unravel? What would extra Reach do for an Imbued Item that can be activated? Give the Wielder a pool of Reach to use? I would guess that with persistent spells it would only activate Reach options or impose Abyssal Conditions.
