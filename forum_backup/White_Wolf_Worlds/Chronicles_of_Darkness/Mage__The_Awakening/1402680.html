    Lex Magica, and Time Travel

    07-13-2020, 05:16 PM
    Greetings,

    What is the Lex Magica position on crimes committed and unmade with time travel?

    Let say Jack commits a crime (no petty theft, something seriously bad: murder or rape of sleepers, soul-eating) and uses Shifting Sands with reach to go back a couple of hours before anything happened. The Consilium finds out (mind-reading, post cognition; doesn't matter how, the thing is they find out); Jack's defense is that nothing actually happened, whatever he did was undone when he used Shifting Sands.

    Will the Consilium condemn him? What are the charges? There are no victims since that timeline did not come to pass, but he reaped the benefits.

      Generally speaking, this is going to fall into the "who has jurisdiction?" bin.
      The two orders that have the most interest in such a figure as I see it are:

      The Silver Ladder, because Jack is screwing up his soul, even if he's figured out how to make his crimes victimless.(And one could play with eternality of souls and have a mystery spring up from memories transmitted outside of time.)
      The Guardians of the Veil tend to police mages, in general. They would probably just monitor the guy and, if he breaks a law, drop the axe.

      In any Pentacle order are individuals who'd not mind or defend the acts and those who'd condemn it. Legally, though, it's likely fair game.

              It's might be violation of the Precept of Hubris regardless.                                                       That's a circular argument because it assumes that Wisdom is a measure of "common" morality. It's not though.

                                                      Wisdom reflects how careful a mage is with supernal magic. It only declines due reckless actions (acts of hubris) that involve magic or supernal phenomenon. The key factors are (1) how likely the action is to have consequences that the mage didn't intend or can't control and that could bite her in the ass, and (2) how severe those consequences might be.

                                                      Note that use of legacy attainments never provoke degeneration checks. It's because legacy attainments aren't supernal magic -- they're intrinsic ("template") powers of a mage similar to Vampire Disciplines. That's also why they require two pages of text to outline all their many exceptions to the normal rules for supernal spells. The point here is that degeneration checks occur because of the use of supernal magic, not because of mundane actions, no matter how "evil."

                                                      Also note that in the Wisdom system a premeditated,cold-blooded murder is a less severe example offense than a murder undertaken in a fit of rage. In a court of law it would be the opposite, yeah? Crime of passion and all? But for a mage, the calculated murder is less likely to have consequences the mage didn't intend and so it's considered less egregious.

                                                      High Wisdom mages risk degeneration from any unnecessary use of magic because using magic at all is inherently risky and prone to unforeseen consequences despite the best laid plans of mice and men.

                                                      To compare Wisdom to Humanity from Vampire: Wisdom reflects the battle between Man and Magic, while Humanity reflects the battle between Man and The Beast. Vampires retain Humanity by resisting the urges of the The Beast, and Mages retain Wisdom by resisting the urge to use magic indiscriminately/impulsively.

                                                      The bottom line is: Don't interpret Wisdom as some good/evil or nice/mean spectrum. It's not. It's an alien code of behavior for beings who have transcended humanity.
