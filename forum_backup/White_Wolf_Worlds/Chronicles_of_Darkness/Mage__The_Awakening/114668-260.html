                                        Originally posted by Flinty View Post

                                        If Hermium essentially solves a magical alloy as it solves a perfected metal it would destroy the alloy doing so, I'd think. Either way it wouldn't facilitate the Mana transport from Sophis to Thaumium.
                                        I appreciate the attempted assist, and some of your proposed solutions have been thought provoking, but I'm just not following you.

                                        Amalgams
                                        Hermium is capable of forming an amalgam with any of the other perfected metals. The resulting amalgams retain the Supernal properties of both of the combined metals.
                                        The entire point of hermium amalgams is that one retains all special effects of the other metals included in the amalgam. Nothing loses its special properties or abilities when added to one. That is one of the two primary distinctions between amalgams and magically forged alloys. If you make an amalgam of metal x,y,z you get a new object that has all of the effects of x,y,z. If you make a spell-forged alloy, you get a new object that loses effects x,y,z and gains effect 123.

                                        If it doesn't work with alloys, that's one thing, but it does not follow that the material that explicitly combines metals while preserving their effects would combine magically fused alloys and destroy their effects. The special effect of Thaumium is the warding, the special effect of Sophis is the scavenging.

                                        If they can be added to an amalgam, those effects would necessarily be preserved in the resulting amalgam because that is what an amalgam does.

                                        tl;dr Mostly I just want to know the arguments for or against adding an alloy to an amalgam, as I don't believe conversation around what the effects would be if one were to succeed is going to be productive.
