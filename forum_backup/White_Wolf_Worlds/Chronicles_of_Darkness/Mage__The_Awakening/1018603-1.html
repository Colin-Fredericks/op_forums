                I think that there's something else rather important that needs to be touched on. Well, Axelgear did touch on it, but it was in a manner that wasn't really expanded. Functional hierarchy or not*, one of the biggest things that being a Seer gets is actually better Mysteries at easy access. There are the Profane Urim, yes. There's the ochema as well. Its part of the draw of joining the Seers - easy access to powerful mysteries to study. Hells, the Exarchs themselves are a Mystery to study in the first place. And that doesn't touch on the orders that are handed down - each is an individual mystery that is divined and fulfills Obsessions.

                To put it simply, the Seers get handed easy power that indulges all their power fantasies with little effort. Not only do you get more toys to play with in the form of free Artifacts and specialized Supernal allies, but each and every one of those toys helps fulfill your Obsessions and grants you Arcane Beats. You are basically getting two kinds of power boosts for every gift, and at a low cost. Well, until your Exarch asks for something complex, but by then you're hooked into the life, willingly go out to fulfill your Obsession and get more Arcane experience points, then come back, get rewarded with more mysteries to look at, and get even more Arcane xp. Its a virtuous cycle that just keeps building. You have the mysteries, you grow more powerful, letting you acomplish more. You get an order now achievable, follow it, getting more mysteries that make you more powerful, which lets you get even more complex orders to acomplish.

                The sheer amount of magical resources that Seers have access to can't be denied. They grant an overwhelmingly huge advantage that mere numbers can't quite make up. Its the difference between being born the heir to a company, and trying to build a company from the ground up. Both are going to be in charge of a company, but clearly the former has a big advantage and more likely to be able to do more in the long run.

                * I think its important to remember that the Seers aren't a monolithic Order. In fact, calling them an Order is misleading in the first place - they're a series of Ministries that happen to all be called Seers. Its like saying the Diamond in reference to the SL, GotV, AA and Mys. Each Exarch has its own agenda that isn't necessarily united with other Exarchs. When the General says "Jump," every Praetorean in the world will jump, sure, but no one in the Paternoster will. Hells, the Exarchs might not even talk. The Father could be the God-Machine, the Raptor could be the Dark Mother, the Eye could be an Asceneded archmage from Time Before, Helios could be the Nemesis, etc. Each concerned with only their own sphere of interest and staying out of each other's ways.

                            Wait, I actually have a point that hasn't been brought up (sees MCN's post)...aaaw. Well what about (sees Caladriu's post) damnit!

                            Ok, (double checks thread again) I think I actually got something to meaningfully contribute this time. And it's basically let's not forget about the possible origins for the Seers from To the Strongest in Dark Eras, or Neolithic Mage for that reason.

                            Because the Exarchs are no more real than Atlantis is, which is to say that just like the Time Before has Supernal resonance with the idea of Atlantis thanks to the Diamond Orders, the Supernal Symbols for why reality is Fallen have resonance with the idea of the Exarchs because of the Seers. And that's because the Seers and the Diamond have similar origins, and that they're both really not that old in the grand scheme of things since they both supposedly came about during the Bronze Age. In the Neithic Age there was no concept of the Supernal, the Time Before, and definitely the Exarchs, and in the Bronze Age the closes concept that existed comparable to the Exarchs amongst the Great Cults (the Arcadian Mysteties specifically) was the Olympians (the symbols that improsoned the primordial shapes on Tartaris to usurp and rule their creation it their stead, and both maintain the world to their capricious whims while also ensuring the world isn't returned to primordial chaos should the "Titans" ever break free.) And in time the Olympions would be reimagined as the Exarchs because the Arcadian Mysyeries would eventually split into the Seers and the Ladder, but the actual term Exarch probably didn't come about until the Ladder first started crafting their Atlantean shadow name.

                            And yet, the Supernal truth about what the Exarchs represent have always existed in the universe that exists now in the Chronicles of Darkness. Whether it's brutal forces of nature that are both mysterious and malicious that try to scour the Awakened as they try to break through to the Supernal, or capricious gods that delight in the entertainment of their mortal creations while demanding supplication and love despite their cruelty, to the forms were most familiar with: tyranical rules of reality from the Time Before who oppress mortal souls in an attempt to rule squash any chance of an awakened rebellion. No matter what form They take, there will always be foolish mortals and Mages who will try to use Them as a source of quick and easy power. Because They are powerful, and They do represent everything that keeps humanity from being something better than they are now.

                            (P.S. Another fun thing to remember is that the Seers and the Diamond have something of a sinister relationship because they do share a groups shadow name. The Diamond needs servants of the Exarchs to rebel against to restore reality, and the Seers need rebels to squash an reaffirm their absolute rule. If at any time this relationship breaks down the Orders become in serious trouble, and that's why the Seers and Diamond have allied themselves with eachother in the past. Because they need eachother.)

                                            Maybe? Only in the broadest sense? The thing to remember about Seers is that, as a whole, they're not driven by some desire to rule the world. They're driven by personal Obsessions and are just as Addicted to Mysteries as the next mage. Their main goal is to study things, to experiment, to mess with magic like every other Awakened. They end up actually ruling the world because that's what the Exarchs need to keep existing as Supernal symbols, and thus demand of their followers. Those who obey get their Obsessions and addictions fulfilled with ease, and free access to power as a result. The Exarchs stay in power for whatever reason (see below), and the lackies get their desires met. Its a symbiotic relationship.

                                            Now, in 2e, we don't know what the Exarchs are. The Psychopomp could be a Rank 10 Keroberus, a really ancient vampire, a death-spirit, or one of the Judges as easily as an ascended Death mage for all we know. And, to be frank, the answer probably will change depending on the needs of individual chronicles. What's important to note here is that, depending on which of those things the Exarch in question actually is, what the Exarch will demand of its followers will change. A Death Spirit will need its followers to do death-y things to feed it Essence. The keroberus will need essence from Memento Mori as much as it will need its Deep Laws fulfilled. And so on. Psychopomp-Seers will be executing missions that relate to fulfilling these death-based needs of their master. I mentioned earlier that, perhaps, the Father could be the God-Machine: the G-M does want control of the world, but only because its easier to build Infrastructure and fulfill its ultimate Mission, whatever that is. Meanwhile, ascended mages in the form of Supernal Symbols will likely have spells they want to cast, and each arch-level spell will require changing the world in different ways; again, a process that is expediated by control of humanity.

                                            Seers aren't vampires. They don't want control for its own sake or to fulfilll a psychological need (at least for the majority). Instead, they want these systems of control in place because the magic of the Exarchs / archmasters and the Seers all depend on these individual systems in some way. Its a means to acomplish an end. And that's why I say that the goals are different - even if we go with the Exarchs-are-all-former-mages idea, the spells cast and power attained are for different magical reasons. Everything with mages always, always, always comes back to magic in some way. But the thing with Supernal magic is that its so varied, and uses so many different angles, that there's lots of directions to take with it.

                                            And that's not to say there's not overlap - as many know, church and state work horrifyingly well together, and often overlap on using the same people. Normally, this would lead to a power struggle over a limited resource; however, because this resource (people) can be used in different ways without consuming them or used in conflicting ways, there's no reason to fight over the resource, but instead to share it, reinforcing a hold at a fraction of the effort.


                                            Anyways, the point is that tyranny or causing suffering is closer to a Yantra than anything else. A tool, not a goal.
