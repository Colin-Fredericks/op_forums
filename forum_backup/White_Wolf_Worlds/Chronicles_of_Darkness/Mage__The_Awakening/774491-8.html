        Quiescence doesn't make you blank totally on what happened. Mage beating you with shadows and breaking your legs will not be forgotten. Why is not that a problem exactly, other than "I can use fire to do aggravated damage!", which won't help you with "1 Blood on Celerity to auto-win initiative, 3 Blood on Physical Potency, HOMERUN BABY HOMERUN!" ?
        War in which hunter fight is assymetric warfare. You *can* win it. Making you equal to your opponents makes it symmetrical, which makes it kind of self defeating. Why would a Hunter shoot me with Sniper Rifle or bomb me? Because it's the thing people who are outclassed to equalize the playing field. And yes, even a hunter who fights people with knives (because...it's cool, I guess?) can get a drop on Mage, because stabbing wizards with pointy knives is dangerous as hell, especially if you win Surprise Check and get two rounds to stab them, because unlike all other things, they just go "whooosh" at full bash health meter.
        Hunter has no chances at fair, arena type fights. Be it versus Mages, be it versus Vampires, be it versus Werewolves. Of the three, first one is easiest to smack down, because both Vampire and Werewolf can tear you up even without being built up as combat machines. Starting Mage can probably cast spells that can disable you in two or three turns (not sure if it's that easy to instant disable in one single roll), as their direct damage potential is "bashing damage equal to potency". Mages, like hunter, have to prep too; Forces 2 mage without a lighter or handy power socket won't be much of a threat to murderhobo Hunter, for example.
        BTW, all dear Hunters - get Stealth as your Proffession Skill. Rote Ambush with Risk Willpower is your greatest strength in direct combat.

                                You know, I'll agree with Loki here. It's definitely possible to remove Quiescence and still maintain the tone and feel of Awakening. As long as magic still hurts sleepers and their presence invokes the Abyss that's plenty of reason right there for Mage's to feel separated and alone. There's some revamping to do with the Orders, The Diamond would likely have to be more active in hunting down and suppressing sleepers who insist on trying to out magic (which, IMO, puts Hunters at an even greater disadvantage, because now Mage society is deliberately targeting them. For their own safety no less). But overall its entirely doable.

                                However, and this is a big however, Quiescence is a big part of the Mage setting, and has been since the formative moments of 1e. Removing it would be a humongous change to the world Mage presents. For that reason alone it should remain. Don't like it? The books account for that. The very first rule in all the nWoD books is the Golden Rule: discard what you dislike.

                                In that regard it's very similar to Ascension (which people knock for being unclear, but I laud for recognizing the advantage table top games have over all other genres: Storyteller moderation).

                                                        Sleepers
                                                        Mechanically, every human character in the World of Darkness that isn’t Awakened or a Sleepwalker is a Sleeper. In the Fallen World Chronicle, the Sleeper rules extend the Integrity rules found in the God-Machine Chronicle, Strix Chronicle, and Idigam Chronicle, centered around that most Magely of Breaking Point questions:
                                                        What Have You Forgotten?
                                                        The basics of being a Sleeper are as follows;
                                                        • If a Sleeper character perceives an obviously magical effect caused by Supernal magic (whether it’s the powers of a Supernal entity or a mage’s spell) they immediately suffer a Breaking Point against Integrity.
                                                        • If a Sleeper is present when a mage tries to cast a spell with an obviously magical effect, they add a die of Paradox Risk. Multiple Sleepers don’t add more dice, but apply a dice trick to the Paradox roll: 9-again for a couple of people, 8-again for a dispersed group like urban foot traffic, and rote factor for a large crowd.
                                                        • Sleepers who come into contact with obvious spells after they’ve been cast slowly dispel them, in an effect mages call “Dissonance”
                                                        • Quiescence: The scene after a Sleeper witnesses a spell – whether they caused it to crumble under Dissonance or not – they forget about it. Their memory warps, and they rationalize the event away or substitute a non-magical equivalent. Convincingly reminding them (including using magic to do so) causes them to suffer another Breaking Point, and then forget again.

                                                            Sure, but Quiescence having graduated effects wouldn't actually contradict the general rules there - the book itself might get more specific.

                                                            And even if it doesn't, I imagine it wouldn't break the game to houserule it.

                                                            Also, having just looked it up again, it appears that the "Sleepers forget magic" was part of "Disbelief" in 1e, which explains why it's actually called Disbelief - it's less about the unravelling spells and more the part where Sleepers disbelieve what they see and substitute it either with nothing (if their willpower score is low), or remember it as a dream or substitute a rational explanation (if their willpower score is middling). Based on 1e core, the Quiescence is just "the general tendency for people not to Awaken to the truth", which I believe has been folded into the definition of The Lie in 2e.

                                                            I like 2e's terminology better, if only for separating the terms for "Sleepers forget magic" and "Sleepers unravel spell effects".
