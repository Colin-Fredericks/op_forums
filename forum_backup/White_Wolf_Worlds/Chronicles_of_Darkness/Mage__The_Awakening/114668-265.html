        Page 87 makes it clear that any act of Blood Sacrifice (killing a person or animal for Mana) is an Act of Hubris, however the section on Wisdom doesn't bring it up. My question is, what level of AoH is an animal sacrifice? My first thought is that it could be a sin against Enlightened Wisdom, after all, for most people devouring the essence of a dead animal for sustenance is about as mundane as getting dressed or brushing your teeth. However that would imply some very interesting things about the setting, as it would mean that most mages could gain mana this way with very little penalty. "Don't bother scrapping with your neighbours about who gets to keep that Hallow, we can just swing by the pet shop instead."

        Is there an official ruling somewhere that I am missing? Or do most folk think it should be a crime against Understanding wisdom?

        P.S: This only came up because I just had a PC roleplay through the Mysterium Arche ritual (check there order book) which incorporates an animal sacrifice, and only now days later do I recall I should have probably asked for some kind of wisdom check.

                    There's a huge psychological difference between using animal products and personally killing an animal. I think a lot of people in the wod (at least the city-dwellers we tend to play) could eat a hamburger without flinching but would suffer a Breaking Point if they had to personally slaughter a cow - though a butcher of course wouldn't.

                    Wisdom, like Integrity, is a flexible, personal scale. For many mages, animal sacrifice is probably an Act of Hubris against Understanding wisdom, like the cow/hamburger situation. But for others (presumably the same sort of people who would already be comfortable slaughtering an animal for food) it wouldn't.

                    Making a habit of going down to the pet store for your weekend mana binge probably still would be, though. Carelessly burning though mana because "there's always more where that came from" is kind of dangerous and arrogant in itself, and habitually sacrificing droves of animals shows a callous disrespect for both life an magic that cannot be described as anything other than Hubristic.

                    Plus it's a hassle just from a practical standpoint. You can't really go down to Petsmart and just buy five dogs without getting questions, especially if you already came in and bought five dogs two weeks ago. If you want that much life, you're gonna have to throw in with puppy mills, factory farms or other shady folks or straight up steal animals, both of which are potentially Acts of Hubris unto themselves. And once you're done, you have to dispose of all that meat somehow. And the spirits! Just imagine what the Shadow would be like around the Sanctum of a mage who routinely slaughters large quantities of animals for magical power.

                                                Originally posted by Flinty View Post

                                                Sure. And that effect is the Defense bonus from mage armor, I don't see why your usual defense would be enhanced along side of that effect.
                                                Why not? One narrative effect doesn't have to correspond exactly to one and only one mechanical effect. Look at something like The Outward and Inward Eye (Space 1). The narrative effect that has is allow you to perceive in all directions, which is represented mechanically as allowing you to cast at sensory range outside what would normally be your sensory range, making you practically immune to ambush and reducing penalties due to range, cover or concealment by Potency.

                                                In the same way, applying Space Mage Armor distorts space around you to slow down and deflect attacks, which is mechanically represented as getting a bonus to your Defense and letting you use Defense against firearms.
