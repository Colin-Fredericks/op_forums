    Mages on the Sea

    05-26-2018, 02:55 PM
    In a low-tech setting (roughly Bronze/Iron Age tech level), a cabal of mages, one of each Path, offers to trade services for passage on a sailing vessel. They're all starting level, with two dots each in their ruling arcana. So far, I've thought of services than some of them can provide reasonably: the Obrimos can call the winds as needed (weather control), the Moros can keep the ship's water supply clean (Matter 2 should be enough), the Thyrsus can drive rats and insects out of the ship, the Acanthus can look forward to see if stopping at a particular island or port is a good or bad idea (Divination), and I'm kind of stymied concerning the Mastigos. Yes, she can act as a back-up navigator (Space makes it pretty much impossible to get lost), but that's not really sufficient service, and I'm having trouble coming up with maritime applications for Time and Space. I'm not sure longitude is even a concept in-setting.

      A Thyrsus could also be well-suited to communing with the spirits of the oceans and islands, either to gain allies or destroy them for going against you. The Acanthus makes an excellent navigator, using Fate and Time to discern the best possible courses of action, and the Moros can also undo sunstroke and heat stroke through cryomantic effects(The Thyrsus can deal with maritime illnesses, like food poisoning and scurvy). The Mastigos' best trick however, is their capacity to navigate the Astral. Through Space and Mind, they can witness events occurring from vast distances away, in addition to also being able to deal with the effects of sleep deprivation, psychic attacks, and also functioning as the moral support and diplomat of the ship. What better way to deal with "ocean madness" than a Magician with a natural affinity for emotional urging?

                                      Smooth sailing is an Acanthus specialty. If they use a simple compelling of Fate, they can ensure that all the everyday details go smoothly, the Obrimos can counter any large scale manipulations of the weather, but barring magical shenanigans, the Compelling of Fate will ensure weather is nice, less people get seasick and that the trip is more pleasant in a lot of small, narrative ways. Using Red Light/Green Light ensures the ship will take the smoothest and fastest route ("we got a nice wind AND we found a current that goes our way ? Today is our lucky day"). Running out of supplies ? Low on money ? People are starting to get sick ? Nothing Shifting The Odds can't fix. The area is afoul with bandits ? Not a problem, a simple Veiling of Fate on the ship will make you luckily avoid all non-supernatural ones. Getting into a ship fight, huh ? Not a problem, give the captain Superlative Luck and Fools Rush In to make him have a really inspired performance.

                                      The powers of Mastigos are a huge boon in Bronze Age Navigation. Remember that cartography and astrology were nascent fields, the navigational instruments and maps were very imprecise. A simple Knowing of Space can let them revision maps into incredibly precise ones. Not only that, since even astrolabes could be imprecise enough to steer the ship hundreds of kilometers off course, having someone who could point the ship the right way is also very good. The practice can also be used to find stowaways, contraband, detect the general state of the ship (a screw coming lose, a part of the hull becoming rusty). Even the first two levels of Mind can be a big help in parlays and making good impressions with traveling ships, getting good prices out of merchants, etc. In fact, reading surface thoughts helps detect growing discontentment and talk people out of mutiny before it even begins.

                                      By the way, have you ever had food cooked by an Obrimos ? Everything cooked to perfection, every ingredient prepared to the right temperature, roasts that melt in your mouth. And all this done with sunlight, saving all important firewood for cold weather. And when the fireplace is lit, they make it so everyone enjoys the warmth. All the manual labor is making the workers get heat exhaustion ? Not while she is around. Some Obrimos save up spherical motes of sunlight over the day, lighting up the ship at night, its so much easier on the eyes than candlelight, some more jovial mages even swap their colors and shapes, putting a storytelling light show for the kids.

                                      Do you know what a Thrysus on cleaning duty looks like ? Pretty much like the dishwashing scene from Sword in the Stone, efficient and awesome, commanding the ephemera on this side of the Gauntlet is such an incredibly flexible skill ("quill, take dictation on this letter"). Just be very careful with the Scale of the spell, or the ship might listen and try to bathe. Even if you can help the passengers live through it via Body Control, I find that people don't react well to the impromptu invention of undersea travel (unless it saves them from being boarded).

                                      Those are the first ideas that come to mind, are they more or less what you were looking for or did you want more subtle applications ?
