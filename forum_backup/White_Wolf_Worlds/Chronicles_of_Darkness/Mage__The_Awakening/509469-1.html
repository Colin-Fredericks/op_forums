                    As presented in Boston Unveiled, the Nemean has a fairly large apparatus to enforce his rule. He has the Ebon Noose, the biggest cabal in the consillium, as well the Gravediggers, and entire cabal of dedicated assassins/warriors under his command. In addition to that, the White Putnams support him in everything. Then the council has also let him appoint most of the "police force" of the consillium, which are the ones who are doing most of the enforcing of the rules. So something like 20 of the mages are there to help him enforce his will, a direct conflict with him involves picking a fight with them. As long as the rest of the mages are disorganized and insular, it makes ok sense for the biggest group to be able to boss everone around. If everyone else bands together, then he looses, but until then he is king.

                    I never got around to doing much with Boston back in my last game, but once 2nd comes around I am returning to Boston with a small group. I am thinking a lot about how to change or update Boston to run it in the new edition. Looks like I am going to be running a game where two disciple-level Guardians arrive in Boston, coming under the command of the Shadow Chorus and pursuing the goals of the guardians in the consillium.

                    So a related question to "what have you done with it?" would be "how would you change/update Boston?", both for the new edition and also just based on what worked and what didn't.

                    The things I am thinking about are...
                    Making it more clear/focused what the central mystery of Boston is and how it draws mages in. I know that it is the Secret Concord, but how do mages get involved in that? I think I would bring the particular qualities of the astral in Boston into focus as well, as something that attracts mages. Maybe expand on the combat zone, as an area of interest. Make sure that some of the cabals are dedicated to exploring those things. Still, the concord is cool, but it is hard to see how mages investigate it, get involved with it, when it's so deadly and there's almost no way to investigate it, without trying to turn members of the tyrant's or his friends' cabals into informers.

                    Advancing the whole Nemean storyline - dunno how interesting it is to run a game about toppling the old Hierarch, especially since mages have to be powerful and/or well-established to do it. Very long-term. So how would a Boston on the edge of disposing the Nemean look, how would Boston look after the Nemean has just been ousted? Who could actually do it in the setting? Who would be an interesting new Hierarch?

                    The Free Council of Boston has always annoyed me. Their order chapter in the book talks about democrats, libertines and revolutionaries, but the libertines of Boston are pirates and borderline apostates. None of them are doing anything politically, all of them are just playing around or delving into some personal thing... or being hippies in the most passive way possible. How to shatter that? I think part of the point is them is that after destroying the Emerald Scroll they lost their purpose and have just been becoming more and more inactive and powerless since then... with the loss of a common enemy and purpose. Still, I like the council with an edge, so how would they be changed for a new edition?

                    So what about you guys? How would you change the things I mention above? Or do you think there are more important/interesting things to change around? There's also the fact that a decade (more or less) has passed since the book came out, how should the setting change to reflect that? Not necessarily advance the setting by 8 years, but perhaps reimagining things a bit to include more recent events (more marathon bombing, less big dig, etc)

                            Me, I've used BU as support for one story of my Mage chronicle.

                            A PC mysterium censor is sent to Boston to "acquire" the sinister artifacts of one "Ahriman". The investigation leads to a forceful confrontation and the PC killing Ahriman in self-defense with a vulgar Death curse, not before becoming tainted with the abyssal artifacts contained in his personal sanctum. Meanwhile, the rest of the PCs come looking for his missing cabalmate and indulge in a bit of politics with the White Putnams and a short fight with some Red Word cultists.

                            A month later, Chain Parris returns the favor by visiting some of the PCs at their home back at Portland (actually a fictitious Portland-equivalent, long story). Cue the Red Cult killing in their home turf, a creepy wooden statue of a creature not of this world sent by a "Mr Mark Long" for a museum exhibit in the city and the Seers of the Throne trying to avert a sinister prophecy. The PCs find out about the Blasphemous Scribe, kill all cultists and wizards involved (some in cold blood) and swear a pact of silence to avoid strengthening the Prince. Chain goes back to Boston, and just when the Ebon Noose starts to figure out what happened with Ahriman, the Convocation kidnaps The Nemean, putting the Noose mages in confusion and disarray.

                            Three months later, and the Noose is ready for payback, with Anacaona in command. The PCs have been forewarned about something nasty coming their way, but they have no idea the kind of shitstorm the Noose is about to release on them.

                            So, while I'm preparing a potential Boston game, there are some changes that are going to happen if the PCs don't intervene, partly inspired by the now-defunct chat mage venues and some ideas of my own:

                            * The Council is just a big farce right now, deadlocked in voting for the next Hierarch between Numa and Potestas in one hand and Anacaona and Parris in the other. Hydra hasn't left her mansion since her defeat at the hands of the Ax. The Gravediggers are only answering to the Hierarch, not the Ebon Noose. All parties are getting ready for open hostilities if any other party makes a wrong move.

                            * The Convocation names Khumeia the new Deacon, but shortly afterwards she will lose her mind and will kill a few apostates and Consilium mages with vulgar magic in open street. Thanatos is still in intensive care after the failed coup against the Hierarch and Faustina will wisely leave Boston, presumably for New Haven.

                            * The Dead Wrens lose the Emerald Scroll to a magical fire provoked by the Shadow Chorus, and they lay low in a safehouse down the harbor, while they reorganize and plan revenge. Eventually they will stage a heist against the Shadow Chorus' sanctum that will end in blood and horror for both parties.

                            * SMG have created a magical software that can predict where a hallow is most likely to appear in the Great Boston area and they plan to hold an open auction at the regional Assembly to earn big money, artifacts, and convince other libertines to move to the area. This will backfire when Scorpio shows up, spoiling for a fight. If they defeat the Seers and the software works, however, SMG's actions will provoke a flood of new mages arriving in Boston, Harvard and Salem, and no small amount of conflict with the more established cabals.

                            I'm using three more cabals to flesh the Consilium up:

                            * The Broncinean Tripod of Ophidian Logic exists and has fought against the Red Word cult, though right now they are wounded and laying low, protected by Aurem and struggling with their own abyssal tainting.

                            * Solon's Witnesses is an all-female, mostly Guardians new cabal that throws its support for a Guardian Councilor, but nobody knows if that means the Shadow Chorus, her own cabal or someone else entirely.

                            * Franklyn Hill is a gang-style libertine column that is carving its home turf right below Vision's nose, fighting a shadow war in the streets against the Company.

                            All of that may or may not appear in my game, depending on a series of factors. I'll go along with what my players throw me, though.

                                        I think the reaction to the setup in Boston really shows the difference between readers and players. I designed Boston for play. When people approach Boston that way they get a great deal out of it. When they want the book to be something to read about an archetypal/typical city, it's not as useful. Fact is, none of the three original signature cities are really typical.

                                        Anyway, it's deliberately unstable with an antagonist front and center. At the time, it was also written to make a clean break with Ascension, where the Traditions are an alliance pointed at a greater goal. Consilii aren't "alliances." They're governments that exist out of the need to limit violence between groups that may not have common objectives at all. Consilli aren't your friend. They're not vampire courts either, because mage society isn't feudal and isn't concerned with managing "herd" access. So mage claims an entirely different place than its predecessor or other games. Let's say that for people with . . . less expansive imaginations . . . it was easier to not read it and mentally paste in shit from other games than to read it and run with it.

                                                        At risk of derailing this thread, I think BU reads different with the right context. Reading it as a first supplement may have left the reader with the feeling of the book not explaining enough; reading it after Sanctum & Sigil and Guardians of the Veil is a completely different story. Reading it now after all these years worth of supplements, well...

                                                        The core book at the time suffered IMHO of presentation problems.
