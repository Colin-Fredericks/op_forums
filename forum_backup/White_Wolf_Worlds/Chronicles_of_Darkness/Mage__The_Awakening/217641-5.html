                                            I think Dataweaver and Joker are on to something.
                                            Sleepers have one advantage their awakened counterparts don't: Paradox. A wizard may try to bend us mere mortals to his will, but like a rubber band, we'll bounce back with a vengeance, likely poking that fool's eye out in the process. Further more, a sleeper doesn't have to be a hunter to hunt the supernatural. Take the police for instance, a magician may be questioned for being at the scene of the crime/freak incident. If he's caught lying, they'll hold him accountable for that crime. If he tells them the truth, they'll throw him into an insane asylum. If he tries to use sorcery to get out of this mess... well at that point, he's probably too far gone to help now. Note that this is a gross oversimplification of the point I'm trying to get across.
                                            I'd like to see this after action report tactic expanded upon.
                                            Has anyone thought of making willpower into a sort of pseudo power stat for hunters? It could be used to resist supernatural things like lie induced amnesia and whatnot. In order to activate it, the player must risk one of his character's willpower dots, and roll the rest.

                                                I actually had my players get arrested. They were lead by a mystagogue reclaimer and we're attempting to steal an artifact from a museum before it's display opened. The cops showed and the cabal very proudly went to counter them... And got their butts kicked. I pulled a story teller fiat and kept the Cabal alive (I had the scene play out like the opening of Leverage in the hospital), but between paradox, numbers and guns, the mages got taken down hard (to be fair, this was our 4th session, so they were pretty green both as players and stat wise, but still). It also could explain why the older/larger conspiracies get into so much questionable stuff (Grafting Abyssal parasites and other supernatural body parts to your agents? Hunting and Raping captured monsters? Tapping into demonic forces? Illegal supernatural drug cartels?): the nature of the Lie twists these conspiracies. I know it is mage once again forcing an explanation onto other lines (sorry) but I like the idea that the Lie has multiple ways to enforce itself.
