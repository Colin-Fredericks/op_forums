    Critian Caceorte's Awakening Homebrew

    05-27-2022, 02:48 PM
    Since I've had more ideas for Mage homebrew these past few days, I felt it better to just create one general thread for this stuff rather than creating new threads for every single entry. Hence this hub for my work. I've listed what I've done down below, with links going to entries outside of this thread. If it doesn't have a link, then its here.

    2e Legacies:
    • Crawlers of Yggdrasil (Thyrsus/Time), a group of time travelers who emulate the squirrel Ratatoskr by working as messengers and moonlighting as pranksters.
    • Roboticists of the New Era (Obrimos/Matter), a collective of engineer-mages who, inspired by the Chicago World's Fair, looked to create a brighter future through robots.
    • Fishermen of Dead Streams (Moros OR Thyrsus/Mind), various traditions of fishing that have all evolved into a similar Legacy of Underworld travelers, who "fish" for the memories of the dead.
    • Canopic Collective (Left-Handed; Thyrsus/Death), a Legacy of Scorpion Cultists who work to study soul stones and recreate the feats of the Arisen, all while under attack from the Tremere.


    More entries will be added with time, if you have any questions, this is the perfect place to ask! Honestly, these creations can only become better with criticism.

      Roboticists of the New Era

      Science Fiction as a genre has dominated the human consciousness for the past century. As human ingenuity catches up to imagined spectacles, it only stands to reason that some, magic or mundane, have taken to viewing science fiction as a template for what must surely come. Among mages, these individuals are known as the Roboticists of the New Era.
      The Tinkers as they are sometimes called drew inspiration from stories beginning in 1920’s American comics, but actually formed as a group at the 1934 Chicago World’s Fair. A small congregation of mages attended the exhibitions, initially searching for any potential Mysteries they could investigate. Instead, what they saw astonished them. Some of the invention showed off at the fair, while seemingly trite compared to today’s standards, were amazing to behold to the people of that era, and those mages present were no exception. After a few months of speaking with the inventors in question and recruiting newly Awakened Mages, of which there was a sizable glut compared to the norm, the eldest in the group decided to embrace this methodology of the future and turned their hand to studying what would later become known as “robotics”.
      It took a few decades of work to formulate the Legacy, even as a cabal, but by the time of the 1st moon landing all of the attainments had been created and the Legacy fully developed. The Roboticists would become a very popular legacy for the young mages in the world as they saw the wonders brought about by circuit and steel. Today, they remain a respectable group, though these days they focus more on man-sized machines then the larger golems of yesteryear. After all, large flying robots tend to draw the eyes of unscrupulous groups more often than disguised or hidden away pieces.

      Origins
      Parentage: Obrimos, Mysterium, Free Council
      Nickname: Tinkers
      Background: Every prospective member of the Tinkers has some interest in robotics, without question. Most come from engineering backgrounds, but a few are actually doctors interesting in limb replacements, and a very select few are more interested in the legacy’s use for espionage.
      Appearance: Tinkers come in every shape and size, but they generally have some sort of streaming device on-hand to transmit their creation’s camera feeds to them. A lot of the old guard also use RC (remote control) controllers for better handling.

      Doctrine
      Prerequisites: Matter 2, Computer 2, one of the following at 2 dots: Crafts, Drive, Expression, Medicine, Science
      Initiation: Bringing someone into the fold of the Tinkers is an interesting process. When a mentor is sure that a prospective student is ready to be initiated, they will break off a portion of one of their own robots and give it to the student. This isn’t the initiation, rather, the student must then take this part, build a robot of their own design around it whilst ensuring that the original part does not stick out. The process of trying to mold their design around their mentor’s contribution is reflected in their own soul, and if the student has taken all of their lessons to heart, they will have molded their soul in a similar fashion as their mentor’s.
      Organization: While there can only be one true “mentor” for a student in the Legacy in terms of initiation, newbies are typically taught everything they need by a group of Tinkers, who band together both for solidarity and to cover each other’s faults. Mass groupings of the Tinkers take place at every World’s Fair (which occurs every five years), though the tinkers tend to withhold their own contributions from exhibition until they all gather in specially warded locations. All told, this makes the Tinkers one of the few Legacies to regularly communicate with other members across the globe. In terms of mage demographics, the majority of members tend to be either Obrimos or Acanthus, whose visions of a brighter future are very real. Moros members make a reasonable third, as few can trust in a better tomorrow when surrounded by today’s horrid circumstances despite their own inclination to the Matter Arcanum. Mastigos and Thyrsus are quite rare, but highly sought after for their ability to give guiding intelligences to their own robots and well as others.

      Magic
      Ruling Arcana: Matter
      Yantras: An image of the Sky Ride (+1), taking an extended Dexterity + Computer action to solder some circuits (+2), RC controllers (+1)
      Oblations: Designing blueprints for machines, attending international events, driving or flying RC toys

      Note for Attainments: For the purposes of clarity, “Robot” refers to any machine capable of completing a complex series of tasks automatically. To further specify, Robots mentioned in the following attainments are machines which use electrical power (the source of which is up to the mage) to move and complete their functions. This is to distinguish them from other machines such as cars (which do not generally use electricity) or devices like computers (which cannot move) or magical entities such as eidolons (which run on Mana to function). Some Attainments have more Reach then they otherwise should, but compensate for this by being limited to robots which the Roboticist has built themselves, something which can take weeks or months of work.

      Attainments:
      First: Electromagnetic Imaging
      Prerequisite: Initiation
      With a steady pulse of energy, the Mage peers beyond the fallen veil to see what exactly constitutes the item in their possession. With a scene of study, the Tinker uncovers the density, the weight and the precise composition of an item they touch. In addition, until the item is fully repaired or destroyed the Tinker reduces its Durability against their strikes by the Mage’s Matter dots. This emulates the spell Discern Composition with Reach spent to discover weak points.
      Optional: Forces 1
      It’s not enough to simply give off electromagnetic radiation, a Tinker must also be able to understand it as well. Their eyes see Wi-Fi-broadcasts, their ears hear radio waves, and with this Attainment the Tinker becomes permanently connected to the world of the future. This emulates the spell Tune In with Reach spent for advanced duration.

      Second: Wave-Form Reflection
      Prerequisite: Computer 3
      At this stage, the Tinker has discovered how to cloak their creations from the sight of intruding devices. This attainment is part of how the Roboticists were able to hide their creations from prying eyes in the past. With an instant action, the robot temporarily erases their presence in the sight of cameras and recording devices that would normally see it, except for cameras built into the robot itself (which is how the Tinkers retain control of their machines). This benefit only lasts for a number of turns equal to Matter dots, and can only affect robots which the Tinker has built themselves. This emulates the spell Machine Invisibility with Reach spent for Instant Casting, Sensory Range and Remote View Range.
      Optional: Forces 2
      Distorting the reflection of light across the robot’s carapace, the Tinker becomes able to duplicate the above benefit but for living entities as well. Now no light can betray the robot’s presence, whether the viewer be a camera or a human being. This benefit lasts for Matter dots turns, and can only affect robots which the Tinker has built themselves. This emulates the spell Invisibility with Reach spent for Instant Casting, Sensory Range and Remote View Range. Unfortunately, sound is not covered by this protection, forcing the Mage to cast spells to do so instead if they want their machine to be truly undetectable.

      Third: Taking the Controls
      Prerequisite: Matter 3, Forces 3, Increase the other skill to 3 dots or another skill at 2 dots
      Combining the natural impulses of the machine with control over its electrical systems, the Tinker takes control of a robot they built, controlling the movement of its limbs to perform the tasks that it should. When manipulating a robot up to size 10, or size 15 at Forces 4, this way, any Skill roll performed is considered to have rolled successes equal to the higher of Matter dots. Unfortunately, this control only lasts for a few seconds, equivalent to 2 turns, or 3 turns at Forces 4; meaning that the Roboticist has to maintain control of their creations the entire time if they wish to move them for extended amounts of time.
      This effect lasts for as long as the Roboticist maintains sight of the robot either through their own eyes or through cameras. Thus, a common practice is to install a camera on top or behind the robot to maintain constant vision of the robot even as it moves. Other cameras are also installed to get multiple perspectives and thus prevent any ambushes.
      This Attainment uses the Control Electricity spell to enhance Remote Control, effectively giving it extra reach. This Reach is spent on Instant Casting, Sensory Range, Remote View Range, Advanced Scale and Complex Actions.

      Fourth: Hidden Compartments
      Prerequisite: Matter 4, Computer 4
      By installing hidden compartments within a robot’s limbs, appendages, head, or torso, the Tinker can add miniaturized versions of useful gadgets. A given limb can have up to 3 added gadgets, which emulate other machines of a similar size or smaller. An arm can fold it’s hand out of the way to reveal the barrel of a shotgun, a finger can flip open to show a lighter, a head can have cameras installed, and a torso with a gyroscope can swivel and turn around 360 degrees. Once installed, these qualities are permanent until the Tinker replaces them with other items. This emulates the spell Wonderful Machine with reach spent to change the primary factor to Duration, and for Advanced Duration.
      Optional: Forces 4
      With precisely arranged circuits and heat sinks, the Mage defies thermodynamics and makes their machine energy independent so long as it remains relatively intact and the robot remains within Size 15. This emulates the spell Perpetual Motion with Reach devoted to Advanced Duration and Advanced Scale.

      Fifth: New Age Robotics
      Prerequisite: Matter 5, Increase the other skill to 4 dots, or the third skill to 3 dots, or another skill at 2 dots
      Previously, it was the work of many days and months of hard work to create the robots which the Tinkers used. With this final Attainment, the Masters among them figured out a way to make permanent creations in a much faster manner. With just a scene's worth of work, the Roboticist can create robots up to Size 15 that can move autonomously, albeit in a very simple manner. Robots created this way are treated as being 1 dot Retainers when not being directly controlled. This emulates the spell Golem with Reach assigned to changing the primary factor to Duration, Advanced Duration and Advanced Scale.
      Optional: Forces 5
      One of the greatest visions of the future involves not just robots, but flying robots, a combination which has entranced children across the world for generations. This effect fulfills that dream, allowing the Roboticist to install with a scene’s worth of work a jetpack, anti-gravity field or some other means of aerial locomotion into one of their robots up to size 15. Such robots fly at a speed of 9, slow, but omnidirectional. This emulates the spell Levitation with reach assigned to Advanced Duration, Advanced Scale and free flight.

          Fishermen of Dead Streams

          Since the dawn of history, mankind has gone out into the deep waters in order to catch fish for food. This noble profession has extended from the time of Atlantis into the modern Fallen day. While the size of catches for groups of fishermen has increased substantially, the overall intent is the same: To collect enough fish to feed themselves and others. The Fishermen of Dead Streams have taken this idea and applied it to the eroding rivers of the Underworld.
          Known by some as “Anglers,” these individuals do not (generally) fish for actual fish in the streams of toxic water, although with the shape of certain Cthonians that might still be possible. Generally, they instead fish for “memories” among themselves and the dead, “reeling them in” by recalling them or taking them from others to eventually be shared. To Ghosts, the Fishermen are a friendly face willing to help them remember who they used to be and who they used to know. Why they help varies from person to person, sometimes good, sometime bad, but they generally do.
          The Fishermen do not have a clear nation of origin, having existed for millenia, but they do have a shared origin in the art of catching sea creatures. Some rely on ancient techniques passed down from elders on the Pacific Islands, Christian members often trace it back to Simon Peter, while the more history-oriented among them believe the most likely candidate nation of origin to have been Egypt with the Nile River. Regardless of personal beliefs, the Legacy has easily endured just as fishing itself has.

          Origins
          Parentage: Moros, Thyrsus, Free Council
          Nickname: Anglers
          Background: Experience with fishing is an absolute must, you cannot fish for memories if you haven’t tried the fallen world equivalent of such.
          Appearance: Looks heavily vary among members, who are generally influenced by their home culture. One feature that is often shared however is a gentle smile, meant to display a person’s inability to commit harm to you. Whether that smile is real or fake depends on the member.

          Doctrine
          Prerequisites: Mind 2, Survival 2, one of the following at 2 dots: Athletics, Drive, Medicine, Occult, Socialize
          Initiation: An experienced Angler can take a look inside the mind of a prospective initiate, looking at their fondest memories. With a tug of their invisible reel, they bring that memory to the forefront of the mage's mind. Concentrating on this happiness as they fish, they must maintain a hold its warmth until they catch a great enough catch to be impressive. This process is what develops the mage's soul into that of an Angler.
          Organization: Anglers tend to be a more easygoing sort, and so while there is a tendency to stay in their own lanes as it were culturally, when a rare intercultural meeting does occur, it often goes quite well. Moros and Thyrsus mages make up the majority of the membership, the Moros because of their affinity with the dead, and Thyrsus because of the savagery inherent to catching one’s own meals. Acanthus members also “reel in” other things such as fates and promises, and Mastigos Anglers use their inherent affinity for the Mind to better catch prey. Only the Obrimos sees very little representation, as the Underworld remains a location with which they have little desire to venture.

          Magic
          Ruling Arcana: Mind
          Yantras: Presence + Socialize extended roll to get someone to reminisce (+2), A fishing rod or net (+1), worms or other types of bait (+1)
          Oblations: Fishing by a river, collecting bait, interacting with ghosts, row a boat down a stream

          Attainments
          First: In Your Own Waters
          Prerequisites: Initiation
          Before you can go out into the great unknown, it might be best to fish for your own memories instead. With an instant action, you can recall any memory perfectly, up to the most minute of details. This emulates the spell Perfect Recall with Reach devoted to Instant Casting.
          Optional: Death 1
          You can’t talk with the dead if you can’t even see or hear them. With this Attainment, the Angler permanently becomes able to see or hear ghosts in Twilight. This emulates the spell Speak With the Dead with Reach given for Advanced Duration.

          Second: Tossing Out the Guppies
          Prerequisites: Survival 3
          Some memories just do more harm then good, best to just get rid of them entirely. With a scene of meditation, you can toss away a memory for good, losing it permanently. This emulates the spell Memory Hole with Reach spent on Advanced Duration.
          Optional: Death 2
          You greet a friend by shaking their hand, don’t you? Doing the same with a ghost is sure to set a good tone, and this Attainment makes it possible. The mage can now physically interact with Ghosts in Twilight as if they were flesh and blood. This emulates the spell Touch of the Grave with Reach spent for Advanced Duration.

          Third: In the Face of It
          Prerequisites: Mind 3, Boost other skill to 3 dots or another skill to 2 dots
          As nice as some memories are, sometimes that nicety can be exploited for personal gain. With an instant action, the Angler retrieves an emotional memory for a ghost, opening or closing Doors for 1 day (1 week with Mind 4) equal to [Mind – Resistance]. This emulates the spell Emotional Urging with Reach for Instant Casting and Advanced Duration.
          Optional: Death 3
          Memories can have an impact on someone, and it never hurts to exploit that fact against more aggressive specters. After a scene of meditation to call upon a strong memory, whenever a ghost or Cthonian tries to hurt or affect the Angler in any way, they must succeed on a Clash of Wills first to do so. This effect lasts for 1 day, or 1 week at Death 4. This emulates the spell Ghost Shield with Reach dedicated to Advanced Duration and physical attack protection.

          Fourth: Fishing in the Depths
          Prerequisites: Mind 4, Survival 4
          Now that the Angler has become an expert in retrieving their own memories, they can now dedicate themselves to reeling in others. And if one of those memories is perhaps a bit different from how it used to be, well maybe that’s for the better. With a scene of conversation or meditation, the mage can view a ghost’s memories and modify one of them for their own purposes. This modification lasts for 1 week before it returns to its former shape. This emulates the spell Read the Depths with Reach dedicated to Advanced Duration and Modifying Memories.
          Optional: Death 4
          The Angler becomes a welcome sight in the Underworld, and its denizens respect their role. This replicates the Spirit 4 Attainment Honorary Rank, with the mage’s rank being equal to their dots in Death, but it works for Ghosts and Cthonians instead.

          Fifth: Now There’s a Story to Tell
          Prerequisites: Death 5, Mind 5, Boost other skill to 4 dots or third skill to 3 dots or another skill to 2 dots
          Many fishermen have stories to tell, stories of their biggest catches or funniest errors. With this Attainment, the Angler can preserve this story, this memory, as its own entity. This spell works as a fusion of Create Ghost and Psychic Genesis, requiring a scene of concentration or story-telling to bring the story to (un)life as a Ghost of its own. The ghost generally takes on the appearance of one of the characters in the story, and is typically Rank 1, or Rank 2 with a point of Mana. The Ghost cannot exist outside of the Underworld without Anchors, and unusually it manifests in Goetic Twilight as well as Deathly Twilight. Finally, it has Influences and Numen appropriate to the details of the memory. It remains within the mage’s service until released, as the Angler’s ownership maintains a hold on them. Reach in this case is spent for Advanced Duration and Rank 2.

              I think there is a lot of overlap with the Transhuman Engineers, since the first 3 Attainments of the Tinkers are exactly that Legacy's first edition ones. I think it would be interesting if they dedicated some Attainments to logistics. You can't safely explore the danger zone of the Atlantean ruin if your robot loses power halfway, so having access to Perpetual Motion (Signs of Sorcery page 65) would be very helpful, especially if their inventions have functions that are power hungry. A secondary problem is that they can run out of bullets, beacons, flares, chemicals and any other number of useful supplies, with that in mind, I think being able to grant them Endless Bounty (Signs of Sorcery page 66) would provide a lot of utility for both the robots and the mage (never run out of parts, supplies, money, etc). Lastly, I think the last optional Attainment is covered by drones, propulsion modules and similar feats of engineering. Building upon the theme of the main one, why not create a being halfway between matter and energy, inspired by the Codex from XCOM2 ? This way they can move at the speed of lightning, travel through electric grids, have an AI (literally a neural network of electric impulses), download and upload information from electronics and still be able to manipulate matter. It would take a custom spell, but I think it would be interesting.

              Regarding the Anglers, their Attainments sound suspicious enough that they could be theorized to be an offshoot of the Logophages. Their Attainments are meant to help, but they can be abused in a similar way. The first Attainment helps them recall things better, which is generally good. But the second one represses memories, so rather than say getting Telepathy so they can keep in contact with those they care and help the mage in the search even when staying by their Anchor, it allows the Anglers to cause the problem or redefine it in their own terms. The third one forces positive or negative emotions on the ghost, rather than providing a discussion space where cool heads can prevail and trauma can be conquered (Clear Thoughts from page 161 of the core book). If I understood correctly, their ethos is on recovering memories, processing them and in so doing give a bit of help for the ghost in resolving their Anchors, but the Attainments themselves suggest a far more heavy handed approach.

              Those would be my initial thoughts, hope that helps.

                  I'll try and break down my thoughts into bullet points, since there's a lot here to cover.
                  Roboticists:
                  • The comparison between the Tinkers and the Engineers is a fair one, there's definitely a lot of overlap in terms of Attainments and ethos. What I tried to go for with the Roboticists is more of an emphasis on machinery as opposed to the Engineers' focus on energy, but your suggestions do sound good. And yeah, Perpetual Motion is awesome, hence why I made it one of the optional Attainments. One of the potential routes I was considering was a focus on the robot's ability to be self-reliant, and while I didn't go down that path I think it might be a viable approach for a 1.1, probably replacing Invisibility with a permanent Environmental Shield (to protect it in harsh environments) and Machine Invisibility with Alchemist's Touch to protect against acid and the like. Just to better differentiate them from the Engineers.
                  • I went with Wondrous Machine as the 4th Attainment because of some inspiration from Data from Star Trek, an android that has hidden compartments beneath his fake skin, as well as James Bond gadgets. Having a machine that can shoot bullets from its fingers, a missile from its arm or a drone swarm from its stomach was just such a cool idea to me that I went with it as the choice. And the Roboticists as I've written them are very much enamored with the Rule of Cool.
                  • For the last optional Attainment... I'll be honest, I literally forgot that drones were a thing because I was too busy imagining my Gundam power fantasy, hence why Levitation was one of the Attainments. But the Codex is a cool idea, a Golem made of energy, and I might run with that if I can't think of another optional Attainment like using Velocity Control for a railgun or whatnot.

                  Fishermen:
                  • For the Logophage comparisons, that wasn't intentional on my part, but I can see where you're coming from. While I would never state definitively that they share an origin, having a comparison group that might make the Fishermen look bad by association is an interesting idea. Both have at least some relation to memories, and while the Logophages focus more on the "information" aspect of memory and the fishermen the "emotional" aspect, there is definitely a similarity.
                  • The potential for good and evil is an intentional design choice. The fishermen typically give off a "nice" demeanor, and many, likely even most, of them are actually good people. That just means that those who aren't so good however have an oppurtunity to exploit the good reputation their Legacy has for ill gain. The heavy-handedness is in part connected to the Thyrsus idea of fishing as being a "predatory" act in the animal sense. You're still killing or trapping a living thing, removing it from its natural habitat to suffocate and then eat later.
                  • Yep, one of their main selling points are as people who can go into the Underworld or even just into Twilight, talk to ghosts, work them through their issues and have them move onto to whatever lies beyond this world. They don't, however, have finesse in doing this as mentioned in the prior point. And that heavy-handed nature has undoubtedly caused them issues in the past.

                      The Canopic Collective (Left-Handed)

                      In the heyday of the Roman Empire, when their borders stretched across Europe and Asia Minor, a lone soldier, celebrating some break time in a military tour of Egypt, discovered a hidden tomb among the hot sands. Entering deep within, he found a repository filled with wonders, which drew his eye with the Mysteries they presented. Grabbing a valuable necklace to take with him, it wasn’t until he got to the Tomb’s exit that he saw the horror beneath the earth he had just unleashed. Fleeing from the bandaged creature that chased after him, he dropped the necklace behind. It was not needed anymore. He had already entered the Mystery Play the moment his feet stepped forward to run.
                      Succeeding in his Awakening, he later returned to the tomb, desperate to uncover its secrets. The mummy was for whatever reason not present, so he was free to observe the items before him. He figured that not touching or disturbing the jewels and artifacts would not trigger whatever alarm that caused the mummy to awaken in the first place, and for a time he was correct. He spent what seemed like days underground, just examining these treasures. Unfortunately, being fueled by obsession does not truly substitute for actual food and drink, and he found himself famished after emerging from his Scrutinizing fugue. When it seemed like he would pass out from fatigue, out stepped from the shadows the mummy which had chased him initially. Too tired to even move, he accepted his fate, but was confused to see the now much calmer mummy call out in a tongue he did not know. This brought in an attendant from a hidden alcove, who supplied him with the sustenance he required. When he had rejuvenated, he asked the mummy why it had spared his life this time, as compared to before. It’s reply was simple.
                      “My cult has need of sorcerers. Consider this fruit and these sights as your debt to me.”
                      What happened from this point, the histories are unclear about. What’s known is that the soldier turned mage joined as a priest in the mummy’s cult, and in exchange was permitted to study the ancient Mysteries the mummy had at its disposal. From there, the mage would meet and collaborate with other mages in the area, exchanging knowledge and favors among each other. This collaboration would eventually unite together as a legacy, the Canopic Collective.
                      The Collective are a Legacy of Reapers who use their knowledge of Arisen powers to simulate the same with Awakened Magic, namely the removal of organs without dying and rising back from the dead. Their other main focus, as their Reaper designation implies, involves the study of souls, particularly soul stones, for the use of their arts. The Collective steal these soul stones from other mages not just to gather favors, but to also use them as fuel for their own practices. At the highest levels of Mastery, a member of the Collective can change a soul stone’s creator to be themselves, and return from the brink of death by forming and possessing a newly grown body based around their own soul stone-organs. For many years this Legacy has existed in the fringes of Egypt, only truly welcomed in the cults of the Arisen and occasionally among Seers. That isolation these days is now harming them.
                      Whispers abound about a Master of the Legacy disappearing without a trace. Rumors state that a number of initiates are also vanishing, and recently higher-level members are also going missing. While many of these mages can claim membership in a Mummy’s cult to protect themselves, that isn’t always the case, and the Mummy’s are not universally protecting their magicians. Something is going around killing Legacy members at an alarming rate, and the likely suspect is fairly obvious.
                      The Tremere are on the hunt, and they have their scent.

                      Origins
                      Parentage: Thyrsus, Seers of the Throne, Scorpion Cult
                      Nickname: Jars
                      Background: A large portion of the Jars Awakened while in service to Arisen masters, as such they have a high tendency to be part of those same groups. This isn’t always the case, and there is a branch of the Legacy operating under the Seers (which ministry in specific isn’t known, but some believe it to be the Psychopomp, Exarch of Death).
                      Appearance: Members are generally heavily tanned due to operating within the crushing heat of Egypt’s days. They look healthy, but thin to the eye as the excess fat in their body slowly burns away not from exercise, but from ritual starvation.

                      Doctrine
                      Prerequisites: Death 2, Medicine 2; EITHER one of the following at 2 dots: Academics, Crafts, Expression, Larceny, Occult; OR Scorpion Cult Initiation at 2 dots.
                      Initiation: The initiate spends a few days building five canopic jars, one for each Pillar, which they might use later if they ever reach the 4th Attainment. The mentor then wraps the initiate in bandages, before burying them and their jars in a coffin overnight. Preferably, this coffin might be placed in a Mummy’s tomb, but needs must in this day and age. Simply burial works as well.
                      Organization: Most of the lower-level members of the Legacy remain as part of whatever cult or hierarchy they originated from, whether that be a Mummy’s cult or a Seer Ministry. The more powerful the mage becomes, the higher the likelihood of them leaving their group of origin to set out on independent investigations, the Mystery of the Legacy driving them further then their group can. If the Jar publicly turns their backs on the Exarchs, the Seers are likely to act as providers of their god’s vengeance, though this can happen even without a public withdrawal. Mummies can be more understanding, but not always, and the cults tend to take it even worse. Hence, while there is a preference in the older generations to go their own way, only the most powerful often do.

                      Magic
                      Ruling Arcana: Death
                      Yantras: Scorpion Imagery (+1), An extended Dexterity + Crafts action for pottery (+2), five “pillars” which could be stone monuments, fingers, trees, etc. (+1)
                      Oblations: Assisting a Mummy in their projects, Ritual self-surgery, Feng Shui, Meditation in a tomb

                      Attainments
                      Note: The attainments of the Canopic Collective are very difficult to meet the prerequisites for, as they require all of the Subtle Arcana at some point or another as well as Life and Space. For most mages, this septuple arrangement is impossible, at best they would have 2 of those Subtle Arcana as Ruling, and the amount of dedication needed to advance makes the effort seem worthless compared to other, easier legacy options. This utilization of the Subtle Arcana is part of why the eyes of the Tremere are trained on this Legacy, as it fits their requirements for a house to a tee. But, if a Mage were to somehow become a Master, then a very safe method of immortality would be at their fingertips. At least, until the Tremere find you and figure out the trick.

                      First: Studying the Stone
                      Prerequisites: Initiation
                      To best understand the potential of soul stones, one must understand what the soul itself looks like. Touching the object or person in question, the Jar gazes deep to understand its secrets. This replicates the spell Soul Marks, with reach devoted to Instant Casting.
                      Optional: Life 1, Spirit 1
                      The Mage sends out a pulse along the flows of Sekhem, eager to find where it converges. This takes a scene of effort to best align themselves and only lasts for [Life] turns. However, it allows the Mage to detect the presence of Ab and Sheut Pillar points in both the living and the dead in a large area, approximately a large building (or a parking lot at Life 2, and a supermarket at Life 4). If the Mage has 1 dot in Fate, Mind or Prime, they can add Ren, Ba and Ka Pillar points to this search as well. This is a modification of the spell Web of Life, with Reach devoted to Advanced Scale (Area).

                      Second: Closing the Jar
                      Prerequisites: Medicine 3, Death, Fate 2
                      Before the mage goes around messing with the souls of others, perhaps it is best that they protect themselves first. This attainment can be used with a scene of preparation on a soul stone which the Jar has created or stolen with the higher attainments. Once placed, the portion of the soul within the object cannot be manipulated or destroyed without triggering a Clash of Wills, unless the Mage so choose. In addition, the stone insulates itself from the mage’s destiny, preventing other entities from using the soul stone to manipulate the mage’s Fate without again triggering a Clash of Wills. This enchantment is permanent once placed. This emulates a combined spell of Soul Armor and Warding Gesture, with reach for both used for Advanced Duration.
                      Optional: Life 2, Space 2
                      The Jar further shrouds the piece of themselves to prevent exploitation by other thieves. With a scene of effort, the soul stone becomes veiled with yet more enchantments. The first hides the DNA and vital information of the soul stone, if it is biologically alive, stopping other mages from figuring out who it belongs to without a Clash of Wills. The attainment also hides the soul stone’s connection to the mage, making the sympathetic connection invisible to mystical sight without, again, a Clash of Wills. This emulates a combined spell of Veil Sympathy and a custom Life + Veiling effect. Reach is dedicated to Advanced Duration.

                      Third: The Malleable Soul
                      Prerequisites: Death 3, Prime 2, Boost a chosen ability OR Scorpion Cult Initiation to 3 dots
                      At this stage, the mage has progressed enough in understanding the Pillars to finally utilize its energies for their own purposes. By sacrificing a soul stone, whether their own or another’s, they can sanctify an object for the purposes of relinquishing a spell. This emulates the spell Reaping Relinquishment, with reach given to Instant Casting.
                      Optional: Life 3, Space 2
                      The soul requires a body to function properly, but so too does the body need the soul. Its instincts are driven by the soul’s urgings, and through understanding the soul the Mage can replicate these urgings to fit their own desires. By spending a point of Mana and taking an instant action while holding another mage’s soul stone, the Mage can manipulate that mage’s instincts without notice so long as their Composure is less than the Jar’s Life dots. This emulates the spell Control Instincts, with reach given for Instant Casting, Sensory/Sympathetic Range, changing Primary Factor to Potency and affecting Supernatural Creatures.

                      Fourth: The Changed Soul
                      Prerequisites: Death 4, Prime 4, Medicine 4
                      A soul stone is a piece of a mage, a bare expression of their soul and path, allowing them to form Demesne’s oriented to their Watchtower. What would happen, then, if that orientation were to be warped, changed to fit in line with another Supernal Realm? The Jar carefully manipulates the piece of soul in the object, fiddling with its connection to the Supernal until it replicates the Jar’s own connection. If the other mage’s Resolve is higher than the Jar’s Death dots, this spell cannot be used as their willpower prevents tampering with their soul. This takes a scene to do, and lasts for a week before maintenance must be performed. If the soul stone is ever destroyed while this enchantment persists, the piece of soul must be manually changed back and fused into the mage’s Gnosis for the price of 1 XP. This is a custom spell utilizing Potency as the Primary Factor and with reach dedicated to Advanced Duration.
                      Optional: Life 4, Space 3
                      It is with this attainment that the Canopic Collective finally live up to their name. Through a scene of ritual surgery and a point of Mana, the mage removes one of their organs in terms of location, if not bodily. The organ still functions as normal: A heart still pumps blood through the body, a stomach still digests food, an eye still sees as usual. The only difference is that the organ is not present within the mage’s body while retaining its functionality. Jars often place internal organs in, well, canopic jars and use them as soul stones both for personal use and as preparation for the Legacy’s final attainment. This emulates the spell Co-Location, with Reach dedicated to Sensory/Sympathetic range and Advanced Duration.

                      Fifth: The Soul Lives On
                      Prerequisites: Death 5, Life 5, Mind 5, Space 3; Boost a chosen ability OR Scorpion Cult Initiation to 4 dots
                      The ultimate attainment of the Canopic Collective and undoubtedly the hardest art of theirs to learn, this power is ultimately worth it in terms of security and immortality. As an instant action at any point up until the moment of death, the Jar can sever their own soul and mind and send it flying towards one of their soul stones, ideally one prepared with the optional fourth attainment. Once there, a new body is grown around the soul stone, consuming it in the process, with the mage’s mind and soul settling into the new flesh. This transfer unfortunately is not perfect as some of the mage’s enlightenment burns away the process, reducing their Gnosis by 1. If this would put them below the threshold for this attainment, they cannot access it until they increase their Gnosis to that level again.

                      This emulates a combined spell of Create Life, Sever the Awakened Soul and Psychic Genesis. Reach is devoted to Instant Casting, Sensory/Sympathetic Range and Advanced Duration.

                          Wardens of the Round Table

                          In times of yore, many legends were created about the Knights of the Round Table, and the castle they centered around known as Camelot. Though Camelot's true origins are a Mystery known to very few in the modern day, the inspiration it has given lives on. Countless warrior-romanticists roam the world, chasing down the horrors of the night and protecting the innocent because it is the right thing to do. Of course, when those horrors prove to be far gentler and kind than expected, then the image of the dashing princely rescuer replaces the resolute sword-master. It is a tradition of chivalry which has persisted for a thousand years, and, supposedly, even further.

                          There is some debate about who was the founder of the Legacy, and of whether they actually were a knight of Camelot or not. With how few records remain of Camelot's time, it's difficult to determine what is true and what is fantasy. Currently, there stand 3 potential candidates for the position: 1 is well-known, 1 has some backing to it, and the 3rd is the only confirmed member of the Legacy:
                          • Lancelot is undoubtedly the most famous Knight of the Round Table, assuming you aren't including Arthur in that group. Skilled in all arms, dashing to behold, the idea of Lancelot being the true founder of the Legacy is sadly greater than the actual probability of such. While many Knights strive to follow in his example (at least, for his positive traits, rather than his flaws), there exists little evidence that he was even a Mage, let alone a Legacy Founder. Many conversations had with the Changeling Courts of Britain, rare as they can be, have them claiming the invincible knight as one of their own, something they also have little evidence for, but it's still enough to contest the Founder claim.
                          • Bedivere, first Knight of the Round Table, would be a logical person to look towards in regards to the Legacy's founding. Legends say that he himself was a sorcerer who required Arthur's pardon to remain unmolested by townsfolk for his strange powers. Others point to his disability, something which should have prevented him from fighting in the first place, causing him no issue, which would be possible with magic on one's side. Still, there isn't a ton of evidence for Bedivere's claim, even if the Changelings do not claim him to be one of their's.
                          • Lanval, one of the most minor Knights of the Round that is known today, is the only absolutely confirmed member of the Legacy. His Daimonomika is the oldest the Legacy still possesses, a silver chalice with runic inscriptions lining the inside detailing his story. Though he ultimately left Camelot in disgust, he still tried to maintain its ideals for the rest of his life. Because of the solid evidence for his existence, the majority of the Wardens believe him to be the true founder, but there are two discrepancies that prevent it from being certain. The first is that Lanval's Daimonomika is roundabout a 1000 years old, and not 1500, when Camelot would have existed. Time Magic could of course distorted the chalice's past, but the fact remains that the only evidence we have for Lanval being an actual Knight of the Round is his own word. Worse, Lanval himself states directly that he did not start the Legacy, that he was only a minor player amongst its greatest heroes. This was what caused the current debate regarding the Legacy's past, and it still rages on to this day.

                          In the modern day, not all Gallants wear chainmail armor and carry around longswords and shields. When one is skilled in Matter, all apparel can become armor at a moment's notice. But there is still burning in their breast the concept of being a protector, their soul molded into an armor to defend others. Wardens strive to be remembered just as their heroes were, and the best way to do so, at least in good conscious, is to be just as heroic as they were. Though not all succeed, some prevail, and their stories are told by the Wardens for centuries to come.

                          Origins
                          Parentage: Acanthus, Adamantine Arrow
                          Nickname: Gallants
                          Background:
                          Appearance: Wardens are typically strong in body, and many when "on the job" wear some time of gleaming armor and carry a melee weapon or two with them into battle. The sun always seems to catch their hair just right, giving them a halo effect on occasion.

                          Doctrine
                          Prerequisites: Matter 2, Weaponry 2; 2 dots in one of the following Skills: Animal Ken, Athletics, Brawl, Crafts, Expression
                          Initiation: Wardens rely on the traditional squiring relationship of the medieval ages, aged up to adjust for more mages being fully grown adults. When the squire's teacher believes that he understands the basics, he gives him a sword and a suit of armor and sends him off into the world. This does not complete the initiation, it is only finished when the squire suffers a grievous injury in the defense of the innocent. At that point, the instructor is alerted through Fate and/or Time magic and rushes to the squires side to heal their wounds and give them their oaths.
                          Organization: At the round table, all knights are equal, and even a king is not superior to any one of them. So long as a Gallant has been fully initiated, they hold a say at the pentannual meetings, and can vote just as the senior members can. There is still respect to be given to the most senior members of course, but every man can (and often does) have his say, at least a little.

                          Magic
                          Ruling Arcana: Matter
                          Yantras: Gleaming armor (+1), noble heraldry (+1), an Extended Strength + Crafts roll to hammer out the dents in one's armor (+2)
                          Oblations: Traveling to a foreign land, hunting down a monster under your superior's direction, defending someone's life with your sword.

                          Attainments
                          First: Fletcher's Hated Shield
                          Prerequisite: Initiation
                          A true protector never need fear the sting of arrows sent his way. With a brief thought while clenching a shield or some other kind of protective surface, the Gallant draws any incoming projectiles towards the surface, raising their Defense by 2 (3 at Matter 4) against such attacks, which lasts (Matter) turns. This only affects arrows and thrown weaponry: Bullets are simply to fast for this weak pull to affect, and melee weapons tend to be firmly grasped to the point that they cannot be redirected. This attainment is a modification of the "Lodestone" spell, with reach devoted to instant use.
                          Optional: Fate 1
                          A Knight should never serve an unjust lord, and so the Gallant protects himself against betrayal. With a blink of his eyes, he can see if a superior, potential or real, has violated an oath they have made, and vows they have undertaken in the past. This emulates the spell "Interconnections" with reach devoted to instant use.

                          Second: Lancelot's Invincible Hand
                          Prerequisite: Weaponry 3
                          It is said that Lancelot could wield any weapon, even a tree branch, with enough excellence to take on an army. The Gallant replicates this myth by studying the objects they hold in their hands. With a moment of analysis, they gain enough insight to wield an improvised melee weapon as if it were a normal weapon that deals 2 (3 at Matter 4) damage. This benefit lasts for (Matter) turns. This is a custom Ruling effect, with reach devoted to instant use.
                          Optional: Fate 2
                          With a terrific toss, the knight's weapon is sent flying towards their foe with pinpoint accuracy. By taking a turn to aim while wielding a weapon blessed with the above effect, the Gallant gains a bonus on their next attack roll with the weapon equal to (Fate) dots, so long as the Gallant throws the weapon in question. They can use the higher of Athletics or Weaponry for this throw. This emulates a combination of "Quantum Flux" and "Serendipity," with reach devoted to instant use and using an alternative skill.

                          Third: Sword of the King
                          Prerequisite: Matter 3, Increase another select Skill to 2 dots
                          A Knight's weapon can never fail them, so long as they continue to care for it. With a scene of maintenance, the Gallant can adjust their weapon's internal balance, granting them 8-again on any roll to use it in combat, which lasts for a week (month at Matter 4, year at Matter 5). If the Gallant uses this effect on a weapon blessed with the second Attainment, it retains its potency as a weapon for a day/week instead, but does not gain the 8-again benefit. This emulates the spell "Find the Balance," with reach devoted to 8-again and Advanced Duration.
                          Optional: Fate 3
                          Knights take their oaths very seriously; when a promise to them or another is broken, they must act to set things right. Should someone break their word to the knight, regardless of whether or not the Gallant is present, they know what has occurred, and can track down the target over the course of the next week (month at Fate 4, year at Fate 5) to settle the score. This emulates the spell "Oaths Fulfilled," with reach devoted to instant use, Advanced Duration and the tracking effect.

                          Fourth: Soul of Mail
                          Prerequisite: Matter 4, Weaponry 4
                          Even when they shirk off their ornamental protection, a Gallant is never free of armor. A single clenched muscle causes the armor or clothing on top of it to clench as well, making it difficult to scratch and increasing its general armor rating by (Matter) dots for 3 turns. This emulates the spell Aegis, with reach devoted to instant use and changing the primary factor to Potency.
                          Optional: Fate 4
                          Many oaths prove to be the downfall of a knight in legend, and yet until that day comes, the knight remains as resolute as ever. The Gallant can choose to add a fatal flaw to their armor with the above attainment, a type of weapon or force that is guaranteed to ignore its armor rating when applied and destroy the armor in the process. In exchange, the armor's effectiveness now lasts for a year, and reduces its Defense penalty by 3 for that duration. Only 1 suit of armor or apparel can benefit from this effect at a time.

                          Fifth: Passing of the Sword
                          Prerequisite: Matter 5, increase another select skill to 2 dots
                          As great as legends can be, they are ultimately ephemeral to the passage of time. But, that spiritual nature can sometimes prove a benefit to the knight. By spending a point of Mana whenever the Knight would be hurt by a physical non-living object, the object turns see-through the moment it touches the Knight or their equipment and passes through them like a ghost. This object can be absolutely anything, so long as it is not alive, and is Size 7 or smaller. This attainment can be used multiple times in a turn by spending points of Mana each time. This emulates the spell "Ghostwall" with both points of reach devoted to making it reflexive, with a point of Mana.
                          Optional: Fate 5
                          Knights live to protect others, and through this mindset, protect themselves. When a Knight is fighting to protect someone they have sworn an oath to, they can extend the main attainment's effect to whoever they are defending as if the object had struck them instead, so long as that individual is within the same area of the Gallant up to a large warehouse in size. In addition, the size of object which can be made insubstantial is increased up to Size 15, but ONLY if it would hit the ward. If the knight would be solely struck, then they cannot handle such large objects.​

                              The Inheritors of Ichor

                              There exists a tradition among Spirit Mages, one where a given Mage entreats the leader of a spirit court to "adopt" them as a metaphysical child, giving up some agency in exchange for Fallen, malleable power. The Inheritors of Ichor are not a Left-Handed Path, but they are looked down upon a bit by most of the Orders for their willingness to stray away from humanity. Becoming a Demigod means accepting some of the weaknesses of the ephemeral as well as the benefits, but the Legacy's members view it as a worthwhile deal. After all, one of the downsides of most Legacy Attainments is how limited they can be. In comparison, becoming a Demigod grants flexible strength which nonetheless invites no Paradox or Dissonance. If that power comes with some strings attached, well, that's just the price of doing business with spirits.
                              The Inheritors have a presence among the Seers of the Throne, but NOT among those of Nemesis' get. Their goals of dividing the Material from the Ephemeral run counter to the Legacy's goal of blending the two sides of reality in one's own body. Rather, it is the Raptor and Unity whose ministries most often promote this Legacy's benefits, for it fits their goals well of taking away free will and individuality, including one's own.

                              Origins
                              Parentage: Thyrsus, Silver Ladder, Seers of the Throne
                              Nickname: Demigods (formal), Half-Bloods (informal)
                              Background: Demigods generally come in two types: Those who lack power and want a quick way to obtain it, and those who have it and want to further tie themselves to the spirit world. The former are not looked down upon for their desire, for most mages have at one point or another felt the same way, but the latter do end up being a bit more respected.
                              Appearance: The further a Mage advances in the Legacy, the more off they end up looking. A Demigod with Influence over fire might end up looking brighter, with hair that gleams red in the light when they use their powers. A Master with Influence over Wealth might have change literally falling out of pockets and sleeves.

                              Doctrine
                              Prerequisites: Spirit 2, Life 1, Persuasion 2, 2 dots in one of the following Skills: Athletics, Empathy, Occult, Socialize, Survival
                              Initiation: Under the watch of a senior member, who acts as a witness, a potential Demigod entreats a spirit court leader to adopt them. This spirit MUST be at least Rank 5, reducing the list of potential candidates considerably. If they are successful in persuading the spirit, then the spirit cuts itself, suffering a level of lethal damage and letting the student drink the spilled blood. This Essence melds with the student's body and soul, causing the Initiation to be complete. Every time the student wants to advance in the Legacy, they must repeat the appeal process, with a senior Legacy member overseeing the process.
                              Organization: Demigods treat the master - student bond as mattering very little, because a Half-Blood's true master is their spirit parent. Thus, most students will advance through self-study then actual instruction. If the teacher is contacted for assisting the student's promotion, they will first test the student's advancement in the Spirit Arcana to ensure that their time is not being wasted, as callous as that might sound. After all, if the student isn't ready, then they aren't ready, it is as simple as that.

                              Magic
                              Ruling Arcana: Spirit
                              Yantras: Imagery evoking the Demigod's Influence (+1), Bargaining for favors with spirits using an extended Manipulation + Persuasion roll (+2), the spilling of one's own blood (+1)
                              Oblations: Consuming spirit flesh, meditating in proximity to one's Influence, sleeping in the Shadow

                              Attainments:
                              First: Divine Inheritance
                              Prerequisite: Initiation
                              After joining the god's bloodline, the Demigod gains access to one of the Influences held by the spirit. This Influence must be one the spirit has at 5+ dots. It has a number of dots equal to the Demigod's learned attainments in this legacy. In addition, the Demigod gains access to the Honorary Rank attainment early, instead of at Spirit 4.
                              This blessing comes with the drawbacks of the Ephemeral. First, the Demigod gains a Ban, an action they either cannot do, or must do, in a given situation. This Ban is dictated by their spirit parent. In addition, the spirit parent always counts as the Demigod's Bane, regardless of Rank.

                              Second: Hidden Cousins Unveiled
                              Prerequisite: Persuasion 3
                              The Demigod attunes themselves enough to the Shadow to see its subjects dwelling among the Material. The Mage can see and speak with spirits in Twilight, inhabiting a physical vessel, or any Manifestation Conditions in the area. This emulates the spell "Exorcist's Eye," with Reach devoted to making it Lasting.
                              Optional: Life 2
                              Animals are closer to the spiritual than mankind is, and sometimes they hold important secrets to both sides of the Gauntlet. At a moment's notice, the Mage can communicate with a given species of animal as if they were one of their own. This emulates the spell "Speak with Beasts" with Reach devoted to instant use and Advanced Duration.

                              Third: Relative's Plea
                              Prerequisite: Spirit 3, increase another Skill to 2 dots
                              When in danger of being attacked by a distant relative, the Demigod can quickly persuade it avoid doing them harm. At any time, by spending a point of Mana, the Mage converts the next (Spirit) failures on the spirit's (or its material representation's) part to Dramatic Failures, as long as they occur within the next 2 (3 at Spirit 4) turns. This emulates the spell "Gremlins" with Reach assigned to Sensory Range and making the spell reflexive by spending Mana.
                              Optional: Life 3
                              The Demigod develops more fine, though subtle, control over spirits. With an action, the Demigod can touch one spirit or living thing and control its natural instincts, commanding them to feast on Essence or avoid it's Bane. Only spirits and their living representations can be controlled this way, and only if their Composure/Rank is less than 2 (3 with Spirit 4). If the spirit shares an Influence with the Demigod, this is increased to less than (Influence) dots. This emulates the spell "Control Instincts," with Reach used for instant use and commanding living supernatural entities.

                              Fourth: Child of Two Worlds
                              Prerequisite: Spirit 4, Persuasion 4
                              Taking one step in the Material, the Demigod takes the next in the Shadow. With an instant action, the Demigod transports themselves across the Gauntlet so long as the local Gauntlet Strength is less than the Demigod's Spirit dots. This emulates the spell "World Walker" with Reach assigned to instant use.
                              Optional: Life 4
                              The more Ichor flows through the Mage's veins, the more malleable their form becomes. The Mage can, with a scene-long ritual, turn into any animal with a Size between 0 and 7 for as long as they wish. This animal must be related to their Influence, either directly (Influence: Dogs to turn into a dog or other canine) or indirectly (Influence: Air to turn into a bird). Every time they encounter something that would trigger that animal's instincts, they must roll Composure + Resolve + Gnosis to avoid giving in to the instinct. This emulates the spell "Shapechanging," with Reach used for Advanced Duration.

                              Fifth: Full Inheritance
                              Prerequisite: Spirit 5, Life 5, increase another Skill to 2 dots
                              At this point, the Ichor in the Demigod's veins has overtaken their natural blood, and their form follows suit. The Mage permanently takes on a spirit form, entering Twilight as Spirit Ephemera. The Mage no longer ages, and can acquire new Influences at 3 XP per dot, so long as they study how the Influence works from a consenting spirit. With a scene-long ritual, the Mage can return to the World of Flesh physically by granting themselves Manifestation Conditions for up to a Week. Finally, the Mage now qualifies as an eligible spirit parent for new members of the Legacy, so long as they are in their spirit form for the ritual. This emulates the spells "Twilit Body" and "Bind Spirit", with Reach used to make it Lasting (for Twilit Body) and Advanced Duration plus changing the Primary Factor to Potency (for Bind Spirit).

                                      The Dragons of Decadence

                                      Wealth is the only reward that matters, the only thing which matters in this world. Emotions, Ideas, these ephemeral concepts will be debunked, countered and forgotten in due time. Only cold hard cash and resources can provide one with what is good in life, not the other way around. At least, that's how the Dragons would put it.
                                      Founded by a Seer Tetrarch of Mammon, the Dragons of Decadence are unapologetically greedy individuals. They desire luxury like the common man desires air, it is something they must obtain, they MUST have. And what they have, they guard fiercely, allowing no one to take their treasure except as part of a ploy to gain ever more. Few have stepped into a Dragon's lair and walked away both alive AND with treasure in hand, for distance and space are a resource all on their own, and the Dragons make their intruders spend them heavily.
                                      Obviously, the Dragons are almost exclusively Seers, what with their indulgences and greed. Those very few members on the side of the Pentacle conceal their membership as best they can, but sooner, rather than later, their desires for more overwhelm their discretion and the truth soon comes out. It's not illegal to be a legacy member per se, but the obvious connection to the Exarch of Matter leaves few doubts as to a member's "true" allegiance.

                                      Origins
                                      Parentage: Mastigos, Seers of the Throne
                                      Nickname: Drakes
                                      Background: Drakes are always greedy, and often come into the Legacy with a sizable hoard already. Senior members even look down upon potential applicants with a less than 7 figure income, but on rare occasions, a sufficiently motivated "commoner" manages to squeak their way in.
                                      Appearance:​ The appearance of dragons is always a luxurious one. Whether that be the best tailored suits, extravagant dresses, or things which barely look like clothes and more like art statements, so long as it LOOKS rich, then it's condoned. Regardless of the clothes, Dragons are never without several pieces of jewelry to highlight their value.

                                      Doctrine
                                      Prerequisites: Matter 2; Intimidation 2, 2 dots in one of the following Skills: Academics, Drive, Larceny, Politics, Socialize
                                      Initiation: The would-be Drake must acquire, in some form or fashion, enough gold or platinum (silver is too cheap for such an act) to shape into an egg which fits into their palm. The senior member then snatches the egg from the Drake's hand, to show how frustrating having one's hoard taken will be, crunches it into pieces and jams those pieces into the initiate's eyes, which are miraculously unharmed by the experience.
                                      Organization: Those who have the most, rule the most. The higher one's net worth or material wealth, the higher they climb the ranks. Seniority means nothing in a Legacy ruled by greed.

                                      Magic
                                      Ruling Arcana: Matter
                                      Yantras: Clothing with reptile scales (+1), diamond-encrusted jewelry (+1), a lengthy and aggressive negotiation to siphon a target's wealth using Manipulation + Intimidation (+2)
                                      Oblations: Eating dishes garnished with gold leaf, liquidating assets, "discussing" matters with fellow elites

                                      Attainments:
                                      First: Find the Missing Coin
                                      Prerequisite: Initiation
                                      With a slight scrunch of the eyebrows, the Dragon inspects their hoard, to see if everything is as it should be. With a scene-long ritual, the Dragon enchants an area up to a small room (a large room at Matter 4) in size, so that they know exactly how much of their wealth persists therein. Only one such space can be maintained at a time, and it lasts for a day/week/month/year (Matter 2/3/4/5). From that point onward, as an instant action, the Drake can detect up to (Matter) chosen substances within the area. These substances must be items which the Dragon owns or once owned, the Mage cannot lay claim onto items they've never seen before. This emulates the spell "Detect Substance" with reach devoted to Advanced Duration.
                                      Optional: Space 1
                                      Whenever an owned item is removed from the location, the Dragon is able to continue tracking it even beyond the hoard's space. So long as the hoard itself persists, anything taken from it can be traced back to the thief. This emulates the spell Locate Object, with reach used to track missing objects.

                                      Second: Fear-Shaped Direction
                                      Prerequisite: Intimidation 3
                                      Dragons are terrifying, and nothing, not even the inanimate, is immune to the paralyzing terror they emit. As an instant action, the Drake compels an object they can see to activate, triggering as if it had been normally operated. This emulates the spell "Remote Control" with reach used for instant use and sensory range.
                                      Optional: Space 2
                                      Sometimes fools speak far more than they should, endangering the Dragon's goals with their blithe ignorance and loose lips. The Drake can put a stop to that with an instant action, causing a target they can see to suffer paralyzing fear as long as the Dragon's Matter dots exceeds the target's Composure. For 2 turns (3 at Matter 4), the target suffers a penalty on all social rolls equal to the remainder of Matter - Composure, and cannot even attempt social interaction without spending 1 Willpower. This emulates the spell "Isolation" with reach assigned to instant use and sensory range.

                                      Third: Hide the Precious
                                      Prerequisite: Matter 3, increase another skill to 2 dots
                                      To best avoid one's wealth being stolen, the number one priority is to ensure that no one finds it. With a scene of locking up the items, covering them up or something else to hide them away, the Drake disguises their precious objects as being worth dirt, preventing any form of mundane detection for up to a week (a month at Matter 4, and a year at Matter 5). In a Clash of Wills, the Drake gains (Matter) automatic successes against mystical detection. This emulates the spell "Hidden Hoard," with reach used for Advanced Scale and Advanced Duration.
                                      Optional: Space 3
                                      The Drake no longer needs to be there in person to hide their treasure, not when they hold the strings connecting them. The main attainment can now be used at sympathetic range, so long as the Drake has an object at hand which formally shows their ownership of the treasure in question and spends a point of Mana. This also expands the attainment's effects to hide immaterial wealth as well, such as bank accounts or encrypted secrets. So long as the Drake has some material item which represents the immaterial treasure, they can now hide it as well, concealing up to (Space) Merit dots of the stuff from detection. Needless to say, tax collectors despise the Dragons of Decadence for this reason.

                                      Fourth: Deny the Songbird's Flight
                                      Prerequisite: Matter 4, Space 4, Intimidation 4
                                      Once something is part of the Dragon's hoard, it will never leave, no matter how hard it tries. After a scene of aligning an area's design to fit the Dragon's tastes, it becomes enchanted with a curse to return from whence it came. Whenever a piece of treasure would leave the space, it immediately dissolves into a gas and flies back to the center of the hoard, where it reforms back into it's normal shape. This Attainment combines the spells "State Change" and "Ban," devoting reach to Advanced Duration and changing an object directly to a gas (for State Change) and to Advanced Scale and Advanced Duration (for Ban).

                                      Fifth: My Ever-Growing Value
                                      Prerequisite: Matter 5, increase another skill to 2 dots
                                      Inflation is a terrible thing, for it ruins what makes a Drake's treasure so precious. Of course, that only applies to what's on the open market; Dragons love to stack their piles high with gold and jewels and then never actually use them. With a scene of inspecting their treasure, the Drake causes it to grow and expand, increasing the object's Size by 3 minus the object's durability. This is a permanent change, which allows the eldest of wyrms to accumulate obscene amounts of material wealth over time. This emulates the spell "Shrink and Grow," with reach used for changing the primary factor to duration, and making the change Lasting.
                                      Optional: Space 5
                                      Of course with growing objects, one must need a suitable space to grow alongside them. When using the main attainment, the Mage can also choose to increase the size of the room the treasure is located in by (Space) steps on the Standard Scale (Area) chart, so long as the room is not already bigger than a large room in size. A given space can only be affected this way once. This emulates the spell "Secret Room," with reach devoted to making the change Lasting.
