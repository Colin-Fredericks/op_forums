    Mystery Cults and "Darkness"

    10-24-2024, 08:49 AM
    So I have a player who is a Silver Ladder mage. 1 dot in Order Status, 5 dots in Mystery Cult Influence - so low level within the caucus, but super important to this cult. I understand the mechanics of what he wants, but I've been thinking about flavor and tone the past few days.

    Chronicles of Darkness is supposed to be "like the real world, but darker". And one of the things I appreciate about the game is the relative realism of the setting. In the real world, cults can get quite dark indeed. I'm just trying to imagine what this mystery cult is like from the perspective of an NPC the player's mage is interacting with.

    In the real world, the deeper you get into a cult, the more likely it is to involve financial loss, risk of public shaming, or worse, kidnapping, confinement, slavery, human trafficking, physical and sexual abuse. I'm pretty sure neither the player or I are interested in getting that dark. But I also want to maintain an element of realism with touches of creepy spookiness which fits the overall tone of CoD and MtAw.

    I'm interested in how other Storytellers and players have handled this element of Mage.

          I would point out that "Mystery Cult" is just used as a generic term for any secretive group with some level of engagement with or connection to the supernatural weirdness of the setting, rather than to specifically reference high control groups based around charismatic narcissists.

          Your average mystery cult more closely resembles a chapter of the Freemasons (and some conspiracy theories attributed to them) rather than NXIVM.

          In terms of the ones connected to the Awakened in particular, you need to think about what the motives of mages are in gathering such groups. While the occasional one who wants to gratify themselves by setting up like a living god is not non-existent, the networks built and maintained by the Orders as a whole are generally concerned with being a means to gather and exert influence, investigate Mysteries, and attempt to direct know the members of that group engage with occult knowledge in accordance with ideology. The kind of abuse you're talking about is not unknown in the Silver Ladder Cryptopoly, but it's the kind of thing somebody has to keep hidden from the caucus lest they be deemed the wrong kind of thearch and punished accordingly.

          That's not to say mystery cults under the Awakened aren't subject to their own kind of abuse. Look at one of the sample groups from the corebook, the Guild. A network of people through an MMORPG isn't exactly the best way to traffic and sexually abuse people, but it's still a group being utilised in a Free Council experiment that they did not consent to. Between that and how the members might be directed towards doing things that may be hazardous or traumatic to them (especially considering that being Sleepers means they still suffer breaking points to Integrity for supernatural encounters), there's still a lot that can be morally dubious to them.

              I guess there is a larger philosophical question of to what extent mages in MtAw are "baddies". The overarching theme of the game is that even good intentions, ruthlessly pursued to the point of obsession, can lead to bad results. Personal horror is somewhat downplayed in CoD games as compared to WoD, but it's still in the DNA of the games, especially in the integrity/wisdom mechanic. Lovecraft's Charles Dexter Ward is just your average Mysterium Moros in game terms.

              I'm not sure that the Freemasons were entirely harmless in their Victorian heyday. There is a spectrum between like Charles Portis' ​Masters of Atlantis and Moore and Campbell's From Hell. Or think of the various mystery cults represented in the Sherlock Homes stories. I remember when I was 10 reading "The Five Orange Pips" and being terrified that the Klu Klux Klan was going to murder me. My mom had to patiently explain that it was extremely unlikely that the Klan was going to come after a white kid living in the suburbs.

              Not to tip my hand too much to coming attractions for wyrdhamster, but even in the 60s, for every Ken Kesey and the Merry Pranksters or Robert Anton Wilson and Discordians there was a Charles Manson and the Family or Jim Jones and the People's Temple.

                  The big things, as this thread are concerned, are a) what all are your players comfortable with and have consented to to having as part of the game, b) keep in mind that the cult-like abuses you're exploring are more likely to manifest in Awakened abuses of Sleepers rather than strictly Awakened-on-Awakened abuses, with the caveat of c) Awakening is an allegory of the 1% and how their power tends to excuse their abuses as far as society goes, so do with that what you will.
