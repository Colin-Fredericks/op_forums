                It's not stated directly, but implied by word choices the write-up on Sympathy. The book consistently uses "the connection from A to B" rather than "the connection between A and B."

                It also matches up with a common sense understanding of those connections in real-world scenarios. Consider a stalker and stalkee, where the stalker has yet to reveal himself. The stalker has a connection to the stalkee, but the stalkee doesn't even know the stalker exists and so has no connection to him.

                EDIT: For sympathetic range spells, you're meant to use a connection from the caster to the spell's subject. Suppose the stalker above is a mage. He might have a strong connection to the target for sympathic range spells. The stalkee would have no connection back to the stalker for sympathetic range spells, though.

                                            On my earlier sympathy question, I actually found something after reading everything through more carefully: On p. 175-176, under the Veil Sympathy spell, under the +2 reach option, "The mage may suppress all the subject’s sympathetic links. This effect applies in both directions; not only does the subject lose his links to loved ones, but those people lose their links to him. The spell is Withstood by the strongest connection the subject has". This does actually quite unambiguously demonstrate that Sympathetic links are in fact only one-way, as the other person's potential link back to the subject seems to be explicitly distinct.
