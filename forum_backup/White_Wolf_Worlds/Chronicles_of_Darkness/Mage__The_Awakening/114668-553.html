    Originally posted by KaiserAfini View Post
    What are acceptable limits for Rewrite History with only temporal sympathy ? The spell says that, with Temporal Sympathy, it may change any detail of the subject's history, so long as its possible. But then we have Reach options for skills, attributes and backgrounds. But shouldn't the default use allow that, provided you have temporal sympathy that goes back far enough ?

    Wouldn't that mean the Reaches are more of a brute force approach that doesn't require temporal sympathy ? If yes, then it feels thematically inconsistent. If no, then why all the extra Reach requirements? I can understand the inertia of moving things related to supernatural templates, but moving skills and merits seems like a driving thematic use, like becoming a well established member of a Mystery Cult or gaining some Status in a suspicious organization (like becoming a high clearance guard or researcher) when infiltrating them.

    So does that mean that by default, the spell with temporal sympathy could only rewrite minor things ? If Rewrite History only affects the subject, what could those things be ? How does it account for social merits, like Contacts, Allies or Resources ?
    My take is that baseline Rewrite History allows the player specify changes they want to make (Person X failed their AP History exam), and the ST choses how those changes affect the nitty-gritty of resultant timeline. By default, I assume this means the new timeline deviates as little as possible, adjusting much like one affected by Time-conjunctive Quarentine. The Reach effects then are the player exerting fine control over the changes (Person X failed their AP History exam, and thus never got into their first choice college, so they never joined that Mystery Cult)

                            Originally posted by John Courage View Post

                            Assuming that can happen
                            It can, and the supplement Signs of Sorcery even talks about it.

                            Originally posted by John Courage
                            how would you apply post-cognition?
                            By the usual way that this works, Postcognition is going to reveal a past as though the person always existed in the same universe as everybody else, even if they have no memory of those events. Sometimes it may look at things as though the person's existence only even started when they Awakened.

                            I think sometimes the easiest way to manage the whole thing is to say that their Awakening is such a profound transformation that it severs most or all of their temporal sympathy to their Sleeper life at all, which can leave some mages wondering if they might not have had a previous life they're just highly deluded about.

                            Still, I think it's okay to occasionally have an instance where a person can be examined to find that they have an impossible past. I'm sure there would be locations where the Mystery is that is how Postcognition works on everything.
