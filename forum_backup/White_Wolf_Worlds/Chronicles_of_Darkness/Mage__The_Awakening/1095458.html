    Why M:tAw Resonates with Me (And Probably Several Others)

    05-22-2017, 09:13 AM
    Ever since I began reading the 1st edition corebook of Mage: the Awakening back in mid-2014, I was instantly hooked. Since then, I've bought several supplements of it, still looking at getting more, played in several chronicles, and setting a chronicle myself. One thing is certainly clear: I. Love. Mage. When I first read the corebook, I couldn’t put my finger on it, but the game spoke to me on a deep level. Now that I am more learned, I’ve begun to point my finger on all the different aspects that relate to me, as well as quite a few others.

    Alright, I’m just gonna say it and it doesn’t matter to me how you react: I am a sorcerer. Yes, I mean someone who practices actual magic, casts spells, enchants items, etc. No, I am not someone who dabbles in New Age mumbo-jumbo mistaken as occult practices, I use real magic.

    What is “Real Magic”?
    Now, it’s important to note what I actually mean by “real magic”, since there are so many conceptions of it. Thankfully, real magic isn’t like Supernal magic (though there are some commonalities, which I’ll get to later). It’s not flashes, bangs, and rapid physical transformations, it’s much more subtle than that. Conceptually speaking, The Force from Star Wars is pretty accurate. While it’s certainly not gonna let you make force grips or sprout lightning from your fingers, it is certainly about manipulating the natural energy that permeates throughout all life and the world, and can guide you in ways that pure logic and reason simply can’t. Another accurate depiction can be seen in the film Spirited Away, which comes as no surprise since it was literally made as an instruction manual for magic (but that’s for another thread).

    Real magic is not supernatural in nature (I suppose you could argue that it’s paranormal, but I wouldn’t say that’s completely accurate), nor is it loud, glittery, or rare. It’s as common as birches, and just as natural and showy. It does involve spirits, and you might argue that would make it supernatural, but I would argue that spirits are also perfectly natural as well, they just happen to be incorporeal is all.

    Who Are Sorcerers?
    Now that real magic has been defined, what exactly is a sorcerer? Sorcerers are simply people who have magical talents and are more sensitive to spiritual activity. That’s really all there is to it, no deals with the devil, no superpowers, just people who happen to have talents and senses most do not, just as they lack talents many others have.

    Being a sorcerer myself, I can sense things like spirits and I have some magical talents (I’m particularly good with divination, which is a generic term for gathering information in general, not just telling the future), but I’m no good at sports and I’m anosmic (I have no sense of smell).

    In other words, sorcerers aren’t special; we’re just like you. You may not be able to speak with spirits.... but there’s probably something you can do that I can’t.

    I Don’t Believe You
    That’s fine, and to be fair, your disbelief is probably a rational reaction. That doesn’t mean you’re right, but you’re not being irrational, either. How does that make sense? Lemme explain:

    What is the world to a mole? Dark, cramped, a series of chambers and burrows, and colored with scents. Where then, do stars fit in the picture? They don’t. You can try to explain what they are, how they form, what they do, etc. but at the end of the day, all you’ll wind up doing is wasting your time and frustrating the mole. It can’t sense them in any way and they don’t impact its life in any significant way.

    Does that mean stars don’t exist? Of course not, but damned if you’re gonna convince the mole they do, let alone teach it astronomy. Same deal with magic. Most people can’t sense the phenomena that sorcerers do in any real, significant way. The best they can get is a few hints here and there; and usually only in places where such things concentrate, much in the same manner as the mole feeling the sun’s heat through the earth when it’s close to the surface.

    So, much like the mole, disbelief of magic is a rational reaction for those who are not sensitive to it. But just because you can't sense it, doesn’t mean it isn’t there.

    Awakened & Sleepers
    Now that we’ve got all that outta the way, it’s time to get into the meat of this thread: how Mage: the Awakening relates to real-life magic and sorcerers.

    We’ll start with the difference between the Awakened and Sleepers. While you’ve probably already noted that the Awakened have many differences to what I described with real-life sorcerers, there are some correlations. For starters, the Awakened have senses that Sleepers simply do not: they see the invisible and hear the inaudible, much in the same way that sorcerers are more sensitive to things outside of purely material existence.

    Sleepers are like the mole in the above analog;, they simply can’t sense the supernatural like the Awakened can. Sure, there’s no Sleeping Curse that harms non-sorcerers when they’re faced with magic or anything, but the alienation that the Awakened suffer is a common experience for sorcerers, especially in this day and age.

    The Lie
    While the real world is no illusion, there’s certainly more to it than most people acknowledge, especially in the western world. Much of modern society says that magic isn’t real, that science is the be-all and end-all of truth. If you think magic is real, then you clearly need to be reeducated.... in the psych ward. Okay, that’s a bit of an exaggeration, but not by much.

    There’s a very real denial of magic in today’s world; those who practice it are often ridiculed and sometimes even ostracized. Most sorcerers don’t even realize their magical potential, simply because they’ve been told all their life that magic isn’t real, so they disbelieve it themselves, even if their senses are literally telling them otherwise.

    Awakening
    There’s this thing often termed The Trials. Roughly every 27 years in their life, sorcerers experience a time where their magic and senses heighten significantly. The first time this happens is generally the all-or-nothing point of a sorcerer’s life where they either accept their magic or lose it.

    For the weakly-gifted (just like any other talent, there’s variance among sorcerers), this time doesn’t make as much fuss. For the more strongly-gifted though, it’s a lot more stressful. Combine a strong sorcerer and a society that says they shouldn’t believe in their magic, the results can be devastating.

    Sounds quite a bit like Awakening, doesn’t it?

    Mage Sight
    This is actually a real talent. Granted, it’s nothing like what’s described in the game. It’s simply a general sense of magic. It’s also not something every sorcerer has, in fact, it’s pretty rare. Still, it’s very much a thing in real magic.

    High Speech
    Like Mage Sight, this is also very much a thing in real magic, although it’s entirely different from what’s described in the game. In real magic, High Speech are particular words sorcerers say when casting spells. What the words are varies from individual to individual, as it’s purely instinctual. My High Speech is going to be very different from another sorcerer’s High Speech (I don’t know mine yet, and I don’t know when I’ll learn them; like most things with magic, it comes when it comes).

    Yantras
    Remember when I said Spirited Away is essentially an instruction manual for magic? Well, what instruction manual would be complete without teaching a common spell? In the film’s case, it teaches engacho, a spell used to dispel negative influences.



    When Chihiro puts her fingers together, she’s performing a real-life equivalent of a Yantra. She’s channelling the idea of containing the negative influence into the space of her fingers, and when Kamaji chops her gesture apart, he’s channelling the idea of destroying the influence, which is why he says “Evil begone.” as he does it.

    One well-known example, and one that relates more directly to the game itself, is the Tarot deck. Each card represents certain concepts and you put them into a certain context in spreads, which allows a sorcerer to read a specific message from the cards they drew, their talent guiding them to draw the right cards.

    Wisdom & Paradox
    The way of magic is often termed as the Way of the Wise, and for very good reason. Magic isn’t a plaything; it’s a powerful force, like fire or electricity. Misused, it can harm you, maybe even kill you. It’s something you need to control, otherwise you’re gonna make a huge mess; and not just for you, but for a lot of others.

    Being a horror game, Mage: the Awakening focuses on this particular aspect of magic and makes it a lot more horrible. After all, that’s what horror is supposed to do, it shows us the darker aspects, often by exaggerating them or at the very least fully exposing them. Real magic doesn’t tear away at people’s sanity in the manner that Awakening magic does, but it can still do some serious damage nonetheless.

    Conclusion
    Mage: the Awakening, at least for me, but probably for a lot of other sorcerers out there, says that magic is real, that our senses aren’t lying to us. It tends to focus on where magic can go wrong, but that’s simply part and parcel with a horror game, and it’s a valuable lesson besides.

    What’s even better is that it doesn’t just relate to sorcerers, it can relate to just about anyone. Magic can simply be an analogy for potential and the consequences our actions can have in a generic sense, not just in the specific context of real magic. To think before you act is a valuable lesson for anyone to heed. It just so happens to resonate more strongly for sorcerers in particular, in a similar vein to The Matrix, a film that audiences worldwide can relate to on some level, but hits home a lot more for trans people, because it was made by trans people.

    I don’t doubt for a second that at least one developer of the game has the gift; there’s just so much that aligns with my experiences in real magic. Just as there’s so much in The Matrix that aligns with trans people. So, if any of you guys are reading this: thank you, and excellent work. If I’m wrong about that, though, still kudos to you for your outstanding research.

    And for those who find this entire OP to be ridiculous: well, at least we can all agree that Mage: the Awakening is an awesome game.
