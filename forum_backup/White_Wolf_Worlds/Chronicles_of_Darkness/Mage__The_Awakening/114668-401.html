            Thought it over a bit (and reread the original question). What I meant is that the way Harry convinced Slughorn seems to be a combination of Charmed, for nudging the two into the path that leads to Slughorn drinking and reminiscing thus removing one Door, and bonus dice and/or dice tricks on Socialise and Persuasion rolls during their conversation, just like how Exceptional Luck works.

            Applying Charmed in that way removed a Door by making Harry put them into the best situation possible for what Harry wanted, and as such it did not actually affect Slughorn personally and it shouldn't count towards supernatural coercion.
            Your other suggestion, casting on a subject to make them end up in a more receptive state of mind, is more of a grey area. That is a much more direct manipulation of your target, and if the spell would force the target into becoming intoxicated (which I think would be Fate Weaving at the least) regardless of whether they wanted to imbibe or not then I'd definitely say it's forced.

            What I think a Fate mage should do in order to lower Doors (without it being supernatural coercion) is to use a spell that's kinda like Shifting the Odds and the Charmed Condition in that it makes the subject of the spell (possibly the mage) come across the target in a more receptive context. I don't think it should remove more than one Door, and might also end up failing if it's not possible for the target to end up in such a context (just like Shifting the Odds can fail if what you're looking for literally can't be reached). After that, Exceptional Luck on your own rolls to make you more persuasive.

                            No, definitely not.

                            Giving Perfected material banes a damage boost would involve somehow predicting the effects of all material banes - does Perfected Fire do extra damage to vampires? Should Perfected Gold do extra damage to a spirit that has gold as its bane? What about material weaknesses that aren't straight damage?

                            Simply too much bother, which is why we went for "removes all other specifications, then acts like X". So Siderite counts as cold iron, meteroic iron, flame-forged iron, stainless steel, cast iron - whatever adjectives the supernatural being of choice bolts onto "iron", Siderite counts as it. As Lunargent counts for Silver.

                                    On top of being much simpler, I also prefer that perfected materials won't cause more damage.
