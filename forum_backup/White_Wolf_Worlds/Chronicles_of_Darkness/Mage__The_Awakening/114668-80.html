                I think it's more appropriate to say that there's a spirit for every kind of thing. Thus, if the spirit governing dogs in a given area is destroyed, and a suitably similar proxy can't be found, dogs in the area may move on, start behaving erratically, sicken, and in general disappear. But at the point where the spirit's destruction starts having a noticeable impact on the Fallen World, you aren't dealing with the Shadow patron of one dog, but rather the patron of all dogs within a given area. As for those it influences, I'd imagine losing or coming to a place where the spiritual makeup doesn't support dogs, for example, would be akin to moving into a new neighborhood, in that there'd be a need for adjustment, but eventually a new spirit would arise to take the reins. But losing a patron wouldn't necessarily harm those that it influences, beyond possibly impacting their behavior.

                    If the spirit of a thing dies it doesn't necessarily damage the thing. Without a spirit though it might become a thing that no one will bother wasting any emotional energy on so it could easily get worn down or thrown away. It could get a new spirit inside it in the unlikely event that someone managed to fight against the spiritual ennui it naturally generates and take care of it enough for a mote to take hold and grow from there.

                    Another, similar way to look at it is as if the thing that lost its spirit has become soulless. It becomes a thing without "purpose", fortunately for most things they're either inanimate or can run on instinct so it may not appear to affect them that much. Metaphysically speaking though, they're dead on the inside. On the bright side, like a soulless person they can have that intrinsic spark restored. Even more fortunately, they can have it more easily restored than a soulless person can have a soul restored to them.
