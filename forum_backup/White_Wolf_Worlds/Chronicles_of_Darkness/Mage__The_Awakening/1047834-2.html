    Originally posted by basketcase View Post

    Sure (although their is an ST in every game), but my question to Bradford although if you Mrmdubois or anyone else for that matter have a thought on why you would want yantra to be limited?

    It not a matter of whether I want Yantras to be limited, but rather that the actual rules clearly both limit the type, scope, nature and use of Yantras and detail how a mage may expand their repertoire of potential Yantras with merits and legacies (and I expect more rules in Signs of Sorcery).

    Nevertheless, I believe that the listed Yantras are indeed sufficiently broad to still be meaningful and thematic, and that the effective free-form Yantra rules as suggested by some would be problematic. Mage abilities are already sufficiently broad and powerful enough without the need to permit virtually anything to provide even more bonuses. It also eliminates potential arguments or delays at the game table in that there's no need to discuss how every rock or paperweight is relevant to every spell. Lastly, limiting Yantras makes matters like legacies and purchasing and using the shadow name and similar merits all the more important, something that was definitely a design decision in 2e.

                Okay, folks. Here is an exhaustive list of everything that can be used as a Yantra, paraphrased from the book.

                Places:
                • A Demesne that is semiotically linked to the spell (+2)
                • Any environment that is semiotically linked to the spell (+1)
                • A supernal verge that is semiotically linked to the spell (+2)
                Actions:
                • Concentrating on the spell (+2 to a spell with Duration > 1 turn, but the spell ends early if the mage takes any damage or a non-reflexive action during the spell’s duration)
                • Intoning the spell in High Speech (+2 dice, requires High Speech Merit, cannot be used reflexively)
                • The Skill associated with a Rote, also known as a Mudra (+ [Mage’s dots in the associated Skill], to a maximum of +5. Only usable with Rotes.)
                • Writing the spell in High Speech (+2 dice, requires High Speech Merit, the spell ends if the rune is disrupted or damaged)
                Tools:
                • A coin, cup, mirror, rod, or wand made of a Path-appropriate material that is semiotically linked to the spell (+1)
                  • Appropriate materials for Acanthus include glass, crystal, silver, rapiers, bows, any “precise” weapon, and anything reflective
                  • Appropriate materials for Mastigos include iron, brass, leather, curved swords, whips, any “cruel” weapon, and anything that has been worked
                  • Appropriate materials for Moros include lead, bone, gems, hammers, maces, any crushing weapon, and anything that has been buried
                  • Appropriate materials for Obrimos include steel, petrified wood, gold, double-edged swords, spears, any “noble” weapon, and anything that has been “perfected”
                  • Appropriate materials for Thyrsus include wood, copper, stone, slings, any “hunting” weapon and anything natural
                • Any Order-appropriate object that is semiotically linked to the spell (+1)
                  • Appropriate objects for Adimantine Arrows include any martial tool
                  • Appropriate objects for Guardians of the Veil include cloaks, masks, and veils
                  • Appropriate objects for Mystigogues include books, any writing, and any language
                  • Appropriate objects for Silver Ladder members include any sign of authority
                  • Appropriate objects for Seers of the Throne include any one symbol or word
                  • Appropriate objects for Libertines include any “complex” mechanism or system, as well as any practice from Sleeper culture that the Libertine has the Techne Merit for
                • The symbol of an Exarch that is semiotically linked to the spell (+ [Mage’s dots in the Prelacy Merit], requires Prelacy  ••+)
                • Any object that represents the subject of the spell (Bonus varies based on the object’s Sympathy with the subject - +2 for a representation of the subject as they are in the present, +1 for a representation of the subject in the past, +0 for an indirect representation of the subject. Required to cast spells on the subject with the Sympathetic Range and/or Temporal Sympathy Attainment(s))
                • Any object that is semiotically linked to the spell (Bonus varies based on the difficulty of obtaining the object - +1 for most objects, +2 if the object requires significant effort to obtain, +3 if the object comes from somewhere other than the material realm. The object must be destroyed in the process of casting the spell.)
                • A Shadow Name that is semiotically linked to the spell (+ [Mage’s dots in the Shadow Name Merit], +1 If the Mage’s Cabal all have an appropriate Cabal Theme Merit {stacks with Shadow Name})
                • Spending Mana from a Leyline with resonance that is semiotically linked to the spell (+1)
                • The Mage’s Dedicated Tool (-2 Paradox dice. Can also be used as a Path or Order Tool if it is semiotically linked with the spell)
                Legacy:
                • A place, object, or action that is specifically used as a Yantra by the Mage's Legacy and semiotically associated with the spell (Bonus varies, +1 for most places, objects, and actions, +2 for places, objects, and actions that impose negative Conditions or require rare materials, extra time, or special care. Usually can't be performed reflexively.)
                So, are Yantras limited to the things listed in the book? Yes. Can practically anything be used as a Yantra as long as it is semiotically linked to the spell? Yes. That list includes a fucking lot of shit. The thing that I think gets people mixed up is Path and Order Tools, which are significantly more limited than most other forms of Yantra. But just because a thing can’t be used as a Path or Order tool doesn’t mean it can’t be used as a Yantra. You're really more restricted in what type of Yantra a particular place, action, or object can be used as (and by extension what bonus it can provide) than by whether or not it can be used as a Yantra at all.

                EDIT: Tagging One Dawn in, because I think they would want to see this and I worry they might have come to the conclusion that Yantras are less flexible than they are and abandoned the thread.

                            Originally posted by branford View Post
                            No, practically anything cannot be used as a Yantra just because it might be semiotically linked to the spell, other than some very specific and limited places, shadow names or one-use sacraments.
                            Potential sympathies and sacraments make up a significant portion of the Phenomenal World and sacraments in particular serve as the primary means of actually using Praxis as a means of initiating into a Legacy without the right Path or Order.

                            The problem is that people are misapplying the degree of universality relative to the degree of specificity when it comes to what you can use as a Yantra — I'd clarify that Prime Sight only works on recognizable symbolism; most potential Instruments of Sympathy aren't going to ping it for the simple reason that most mages don't know everyone around them and the things connected to them without effort, while prospective Sacraments are going to have their usability (and thus their visibility) constrained by lack of knowledge of their composition. (Environmental Yantras tend to have their availability enhanced by the basic Occult grounding the Orders facilitate.)

                            Notably, this means most forms of identification still register to the Sight.
