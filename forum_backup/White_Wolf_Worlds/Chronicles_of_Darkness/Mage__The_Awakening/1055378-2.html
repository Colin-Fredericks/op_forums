                            About 'eating Potency', its RAW that a spell that targets multiple Targets is Withstood seperatley for each, but would be Dispelled as a single spell, so the Potency of a spell itself is not reduced if one target Withstands it, it just doesn't affect that Target. I think it is logical that if the Target that did Withstand were to have a change in their Withstand rating then the spell could start to affect that target as well as any others. And then there is no reason not have that sam e apply to s single Target spell.

So, it might be reduced to 0 for one target, meaning it has no effect on them, but be only partially reduced for another target, still taking effect, but to a lesser degree than it would have if it was not Withstood. The question is, if said spell has a Duration longer than an instantaneous one-off effect, and one or more of the targets have their Withstand trait increased or decreased during that time, does the spell's Potency increase or decrease for that (or those) target (or targets) accordingly?

If in the five people affected, three have Composure 2 and two have Composure 3, two will have a -1 penalty to attack rolls for the Scenr and three will have a -2 penalty. If one of the people with Composure 3 somehow gains a dot of Composure during the scene, does it stop affecting him?                                         In the mind control example, I think I have been able to resolve it somewhat in my mind.

                                        Compare a starting Vampire with Dominate 2 trying to do the same thing, the vampire can look into the eyes of another supernatural and roll a larger dice pool than his opponent since he has 3 traits rather than one plus a supernatural trait, so lets say 8 dice is resisted by 5 dice and the vampire can reasonably control someone by looking in his eyes. He gets all the stuff that the Mage was trying to do with reach only he gets it automatically. The only thing he does not get is the ability to control multiple targets but all other factors are taken care of more or less. Also recall that I am using a buffed up mind control mage which buffed up his mind control power which is something of an outlier anyways. Sometimes I feel that Mages must fight for things that other supernaturals get automatically. Even a mage who wakes up early in the morning to perform a 15 hour ritual to buff his strength to 9 will probably still get mauled by a werewolf or vampire since he would not have the other bells and whistles such as regeneration, speed, claws etc...

                                        Another thing is that I recall that some powers such as gifts and promethean powers are designed to withstand mental influence which would cause a clash of wills, and to give a power like this a counterweight.

                                        Ironically, the said mage would probably be best doing this spell early in his career since later on, as his gnosis goes up, the paradox from so much Reach could probably cause the spell to (literally), blow up in his face.

                                        Also, a mage in an alley way would need to use 2 Yantras which would take at least 2 turns, and each subsequent attempt would as well only with more paradox. During this time the werewolf pack would see someone waving a magic wand at them and uttering some strange language. They would feel someone trying to mess with their mind which they would, naturally, try to resist. The situation would probably escalate quite quickly in which the werewolves would probably react in a spectacularly brutal fashion, ending the mage right then and there.

                                        Finally, as great as crossovers are, the World of Darkness is a busy place. All the supernaturals are so busy with their agendas (doing politics, fighting spirits, opposing Seers...), that they cannot bother with each other. A mage's main antagonist will probably be another mage such as a Seer who has lots of ways to counter his magic.
