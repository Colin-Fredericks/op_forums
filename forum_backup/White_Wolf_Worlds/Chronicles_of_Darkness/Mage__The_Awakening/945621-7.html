                        Actually, I'm not at all upset about the search for immortality being connected to inhumanity in a horror game.

                        It's just that this is a setting where you can be a vampire, a werewolf, a mummy, or a demon- and all of those creatures are treated with a great deal of depth and sympathy despite being obviously inhuman and monstrous. Why do immortal mages not get the same treatment?

                        And why the hell can't I be a mad scientist, anyway?

                        About the first part, if I were going to play Awakening instead of Transgression, I see Awakening as a dark but hopeful game about enlightened heroes fighting against an evil conspiracy that controls reality. That's an obvious setup for a story about fighting not just the laws of society but of reality itself. And challenging the dominant narrative that mortality is necessary and good would be an excellent addition.

                        Now, "magic heroes beat up death and everyone lives happily ever after forever" does not fit in a horror game at all, but I've never considered Awakening to be a horror game. In Transgression, immortality inevitably makes you inhuman to some degree, but it's much more easy to obtain and much less hazardous than many other uses of magic (it's not even a Wisdom infraction, unless it's done in a particularly reckless way).
