    Cabal Theme/Shadow Name

    03-02-2018, 09:24 PM
    So I'm at an impasse as a GM slightly. I'm trying to get a game off the ground, and my players are thinking about maybe in play working out a cabal theme as they find their footings and buying the relevant merit. However, since this would also strongly encourage thematic shadow names, their doing after char gen (possibly even well after if they wait an arc or so to do this) means they're certainly not going to have well coordinated Shadow Names. I'm not even sure how a cabal theme would exist without the Shadow Names helping feed into it, honestly since it specifically is about boosting their own use of that merit.

    Does anyone have some suggestions on this? Maybe have them try to re-tailor the behavior or physical presentation of their shadow identities or something to make the theme work? This is a really minor, mostly superficial narrative thing but it is causing a weird personal stumbling block in my mind.

                          You can also just tell them that they can build their characters as normal as if without a cabal theme in mind, and as they develop a theme in character they're allowed to alter their Shadow Names to fit the theme. It makes sense to me, Mages usually expect to end up in a cabal, but that doesn't mean they know what kind of symbolism they're going to be working with so establishing a personal identity takes precedence until they get more comfortable working together as a group and modify their identity to fit the new dynamic.

                          You should also feel free to give your own thoughts on what their group might be about based on the actions they take in the game.

                          As a pseudo-example, I have a friend who's been running a game for years. His group although an established cabal have never chosen a name for themselves. The Consilium has ended up giving them an unofficial name though, the Golden Road. As in, the road to Hell is paved with good (gold) intentions. They mean well, but overall they're highly individualistic and are a definite wild card in Consilium politics and frequently cause chaos as a result. They'd probably be justified in picking up the Cabal Theme Merit and using it for things that fit that definition of their group that's been laid on them by outsiders.

                              In my limited mage experience, mages do change shadow names. In my own setting/head canon this is pretty common.
                              Mages usually choose their own or is appointed a shadow name by their mentors, however like identity in real life it's bound to be changes in it. Developing or changing it outright to fit would be possible and appropriate. Exploring your own supernal reflection and thinking about whom you really are and want to be is sure to bring changes. Usually this comes with greater understanding, dramatic changes in perspective and ones life or increased knowledge and understanding. This could happen for example when you gain a new title or position within the Consilium or Order or when gaining Gnosis. In my mind, if a new dot of Gnosis is enough to change your Virtue or Vice one should consider if ones Shadow Name is still fitting for the 'new' you. In that you also get a new, or can get a completely new Nimbus as well speaks highly to the possibility that your supernal identity can change dramatically when gaining deeper understanding of the cosmos.
                              Not to say that every, or even most mages change their Shadow Names, but surely enough to be considered common.

                              Cabal theme, as with the merit, is a cabal that is highly themed around some form of concept and every member is either one part of that or a symbol of the idea in general, their practice and collective identity strengthening each other. What this theme is should probably be talked about before making the characters so each can fit but if they don't surely they can find common ground in character while playing it out.
                              However if we got some short descriptions of the player characters and their shadow names, we might give some inspiration of possible themes.

                              Some examples of themed cabals in my own games include The Augurs, a cabal themed with prophecy, foretelling and omen reading made up by members of the guardians and the mysterium, all taking Shadow Names from famous oracles, prophets and the like. Another themed cabal is The Unknown Hand, themed with shadow manipulation of sleeper society and guardian labyrinths members taking Shadow Names corresponding to subtlety, changes, and manipulation. The Brotherhood of the Rune-carver is themed around Nordic mythology and the old sagas connection to the myth of the Awakened city, with names taken corresponding to different parts of old Norse myths and life.
