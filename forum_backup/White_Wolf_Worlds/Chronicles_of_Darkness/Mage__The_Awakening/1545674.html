                      Human mortality and the fact that the Orders do not generally have knowledge of each other's secrets nor share that information (nor other information) broadly, particularly not before the advent of modern communications technology when news of covert and/or self-hiding action like the removal of knowledge by a small and far-scattered former Order with no centralized Caucus leadership has few avenues of travel to reach the grapevine by the nature of the event.

                      The Tremere joined the Diamond on their own terms over 1300 years ago and were expelled 400 years later, before and during which time they were known primarily as an Order of monster hunters with a habit of antagonizing the Keepers. The Apocalypse Mysteriorum, which would be the point where the Keepers' knowledge base would logically start being integrated with the Pancryptiates' techniques to give rise to the Mysterium's Egregore, did not happen for another 150 years. It's a point of lore that by modern day many mages learn rote mudras without being taught the deeper meanings of their symbolism, and Mage being a game about seeking out hidden knowledge does not negate (and rather relies upon) the fact that the Chronicles of Darkness are a setting where true and full information about supernatural topics is obscured and buried behind layers of misinformation, disinformation, and rumor.

                      The Scions of the Seventh Watchtower were led by the same sorts of beings who went on to become its sole membership after they were exposed as Reapers. They could live forever on a diet of souls, innately have a subtler Nimbus and aura than other mages, and institutionally get Occultation along with Subterfuge as a Rote Skill. The part of their lore that got passed down — that they were soul-eating liches — is easy to propagate compared to the more detailed knowledge that would've been known about their practices and beliefs and the elements of their history that were dangerous to the ones in a position to share them — that they were part of the Diamond and considered the subject-matter experts on the soul and how unAwakened monsters affect it — which the Diamond itself had reason to keep quiet on for the same reason it doesn't advertise how easy it is to Befoul a spell. "Steal a few treatises and memories and wait six to ten longish mortal lifetimes for the heat to die down" does not beggar credibility as a feat for an organization whose upper echelons have "reach the equivalent of the top third of the Gnosis ladder and wipe out and symbolically subjugate a Legacy that messes with souls" as a prerequisite.

                                              There are a few other things worth considering.

                                              One is that it's probably kind of difficult writing fictional equivalents to a lot of grand historical events. Logically, the in-character process of that would have had a lot of things that were touch and go in the moment which, by virtue of the fact that they end up working, makes them superficially look like unstoppable juggernauts. In real life, people often don't know the deeper intricacies of those things, but they can maintain credibility by virtue of the fact that they actually happened.

                                              As far as the extent of magical defences and countermeasures go, I feel as though in these kinds of discussions people often assume mages have endless time and means to apply as many possible back ups and contingencies as possible so as to make any kind of assault on them basically impossible. And they forget things like the limits of spell control and the high costs of relinquishment and even just the idea that an accumulation of mages will want to be focused on things other than doing that. That's the kind of thing that really needs to cultivate a setting picture of "mages are actually very sparing and strategic about where they apply things like their long term spells", with just a dash of "the best defence is that nobody actually wants to attack you 99% of the time".

                                              And, well... for better or for worse, suppressing or ending spells is something that can be done with one dot of Prime and one other Arcanum. And the necessary Potency is based on a fixed rate for the mage who originally cast it, rather than something they might put a lot of extra time and effort into reinforcing. There are sharp limits to the extent to which mages can expect their spells to protect things from other mages.
