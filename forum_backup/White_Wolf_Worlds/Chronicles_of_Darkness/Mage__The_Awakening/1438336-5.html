                                                    Originally posted by EW-Matias View Post
                                                    If the point of the spell is making it more valuable or useful as a tool, yes. But that seems a very niche case and State Change offers a better solution for most practical situations.

                                                    If my solution doesn't work, then I'd like to know which one does. What would the required spell accomplish that Crucible doesn't?
                                                    Since OP asks about allotropes, and not only coal to diamonds, I'd design a spell which specifically turns a material into a different allotrope. Even with your intepretation of Crucible, it can only do so from a less valuable allotrope to a more valueable allotrope. You could design a Fraying version of Crucible to make the reverse possible (thus also creating a second spell for this purpose), but you're still constrained by the spell changing value and only within certain limits. You would not be able to turn a material into an allotrope which does not have a different value (as represented by Availability) as that would definitely be outside the scope of Crucible. You're also constrained in how much Availability may change. It may at most triple in value, provided you spend a Reach on it.

                                                    The spell I'd design would be able to turn a whole lump of coal (provided it's already pure enough; zCrucible could actually help with that step) into a diamond of equivalent mass, which Crucible can't do because Crucible (with the Reach option) can at most turn a lump of coal into a diamond with an Availability rating of 3. Such a diamond would be tiny and/or relatively impure.

                                                    I would personally argue that a single lump of coal likely has no Availability at all. Rather, a sack of coal for a household coal grill would have a collective Availability of 1, which would definitely be impossible to transform into diamonds that would have anything close to the original object's size without exceeding the Availability limits of Crucible. And transforming a whole sack of coal into a single, small diamond would definitely be outside the scope of Crucible as nothing in the spell even remotely suggests you could compensate by removing a majority of the subject's mass.
