                                    Here is how quickly you can run out of Willpower in a stressful situation [1 round]
                                    1. You want your spell to succeed; you can't spend additional turns adding yantras, so you add the biggest one reflexively, and burn a WIllpower point to get sweet +3 to the roll so you have a decent chance of succeeding. [WP spent: 1]
                                    2. You lacked Reach to do it safely, so you decided to make a gamble and Throw the Spell; you roll Gnosis+Athletics, and because of Defense/Penalties/Whatever, you Willpower to get +3 to it so you won't miss [WP spent: 2]
                                    3. Some asshole decided to Counterspell your spell! You Clash of Will. Dude Willpowered his roll, so you need to match the stakes, and willpower too [WP spent: 3]
                                    4. Annoyingly enough, there is some kind of effect that interferes with your spell, and you need to beat it in Clash of Will to get it going. Again, Willpower to rescue [WP spent: 4]
                                    5. And even after everything, Jerk#2 casts a spell at you which you Withstand, and you really need to survive it, so you Willpower to get +2 Withstand rating [WP spent: 5]
                                    6. Oh, and if any of the Jerks decides to contest some of your Shielding spells that operate on Clash of Will, prepare yourself to Willpower away [WP spent: 5+]

                                    Is it a white room scenario? Kinda. But chances are, in high risk high tension situations, at least some parts of it will come into play. The fact that your Mage can burn through 3 points of Willpower just to make sure his spell works can be nuts.
                                    *And yes, I'm assuming that you can spend multiple Willpower points per round, but only one per action, as more than one 2nd ED book claims so.

                                        I was tidying up my house rules when I figured out one that might help for those situations when you want your players not to spam stuff (normally, just telling them not to do it is enough, but yeah, this might help "gameist" types).

                                        Repeated Spells

                                        When casting a spell that the mage had already failed to cast during that scene, the mage gets a cumulative (for that spell only) -1 die penalty to her spellcasting pool (so far this is the basic CoD rule). On top of that, if the spell did not normally risk any Paradox, now it risks 1 die of Paradox. The mage must use different Yantra on the new attempt or it automatically fails. This spell grants no extra benefits on an Exceptional Success.

                                                Originally posted by WHW View Post
                                                Honestly, like?
                                                You can't use all flavors of mage sight on everything. You can't dump tons of mana into mitigating paradox at every convenience. You can't spam powerful spells like Shifting Sands at every convenience. You have to spend around at least an hour or so to recover any mana you spent frivolously, and there's the chance that you might well run out of ways to recover mana until the following day.

                                                It also means you're incentivized to use your legacy oblation to regain mana, which provides interesting thematic flavor and story hook possibilities. If you leave praxis spam, then all other forms of mana recovery are obsolete.

                                                Originally posted by Justin Sane View Post
                                                Wait, so the players got a moment of relative peace, and for some reason, they're rolling for some trivial stuff just to get their mana back? Follow these steps, then:

                                                1st step, is there a chance of success? If no, the action fails. If yes, next step.
                                                2nd step, is there a chance of failure? If no, the action succeeds. If yes, next step.
                                                3rd step, is failure interesting? If no, the action succeeds. If yes, roll.

                                                The real issue here is why the ST is calling for those rolls in the first place.
                                                It's not trivial. Discovering an unlimited mana engine is a fascinating mystery that any mage should seek out. That's the fundamental problem here. By every metric, mages SHOULD be spamming praxes to recover mana. It fits in setting because praxes are spells that you've practiced by casting repeatedly. It fits in theme because discovering a complication-free unlimited mana engine is a monumental accomplishment. It fits in character because your character should be fascinated by this.

                                                It fits in every way except that it's not fun. It takes interesting methods of mana recovery, like Legacy oblations, and renders them obsolete. It allows mages to recover mana on a much smaller timescale than any other reliable method of mana recovery. That's why I insist that it's a rules problem, and not a player problem. The player has every reason and justification to do it. It's the system that can't handle it.

                                                Originally posted by Servanous View Post
                                                A master of Prime or a master of Spirit with a familiar (essence fountain) won’t have as many problems with mana between encounters. Keep in mind that these are master level spells. Higher level mages should be able to call on more magic. However I don’t see to many spells that flat out heal willpower. Yes you can rip it out of people with Death magic or heal one point a day with the Knit spell (equivalent to a good night’s sleep) but I don’t see any spell that explicitly heals willpower to full from nothing. Taking advantage of critical successes in a literal wording of the rules so that EVERY SPELL heals willpower reliably between encounters with no storyteller input seems to cheapen the other mechanics of replenishing willpower (virtue and vice, rest ect). This is another reason that if I were storytelling a game I would only allow critical successes on spells to replenish willpower and mana if that critical success helped resolve a stressful encounter.

                                                The essence fountain “mana engine” or the ability to create permanent hallows as a master of Prime are the equivalent of having a solar powered car (essence fountain) or a solar powered house (lasting create truth). Both have their uses, both have their limits, neither spell replenishes willpower.
                                                That's not true. A Master of Prime will absolutely NOT always be at full mana. Creating Hallows has widespread repercussions to the surrounding area, and is highly likely to be regulated by the local concilium. Furthermore, even if you have a few powerful hallows, you certainly aren't going to be able to run back to them between every scene to top yourself off. You'll have to manage with what you have. That's why I'm ok with this form of mana engine.

                                                A master of Spirit, on the other hand, has no such restrictions. They can recover all their mana at any time, in only a few seconds.

                                                I absolutely agree with your assessment on Willpower. Unlimited willpower ruins the Virtue and Vice systems.
