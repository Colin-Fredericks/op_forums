    Originally posted by bonerstorm View Post

    I think you're missing the point: There is no such thing as instantaneous transitory spells in 2e so there's no such thing as a spell that wouldn't be suppressed by an AOE dispel during the AOE dispel's duration. In 2E, "transitory" means spells without Advanced Duration... of which the minimum "transitory" duration is 1 turn.

    Example with Thyrsus Mage 1 and Obrimos Mage 2:
    Mage 1 has Potency 20 W/S up from his buddy with lots of time on his hands and Prime Arcana 4. Mage 2's initiative comes up first and they cast an Advanced Scale area combined praxis spell of Celestial Fire and Supernal Dispellation with a reach for Aggravated damage at Mage 1; the spell has a potency of 7 and a minimum duration of 1 turn and Mage 1 gets an exceptional success with a roll of 3, opting to ignore Withstands.

    Mage 2 uses 4 Mana to power the 2 Reach and doesn't need to use Sensory Range because he can just chuck the spell with an attack roll of Gnosis + Firearms/Athletics. With Fast Spells, Mage 1 could only apply defense to a chucked spell if they could apply defense to bullets; even if they could, they can't apply defense to or dodge an area spell anyway. Just for giggles, because Mage 2 has Fate 1, they specify that it will only suppress spells that don't hurt Mage 2.

    Mage 1 can't dodge this and, of course, can't counterspell it without Prime magic. His withstand of the CF from Potency 20 W/S is ignored because it's an area spell. Even if it didn't, it would be ignored because of the exceptional success. Even if it didn't, the Supernal Dispellation beats any Withstand of W/S. Even if it didn't, the Potency of 7 means it would dispel W/S even if it had been cast with Advanced Potency. Even if W/S was somehow from an attainment and thus couldn't be dispelled, its withstand rating would be ignored by Celestial Fire because of the exceptional success.

    So Mage 1 is in a "large house or building" sized area in which all spells are subject to ignores-withstand Supernal Dispellation and everything is subject to 7 points of aggravated from Celestial Fire (including beings in Twilight). Arguably neither his Spirit Armor nor Life Armor apply against this damage. Thankfully, Mage 1 has Life 4 and Mend as a rote so they cast Mend with a reach to heal aggravated damage... unfortunately the area dispel is still in effect and nullifies Mend because it is within the area during the duration (1 turn). The Supernal Dispellation would nullify ANY non-archmage spell because of the ignoring of withstands from exceptional success and, even if the Storyteller houseruled against that, Mend's Withstand wouldn't beat SupDisp's Potency of 7 even with Advanced Potency.

    Mage 1's player says: "But wait! Mend is a transitory spell! It takes effect instantly and can't be dispelled." The Storyteller says: "That is literally not what Transitory means. If you look at the rulebook, transitory just means it doesn't have Advanced Duration. The only reference to 'immediate' effects are on page 113 in a section saying what happens when a heal or damage spell etc is cast with an extended duration. If the spell takes effect immediately, it still gets dispelled immediately. The book does not say what you say it does."

    Supernal Dispellation:
    "Supernal truths can never truly be unmade, but with this spell
    the mage may cast them back across the Abyss, effectively erasing
    any spell she comes across. This spell is not potent enough to
    dispel archmages’ spells.
    A successful casting suppresses the spell for the Duration of
    Supernal Dispellation."

    If an area spell treats all possible subjects within its area as its subject which each withstands separately, then it suppresses every spell cast in the area during its duration. Period. The only way for Mage 1 to escape Mage 2's spell is to escape the "large house or building" sized area if their Speed allows.

    This is why I think area spells treating everything within AOE as subject, which is otherwise limited, is bats*** f****** insane. If it's RAW, that's cool. Still insanely OP.

    Supernal Dispellation's primary factor is Potency, like an attack spell. Attack spells pulse when you pump their Duration and reoccur at intervals relative to your Gnosis.

    With 2+ Reach you could destroy any spells you catch on the initial casting, but people could cast other stuff in the mean time. The spell does not say it continuously suppresses all new attempts to cast spells. It merely states that ongoing spells, upon successful casting of Supernal Dispellation, are suppressed for the Duration of Supernal Dispellation - which is by default 1 turn (or 3 seconds). If you switch it to Advanced Duration, you simply extend the interval at which your spell will ping off and capture new spells by an interval designated by your Gnosis.

    A casting of Supernal Dispellation with Advanced Duration at Gnosis 10 would go off every 20 turns and capture any non-archmage spells in its radius at that interval.

    My reason for reading it this way is the inherent reason for why Duration and Potency are not supposed to be arbitrarily decided. The intent of Supernal Dispellation is to suppress magic happening at the time of its casting.

    If we're going RAW, it says nothing about intervening in any new spells. It explicitly refers to what is already on the plate. This is why you've been told that Counterspell is more effective for real time dispellation because it counters the spell live, no matter if its duration is 1 turn or 1 year. It happens in the moment.

    Supernal Dispellation would only work against the spells on the plate, then you would need to wait to snag any future spells that are cast with durations that meet with another interval of Supernal Dispellation. In the meantime, you would still need to use Counterspell to mess with any new spells, especially if they only exist for long enough to do what they need to do and then end before your Supernal Dispellation interval comes up.

    Mage 1 would be free to cast Mend. However, if Mend is cast with a long Duration, and that Duration crosses the next interval of Supernal Dispellation while still being in its radius, Mend is suppressed.

    So the Mage is better off keeping it short and sweet, or dispelling your Supernal Dispellation as soon as possible. Or just leaving the area.

    Supernal Dispellation seems like it matters more outside of combat than in the fray. Granted, you could probably use Time or Fate to get it to do what you're trying to do with it (aka go off every turn or go off specifically when new spells are cast).

    RAW "effectively erasing any spell she comes across" is disingenuous because spells are not erased unless the Reach cost is paid. "A successful casting suppresses the spell for the Duration of Supernal Dispellation" sounds like it makes more sense within the context of addressing ongoing phenomenon at the time of casting, not all phenomenon that will be cast.

    You can read it that way and get an overpowered reading, or you could bring it back (while acknowledging it says nothing about suppressing all new spells as they arise in real time within the vicinity) and give it a more reasonable Heal/Damage spell reading - again, on the basis that it is a Potency spell. Read it as though you've cast an AoE healing or damage spell.

    If the solution is to "balance" it, then all it takes is to read it within the context of primary factor and spells like it. If your only interpretation is to give it the most broken effect based on words that are explicitly not stated, then I don't know what to tell you. Sometimes the stuff written in the book doesn't match what was meant exactly. In those cases we have to read the spell with the context and spirit of the game and hopefully come out on the other side with something workable.
