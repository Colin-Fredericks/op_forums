                    Your conditions present an absurdly optimized scenario where you assume the character always succeeds at a coin toss, only ever uses prime magical real estate, can enlist the aid of five other mages with identical capabilities to their own, burns through their Willpower and gets their allies to do the same, and has minimal to zero concern about their summoned entity immediately getting weaker.
                    This was before the condition thing, so I see where you are coming from.

                    As an extended action, summoning takes an hour per roll and every time you fail a roll you have to take a Condition or start over from the beginning. Without spending Willpower on every single roll in the process, most characters have a chance ranging from one-in-ten to one-in-twenty of failing any given roll in that process. You don't think the results of failure in a delicate process like Supernal summoning might have an impact on the chances of something going wrong?

                    This is a process for summoning a time-limited entity with a prerequisite for its support. Most mages performing a Supernal summoning are not doing so just to get the guest of honor to show up, and Abyssal intrusion represents a rare disastrous outcome in the first place. The outside possibility that your attempt to evoke an intermediate forge-god instead draws down an alien thing with bones of molten glass is there to make the attempt a tense process, not to deter you from attempting it at all, and using almost literally the best possible scenario for checking the likelihood of it happening is bad math.
                    Ah, I had not considered gaining a Condition, you are correct. So, you can take a Condition or give up then and there. Interesting potentials, thanks for this Satchel. I imagine the Condition is Supernal in nature, maybe it subtracts 1-2 from your die rolling if you continue. Perhaps it increases in penalty each time, I see where you are going with this. Perhaps your safe rolls decreases by 1 each round giving a greater chance to your abyssal incursion, that could work as well.

                    In that case, Summoning Supernal Beings could be dangerous if you failed 1 round. With a die pool of 4 that is probably going to be somewhere in the neighborhood of 24% based on probability. So, literally 1/4 rolls you will get a Condition. However, spending Willpower each round will net you a set of 7 die pool making this chance to 1 in 10. However, since this is an extended action and not spell casting you could get the Rote Quality on the die pool. This would reduce your chance of getting a failure to less 1 in 100. Those odds are looking pretty good. If we assumed Rote Condition plus 8-again quality even with 4 die you are going to do a pretty good job of never risking an abyssal intrusion and this is all possible with starting characters.

                    So, all in all, team up with an Acanthus to give you some buffs or get yourself an imbued item that does the very best.

                    So, let's do a non-optimized scenario. Let's take 1 Mage with Resolve 2, Composure 2, Gnosis 1, Arcana Rating 3 and knows a buddy Acanthus. He doesn't want to waste the willpower dot on a demesne bonus, but he hasn't invoked a Paradox within the last week so he doesn't take that penalty. He gets Rote Quality plus 8-again on his rolls and decides to take a crack at summoning a Supernal Being. They decide an hour long chat should be enough and plan to increase the duration by 2, they also get an Occult Bonus luckily with 1 reducing their successes by 1 to counter one point of duration. For the purposes a Condition Chance is ST Fiat, you may decide to invite Abyssal Intrusion or basically cause the summoning to essentially fail since there are no RAW mechanics on how this should work when summoning a Supernal Being.

                    Here are the results given all the above, and let's just say for this instance they don't use willpower, his chance of success is the following:
                    Rank 1: 33% Summon Success, 35% Condition Chance* (maybe multiple), 0% default Abyssal Intrusion

                    *Condition chance considers at least 1 failure happening during all the die rolls

                    So, yeah a novice probably should not try summoning a Supernal Being for sure, unless they plan on going all out as the success is determined down below.

                    If they spent willpower this is the result they would have:
                    Rank 1: 82% Summon Success, 3% Condition Chance, 0% default Abyssal Intrusion

                    If they used a Demesne associated with the correct path and don't spend willpower this is the results:
                    Rank 1: 71% Summon Success, 29% Condition Chance, 0% default Abyssal Intrusion

                    If they used a Demesne associated with the correct path and spend willpower this is the results:
                    Rank 1: 95% Summon Success, 2% Condition Chance, 0% default Abyssal Intrusion

                    So, get a Demesne, get some willpower, and some die tricks if you are low powered.

                                    Originally posted by One Tin Soldier View Post
                                    Hey guys, I'm hoping you can tell me a bit more about Goetia, because what's in the core book seems to be lacking. Specifically, how do you run them as supporting NPCs and antagonists? What would you expect from an encounter with one in someone's Oneiros versus the Tenemos versus the real world? What do they want from people? I want to understand what makes them interesting and engaging to players, more than just another type of ephemera that can be bent to the player's will.
                                    Have you read the Astral book from 1e? That covers a lot of it. Summoners has some more info.

                                    One of the key features is that if you pull a goetia from an Oneiros the person stops being affected by it completely. Like say you yank out their goetia of anger, they’ll not be angry again until it’s put back, if you kill it they won’t be angry until it has a chance to regrow.

                                    They’re not as overtly predatory as spirits are. Typically you just don’t see them in the Fallen because they don’t normally have a means of leaving the Astral and don’t have Manifestations when they do, though exceptions exist like in the L.A. setting.

                                    In the Oneiros the weaker ones aren’t very well established characters, they’re actors that grab roles relevant to the scene being played out in the Oneiros at the moment and their role is indistinguishable from themselves. There are more established and stable goetic like Virtue or Vice, etc. Typically what any goetia wants is to expand its influence, they can be similar to spirits like that, but their Influences are really the only way to directly influence a person most of the time.

                                    Goetia in the Temenos tend to be more stable like the greater goetia in the Oneiros. They’re more nuanced too because they’re fed by the ideas of more than just one person. They are living repositories of information and symbolism, and they want to get that information out on an instinctive level. Since they’re not spirit’s starved for Essence they’ll typically do this for free, whether or not you want them too. They’ll do what’s appropriate for their character to disseminate it which may or may not be in a friendly way. Some particularly powerful goetia can induce permanent shifts in the ideology of people who happen across them.

                                    In the Anima Mundi things get more intense and primal, but it can be an even greater resource for information that doesn’t fall under the domain of human knowledge. A tiger goetia may want to spread understanding of tigers, not humanity’s conception of tigers, but the shit tigers know about themselves. It might eat you to do this, but you might be able to avoid that by becoming or demonstrating acceptable levels of tiger-ness.

                                                            So, there are two ways to cast a spell on a target that's outside of sensory range. One way is to cast remotely, this requires spending a Reach to make the spell sensory range and another Reach to cast the spell through whatever is allowing you to remotely view your subject in real time, be it a security camera or a scrying window. The other way to cast outside of sensory range is via Sympathy. This requires that you have Sympathy with your target, and that you employ a sympathetic yantra. Once you have those you spend 1 Reach to move the spell to sensory range, and then you pay one point of mana to activate the Sympathetic Casting attainment you get when you have Space 2.
