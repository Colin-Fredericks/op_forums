        Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post
        I've been wondering if there was a way to accomplish my goals for Mage: The Transgression on a smaller scale, by slightly hacking and nudging Awakening. The primary goal isn't just to make it more fun or flavorful or mad science-y, but to really make it feel like a personal horror game about broken monsters. The most important thing is, what would be the best way to run or hack Mage in order to emphasize the ambiguity and horror of the setting? To really make the players question everything, and to create the genuine possibility that a lot of the principles of the setting are either false or much more complicated.

        Here are my goals/ideas so far:

        • The main thing I want to do is present being Awakening as a cursed, monstrous condition rather than a blessing or a "higher state" of evolution, make it so that "Awakened" and "Sleepers" are just terms Mages use to feel better about themselves, and close to or more than half of Mages wish secretly or openly that they were normal humans again.
        • Paradox and Obsessions are both much more dangerous and punishing.
        • Archmastery is equivalent to becoming a True Fae- the Mage becomes an utterly inhuman entity.
        • Much of the setting's assumptions are ambiguous or wrong, and the possibility exists that much of what Mages believe is true is true because their disruptive presence in reality forces it to be true.
        • Moral ambiguities of the main conflict are emphasized. The Seers have a genuinely good case for why they're right.
        • As Mages gain power and lose Wisdom, they must take Bans and Banes related to how they can perform magic, like other supernatural creatures.
        • Crossover possibilities are enhanced, with Mage supremacy being actively fought with nerfs.
        • The subjective nature of the Supernal is emphasized, as well as the possibility that it's essentially a shared delusion that Mages can force into reality. Players only have to pick one of their Path's Arcana, and choose any other as their second ruling Arcana. Experiences of the Supernal realms are vastly different and subjective.
        • Some Mages have no Path. They are stereotyped as being more likely to become Scelesti or Banishers, but the truth of this assumption is questionable.
        • A significant and ever-increasing number of Mages Awaken as Gifted Scientists from the Mage Chronicler's Guide.
        • Presumably other stuff I'll think of later.

        Feedback and suggestions are welcome.

            So a comment from me, not trying to downvote but just trying to show a few things can be acomplished using what's already in the books.

            Archmage's - IMHO they are already pretty inhuman. After reading IM my image of a archmage is a guy who believes in having a morality licence to do whatever he wants and so absolutely obsessed about something that they'll do ANYTHING do acomplish that goal. That's pretty scary for me.

            Obsessions aren't meant to be dangerous because players would never pursue them. I always had this problem in RPGs that players were always doing the most "optimal" choice and that frequently meant not engaging with the game. Obessions are meant to be something the players work for, it's up to the ST to make them have consequences. Try to encourage players to take obsessions that would lead towards "bad" decisions.

            For me it seems like you want something more obvious than the type of horror that Mage: the Awakening tries to do. From what I read in your posts seems like Ascension would be a better fit for you.

                        Dave's current project, Deviant, actually provides a perfect outline of how I see supernatural power working in a horror setting: the more power a Deviant has, the more Scars they have. This holds true for all the games other than Mage. Demons and Mummies are the most powerful supernaturals after Mages, and they're probably the least human and saddled with some of the biggest drawbacks. Vampires have comparatively limited power next to mages, yet they're stuck with a host of downsides and monstrous tendencies. I've said it before: I want to make Mages either less powerful or less human, because my vision of ChroD is that you can only pick one.

                                    Pretty much this. The horror of Mage isn't that the mechanics force them to go on killing sprees with magic or die, or to rip the souls out of everyone they meet or risk losing their powers. The horror of Mage isn't that the mechanics force them without question to do horrible things. The horror from Mage comes from the fact that even though they have power that they can do whatever they want to with it, they choose to do some pretty terrible things with it. They aren't forced to do it, they do it because they want to.

                                    I honestly find that more horrific than a vampires need to drink blood or anything of the like. Vampires are forced to harm people for survival, when a Mage chooses to harm someone it's not because they have to to live, or that they're irresistibly compelled to (though while not irresistible, they might still be compelled to). No, if a Mage chooses to harm you, it is because they've decided with their very soul that whatever harm it is that they are inflicting upon you is a reasonable price to pay to attain whatever it is they think they're getting from it. Simply put I personally find that much more horrific than draining a bit of blood from someone to live another night.

ChroD isn't just horror, it's horror where the players take on the role of horror archetypes.                                                         You're free to believe whatever you want about what CofD is supposed to be, but in this case you have nothing to cite to back up your assertion (that players must take on the role of horror archetypes), and it remains a personal belief. It's a game about playing monsters. Mages are monsters because of what they do, not what they are. Can you play a Mage who defends the innocent, subtly helps those around them, and generally uses their powers selflessly and without going too far? Of course, just like you could play a vampire who only feeds off of animals or the willing and who keeps themselves away from situations where they might frenzy, or a Harmony 5 Werewolf who manages to perfectly balance their lives as an apex predator and loving human, or a Promethean who manages their relationships to humans with remote correspondence and only choosing friends who can probably handle Disquiet and never staying in one place long enough to cause Wastelands.

                                                        But the point of these games is to play non-aspiration characters - monsters - who do terrible stuff. Not because they want to, but because they're either left with no choice or because it's the easy way out. It's hard for a Mage to be a "good guy" because the temptation to push themselves too far and cause Paradox, or to enact vigilante justice, or scry on their neighbors "Just to make sure nobody's a serial killer or something, I'm just being practical". All of those actions are monstrous, and Mages can only do them because they are mages. Vampires aren't monsters because they have to drink blood to survive, it's because they typically choose humans as their prey. Pretty much the only group that must behave like monsters are Beasts, and thus they don't get a trait to reflect how monstrous they're behaving.
