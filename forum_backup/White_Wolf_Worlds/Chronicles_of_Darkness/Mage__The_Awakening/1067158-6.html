                    Consilium (unless something is really wrong with it) wont force you into paying a tribute of Mana and research notes if you refuse to participate in it. It won't send Sentinels to invade your Sanctum, dispel your shields, drag you in front Hierarch, and then spoon feed him the Mana you "owned him" for "right to exist in this region". It will, however, pester you about not returning a borrowed book, and it might refuse to let you use community resources if you are in bad standing, because you didn't pay the "Mana Tax".
                    But it won't require you to wear a funny hat if you are not a subscriber, and it probably also won't negotiate safety of your Mysteries with other Mages. Or of your territory, when it comes to Seers.

                    Mages don't really need a government; if they don't like how things are, they can literally just stop coming and tell you to stop pestering them. And then you are going to do...what? Magically break their legs and tell them to make sure they have protection money ready tomorrow, because what a cute Mystery you have here, would be a tragedy if someone got their hands on her? They are more like secret societies aimed towards progressing goals of their members; and these goals tend to be "leave me alone, let me Mystery in peace, and maybe lend me a book or two when I need it, yeah sure I will cast a spell for you from time to time whatever"

                    It will, however, slap you on the hand if you harm their members, because that's their duty. Soul Stealing and Abyss are a special case; Diamond seem to consider health of soul their mission, kinda like how Catholic Church will push for protection of unborn babies.

                    Silver Ladder - dominated Consilia will, at their best, protect even Apostates, Free Councilors, Nameless Orders, Sleepwalkers and Sleepers from Banishers, Seers and other threats simply because they consider it to be inborn right of all Awakened and potentially-Awakened. At their worst, they are invaders who will tell you how to live your life and act like you subscribe to their ideology, because they believe that as a member of Awakened Nation, you need to act in certain way. They are the priest who invades your bedroom and tells you what you can or cannot do in it, and will not care about the fact that you don't follow his religion.

                                                Originally posted by nofather View Post
                                                It's because that definition is so broad it could encompass this very forum, which makes and enforces rules for the forum's community but can't seriously be considered a government. And a number of other things, from high school and fraternities to public swimming pools and baseball games.
                                                It is a broad definition. It's a broad definition because "government" is a term that has applied to everything from an absolute tyranny to pure democracy; from societies where ideas of currency do not even exist, to the economies of the developed world today. We even have terms like "student government". It is a broad term.

                                                We tend to only refer to the term "government" in general instances when talking about a society in the larger sense but that's an arbitrary limitation we put upon the word, which is why I can say...

                                                Originally posted by WHW View Post
                                                Do you consider your local RPG club a government? Or the forum moderation? Or organised religion?
                                                These all probably fit the broadest definition of a government. RPG clubs, moderators, and organized religions can all create rules and generally exclude people from their groups if they refuse to obey them. Today, some organized religions are governments; the Pope is the head of a nation-state, with the power to set fiscal policy for banks, imprison people, and hire mercenaries; all things he has done and continues to do. Saudi Arabia outright runs on religious law.

                                                Taxation is not a necessity for government and never has been. It is simply a descriptor of how a society is controlled and regulated (i.e. governed). That societies with chieftains and reeves and mayors have existed without any form of direct taxation across the centuries is proof enough that this is the case. Many communes have no sense of private property, and hence no taxation, yet still have governments in the form of direct democracy or elected representatives.

                                                Consilia make and produce laws and enforce them within their jurisdiction. Some might only apply to members but others apply with force and vigor to all within the territory. The Boston Consilium is noted as banishing any non-members who make waves, and... Being unkind to those who refuse to take the ban with good grace. They have elected officials who make, decide on, and adjudicate that law. If you don't consider it a government because they don't demand a tithe, I don't know what to tell you.

                                                    Ok, if you are using such definition, I'm going to agree that rulebook friendly Consilium is basically a secret society self government with voluntary membership that ignores rules and laws of sleeper society, and self-governs in order to provide it's members with benefits, safety and will expect signed up parties to follow the rules they agreed to following by being a member (even if they had no say in estabilishing these rules). The "ignore sleeper society rules" thing means that Consilium might be like a very hardcore Research Club that might punch you for not bringing back a book.I mean, I can understand what you are aiming at, because a thesis of one of my papers is basically that certain organized crime societies have risen up as a competition government in response for dissatisfaction with their proper rulers.

                                                    But, however, "healthy" Consilium shouldn't be much more than a glorified Hobbyist Club. It shouldn't exist to progress political agenda of hierarch or councilors, and it shouldn't be interested in ruling the Mages instead of providing them with a safe space to research and occasionally complain at each other for stuff. And when you talk about "Consilium as Government", I think that most people see a political body with it's own political interests that intends on ruling the Mages under it.
                                                    Taxation was an example of something that Consilium can declare it members should do, but what it should not enforce on unwilling Mages that don't participate in the Magic Club.

                                                    Anyway, everything I hear about Boston Consilium (excuse me, but my collection of 1st ED books is pathetic, and I don't intend to buy books from it now, and it really irritates me that it seems to be a default assumption that in order to play 2nd ED you are expected to possess most of the previous game) makes me feel that it's not really a good example "default" Consilium - it seems to be a dark twisted corrupted version of Consilium that exactly shows why it shouldn't end up being a government in the popular meaning of the word.


                                                    My own Consilia tend to portray the opposite end of the spectrum - they are very uninvolved, caring only about it's members, and existing only for the specific purpose of helping Mages do their stuff in peace. They don't care if you are killing Sleepers with your research, or mutating animals, or torturing ghosts - it's not their business. As long as you don't destroy souls, as long as you don't hurt another member, you are free to do whatever you want. Consilium dosen't care. It has no right to judge your research and your Obsession. It exists specifically in order to allow free pursue of Obsessions.
