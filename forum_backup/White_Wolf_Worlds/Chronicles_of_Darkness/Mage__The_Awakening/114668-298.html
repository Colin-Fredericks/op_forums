    Okay, I've clogged up the Werewolf forum enough with questions about how to make my stupid wolf-blooded mage character work, so here's a question for the mage perspective:

    When a werewolf is doing their "unstoppable killing machine" frenzy thing, attempts to "influence, intimidate, or otherwise change the Uratha’s course of action by mundane or supernatural means suffer twice the werewolf’s Primal Urge as a penalty." This is obviously intended for when a power involves a contested roll (stat+tolerance vs stat+tolerance+raging bonus) but no Awakened magic uses contested rolls (except for clash of wills, but the werewolf forum already decided that a clash isn't appropriate since it's not "two powers clashing" as much as "calming down a rampaging werewolf is storming hard). So how would this actually work for the mage? A direct die penalty on the casting roll seems equally out of place. Increasing the werewolf's Withstand somehow is clunky. Or does the mage just ignore it can cast like normal because "Magic"? Is Clash of Wills the only thing that makes sense? Clash of Wills is the only thing that makes sense, isn't it...

    The longer I study science the more I am convinced that it is functionally indistinguishable from what our ancestors would refer to as sorcery. And I would know, being both scientist and sorcerer.

                It’s not a Clash? Strange. I love Clashes.

                Anyway, in that case the werewolf’s Primal Urge won’t have an effect on the mage’s Mind spells to calm them down, unless you employ a house rule to make it so. “Use the Supernatural Tolerance trait if it is higher than the standard Withstand rating” seems popular.

                On the other hand, if the raging werewolf is in Gauru form... Gauru increases Size beyond 5. IIRC, that means the mage needs to spend a Reach for Advanced Scale. And did Gauru increase Resolve and/or Composure? My Werewolf lore is fuzzy at the moment. If those two are increased, depending on the intent of the spell, the mage may face an increased Withstand rating.

                Lastly, I highly doubt an average mage will survive long enough to cast a calming spell in front of a raging werewolf anyway. Or that a given mage will have enough Mind magic in the first place. So making the calming spell bypass Primal Urge if the mage manages to cast it doesn’t seem too unfair.

                EDIT: Reading Malus’ reply and the exact wording of the Werewolf rule again, I see there’s a strong case for Primal Urge penalizing spellcasting after all, despite spellcasting rolls usually being unaffected by penalties originating from the subject’s natural properties. ST call territory, I suppose.

                                        A much-delayed read on a thing:

                                        Originally posted by Katana1515 View Post
                                        The books section on Mage Sight contains the following example, which i am struggling to understand, was hoping you guys could help.

                                        Concealment magic, of whatever type, can hide a target from Active Mage Sight, but only if the concealment would logically mask the target from the purview of the Arcanum in question. Even then, if the concealment power uses the same magical principles as the detecting Arcanum, the mage still has a chance to see through it. For example, a light-based invisibility spell would conceal a target from Mind Sight, but Life Sight could still detect the living being, with or without the assistance of photons.

                                        If Life Sight could detect an invisible mage by picking up on his lifesigns, why on earth does Mind Sight not have a chance to pick up on the presence of a sentient mind?
                                        Follow along logically from the Periphery's "any supernatural attempt at concealment hides the effect from the mage" and recall the specification that "the mage can only determine the symbols related to phenomena within her senses" — if Mind Sight can't let you see an unmanifested Goetia without the benefit of the Attainment, it's certainly not going to tell you that there's a mind occupying the space where you can't see a person, but Life Sight deals with an entire class of phenomena for which camouflage is a natural ability and which exists as part of a wider ecosystem; breathing and having a temperature are both things that Life Sight can pick up on that have no bearing on light-based occlusion or the question of whether or not you're a thinking being.

                                        Active Sight lets you take something you can perceive and extrapolate a bit more out of it, but it's not a universal revelatory lens.
