    The thing is that most supernatural powers to control minds are predicated on the idea that there are degrees to which it's harder to push people, and they can actively struggle against that. For a contested action, that's represented by an actual roll to represent the exertion; a more objectionable thing is something that they will fight harder against. For a resisted action, that will be in a greater penalty against it, which is significant because the extent of powers that are resisted is usually determined by how many successes they roll. These are the context of a Merit like Indomitable.

    But Mage doesn't place the extent of how objectionable the outcome is in the part that represents effort, or even opposition. It's in Reach, a somewhat abstract representation of something like the depth of what the spell can affect. A mage might have to Reach further for an imago that can Rule a mind towards all ends, but once that cost has been paid it's in the same space as any other spell, a binary of "rolls a success and works, or doesn't".

    There's something in the metaphysics that lets a thinking being spend Willpower to make it require more Potency for a spell to work in this binary, but that's still not really in the space of an active struggle against an objectionable effect, so it's comfort against something like mind control is kind of limited.

    Never mind that spells with exceptional success can choose to just ignore Withstand altogether, so it could conceivably cut through Indomitable even if it was ruled to make for higher Withstand.

    Really, I think often the greatest defence against being mind controlled by a mage, at least over the relatively long term, is being able to erode that spell with Dissonance.

            Originally posted by Michael View Post

            To me it reads like an artifact of magic having it's own mechanical structure.
            It has its own mechanical structure because the idea of contested rolls against it was deemed to be nonsensical.

            Originally posted by Michael
            Like, the entire design ethos of 2e magic seems to be to narrow it down to a single roll.
            That would make it consistent with the baseline for how the Second Edition system resolves actions. Other supernatural powers that base degrees of effect on number of successes are the aberration.

            I'd put it in terms of "Mage already had a premise of penalties as a way to determine spell factors, and something resembling Yantras in terms of several unconsolidated mechanics for ritual actions to enhance spells. So it made sense to make it consistent with the core system that determines success or failure with the result of a single die, and turns assets and liabilities into dice bonuses and penalties rather than how many successes you roll."

            And the thing is, that system made the only time it matters how many successes you roll into when trying to exert yourself in opposition to somebody else. Magic could have continued to line itself up with that, but the idea of resisted rolls against it was explicitly rejected as not being consistent with the character of how Awakened magic works. I think being able to spend Willpower to increase Withstand needs to consistent with that decision.

            For one thing, I wouldn't put it in terms of struggle given the context of how Withstand works mechanically. You're not shaking off the spell, it's just that the Potency needs to be higher to do anything. It's like, a person of Resolve 4 who had a Potency 3 spell targeted at them wouldn't feel that as a spell trying to grab them and then shaking it off, the spell would just do nothing because it can do nothing.

            So I guess it could be something like a conscious pattern that is actively knowing itself takes on additional complexity that the imago needs to account for.
