    Mage the Awakening Hack Evaluation [Game two days!]

    01-07-2018, 10:04 AM
    This is the list of house rules that I'm intending to present to my players (some of whom have never played Mage before, so I tried to keep this light weight). I'm looking primarily for feedback on if they fix the problems listed in the reasoning section, and alternative solutions. While I appreciate some might disagree with my reasoning, I don't really think it'd be terribly productive to have that argument so I'll likely ignore an attempts to engage in it (I'll still read your posts if you really feel you need air out a philosophy difference for context in your other feedback or... just because). Thank for your time regardless.


    Simple Hacks for Mage: The Awakening 2e.

    Below are a list of simple hacks to clean up and clarify the rules in order to lead to better gameplay and storytelling.

    Changes to Chapter One: Faces of Magic

    Inferior Arcana
    Acanthus Inferior Arcanum: Prime. Magic is not a scientific force that can be transformed as easily as one turns electricity into heat. The Acanthus struggles to treat mana, hallows and the trappings of the Awakened world as dymstified and controllable elements that they can simple drain or charge like a battery. The unpinning of their mystical stories are infinitely more difficult to grasp for a Witch than the subtle exceptions and deviations of the threads of fate.
    Mastigos Inferior Arcanum: Fate. There is an age old conflict between those who believe in predestination and those who believe in free will. For a Mastigos this conflict is an immense hindrance, understanding the fine workings of fate means questioning many of the fundamental aspects of Mind both as a base level and in the deep communal sense. Warlocks enjoy manipulating the relationships between objects, bending and folding Space to their whims and yet Fate seems to ignore space entirely and see relationships through a different and maddeningly subtle lense that Mastigos find difficult to swallow.
    Reasoning: Inferior Arcana follow no set pattern and as such there is little justification for why they exist beyond the original writers feeling like they fit. This leads to some strange results since both of a Moros's ruling Arcana show up as inferior which seems like it should say something about reality or the Moros path or... anything. But it's meaningless. Therefore each path was given an unique Inferior Arcanum that is always a subtle arcanum that was deem most at conflict with their ruling arcanum. The reasoning can be easily argued that those who strong character concepts that don't fit into nicely into these new paths should talk with me so we can work out a unique Path deviation (which, yes, is a thing in my games. You're welcome.)

    Changes to Chapter Three: Supernal Lore

    Artifacts
    Artifacts are not merits, though a mage can liquidate any of their starting merits to pay for a starting artifact.
    Artifacts have their own spells, gnosis and mana which also determine their maximum size and durability. Here is the artifact creation proccess.
    Step One: Choose an amount of Gnosis between 1 and 5. For each point of Gnosis an artifact possesses increase its experience cost by 1. Artifacts in the fallen world can have no more than 5 Gnosis.
    An artifact has a maximum and starting mana pool equal to twice its Gnosis.
    An artifact has a starting structure equal to twice its Gnosis, divided however you desire.
    Step Two: For each point of gnosis, the artifact may have up to five spell effects but typically they have less. For each dot of required for a spell effect the artifact costs 1 experience, adding a Utility attainment such as sympathetic range also costs 1 experience. No more than 5 experience may be spend on an individual spell effect.
    Step Three: Determine what triggers the spell effects. This can be anything really, but cannot be shorter than the action required to cast the spell.
    Reasoning: The artifact system is clumsy, and I'm replacing it with something similarly clumsy that just weights a little less on my head.

    Imbued and Enhanced Items
    These merits are limited to five dots. Additionally, Imbued items start with two points of mana.
    Reasoning: This is just a default understanding of the system and there's not a compelling enough reason to violate it. The change to an Imbued item's starting mana pool is simply for elegance.

    Changes to Chapter Four: Magic

    Casting During a Grapple
    Under normal circumstance, a mage cannot cast an instant action spell the turn they struggle with an opponent to grapple. They can however choose to give up resistance and cast a spell instead, doing so requires the roll of a chance die. On a dramatic failure that opponent's may perform an additional move, but the mage can cast a spell. The -3 penalty listed in the default rules is ignored. The spell is cast and resolved first is the mage's initiative is higher than the entity grappling her, and last if it is lower.
    Reasoning: Combat magic is a tricky subject. In general it is my storytelling and design philosophy that mages should be less potent in combat than any other supernatural unless they specifically are using their magic to improve (not replace) their combat ability. Being able to act in a situation where a werewolf or vampire cannot should come at a price, and a single willpower point is too low. Note, this means a mage in a grapple with an opponent with higher initiative is very likely to just lose due to the possibility of the opponent getting three moves in one turn. This is working as intended.

    Withstanding
    Supernatural beings like mages, vampires or demons do add the difference between their Supernatural Tolerance trait (Blood Potency, Primal Urge, Azoth, etc) and the Mage's Gnosis to their resistance attribute to withstand if positive. Character may always spend willpower to increase a Resistance Attribute used for Withstanding, regardless of whether they are aware of it.
    Reasoning: Supernatural Tolerance is a primary factor to crossovers and game balance. Likewise Willpower is the great equalizing resource for all creatures that have access to the trait, and keeping it available is necessary to curtail thoughtless sympathetic assassinations.

    Exceptional Successes on Spellcasting Rolls
    The option to ignore any Withstand levels and have the spell take effect as full potency is simply not an option. Instead consider giving a bonus step in the primary spell factor.
    Reasoning: This rule essentially turns all Mages with four dots in any arcanum into luck based nuclear bombs in terms of damage potential, allowing them to kill all sorts of things that should frighten them such a Kereboi, Idigam, True Fae... Exarchs.

    Fraying, Unraveling and Unmaking
    Unraveling spells cannot have their damage potential upgraded with reach, and unmaking skills are not immediately fatal upon a single success. Unmaking spells inflict aggravated damage equal to their potency to subject capable of resisting.
    Reasoning: This is where I want to stress that the point of these changes is not to make Mages less fun or incapable of flinging spells in combat, but a Mage should require just a tad more work than "spent 8 experience" to kill any creature with statistics (plus having to go to the excuse 'Well, this creature doesn't really have stats because it's too powerful' is bad game design. Tools exist to be used.). This has a lot less impact than one might think, most supernatural creatures have distinct weaknesses that can be discovered and then conjured to inflict aggravated damage without needing mastery.

    Sympathetic Names
    A mage’s sympathetic name is defined by their identity outside of Awakened society. It is not necessarily the same as the character’s birth name.
    Reasoning: [Redacted]

    Changes to Appendix One: Supporting Cast

    Family Lines
    The term ‘family line’ is incredibly misleading as to how it applies to magic. The magic of Proximi is transferred through strong sympathy rather than blood relations, certainly it’s very common for Proximi to inheret magical powers from their parents, but just as likely (and more likely in an age filled with found families and broken blood ties) is a bond between lovers, best friends, and other similar but more esoteric bonds.
    Reasoning: Without this rule Proximi hover too close to a ‘eugenics works’ subtext for my taste.

          Vent0,

          Interesting notes on the inferior arcanum. I personally was considered more traditional witches like the Fates and various crones from mythology, but you're right to remind me that brewing potions and enchanting items is a common theme in more (relatively) recent works.

          I'm not sure if I'd handle this as a Legacy or Path Deviant. Obviously I cannot switch Mastigos and Acanthus' inferior arcana, and I quite like the subtle ones already assigned, so I believe I'll have to sort out what to do about this disconnect. I'm tempted to replace Prime's attainment with the ability to create Hallows, but then that would set off another domino and I'd have to redefine Prime Making. A problem I'll have to chew on.

          For Mastigos, I understand the Oath spells can be quite thematic for Warlocks who wish to play the tempter, but I honestly think it's more resonant if Warlocks are the ones 'accepting' the deals given to them by supernatural beings and other mages rather than the ones defining the terms. In this case I feel comfortable kicking this more squarely back into Acanthus' court.

          I agree that making Artifacts like physical legacies is hitting on a lot of the right cylinders. I don't think I have the time to refine such a system but I may adopt a more restrictive number of powers in exchange for paradox immunity.

          As for the Imbued Item pool, I'm using the Imbued spells rating + Supernatural Tolerance, as suggested. Unless I'm missing something and this should be changed. For mana, I like the amount set at 2 just for elegance of the math. To me it's easier to remember "Okay, so three dot spell, and then you have two more dots for extra mana. That's 6." It's personal preference mostly.

          Yeah, I considered having it always resolve afterwards, the main thing holding me back is I worry it's just a bit too harsh, but it might be the better and simpler solution. I don't often worry about grappling specifically, but more about their being no 'weakness' when fighting a mage when in traditional magic games restraining them is how you hit the 'off' switch.

          I agree a significantly strong supernatural should require a lot of thought and preparation on the part of the mage/cabal to compensate for their potential layered defense with my withstand rules. To me, that's working as intended.

          I think the suggestions for boosting damage by more than one on ES are pretty good, but I believe I'm gonna shy into what I consider safe waters on this one and just leave the option flat removed for now.

          I really like that definition for sympathetic names because it's a very concrete and yet personal one, so I think I'm gonna nab it. Thank you very much. (Also I can't help but think that Bruce Wayne's sympathetic name is Batman now because of that one Batman Beyond clip.)

          I'm glad to hear I'm not the only one treating Proximi like MysteryCult+.

          Thanks for the feedback.
