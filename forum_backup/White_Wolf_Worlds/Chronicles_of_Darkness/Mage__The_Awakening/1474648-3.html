            This is a combined spell of “Lucky Number”-Fate Ruling and a Matter/Time or Fate or Space Making. “Lucky number” is for the information you don’t have and are going to need, Matter is to make the material form of the book, and Time or Fate or Space can cause the thing to have been written by a particular person. The point of the spell is to acquire information you don’t have, and that you would need, and that can just be done by “Lucky Number”.

            Matter doesn’t create anything not directly related to the object on it own, it doesn’t have a history outside of just being there when the mage calls. You have to add that on with other Arcana. Matter also has nothing to do with information you don’t have, unless that information is directly Matter related. Think more; what is this weapon made out of, and what’s the boiling point of fluoride, and less; who used this weapon, and what boiled that fluoride? Technically I suppose you could force a book to appear with everything you don’t know on it, but that would be a infinitely sized book since there is infinite information you don’t know. Or has the text so fine it’s impossible to read. So you need to know what you don’t know to reduce the information volume. Even if you do know what you need to know, Fate is required to make a filter to make the spell usable, and at that point, why don’t you just ask Fate directly?

                    Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
                    This is a combined spell of “Lucky Number”-Fate Ruling and a Matter/Time or Fate or Space Making. “Lucky number” is for the information you don’t have and are going to need, Matter is to make the material form of the book, and Time or Fate or Space can cause the thing to have been written by a particular person. The point of the spell is to acquire information you don’t have, and that you would need, and that can just be done by “Lucky Number”.
                    Okay, this is a case where I think there is actually relevance to understanding who spells in general and some of the specific examples function, so that one has a working idea of what to extrapolate from them for creative thaumaturgy.

                    So as a reminder: Lucky Number does not produce information, it significantly focuses the odds of a random selection of numbers or letters being useful. It works according to a logic of it being plausible, but highly unlikely, that a first guess at what a password will be correct, and kind of brute forces fate in the direction of that improbable circumstance. I think it can reasonably be assumed to be something that works because the parameters are fairly straightforward.

                    A million monkeys working at a million typewriters might eventually produce the works of Shakespeare, but I don't think a Ruling of Fate can guide directly to that outcome, because it has too many moving parts. Even something like a Patterning such as Strings of Fate can designate an outcome, but needs to let the sequence of events that leads up to it work itself out according to a plausible timescale over which the mage has little fine control.

                    That, I think, precludes mages can being able to use Fate at the conventional levels to make it so that when they make a stab at guessing something like complex or specific information as yet unknown to them, or fully replicate an individual's writing style or something, their first go at it happens to get everything right the first time. You could use Strings of Fate to put yourself on the path to discovering a desired secret, but it's going to need time to get done (and possibly some degree of the mage forfeiting a bit of agency in how they act while the spell works, at least insofar as they need to follow the paths offered for them).

                    I think this outlook holds up logically, but more importantly, it means that the objectives in a game predicated on the idea that characters go out to uncover mysteries and discover information isn't totally overridden by a two dot spell that can allow them to know basically whatever they want from the outset conjured from nothing.

                        A Matter / Life mage can Make a book / cat. Not a specific book / cat.

                        Sure you can create a cat with particular features, such as an eye injury, particular pattern of fur or extra gills.

                        Likewise you can make a book with features, like hardback, pop-up pictures, or built-in flamethrower attachments.

                        You can't make a specific cat which was at the scene of the crime you are investigating and so has a specific memory of what happened (with only Life). Similarly you can't make a specific book which happens to be filled with specific information with matter alone.

                        Sidenite: you can make a digital book with forces (even though the hard drive you are storing it in is in the domain of matter, flipping the bits is something you can do with forces...) But you still don't get specific information like that.

                        EDIT: it seems fate would be entirely capable of producing circumstances where the book simply arrived in your hands, maybe a delivery driver makes a mistake and leaves it at your door when someone else ordered it.

                        The difficulty depends on how improbable the circumstances required are.
