    Does Magic Require Explanation?

    02-14-2022, 05:40 PM
    Hello Everyone, so after many years I'm finally running my first 2nd edition campaign (yay \o/) and with it I'm having some difficulty thinking about some issues, to which I ask for help.

    My question is, in a nutshell, "can mages (without Arcanum 5) say: I'm doing this 'cause it is magic and that's that" ?

    Now, for an ellaborate analysis of the issue (I'll try to keep it as short as I can):


    Problem:

    Mage A, with life 4, wants to change his friend's body to produce electricity like an eel.

    Mage A has no knowledge of biology, medicine nor anything "scientifically" about how any of this work.

    Question: Should mage A's Arcanum + gnosis be penalized somehow?

    Argument 1: No. It is magic.

    Argument 2: No, it is magic and Arcanum gives some institive knowledge about this stuff. Besides, it *is* magic

    Argument 3: Yes. This is an alteration of a complex system, mage doesn't truly understand and may have consequences for the body.

    Solution 1: A prior test of, let's say intelligence + medicine / science, so that Mage A may show understanding of the issues

    Solution 2: Casting a Knowing Practice, to gain the true knowledge of what he should be doing AND cast the changing spell under this spell influence.

    Variations:

    Mage A wants to create new functional limbs (arms or even wings: I think this could be a lesser penalization cause it is not that drastic)

    Mage B, with Matter 4, that wants to build a Gun, or a Car (fully out o magic), without no knowledge of science, mechanics, or even Cars in general.


    At first, Argument 3 does appeal to me, however, I soon realized I would have treated my mages unfairly, for different Arcana.

    Case:

    Mage C with Mind, reading thoughts or anything else. We have very little understanding of how thoughts and memories work and are stored/accessed inside the brain, and anything dealing with them, by the same metrics, should have a hard penalty.

    Mage D (even more difficult) with Time. any time effect seems to end my arguments that magic should have explanations of be reasonable somehow. Even if time itself my have some resistance to being changed, it doesnt truly enters in the question of *how* it is being done. It is just magic.


    Exception:

    I had came to the understanding that by having Arcanum 5, the Making practice just doesn't care about this stuff. So you can pretty much make stuff as you go without concerns about how anything is truly working: it just came to exist in that way. (like many software and their code I guess :P), so I would never penalize a 5 Making practice to do the same effects, these mages are trying to do with 1-4 dots.


    However, none of this helps me answer the problems of Mind and Time magic when it comes to penalizing or not acts of magic, and as I said I really do not want to treat players unfairly, nor to nerf them, but my mind just break at the idea that Mage A could do that to a human body without any penalty.


    So, could anyone try to help me with that?
    (and not in a: "do what you feel" kinda way, cause I'm conflicted, and players by default are against penalties :P).

      Argument 1 is correct - it's magic.

      Knowledge of Phenomenal science benefits spellcasting by giving you a symbolset to leverage for bonuses, and lack thereof offers one way the ST can angle the spell going wrong in the event of Paradox, but spellcasting relies on your ability to conceptualize the end result.

      Penalties come from more intensive spell factors being harder to balance and maybe things that would normally mess with your ability to concentrate, but if a spell is producing an outcome, it produces the outcome — stuff like Shaping requires rolls because the spell makes the material shapable, but Transform Life gives a dog wings because magic was used to give it wings, and magic cast with mnemonic aids happens to be more likely to succeed even if you can also reach that outcome with no explanation or visible activity whatsoever.

          Argument 2. If Awakening wanted you to have to have dots in Science or Medicine to do Life effects, it would have including them in the default casting pool. Having knowledge of the Fallen World's rules to better use your magic is expressed through Rotes, and things like the Techne Merit.

          It's also worth considering that Transform Life's primary factor is Potency,not Duration. So if you want it to last more than a turn, you're going to be taking penalties and/or spending Reach as it is. It's not a lasting spell. It might not be the penalties you're thinking of, but it's worth keeping in mind that just because a mage can, in theory, perform this feat with no knowledge of the science involved thanks to their understanding of the pure supernal truths of how life really works, doesn't mean that it's easy for them to do so.

                  I find it easier to think like this from a ‘crunchier’ perspective: Spellcasting is already an Attribute + Skill roll, just like most other rolls are. It’s just that the Attribute here is Gnosis, and the Skill here is the Arcanum used.

                  So, just as you wouldn’t need to add Crafts to a Dexterity + Medicine roll to do simple surgeries just because the surgery involves fine tools, you wouldn’t need to add Medicine to a Gnosis + Life roll to do shapeshifting just because the healing involves biochemical reactions.

                      In-character, mages have ways of explaining how their spells work to an extent, but they're often in philosophical and metaphysical terms, because everything that happens around gaining and using magic suggests to them that those are a more genuine form of reality than physical phenomena.

                      So a mage has understanding of the nature of their target and the features they want to change, but not in terms of scientific biological knowledge.

                      In the system that's encompassed by the Gnosis trait, a combination of reason, observation and intuition from which a mage is able to assemble the symbolic underpinnings of the world that they use to make the visual images of spells.

                      To use a simple reference, a Matter spell to change a cube into a sphere isn't based on knowing and changing molecular arrangements, it's based on viewing roundness as a property of a subject and knowing how to give something more of it.

                                      This meta of magic can be very hard to clear up. In some (mage the ascension, but the idea stands) editions, correspondence was basically always teleporting, occasionally coincidental if nobody watched you doing it and you could justify getting to the place somehow (it could be said that it was vulgar without witnesses, but that's the old debate). In other editions, you could use correspondence coincidentally to get a plane ticket to your destination.

                                      So, if a mage uses correspondence, would a plane ticket just materialize? Would a seat show up open, would the company be hacked? Would a plane be conjured from nothing if no flight was available to the destination? Or would you have Matter to make the ticket, then hack the company with Forces + correspondence, etc? Yes, it is magic, but how finely do you define 'magic'? Could I grow wings to fly using correspondence?

                                      It helps a lot to have everybody in the same general mind, but it is very easy to be both too restrictive and unfair. Try to be as lenient as possible with the players, but know that they will try to abuse the game if you do it too much. Accept that you will fail sometimes.

                                              Originally posted by pcontop View Post
                                              This meta of magic can be very hard to clear up.
                                              Not really. Awakening doesn't have Ascension's problems with how things work. There is, as there is in any highly flexible system like this, areas of interpretation. But the fact that Awakening is not a subjective consensual reality, and magic doesn't work by imposing personal belief over the current consensus-state, means none of the weird RBD stuff from early Ascension has any bearing on understanding Awakening (beyond things Awakening changed specifically to avoid that sort of debate). It should be noted that most of what you're talking about has been firmly addressed in Ascension by this point as well. M20 is abundantly clear that Correspondence can't summon a plane ticket as the "effect" of moving you from A to B.

                                              Space doesn't create a taxi out of thin air to transport you more quickly from A to B. There is no Practice that, when applied to Space, can have that result. The most you could do with Space is summon an existing taxi to where you are, but at that point you could have just teleported yourself to where you want to go.

                                              Awakening 2e doesn't have an RBD vs PBD debate. It's a PBD system. It doesn't have a HAP vs HOP debate, because there is no coincidental vs. vulgar w/o witnesses consideration. It only matters if you magic is obvious to actual witnesses or not.

                                                  It's funny, with the way that the Supernal works, there is technically a level of power at which your desire to have a taxi consists of the universe being rewritten for a taxi to have always been there. It's just a bit beyond what really comes up in play, and is a tad overkill.

                                                  But I do think that gets to a point that understanding the process of spells as altering Platonic Forms can help one grasp the idea that Awakened magic is usually very direct and elemental in what it does. Manipulation of the fundamentals doesn't readily lend itself to something like causing liars to spew beetles.

                                                  One other thing I regard as a neat way to think about the magic is that it ought to function rationally, but doesn't have to. My interpretation of still using the term Paradox along with the newer rules is that Reaching beyond what is safe entails forming an imago that is messy and shaky, that does not constitute a fully coherent Supernal explanation of how the effect comes about. It still takes place, but it's that irrationality which provides an entry point for the Abyss.
