    Archmage: The Seeking 2E (Homebrew System)

    03-14-2018, 10:31 PM
    [Ths post is a work in progress: expect multiple edits and additions over the next few weeks. Comments, suggestions, contributions, etc. are welcomed and appreciated!]


    Archmage: The Seeking

    "I once received a black belt. In that moment, I felt that I mastered my craft. Only later did I find that it was not the end of learning, but the true beginning. Only a master can perceive that fact. You are a Master, as well - one who, Awake, dreamed he was awake. The difference between what you were and what you are becoming is the difference between Earth and Heaven.

    You are Awakening - again.

    The worlds are now dust and nations, which will flourish with your joy and reel with your pain. Your allies will be a constant threat, your enemies the only ones who can understand you. The weak will come to lean on you. The lepers will call you Lord. And all will seek your wise counsel.

    But knowledge is cruel. It will break your heart and test your allegiances. Are you certain you want this curse?"
    -Oreus, Paragon of the Adamantine Arrow

    Archmage Basics
    (Note: Archmages are far too potent to use in a normal World of Darkness game hosting default templates, such as Mage or Vampire. They are designed as Greater Beings - as such, Archmages should not be PCs without prior knowledge and consent from all players involved.)
    *Archmage's souls cannot be tampered with or destroyed
    *Archmages automatically win Clash of Wills in all scenarios except against Greater Beings (entities with both Power Stat [Blood Potency, Primal Urge, Gnosis, etc...] and Template Abilities [i.e. Disciplines, Gifts, Supernal Magic, etc] at 6+ dots). Against Greater Beings, CoW functions normally.
    *Archmage's spells are immune to being counterspelled or dispelled by anything except other Archmages (and Greater Beings when applicable)
    *Archmages no longer follow a Path; they rather forge their own trail to the Supernal called the Golden Road. As such, they treat all Arcana as Ruling.
    *When an Archmage physically dies in the any reality which qualifies as "part of "The Lie," she is immediately transported to the entrance of her Golden Road (or Chantry, or any spot previously designated as a "respawn point") with the "Soul Shocked" condition.
    *Access to Imperial Magic, also known as Higher Practices. These use a new Imperial Factor chart, and are always Ritually Cast.

    Archmages study what they call Concepts: in practice, these are "tiers" of magic that each have two "Interdisciplinaries" (sub-sections). The following is information gleaned regarding these Concepts, and the Interdisciplinaries they contain.

    Please note that the following examples are merely suggestions. The full possibilities of each Interdisciplinary - and the Concept that rules them - are far more numerous than what this post can possibly list. Truly, Archmages are unharnessed from lists of specifics: creativity and planning, necessity and experimentation make each Concept a world unto itself, and as such virtually unlimited in scope within their purview. (The only exception is the Interdisciplinary of Proficiency, which by its nature a finite (yet potent!) list of improvements.

    Arcanum 6 Dots
    The Concept of Intrinsics (Themes: Completion and Initiation)
    -Interdisciplinary of Proficiency
    The first thing a new Archmage does is cement her mastery of the Common Practices. Each benefit is studied and purchased separately.
    Note that all the following benefits of Proficiency apply to Arcanum Dots 1-5 only.
    Among the benefits of Proficiency:
    *Unlimited, free Reach
    *Ritual Casting becomes Instant Casting (Spells can still be Ritually Cast, but the process is instant.)
    *Common Practices no longer invoke Paradox
    *An Exceptional Success on a spellcast may evoke all benefits available to such
    *Common Practices no longer cost mana
    *All Common Practices can be cast at Intimate Sympathetic/Temporal connection (not knowing True Name still reduces level of connection by 1; applies to "Lesser Beings" only)
    *Rotes and Praxises of Common Practices may be combined
    *Any number of Yantras may be applied as a Reflexive action
    -Interdisciplinary of Dynamics
    The Archmage has her first taste of Imperial Magic. These spells are examples of the precept that Magic is Alive.
    Among the benefits of Dynamics:
    *Spells that adapt and evolve, having a base intelligence and will of their own
    *Shielding spells that adapt to the current threat
    *Spells that are passed like a disease or idea from target-to-target
    *Spells that can cast spells

    "I once met a Seeker who slew Luna herself. It was a titanic battle, that lasted several days in Fallen time. It was the most gratituous thing
    I've ever heard of -- I suspect he did it just to prove he could. Luna didn't stay dead for long, mind you; her patronage saw to that. But
    that Seeker is now considered a danger, unstable. His reputation took more damage than what it gained. The Pax falls hard on hubris."

    Arcanum 7 Dots
    The Concept of Establishment (Themes: Creation and Sundering)
    The Archmage has reached a level of power that allows her to transform what exists in accordance to her will. She can manipulate The Lie, herself, and other beings in impossible, wonderful ways.
    -Interdisciplinary of Evocations
    *Shape the Soul, creating Chanties and Ochemata
    *Modify template of self, adding to what exists
    *Modify templates of other beings, adding to what exists
    *Add multiple Legacies
    -Interdisciplinary of Excisions
    *Modify Template of self, removing unwanted qualities
    *Modify Template of others, removing unwanted qualities

    Arcanum 8 Dots
    The Concept of Metamorphoses (Themes: Conceptualization and Oblivion; Imagination Made Real, The Real Made Into Imagination)
    The Archmage has finally reached the cusp of godhood. At this level, the rules of reality can be rewritten wholesale. If "Establishment" is like the Common Practice of Weaving, "Metamorphoses" is the equivalent of Making/Unmaking - unshackled of concepts such as "possible" or "real."
    -Interdisciplary of Incitements
    *Grossly modify the template of herself: create new rules, increase limits of Gnosis/Arcana/Mana
    *Grossly modify the template of others: create new rules, increase the limites of Gnosis/Arcana/Mana (or equivilents of other Templates)
    Interdisciplinary of Extirpations
    *Remove aspects of reality of the self at a dramatic level, removing the ability to die or take physical damage
    *Unmake aspects of reality of others beings, and the world, at a dramatic level, removing the ability to cause harm, think, form Imagos, use
    template resources, or innate weaknesses
    *Remove the ability to activate or utilize abilities
    *Remove something or someone's existence at a fundamental level

    "Leovold has several Ochema, perfect clones down to the birth mark on his ass and free will. Well, turns out that some random upstart - no offense - lured
    one into his sanctum under some false pretense, and somehow managed to kill him. Now Leovold is claiming he was murdered.
    Some are sympathetic to his claims -- hell, at this point it's hard to say Leovold isn't just as much his Ochema as he is himself. Others say he is playing a rhetorical game, playing the victim for sympathy from his peers. The case has garnered quite a following."


    Arcanum 9 Dots
    The Concept of Incarnations (Contained Omniscience)
    The next logical step from Metamorphoses is to seek a way to more readily access the power it grants. The Interdisciplinaries of Incarnations are both Quintessences, which combine to give the Archmage a scene's worth of time to freely cast Imperial Magic, up to and including Metamorphoses-tier Supernal Magic. The only caveat is that a spell cannot be cast to make this effect permanent: if that is accomplished, it is the logical equivelant of Ascension.
    -The interdisciplinary of Selves
    -The interdisciplinary of [X Arcanum]
    When combined, grants access to this state-of-being

    "I drank your nectar, Beloved
    and grew in radiance. Now,
    emanating your sweet fragrance,
    even the Angel of Death has no hope."

    Arcanum 10 Dots
    The Concept of Ascensions
    The state of "Incarnations" made permanent. The Archmage ascends. This is one possible route.
    -Interdisciplinary of Representations
    A quintessence which represents the state of the Archmaster and the Arcana used becoming one. Rumored to be attainable while in the elevated state of Incarnations itself.
    -The interdisciplinary of Eternities
    A quintessence which represents permanence of power. Finding this quintessence is a true test for any Archmage. One can never prepare for it. It can only be
    obtained through a method unique to each individual. Often, this is the ultimate expression of mastery: a challenge so tailored to the individual that any attempt to aid another in obtaining this quintessence automatically fails.

                      Can I ask what the main objective is for your re-write?
                      I recently read DaveB (author of Imperial Mysteries, for those who don't know) say something to the effect of "Imperial Mysteries is out-dated, and much of it is no longer compatible with Mage 2E." I'm paraphrasing, and I don't want to misrepresent DaveB - but I do agree with that sentiment. Archmage is something that has always caught my interest, and I think making it more compatible with Mage 2E is not only great fun, but also opens up opportunity for new gameplay. It's my pet, passion project.


                      Also I would STRONGLY suggest never offering Infinite Reach as a universal spell option.
                      That offers infinite spells, and removes the largest source Paradox dice in the game.
                      I'm not 100% sure if you're aware of not, which is why I'm repeating it...
                      but the Paradox-free casting only applies to the "Common Practices" i.e. Arcanum magic of 1-5 dots.

                      Anything 6+ dots, and/or any magic that draws upon the Imperial Spells Factors (not yet included, but I'll be adding it) DOES INDEED invoke Paradox - and containment makes the Archmage take *lethal, resistant* damage. Paradox-free magic applies to the Common Practices using the standard spell factor chart only. It's part of the Interdisciplinary of Proficiency, which can be summed up as "mastery of the old."

                      I drew inspiration for the "unlimited free Reach" idea from the Core 2E books itself! While not explicitly stated, it is implied that Archmasters can cast common-level (not Imperial/Imperially-altered) magic extremely freely, at a Platonically ideal sense.

                      Supernal Magic is essentially perfection, and the Awakened will is a conduit for it, This is why Exceptional Successes are a - stay with me haha - perfect expression of perfection. When an Archmage casts the Common Practices, my logic is that Reach introduces the possibility of a flawed representation of perfection. Archmages don't have that problem with the Common Practices - therefore Reach isn't posing an issue like it does to the ranks of merely Awakened Mages.

                      I think it bears repeating once more, that this free Reach is limited to the Common Practices only; unmodified by Imperial alterations. Such is their mastery of the old.
                      When dealing with "the new" e.g. casting Imperial Magic, Paradox is even more lethal and prevalent than ever before (at least, until the Archmage in question reaches both Metamorphoses AND has the tools to deal with Paradox alteration (likely 8 dots in Prime).

                                  Originally posted by 21C Hermit View Post

                                  Concepts and Interdisciplinaries. I assume Concepts are, well, concepts, but what are the latter in lay-mage’s terms? Practices? Attainments? Quintessences?

                                  Good question. The simple way I can describe Interdisciplinaries are a mix between Practices (weaving, unmaking, etc) and Attainments.

                                  They are different, new ways of applying magic. The "Concept" is the Practice (Making/Unmaking for example, but described in one word), and the Interdisciplinary is the subdivision (Making = Procifiencies, Unmaking = Dynamics). That's an example, as Unmaking and Dynamics aren't functionally the same, neither is Making and Proficiencies.

                                  In order to master the Concept and move on, one must master both its subdivisions -- the Concept of Intrinsics contains both subdivisions of "Proficiencies" AND "Dynamics." Once both Interdisciplinaries (i.e. subdivisions) are completely purchased via XP, the Archmage can move on to a new Concept. which also contains two Interdisciplinaries (subdivisions). And so on...

                                  It would be like renaming a Master of Life
                                  as a student of the Concept of Creations
                                  with Interdisciplinaries of "Healings" and "Transformations"

                                  Now the Life arcanum is far more broad that just those two abilities. BUT Archmages no longer study a particular Arcanum as much as they study NEW WAYS TO USE IT.
                                  Therefore, Concepts is the sweeping term, the label - the Interdisciplinaries are two (generally opposite) ways of applying that Concept.

                                  At the very least restrict those benefits to whatever Arcanum you've reached Archmastery levels with.
                                  I'm sorry that I didn't make this more clear, but that is already the case. The Interdisciplinary of Proficiencies only applies to Arcana that you've reached 6 dots in.

                                  Also that infinite reach was reserved for certain spells while standing in a Demesne, and for Supernal Entities, who can only use it for very limited Arcana, and are literally the Supernal made manifest.
                                  Archmages travel to the Supernal Realm that corresponds to the Arcanum they've reached Archmastery in, and must make it back alive in order to become an Archmage.
