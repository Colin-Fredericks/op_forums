    Beat Engines

    08-03-2016, 12:45 PM
    I’ve been looking for good beat engines in Mage the Awakening. I think I’ve found a really good one that I’d like to share. I name this the Beat engine thread! Contributions welcome! Enjoy!

    I studied the Astral travel section in Mage the Awakening and found this little tidbit on p 250.

    Mages can return from their personal Oneiroi as a reflexive action. They just wake up. Coming back from anywhere else requires the same Resolve + Composure rolls for each Astral threshold, but doesn’t require the mage to pass through vision challenges. She walks back along the now-resolved Astral Path. Alternately, she can deny herself full psychic reintegration with the material realm to wake up in a single turn, but leaving part of herself behind this way inflicts the Soul Shocked Condition.

    Reading the Soul Shocked Condition on p 318 states that regaining all willpower ends the condition.

    The Clear Thoughts spell as a rote now becomes very useful to end the Soul Shocked condition and get an arcane beat.

    So as a character get Astral Adept meditate into the astral realms and snap back every single night. Heal willpower with Clear Thoughts and get a beat. I think to keep things fair characters should only be able to gain beats during a game session, otherwise a year of downtime turns into 73 arcane experience points. Sleep deprivation might also become an issue without some life magic. This potential for a beat engine makes having a demesne and a warlock with the Clear Thoughts rote very important to a cabal of mages.
    Any thoughts on this?

          You are meant to be able to intentionally inflict conditions on yourself with magic and resolve them as a means of growth, but the ST for a game where you tried this would be remiss if she didn't create some kind of consequence for doing it over and over. These aren't just numbers, you're repeatedly shredding your own consciousness for enlightenment. Conditions can emerge organically from gameplay in addition to being formally caused by specific triggers. If I had a PC do this, I'd probably start the Soul Loss condition track after they'd gotten 4 or 5 Beats out of it. It seems like a Wisdom sin in the same way as creating a soul stone too IMO, not that I'm a huge fan of using Wisdom punitively.

          Also keep in mind that you can only get 1 Beat per scene from resolving Conditions, so there are some pacing issues with any Condition-based "engine." If you really want to spend your ~4 hours every 2 weeks (or however often your group plays) on grinding, be my guest, but surely actually using all those dots you bought would be more fun.

          Also also: c'mon dude, if you want to play a higher-level game, just tell your ST. You don't need to justify it with a rules loophole.

                                                  I mean, we can do the math on it. I would argue that knowingly taking on the risk of paradox as well as deleterious conditions constitutes a form of magical self mutilation which can take you down to 4 wisdom.

                                                  For a hubris 6 act, you get 6 dice which give you roughly a 12% chance of failing your Wisdom roll, or an 88% of not losing any wisdom. You'll statistically be able to pull this off 6 times before losing a dot of wisdom. Six beats (1.2 experience) for that first wisdom dot. For hubris 5, the math comes out to 4 times (0.8 xp), and for hubris 4 acts this comes out to 3 tries before you're statistically likely to lose a dot.

                                                  So, if you're OK with considering this an act of hubris down to Wisdom 4, your mage can trade 3 dots of Wisdom for an average of 2.6 experiences (13 beats). Of course, he may get more or less, but it would be statistically really unlikely (<3% chance) for him to get more than four experiences (23 beats) out of this process.

                                                  Assuming he also gets 1 beat per success for each condition resolved, this is looking at an average of 13x2=26 beats, with just as much chance of only getting 16 beats as 36.

                                                  Is trading 3 wisdom (+ the time it takes given only once per scene) for 3-7 Experiences worth it? Maybe your player thinks so (or maybe my math is wrong!!).

                                                  All in all this feels like much ado about nothing. Yes, technically this is all allowed by the game system but you'd have to have a pretty permissive storyteller and a pretty petty mage to see it happen, no?

                                                          @Kvark: My hats off to you for the number crunching, I wouldn’t have bothered, and thanks to Darksider for your ground rules. Actually thank you to everyone that responded. Let me pull back the curtain on what started me off on this rampage.

                                                          When I read the thread about recharging mana and willpower by spamming praxis spells I was struck by how many people in the community seemed to be in support of taking advantage of a loophole in the rules to gain in character resources (mana and willpower). I have no problem with specific spells that do that. Knit and Mending heal hit points, Clarify Thoughts heals willpower and Essence Fountain heals mana (indirectly). I was fascinated by the idea that people wanted to replace Clarify Thoughts (Mind 3) and Essence Fountain (Spirit 5) with any old one dot spell that could be used to exploit the rules as written. The curiosity bug bit me and I asked “How far will this go?”

                                                          I always look over a game system as completely as I can, especially the experience/improvement portion. I had been considering how to advance a character quickly. Comparing the responses between the Praxis spamming thread and this thread I’ve noticed a powerful split. The same community that largely supports dropping dramatic tension to exploit a loophole in the rules to grant a character in game resources also largely rejects the exploitation of a similar loophole to grant a character experience points (a meta game resource).

                                                          In short most people want their characters topped off at the expense of dramatic tension, but no one wants to see characters freely advance in power without participating in the story.

                                                          I think between the two threads mentioned we’ve uncovered a subject that should be explored further.

                                                          As a final note the best experience engine is to attempt a roll with a poorly know skill or spell once per scene and volunteer a critical failure. That’s the “beat engine” that I would actually use.
