                So first off, apologies for this thread necromancy. I'm finally (and temporarily) crawling out from under the rock of dissertation proposal writing. Enough so that I can finally finish reading Mage 2nd ed and post some 10 (or 11) Path/Watchtower hacks that I've been sitting on for months. Remembering all of the controversy about the rules when 2nd ed was first released, I will say that after reading through the book I've personally found the rules as sound as those produced by the other 2nd editions (which is to say a step above those for Demon and Beast -- but to be fair Demon and Beast didn't have over a decade of playing to inform the shape of their mechanics so...and really the mechanics of those games are solid--they just have some rough edges).

                The primary reason for these alternative Path hacks is to create a more heterogeneous Supernal with more Paths and more player options. One of the things I've chosen to do is to separate Gross magicians from Subtle magicians. This is partially because I find the choices of minor arcanum to be a bit arbitrary (but that is the effects of choosing 'good for the story' over 'logical consistency').

                Since to some extent the Mage ecosystem is a reflection of academia, I wanted to better highlight how heterogeneous yet systematic differences between Paths would be in a rendition that resembles the academic milieu even more closely. This take on the Paths varies greatly from what's presented in the 2nd ed. book, so don't expect a minor change vis-a-vis Paths. I also wanted to play around with the symbolism of the Awakened Tarot (recall that there are 22 major arcana in the tarot deck) to a much greater extent than the main 2nd ed book does. Hopefully we'll see some hacks similar to these in upcoming Storyteller focused books.

                Without further ado:
                Gross Paths:
                Alphaios: Ascetics & Gurus on the Path of Scourging
                -- Alphaios employ Life and Time to achieve mastery over themselves. In human tales and legends they appear as wild hermits and lonely figures dwelling far beyond civilization. When they can be engaged in sharing their knowledge with their fellow human beings, we call them Gurus and thank them for their guidance in overcoming our own inner demons, but far more often we call them Ascetics: those who have abandoned society and relationships with others to cultivate their own Inner Being.

                You think you've seen them but they often take forms you hadn't considered.

                The kindly old man down the street offers you a bit of advice in passing. You've heard the tales that he was once married but his wife died young. He once had children but they died too. He's a miserly fellow that pays no attention to the community. He just dwells in his ramshackle old house and keeps his neighbors at arm's length. But if these things are all true why is he telling you that dietary changes will help you overcome your night terrors? How does he know that you suffer them at all?

                Everyone thinks the local crazy lady is homeless but one day you and your friends got lost hiking in the woods. As you hopelessly weaved to and fro, unable to find your way back, you caught sight of her. Her simple stare from behind a far off tree seemed disappointed. She was long gone by the time you reached that tree, but you caught sight of her again and again until she delivered you back to familiar places.

                That's what Ascetics do: pave the path to better living by example.

                Alphaios Magic

                Ruling Arcana
                -- Life and Time: They're the Inanitan powers that govern physical change. They're the twin motive forces of birth and entropy that make choice possible and scribe out the terrible weight of consequence into the world.

                Life is the wellspring from which agency is born. From it comes the ability to make choices. It governs growth, health, and abundance. But it also brings about largesse, indolence, and disease. It is the power of shaping oneself and those around one. A wise Ascetic knows how to temper herself and those around her into their optimum forms.

                Time is the river that folds and hammers the living into their new forms as they constantly choose and change. It carves experience into their souls and lets growth unfold at the pace it is meant to. It trains the body through cycles of plenty and scarcity. It delivers the consequences of choices onto the choosers.

                Inferior Arcanum
                -- Space: The endless wastelands of Inanitas are a blasted landscape that look the same no matter which direction one faces. They are not to be navigated by choice and quickly punish the living who try. By some supernal design, those who wish to travel the wastes must first still their bodies.

                Symbols and Myths
                -- The Hermit and the Hanged Man. Loners, demons, and ascetics. Stories of enlightenment, inner powers, and great temptations. Pain and privation.

                In the Awakened Tarot, the Alphaios personifies the Hermit: an introspective soul constantly seeking to know himself. Ascetics pursue their introspection by denying themselves the pleasures of the world. The live simple lives and employ their magic as a means to bolster their ability to endure adverse conditions around them. Their mysteries involve the Hanged Man, a card representing the perpetual need to sacrifice.

                Ascetics are well known for invoking Supernal Demons who harry, castigate, and tempt them. It is only by constantly testing one’s mettle that one can achieve their perfect form. Many Alphaios bind personal demons to themselves so that they are perpetually challenged on a daily basis, for only by having their decisions cross-examined can they truly understand the consequences that spiral forth from them.

                An Alphaios usually employs tarnished, decayed, or worn materials as yantras for her spells. Clay bowls symbolizing the Watchtower of the Empty Bowl are a typical ritual tool employed by Ascetics. Staves of dried, twisted wood, tarnished coins, and rods fashioned from sunbleached bones are also commonly seen tools. When the Mages who walk the Path of Scourging are forced to defend themselves they employ barbed clubs, whips, and other cruel weapons that are often coated in rust or stained and tarnished.
                • Approach: Choice & Consequence
                • Major Arcana: Life & Time
                • Minor Arcana: Space
                • Oracle: Alphaios
                • Watchtower: The Empty Bowl
                • Awakened Tarot: The Hermit & The Hanged Man
                • Supernal Entities: Daemons & Daimons
                • Supernal Realm: Inanitas
                • Yantras: Tarnished coins, clay bowls, dried wood, sunbleached bones, distressed materials; Barbed clubs, whips, cruel weapons

                  Acanthus: Alchemists & Enchanters on the Path of Wonder
                -- Acanthus wield power over Time and Matter. In legend they frequently appear in the form of apothecaries and smiths. When they can be persuaded to set aside their arcane experiments to craft wonders for their fellow humans, we name them Enchanters and marvel at the marvels they forge. Far more often they cannot be roused from the sanctuary of their laboratories where they pursue knowledge for its own sake and we name them Alchemists.

                Acanthus often hide in plain sight much as they have done since bygone ages when they and people like them played important roles central to a community’s health.

                The young woman at the pharmacy window hands you your medication and cautions you to use it as instructed. You don’t realize that after work she retires to an abandoned school-house. It’s ramshackle chemistry lab looks like someone has repurposed it to cook meth and other designer drugs. It’s real purpose is hidden in the arcane symbols and otherworldly reagents that she has gathered in her quest for the Philosopher’s Stone.

                Your teenaged boy retreats to the family basement every night. You think that he’s just playing with the old chemistry set that you gave him one Christmas when he was eight. Your sleeping eyes never apprehend the fact that he’s moved beyond transmuting lead into gold. You never realize that the special tea he keeps brewing for you is the only thing holding the cancer you don’t even know you have at bay.

                Acanthus Magic

                Ruling Arcana
                -- Matter and Time: The Pandemonic forces of creation itself. They are the dualistic forces that weave existence itself from the nothingness of the void. Every Acanthus knows that it isn’t enough to simply create things; true creation requires time to become. The Acanthus approach to magic is the most patient of all Paths and, it is not unusual for their arcane works to take years or even decades to mature into fruition.

                Matter is the arcanum that holds dominion over the stuff of creation. Its constituents fashion the basic building blocks from which all things are woven. It represents the firmament upon which all things extend, energy given physical form. A wise Alchemist always seems to know exactly which substance is needed for a given task.

                Time is carrier through which one substance changes into another substance. It is the process of chemical interaction itself. Its passage blazes the trail by which material substances share their essential essences and participate in the perpetual cycle of bonding and unbonding. It is the experience through which things mature into new forms.

                Inferior Arcanum
                -- Forces: The chaotic, ever-changing landscapes of Pandemonium are a living history of substances coming together to form new substances before breaking apart to reemerge as their sovereign selves. While heat and cold are by-products of these processes it is the materials forged through the passing ages that define the place and, their greedy interactions with one another subsume the forces around them, leaving little ambient energy for visitors to benefit from.

                Symbols & Myths
                -- The Lovers and the World. Alignment, union, integration, and accomplishment. Tales of invention, creation, adulteration, and maturation. Chemical reactions.

                In the Awakened Tarot, the Acanthus stands for the Lovers: the fundamental relationships among all things. Alchemists’ obsessions are rooted in the unions of the simple materials of the universe which lead to more complex substances which themselves can be bound together. They use their magic to uncover the fundamental truths of all of creation. The World lies at the heart of their mysteries, signifying their need for accomplishment and to complete their imperfect understanding of everything around them.

                Alchemists sometimes summon Supernal Chimeras, amalgamations of mythical and living beasts, to aid them in their arcane science. These creatures provide valuable ingredients such as fur, ichor, spines, and venom that Alchemists employ in their experiments. In turn the Acanthus pay the Chimeras tithes of Mana or favors, which occasionally lead to substantial bonds between the two.

                Of all the Awakened, Acanthus are the ones who employ impossible, refined, and volatile materials as components of their spellcraft. Heavy, metal scaled gloves fashioned from steel, orichalcum, mithril, or adamantine symbolize the Watchtower of the Iron Gauntlet and are often employed in the alchemical practices that shape Acanthus’ rituals. Wands and rods of glass, staves of wrought iron, steel coins, granite mortars, and glass beakers are common tools of the Alchemist’s Path and, in times of conflict Mages who walk the Path of Wonder gird themselves with blades fashioned from star metal and other rare or difficult to manufacture substances. Contemporary weapons that employ the fire of chemistry, such as guns and grenades, are also popular choices in war comes calling.
                • Approach: Maturation & Creation
                • Major Arcana: Time & Matter
                • Minor Arcana: Forces
                • Oracle: Acanthus
                • Watchtower: The Iron Gauntlet
                • Awakened Tarot: The Lovers & The World
                • Supernal Entities: Anachronisms & Apeirons
                • Supernal Realm: Pandemonium
                • Yantras: Iron coins, glass beakers, granite, sulfur, volatile materials; Fire, grenades, weapons forged from impossible materials like adamantine and orichalcum

                  Paramonimos: Geomancers & Masons on the Path of Control
                -- Paramonimos are privy to the secret workings of the Space and Forces arcana. In tales they are often cast in the roles of leaders who are responsible for choosing the locations of new settlements and planning out the layout of those municipal centers. When they play the role of Mason they are typically responsible for the construction great civil works that all share a stake in. Far more often they wear the mantle of Geomancer and are the architects of great arcane designs that weave Sleepers and Awakened alike into the weft of their fabric.

                Paramonimos sometimes hide by operating in the open as professionals that work in civil engineering or architecture. They are just as likely to work from the shadows though.

                You thank the middle-aged woman who handed you the architectural plans for your house’s new addition. Happy with the shape of the new space, you never suspect that she arranged the foundation, walls, and ceiling to provide an invisible sanctuary for you from the myriad things that creep about in the night. All you know is that months later, after the carpenters and contractors have finished building it, you always awaken feeling refreshed and new anytime you have the occasion to fall asleep in it.

                The aged politician calmly explains to you and everyone else assembled why the street intersection needs to be broadened into a roundabout. You find his explanation for why a grand fountain at the roundabout’s center is necessary to complete the project compelling. You know nothing of the ley lines running below the streets that he hopes to constrain. Nor are you aware that his staff was responsible for arranging the seating and interior decorations for the municipal council’s public hearing.

                Paramonimos Magic

                Ruling Arcana
                -- Forces and Space: The Tractian arcana that rule motion. They are the primary colors in which locomotion is painted. By mixing them together a Paramonimos can achieve control over their surroundings by determining where and what kind of force is to be applied.

                Forces is the arcanum that weaves the elemental energies of the world into one vibrant spectrum. The chill of cold, the heat of fire, the lift of air, the crackling sizzle of electricity, and even the impulse transmitted by the impact of solid earth, all of these things can be mastered through knowledge of Forces. And once some modicum of mastery has been achieved, all of these things can be applied to bring about some effect.

                Space is the infinite canvas upon which reality is etched. While it’s essential nature is timeless, every instant of its existence is tightly bound by the situation that is carved into its many-dimensional form. A knowledgeable Geomancer employs their knowledge of Space to ensure that every application of Forces, be they natural or arcane, is carefully constrained to its proper place.

                Inferior Arcanum
                -- Time: The ageless structures and landscapes of Tractus never change. This place forms an endless labyrinth of temples containing whirlwinds that never stop spinning, columns of fire that never cease burning, and bolts of lightning suspended between walls. Whole cities, both those lost to the sands of time and those that have yet to be conceived, stand here. While visitors can find any construct, building, and physical place that they can imagine, nothing new can be fashioned for the abstractness of form itself exists outside of the linear thought processes of living minds.

                Symbols & Myths
                -- The Emperor and the Chariot. Control, assertion, structure, and foundation. Stories of constraint, the elements, and the application of forces both arcane and natural. Ley lines and configurations.

                In the Awakened Tarot, Paramonimos wear the mantle of the Emperor. Geomancers arrange symbols, materials, and structures to control, constrain, and manage the forces that ceaselessly move around them. The Chariot bears the essence of their mysteries, carrying them to the places where control needs to be asserted and marking the extent of their mastery.

                As the Mages whose magic is the most reliant on specific places, Geomancers frequently summon forth Supernal Genius Loci. These beings are often yoked to harvest mana or seal elemental forces into place. Many Geomancers will bind themselves to the beings who represent their sanctums lending them a small amount of substance in exchange for ritual aid, arcane tutelage, and even supernatural defense.

                Geomancers often employ valuable materials such as jade, silk, vellum, and even hand-crafted paper in the material workings of their spells. Highly detailed, ornate compasses, hand carved from ebony or other precious woods represent the Watchtower of the Ebon Compass and play a vital role in a Geomancer’s ritualistic spatial planning. Plates fashioned from jade, staves of lacquered ironwood, and alabaster wands decorated with silk ribbons or lengths of carefully knotted paper are frequently employed as implements by those walking the Path of Control. When Geomancers are forced into physical conflicts that deny them the direct application of elemental energies, they are known to employ chains, chakrams, and other esoteric or improvised weaponry. A cornered Geomancer can be a dangerous opponent because they always seem to know which of the things in their immediate vicinity best lends itself to their defense.
                • Approach: Constraint & Application
                • Major Arcana: Space & Forces
                • Minor Arcana: Time
                • Oracle: Paramonimos
                • Watchtower: The Ebon Compass
                • Awakened Tarot: The Emperor & The Chariot
                • Supernal Entities: Architektonias & Orismas
                • Supernal Realm: Tractus
                • Yantras: Ebony coins, jade plates, paper, silk, valued materials; Chakrams, chains, esoteric or improvised weapons

                  Daidalos: Conjurers & Thaumaturges on the Path of Progress
                -- If Acanthus are mad chemists, and Paramonimos are grand architects, then Daidalos are the tinkers among the Awakened. Stories focused on them often tell the tales of the creation of things that were better left uncreated. When they affect objects from afar we know them as Thaumaturges. Typically Daidalos are far more renowned for their skills as Conjurers, bringing ordinarily impossible things into existence by exploiting the secrets encoded within the hidden essence of the Matter and Space arcana.

                Daidalos maintain hidden, sometimes hermetic, existences. The world’s Sleepers almost never realize that they’ve encountered one until they are confronted by things that are clearly impossible.

                You’ve always felt pity for the homeless man who dwells in the junkyard two blocks over. It’s clear from all of the steel sculptures he makes that he’s some kind of savant. You would have never guessed that each of his creations possesses some unnatural spark of life, even though you get the strange sense that they’re watching you as you run by the yard every day during your morning jog.

                Everyone is amazed by the skill and luck of the cat burglar who has become the scourge of the region. You’d all be even more amazed if you saw her in action and witnessed how she seems to have a tool for everything. No matter what the contingency, there always seems to be something in her pockets for overcoming it. Even the fluffy white rabbits she leaves as her calling card are produced from spaces no one imagines.

                Daidalos Magic

                Ruling Arcana
                -- Matter and Space: The Voluntian opposites that form the essential building blocks for the Fallen World. They are both presence and absence, existence and nothingness, two faces of the same coin. The caricature of life. The proceeds of artifice. Daidalos learn their secrets to refine the world around them.

                Matter provides Conjurers with the grist they use to mill the world anew. It allows them to realize even their most feverish imaginings in the permanence of raw substance. While they might not craft life itself, any manner of unaging simulacra might be fashioned. Tools and raw materials are always at a Conjurer’s fingertips.

                Space symbolizes the absence of substance to Conjurers. They employ its secrets to bring materials, tools, and even people and animals from afar to their side. They can also employ it to send the summoned away again. The greatest among them though, understand that it can be used to fold substance in upon itself to create objects that are bigger on the inside than the outside.

                Inferior Arcanum
                -- Life: The lands and seas of Voluntus are seemingly full of creatures of all manners and descriptions. Vast cities and sprawling factories dot its surface. But nothing living moves in any of those places. Those making the pilgrimage to source of Conjurers’ power find a strange realm filled with clockwork animals, clay people, and stranger facsimiles of the living.

                Symbols & Myths
                -- Justice and the Devil. Truth, law, the unnatural order of things, permanence. Materialism, artifice, transgressions, forbidden acts of creation. Simulacra and facsimiles.

                Daidalos act as the hands of Justice in the Awakened Tarot. It is only through their familiarity with the Fallen World’s supernatural laws that they are able to work around them, bend them, break them, and occasionally, ignore them altogether. Daidalos mysteries are symbolized through the Devil.Every Conjurer must make some amount of peace with their ingrained desire to push the envelope of what can be done with their arcane mastery over substance and its absence.

                Daidalos frequently dabble in the creation of artificial life. More than a few of their number have been known to summon Supernal Golems to aid them in their explorations. Especially skilled or foolishly curious Conjurers have even been known to persuade such entities into agreements through which their raw essence is reforged into supernatural clockwork automatons, or other even more unusual artificial beings.

                Conjurers employ hand-crafted implements, mass-produced substances, and other products of human artifice in the spellworks. Small jeweler’s hammers carefully crafted from pure aluminum are indispensable ritual tools representing the The Watchtower of the Aluminum Hammer. Concrete troughs, aluminum rods, wands hewn from granite, and rune-etched oak staves are common accoutrements employed by those whose feet are on the Path of Progress. When Conjurers are confronted by conflict the prefer to employ mechanical weapons like crossbows, or more recently compound bows, and any weapon that resembles or can be employed as a tool, such as picks, hammers, and similar objects.
                • Approach: Permanence & Transgression
                • Major Arcana: Matter & Space
                • Minor Arcana: Life
                • Oracle: Daidalos
                • Watchtower: The Aluminum Hammer
                • Awakened Tarot: Justice & The Devil
                • Supernal Entities: Idealisms & Myths
                • Supernal Realm: Voluntus
                • Yantras: Aluminum coins, concrete troughs, carved wood, hewn stone, worked materials; Crossbows, picks, tool-like weapons

                  Mastigos: Warlocks & Wizards on the Path of Thorns
                -- Mastigos are the stereotypical magicians that appear in traditional high fantasy stories. They wield the elemental forces of the world as though they were living things. In stories that cast them as supporting characters who aid the main characters with their wisdom and knowledge they are called Wizards. More popularly they are cast in the roles of villain or anti-hero and we know them by the name, Warlock.

                Even when they make their homes in bustling cities, Mastigos often dwell apart from the world. When they bother with employment they tend to hold jobs that permit them to cultivate their interests from the relative safety of plain sight.

                The woman from the forestry service was nice enough to let you and your boyfriend off with a warning. You knew that the forest preserve was closed at that hour of the night which was why you chose to park there. You’ll never realize the real reason that she chased you away is that the invisible, arcane energies born from your lust interfered with her own after-hours rituals.

                Everyone agrees that the philosophy lecturer at the junior college is an odd bird. He’s late to his own lectures, seems to daydream during office hours, and spends more time wandering around the campus than in the building outside of those specific times. You don’t understand that he’s constantly marveling at the world around him. The perpetual information overload allows him to see how the world is alive in ways that you could never perceive.

                Mastigos Magic

                Ruling Arcana
                -- Forces and Life: The Arcadian principles of energy that align and link all things. The results of fear, anxiety, and illusion released unto the world. The vitality, positivity, and success that results from confrontation. The exposure of all that is hidden.

                Forces provide Warlocks with the means to release the world around them from Abyss-induced Sleep that confines it. Mastery of its elemental secrets provides its practitioners with the strange and violent abilities popularized by genre literature and fantasy culture. It is the illusion of strength.

                The secrets of Life hold the means for Warlocks to confront the world around them. Without the energy of the living, existence can have no meaning and the knowledge hidden in all things cannot be revealed. It is the font of warmth that comes from understanding one’s connections among the web of all things.

                Inferior Arcanum
                -- Matter: Arcadia is a supernal realm that seethes and roils with life. Each and every thing, no matter how base of an element is its own vital entity. From the wind, lightning, and rain, to the most sullen and stagnant puddle, nothing is inert. Mages whose spellcraft calls for raw materials find that there is a dearth of such things there for even Arcadia’s seas and deserts breathe and move.

                Symbols & Myths
                -- The Moon and the Sun. Illusion, fear, and anxiety. Success, positivity, and vitality. Confrontation and release. Overcoming obstacles.

                Mastigos symbolize the Moon in the Awakened Tarot. Their world-altering arcane powers inspire insecurity in others which suits these Warlocks fine for they have long realized that only by confronting one’s insecurities directly can one hope to dispel life’s illusions and arrive at a more fully actualized self. Their mysteries involve the Sun. Possessing and wielding power isn’t an end in and of itself. Release from the prison of one’s own illusions can only come about through the proper application of power.

                Supernal Elementals are often summoned by Warlocks as a means to further their goals of Awakening those around them or Ascending beyond themselves. Particularly powerful Warlocks will bind such entities to their service in order to accumulate even more power over their surroundings.

                Warlocks fit long established fantasy tropes by employing polished metal, still water, crystals, and other reflective materials in their spells. They frequently embroider their clothing or other cloth based sanctum objects with a single silver long-stemmed rose to symbolize their deep connection to the Watchtower of the Lunargent Thorn. Particularly skilled Warlocks sometimes cultivate actual, unnatural silver roses. Spotless porcelain mortars, brilliant silver coins, hardwood staves polished to a reflective gleam, and wands tipped with crystals or gems are frequently employed spell-casting implements for those who walk the Path of Thorns. Warlocks employ long knives, swords with curved blades, and other practical weapons (even well-maintained handguns and rifles) when they march to battle.
                • Approach: Release & Confrontation
                • Major Arcana: Forces & Life
                • Minor Arcana: Matter
                • Oracle: Mastigos
                • Watchtower: The Lunargent Thorn
                • Awakened Tarot: The Moon & The Sun
                • Supernal Entities: Elementals & Imps
                • Supernal Realm: Arcadia
                • Yantras: Silver coins, porcelain mortars, polished hardwood, crystal, reflective materials; Curved swords, long knives, practical weapons

                    Subtle Paths:
                    Moros: Necromancers & Sages on the Path of Loss
                    -- Moros tread a path that reveals truths lost to the entropic forces of the past. They explore arcane forces that normal mortals dread: the crucible of change--Death, and the echo of meaning--the Mind. Fables that ascribe beneficial roles of advising people how best to cope with loss name them Sages. In darker fairy tales they are more commonly described as performing all manners of unsavory congress with the dead and so name them Necromancers.

                    Among the the denizens of the Fallen World, Necromancers make themselves easily overlooked by cleaving close to the objects of their arcane study. They typically find positions that will regularly bring them into contact with the dead and the dying.

                    You thanked the pathologist who calmly told you that your brother’s unexpected death was quick and painless. You never discover that she merely confirmed the cause of the death by sawing his skull open and, that word of its quickness and painlessness came from the lips of your brother’s ghost. In truth, she learns as much from the deceased themselves as she does from their inert bodies.

                    You had hit rock bottom when the young priest visited you in the hospital. You never knew that it was your late great grandmother’s ghost that brought him to you. But you were thankful that your talk revived your memories of her. If she survived Nazi death camps, then surely you can survive your crucible too. The memories the conversation brings forth helps steel your will and by the time he leaves you know you’ll persevere.

                    Moros Magic

                    Ruling Arcana
                    -- Death and Mind: The Stygian doorways to other-wheres and other-whens. From them unfurl manifold resting places for secrets both unknown and forgotten. They are the pathways, both brilliant and shadowed, that lead to every place ever imagined.

                    Death is the primordial force of entropy. All too often cast as the reducer and destroyer of all things, Necromancers realize its true nature is that of transitional force. There is an impermanence to all things. Even apparently immortal beings have realized their own ever-changing nature, saying, “Transient beings are we.” Death is the principal force of change for whole beings, entire systems, complete communities, and similar entities of scale.

                    Mind is the solitary fortress of memory. And even when a newborn butterfly emerges from its chrysalis, it is Mind that recalls that it was once a catepillar. Necromancers plumb its depths to learn secrets kept only by the legion of dead. It allows things long forgotten to be found and known, if imperfectly, through the lens of imagination.

                    Inferior Arcanum
                    -- Spirit: Stygia is drab grey wasteland where only the dead dwell. As such the spirits of the living have no power and Mages whose spellcraft relies on them find their powers drastically constrained. So inimical is this supernal realm that spirits who accidently wander into its endless ashen plains or find themselves upon the cold black waters of the Styx quickly fade away or slowly turn into unliving ghosts of themselves.

                    Symbols & Myths
                    -- Death and Judgement. Transition, change, and transformation. Absolution, reincarnation, and rebirth. Memories, both remembered and lost.

                    Unsurprisingly, Moros are synonymous with Death in the Awakened Tarot. They deal with ghosts, the unliving, and other entities that have transitioned from one state to another. Their mysteries are centered around Judgement which delivers the new beginnings promised by every ending. For Necromancers, every change comes layered in hidden meanings.

                    When the ghosts of the Fallen World are no longer able to answer their questions then many Necromancers will turn to Supernal Shades, shadows of memories long forgotten, for inspiration and counsel. The greatest Necromancers will sometimes bind such entities to their will so that knowledge of the past is ever at their beck and call.

                    Necromancers employ materials that were previously entombed within the earth for their magical practices. Every Moros bears with her a lead coin that represents Watchtower of the Lead Coin. Those who walk the Path of Loss frequently use pewter chalices, wands or rods fashioned from old bones and gemstones soldered into rings or medallions as aids in their rituals. When conflict arises they prefer to arm themselves with hammers, maces, and other blunt weaponry that is capable of crushing bones for it is not unusual for an enraged Necromancer to find occasion to employ the dead themselves as weapons.

                      Thyrsus: Mystics & Shamans on the Path of Ecstasy
                    -- Thyrsus tales involve intervening in spiritual matters. When those tales speak of interventions that bolster the boundaries between the world of spirits and that of humans, they speak of the works of Shamans. Many tales speak of interventions that rupture the boundaries written by the fickle hands of the fates. Such tales speak of the works of Mystics.

                    Divided from the spiritual world by the Gauntlet, most Sleepers have little need for a Mystic’s interventions. The majority of Thyrsus are forgotten by their mortal contemporaries and lead furtive lives in the shadows of decaying cultures.

                    The stomp dance leader looks like a medicine man. You don’t believe for an instant that he actually has magical powers, no matter what stories your grandmother tells you. You put your trust in modern technologies: pills in bottles, chemical disinfectants, and machine diagnostics. You never realize that those practices all directly derive from older ones he still employs or that they still serve the same purpose of keeping the spiritual in equilibrium with the living.

                    You look down on the big game hunter. All you can see is the obvious damage he causes to the environment around him. You don’t comprehend the true nature of his intercession. You never see the prey’s acceptance of its fate and you can’t understand the role he plays in helping it transition from one state to the next.

                    Thyrsus Magic

                    Ruling Arcana
                    -- Fate and Spirit: The Lustrian pathways to the future. The inexorable progression towards becoming. The boundaries between what-is and what-is-yet-to-be.

                    Fate weaves the story of existence itself. In its knotted threads every Mystic can see what a thing was and what it will become. It is the immutable boundary between the past and the future which must be pierced in order for things to find their proper places.

                    All things contain a Spirit and skillful Mystics can converse with them. In doing so a Mystic can learn the secrets of what is meant to be and recognize violations in the natural order of things. Particularly skilled Mystics can even pierce the boundary between the Fallen World and the Spirit World and interact with the Darwinian entities that live there.

                    Inferior Arcanum
                    -- Mind: Lustra is a purified realm filled with fauna, flora, and fungi of all descriptions. These highly spiritual beings exist in perfect harmony with one another. Predators never consume more prey than necessary, herbivores never overgraze, and vegetation never overgrows. This perfect harmony is only possible because nothing reasons about its place. There are no questions asked and no thoughts had. Everything acts in accord to its primary nature. Mages whose spellcraft relies upon the output of living minds are at a disadvantage for harmonious beings do not distinguish among their bodies, minds, or spirits. All are one.

                    Symbols & Myths
                    -- The Empress and the Star. Boundaries, barriers, and walls. Intercession, action, animus. Fertility, nature, abundance. Spirituality, renewal, inspiration.

                    In the Awakened Tarot, Thyrsus are signified by the Empress. They are deeply connected to the natural world and the animistic spirits that inhabit the things within it. Their mysteries are closely intertwined with the Star which guides them on their journey.

                    Mystics often summon Supernal Beasts to their aid. These entities provide valuable insights or can be employed to guide Mystics to places they are needed. Frequently appearing in the guise of animals, many Mystics form special bonds with creatures that they repeatedly summon and interact with.

                    Stone, wood, leather, earth, and other natural materials form the base upon which Mystics build their magical practices. A smooth round stone, sometimes granite or limestone, and sometimes a semi-precious stone like quartz or geodes, is used to symbolize their connection to the Watchtower of the Singing Stone. Turquoise coins, wooden mugs, and wands, rods, and staves fashioned from wood and decorated with uncut, nature-shaped stones round out the ritual tools employed by those on the Path of Ecstasy. When Mystics find they must employ violence to champion a cause the typically employ axes, javelins, and other weapons used specifically for hunting, like rifles and shotguns.

                      Chrysanthos: Psychonauts & Sorcerers on the Path of Might
                    -- Stories of the Chrysanthos involve the universal principles of that are fundamental to all of existence. They are the namers of all things and through naming they spin the fabric of knowledge into being from the nothingness of ignorance. In stories that have them turn their keen perceptions outward, we call them Sorcerers and attribute powers both terrible and malign to them. More typically though, stories showcase them turning their perceptive minds inward, growing cold and indifferent to their fellow human beings as they take up the awesome mantle of Psychonaut.

                    Psychonauts hide right beneath the noses of the Sleepers around them. They take on appearances that are both familiar and alien to us.

                    You watch your comatose daughter as she lies still and asleep. You morne all of the things she will never learn about had she the normal life that you’ve had. You remain blissfully unaware of the universe of infinite possibilities that her Awakened mind has just discovered. You never understand that your tears are for the experiences you are missing out on. She lives a silent life that you wouldn’t be able to fathom even if you knew of it. Through your dreams, she gives you brief windows looking out onto the myriad places she has seen, all in the vague hope that they’ll shake you Awake but, you never stir.

                    You shake your head in pity for the drug addict shooting up in the corner. You believe that he has settled into the corner in order to set himself adrift in a soporific sea of dull sensation. You don’t know that the needle contains a harmless quantity of saline or that he’s just beginning his work day. You are totally ignorant of the inner worlds he has to traverse while he seeks out the deepest held secrets of existence itself. If you had an inkling of what he was truly about, the knowledge might drive you insane.

                    Chrysanthos Magic

                    Ruling Arcana
                    -- Mind and Prime: The Signian forces responsible for conceptualization, action, and actualization. The communicative principles interlinking all things. Arcane mathematics.

                    Mind is the principal force that empowers Psychonauts. It permits them to make sense of the world around them. Concepts, propositions, language, and meaning all spring forth from its infinite depths. It is the framework through which communication between intelligences becomes possible.

                    Prime is the motive force that enables Psychonauts to realize the products of their minds’ toils. Symbols, systems, utterances, and intentions all descend from its ascendent heights. It is the substance through which communiques propagate outwards, like ripples in water.

                    Inferior Arcanum
                    -- Fate: Among Pychonauts, the mind’s processes and the reality written out through their application are paramount. The Supernal Realm of Signum realizes the ideal form of these interactions. In it is a place where the will to envision and the will to realize are principal forces. Mages whose spellcraft relies on the happenstance of destiny find their powers are greatly weakened for Signum’s denizens are masters of their own destinies.

                    Symbols & Mythology
                    -- The Magician and Strength. Power, skill, action. Courage, control, compassion. Command of one’s self and one’s destiny. Perception, conception, the naming of all things.

                    Chrysanthos symbolize the Magician in the Awakened Tarot. Theirs is the power over the very stuff that comprises magic--motive power and the will to use it. While most Psychonauts spend their time obsessing over esoteric things that are far beyond the simple borders of the Fallen World, their mysteries are deeply intertwined with Strength, which provides them clues regarding when they should employ their arcane might to affect the Sleepers around them to most efficaciously Awaken all.

                    From time to time Psychonauts summon Supernal Taxi to aid them in furthering their understanding of the Supernal. Particularly experienced Psychonauts often maintain close relationships with these esoteric beings of raw conceptualization, sometimes going as far as to Name them anew.

                    All Psychonauts bear an indigo tattoo of a glyph of a concept central to their spellcraft. While each Psychonaut’s glyph is particular to her, the tattoos all signify a membership in the family of the Watchtower of the Azure Glyph. Inks, dyes, berries, and materials that cause stains are important tools of their trade, allowing them to mark their passage through the places they’ve passed on their voyages. Electrum coins, brass inkwells, wooden staves painted in brilliant colors and wand-like or feathery pens are all vital implements for those who walk the Path of Might. When Psychonauts prepare for war they gird themselves with rapiers, bows, and similar weapons of high precision.

                      Obrimos: Clerics & Theurges on the Path of Enlightenment
                    -- A surprisingly large number of humanity’s ancestral legends describe the activities of Obrimos. They have watched over the spiritual side of human beings since before the world’s Fall. In those legends that focus on illuminating the inner processes of the human soul and dispelling ignorance, they are known by the name, Theurges. Far more legends describe individuals who employ their enlightened abilities as bastions that uphold the traditions, be they capricious and insular or liberal and dynamic, of the Sleeping masses around them and, in these tales they are known as Clerics.

                    Obrimos frequently cloth themselves in traditional garbs that allow them to move through the masses using the anonymity of familiarity.

                    The voudoun sets your offering of tobacco afire and utters your pray to the Loa. You miss the magic she has woven into the ritual and when your brother begins to recover from his alcoholism you ascribe it Their intervention. Perhaps your ignorance of the spell she has cast actually reinforces its power, its effects making themselves known in ways that you fully expect according to your spiritual traditions.

                    The altar boy goes about his day-to-day tasks largely unnoticed by you. The candles are always lit and extinguished as prescribed by Church-law. The altar is always clean and the holy water basin is always full of clean and pure water. You remain completely ignorant to the subtle spells he ritually casts through his mandated movements. You ascribe the sanctum’s beneficent effects on those who come each day to the holiness of the space itself and never suspect that it isn’t the space that sets them at ease but rather the Mage who works within it.

                    Obrimos Magic

                    Ruling Arcana
                    -- Prime and Spirit. The Aethereal forces that descend from the universe’s innate divinity. The substance of the soul. The font from which true belief springs.

                    Prime is the creative principle that allows Clerics to forge substance from nothingness. Its material essence flows through all things. Energy in its purest form. The hottest flame, the brightest light, the most powerful lightning. It is the wellspring from which every Cleric draws her strength. The final arbitration of purpose.

                    Spirit is the supernal given form. It is the ethereal base from which the world was originally fashioned and to which it will inevitably return. True substance. The miraculous written out into the world. It is the bedrock that supports the faith that sustains every Cleric. The internal truth of all things. Identity from conformity to tradition.

                    Inferior Arcanum
                    -- Death: In the Aether what comes before, during, and after life are one and the same. Practioners whose magic relies upon the concepts of change, where one thing becomes another, find that their spells falter and fail, for whatever a thing might be elsewhere it is always its purest form that is revealed within the supernal realm’s confines.

                    Symbols & Mythology
                    -- The Hierophant and Temperance. Faith, conformity, and tradition. Balance, patience, and purpose. Purity of substance and form. Prime motion. True belief. Enlightenment. Community.

                    In the Awakened Tarot, Obrimos are identified with the Hierophant. Given to them is the secret knowledge of the true forms of all things. They deal in purified energies and materials both. Their mysteries are closely linked to Temperance. The Fallen World’s truths can only be revealed by striking a balance between motive force and substance.

                    Theurges sometimes call upon Supernal Angels to champion their causes. The most tradition-bound of their number sometimes yoke such entities into extended servitude for the sake of their communities. Such beings often advise, inspire, protect, and watch over the faithful. Occasionally they are even employed to destroy on the faithful’s behalf.

                    Clerics employ bleached linens, distilled liquids and other perfected materials in their spellcraft. Each and every Obrimos bears a soft key fashioned from pure gold that unlocks the door leading into the Watchtower of the Golden Key. Golden coins, steel cups, wands of petrified wood, and staves of carefully cultivated hardwoods are often employed as ritualistic tools by those walking the Path of Enlightenment. When called to defend their communities, Clerics often wield double-edged swords, spears, and other weapons associated with nobility.

                      Aethos: Soothsayers & Witches on the Path of Doom
                    -- Myths that describe Aethos often ascribe powers of insight and prophecy to them. Theirs is the burden of knowing that which is yet to come and exacting the price by which that knowledge is earned. They are known as Witches in those myths where they employ knowledge of the future for their own ends. More frequently though, myths paint them as Soothsayers who share their insights with all who are willing to listen to their mad ravings.

                    Soothsayers lead hidden lives and often dwell in rural locales. After millennia of persecution at the hands of others, most rarely share their knowledge.

                    The neighboring farmer was slow in sowing his fields this year. You thought he was merely being lazy but the late rains drowned most of the seeds you had planted. You never suspected that he saw patterns the weather and environment that you were beyond the patterns recorded in your almanac. You just chalk up his avoidance of the disaster to the luck of indolence.

                    Everyone thinks that the class goth girl is crazy. You go so far as to actively make her life miserable, after all, conformity is an important part of growing up. You had it coming when she snarled back at you and pronounced a curse. You don’t believe in such things though. You chalk up your stubbed toes and flat tires to simple bad luck, never realizing that the magic she used against you was very, very real.

                    Aethos Magic

                    Ruling Arcana
                    -- Death and Fate. The Malic forces that record the shape of the future. The paths laid out before the traveler. The web of destiny. The end and the beginning.

                    Death is the end of all things. Soothsayers use their knowledge of it to predict when endings are nigh. It is the all consuming force of oblivion that collects all to its breast. Tears shed from Cassiel’s eyes. The one true door leading from what was to what will be.

                    Fate is the tangled skein of threads that chart out the courses of all things through their lives. Understanding its complex, multi-layered patterns give Soothsayers insight into paths that each thing inevitably walks. Every crossing, every meeting, and every parting can be found within destiny’s weave.

                    Inferior Arcanum
                    -- Prime: The labyrinthine expanses of Malum are a dark and shadowed space where no fire can burn. Travelers must rely on the energy they bring with them to navigate its twisting passages and cavern-like expanses. Mages whose spells invoke the primal energies of the universe find that their magic operates at a tiny fraction of its normal power.

                    Symbols & Mythology
                    -- The High Priestess and the Wheel of Fortune. Intuition, insight, and prophecy. Luck, karma, and destiny. Inevitability. The Collection of that which is due. Crossings, meetings, and partings. Departure from the familiar. Oblivion.

                    Aethos are represented by the High Priestess in the Awakened Tarot’s arcana. Given to them is the fortitude of vision to pierce veils and resolve mysteries. A Soothsayer’s intuition is her greatest asset. Aethos mysteries revolve around the workings of the Wheel of Fortune. They are privy to the Fallen World’s cycles, both visible and invisible. It is through charting its revolving path that they can see the path that lies before them.

                    Soothsayers will occasionally summon Supernal Spectres to aid them in charting destiny’s byways. Echoes of things consumed by oblivion, Aethos treat with such creatures carefully lest they fall victim to the dark energies that course throughout their supernal substance. Particular daring or completely doomed Soothsayers sometimes go so far as to bind these entities into servitude.

                    Blood, fungi, and decaying things are the tools of a soothsayer’s trade. A simple copper-bladed sickle is the badge of her allegiance to the Watchtower of the Copper Sickle. Maps, charts, copper coins, bronze cauldrons, and brass astrolabes are important tools for the rituals and spellcraft employed by those walking the Path of Doom. When made to defend themselves, Soothsayers employ subtle weapons like poison or small weapons that are easily concealed like daggers and darts.

                    The Pathless

                    Mavericks: Bards & Savants with no Path -- Not all Mages Awaken to the well-defined track of a path. These Mavericks make do without the guiding tutelage of a mentor and literally blaze their own trails through the Supernal wilderness. Those that share the fruits of their difficult explorations with their fellow human beings are known as Savants in tales told by those they’ve aided. The vast majority use the knowledge gained from flitting from one destination to the next for their own aggrandizement. People who cross paths with them, name them Bards.

                    Extraordinarily rare among the Awakened and oft-times confused for the Mad, Bards find it hard to fit in with the Sleepers that surround them.

                    The chubby girl sitting in the coffee shop’s corner listens to music all day long while she writes in her notebook. You’ve already confused her for some kind of local poet. After all, it’s a better explanation for why she constantly murmurs to herself and seems deep in thought. If you ever managed to look over her shoulder and glimpse the arcane truths she fills page after page with, one of you would certainly be institutionalized.

                    The middle-aged stock boy always has a far-away look in his eye. You think it makes him clumsy and error-prone and in a way it does. But you’d collapse under the deluge of information he is constantly observing. If you knew the truth, that his mind travels afar, unshackled from a body that has been left to go through the automatic motions of a simplified life, your own grasp of reality would very likely unravel.

                    Maverick Magic

                    Ruling Arcanum
                    -- Bards Awaken to find themselves in the Supernal wilderness, far from the paths that lead to the ten known Watchtowers. The may select any one Arcanum of their choice as their one and only ruling Arcanum.

                    If at any time they find a master Mage willing to form a mentor/pupil relationship with them they replace their chosen ruling Arcanum with their mentor’s ruling Arcana. This acts sets their feet on a specific Path which they cannot, thereafter, abandon.

                    Inferior Arcanum
                    -- Since Bards wander back and forth across all of the Supernal Realms they do not suffer the disadvantage of possessing an inferior Arcanum. Should they ever find a mentor and, thereby, elect to trod one of the ten Paths they gain the disadvantage of that Path’s inferior Arcanum becoming an inferior Arcanum for them.

                    Adopting a Path
                    -- Most Mages who Awaken as Bards have fierce independent streaks. It is not easy for them to enter into a mentor/pupil relationship with a master who walks one of the ten Paths. In addition to purchasing a dot of the Mentor merit to represent their new master, they must sacrifice a dot of Willpower. The master must expend a point of Willpower to seal the relationship and bind the pair in the sacred rights of tutelage.

                    Selecting Arcana
                    -- During character creation, a Maverick’s ruling arcanum may have from one to three dots. A generalist may distribute her starting Arcana dots evenly across six different Arcana (i.e., 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1) in addition to the other options.

                    Symbols & Myths
                    -- The Fool and the Tower. Innocence, spontaneity, and freedom. Disaster, upheaval, and revelation. Experimentation and theft. Making one’s own way through the world.

                    While Mavericks do not walk a particular Path they still find representation within the Awakened Tarot through the Fool. They revel in their unbound ability to walk among all of the Supernal Realms and rely on their innocence to pilfer knowledge when it cannot be won through experience and experimentation. Their mysteries are bound by the Tower. Wrestling one’s way through the Supernal wilderness is not without risk and more than a few Bards end their careers as one of the raving Mad.

                    Bards are not completely helpless amongst the warp and weft of the Supernal. Every Bard can call for Supernal Guides to help them make their way from one place to the next. Particularly wizened Bards or those who have mastered a great deal of Magic have been known to bind such entities to serve at their beck and call.

                    Even though Bards are relatively promiscuous with the materials they employ in their rituals and the tools they use to aid their spellcraft, each and every one of them maintains some form of personal journal representing the secret eleventh Watchtower, the Watchtower of the Hidden Tome to which no Path leads. Every Maverick must find their own way to its seemingly empty halls.

                    Empty Legacy
                    -- Bards may not learn Attainments. Since they are all self-taught, they have no Legacy to ascend to.

                    In the Orders
                    -- Note that Mavericks may still participate in Mage society as normal and benefit from membership in one of the Orders. They might also choose to forego such membership and dwell as one of the Nameless.

                    You're Doing It Wrong (or the Left-Handed Paths)
                    While some practices like human sacrifice and ritualized abuse are obviously Left-Handed, there are those among all of the Paths that consider entire magical traditions to be subtly and inherently Left-Handed. This is because the threshold for abusive practice can be a blurry and ambiguous thing. More than a few Mages walking Gross Paths consider Subtle Paths, with their command over magics that interfere with the fate, mind, and spirit to be Left-Handed in nature. Similarly, some Mages walking Subtle Paths consider Gross Paths, which are all more than capable of shaping the physical world around them, to be Left-Handed in nature.

                    These distinctions don’t stop at the divide between Gross and Subtle Arcana. Some Awakened consider Paths that employ their Inferior Arcanum as a Major Arcanum to be Left-Handed. After all, there must be a reason why they are denied such powers while others use it freely. What else could it be other than the inherent wrongness in such spellcraft?

                    And there are many who deny that Mavericks wayfare across throughways off the known Paths and instead choose to believe that these masterless Awakened walk a dark Path that leads directly into the maw of the Abyss. To these Mages, being self-taught is, in itself, sign enough that one is walking a Left-Handed Path.

                    Ultimately, while some magical practices are obviously Left-Handed, there is also a large grey area of practice that is left for individual Mages to decide whether or not they believe them to be Left-Handed in nature. Like walking a Path and learning from one’s mentor, which practices fall into which categories is mostly a matter of personal perspective for the Awakened. And thus who is Left-Handed varies from Cabal to Cabal, Consilium to Consilium, and Caucus to Caucus.
