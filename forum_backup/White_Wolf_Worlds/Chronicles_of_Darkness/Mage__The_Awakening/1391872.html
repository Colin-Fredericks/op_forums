          It all depends on the themes you want it to have. For example, what kind of person is the Hierarch ? A competent boss dealing with a balancing game ? A near tyrant that keeps everyone in line ? An incompetent mage that got there via political savvy ? A folk hero that was elected to an office he never wanted ?

          What kind of infrastructure each of the Orders have ? What kind of political influence do they have ? What are their political rivalries? For example, the Free Council could have the best quantum thaumaturgy lab in the world in this city, but little political power. Maybe its due to the Guardians gaining favor after suppressing an especially nasty Invader. How does that color the power dynamics and the Consilium's policies ?

          Does the Consilium have a budget for magical defenses, a command structure and a plan in case of an emergency? It might if the Arrows hold sway.

          How is the Lex Magicka enforced? What freedoms do Sentinels and Suspectors have ? Are they subtle or just kick in the door ? Does the Consilium believe in promoting Wisdom to prevent creating Rapt ? Or do they only solve the issue if it appears ?

          It all depends on the feel you want to create and the number of moving parts that seem right for your story.

                                                      On the "Too many titles for the number of Mages" problem, I generally find that its fun to have NPCs wear multiple hats. Its realistic considering you probably aren't going to want dozens of master mage NPCs in a single city and it tends to add lots of opportunities to add depth and unusual political intrigue to their interactions. The Consilium Herald might also be a Ladder Factotum and the leader of a major local Legacy for example.

                                                      This is doubly true at the top end. In every game I have run or played in the local Hierarch has doubled as the leader of his local order caucus and its not unusual for the Councillors of the various paths to include the local Epopts/Adamant Sages etc.

                                                      If your looking for the various titles and their job descriptions the "Mage Society" section of the 2e Corebook has a surprisingly thorough overview of them all, which should be plenty for a crossover game/setting outline. The 1e Order books are great for additional details and I think someone already mentioned Sanctum and Sigil.

                                                      Leaving a few positions notably vacant is also a fun hanging plot thread if you have Mage PCs who might be interested in claiming those jobs.

                                                          I see others have come up with some good advice, but I'll add some of my own:

                                                          - In LA you can get away with a really high population of supernatural individuals, higher than you could reasonably stat properly, actually. Taking into account inner city /suburb population, transient population and some rural odd members, you can go crazy researching and creating NPCs. What you really need is to answer two questions: How cosmopolitan/political is my chronicle going to be? and, What are the themes and mood I'm trying to portray? The answers to these questions would put some boundaries to your design, while allowing you to focus on what really matters for the chronicle.

                                                          - What gamelines/creatures are you intending to fully describe, which are only going to be in the background and which are non-existant in YOUR Chronicles of Darkness? Again, talking about the games your players are really going to make PCs for can help you bring up the main themes and conflicts of the chronicle. You should build your setting around that, and not the other way around, IMO.

                                                          - What degree of interaction between supernatural communities are you aiming for? Each CofD gameline exists in the same shared universe, but the modular nature of these games means that there is little to none official lore regarding crossover and/or multisplat games, barring the upcoming Contagion Chronicles. Are each supernatural community aware of the other ones in the area? Is any community in conflict with any other? Are there any isolationist communities? Is there a non-aggresion pact in place? What about Tier 1 groups? Is it common to find multisplat groups and/or factions? Is there a goberning, arbitral and/or legislative body regulating interactions between each supernatural faction in the city?

                                                          I have my own example to show. My chronicle is set in New Orleans, with a heavy emphasis on Mages and Vampires over other splats. No splat is absent from the setting, but Demons and the God-Machine are fairly in the background, and Beasts are not a playable option at the moment. I was aiming at a Tier 2 game, with some local politics that are more about personal ambitions and vendettas than any overarching faction's agenda. In New Orleans all communities are more or less aware of each other, and multisplat parties are not unheard of, though most such groups are usually temporary alliances more than established parties. Althoug there is no direct competition between splats, there are certain "niches" that each community is known for, which the other factions are adviced to stay clear of them. Vampires have their bloody fingers in most high-level politics and financial companies; some of the bayous are under direct protection of certain werewolf packs; changelings are very discreet and are only seen with any regularity only in certain points around the city, especially in the French Market; mages oversee several mysteries in an around the city, but tend to avoid the French Quarter and the Superdome to avoid clashing with the vampires there. Oh, and the local Beasts have made themselves a reputation for keeping the peace among the communities and serve as a go-between among factions, but only because they viciously hunt and devour any serious offender to the status quo.

                                                          Well, there is more to that, but I hope this helps you.
