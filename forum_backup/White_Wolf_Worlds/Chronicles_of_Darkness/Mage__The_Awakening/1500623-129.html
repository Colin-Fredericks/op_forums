                        Even outside considerations about Familiars, but simply going by spirits and other Twilight denizens, I think it very weird to have this hard rule that vehicles are intangible for them.

                        Bringing strange entities with you is an entire trope and common folklore motif. The idea is not particularly tied to the entity being capable at attaching to things or something, and isn't even tied to a particular kind of entity.

                        Particularly in this case I don't think it fair to call this interpretation a "projection". The idea is intuitive. A lot of people would naturally have this understanding. The way it's written may be technically clear, but it's easily something that they could have missed for thinking it obvious rather than intended like that.

                        This should have received more attention since actual games will face all sorts of liminal cases. If the God-Machine, or just a really big machine, moves a mountain, do the spirits stay or are they moved? Where's the litmus test of what's a vehicle and what's ground for the entity?

                        In the specific case, I don't see a benefit for the game, either. If a Familiar can't get into a car, the Mage will have to be on foot whenever they want to keep the Familiar they paid for? How is the fiction benefitted by having to left your Familiar behind when traveling abroad? It isn't even in tune with the narrative of a Familiar, as yes, those entities are totally expected to be close by to their masters, always.

                        Sure, the book is written the way it's is and the methods to solve this are there, particularly fetter, so whatever floats your boat.
