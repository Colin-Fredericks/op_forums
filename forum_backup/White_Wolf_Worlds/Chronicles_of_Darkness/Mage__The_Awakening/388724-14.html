                            Is it relevant whether those Supernal symbols exist or not? Radio waves exist, they havr always existed, but it does not mean that a prehistoric Obrimos understood what they were or what they could be used for. Likewise, a prehistoric Moros can command the earth, but would they know that mixing tin and copper makes bronze? The power of control does not necessarily come with requisite knowledge or understanding.

                            A Moros may command metals, but why? To what purpose? Most pure metals are soft and useless for tools. Would the concept of alloys occur to a Moros? Or would they just magically tweak what's there because that's so much easier? And even if it did, would they have the requisite understanding of how to make the alloys naturally? Shape them? Cool them?

                            It's a fallacy to think these sorts of inventions are obvious. They only seem so in hindsight; when the benefits are manifest. A Moros COULD tinker with this stuff he finds in the stone, but why? To make shiny things? His tribe has ancestors that need appeasing and stone or bone tools that need enchanting! Leave that muck to the vain chieftains and their strutting priests; there's work to do.

                                        The way I view it is that the Supernal Symbols are akin to another set of symbolic representations: letters, words, sentences, and languages as a whole. The reason being is that it actually ascribes to both the Diamond and FC and with what we know from IM.

                                        So letters are simple symbols. They represent a sound, and some can even represent things beyond that, such as an A being a great grade or an F being a failing grade. But when combined in certain ways, they can become a more complex entity that is itself a unique symbol: words. The letters C,H,A,R, and T all are symbolically unique, but combined form the word a charge, which itself is a unique symbol of something. The key is that those individual symbols (the letters) only have meaning if properly aligned, as "thcra" and "arcth" are just a jumble of the individual symbols and are meaningless (at least to my auto-correct ). Then these more complex symbols can be combined, there meaning changing not as significantly but still shaping an even more complex symbol: a sentence. "The please chart the patients vitals an overnight medications" and "As you can see in the chart, 4th quarter earnings were at an all time high" use chart in 2 completely ways, and these meanings, in the conetext of the other words, gives the sentence even more complex and unique meanings. But again, order and associated symbols matter, as changing the syntax or word choice can create numbed messes that are meaningless.

                                        So the Supernal is akin to this. In the very beginning, mages likely could only observe the simpler symbols, the "the letters". While the idea of the device that is a radio likely was always a part of the Supernal, it is likely akin to a "word": a collection of simpler force and matter symbols (radio waves, conductivity, etc.) akin to "letters" that when oriented properly form the symbol of the device we call a radio, a "word", which itself is a more complex but unique Supernal symbol. But without even the idea of such a device, mages would never see this orientation of symbols or have the idea of such an orientation. And even more complex symbols, such as the "warrior-sage" or "philosopher-king" would not arise until significant social development from basic symbols of "ideas" and more complex such as "dedicated specialize labor" manifested from the orientation of Supernal elements.

                                        The reason this works for me is that it doesn't dismiss either the Diamond or the FC. The Diamond is right:the Supernal does contain all elements of things that ever rightfully should be, the language developed already. Yet the FC is right, as symbols arise from human development, as the patterns arise as unique symbols form from the convergence of other symbols as Humanity explores the world and create new things that draw on the existing to create something new/more complex (electromagnetism, copper and radio waves always existed, as did the potential for a radio, but the actial symbol never coalesced till someone posited and built a Radio). And it means that the timey-wimey issues of the Supernal changing aren't an issue here without also needing to explain why there were no Neolithic DJs, as the symbols were present just like words of the English language are present in a word scramble: if you don't know any words, all you see is a jumble of letters, even if the words are right in front of you, while someone who has a broad vocabulary might pick up words that weren't even supposed to be part of the puzzle.

                                        (Posted from my phone, so as always, I apologize for any typos).

                                        Edit: Oh, and IM: it is editing the language directly. It is making it so "Thrca" has a meaning now, and is not just a random jumble of symbols, or making it so "Chart" has additional or different meanings. It is why you need to align to world to fit your omen: you are shaping the "letters" and then defining that collection with the phenomenal. And like all changes or new additions, the change can take on a life of its own, or alter the use of other words associated with it. It also explains why Omens and Imperium have such drastic effects: unlike the emergence from converging symbols, these are wholesale additions that were artificially added.

                                                For what it's worth, the Vinca made (mostly ornamental) things our of native copper, so they would have a decent idea of the physical properties of metal as opposed to other types of materials.

                                                It might also be worth noting that things like stone knives make poor weapons; chipped stone tools are pretty much single use, though an individual piece of flint can be worked multiple times to produce different kinds of edges for, say, butchering an animal. The cultures of Neolithic Europe were known for polished stone axes, and these were more durable (you could use one to chop down a tree), but would probably make unwieldy weapons.

                                                On the other hand, the hunters of Europe already had pretty powerful bows by the Mesolithic, which could absolutely be used to kill a large animal or human being. Altering the power of a bow or the speed of an arrow seems like exactly the kind of subtle magic that Neolithic mages might use to take down powerful foes.
