    2E Prime

    04-27-2017, 06:12 PM
    I have a bad habit of changing topic with disregard to OP which ends in derailing threads or being ignored(second option is probably better). So this time I'm making a proper one.
    Here we talked about Dispelations and Museigen mentioned that Supernal Dispelation should be a Ruling spell. While I'm not sure it is enough there is a strong case for it:

    Ruling spells grant fuller control over phenomena than a
    mere Compelling spell. Water can be made to flow uphill
    or into unnatural shapes (Matter), animals (or even human
    beings) can be commanded (Life or Mind), or time can be
    momentarily made to accelerate or slow down (Time). A
    Ruling spell can’t fundamentally alter its target’s abilities:
    Water can be directed, but not turned solid or gaseous.
    Time can be altered, but not overwritten. An animal can
    be commanded, but not made stronger or fiercer.
    But cancelling a spell is fundamental part of the spell. I can be canceled.

    Thre is so many things that are wrong here that I don't know where to start.
    Firstly, Dispelation are almost directly ported into 2E with conjunctional arcana at first dot and none for fourth dot. Why the conjunctionals? If you want to influence magic with Prime you shouldn't need any of that, its in your purview. Mind doesn't need Spirit to work on spirits. It's a relic from 1E.
    If Compelling can DESTROY! the spell completely what could Ruling or Fraying do?
    S.Disp. description with all this 'casting into abyss' seems to be a huge overkill.

    PS: I'm really afraid that SoS will have plenty of conjunctional spells that will throw 2E back on 1E and we are just lucky now that they haven't got enough word count and decided to publish mostly standalone working spells.

    Edit: Even the book contradicts itself with Universal Counterspell Attainmant (2nd dot). You can affect any Arcanum with Prime at this level.

      You also keep assuming that "there are fewer spells that require conjunctional Arcana to target particular phenomena" means "there should be no target-based conjunctional Arcana whatsoever." A spirit's mind is a mind, however alien, but that doesn't extend to all potential overlaps. If you can't stand the thought of Awakened magic running on more stringent metrics than spell factors and raw semantics, you may have to resign yourself to the fact that that's going to continue to be a thing.

      I've pointed it out before and I'll point it out again: Dispellation got easier in 2e at the cost of having the default state of the effect be suppression rather than termination. Spells can end, but they don't then naturally arise again with undiminished qualities.

      Compelling the flaws in a spell that allow Phenomenal rules to reassert themselves to suppress it requires knowledge of the principles that that spell interferes with; Unraveling an active spell into an inactive spell just requires the capacity to target the spell itself as an extension of its caster.

                          Okay - So looking at the two. I think that the real question is - why is it that dispellation can be compelled so long as you have a dot in each arcana, and yet it requires unravelling in order to perform the same feat without that same Arcana.

                          So - the understanding of Dispel magic is that all Awakened spells contain the capacity to end and for the Fallen World's laws to reassert themselves - You compel the already existing flaws in a spell in such a way that the spell ends prematurely or is suppressed

                          Supernal Dispellation goes a step beyond this, attacking and tearing a spell apart. In all mechanical ways, it is identical - with a single exception of the fact that you do not need to apply additional Arcana in order to affect the spell in question.

                          Honestly, I'm not going to say that I believe that Supernal Dispellation would fit the Ruling Practice - I don't think it fits. The thing is I don't necessarily think the explanation of 'compelling the flaws' works well for Dispel Magic either. It feels like that explanation works better if it were a fraying practice than if it were a compelling practice.

                          Originally posted by MtA p. 123

Fraying spells can weaken targets under the Arcanum’s purview: damping a fire (Forces), sapping Strength (Life), or eroding the barrier between worlds (Death, Spirit, or others, depending on the worlds in question).
