                                        Originally posted by galivet View Post
                                        Is there a pre-existing resource for handling Spirits and the Spirit Arcanum as a storyteller beyond what's the in the 2E core sourcebook?
                                        • How to incorporate Spirits into every setting (planning ahead)
                                        • How to adjudicate unexpected uses of Spirit by creative players (rules of thumb for what's reasonable and what isn't)

                                        The material for Spirit feels a little abstract and unintuitive for me to handle consistently. I'd love it someone could point me to a play-book or blog post or something from a ST who has it down pat.
                                        Look at the purviews in the Spirit Arcanum section and you will get a sense for what Spirit can cover.

                                        My own advice is to remember that spirits are omnipresent but also follow a kind of path of least resistance. Most spirits are little more than animals and don't have grand plans or thoughtful schemes. They influence the world, but they do so by perpetuating and strengthening resonances, rather than by possessing people or deliberately scheming. Now, don't get me wrong, those things also definitely happen, but they're the exception, not the rule.

                                        For example of how this works, and how important Resonance is as a concept, consider the following scenario:

                                        You are out hiking in the forest in winter, when suddenly a storm you didn't prepare for picks up and blankets the area in frigid winds and deep snow and you totally lose your way. Seeking shelter, you stumble across an old log cabin and decide it's better to shelter there and wait for the storm to pass rather than risk freezing to death. You light a fire in an old iron stove and find an old bed. Snuggling up under your emergency blanket, you settle in for the night.

                                        Now, we consider a divergence. In one scenario, the cabin has Resonances of warmth and comfort. In another, it has Resonances of isolation and desolation.

                                        In the former case, when you settle into the cabin, you'll hear winds whistling outside and the crackle of the fire and feel strangely cozy; like the cabin is a fortress and you're safely secure from the natural dangers outside. It's oddly peaceful and serene and you enjoy an evening with your thoughts. Then, outside the window, you spot the shape of a stranger approaching. They come to the door and bang their fist upon it.

                                        In the latter case, when you settle into the cabin, you can practically hear the holes in the old sealing; the shriek of wind as it slips through and bites at any skin you have exposed. The fire feels low and barely radiates heat more than a few feet, no matter how much fuel you give it, and the cabin itself feels like a solid gust might simply save the storm the trouble of freezing you and bring a roof down on you instead. You can't sleep, because you feel utterly alone and terrified that your end is coming. And then, from the darkness, a stranger approaches the cabin. Raising their fist and smashing it against the door.

                                        Nothing materially has changed here. But in the former scenario, you're more likely to assume it's a wayward traveler, like yourself, in need of shelter, and not a threat for which you should ready your gun.

                                        That's how spirits affect the world. They take resonances like that and they amplify them; strengthening them; feeding on the essence they produce in a constant feedback loop.

                                                    Something to remember is that most physical objects have a spirit in them, but it's a very weak and quiescent spirit. That's the basis of Coaxing the Spirits, which lets you do things like make doors open or engines start or guns jam, or what have you, because these are all things within the purview of the spirit of the object and something that could reasonably be affected by said spirit were it active. You're essentially casting a spell to tell the spirit of that object to use its Influence on that object.

                                                    The thing about that power is that it doesn't actually scale up, because the target is ultimately the same weak spirit. So, for example, a casting of Coaxing the Spirits could make a burning flame move in a given direction or grow more intense, but a Spirit Ruling spell couldn't command it to go out. Likewise, a Spirit Ruling spell couldn't make water run uphill. The spell is still targeting the same weak spirits and they don't get stronger just because you have.

                                                    Spirit's a very powerful arcanum, though. In no small part because it's the only one that can move essence, regardless of the type of entity involved (though you need dots in the relevant arcanum to affect entities other than Shadow spirits). That means that Thyrsus are in a unique position to bargain with alien entities, regardless of type. A Thyrsus can snatch essence from Resonant Conditions and feed it to their pet spirit pretty much as much and as often as they want, which makes them unmatched for chiminage.

                                                    Likewise, while big and powerful spirits are rare, small and weak ones are common, and most anything you could care to find is available somewhere. A Disciple or Adept of Spirit can, with relatively little effort, gather up an obedient servant that most other Mages can't even detect, yet alone effect, before even considering how powerful such things are compared to Sleepers. Even a Rank 1 spirit with the right Manifestations or Numina is a world-class thief.

                                                    So, yeah. Really, Spirit is a fairly potent arcanum. The only real question is how unWise your player(s) (is/are) willing to be.

                                                            Uuugh I looove spirit. But it doesn't seem like ST's care much for the arcanum or down-right hate it. I thought it would get better when I tried to get a werewolf game started and play as a mage, but then it became about forsaken vs pure and then the spiritual landscape just disappeared from thought.

                                                            But hey! I've got two cents to spend on the Acceleration spell thing.
                                                            I can understand having difficulty controlling your speed if you are using forces or space, but why Time? Isn't the shtick that you are slowing down time slightly except for yourself? So you'd be able to control your speed just fine, what you're not controlling is the illusion of your speed cuz how much you are affecting time is set. So you're not at risk of losing control of how fast you are going, but you are going to need to recast the spell if you do not want time to be moving as slowly. The spell does seem to care about concentration. I think it is one of those strange instances where a spell is still very similar to a previous one but given more powerful effects simply because it is a different practice, like how if you want gross control of a lifeform you need life 4 and not just life 2, as opposed to Mind 2's control just requiring reach...

                                                            but I do freaking love the spirit arcanum. If I may, I think there are two arcanum that are pretty much double-edged sword, catch-all types, which will get varying amounts of ire out of your ST because their nature is able to get things done the other arcana are better suited or more appropriate for.
                                                            Those are Fate and Spirit. However, they are double-edged swords. If you want to start a storm, the appropriate arcanum is Forces. However, if your ST is okay with it, I personally see a lot of justification for Fate and Spirit being able to cause storms too. The problem is in the details, and I think that's why it is a problem for ST's and their players. Players want their spells to get the effects they want as soon as possible, and ST's want to further the plot.
                                                            A Fated storm is going to take longer to show up than one made with Forces, or it may appear under very different amounts of control or purpose than your Forces storm would. You asked Fate, and Fate delivered, but it does so under its terms, not that of your Imago, unlike the Forces version.
                                                            Spirit has the same issue, except it would work faster, cuz it uses another creature(s) magic. The storm may happen much faster, but it is going to come under the terms of the creature who you compelled/asked/bribed to make the storm. The next issue is that all your spirit-magic is going to involve you in the vicious, feudal politics of the Shadow. Unless you're prepared to maneuver that delicately, or are already in a position to, as Axelgear said, be unWise and just be a Shadow-tyrant yourself, all your spirit spells are going to build yourself a dangerous reputation with other powerful Shadow monstrosities.

                                                            So Fate can get almost anything you need done, but it will require a lot of grease, a little more effort from you, and may not show up the way you wanted it to, or take significantly longer to produce results.
                                                            Spirit will get almost anything you want done quickly, but its going to come with a lot of Shadow-related baggage as you entrench yourself deeper into an alien world's doings and goings-on.

                                                            And these are the reasons why those arcana are both frustrating, and very fun. But they can also feel like cheating to some people. If your ST lets you make fetishes like crazy you're going to have an armory of paradox-free spirit-magic, and that's just unfair to other players.
                                                            Unless the ST arranges for something to go disastrously wrong for your cache and you fail to protect it Wouldn't it be awful if you, say, pissed off the Seers of the Throne or the Guardians for using Quiescence-free magic in public using fetishes, and they just so happened to tip off a tribe of Pure-werewolves with a powerful totem that would just love to have a new slave/pet-mage. :3
