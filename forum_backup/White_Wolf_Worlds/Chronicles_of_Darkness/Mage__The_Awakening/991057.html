    [2E] Orders' Obsessions

    10-21-2016, 03:08 PM
    Opening sum up of idea - Characters can take more broad interpretations of their Orders Mysteries as his more broader Obsession.

    * Adamantine Arrows - Oaths - Examples are classical 'Protect my cabal'
    * Free Council - Techne - Examples are various Sleepers occult traditions or technical disciplines Also, parts of Libertine Creed - one players choose for our game 'Servants of Lie' as his Obsession.
    * Guardians of Veil - Persons and Beings - Abyssal Intruders and Hieromagus are examples, particular Sleeper in Labyrinth would be other.
    * Mysterium - Disciplines of Order - I.E.
    • Magosophy
    • Supernology
    • Thaumatology
    • Occultism
    • Sidereology
    • Teratology
    • Exoterics ( Each one of Sleepers arts and humanities )
    * Silver Ladder - Persons and Institutions - Elemental Pilgrimage and Lion-Sage-Stag are particulars in here, promising Sleeper student would be other. Looking over church, company or government office.
    * Seers of Throne - Signs of Exarchs - Mystery Commands are particulars here.

    Rest of opening idea that sprung this take on Obsessions:
    One of my players have very cool idea for Mysterium characters - instead of normal Obsessions of rest mages being particular and defined Mysteries - Mystygoges can choose one of thier disxiplines of Knowledge. It lead to interesting characters as Mysterium members devoted not only to particular supenatural being or realm, but rather whole category. Like mage can be Obsessed over werewolves or vampires. Mysterium members can be over all supernatural races.

                                                  Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                                                  And how would those works in play?
                                                  To answer this question is something that has to be at least modestly taken care of on an individual basis. Different Mages will pursue different Mysteries in different ways.

                                                  Generally speaking, if you wanted to include a system like this, what I wouldn't do is just let a character take "Teratology" or what have you as a Mystery, but ask your players to list on their sheets somewhere a small constellation of broad Mysteries that the character easily obsesses over. Werewolves, Sleepwalkers, "Magic Is Alive", Masques, etc. Then just use the existing rule that, at any time, a character can switch out an Aspiration/Obsession to one more relevant to the story if appropriate.

                                                  In the case of Guardian Masques, "Acquire Mask X" is probably kinda dull on its face if the character is literally just spending XP to get it. That's not an Obsession because it doesn't actually change the gameplay; it is just twisting mechanics for an XP discount (and a minor one at that). If a character lists that masque as an Obsession, the only smart thing to do is to not let them buy it. At least, not straight away.

                                                  Instead, provide the character with a teacher. This doesn't need to be a person, but it does need to be a related opportunity; something that requires them to immerse themselves in the character or nature of the Masque in such a way that puts them in some sort of danger. This need not be violent; it could be social or mystical danger.

                                                  Off the top of my head, let's say you've got Lucifer, a Mastigos Guardian who wants to acquire the Adversary Masque. Lucifer wants the mask because it lets him engage in subterfuge and deception without guilt (or with less guilt, at least); adopting a persona whose nature is to oppose, subvert, and ruin. Developing an insight into a mystical personality is hard, though; you can't just go "Oh, okay, I'm gonna be a professional bastard today". This is serious mental programming and mystical union. It requires serious difficulty and effort.

                                                  So Lucifer spends his days reading history books on famous betrayals; psychology texts about all the human safeguards against betrayal buried in our evolutionary past; forces himself to commit small betrayals like short-changing stores (something for which his Wisdom might suffer).

                                                  The whole thing comes to a head when Lucifer has a vision of his own conscience standing watch over his psyche; pleading with him to turn back from this path; to refuse to engage in this behaviour. When he refuses, it transforms into a great flaming angel and declares that so long as it lives, his conscience shall never be clean. So long as his conscience is not clean and pure - so long as his mind is not absolutely dedicated to the effort - he shall never become the Adversary.

                                                  "I'll give you an A-Beat if Lucifer flatly ignores this and goes diving in his Oneiros to defeat his conscience," the ST says.

                                                  So Lucifer does what any sane and sensible person does and goes to fight his conscience in the Astral. An adventure is had in which he and any other players go traveling through his inner world and he is forced to reveal parts of himself to them that maybe he didn't want to show off.

                                                  (Don't make it too overt but, yeah, this is his own mind betraying him to his closest friends; how more intimate a betrayal can you get?)

                                                  Until at last, he finds his conscience, which pleads with him to not go any further.

                                                  "I'll offer you an A-Beat if you refuse," says the ST.

                                                  So Lucifer goes on to beat his conscience into submission before all and sundry; bending and breaking its will with spells and magic; grinding his Wisdom against a rough stone with every casting until the bright angel is broken, its pure body split with wounds, its blazing wings cut to bloody stumps, and its sublime face made to shed bitter tears as Lucifer binds it in chains and makes it submit before his indomitable will.

                                                  Lucifer's Path is now clear (yes, that's deliberate capitalization). He has been betrayed by his own soul and betrayed it in turn. Nothing within himself can conquer his highest drives; his self-determination; his self-realization; his self. His ego.

                                                  There, at the entrance of a tainted and corrupted garden, Lucifer finds a snake's skin and slips it over his body, and becomes, at last, the Adversary.

                                                  "Alright," says the ST, "you can buy the merit now."

                                                  That's how you make an Order-focused Obsession interesting. Not just "Okay, buy it off your sheet".

                                                      1. Guardians are all about security and secrecy. They do not share their Oneiros stuff with other cabalmates if they do not need it. It's both positive to both sides, as imagining Oneiros of long term Guardian, it would be just one big trauma to everyone.
