    Worlds Stretched Thin [A Lower Depths Essay]

    07-19-2020, 11:38 AM
    (I apologize in advance for any spelling mistakes you will see here. For some reason, the auto correct no longer works here for me, so my regular spelling issues become so much more severe).

    Every once and a while, someone mentions the Lower Depths, and every once and a while, things quickly spiral into the endless argument about what the Depths actually are. While I do like reading about the Depths and writing about the Depths, the thing is that between Mage being the gameline with the most consistent and explored cosmology (well, maybe beside Mummy, but that's actually a part of the reason why such discussion arise *pun intended, goes to the corner to be ashamed of myself*) and the tendency of the Awakened to feel so sure of themselves (and again, not without just), it is easy to mix up objectivity with subjectivity. I mean, the overall discussion about whether or not a realm is considered to be a Depth is by itself a derviative of the more general "Mage is right you are wrong" kind of argument that resurface every now and then, even though it is mostly done by the fans of other gamelines who feel that Mage tries to tell them them that it knows better about their gamelines their their own corebook (even though that game itself isn't too eager to claim so, but nevermind that).

    Anyway, the thing is that there is some misconception about the role of the Lower Depths in Mage- not a widespread one, I believe, but it is still there. The misconception is that the Depths is the way that the worlds and phenomena of other gamelines fit the overall cosmological model of Mage. The thing is, that it is the other way around- the Lower Depths are generally things which do not belong to Mage, while the Awakened try to squeeze them into their model by force. While this was outright stated a number of times, people still have the idea that the "Lower Depths" refer to some large category of many minor realms and planes of existence, and that they are simply the "shadow of a shadow" being cast by the light of the Supernal Truth. That the Depths are places which are barely real, and as such have no significant impact upon the overall tellurian, unlike the Supernal, or the Abyss, or even the Shadow or the Underworld. That the Depths are negligible- after all, there are so many of those, and once you have endless copies of the same object, its value falls. The Depths are not important- they are a threat, sure, but they are only the periphery of existence, not its center. They are at the bottom, while all that is important is at the top. The Supernal is everything, and as such, it is the only thing that is important.

    That's probably wrong, of course.

    I mean, it is subjectively true, but objectively wrong. When I wrote my overall Standard Model for the Chronicles of Darkness, I came to realize how subjective is Mage's view of the cosmos. Even before we start talking about the Lower Depths, the Empyrean, the Hedge and Arcadia and any other anomalys, the model is incerdibly tilted and shaped by the identity of who actually views it. The Astral is a perfect example for it- for we see how werewolves and changelings and beasts all interact with it in a way far stranger than how mages and humans do. I mean, the overall structure of Temenos is based on humanity, as it is its collective soul. The view of the Whorl and Anima Mundi requires the reference of Earth as the center of the universe. Heck, even other realms are touched by this "anthrocentric" and "geocentric" view- Luna and Helios are the greatest spirits in the world, but is it still true when you enter Hisil from the other side of the universe, where their light will never reach? Is humanity and the planet of which it exist are really that special, or maybe we are simply talking about one, single species, which lives on a small, pale dot, with a very limited view of the cosmos created by their own limited tools and understanding?

    The easiest answer will be "yes, humanity is special, and Earth is special. Only humans have souls, only Earth has life, and that's it". That may indeed be true, but who is to say that other animals may not have some "soul equivients" of some kind, ones with which human mages can not interact because they lack the tools to do so? Who is to say that certain animals may not evolve into having souls or equivilents? I mean, sure, you could find arguments against it- but for those of you who watched A Certain Magical Index, there was that arc about that Esper who just wanted to know whether or not animals could too become Espers, because those are questions that needs to be asked. And while we can justify again and again that only humans are special and only Earth is special, there is still one big hole in the overall theory- that the tools that the Awakened have in order to justify their claims are based around them being mages, and humans, and the fact they live on Earth. While technology have advanced dramatically since the time homo spaiens have just learned how to play with fire, the arsenal of tools that the Awakened have access to hasn't changed by much. Oh, sure, they have developed new spells and legacies and understood new ways to use magic, and the Watchtowers have grew so much since the times of the Neolithics, but the very base of it have not changed. As human civilization evolved, it learned how to utilize new tools and sources of energy in order to better interact with the world and discover things which are found just outside of theri reach. Mages, on the other hand, haven't changed by much- for they still have the same ten Arcana, and as such their understanding of the world is limited to those ten Arcana. Oh, sure, the Arcana should comprise everything which exist and could ever exist- but what about things which fall outside of the Arcana's reach? What about places which are found outside of the human soul? What about worlds which exist so far from Earth, that we can never even dream that they exist?

    Magic is limited only by understanding- and while they are enlightened, the Awakened are still human.

    While it may feel like I am trying to bash Mage or something, it is not my intention. The point of this essay is to criticize Mage's view of cosmology just in the same way that one should criticize any other theory, both scientifc and otherwise. Those arguments about how limited actually is the Awakened's point of view is something that should also take place in the setting, and probably does. Philosophers do it, theologists do it, scientists do it, and so it is highly likely that while most mages follow the same model, there should be enough groups which have their own views, especially among the Free Council and the Nameless Orders.

    Anyway, to sum this whole concept up, what we get that if something is fundmentially none human, and that whatever forces which describe it exist outside of the preview of the Arcana, mages suddenly become very limited in their understanding of that system. It would be like trying to use classical mechanics in order to describe a quantum system, or describing a certain part of the none visible electromagnetic spectrum in terms of the three basic colours. It would be like telling a person bound in a cave that all they see is just shadows on the wall. The problem is that unlike the Fallen, the Supernal is eternal, static and unchanging- unless someone were to rewrite the very nature of reality, it is outright impossible for Mages to see things which are outside of their preview- and that would require Ascension, and you don't me to tell you how goddamn hard it is to Ascend. Perhaps Ascension is not as much the mage rewriting reality as expanding it- maybe as a soul Ascends to the Supernal, it does not create a new Truth or replace it, as much as it pushes further the limitations of the Truth and explores new places which were engulfed by darkness. After all, the Scelesti claim that the Abyss have always existed, and as such you do not create truth as much as "take" it from the Abyss. Each Ascension shrinks the Abyss by making something that once did not existed into existence- like creating a Watchtower for cats. Oh, sure, it sometimes also causes a lot of ruin, but hey, you can't just reduce entropy. Ask the Arisen, they know it.

    So why am I talking about all of this nonesense, and how it relate to the subject of this essay- the Lower Depths?

    Here is a thing- the mage cosmology actually has three main parts: the Supernal, the Fallen and the Abyss. The Supernal is the Truth, the source of all symbols and all things. The Fallen is the world the Awakened live in, and are trapped in. The Abyss is the boundary which stops one to cross to the other. Ironically, the Supernal is the most understood between the three- but it is probably because we don't actually know much about it. I mean, it is there, it contains all of the symbols of all existence, and it has the Watchtowers in it. That's preety much it. Oh, sure it also has the Exarchs up there, and Ascended beings and Old Gods and Realms and whatever, but it is basically exist to exist. An unmoved mover of the plot, so to speak. On the other hand, the Fallen is basically the collection of worlds which are governed by the Arcana- the five Gross Arcana make the material. Mind for the Astral. Spirit for the Shadow. Death for the Underworld. Prime and Fate are a bit tricky, as their closest analogues are Faerie and the God Machine or the Divine Fire, and we know that mages do not know how those things really work- but that's perhaps means that such realms simply do not exist, or maybe that there is something which stops the Awakened from reaching them. Or maybe a stranger possibility- after all the Astral is not Mind as much as it is a Soul- and as the material gathers the overall five Gross Arcana, the Astral is made of the Five Subtle Arcana. The Shadow and the Underworld? The fact that mages can interact with them is "just" a happy councidence. They are a part of the Fallen out of flux, and that may explain some similarity they have to subject of this essay- the Depths.

    So we have the Truth on one side and the Lie on the other, but what about the middle? Here is that first hole in the model. The Abyss- and it is a huge hole. Ask the Pentacle, and they will tell you that the Abyss was created by the Exarchs, and that it serves as a boundary between the Supernal and the Fallen, to stop mages from Ascending. Ask the Seers, and they will tell you TO SHUT THE HELL UP- because apperently, the Exarchs are terrifyed of the Abyss. Heck, the Exarchs need humanity as a buffer against the Abyss. Oh, they need it too, sure- but it is still terrifying. Heck, they have an Exarch to govern it, and their attitude towards them is WE ARE NOT TALKING ABOUT THE GATE. Then you go ask the Scelesti about the Abyss, and you discover that the Abyss has always was, and we merely live in a small bubble within a sea of darkness which hates us for existing, because it forces what does not exist into becoming aware that it does not exist. The Exarchs has the Gate to govern the Abyss as if it was an Arcana, because it is- it is the "Arcana" of everything which does not fall within the ten Arcana. It is the "Arcana" of "Nothing", and by serving as nothing, it is everything which the other ten are not.

    Again, we are not talking about the Abyss, but we do need to understand that saying that the "Abyss" is a part of Mage's model is like saying that the Wavefunction Collapse is a part of quantum mechanics- it is required for it to work, but we don't understand it, and lack the means to actually describe it without stating that it exists (hence why other interpertations have been formulized and why I prefer Everett's interpertation). Mages need the Abyss in order to explain their model, but they can't actually interact with it- as they lack the Arcana to do so. That is, they can't without becoming Scelesti, and that is because the Abyss is, in the end, still tied to the Supernal- even if by negation. So in order to describe the Abyss, you need to define it by what it is not, using antimonian logic and corrupt truth into lies, and turn lies into truth. Again, the Abyss is tied to the Supernal, but saying that an object, phenomenon or entity is "abyssal" simply means that it exists outside of the world, and does not belong to it.

    And what about the Lower Depths?

    So here is the thing- the Abyss is the "garbage term" of the Mage cosmology. In Density Functional Theory (which is what I work with, and I am sure is meaningless to the most of you), it is the "exchange correlation potential"- a term into which all the messy concepts are thrown in, and could only be approximated and almost never completly understood. The Abyss is everything which does not exist- but what about other worlds? Other realms which do exist, but still do not belong to any of those regimes? Those realms would be described in terms of how "close they are to the Truth" by "how well mages can interact with them". The Shadow and the Underworld? We can use Spirit and Death to interact with them with little issues, turning them into "Realms Invisible" and a part of the Fallen. But what about realms with which the Arcana can't interact well, but are still somewhat connected to the overall structure? I mean, you can look on Arcadia and say "oh, I see a lot of Fate and Time going crazy up there!" or examine a qashmal and say "Ah, that's some weird thing with some aspects of Prime". Those things are not strangers to the world- but they are also not easily described by the Arcana, as their powers clash in weird ways against Supernal Magic. So you notice that some of those entities are very "true" but not "True", by the fact that they corrupt the Lie around them or seem to be "pure" from it- and so you place them as some form of Emanation of the Supernal- or maybe even above it, calling the place as the Empyrean before you get persecuted for heresy. Or maybe you just invent some "Outer Channel" meant to explain those realms that are weird and you can't interact with well. And then you notice that most of the realms that you can't interact with are not very friendly, very hungry and usually make things worse for everyone who touch them. So you invent a term for them. The Lower Depths.

    The term "the Lower Depths" is much like the real life terms "dark matter/ energy". "Dark" in that context means one thing- that we simply can't interact with those subjects. We claim that they are real, because without them our models for cosmology is wrong, and our model works so well that the option that it is not correct is simply unthinkable for many scientists. Like, seriously, if you want to make a physicist to go angry at you, throw "dark matter" on them. I did, and it was hillarious. Anyway- the Lower Depths are the same concept as those terms. They are "Lower" and "Deep" because the "Truth" must be high, at the top of all existence. The closer you are to the Truth, the more real you are, and the easier it is to interact with those worlds. The further you are, the less real you become, and as such you must be more false. The Lower Depths interact with the Supernal less than the Fallen, and as such they must be bellow it. Does using only one dimension (that is, how close or far you are from the Supernal) as a tool to describe the overall cosmology is very limited? Yes, but that's the only dimension that mages have, because it is the only one they have access for. Heck, the Abyss should not be between the Fallen and the Supernal- it should encompass them both, but again, we are projecting a N dimensional system to only one dimension. Of course that information will be lost in the process.

    The Lower Depths do not exist to undermine the importance of other worlds- they exist to remind everyone that the Awakened do not know everything. Inferno, Duat, Dis and other worlds are important- their presence is felt strongly all across the Fallen. Oh, sure, we can say that they are "closer" to the Fallen than other worlds, but again, that is mostly because we project a very complex system to a single dimension- it is true, but is not the overall truth. You ask why the Depths hate the Abyss and love our world? It is because they exist, and the Abyss does not, and everything which exists is an enemy to the Abyss. Are those realms also antagonistic towards the world? Well yeah, but that's why they are grouped together as "Depths" (even though they would deny that claim- they love us. Well, at least Inferno does. I am preety sure that Duat hates our guts and just like how we taste, but it is still better than the Abyss.. I think). Oh, sure, there are worlds among the Depths which are truly not important- but that's also true to some of the other realms on other levels of reality. Do you really think that every corner of the Temenos is important? Does every Abyssal bubble or Supernal Emanation is important? They are not, but some are.

    In the end, the term "Lower Depths" is more of a political term than an scientific one- it refers to all of those realms which can not be interacted easily with the Arcana which are also seen as harmful for existence. They are "bad worlds", ones you should be warry of and not deal with, and so they are given some mystical dark name to serve as a warning for all of those Wise mages. If you can interact with them, or develop the tools to interact with them, they become "Invisible"- still scary, but not a taboo. If you can't interact with them easily, but they are not "evil", than you can shove them to some other corner of the cosmos. If they are very, very preety, like Supernal level preety, and still not Supernal, you start wondering if the Empyrean does indeed exist. The truth is, that all of those realms are one and the same- they are everything which exists, yet can't be easily described by the Supernal. They are real, yet strange, and usually dangerous. And yes, they usually lack something, and they indeed usually devour something, and some of those indeed depend on the material to give them meaning just as the Fallen needs to the Supernal to give them meaning. But in order to actually define which is which, and seperate those realms which are indeed "just" murky corners of the cosmos from those who are actually important, or those realms which are indeed require the Fallen to define them from those who are "just" very unfriendly and accidently mages lack the ability to interact with them, and from those realms who are just weird, the Awakened must do research, develop new tools and challenge their own model- and isn't solving such Mysteries the whole point of Mage?

    Who knows- maybe one day cats will Awaken, and then we would at least have one less question to answer before they would take over the world :P

      I think one of the most interesting lore tidbits is that Atlanteans were originally guided to the island by dragons. But we have no record of dragon archmasters or Ascensions, yet we still have dragon bones. They echo almost like sariras and work in the ruins of the city that never was, almost like being preserved by a Verge from the Lie. Yet certain cultures say dragons evolved from snakes, an unproven yet lingering tidbit of supernatural lore, like the Atlantean paradigms. So perhaps snakes evolved into dragons and Ascended, then they had to lay the groundwork for the next cycle, where primates would evolve into humans and Ascend. In this way, it is possible that the paradigms which define the Supernal are always being built upon, renewed or perhaps even redefined. Maybe the Watchtowers and the number of Arcana haven't changed that much because they aren't fully explored, the "theory of the Supernal as seen by humanity" is still work in progress. Perhaps cats will be the next to evolve, guided by some incredible mythical creature that we would recognize as the next evolutionary step a post-human archmage could achieve.

      As archmages know, as above so below. This is very convenient because it lets you use Fallen symbols to alter the Supernal in incredibly complex ways. So if the symbols that exist outside the Abyss of possibilities are only partially defined, then are the Lower Depths simply less true or are they a reflection in the Fallen below of this cycle's incomplete paradigm above ?
