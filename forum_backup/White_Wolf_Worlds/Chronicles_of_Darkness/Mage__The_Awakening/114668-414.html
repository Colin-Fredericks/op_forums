                                            I think the trouble is that you've misunderstood the rate of Mana generation, so here's the breakdown:
                                            A Hallow produces one Mana per dot per day, meaning your 3 dot Hallow produces 3 Mana per day. Since the tass cap is three times the dot rating, your Hallow is capped at 9. It takes 3 days, not 9 or 12, to fill up the cap.
                                            Note that the Merit states that the Hallow stops producing Mana once the tass cap is reached, meaning that once the non-tass Mana congeals into tass to the point where it's fully saturated, it does not produce any additional non-tass Mana, thus limiting it to storing a maximum of 9 Mana and only in the form of tass.

                                                            There are many differences between AoE and subjects, and just as many reasons to use one over the other.
                                                            1.) You can affect subjects you can't see (either by being in cover or by being invisible) by covering a building or a room.
                                                            2.) You can affect multiple subjects without having to resort to sensory range (due to the impracticality of having to touch multiple targets the same turn).
                                                            3.) You tie the effect to a location, meaning it stops for subjects who leave the area where you want the spell to be active.
                                                            4.) You can affect subjects in the future without even having to see them as long as they enter the area.
                                                            5.) You don't need to know that all subjects exists within the area, but also risk affecting subjects you didn't anticipate.
                                                            Et cetera.

                                                            Having all targets within an area actually be subjects reduces the many differences by a marginal amount. Especially considering that, according to you, all spells that doesn't deal with charges in one way or another, would work identically.

                                                            You're introducing an extra step between subject and effect, an exemption to the magic rules that is never mentioned in the book, nor accounted for in any spell descriptions that I know of. It also leads to weird situations where you're applying certain effects separately but other effects shared. And how do you factor Withstand into account? Grave Misfortune, for example, has a number of charges equal to Potency, but since it's Withstood everyone within the AoE could have different amounts of charges.
