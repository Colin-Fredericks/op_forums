                Mortality being part of the Lie and immortality being morally corrosive are not actually mutually exclusive. It wouldn't be possible to achieve immortality any of the ways presented if one weren't Awakened and capable of seeing that mortality is not necessary. There's a difference between knowing you 'can' do something and knowing whether you 'should' do something. Mortality is definitely part of the Lie, but at what cost come conquering it? For as many references as exist to achieving immortality through enlightenment there are as many cautionary tales. The quest for immortality in history and fiction is possibly one of the most prominent examples of hubris we have as a species, and hubris is one of Mage's core themes.
                Mage is about having incredible power with a big fat asterisk on the end, a "but..." If you remove that qualifier then what is the point?

                            The focus of mage is on the dangers of hubris. Yeah, you wield godly powers that most humans can't even conceive of, but don't let it go to your head.

                            Kind of like the Tevinter Imperium, they thought themselves equal to the Gods they served, and when they fell, they hit the ground like an extinction level meteor.

                            As for mortality being part of the lie, I can understand that point. I mean you have an entire watchtower dedicated to manipulating death itself. Even if the Necromancers do accept death as a part of life, they're still reanimating corpses and consorting with ghosts who shouldn't be up and walking around anymore.


                            Originally posted by HardcoreHannes View Post
                            Even completely appart from Mage as a game: from an ethical standpoint I would generally argue that everybody, who fanatically chases to prolong his lifetime instead of using the time he has to achieve something has issues.

                            Nothing you achieve in life matters in the long run. Any contributions you bring to society will become obsolete in a few decades. And just when you've reached your prime, your body starts to degrade and your accomplishments along with it. You'll die, you'll be forgotten, and the world will move on without you. Life is ultimately pointless for a mortal being.

                            But if you're immortal...? You don't have to become obsolete, you don't have to become forgotten, and you can remain in your prime until you run out of ideas, rather than becoming too degraded to continue working.

                                            I would just add that I agree that fear of death is bad, simply because it will cripple you and prevent you from actually living your life. Fearing death and not wanting to die are two separate things, though. However, Death arcanum is about change in general being a necessary part of existence, which doesn't really mean anything in itself.

                                            The key thing here is to understand, that you can and have to choose which change is good and which is bad with every choice you make in your life, all the while recognizing what choices are even allowed to be made by the rules the universe is build upon, and occasionally you may even have the ability to change the rules, if you possess the knowledge of how to.

                                            Why am I saying this? Because cessation of continuation of a specific sequence of perceptions that define a single human mind, happening by the virtue of degradation of brain cells and organism as a whole, with similar processes not even happening in some existing animals, is a very specific kind of change. One of many changes happening in the world. Just like some things simply *don't* happen, like heat suddenly changing into a tree. And with aging, like with everything you can or could affect, you and only you can decide, if that change is "good" or "bad" for you, and if it's bad, than you can attempt to 'change the rules' for it to not happen. There is nothing that makes human death as a condition inherently a taboo to change (notice the irony here, even certain aspects of death can die), in comparison to all the other possible changes, that you could make in the world.

                                            True immortality is most likely achievable through Ascension, after all, anything immortal basically is a concept around which the universe is built anyway, might as well be Supernal. In comparison, defeating human aging is perfectly concievable without Ascension, if done properly even without breaking any metaphysical barrier. The death of you has no more or less symbolic significance in the Death arcanum than a death of a tortoise. And a tortois can live for a very long time. A mayfly for a short while. The difference between either of them and a human in lifespan is biological. You change your biology, possibly with a little help from Life magic, you prolong your life, and metaphysics won't be able to hate you for that, like it can't hate you for any other non-soul mauling change you've caused by your life.

                                                        Umm.... *scratches head* First, I've been using "fight," not "war" for this conversation. And fight can imply non-violent, non-physical struggles as well as military-style fights.

                                                        Secondly, I feel compelled to point out that one of the basic underlying principles in Mage is that the Pentacle and Seers are engaged in a cold war. Cold wars come with implications of espionage and propaganda instead of bloody battles, and that comes with different connotations. This is Mage, a game of mental challenges, not Werewolf, the game of hunting prey.

                                                        Third? I think that you can't assume that war is carrying the same baggage for everyone, nor critize someone for using the term correctly - even Marriam-Webster says that "war" can mean "a situation in which people or groups compete or fight against each other." Even the most literal interpretation of the use of war here is correct. And we don't live in a literal world - we live in one with figurative meanings and different perspectives.

                                                        So, when you say that "fighting an oppressive false reality is not a focus in your typical Mage game," I have to disagree. There are fights of all sorts, and there is a focus on opposing the Exarchs and Seers, as those are the major antagonists of the whole game. I think its rather typical in most Mage games to have fights against the Lie.
