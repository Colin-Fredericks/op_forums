    Which 1e Books are still Useful with 2e?

    08-18-2016, 02:33 AM
    I haven't delved too deeply into 2e yet, but from reading some of the other gamelines, I know some middlingly large changes have been made. Are there any 1e books I should either wait for a 2e replacement for or skip outright? Are there any that would definitely be useful?

    I'm considering starting a Chronicle that might involve exploring Atlantian Ruins or Abyssal Entities. Is Secrets of the Ruined Temple still good? Should I buy Intruders or just try my best with the Mage 2e book and the Chronicles of Darkness book, since they have info on creating monsters and spirits?

      Secrets of the Ruined Temple is a lot of fun but goes with a stricter view of Atlantis that things have diverged from a lot by default in 2e. Mage Noir is still a fantastic book and is very applicable stuff. I'd steer clear of the Order books, as a lot of their fluff is outdated now. Summoners and Intruders are both awesome.

          I think that SotRT is still viable in 2E, only you need to remember that there are now many 'Atlantisis' - I would rather argue that SotRT is the point of making change into Times Before in 2E in first place, with showing many Awakened Civilizations of previous eras. You will also need to tweak a bit Temple Guardians to decide what Ephemeral Being type they would be in 2E or even maybe if they should be Horrors ( from CoD rulebook ) - because 1E was all about 'if it is weird being it MUST be spirit!', but it will be less problem than it looks.

          Intruders are very funny book - it mechanics will probably need too be rewritten as quasi-Ephemeral beings now Abyssals are. Still, Intruders ideas are mostly good or very good and to this day I used about 5 of them in my games - planning to use more of them. But if you do not like others ideas and like to makes your own Abyssals, you can go without it.

                  Imperial mysteries is still incredibly useful, though imperial spell factors and a lot of speedbumps (like nuclear energy manipulation being a purview of forces archmastery) need to be changed or ignored if you want to use it.

                  The chronicler's guide is another one which is good (plus it has a bloody gorgeous front cover) because the rule hacks found within can be translated over to 2e without much issue (especially the awakened science and super psychic stuff).

                  Night horrors is, mechanics aside, good inspiration for fluff at least.
