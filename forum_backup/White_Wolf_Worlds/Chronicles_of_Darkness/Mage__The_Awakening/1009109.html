    How to encourage / set situations that get Mages to reach?

    11-27-2016, 09:35 PM
    After a few games I have noticed that my group doesn't feel the need / willing to Reach beyond their grasp or risk Paradox in virtually any way?

    It feels like, without Paradox, we are missing out on a lot of interesting and colorful aspects of magic.

    So what are some ideas that would encourage the players to risk paradox?

      Generally speaking, you won't have to try for starting Mages. Any situation that is dangerous enough is one that will make them Reach.

      If you want to create a situation where a character Reaches beyond their natural range, though, look at their arcana, find a Practice used by the top dot they have in it, and make it crucial to use in a way that is big and impressive somehow.

          It seems like what you really want is a greater risk of Paradox. Reaching is just one way to accomplish that. It's pretty easy to set up a situation where they're going to need more than their safe limit in spells in order to accomplish a task, especially at low levels of Gnosis. At higher levels of Gnosis going over your safe Reach even a little bit induces a much higher risk of Paradox. If they risk Paradox in any way it's possible to keep them from buying the Paradox dice pool down too much by placing a time crunch on their spellcasting which limits how quickly they can spend mana to mitigate.

          If your players are using creative thaumaturgy a lot (And by that I mean making up spells that don't exist in the book) it's best to construct the spells so they accomplish something very basic and refinements in application and power cost Reach. It's also possible to add Reach clauses to existing spells for instance one of my players used Gravity Supremacy and I let him apply the Immobilized Tilt by spending a Reach which put him over the safe Reach limit.

          You can also put them in situations where Sleeper witnesses will see them doing obvious magic, or places where Abyssal interaction is more likely.

          In the end though, if your players aren't risking Paradox then don't sweat it. The game isn't about inducing Paradox, and frankly I'm not sure that's where you should be getting your jollies. Games are for running awesome stories, a Paradox can be a nice twist, but it's not the point of the game.

                                              Mmm. That's two successes result.
                                              To the op:
                                              One of was to encourage people to Reach is making Releasing Paradox a tempting deal; it penalizes your spellcasting, but Paradox Condition is applied to the subject, not you. If you want to make Reaching more exciting, try to make released Paradoxes less...antagonistic. If people will perceive it as "we are handling our GM a rope to hang us with", they won't reach too much, and when they will, they will try to contain at all costs. Try to make released Paradox terrible and terryfing...but not always antagonistic. In the Mind Demons example, if a player would release Paradox thinking "they don't deserve being saved from this dox", bestow a negative Condition on the poor demon.

                                              Paradox *can* help players. By making it as much as potential ally as a threat, you make paradoxing tempting and cool. Making all side-effects negative will make people just avoid it as a pain in the ass and direct danger to themselves (this includes all of the "dickish" options - ricochet, un-making Reaches).
                                              Again, one of more interesting aspects of Wisdom is asking yourself the question what side effects of releasing are acceptable and which ones aren't.

                                                      That's cool! As a rule, I try to make released Paradox relatively safe or even beneficial for the caster (I assume that if they managed to cast the spell through the dice penalty bestowed by Paradox, there is no need to kick them more); side effects like this are the best, because they don't necessarily undo the spell, and might be a net gain overall, but...they also challenge your Wisdom. And of course, many player characters will try to help these poor Sleepers. For the first, second, and tenth time...question is, what about 30rd and 80th time it happens? It's very easy to just be all like "meh, can't bother".

                                                      Of course, this isn't the only way to handle Paradox. But it certainly can help to encourage certain groups, especially those that avoid anything that harm their characters directly.
