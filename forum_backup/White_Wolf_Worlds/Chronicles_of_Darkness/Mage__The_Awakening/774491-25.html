                                        Originally posted by LokiRavenSpeak View Post

                                        However he does bring a good point, the game seems focused on a cold war setting wise but for the cold war to have any effect there has to be the danger of a hot war. There is a reason the "doomsday clock" is such a powerful image. Its not a con of the game the inability to run other genres (say high fantasy) but it is a con in the system not be able to run scenarios inside the genre it belongs.
                                        Exactly who is it that determines what games it's supposed to be able to run, here? You guys are literally assigning the game a genre and then being confused when it doesn't fit perfectly.

                                        And that's without addressing whether or not you're correct on any of those points. My issue here is that you're selecting a critical method that makes it impossible to fairly evaluate the game.

                                        To address those actual points? The game is not a cold war setting. This is not Demon: the Descent. (Which is hilarious because imagining what a "hot war" looks like in D:tD is literally the wrath of god smiting shit left and right possibly including timeline erasure as a quarantine measure.) This is Mage: the Awakening.

                                        It is a game about detectives qua academics. It is not a game about international politics and the consequences of secrecy and escalation. It is not a fighty game. It is not trying to be. It is a game about curiosity and its consequences, of what happens when a hard-boiled PI can't seem to stop solving mysteries even though it makes him depressed and hurts everyone he loves. It's about what happens when a professor thrashes his research assistants into staying the night and refusing to let them have lives because his research is the most important thing in the world. Sure, these people live in a society that's filled with such people and, if any of them so chose to figure out how, they could pull out a gun that let them murder perfectly from across the city without leaving a trace or even a suspicion. But none of those people want to do that: they have their pet projects that they actually care about and all the other thick-skulls are either helping or in the way. The petty bickering of sects is just a bunch of blowhards who are trying to do terrible things like "keep all the Mages from killing each other" and "maybe let's help each other out with our research projects sometimes, eh?" and "hey are you down here's something you can believe in" and all that other shit that just flies over our heads on the international stage anyways. Like, isn't there a famine in Somalia right now or something?

                                        Yes, I get it. You want mage fights where one side lobs fireballs and the other side does some chanting and dancing and the fireballs are canceled out by fucking soap water. Cool beans. Lots of people want that. Awakening 2e is not good at it. You can judge it for that and whine about how it's so hard to drive screws using a sledgehammer, or you can, y'know, move on with your life and actually play something that does what you want. Or you can ask for help in hacking the setting and the systems to make it do what you want. I mean, you can totally make a screwdriver out of a sledgehammer.

                                        But Mage: the Awakening, like most Chronicles of Darkness games, is about personal lives and personal situations. It's not about The Star And The Throne, Magical War To End All Wars. It's about you and your cabal and the shit they just wander into 60% by accident and 40% "Oh, we know this is a bad idea, but I can't help it I gotta do this" because shit happens everywhere and you keep finding out about it and it never stops. Really, the point of the whole political deadlock in the upper echelons is to make the consequences of your actions that much more terrible when you realize how many fingers were in the pie you just torched. It's not "you dun fucked up, I'm going to take away your badge and your gun". It's "you dun fucked up, now there are god-tyrants who want your head on a plate and the Hierarch is seriously thinking about just letting that happen because you ain't too popular in the Consilium either after that last fuck up so maybe your only useful purpose in life, to them, is being bait".

                                        This isn't a game where you play the Hierarch and try to figure out how to line up your toy soldiers to foil the Seers at every turn. This is a game about people who are high on themselves and, at their most self-aware, say, "Sorry, man, I can't help it. That's just who I am." And the Seers and the Scelesti and the Liches swoop in and go, "That's cool, man. Who you are is great. Hurting people is the best because we have a bullshit philosophy that makes it okay. And you can do it some more if you swear your life your loyalty your soul, yeah?"

                                        If you want a play a different game using this setting and these rules, that's cool and hell, you can probably make it work with effort and dedication on the part of you and your fellow players. But you don't get to say it's badly designed if it doesn't because it ain't fair to Frank Lloyd Wright that his house isn't spaceworthy.
