            Originally posted by WalkingEye View Post
            Just a general system question. If a weapons damage rating is lower than an objects durability do they just bounce off or can a vampire destroy a tank with a baseball bat?
            Just calculate damage as normal (successes + damage modifier - Durability). If the vampire score enough successes to deal damage to a tank, then they do so. By RAW improvised weapons (which I would consider anything not meant to strike a tank) also takes damage if the vampire were to use Vigor. A more realistic take is to deal that damage to the weapon regardless of whether Vigor was used or not.

                I have a hopefully simple question regarding Practices. Let's say I wanted to take a clump of dirt and turn it into liquid gold. Obviously this falls under the scope of two separate Practices. Patterning to turn the dirt to gold, Weaving to give it the properties of a liquid.

                My question is if you need two Practices to technically create the desired effect, would selecting the higher Practice of Patterning grant you the effects under Weaving simply by being a higher practice?

                I mean, technically Ruling is used to move something under the Arcanum's purview, yet higher Practice damage spells don't seem to really care that the description of the Practice they're using doesn't include descriptive text stating that it does anything other than what it's supposed to. Big offenders are Windstrike(Weaving) and Piercing Earth(Patterning), both of which are essentially the mage basically hurling Matter at the target in various forms but seem to have nothing to do with either Practice they fall under.

                It's almost as if it was intended that lower Practices be encompassed by higher ones. But then, if that's the case is it only the Practices that most align with each other that do this? Ruling, Weaving, and Patterning? Ruling, Fraying, and Unraveling? Or do all lower Practices fall under any one higher Practice? For instance, casting a Patterning spell with the properties described under Fraying included?

                Honestly, it feels like a lot of mechanics are simply implied and left open for interpretation. Especially Practices, which has all of a single paragraph plus a sentence or two per practice that outline how it all works.

                    The Matter damage spells are bad examples and you should not look to their descriptions when designing your own spells.

                    For your example of turning solid dirt into liquid gold, you need to cast two spells. Or a combined spell. This is not because you're using two Practices (if you could cast it as a single spell both Practices would theoretically be included as if you cast a Matter 3+Matter 4 spell, simplified to be just Matter 4) but because you're facilitating two separate effects.

                                                Another, hopefully easier to answer question

                                                I was looking at some improvised spells I could do now that I’m at Mind 3, and me and my group have run into a disagreement on what practice something counts as

                                                I was looking at clear mind as a reference example, and noticed it had the effect of restoring willpower, and I was discussing using it as a way to recharge the willpower batteries. Another player brought up I should be able to cast a spell to just restore willpower itself at Mind 3

                                                I looked into it though, looking at other examples, already knowing that inflicting bashing is usually a disciple effect while you have to wait till 4 to inflict lethal. Then there are spells like knit, which restore bashing and with a reach effect can restore lethal, then at life 4, mend life simply restores the lethal itself

                                                This suggests to me, when taking into account clear mind only restores 1 willpower with a reach effect, that it should actually be a patterning Mind 4 spell to simply restore willpower

                                                However the other player argues that restoring willpower shouldn’t be as hard as healing lethal damage, so it should be able to be cast at Mind 3 as a perfecting effect
