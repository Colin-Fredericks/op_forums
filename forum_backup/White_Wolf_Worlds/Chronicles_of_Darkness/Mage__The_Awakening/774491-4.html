                        Talking about Merits - I used Arcane Experience before 2nd Edition became a thing, and limited my players purchases to "magical stuff only with AExp, Gnosis rises up automatically at critical points of the story, normal Experience can be used to purchase merits skills and attributes as usual". I really like that Arcane Experience is given more spotlight in this edition, and while I don't think it's going to be as restrictive as my personal rules on Exp, I still like the direction they go for.
                        As for merits vs gnosis - due to how it seems that Merits apparently can provide you with reliable and powerful yantras (even +3 ones), it isn't actually inferior to Gnosis now, as long as you cover your bases. Gnosis 3 will give you better ritual casting times (which, due to ability to instant cast anything as long as your Reach, isn't that game changing anymore; your Gnosis 1 mage in 1ED had to waste whole day casting his max Arcana spells, now he doesn't have to wait for Arcana 4 to reliably cast his Arc 3 spells!), 2 extra mana per turn, one extra yantra and +2 to general casting (plus other benefits that remain to be seen). Ritual Casting also doesn't seem to be that much of a deal anymore, because I *think* it's now flat Ritual Casting Time, not Ritual Casting Time * Number of Rolls, which is imho a godsend.
                        So, your Gnosis 3 starting mage will have +2 dice and +1 yantra edge on your Merits one. However, if you grab some good yantra (let's say you managed to get one of the +3 yantras) with your Merit points, actual casting difference will vanish. Why?
                        Gnosis 3 person has natural +2 edge; as long as you can get a yantra that is at least equal to +2, you are fine. In instant casting, you usually want to frontload your spell with big, reflexive yantra in order to not waste your rounds adding extra yantras; which also, accidentaly, makes that extra 3rd yantra less relevant. Getting yantra stronger than +1 seems kinda hard - +2 yantras usually demand some kind of sacrifice, be it resources or opportunity, and "free to be taken" +3 yantras seem to be rare and "quest objective" kind of thing. In non combat situation, your Merit Wizard can utilize her 2 yantras with "My Personal +3 Yantra" and High Speech for nice, +5 bonus. Gnosis 3 Wizard can probably manage +2, +1, +1, or +2 +2 +1. Which gives him a slight edge, but not that much actually.
                        Of course, a lot of this depends on exact rules on pricing yantras. Still, abililty to get that buff to your casting with merits means that you don't *need* that extra gnosis desperately, as it's effect on gameplay seem to be less warping.

BOOM! Done. Just like that.), touch range, and lasts just per turn.

                                    Originally posted by Falcon777 View Post
                                    I like how the orders are getting more of the spotlight than the consilium. 1es consilium had too much politics involved, whereas 2e is more about magic itself. I also really like that Space is getting fixed.

                                    One thing I greatly dislike is the dumbing down of metamagic. In 1e, you could count on a spell to likely be scene long, cast instantly ( not reflexively), and that it could reach your target presuming you could see/hear/smell him. On top of that, so long as you had one more dot in the arcanum than the spell, by at least one interpretation you could increase all the spell's aspects (but not each aspect to its maximum). That was a HUGE selling point of Mage for me. Now you get neither since there's only one natural reach per dot (for lower dots) and all spells are naturally "ritual cast" (absolute worst part of 2e since the point of ritual casting was that it was for your most powerful spells; normal magic is for here and now. BOOM! Done. Just like that.), touch range, and lasts just per turn.
                                    I don't know where you're getting dumbing down from; it's been massively expanded and clarified. 1e's creative thaumaturgy was an absolute mess, with spells seeming to almost arbitrarily use different guidelines on what could use what when; some spells staggered their duration (Scrying, for example) and others didn't; some automatically started with Advanced Duration; some started ranged automatically and others at touched. It was a tangled mess.

                                    What 2e does is streamline it; all spells start at the same baseline, with the same basic conditions, and you can raise it from there with Reach. In most instances, a Mage is actually going to be better off than they were in 1e. For example, a Thyrsus with Life 3 casting a spell to improve their Strength in 1e faced automatic Paradox for doing so, and a random result. In 2e, that same Mage can cast the spell for a concentration duration (the one turn thing is one turn after you stop concentrating, if you use concentration duration, unless I missed my mark) to allow them to do some basic heavy lifting and risks no Paradox. If they wish to make it scene length, they can risk Paradox for it. In either case, they know exactly what they're getting for the risk, whereas the 1e Thyrsus did not. Likewise, if they wished to, the 2e Thyrsus can cast it at range immediately from Life 3, provided they're willing to allocate the Reach.

                                    At Life 4, the 2e Thyrsus can now cast a Strength-enhancing spell on themselves for a scene-length duration for no Paradox risk. The 1e Thyrsus can now cast it at range, but still risks Paradox no matter what.

                                    I would say, based on this, that the 2e Thyrsus has the much better time of it, since the new system allows for a far greater diversity in focusing of casting purpose, a deliberate management of risk instead of making it random, and an allowance for players to be clever instead of every spell being just the same.

                                    Ritual casting in 2e allows you a bit more Reach and has some Paradox-blunting aspect that Dave hasn't actually elaborated on - we'll find out when the book comes out, I imagine - meaning that it's still more powerful than regular casting. When you're casting your biggest, most powerful spells, you'll still want to be casting ritually.

                                    Remember, paradox-free Reach is not the same thing as Reach itself; you can ostensibly take as much of the latter as you want for the spell. It's just that any over your limit risks Paradox. This means that Mages in 2e actually are less likely to inflict Paradox but are also far more versatile in their ability to cast; being able to choose what form the spell takes with far more acuity and, hence, understand and mange (and be responsible for) the risks.

                                                            So far the only thing I can think of that I dislike in 2e is the fact that the new Reach system only determines whether or not a spell risks Paradox if there aren't Sleeper witnesses. When Sleepers are around, you risk Paradox for casting something "obviously magical", which sounds like there's going to be a lot of arguing with the ST in 2e over whether or not your spell effect was coincidental. Hopefully there are guidelines in the book somewhere about what the game considers "obviously magical"?

                                                            I also have serious trepidations about how difficult and punishing it apparently is to raise Wisdom in 2e. I don't like the idea of PCs having to hamstring their own development in order to redeem themselves Wisdom-wise, and ending up worse off than people who never dropped Wisdom in the first place, and people who dropped Wisdom and just didn't care. It's hard enough to justify spending XP on buying back a resource you can lose through gameplay as it is.

                                                            Originally posted by Sp4rt01 View Post

                                                            Dave has stated (I don't remember where at this point, it might have even been this forum) that trading in starting merit dots for Gnosis in 2e is going to be so crippling to your character that he seriously considered not including the option to do so at all.

                                                            I guess Mage 2e is going to have a lot of good Mage merits, which is something I look forward to. I like encouraging players to spend their XP on Merits and the like, while reserving Arcana and Gnosis increases for Arcane XP.
