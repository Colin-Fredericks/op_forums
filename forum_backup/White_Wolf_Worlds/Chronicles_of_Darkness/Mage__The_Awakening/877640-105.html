    Originally posted by Tessie View Post

    When altering conductivity you apply one symbol that alters a material property of the subject. Now when examined through the perspective of Fallen science it will be what you say, a change in the force binding electrons to a nucleus (I think it might be more complicated than that, though, but whatever), but that doesn't mean Matter can change that force however it wants.
    I did not mention forces, I mentioned electron energy states. While there are fundamental forces involved in the electron energy states I intentionally did not mention them, because the energy states are intrinsic to the concept of the "matter" being discussed, regardless of the forces involved.

    And yes, it is more "more complicated than that," if you'd like to understand more and discuss it further, I recommend Introduction to Electrodynamics by David Griffiths; it has an easy to follow topic progression and Griffiths' wit is sharp and dry, making the read enjoyable, I would recommend brushing up on your line integrals and ordinary differential equations and have at least some familiarity with partial differential equations.


    Conductivity is symbolically a material property (despite fundamentally being dependent on a force) and Matter can change material properties. Matter can't change these forces however you want, only as a result of changing something that symbolically falls under Matter. If you want to change these forces directly, you go to Forces.
    Given the context in which I was replying, you seem to be implying a rejection of the idea that Matter can have control over chemical bonds and that only Forces can manipulate such bonds. Are you, in fact, asserting that Matter, the arcanum of alchemy and chemistry, cannot manipulate chemical bonds, breaking complex compounds down into their constituent components or forcing chemical bonds that otherwise would not occur in nature?

    That is the nature Supernal magic. Don't fall into the trap of thinking it needs to adhere to Fallen science in any way whatsoever. It's the result (and how it symbolically resonates with the Arcana and Practices) that matter. Anything else is just fudged by the magic to make it work regardless of how it breaks the laws of our reality, and it can be as inconsistent as is necessary for the results.
    I did not "fall into the trap," thanks.

                                I said that actually. It was flippant and wasn't supposed to be taken literally, but it was taken literally.

                                To return to the tracks of discourse we seem to be derailing from, I honestly feel like both arguments can be true. I can't remember where I read it, but somewhere it was implied that as humanity advanced in understanding of the world, more became possible with magic, other things became easier.

                                Mages operate by imposing Supernal symbolism on the world to make their imaginations reality. However, I feel like that symbolism isn't so linear as to alienate the ideology of either side. A Diamond Obrimos magnifying the heat in the room is subverting the Lie and imposing Supernal truth upon the heat to intensify it. A Libertine Obrimos magnifying the heat in the room is subverting the Lie and imposing supernal truth upon the latent kinetic energy in the world and infusing that into particles to excite them, generating more heat.

                                Both of them understand that they're overriding or warping the Lie to accomplish their goals, their methods simply differ based on their understanding and beliefs. Going back to the allegory of Plato's Cave, mages merely see the shadows on the wall, or semiotics, as filtered through the Lie. Each interpretation is different dependant on perspective, experience, and belief.

                                One mage might feel something is impossible because they believe the realm of their arcana can't possibly encompass something that clearly belongs to another, while another mage might laugh and proclaim them mistaken, using specific knowledge, belief systems, or semiotics as a workaround. Neither is really wrong, magic is fueled by belief and understanding as much as it is by Truth.

                                        Second edition (both the core book and Signs of Sorcery) has specified that different paths view the Arcana differently, but not quite in that way. It's particularly noticeable in Mage Sight wherein different Paths see wildly different things despite looking at the same symbols. It doesn't quite stretch as far as into the Arcana and Practices, though, and it has little to do with Orders and past experiences. Both the Thyrsus and the Obrimos will apply the Supernal symbols of perfecting heat to the already existing heat. The Yantra chosen to do this does carry its own symbolism, but not to those extremes.                                                         The Truth Is Out There (Fate ••, Time ••)
                                                        Practice: Veiling
                                                        Primary Factor: Duration
                                                        Withstand: Composure
                                                        Suggested Rote Skills: Persuasion, Occult, Subterfuge

                                                        This convinces a target that a future the caster selected will come to pass. It creates fake omens, "evidence", dreams and the occassional daydream about it, making the target believe its an absolute certainty, every small clue further reinforcing it.

                                                        Whenever the target or anyone else attempts to divine about it, the results conform to the fake future, any attempts to pierce the illusion causes a Clash of Wills.

                                                        With conjunctional Mind 2, it can be used to make the target "discover" about and/or believe any conspiracy (real or otherwise) the caster chooses, associating it with the fake future.

                                                        This spell was famously invented by a Kingmaker called Scully, who used it to Awaken a Sleepwalker investigator by secretly guiding him on a hunt for Exarch cults and agents of the True Fae.

                                                        +1 Reach: The target becomes exceptionally capable in regards to the fake future, further reinforcing they idea that they are a key linchpin in its creation. Furthermore, the omens change to direct them towards believing it is the best possible resolution. For another Reach and a mana, the merit can exceed the cap of 5 dots.
