    Originally posted by Ashenrogue View Post
    So AoH can lower Wisdom even if it has absolutely no barring on how Wise your actual ability to judge when to cast magic is?
    Yes. Just like a Breaking Point can lower a human's Integrity even if it has absolutely no bearing on the character's spititual health and a Trigger can lower a Changeling's Clarity even if it has absolutely no bearing on the Changeling's Perceptive acuity.

    Originally posted by Ashenrogue View Post
    Then why even say that Wisdom is a measure of that if it isn't really true?
    It is true, because having low Wisdom makes your magic harder to control. Just like Integrity is a measure of spiritual health because having low Integrity makes it harder to resist the spiritual harm done by witnessing magic, and Clarity is a measure of Perception because high clarity gives you bonuses to Perception rolls and low Clarity gives you penalties to Perception rolls.

    Originally posted by Ashenrogue View Post
    Wisdom isn't a measure of your ability to judge proper magic use, because it is quite possible to be able to perfectly judge magic use, have every spell you've ever cast be objectively Wise (at least as objective as you can get), and still end up at Wisdom 0.
    That's correct, it isn't a measure of your ability to judge proper magic use, it's a measure of how much control you have over your magic. Losing Wisdom means losing a degree of control over your magic, as reflected by the increased range of your long-term nimbus.

    Originally posted by Ashenrogue View Post
    The only thing it is clearly a measure of is how many AoH rolls you've failed. I can't even say how many times you've committed an AoH because it can swing pretty hard both ways with lucky/unlucky rolls.
    Which is why I say it doesn't measure adherence to the moral code that AoH are derived from. Violating that moral code is what causes you to lose control over your magic, and control over your magic is the thing Wisdom represents.

    Originally posted by Ashenrogue View Post
    And AoH, as you've admitted, are arguably mostly based on a morality scale, which means Wisdom itself is mostly based on a morality scale, with little to do with a mages ability to judge when/how to wisely use magic.
    AoH are based on a morality scale, yes. Wisdom is not AoH. AoH can make you lose Wisdom, but they are not Wisdom per se.

                    Anyway, going back to an earlier discussion, one of the things I really like about Mage is that they're tempted, not cursed or damned. The fact that players can avoid Paradox and Wisdom loss with careful play is, to me, a feature rather than a bug. "But horror!" The thing is, I don't believe that predestined failure encompasses the totality of horror, and the game still takes place in a horror cosmos and cautious players are still actively engaging with the system by constantly having to be vigilant about Paradox and Acts of Hubris. The game is putting them in the position of constantly introspecting on their own desires and choices and the likely consequences - and I think that's the point.

                    More importantly, though, I just feel like it's bad GM praxis to "solve" this "problem" by constantly engineering no-win situations where a cackling madman is like "Hitler and Milli Vanilli are coming back and you can only stop one! CHOOSE WISELY, FOOLS AHAHAHHAHA!" I think a GM/ST/referee/whateveryouwannacallit's job is to present a situation at least somewhat "fairly" - which is to say, without some kind of adversarial stance - and allow them to attempt to resolve it however they like, and if they manage to outthink you, tell them "GG" and move on. If their actions have an unhappy outcome that makes sense within the fiction, few people will complain (especially if you made it clear that it was a possibility), but everyone will notice the instant you start violating the narrative integrity of your own setting just to crank up the difficulty.

                                    Mrmdubois Wisdom is defined by the book. Just because you can change something with house rulings doesn't mean that the book itself doesn't already cover and define that thing. Just because the book gives you permission to change it (also known as Rule 0 in broader tabletop culture) doesn't mean there isn't a problem if you feel it needs changed (if it wasn't broken, it wouldn't need fixed; this is the essence of the Oberon Fallacy mentioned earlier).

                                    Originally posted by jacobkosh View Post
                                    Anyway, going back to an earlier discussion, one of the things I really like about Mage is that they're tempted, not cursed or damned. The fact that players can avoid Paradox and Wisdom loss with careful play is, to me, a feature rather than a bug.
                                    Having played Mage 2e a while, this is where Mage 2e falls really flat for me.

                                    There is no temptation.

                                    For Paradox in particular: If you decide to Release Paradox, a single success means your spell doesn't happen (at best) making it about equal to flat-out failing, or the spell gets twisted and does the opposite of what you want, or some other variant. That's even worse than just failing. If you're really lucky, your spell will happen as you wanted, but with horrible side-effects that cannot easily be remedied. Releasing at all can be an Act of Hubris, too, further adding to the extreme cost of Releasing.

                                    So Releasing Paradox isn't a temptation because the cost is far too high than anything you could possibly get out of doing it.

                                    Containing can be better or worse. There's been some debate over RAI/RAW for how it works.

                                    In either case, Resistant Bashing damage is only really a concern if you're in the middle of a hostile situation. Bashing heals too quickly to be a factor otherwise.

                                    The issue comes from the Paradox Conditions, which admittedly can also happen with Releasing.

                                    Either you can Scour the Condition away instantly, meaning the only cost to Containing is a single point of Lethal damage if you fail, or you have to wait for the Condition to lapse first. I've heard people justify both sides. Personally, given they each have "scour it away" as one of their Resolution options, I lean towards the first.

                                    In the first case, there's still no temptation. You could fail with ten Paradox successes and your only cost is 1 lethal damage. So long as you're failing to Contain Paradox less often than once every two days, there's really no reason not to.

                                    On the flip side, the second case is not a temptation for the same reason Releasing isn't. The price is too steep to ever justify. The average character is stuck with all their spells for the rest of the session causing further paradox or summoning Abyssal Entities, which is even worse than Releasing.

                                    Depending on how your ST runs things, Paradox is either a non-issue or too steep a gamble to ever partake in. Which I suspect is why people are saying their players either won't touch it with a 20' pole or they're going whole-hog with it.

                                    Either way, it isn't a temptation if it's either a non-option or a total non-consideration.

                                                    I think I've been pretty clear about what I'm looking for, but I'd be happy to clarify anything. In particular, the main focus is making Mages feel inherently monstrous, because that's what I see as the core of the setting, especially minor templates. I don't like "but they could do awful things so they're monsters", because that applies to any game where your character has superpowers. What differentiates ChroD from other settings for me is that it's about exploring horror archetypes. If I wanted to just be someone with superpowers, I'd play something else.

                                                    I'll stop with the heart to heart attempts, but they're completely genuine. I'm not trying to manipulate anyone. I know that when I was in rage mode, attempts to reach out to me seemed disingenuous, so I won't keep trying.

                                                    There's inevitably going to be a lot of Genius in this project, since I love it it dearly and think it has a lot of incredible ideas, and it pretty much captures what I'm going for.

                                                    One of my favorite parts of Genius, for example, was the "you were a scientist, but you can never do actual science again". It's one of the most effective personal horror scenarios I've seen in, well, anything. It's scary in a way that few other things are. The sense of having a beautiful vision but being unable to bring it into reality is something that I'd really like to bring in, that I think would be very effective in Mage, but I'm having a lot of trouble reconciling it with the whole "now you're one of the special elite who knows the secret truths of reality, unlike those dumb sheeple" thing. That's what mad scientists think they are, but the thing that makes them monstrous is that it isn't true. Again, that's something I've been very clear about wanting to emphasize. I'll try to be more diplomatic about Mage, but it's kind of hard to talk about it without insulting it in some way when I'm trying to fix what genuinely seem like problems to me. I love Geist, for example, but I don't object to the homebrew threads focused on "fixing" it, because I can totally recognize that there are things about it that you can definitely make a case for being problems. There's a difference between kinds of homebrew- this kind, and the Geist fixes, are about correcting perceived faults, which is different from those which are about adding content or making adjustments that aren't made to remedy problems. So, making a homebrew Legacy for Mage or a homebrew Key for Geist or a Dark Era can be done without implying that there are problems with the original, but something like this really can't. I understand that I've been a dick about this in the past, and I'm trying to do better, but there's not really a way to ask "how do I make mages feel more monstrous" without implying that the game as it is makes them insufficiently monstrous.
