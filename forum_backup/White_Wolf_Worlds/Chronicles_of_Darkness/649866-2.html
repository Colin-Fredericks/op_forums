    Originally posted by Varsovian View Post
    BTW. Is there a book that deals with gargoyles? There are another part of the core book that I find frustrating. They are mentioned, but there's no rules for them as to how to create one...
    The Circle of the Crone book has some Cruac rituals to raise gargoyles. The Danse Macabre, a big book of miscellaneous hacks, alternatives and optional rules, probably has the best section on gargoyles with an eye towards their use in play, general rules for creating them and several variant types associated with the different clans.

    Originally posted by Varsovian View Post
    BTW 2. What's your opinion on the Bloodlines books?
    Super hit-and-miss. If I were to rank them according to personal preference, I'd go Ancient Bloodlines > The Chosen > The Hidden > The Legendary. Many of them, especially as you go further to the right in my list, are very high-concept. Sometimes this works (insect-like vampires who organize their servants into hive-like colonies, tragically comic pop culture aficionados who are more invested in using their power to seem glamorous than to actually wheel and deal, an insular and distrustful bloodline that sets up in families in rural areas), and sometimes they just come off as silly (the smell vampires who obsessively kidnap people with smells, the body-part-exchanging circus performers). Chosen has a few that are a bit more fleshed out and three-dimensional. I like the Agonistes, researchers and historians who labor to keep knowledge from falling into the fog of torpor, but are especially tempted by the pull of sleep themselves, and the Yagnatia, lordly pre-Christian Slavic Nosferatu.

    If you were to get just one of these books I'd go for Ancient Bloodlines. It's structured to match the historical flashpoints in Ancient Mysteries, with each flashpoint receiving two associated bloodlines born out of or active in that period, plus one miscellaneous write-up of something else (so it's not just a book solely of bloodlines). That includes a new variant of blood sorcery, a syncretic covenant of Buddhist vampires from Thailand, and the Edimmu, strange and vengeful ancient spirits, each seven in number, which inhabit the Kindred as a means of destroying them. Some of the bloodlines are good too: ancient Egyptians who remain loyal to the old gods but have lost their proper memories of the gods to the Fog of Ages and search to recover it; gnostic Gangrel who seek mad and holy wisdom from the voice of the Beast; mad emperors whose blood harbors a malevolent demon urging them to sate their hunger for souls.

                    Originally posted by Varsovian View Post
                    Hello!

                    I have Vampire: the Requiem book at home, but I have yet to play or GM it. Nevertheless, I tried considered both - and there are two questions that I struggle with:

                    1. When coming up with possible characters, I started to wonder: why would anyone actually decide to become a vampire? I mean... being vampire obviously sucks, because you have to literally prey on human beings. Wouldn't that turn almost anyone off the idea of allowing themselves to be Embraced? It would seem to me that only complete psychopaths and very desperate people would be willing to become vampires... How do you have your characters rationalize it, then?

                    2. When I was considering GM this game, I realized that I... have no idea what Vampire games could actually be about. It's a very rich setting, but what stories can you actually tell in it? Could you share any ideas?


                    Not everybody is offered the embrace freely, some people get jumped one night and have it forced upon them. I mean honestly, do you think the Nosferatu would have any population at all if they relied on voluntary recruits? There are also those who spend a period as Ghouls before getting embraced and by then they're too indoctrinated or addicted to turn down the offer. Originally you had to get permission from the domain's ruler to embrace somebody, but it appears under the new rule systems, that is no longer the case. You can embrace whenever you want for whatever reason you want, but once you do so, they become your responsibility. If your childer misbehaves while still under your mentorship, you're the one who pays the price, so typically a sire will do a bit of research before embracing somebody.


                    --------------------------------------------------


                    Originally posted by Varsovian View Post
                    What about the covenant books? I admit they would be my first choice, as the covenant descriptions in the core rulebook are really brief (for once, it's not actually explained what Lancea Sanctum's religion really is...).


                    The Lancea Sanctum believe vampires exist to fulfill Satan's role in God's plan. In the actual bible, Satan and Lucifer are two very different beings. Satan means The Adversary and he actually works for God. His job is to test people's faith and punish those who abandon God's teachings. According to the Lancea Sanctum, It became increasingly difficult to fulfill his role as humanity grew in population, so they have devoted themselves to picking up the slack.

                    The Lancea Sanctum defends the pious and the innocent with righteous fury, while punishing those who fall prey to sin and corruption.

                        As for a setting, the usual story is one of politics, each of the factions are vying against each other for land and privileges, sometimes doing so fairly and other times in a much more cutthroat fashion. You could also have a game centered around religion via a war between the Lancea Sanctum and the Circle of the Crone, or a dispute about ancient tradition and new age progression by pitting the Invictus against the Carthians.


                        You could also do a sandbox mode via Unaligned in which the players hold no allegiance to anyone but themselves and are free to decide who they help or hinder. This, however, can be dangerous as the players run the risk of stepping outside of your planned story line.


                        -----------------------

                        EDIT: And then there is always the favorite idea that the rule book is just a general guideline or example of how you can run the game. I've been told several times that if you want to, you're perfectly able to alter or completely remove certain features about the game. I believe Predator's Taint was often thrown out because the risk of going into a frenzy whenever you met a new vampire for the first time was really frustrating.


                        I watched a live play session in which the ST completely threw out the Masquerade system and vampires were allowed to walk around in the sun because somebody went back in time and performed some kind of massive ritual. The result was a game in which vampires lived openly among the humans as recognized citizens. Of course there were various social groups who didn't approve of their existence, and the government had specially designed law enforcement to deal with vampire criminals, but it was still an interesting setup.

                                By the way, I know how you feel. I only own the first edition book too because I didn't know 2nd edition had come out.


                                Well going by the core rule book (I don't have any of the extended material because I'm poor), the 5 clans would be appropriate for the following roles.


                                Mekhet = Agents and Scholars. They prefer to hide in the dark and gather knowledge both occult and mundane.


                                Nosferatu = Spies and Enforcers. They distance themselves from society, but remain well informed and monstrously strong when threatened.


                                Gangrel = Warriors and Investigators. Savages with amazing survival instincts, they make good trackers and are brutal in close quarters.


                                Daeva = Socialites and Campaign managers. They know how to twist people's emotions so they can't help but faun over every word they say. This (if done correctly) can actually make them even more powerful than the Ventrue, since their slaves are fooled into thinking it's their own free will to follow them.


                                Ventrue = Leaders and Business Associates. Any Ventrue who's not on top of the game and shows no signs of wanting it deserves a stake through the chest.


                                ---------------------------

                                Most Likely Covenants:


                                Circle of the Crone = Mekhet, Gangrel, Daeva. They're devoted to ancient pagan gods and goddesses, practicing rituals believed to have died out centuries ago.


                                Lancea Sanctum = Nosferatu, Mekhet, Ventrue. Following a vampiric interpretation of the bible, they seek to fulfill Satan's role in God's plan, punishing the sinful and safeguarding the innocent.


                                Ordo Dracul = Mekhet, Ventrue, Daeva. They seek to transcend the weaknesses of the vampire condition, they care not for politics but rather for if a student displays a willingness and capacity to learn.


                                Invictus = Daeva, Ventrue, Nosferatu. The new Camarilla, the covenant was created by the elders, for the elders and the neonates can go fuck themselves. It also strongly upholds the traditions and laws of the masquerade to ensure its own survival.


                                Carthian Movement = Any Clan. The new Anarchs, the covenant is much more organized than its original counterpart. They seek to bring about modern social reforms to the archaic society of the vampires, such as the belief that all should possess equal rights and privileges regardless of age or power.

                                            In the Requiem setting, each of the 5 clans embodies one aspect of the vampire mythos.


                                            Gangrel = Bestial and savage. Dusk to Dawn or Fright Night are good examples.

                                            Nosferatu = Hideous and terrifying. Think I am Legend or Blood + and you get the idea.

                                            Daeva = Seductive and charismatic. You don't need to look hard to find examples of them in today's media.

                                            Mekhet = Shadowy and graceful. It's hard to think of a popular comparison for them, but they like to manipulate events from behind the scenes.

                                            Ventrue = Powerful and overbearing. Count Dracula, or Victor (Underworld) are good examples of a Ventrue.


                                            ----------------------------

                                            And yeah, it's very valid chronicle choice. Each of the games can take place in its own separate world or you can mix them all together, it's up to the ST's choice and what you plan to include in the story. Heck, you can even have a mixed party in which some players are Vampires and some are say mages or werewolves!

                                                Since everyone else is recommending books, I'll weigh in:

                                                I prefer just using the core book. I'll probably get the 2e supplements as they come out, but for now I'm blissfully enjoying not having to deal with the supplement/merit/bloodline bloat that tends to happen with RPGs towards the end of their run.

                                                You may be happier just sticking with the 2e book(s) and improvising anything else you need. The books were only ever meant to be an example, anyway. Things like Domains and Covenants are described as running very differently city to city, region to region. Easy to get stuck in a rut of ideas you don't necessarily even like if you have the books right there telling you how it is.

                                                Plus some of 2e seems to contradict 1e fluff, and it can be a mess sorting out which is which edition.

                                                Anyway, that's my recommendation. Everyone else gave great suggestions if you do want books in the end.
