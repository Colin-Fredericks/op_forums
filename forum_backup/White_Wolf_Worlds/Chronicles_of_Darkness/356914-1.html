    Goblin Markets in the Freehold of the Palisades

    The Freehold of the Palisades is home to two major Goblin Markets. The first, Pinochle's Emporium, is officially unofficially sponsored by the Court of the Sun. The second, the Pulaski Skyway Market, is unofficially officially sponsored by the Court of the Moon.

    Pinochle's Emporium

    Pinochle's Emporium opens one or two days a month, always from noon to sunset, and always on a day that is a multiple of the number 7. To find out if the Emporium is going to be held, Changelings in the Freehold must find a copy of the Daily Princetonian; if the Emporium is going to be held on the next day, the paper will contain a reference to the game pinochle somewhere in its pages. Alternatively, Changelings can just show up at the Springdale Golf Course clubhouse, which is the location of the market, on days of the month that are multiples of 7 and hope the market is open. The clubhouse is always mysteriously closed to the "public" (aka regular humans) on the days that the Emporium is held, and Changelings (and sometimes other creatures aware of the supernatural world) are allowed to browse its wares.

    The market is run by a very strange hob named Pinochle. Pinochle appears as a very short (about 4 1/2 feet tall) plump man with the long neck and head of a duck, all dressed in colorful robes. Perhaps more oddly, Pinochle seems to be generally friendly and helpful. In fact, many Changelings find him a bit too chummy and obsequious. Pinochle is a chatterbox and is possessed of a voice not dissimilar to that of Gilbert Gottfried. Otherwise, the vendors in the Emporium change from time to time. Pinochle is happy to have Changelings from the Freehold open booths at his Emporium as long as they abide by the rules he's hammered out with the Court of the Sun (see below), and have asked his permission to sell at a previous Emporium.

    Pinochle does not always have his own stall, claiming that he's just "supervising", but he does occasionally open one. He always sells high quality dream vessels in the form of children's toys that invariably contain extremely pleasant dreams featuring popular children's cartoon characters (both from film and television). Pinochle will take payment in Glamour that his kind usually takes, but he'll also trade the vessels for copies of new (within the past 2 years) animated children's television shows or movies. Unfortunately, the shows must be recorded on a betamax tape, usually making it easier to pay in Glamour. For technically minded Changelings who want the challenge of putting Frozen onto a betamax tape, the opportunity is there.

    Metropolitan Maurikios takes a dim view of Goblin Markets in general, so during his reign he generally forbids Changelings from selling at the Emporium (though he knows it would be folly to try to stop them from buying). Consequently, Pinochle is a political supporter of Chris Grand, and will happily and loudly proclaim this preference for anyone to hear if the topic comes up.

    Other than this change in rules that takes place between the reigns of Chris Grand and Maurikios, the following agreement has been put in place between Pinochle and the Sun Court:

    - Absolutely no vice merchants

    - No tokens that have very dangerous catches (generally meaning only 1 and 2 dot tokens are available for sale)

    - No slave merchants or sale of sapient beings of any kind

    - No goblin fruits that have solely harmful effects (for example, a fruit that has no purpose other than to be used as a poison)

    - Dream vessels cannot contain nightmares

    - No sale of supernatural merchandise "proper" to other supernatural ecologies (ie. no Supernal artifacts, no Sekhem relics, etc)- Changelings just don't need that kind of heat

    Pinochle scrupulously abides by this agreement and vets each merchant to make sure that he is not selling something forbidden by the compact. Pinochle is, however, really as kind and helpful as his demeanor suggests, and cannot fathom that any of his vendors might be less than sterlingly moral individuals. Consequently, his Emporium has occasionally played host to loyalist, privateer, and even disguised True Fae vendors, all there to scope out the Freehold and perhaps mark targets for later.

    Pulaski Skyway Market

    In the darkest part of the night, from 3 AM till first light, the Goblin Market known only by its location opens. The Pulaski Skyway market is held only on Friday the 13th, limiting it to occurring at most three times a year. Unlike Pinochle's Emporium, there are no rules at the Pulaski Skyway Market and everything and anything is up for sale, making this market the "unofficial" market of the Moon Court.

    This market takes place mostly inside of the Hedge; the "gate" is the entire length of the Pulaski Skyway. To enter, a prospective shopper must wear black mittens (not gloves- mittens) and a black mask that leaves only his eyes and mouth open- a ski mask will do. Then, he must pass underneath the Skyway from north to south. The hobs that run the market set up their stalls all along the 3 mile length of the Skyway on the Hedge-side (though some do open up in the more concealed areas of the human-world side). These hobs, and their guards, all have a decidedly sinister aspect- they all appear as creatures made of pure shadow with glowing white eyes and teeth like tiny crescent moons. For reasons the hobs refuse to give, their shadowy guards will throw out any shopper who doesn't keep his mittens or mask on all the time, even in the heat of summer. The exceptions to this are Changelings who have a Mantle rating 3 or higher from the Moon, Winter, Autumn, or East courts. They are permitted to remove their masks (but the mittens must stay on).

    As mentioned above, all sorts of things can be found at the Pulaski Skyway Market, from something as innocent as an old pulp fiction novel, to as monstrous as a child slave. The market is known especially for its Gluttony vice merchants, who have street food stalls that produce food so delectable and in such quantities that even many people who don't have Gluttony as a vice can't help but overindulge.

    The shadow hobs that run the market don't allow any Changelings or any other sort of being to get in on their business- with one exception.

    As long as anyone can remember, the Market has been host to a vendor who gives his name as Mr. Debonair. Mr. Debonair is anything but what his name suggests- he is a very boring and average looking white man in a plain black suit with a white shirt and a black tie- very "men in black"- who talks in a bored, monotone voice. Older Changelings will recall that in the 70's and 80's, Mr. Debonair was a boring, average black man. Even older Changelings will say that before that he was a boring, average white man but not the same boring, average white man that he is now. Mr. Debonair won't talk about himself except in the vaguest terms and quickly steers any conversation back to business. He sells typical Changeling fare at his stall- Tokens, dream vessels, hedgespun clothes, the usual. However, his stall is unusual for two reasons: one is that he'll only trade like for like- Tokens for Tokens, clothes for clothes, etc. Secondly, he'll occasionally have something from far left field- a seemingly normal item that isn't a token, but that Changelings (especially those attuned to the Wyrd) can tell is not right. A picture of a building that everyone knows, but somehow covered in weird wiring or technology. A translation key for a numbers station that can only be picked up when there have been 4 or more murders in Brooklyn the day before.

    Mr. Debonair is an Analyst Demon. He was inserted into the world by the God Machine when the Pulaski Skyway was first built with the mission of simply observing it and making twice monthly reports by dropping his encoded summary in a trashcan in Jersey City just outside of city hall. For reasons that are (probably) completely unrelated to everyone's favorite insane demiurge/divine computer program, the Shadow Hobs decided to set up their market under the Pulaski Skyway at the same time Mr. Debonair was given the mission of watching over it.

    Mr. Debonair was hooked. He found the Changelings and their commercial activities underneath the bridge endlessly fascinating. He was sure the God Machine would too, but he never heard anything back from his creator, and he never received any changes in his mission parameters. In 1942, he fell when he decided he couldn't just watch anymore. He needed to participate in the glorious commerce. The Hobs seemed not to notice him when he set up his little stall with his "seed money" of a couple of Changeling artifacts he managed to "acquire" (ie, pry from the extremely dead hands of their previous owners).

    Mr. Debonair does not have any connection with an Agency or a ring. He bears no ill will toward the God Machine, and in fact still makes his twice monthly report on the Pulaski Skyway and everything that happens near it. He's never been troubled by an Angel and he doesn't expect to ever be. Consequently, he has a small amount of status with the Integrator agenda. However, his focus on the Changeling world coupled with his Integrator ideology means that his covers tend to be paper thin and can easily be blown wide open if someone wants to cause him trouble. Thankfully that hasn't happened yet, and because he's a creature of inertia, he expects that it never will.

    Debonair's desire to experiment with something he was just suppose to observe caused him to fall, and he sometimes gets the itch to do so again. It's on these days he'll offer a God Machine related item for sale- a gadget, or something that reveals a bit of infrastructure, just to see what Changelings would be willing to trade him for these oddities (that they often can't even use). He's been doing this for over 70 years and it hasn't blown up in his face yet, but when it does, there will likely be Hell to pay, both by Mr. Debonair and by the Changeling who has purchased his demonic goods.

        Nearby Freeholds

        The Freehold of the Palisades lies near the heart of the New York Metropolitan area. This is a brief description of the Freeholds nearby and a rumor or plot-hook about them should my players decide to visit.

        Freehold of the Pine Barrens

        This Freehold generally encompasses what would be considered "South Jersey." The Changelings in this area are reclusive in the extreme, and those few who visit the Freehold of the Palisades tend to evince relatively low Clarity scores. The major reason for this is that the Pine Barrens, and indeed most forests of any size in South Jersey, have a much higher than average occurrence of Hollows. The Winter, Autumn, and Summer courts of the Pine Barrens all have their "royal courts" inside of their own large Hollows, and many of their courtiers literally live in the Hedge year round.

        The exception to this is their Spring Court, which maintains its physical presence in Cape May, and whose courtiers tend to be slightly saner.

        Freehold of the City

        This is the Freehold that covers Manhattan. The Spring Queen and the Winter King were once married, but have recently had a bitter divorce over reasons that aren't well understood in the Palisades. The divorce has left a great deal of political tension in the Freehold, not in the least because the Spring Queen and the Winter King had, against all odds, a small human(-like, for now) child together. He's 4 1/2.

        The Freehold of the City has many Changelings who work in glamorous (in the mundane sense) and high-powered jobs, and consequently a high proportion of them are members of the Satrapy of Pearls or the Guild of Goldspinners (about 10% of the Freehold are members of these orders).

        Chinatown

        Manhattan's Chinatown is a very special case; it is not its own Freehold, but it's also not part of the Freehold of the City. Chinatown can best be described as a colony of Hong Kong's East Court, whose members make up the majority of the neighborhood's fae population. The Freehold of the City's seasonal monarchs are very unhappy with this situation, but every time they've tried to dislodge the Hong Kong East courtiers, the foreign court has poured so much mundane money into defending the area that they just couldn't compete (even with all of the money they control- which is a substantial amount).

        Freehold of the Bronx

        The Bronx is host to a typical Seasonal Court system, but the Winter Court is notably more powerful than the others because of their connections to human gangs. Winter's power was also greatly amplified because, in the wake of the 9/11/2001 terrorist attacks, the monarchs of the other three seasonal courts were unmasked as Banished Kindly Ones by the current Winter King.

        Freehold of the Queens

        By unspoken compact, all of the seasonal monarchs of Queens must literally be Queens- they have never had a male monarch and probably never will. The Queens Autumn Court throws a Halloween ball that is the Changeling social event of the season, and any Changeling who is anyone in the New York area must attend. Even Secret Suzan, or at least someone shape-changed to look like Secret Suzan, is known to show up.

        Freehold of the Kings

        Much like its sister borough, Brooklyn is only ruled by monarchs of a single sex, but in this case, the sex is male. The Brooklyn courts are mostly ceremonial- about 75% of the Changelings in Brooklyn are intentionally courtless and the governing system is very weak. Each of the seasonal monarchs is elected from amongst the male populace of the Freehold on a rolling four year basis with universal Changeling suffrage (meaning that there is an election every year). Any male can stand for election, and if he's not part of the court, he will swear fealty to it while he's in office.

        Entitlements are rare in Brooklyn and Freehold-specific noble titles outside of the Monarchs themselves are almost unheard of.

        Freehold of Coney Island

        This tiny little former island (now a peninsula thanks to land fill) consists of the neighborhoods of Sea Gate, Coney Island, Brighton Beach, and Manhattan Beach. It broke away from Freehold of the Kings in the late 1970's, and a court was established around the Crepuscular Courts. The overall political weakness of the Brooklyn freehold saw to it that they were able to do so. Dawn Court holds sway from Midnight to Noon, and Dusk from Noon to Midnight, every day. The small number of Changelings who reside in the Freehold of Coney Island are considered eccentric even by Changeling standards, and they rarely leave their little island demesne.

        Freehold of Richmond

        Richmond was the former name of Staten Island, and its Changeling Freehold retains that name. There are seasonal courts in Richmond, by the island is lousy with Magistrates of the Wax Mask, who are all required to remain courtless in order to "maintain neutrality." In reality, the island's Magister Equitum is the real political power on the island, and he wants all of his subordinates in the Entitlement loyal to him and the Entitlement itself, not to a seasonal court.

        That's not to say the Magisters are corrupt or incompetent, they're (usually) not, but it can be a bit of a shock to foreign Changelings to see how weak the seasonal monarchs are before they learn the truth of whose word carries weight in the Freehold.

        Freehold of Long Island

        The Freehold of Long Island covers Nassau and Suffolk counties. In this Freehold, the Winter, Spring, and Summer monarchs may reign when their season comes up, but they do not rule. True political power lies with the Autumn Queen year round. Though an experienced and powerful occultist herself, everyone knows her power over the others is due to her alliance with two human witches.

        What most people do not know is just how powerful those witches are- they are Seer of the Throne Tetrarchs, the male twin in service to the Ruin, the female twin in service to the Prophet. The direction of the Freehold has something to do with an Omen they are constructing for their eventual Supernal ascension, but only the wisest of master mages would be able to tell a Changeling about Archmastery at all, let alone guess at what the twins are attempting.

                    The Courtless in the Freehold of the Palisades

                    About 25% of the Changelings in the Freehold don't belong to any court. Their reasons are as varied as the stars in the sky; some of them don't agree with a court system at all, some just don't like the Sun/Moon courts in particular (but don't immigrate away for personal reasons), others think that not joining a court will save them from politics intruding onto their already tumultuous lives.

                    Generally speaking, people who want to remain Courtless are permitted to do so so long as they take a slightly modified "Good Neighbors" pledge to one of the Freehold's Monarchs or a suitable stand-in, who represents the whole Freehold. At that time they are considered subjects of the Freehold but are not beholden to (or derive any benefit from) court membership. Those who refuse to take such a pledge must tread carefully because they are considered outlaws. The Moon Court, especially Phantom Tong members, are wont to go soft on these outlaws. Conversely, Modernist Sun courtiers tend to be the harshest on both oath-bound and outlaw Courtless (if you don't stand on the side of Progress, you stand against it, after all). Orthodox Sun courtiers are tolerant of those that take the Good Neighbors pledge but usually unforgiving of those that don't. Again, personal reactions may be very different than the "norm" described here.

                    Those who do join up with a court take a variant of the Commendation pledge that replaces the Good Neighbors pledge, while those given a noble title are expected to take the Knight's Oath.

                    Both Sun and Moon look somewhat askance at Entitlement members who refuse to join a court, with the exception of Blackbird Bishops and Magistrates of the Wax Mask. Secret Suzan, in particular, does not tolerate Phantom Tong members who will not join the Moon Court in a timely manner.

                    Here are two Courtless NPCs that I intend to shepherd the PCs into the Changeling way of life, or at least work as early allies.

                    The Little Bird of Manville

                    Real Name: Karen Blechacz

                    Name used amongst humans: Karen Blechacz

                    Name used amongst Changelings: Karen "Little Bird" Blechacz

                    Titles: Blackbird Bishop

                    Theme Song: Paula Cole - I Don't Want to Wait https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dDWp-zek4Ic

                    Mask: Karen is a sturdy woman of Eastern European descent in her late 20's, with dark blond hair and a heart shaped face. She's otherwise unremarkable, and tends to dress in work jeans and flannel shirts or other serviceable clothes. She keeps her hair up and out of her face.

                    Mien: In her true form, Karen's flesh becomes the light brown color and consistency of balsam wood, and her hair becomes the tiny needles of the balsam tree mixed intermittently with black feathers. In both her mien and her mask, the scent of fresh-cut balsam wood follows her everywhere. The scent is much stronger to those that can see her mien.

                    Early Life: Things were not going well for 16 year old Manville resident Karen Blechacz. Her father was long gone. Her mother lived at home, collecting a meager disability check for nebulous medical reasons rather than trying to find work. And, she was pregnant. The only thing going for her, almost miraculously, was that her boyfriend Carl Bauer decided that he was going to stay with her, and even said that he'd marry her after high school.

                    The due date came, and the Bauer twins were born- one boy, and one girl. It was a moment of happiness in an otherwise hardscrabble life. It was not to last, however, because Carl and Karen drew the attention of a Keeper who loved to steal young couples away and turn them into shrubberies in his castle. He did so the very night the twins were born, leaving fetches in place.

                    Durance: Karen doesn't remember the name or title of her Keeper, and she's pretty sure he never told her. He transformed Karen into a balsam tree in his garden, and set Carl to tend to her (which eventually led to Carl becoming a Wizened Woodwalker). All of the trees in the Keeper's garden were the same- two stolen lovers, the woman became the tree, the man became the tender. Occasionally, the Keeper would take a break from whatever he did in his castle and take a stroll in his garden of kidnapped women. He never spoke to the trees, and as far as Karen knows, he never spoke to the gardeners either. Carl cared tenderly for his tree-girlfriend, but sadly communication between the two was in short supply, though Karen was aware of everything that happened around her.

                    Then, something Karen can't quite explain happened. It may have been her Keeper's doing in some kind of fit of pique, or, possibly, her Keeper had come under attack. A great windstorm whipped through his domain, shattering stone with lightning strikes and bowling over trees with hurricane force winds. Carl and the other gardeners had taken cover somewhere inside the castle. Karen was knocked over by the storm, and she feared that she would die. Instead, by cracking open her trunk and blowing away her branches, the storm revealed her arms, legs, and face. Running with the wind, she ran straight into the hedge and never looked back.

                    That is, until she was stuck inside it's thorny embrace. She remember her love for Carl, how he had never left her side all those years, and tried to push her way back to Arcadia to bring him home with her. She failed, and became hopelessly lost. It was only the timely intervention of a Blackbird Bishop that saved her, bringing her out of the Hedge and back safely into Manville not far from where she had grown up.

                    Changeling Life: Karen found that she had spent ten years as a tree in her Keeper's garden. Her fetch, and that of Carl's, had been terrible parents and were almost non functional as people. Her mother had kicked her out of their small home because they began abusing drugs and the children both. Fetch-Carl and Fetch-Karen lived a marginal existence in a trailer park, huffing meth when they could get it and watching daytime TV when they couldn't. They had never formally married, and the twins lived with Karen's mom. Again, the Blackbird Bishops came to Karen's rescue. They wouldn't kill the fetches for her, but they did give her the resources she needed to get on her feet and find someone who would - including a job as a shift manager at a Home Depot garden center.

                    It wasn't long before both fetches were dead, and her sudden sobriety and job got her her kids back and repaired her relationship with her mother. She claimed that Carl had finally left her and gone way out of state to pursue the druggy life that she had given up. It wasn't that hard to believe, so everyone did, even Carl's parents. Karen owed everything to the Blackbird Bishops, so in order to repay her debts, she joined up. Most importantly, however, it gave her a chance to look for her beloved Carl, the real Carl, if he should ever find his way out into the Hedge.

                    Karen, now known somewhat jokingly as "Little Bird" (she's what some might call a 'thick' woman), works as the chief agent of the Blackbird Bishops in Somerset County, where she scours the Hedge to help escaping Changelings get out, while also trying to get them on their feet once they're back in the real world.

                    .
                    Personality & Motivations: Little Bird is dedicated to the Blackbird Bishop's vocation, but she is a somewhat harried woman. She has to balance patrolling Somerset County's hedge, taking care of her now 12-year-old children, and maintaining her job at the Home Depot. Needless to say, she doesn't get too much sleep and can be a bit irritable. Deep down she's a kind person though, and her love for her almost-husband has never faded. Everything she does in the Blackbird Bishops is out of a deep hope that she'll be able to see him again.

                    Little Bird has 0 time available as it is, so that means she has no time for Court politics. If anyone pressures her on the subject, she tells them to kindly fuck off. Her status as a Blackbird Bishop has prevented any reprisals so far, and she is actually pretty well liked by both Courts. Still, she is amenable to directing newly escaped Changelings to the appropriate Court if she feels it is what they would want.


                    Character Sheet: https://drive.google.com/file/d/0B4s...ew?usp=sharing

                    Notes: Character sheet is mostly self-explanatory. Karen's greatest weakness, perhaps, is that she had ensorcelled both her children, who share the Ancient Pact with her. This was a moment of weakness for her, because she simply could not balance all she has to do AND hiding her real self from her kids. They're taking it well so far, but it has the potential to open many problems in the future.

                            The Arcadian Chef

                            Real Name: Jason DiMena

                            Name used amongst humans: Chef, Chef Montserrat

                            Name used amongst Changelings: Montserrat

                            Titles: None

                            Theme Song: The Beatles - Savoy Truffle https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=erg1aRORJX0

                            Mask: Now in his late 60's, Montserrat appears as an average Italian-American man with olive colored-skin tending towards the fairer end of that spectrum, graying hair, and some wrinkles about his eyes and ears. He is in average shape for a man of his age and otherwise does not possess strong characteristics except for his pronounced Roman nose. Monstserrat can usually be found in his chef's whites, but otherwise does not dress unusually for an older American man of average means.

                            Mien: Montserrat's true face takes his human mask and adds what seems to be another ten years of wrinkles and graying hair. His face looks like it has been blackened by standing over a cooking fire and is constantly stained by soot that can never be washed away. His hands are covered in white flour that seems to be exuded by his skin; to those that can see Monserrat's mien, this flour gets everywhere, but fades from reality a minute or two later.

                            Early Life: In 1968, a fast paced job in one of Manhattan's top restaurants (two Michelin stars and working on the third) was all Jason DiMena, an Italian boy who grew up in Camden, could ever want. The hours were long and the rest of the kitchen staff could be pretty mean, but it was rare for someone of his age to be given a place on the line in such a fine establishment.

                            Needless to say, Jason was very excited when the head pastry chef couldn't make it in because of the flu, and he had to fill in for his boss. It would finally be his chance to shine to the head chef, and he did. Apparently the restaurant was hosting some kind of VIP that nobody was quite able to describe. After the dessert courses were over, the maitre'd came in beaming and said the special guest was very pleased with the pastry assortment, and that he even extended his compliments to the cook- Jason

                            Jason left the kitchen that night beaming, sure that a promotion was in order, but he didn't even make it to the nearest subway station. Something with huge claws, pointy teeth, and... formal dinner attire leapt out of the proverbial alley and dragged its new chef into the Hedge with nothing but a muffled cry.

                            Durance: Jason's new keeper was a horrendous ogre, all gristle and pointy teeth, that had pretensions of grandeur. It renamed all of its servants after "fancy" things, though it had no understanding of what was considered fancy or high-class by humans. Jason was re-christened Montserrat and forced to labor in the ogre's kitchens for an age as a pastry chef.

                            The ogre invited other Gentry to its table, and while they all despised its inept and falsely-cultured grumblings, it DID kidnap the finest human chefs to prepare its meals. So, the gentry attended the ogre's feasts, and Montserrat was able to hob-nob and come as close as possible to having polite conversation with Gentry while he served them his delicious desserts. So successful were his dishes with the ogre's guests, that the ogre entrusted Montserrat with a magical golden spoon, able to create the finest meals.

                            With the spoon in his possession and the ogre's trust, Montserrat was able to hatch a plan of escape. He poisoned all of the Gentry, including his ogre keeper, during one of the creature's feasts. Using the magic of the spoon, he folded the essence of sleep into his soufflé, and when the assembled guests ate it, they immediately fell into a deep, deep slumber. Montserrat absconded with the magical spoon and ran into his Keeper's herb garden, where he knew a door into the hedge was hidden. In short order he had found his way through the liminal dimension and emerged right behind the old pizza shop in Camden where he had his first job.

                            Changeling Life: Montserrat took a "job" as the official chef of the Spring King of the Freehold of the Pine Barrens in Cape May. He used his magical spoon to great effect, preparing feasts for his new lord just as he did for his Keeper, only without the imminent threat of himself being on the menu should he fail. As it happened, Montserrat's polite chats with the Gentry during his durance helped given him a pretty good head for occult matters, and he ended up doubling as the King's go-to guy on mystical matters, something that the Spring Court was not very well known for.

                            Things are never that simple, however. While Montserrat's keeper was not particularly cultured (except for his cultured palette), he was not stupid either. The magical spoon had a terrible drawback; the location of anyone who ate food cooked with it would be known to the great ogre for a year and a day afterwards. Many Changelings in the Pine Barrens ate of Montserrat's cooking, all prepared with his special spoon, and incidents of Changelings being re-kidnapped back into Arcadia rose in the Freehold precipitously. By the early 1980's, the freehold was in a panic, but a hero of the Autumn Court finally fingered Montserrat's magical spoon.

                            Though the Spring King knew that Montserrat was free of any malice, he had no choice to levy a punishment on his head chef. Montserrat was banished from the Freehold and never permitted to return while giving up the spoon that gave him his magical powers.

                            By this point Montserrat was too invested in the spoon to give it up, even if it did summon his old keeper. Meekly pretending to accept the punishment, he used his contracts of Forge to reshape an ordinary spoon to resemble his magical kitchen implement. He was long gone before the deception was discovered.

                            Montserrat traveled around for many years, but, somewhat dangerously, eventually settled back in New Jersey, only in the Freehold of the Palisades. While he was never forced to relinquish membership in the Spring Court, and he never did, he is effectively courtless in the Palisades. Always careful to hide his presence as much as possible in order to avoid retribution from the Freehold of the Pine Barrens, Montserrat now "owns" the Melting Pot restaurant in Somerville through an ensorcelled retainer. He's also found a bit of political protection in bartering his services as a source of occult knowledge to Chris Grand, the leader of the Modernist Sun faction. which tends to lack people interested in these matters.

                            .
                            Personality & Motivations: Montserrat (he never goes by his real name, Jason DiMena) never really got over his Durance. He's completely fixated on his cooking, and it is the center of his whole life. Though he is capable of discussing other things, it's impossible to have a conversation with him without it moving back periodically toward something having to do with food.

                            He's only slightly crazy, though, and realizes that as much as he would like to be able to use his special spoon, he now only saves it for very special dishes. He sees his occult knowledge in mercenary terms, as he came by it mostly by accident, and is willing to trade it to nearly anyone in return for favors, protection, or rare ingredients.

                            In order to protect himself from enemies in the Pine Barrens, over the years Montserrat has ensorcelled numerous mortals to use as screens for his activity. Right now, his patsy is Herbert Lowenstein, a down-on-his-luck restaurateur who is now overjoyed to have an actually successful business (thanks to Montserrat's cooking). Herbert has a modicum of business acumen, but is otherwise nothing special.

                            Notes: Most importantly, the magic spoon!

                            The Golden Spoon of Arcadian Delight (4 dot token)

                            Mask: In its mask, the golden spoon appears as nothing more than a well-used wooden kitchen spoon.

                            Mien: The golden spoon is literally made out of gold (that somehow never imparts a metallic taste to the food it stirs). The very center of the ladle has a tiny garnet set into it.

                            The Golden Spoon gives its wielder a +3 equipment bonus on any roll to make food. This ability can be used by anyone without activating its catch.

                            Its real ability, however, is that once a year, it can make a dish whose preparation it is involved in have the same effect as consuming a Hera Pear- the eater of the food can choose any one disease he has and completely cure it.

                            Drawback: Anyone who eats food prepared with the spoon has his location revealed to one of the True Fae for a year and a day. It does not just reveal the location where the food was eaten, the diner's location is constantly updated to his potential Keeper as it changes. Eating food prepared with the Golden Spoon again resets the year and a day timer.

                            Catch: Non-fae can use the Hera Pear duplication effect by mixing a pint of their mother's blood in with any dish they are preparing. The Spoon can still only produce this effect once a year.

                                        Ok, I'm going to begin my "potential antagonists" section. I plan on making one antagonist for each Court (Sun & Moon) and one unaffiliated with either. Obviously, this is a World of Darkness game, so it's entirely possible my players will not take the bait, but I do want to have some enemies handy just in case.

                                        The Dame of Wilson Street

                                        Real Name: Ashley Dowd

                                        Name used amongst humans: Dana Glimpse, usually just known as Glimpse

                                        Name used amongst Changelings: Glimpse or Dame Glimpse, a few know her as Dana Glimpse from her day job.

                                        Titles: Dame of Wilson Avenue

                                        Theme Song: Hall & Oates - Private Eyes https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cCiPkazVzFA

                                        Mask: Glimpse is a whipcord thin black woman in her late 30's. She's tall for a woman (about 5'7") and likes to wear stiletto heels that emphasize this. Glimpse has high cheekbones and sharp features, but her extreme (almost skeletal) thinness keep her from being considered particularly attractive in a conventional sense. She tends to wear comfortable, nondescript business-wear if she's not expecting a fight, but she always has at least one pistol secreted away on her person. Glimpse usually keeps her hair up and away from her face.

                                        Mien: Deep lines like circuitry, glowing with white-blue power, criss-cross Glimpse's whole body to those that can see her mien. Her eyes glow the same color. Occasionally sparks of electricity can be seen surging through her hair or across any clothing she wears.

                                        Early Life: Spending one's teenage years in Newark in the 1990s would not have, objectively speaking, been a barrel of laughs for anyone, but Ashley Dowd managed to pull through all right. Her father was separated from her mother, but stayed in her and her two older brothers' lives. He was a handyman-for-hire and an amateur electrician for those who couldn't afford the real thing (and the union prices they charged). Her mother was a secretary and sometimes lover for a private eye who worked mostly taking pictures of cheating spouses.

                                        Ashley was relatively bright and made the best she could of Newark's abysmal government-run school system, as did her oldest brother. The middle brother, Anton, fell in with a bad crowd (as the church ladies like to say) and became a petty courier and sometimes drug peddler for the local Bloods set. A good portion of Ashely's life would likely have been spent dealing with the fallout from Anton's criminality.

                                        Unfortunately, fate had a much worse future in store for Ashley. In July of 1995, she was abducted by one of the True Fae. There wasn't much to the kidnapping, and to this day she's pretty sure that she was just nabbed because she was the closest mortal at hand. She was at her father's apartment and a strange man knocked on the door, claiming to want to hire her father for some electrical work. It was late, but her father needed the money and let the stranger it. The electricity in the apartment immediately shorted out, and in the darkness the stranger -one of the True Fae or its servant- took her without much of a fight.

                                        Durance: Handed a machete and told to chop away at the Hedge, that's what Ashley did. Her Keeper was some kind of great prince (or princess, when she caught sight of it its sex sometimes changed) of storm, all great billowing clouds and booming pronouncements. It never talked to her directly and she and some of its lower-ranking servants came to call it the Thunderhead Royal. The Thunderhead Royal was a vain creature, even more vain than most True Fae, and worked its magic so that all the Changelings in its realm came to be a reflection of itself, even if their roles in its realm would have produced a different Kith or Seeming.

                                        In any event, the Thunderhead Royal had a rival that dwelt at least part of the time in the Hedge. The Thunderhead Royal's palace atop a great mountain was completely surrounded at its base by its rival's thorny domain, and he thus employed a team of Changelings to constantly hack away at the encroaching thorns. Despite being constantly at the edge of the Hedge, few of the Thunderhead Royal's "pruning" servants tried to escape- that area of the Hedge was under the control of the Thunderhead Royal's rival, and those few who tried to head back through to the mortal world were brutally attacked by both hobgoblins in service to the rival, and the Hedge itself. After the first two or three fellow-abductees had their heads hung on the edge of the Hedge as warnings for escapees, Ashley got the message and aborted any plans she had to escape that way.

                                        Instead, she put to use something she had learned from her mother's private-eye boss. A stakeout. It took years- to hear Ashely tell it, when she finally escaped, she was a woman in her early 70's. Every day, after her 12-hour shift of clearing away the Hedge's vines as they tried to creep up her Keeper's mountain, Ashley would explore the Fae domain, looking for good places to spy on her keeper. She surmised, correctly, that it had an alternate way of reaching the real world, since its rival kept it from making good use of the Hedge.

                                        After decades of waiting and watching, she finally found her out. Finding a hidden crevice across the ravine across from her Keeper's chambers, she spied it through a great window when it walked straight through large mirror. Wasting no time, Ashley entered the palace, almost unopposed thanks to her lowly station, and made her way as quickly as possible to the Thunderhead Royal's chambers. The guards there were more interested in keeping people out, but they made the mistake of underestimating a 70 year old woman who had spent her whole life chopping things (including the occasional hob) with a machete. Their foolishness cost them their lives, and she stepped through into the Thunderhead Royal's private rooms, and quickly found the mirror. Its glass was a swirling portal into the Hedge- a part of the Hedge far distant from the realm controlled by the rival. Without hesitation, Ashley stepped through. As she made her way back through, the Hedge stripped her of much of her time in Arcadia- including her years.

                                        When she emerged from the Hedge on the outskirts of Harrison, she was once again a young woman. The year was 2000.

                                        Changeling Life: Despite only 5 years having passed in the real world, Ashley had lived a lifetime of hard labor in Arcadia. She looked very different from her old self, all skin and bones, even if her advanced years had been left behind in the Hedge. Assisted by some Sun Courtiers close to Chris Grand in getting back on her feet, Ashley adopted the identity of Dana Glimpse. As Glimpse, Dana approached her mother's old Private Eye employer with an offer to buy out his business. The aging detective agreed, and Glimpse purchased the service from him with a loan secured from her Modernist Sun courtier contacts.

                                        The newly re-branded Glimpse Detective Agency was born, with the nominal goal of continuing the standard cheating-spouse private eye business. In the Freehold, Glimpse offered her services as an investigator, and eventual killer, of fetches. One of the first to go was her own, though it was something of a soft target. The person that had abducted her had killed her father in the process, and left the sobbing fetch behind. With nothing else to go on for a possible suspect, the fetch was convicted of murder and sent away to prison for a long time. It wasn't that hard to bribe some of the prisoners to both "shank" the fetch and some of the guards to look the other way.

                                        Over the past 15 years, Glimpse and her detective agency have become the premier fetch-killers in the Freehold, and in 2012 Chris Grand granted her the title of Dame of Wilson Street (where the agency is located in Newark) in recognition for her services.

                                        All of this is only the surface, however. The Glimpse Detective Agency (which now includes Glimpse herself and two other Changelings in a motley) is more than just a fetch-killing service. It is one of the most successful Militias in the Freehold. Glimpse and her motley-mates hate almost everything having to do with the Faerie. They have, using their excellent detective skills, over the years framed many of their fellow Changelings for loyalism if they felt they were too close to their fey natures. Of course they were lauded for protecting the Freehold in this way, even though the Changelings in question were not loyalists at all, and simply "too fae" for Glimpse and her friends.

                                        In addition to this, Glimpse and her fellows engage in more typical Militia activities, such as directing patsies to "flush out" True Fae sympathizers, sending people too inexperienced to survive into the Hedge to fight hobgoblins or servants of the True Fae, and sometimes even provoking attacks or plots by the True Fae in order to then expose or stop them themselves. Of course such plots usually have a few Changeling or human casualties before the Glimpse Detective Agency can arrive to put a stop to the nefarious dealings.

                                        Glimpse is not without enemies, however. Chernobogist Moon Courtiers, who often dwell or spend a large amount of time in the Hedge, loathe her as they are frequently targets of her set-ups. Orthodox Sun courtiers, who normally would be simply cool to her because she has personal and not ideological connections to the Modernist Sun, are also suspicious of her. More than a few of their number with a mystical bent have been the subject of her crooked "investigations."
                                        .
                                        Personality & Motivations: In person, Glimpse is a fast-talking, hard nosed gumshoe. She comes off as brusque, but is rarely rude and unlike many in her profession, does not curse like a sailor. Changelings who grew up in the 1990's liken her demeanor to Lynne Thigpen's character "The Chief" on the old TV show Where in the World is Carmen Sandiego. All in all, she's quite likable in her own way.

                                        Glimpse nurses a dark bitterness in her heart, bitterness for the True Fae for taking her away from her family and subjecting her to, in her experience, over 40 years of hard labor. Unlike most Changelings, Glimpse did not deal with this bitterness in a healthy way. There's very little that she will not do if she thinks it will be a blow against the True Fae or their influence in the world, and in her own way she's also become addicted to the adulation that her "successes" in protecting the community brings her.
