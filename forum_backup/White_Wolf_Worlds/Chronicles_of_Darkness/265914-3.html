            "Fan Splats? My favorite subject!" he typed into the forum as he wistfully stared at his own, woefully incomplete document; the realization of the irony bathing him in miserable self-awareness.

            Self-flagellation aside, I find most fan splats to be fairly well-done. Despite some of the fan splats dying out due to lack of interest or drive (I'm still holding out for TvTropes's adoption of Pathogen: the Infected), the ideas of certain legendary creatures of horror or strangeness is more than welcome for me. Still sad that Bogey and Janus haven't been completely fleshed out, though. That said:

            Genius: the Transgression: My only complaint about this fan splat is that I'm not smart enough to comprehend the construction of Wonders, which is a terrible thing considering that Wonders are the thing that Geniuses do. I do like the themes and overall feel to the splat; the struggle of great intellect tempered by madness is a wonderful topic that I have not seen discussed in many forms of media. The Beholden's are also a great touch (I am not saying that because I imagine Beholdens as Harley Quinn from Batman and Magenta from Rocky Horror Picture Show. It has ABSOLUTELY nothing to do with that at all! How dare you!)

            Leviathan: the Tempest:             Playable horrorterrors? Where do I sign up? On a more serious note, I find Leviathan to be thematically delicious, meshing the feels of Promethean and Mummy together, blending personal horror with godlike presence. However, I wish the presentation was cleaner; as a wiki-based splat, it's incredibly difficult to quickly go through the pages and find the details for Depths or the Vestiges. As a side note, I tend to mix the term Lahamin with Lahmasu, which has caused severe confusion on my part.

            Princess: the Hopeful: Next to Promethean, Princess is one of the few splats that I find to be entirely genuine. Being the beacon in the World of Darkness rather than the candle of the Vigil is a pretty noble aspiration. However, I have made it a personal challenge to vilify the Princesses that follow the Radiant Queens; despite the Twilight Queens being the obvious anti-hero alliances, I wanted to find out if it would be possible to make Princesses antagonists without resorting to political opponents. Granted, Puella Magi Madoka Magica is on my "to-watch" list and Homura and Kyoko seem to be promising leads to build upon in Princess, but I'd love to find a way to create sinister Princesses without turning them into Dethroned.

            Dragon: the Embers: The threat of extinction is a double-edged sword. On one hand, it's definitely a fear that humanity itself suffers from and having Dragons react to the VERY REAL future of dying out as a race is amazing; living in fear of being murdered for their heart and having to isolate themselves is just the delicious icing on the cake. On the other hand, there is no real way to escape this, so the Dragons' existence is just prolonging the inevitable; there doesn't seem to be an ultimate goal beyond survival and conquering humanity (and as the numerous failed villain's schemes to conquer the world have told me, ruling over humanity never works out well)

            Wraith: the Arising: As far as I can tell, dying sucks. Role-playing the dead, however, does not (for the most part). Although I am not familiar with the oWoD version of Wraith, I absolutely LOVE this fan splat with an undying passion. While I love Geist, it focused more towards the fact that Sin-Eaters were alive thanks to their new best friend. Wraith kicks all sorts of rear ends when it comes to fleshing out death and I love it, regardless of the conflicts it has with canon.

            Hunchback: the Lurching: Is The Hunchback of Notre Dame an iconic monster? Hunchback is fairly charming in what it is representing; the Miserable are quite miserable and live for their misery. The focus on being an outcast that desires affection is absolutely wonderful and the fact that a Hunchback's existence is entirely set-up to inflict even more misery is hilariously dark (and not at all a reflection of how I view my own existence. Not at all. How dare you.) However, there are parts of Hunchback that are incomplete, such as the Oublier and the lack of a distinct Morality/Integrity stat, that do set the fan splat back. It's a pity, especially since I barely find any discussion of the splat.

            Honestly, despite my concerns due to quality or completion, I love all fan splats and believe them to be integral to the community. The World of Darkness is something of supernatural wonder and fear; specifying the canon splats as being the only supernatural creatures in the setting is fine, but that eliminates many other monsters that exist in legend and in media. I like having options, especially options that have mechanics attached to them.

            Now, if you'll excuse me, I must weep over my Shadow: the Perverse.

                On fansplats: most seem to either work better as minor templates or occupy the design space of another splat.

                Princess: the Hopeful is probably the coolest and best designed of the fansplats I've seen; it manages to very explicitly define its themes and neatly separates itself from both Changeling and Mage, the two main splats I feel it is closest to.

                Genius: the Transgression is the one I've been told to "check out" the most, and it is horribly disappointing. To me it occupies a very small corner of Mage's design space, it is magic item making Mage lite, has no themes that make it independent (Hubris is Mage, Disbelief/Quiescence is Mage, Eldritch Abominations is Mage, Atlantis/Lemuria is Mage, seeking Truth is Mage in spades, &c).
