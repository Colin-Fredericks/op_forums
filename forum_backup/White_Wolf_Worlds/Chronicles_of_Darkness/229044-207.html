        From the kickstarter:

        Originally posted by Matthew McFarland
        Heya, Matt here.
        So, first of all, I want to thank all (as of this writing) 1,147 folks who have backed Beast. Your support means everything to the project, not just in the form of your hard-earned cash, but also in the form of the comments and discussion here. It’s been a real pleasure to watch people talk about Beast, analyze it in terms of media, discuss what Dark Eras would be cool, and plan out the characters they want to make.
        But not all Beast discussion has been quite so positive, and I want to talk about that. Stay with me a minute.
        With Beast, we tried doing a bunch of things differently. Some of those things were purely mechanical; the game doesn’t have a “fuel trait” the way our other games do, for instance, nor does its Integrity analogue function the way that other such traits do. Moreover, it was always our intention to take a light touch with Beast “society;” I didn’t want the same kind of structure that, say, Vampire or Changeling uses. The Hero/Beast dynamic, too, came out differently than I was intending.
        The result of that is the game has some issues. If you’ve followed discussion of the game on various online forums, you’ve probably seen it. I’m not going to repeat every point, here, mostly in the interests of time (I’ve got a development pass to get started on!), but here’s the gist: We hear you. There are problems with the game as it stands. Some of those problems come from the fact that we did try and break some new ground on the game, and that always carries a chance of something coming out different that one planned. Some of it just the nature of game development; at this stage of the process, things are getting tweaked. The difference is that it’s more public than it’s ever been. And some of it is that, honestly, when you’re as close to the material as I and the other writers have been, it’s easy to lose perspective.
        Of course, the game hasn’t been received wholly negatively. I’ve had the pleasure of watching folks who really like the game talk about their inspirations, their character ideas, and the mechanical innovations we’ve made. We’ve heard those voices, too, and as we move forward, we’re listening to the positive feedback as well as the criticism.
        What all that means is this: I’m doing another development pass on Beast. I’m going to add about 15,000 words of text (give or take), and do some heavy revision on parts of the game. My plan is to have that done by next Friday, June 19, but if that changes during the week, I’ll let you know. As I revise chapters, I’m going to give them to Rose to link from the Kickstarter, and I’ll be putting them up on Google Drive and doing posts on the Onyx Path blog so you can see what we’re doing.
        Here’s a quick list of the overarching things we’re addressing. Note that I’m enumerating specific changes here (that is, I’m saying what we’re changing but not how), because I’m still compiling my notes and making a plan.
        • Resolving the “are you born a Beast or not?” question.
        • Defining Beast culture; we’re not adding social splats, but we are giving Beast society some structure and giving Storytellers something more to work with (this is where a lot of the extra word count is going)
        • Further definition of the Primordial Dream, its relationship to Beasts and Heroes, and what you can do with it in play (this is where a lot of the rest of the word count is going)
        • Hand in hand with that point, we’re underlining Beast-on-Beast conflict more
        • Redefining the Beast/Hero relationship, and (more to the point), revising how the text treats Heroes
        • Looking at the crossover sections and making sure they reflect the other game lines well, and that they get across our intent with making Beast crossover friendly
        • Making sure that in-character opinions are clearly reflected as such; all of the World of Darkness games include unreliable narrators, but it’s important (especially for a new game) that the reader understands what the game assumes to be true and what a character within the game assumes to be true
        • Giving Beasts something specific to do. That was definitely the most exciting part of the meeting that we had last night. I think you’ll like it. It addresses one of the biggest points of criticism we’ve had, which is that a Beast sating its Hunger is pretty much just cruel and abusive for the sake of it. I want characters in my game to be monstrous; they’re Beasts, after all. But I want there to be a point.
        There are other, minor revisions that’ll happen, too, most of them logical outgrowths of the list above. Stay tuned, watch this space, and thank you for all your feedback. This is going to make for an intense week for me, but I’m excited to do the work.
        Watch this space (regardless of what space you’re seeing this on, it’ll at least get linked) for more information, and again, thank you for your support and your feedback. Criticism stings, but it teaches a lesson.
        And that’s an appropriate note to end on, I think.


        Kelly R.S. Steele, Freelance Writer(Feel free to call me Kelly, Arcane, or Arc)

                Any time you encounter something, whether it's new or not, you change. That change can be better or worse, and while there are times where you can't decide if the change makes you worse, like losing your house or your car or getting some money stolen. Other times you can decide, based on the incident. Like waking up from a nap and finding you don't have the food you want to eat, so you make something good with what you've got left, or your car runs out of gas in some town in the middle of nowhere, but you decide to just have your vacation right there on the road.

                Story.

                There's this boy out hunting for some peccary, and he runs across a rattlesnake on the road. Rattlesnake calls out.

                "Hey, hey, you!" rattlesnake says.

                "Yeah, what?" boy answers.

                "My den, it's all the way up that straight cliff," rattlesnake says. "Do you think you can carry me up there?"

                Boy says no, and that he's afraid of getting bitten. Rattlesnake says he won't bite, he swears. Eventually they work out a deal, the boy'll take the rattlesnake up the cliff if the rattlesnake tells him where to find peccary. Then, real careful, the boy picks up the snake and starts climbing. They get about two-thirds the way up when the snake bites the boy in the arm.

                "You promised you wouldn't bite me!" boy shouts.

                "You knew what I was!" snake shouts.

                They land, boy breaks his legs, feels the snake venom running through his body, rolls to the snake.

                "I don't want to die!" boy shouts.

                "I think I can solve this," snake says, and slithers off into the bushes.

                Snake comes back with a dress covered in diamonds.

                "Wear this," snake says.

                "That's a girl's dress," boy says.

                Snake gives him the "I am disappoint" stare and the boy puts it on. Immediately the boy starts screaming in pain, feeling his body distort and become something else. He realizes he's turned into a woman, and then he starts stretching and stretching and his arms and legs get sucked up into his body and now he's a lady snake.

                "I was saving it for a girl I liked," snake says. "But at least you're alive."

                The new snake's understandably upset, but figures there's at least some good can come out of it. Another hunter comes along.

                "Hey, you!" the new snake says. "Our den's up on that cliff, think you can take us up there?"

                So, you know, you can't avoid getting bit. That already happened. Now all you're getting asked is if you want to bite through the pain and hope the venom doesn't drive you crazy, or you going to put on that dress?

                                Originally posted by Roadie View Post
                                The problem here is that, as written, Heroes literally don't have the free will to choose to act differently. The only way they can break free of the role forced on them is to rise above Integrity 4.
                                I don't follow. Are you implying that people choose to develop maladaptive and unhealthy psychiatric states choose to?

                                Because that wasn't what I said, nor is it true. People don't choose to go off the deep end, nor does everyone who experiences trauma develop significant crises. But few people come through unscathed and have behavioral changes that are their means to cope, which aren't a choice, it is what people need to do as thinking organisms.

                                But some do develop problems. These individuals are often susceptible and have already had other events or poor coping skills that made them vulnerable, and the trauma is the final straw. These patients develop maladaptive behaviors, delusions, and even full blown psychiatric conditions that result in maladaptive and possibly dangerous behavior. But once again, this isn't a choice.

                                Yet while not a choice, it doesn't mean the actions are permissible or justified. Even in criminal law, where there is a plead of insanity, this doesn't excuse the actions or say that they are justified, merely that the person doesn't even have the capability to grasp how bad the action is and needs treatment so they might be able to one day. No matter what, you work to curb the maladaptive behaviors, and

                                The thing is, heroes literally follow the classic model of those who develop maladaptive psych. They are individuals who have a lowered threshold and poor sense of identity, which is represented by their lowered integrity. They are exposed to a massive destabilizer, which causes them to decompensate and develop maladaptive behaviors and thought processes, which like many trauma victims, are focused on the incident that destabilized them. Even a Hero's recovery mimics how we work with such patients: it is about rebuilding their sense of self that is separate from the incident, and helping them break from the maladaptive behavior (in other words, recovering someone who sees nothing wrong back to above integrity 4).

                                But also note that I am not saying the Beasts "kill the heroes, cause they are bastards" is okay either. While the heroes actions aren't excusable, they also are still victims. The optimal solution would be rehabilitation, and the game even gives mechanics for this, but I amssume since this is the WoD that rehabilitation is rare. And Beasts are much like the general population on mental health: They have difficulty either seeing past the actions, or if they do, don't get that the hero can't help it and that they must just be assholes or people who need to be put down or locked away.

                                It's not a perfect analogy. They never are, but the parallels are there. I know I am working with a very difficult subject, and while I have a lot of experience with this type of stuff, I am not a final authority nor do I even pretend to know the game designers intent.

                                PS. I also want to point out, before people counter with Matt's comments on "but heroes aren't crazy", there is a difference between insanity, maladaptive behavior, and delusions. People with maladaptive behaviors and delusions can beotherwise highly cognitive and rational, but work from a distorted view of things regarding the topic of their delusions. I view heroes in the same lens.

                                        A Beast is similar to a Sin-Eater, just, instead of dying, you see something scary and you react to it. If you react to it by letting it overwhelm you, you're out of the game. If you own it and consume it, you become a Beast. If you recoil in disgust and want everything to go back the way it was, you become a Hero. A Beast is like a Vampire, in that it must do terrible things to survive. It's like a Changeling, in that everything it knew is gone. It's like a Promethean, because it's trying to figure out what being alive means. It's like a Werewolf, because the Beast must hunt. It's like a Mage, because it must find the hidden pathways to wisdom. The Beast is like a Hunter, because it knows what's there to lose. It's like a Demon, in that it's a stranger in its own skin.

                                        Whether you own it or not, Beast or Hero, you start recognizing the world for what it is. You see the problems and the monsters and you feel the hunger in your stomach. You have a choice there – you can become the monster, abuse the abusers, kill, maim, or terrify people who don't really deserve it, or you can find the monsters and see what they're doing, what they need, what you can get for them. But you know mortals, too, and you know they're just trapped in the middle. Everyone who could have helped them has been doing nothing but hiding for so long that if everyone knew what was going on, there'd be anarchy. So your other choice comes into play: keep things secret while preparing everyone else to see what you did, to experience it. This means you have to scare people away from things more dangerous than you, while also teaching them how to handle it if it comes down to that, but without them knowing. Become the monster and grow the legend, because if you don't, the whole thing comes crashing down.

                                        Heroes, on the other hand, they went a little crazy. They don't want it to be true. They're afraid of what's real, or they think that what's real is a lie. They're a little bit right, the world is terrible and you have to do terrible things to live in it, but that doesn't mean you should burn it all down – that hurts more than it helps. So, even if you, the monster, are trying to make things better, you have to deal with Heroes one way or another.
