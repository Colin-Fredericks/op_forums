    Here goes nothing. Sorry, spent most of yesterday doing NMR specs, and was exhausted when I got home. This might not be the best idea, but I thought it could be interesting.





    Spirit: the Claimed. (Might need to find a better one)
    Quick version: A game about not letting your nature define you and building your own identity. Players are the claimed, half spirit half human creatures who balance the hunger for essence, bans and banes of being a spirit along with the relationships and aspirations of a human life.

    Long version:The Spirit world is a cruel place. Spirits, born of essence fight for more essence to feed on and consume each other to survive. A hierarchical world, where every spirit has a rank and a place. No wonder so many spirits want to find a way past the gauntlet and into our world. The essence is taster, not being filtered through worlds. There are fewer spirits trying to eat you, Staying here isn't easy though. Spirits have three options. Constantly feed or be sucked back, fetter yourself to something and be stuck around that thing or person forever, or make a deal with a human and become a claimed.
    Claimed are one mind, half spirit and half human. Inheriting the desire for essence, influences, bans and banes of the spirit and part of the human's personality and the ability to sustain themselves outside the umbra.

    Mood: Don't really have the word for it. The best way I can explain it is the feeling of becoming an adult/graduating school/college and realizing that you have no idea what you are doing and need to make it up as you go along.

    Theme: Identity/Self discovery/Change;(I need a better word) Low rank spirits don't even have a name or memories. All they have is a desire for essence, some influence a ban and a bane. You don't know all of the traits of the human half either. You pick up things from the essence you eat, the things you do and the events you go through. From this arbitrary collection of cards, the claimed try to create a place and cohesive identity for themselves.

    Inherent Axis: Type. Initially divided into concept/emotion, Nature, Artificial, elemental, location. Less important overall. The focus on building identity means you can eventually branch. A fire spirit that keeps eating at the Olympic torch might eventually gain influence over victory.

    Social Axis: Path(better name needed); The view of the Claimed on their place and purpose in the world.

    Power Stat: Rank. All spirits have a rank in the hierarchy, measuring how they compare to everyone else. The fact that the claimed still use it is slightly amusing.

    Fuel: Essence; You need it to eat, you use it to fuel your powers. What doesn't it do? Essence has a resonance, which is its "flavour" so to speak. A Claimed can eat any essence, but the one that matches its influences works best. A part-time claimed would gain essence from being around fires for example.

    Integrity: Balance?; Measure of which aspect dominates, spirit or human. Spirit dominating means powers work better and easier socializing with other spirits. Human dominating means more essence Gained by eating and drinking, easier socializing with humans and banes that don't hurt as much. A high humanity half-fire spirit might just be uncomfortable swimming, but a high spirit one might take damage from drinking a cup of water.

    Virtue/Vice: Unsure. Toying with idea of having half of this be players choice and half be decided randomly by the human. For the theme of building an identity from the cards you have been dealt.

    Powers: Influences and "Numinia". Each spirit has rough and somewhat Freeform influence over the thing that they are. This can grow or change with the character. They also have numinia/charms representing more concrete things. Need to give better costing for play so probably won't be compatible with existing numnina.
    Minor Template: dunno. Unclaimed? People who used to be claimed and demelded for whatever reason? Still have minor powers. Either that or children of the Claimed.


    Also thinking about having so if the host dies, you become a free spirit that has to make another deal or get sucked back. Whenever a spirit gets detached, they get a new ban/compulsion/thing that gives them essence taken from the human. So a fire spirit who melded with a firefighter might get " be nice to dalmatians" or "get people out of burning buildings"

    --

    There is material in the spirit book and predators devoted to taken, but it is them as antagonists. The stats are a bit high for playability, and I figured the themes would make for an interesting splatbook and an expansion of nwod

        Dragon: the Ashes
        When the "Heads" of Tiamat fell on Earth, their tongues and claws reached inside the Man's Hearth and corrupted them, taught them the value of Government, only to betray them and take it's rightful place among the rulers of the world. It is said that an Old Pact is the only thing that stops them from destroying the reality and consuming our bodies and blood as meals. An Old Pact sealed in Atlantis or some kind of this bullshit. Conspiracy theorists call them Dragons or something.

        Mood: Political Warfare - as the Rightful owner of the Earth, you've responsibilities and duties to fulfill, alongside with silent wars to battle and the hungers for power that grows stronger. D:TA is a game about world visions and ways to accomplish them, political ladders and backstabbing allies. Nobody gets to the throne being all nice and fair.

        Theme: Restricted Power - The Old Pact put you on your knees. Now you're nothing but a lizard inside some human skin. The Power that cringes inside of you is urging to come out, but the pact stops you from putting your claws out. Maybe, just maybe, if you found the right reason or so...

        Inherent Axis: Head - Which head is your ancestor? Which teeth of Tiamat are you more related with? The lineage of every dragon.
        • Drakes - The Kings, Power hunger sonofa******* that will do anything, ANYTHING to get to power, believe in control by fear
        • Hydras - The Council, Diplomats but venomous, the multi-headed philosophers believe in controlling by alienation.
        • Quetzal - The Gods, if there’s a seed of a religion there's a Quetzal to pour some water on it, then he'll come and bear the fruits, believes in controlling by devotion.
        • Salamandris - The Sages, wiseman or assassins, don't underestimate the power of knowledge, believe in controlling by secrets/knowledge
        • Longs - The Emperors, the prodigal son, respectful, smart and deadly, believe in controlling by discipline
        • Abyssal - The Beheaded, the bad brother that decided to live deep in the sea against all the others, believe in the power of the masses

        Social Axis: Under Cpnstruction

        Power Stat: Heart - The inherent power of the Dragon's heart and its direct connections to Tiamat powers. Also, the endurance of one Heart, making it easier to find but harder to destroy

        Fuel: Embers - The little sparks that make the Hearts chains the Powers of Tiamat.

        Integrity: Control - How can you be this powerful beast and not start destroying the whole world apart? Oh, right, you're very patient.

        Virtue/Vice: Hoard/Hunger What moves you? What makes you move the ladders of power? Better yet, what are those two things that you would like to have and to destroy?

        Powers: Powers come in two sets: The Inheritances, Powers that Tiamat gifted to you and The True Form (ripoff from the ablutions from Dragon: the embers, plus tweaking0 biological powers that fulfill your destiny but are restricted by the Old Pact.

        Minor Template:
        • Wyvern, half-human, half-dragon hybrids that are neither too powerful to be a Conqueror nor too weak to be a commoner.
        • Knights: Humans with half a dragon’s heart

        Well, this is part a Ripoff from Dragon: The Embers and part something entirely new. There's a lot off work in progress and a lot to do, but it's moving. slowly.​

This dark place, it’s just a projection of what She wants you to see. Let’s unveil it. Help get it out. When your Alarm Clock rings twice in the morning or you feel like you’re seeing double? That’s me trying to reach you, for you are no longer human. You have seen under the curtain. Or worse, under the curtains that is UNDER the curtain. You can help me clean this mess. Just help me out. When you hear this voicemail, break your phone and toss it in the river near you house. They know of you, and they are coming for you. And I? Plug your notebook on the toaster in your office by midnight, I left a clue in the Grid for you.

                Social Axis: Function

                • Engineer – You can create the machines, shape them to your benefit

                • Technician – The Dark World is Broken, you’re the one to fix it.
                Power Stat: Synchronization – How close to the Machine are you?

                Fuel: Bytes – unit of data that rushes through the Grid and power the Machine.

                Integrity: Coherency

                Powers: Drives/Upgrades

                                    This thread reminded me of an idea I had. This idea was inspired abit by Bayonetta, Devil May Cry, the Inferno Supplement to the New World of Darkness and abit of negative feelings from the concept of the God Machine (I just didn't like the idea of a malfunctioning Divine Calculator and demons/angels becoming technospies/reality hackers). I'm sorry if what I said was bad and if I post the idea anyways. Long sections shall be put into spoilers to make the post overall shorter and to avoid "TL;DR". Anyways, this is my first time here, I'm sorry if I post badly and it's nice to meet you all. :3

                                    Demon: The Infernal
                                    You were once a human. Walking around on earth, doing mundane things, basically living life as reality ordained. Sure, you did one or two bad things. But you’re ultimately human. It’s normal to kill several people or extort people over their lifetime, right? Or you did several bad things over the course of your life, but that’s normal too. You could’ve been an angel too if were good enough to rise to a higher state and you commanded the world to follow your leaders whims.
                                    But you’re not either of those things. Not anymore. When you fucked up enough, you were cast down from the cosmos or you got dragged from your rotting corpse and into the stinking Inferno, where the worst of humanity lies and the power rule over the strong. Here, you were changed, tortured, beaten and twisted completely at the whims of the denziens there. And the worst thing is, Inferno is a nigh inescapable prison for people like you, and from when you entered YOU became a citizen of Inferno. In other words, you’re a Demon. And your kind has existed since time immemorial as an example to what happens to evil folks and angels for what happens to good folks.
                                    But times change, and so do people and places. And because the concept of "Good get rewarded, evil get punished" is becoming borked, the Inferno is no longer an inescapable prison! Sure, a Demon or two has been called out because they left some part of their legend in the human world, but now they can leave without that restriction! You’d think this was bad, but what would it mean if you wanted to leave and redeem yourself? A broken prison is still a prison, and many of the power mad are glad to wallow in the maggot pile. Oh, and don’t think that the Angels are the absolute good guys. Humanity as a whole is beginning to lose faith in the higher, and lower, powers, and as such they are suffering for it. From the gates of Paradiso, the Angels fly and follow the orders of their leaders to bring the world back into their clutches as it was long ago. Those who don’t follow them to the letter tend to not do anything anymore.


                                    Mood: Temptation (Might need changing or heavy revision)
                                    You're a big bad demon now, there's not much worse that you can do, is there? You've been tortured for Creator knows how long, so it's fine to use the same stuff onto other people, right? All Demons have this temptation, the stigma that comes with being "the demon" to just act it out. Not to mention that you were once human, and if you reject that and embrace the torment then you might as well have not escaped at all.

                                    Theme: Liberation (Might also need changing, general idea is a mix of free will with personal responsibility)
                                    Free will has been a thing for humans, and even moreso for Demons who introduced it to the world. Most Demons seem to long for freedom. From the Inferno, from the rule of Paradiso, and some of the worse off ones from morality and constraint.

                                    Inherent Axis: Houses - Formed usually through how the once human committed the "crimes" that condemned them to Inferno and made part of them as a Demon.
                                    Consumer: The Devourers who go through people and all they know to gain their wants and needs.
                                    Controllers: The Defilers who wield themselves and their powers through force.
                                    Tempters: Fiends who know that knowledge is power and seek it at all costs.
                                    Twisters: The Malefactors who twist people and reality to their whims.
                                    Whisperers: The Devils who inspire cruelty and brother against brother.

                                    Outside Axis: Circles - How demons come together based on idealeology and how they deal with threats from Paradiso and keeping the worse demons sealed in Inferno.
                                    Limbo: The "Humanity F*** Yeah" faction. They tend to focus on human ingenuity and reason as a weapon against Angels.
                                    Heresy: The Community Faction. Seek to bring all of humanity together by destroying the imaginary boundaries that both humans and Angels have made to separate them.
                                    Violence: The Fighting Faction. Decided it's best to fight Hellfire with Hellfire, and they fight to destroy threats to Demons and throw the Hellbound back to Inferno.
                                    Fraud: The Knowledge Faction. They tend to hold onto demonic lore as well as acting as info brokers, spies and assassins, and even abit of witness protection for demons.
                                    Treachery: The Political Faction. They believe that the collective will and faith of humanity is powerful (it's what made Inferno into an impenetrable prison) and seek to wield it against their enemies.

                                    Supernatural Advantage: Infernal Rank
                                    Inferno is a realm where power rules over all, and those with the most power rule the most. Infernal Rank is the measure of power a Demon holds, how many Pacts and Thralls he can maintain at one time as well as how much Faith the Demon can hold. However, the higher it is, the more her otherworldly and ultimately defiling power shows through her mortal form. Did I also mention that Demons can be pulled back to Inferno if they're hostless? Those with higher Infernal Rank face more danger of that actually happening.

                                    Fuel Stat: Faith (Looking for a different name or something that isn't clearly so connected to Demon the Fallen)
                                    Faith is a powerful tool that only mortals are capable of creating, the trust in someone or something. It is the power of the Celestials and what gives them power....because humanity gives them that power. Because of human faith, they believe that natural disasters are divine punishment or curses and attribute them to gods and demons alike. It was the trust that "Evil is punished and good is rewarded" is the reason that Inferno is an impenetrable prison and Paradiso remains a paradise despite it's corrupt nature.

                                    Integrity: Grace (Need help on clarifying or what it should be like)
                                    Grace isn't beauty or tasteful clothing, it's a state of mind of how one is and how they are. To Demons, this means their sense of self-identity after the harrowing torments of Inferno labelling them as just "a criminal". High Grace makes it easier for Demons to remain in the Human World and interact, and even feel less of a tug from Inferno while hostless. Those with low Grace stick out like a sore thumb as beings who are steadily becoming like their tormentors. Grace 0 Demons are known as "Hellbound", those who seek to bring the Inferno to earth and make the world into a new Inferno.

                                    Virtue/Vice: Basically the same as a regular humans.

                                    Powers: Dominions. If Faith is what gives Demons the ability to move mountains, then it's Dominions that give them the actual oomph to do so. Dominions are what Angels lay their claim on reality over, and it's what Demons kept.

                                    Minor Templates:
                                    Nothing can ever get close to the corruption of Demonhood without becoming fully corrupted themselves. These two templates are human in every way, save that they have the backing of a supernaturally powerful spirit or aid it somehow.
                                    Thralls: People who serve the Demon and/or have the backing of. At the least, a thrall is someone who maintains a pact with the Demon, in which the person gains a benefit from the demon while the Demon gains Faith in return for upholding his end.
                                    Masters: Based abit off of Black Butler, the Masters are long term contractors of a Demon. Often, a Master has a sigil of the demon somewhere on his body and the demon must do anything the contractor says (and it cannot violate the pact created, so no self-destructive orders (which includes killing the master as much as it includes making the demon "kill" itself)) and in return when the time the servitude is completed or when the task the Master wants is done, the Demon recieves an extensive benefit (usually nomming up the Master's soul and claiming his body to possess).
                                    Hosts: Technically NOT a minor template. Hosts are empty bodies or souls that a Demon MUST possess if they want to stay in the Human World and not be tugged back to Inferno. The Demon can learn some of the hosts most significant memories, but it takes time to learn them all. Until then, some people intimate to the person might be able to catch something being off. Demons with Masters are capable of forming a body of their own without need of a Host.


                                    Antagonists
                                    Angels. Yes, the goody two shoes guys are the antagonists. Long ago in the dawn of creation ,the Creator and his Angels ruled humanity from the city of Paradiso. However, some Angels were not content with some of the human lack of will, which some saw as slavery. In their attempt to free humanity from this, Humanity realized it's own potential and had once overthrew Paradiso with the help of the Angels that did spark free will. Those still loyal to Paradiso took grave offense, and with the Creator gone after the attack, their enraged counterattack lead to them showing humanity what happens to those who defy Paradiso and the will of the Creator (whom they could TOTALLY be getting wrong). From this, Inferno was born and upkept by Faith, and the Angels believe that if they subjugate humanity underneath Paradiso, slay and send all Demons back to Inferno and ultimately perhaps even destroy Free Will, then the Creator would come back.

                                    They represent unchanging principle and order to the point of tyranny. They try to control the masses through religion (of all kinds to avoid it being singularly abrahmic) to inspire blind faith and bring them underneath Paradiso's banner. Given that today's people are less religious overall than in the past, this is why Faith is dwindling and why "Inferno isn't a prison anymore" as well as some parts of Paradiso falling apart is why they're perhaps more desperate and active as ever.

                                    If there's any reason for people to believe there being any difference between Angels and Demons, at the best, the differences are just superficial. Take away everything that makes an angel an "angel" and a demon a "demon" and they'd be seen as the same thing. As such, Angels and Demons both share and wield alot of the same traits listed above, although with a few different names on both ends. Celestial Rank instead of Infernal Rank. Servants, Beneficiaries and Honorables instead of Thralls, Masters and Hosts. I haven't worked out any factions the Angels have.

                                        I sorta have something like this. It started as a change of fluff to Geist: The Sin-Eaters, but as I kept working on it, it honestly became its own game line, albeit one that borrows 90% of its mechanics from another line. The fluff is so different, however, that I feel it's worth mentioning it here.

                                        I have an entire gdoc on it, and the thing is mostly completed (I'm just missing finishing up some homebrewed Keys I came up with). So let me just copypaste the important bits. I'm skipping the Morality system (Anamnesis) because it's based on 1e rules.
                                        Geist: The Defiance
                                        A game of wilful intervention and second chances.
                                        Introduction
                                        “I got him out, Jaye. I got him out. Same fire, every New Year’s Eve, always targets lone old men. Same one that took me. But this time, I changed the ending. I killed the bastard dead and the old man got to live.”
                                        The players play as geists, also known as the Bound. A geist is a special type of ghost that has the ability to possess its former body at will and give it a semblance of life. When a geist is in her body, it’s said she’s in Revenant form. When she’s outside it, she’s said to be in Wraith form.
                                        Geists are created when a person dies with a terribly important unfinished business (as normal ghosts), but their death had some sort of supernatural component or involvement, even indirectly. Having some sort of supernatural talent also counts (fully supernatural creatures like vampires, mages, werewolves and the like do not usually return as geists, as their souls are generally too damaged or ‘contaminated’ by otherworldly powers, but there is a specific Ceremony that breaks this rule).
                                        When three or more geists swear allegiance to each other through a special Ceremony, they are said to have formed a Pact.
                                        The Defiance
                                        The Defiance is a philosophy that most (but not all) geists share. Defiants believe that free will is paramount, and that an act of will can break the tyranny of fate, just as they themselves defied the ultimate equaliser. Defiants take a far more active (and meddlesome) role in their turf than other geists. Believing themselves to be warriors against the predetermined, they often seek to save lives and meddle in the affairs of the supernatural around them. Defiants can prove to be the thorn on the side of a vampire that thinks she has a stranglehold of a neighbourhood, or a werewolf pack terrorising a stretch of the countryside.
                                        Some Defiants seek to defy death at every turn, using special Ceremonies to foresee soon-to-occur deaths and prevent them. Many Defiants believe that in so doing, they are saving ‘innocents’ and striking back at whatever they hate. Others do so for the thrill of power.
                                        Particularly meddlesome Defiants will swiftly make enemies of creatures more powerful than themselves, such as mages that can destroy them with a thought, werewolves with spirits by their side, centuries-old vampires, frightening fae creatures, mortals trained in the art of taking down the supernatural and more. Geists in general can put up a fight, particularly when a Pact displays the necessary teamwork to coordinate their manifestations to full effect, but that’s not their true strength. What sets the Bound apart from all other creatures of the night is their stubborn refusal to die. A geist that seeks to take out a more powerful foe can do so through relentlessness, returning from the dead over and over until she finally grinds her adversary down. Defiants thrive in this fact, and many die with a laugh on their lips as they seek to unnerve and demoralise their opponents before their inevitable return.
                                        The philosophy of Defiance is an old one, dating back to the Renaissance or perhaps even further back, to the Dark Ages. Given that geists do not age or die of natural causes, they find it relatively easy to pass on traditions and philosophies from one generation to the next. Entire Pacts have been formed by Defiants to join forces against a common foe, and such an act is colloquially referred to by the Bound community as ‘taking a stance’.
                                        Counter-culture is always quick to spring up, and the philosophy that opposes Defiance also dates back at least a few centuries ago. The philosophy of Obedience is far rarer than its mainstream counterpart, but its adherents are still a prevalent minority in Bound society. Obedients hold that since every geist had a supernatural component in her death, and since that is by definition unnatural, then those deaths were never supposed to happen in the first place. Obedients believe that this defiance of death was, in fact, in accordance to fate (or the will of God/Allah/other divine beings) and therefore geists are the agents of fate’s will. Obedients will alternatively work with or oppose Defiants, depending on their goals. Most Obedients will join forces with Defiants to take out anything unnatural, but will also oppose them if they seek to tamper with what they perceive to be the ‘natural order of things’.
                                        Both Defiants and Obedients may join forces with mortals or other supernatural creatures if it aligns with their philosophical goals. For example, Obedients may join forces with Hunters to take out an ancient evil, while Defiants may join forces with Changelings to foil the plans of the True Fae.
                                        Geists that adhere to no philosophy are uncommon but not unheard of, are more concerned with their own individual Purpose than any greater philosophical matter, and many hold both Obedients and Defiants in contempt for their own personal reasons.
                                        Purpose
                                        All geists have a Purpose. This is the reason they refused passage towards the afterlife, and instead willed themselves to remain on Earth, defying death itself. Each Bound has her own individual Purpose, and fulfilling it becomes her mission in (un)life.
                                        A geist that fulfills her Purpose has two options: they can dissolve their tether to their Anchor(s) and “pass on” to the afterlife, or they can attain a new Purpose. Even if one of the Bound is willing to pass on, it still requires an act of will to dissolve her connection to her Anchor. She must roll Resolve + Anamnesis as an extended action (rolling once per hour) and obtain as many total successes as her Psyche. She is allowed to spend Willpower on these rolls, but she may not augment her dice pool in any other way.
                                        If a geist has more than one Anchor, she must do this extended roll for each and every one of her Anchors. Willingly dissolving her connection to her Anchor does not cause the Bound to take Aggravated damage (see Anchors section). However, when she finishes dissolving her connection to the last of her Anchors, she passes on to the afterlife. Once the geist begins the process of dissolving her ties to her Anchors, she cannot stop.
                                        When she passes on, her body drops dead on the spot, and the passage of time from the moment she originally died to the present accrue in a matter of seconds, aging the corpse correspondingly.
                                        Attaining a new Purpose is a reflexive action that requires a successful roll with her Anamnesis dots as her dice pool. This dice pool may not be augmented in any way and may only be attempted once per day.
                                        A geist that resolves her Purpose need not choose immediately. She may elect to take a few days to “leave her affairs in order” before choosing to dissolve her connections to her Anchors, or consult with sources of guidance before resolving to attain a new Purpose. However, every day that a geist goes by without a Purpose, she takes one point of Aggravated damage at dawn and is unable to hold Plasm within her body. This damage may not be healed until the geist attains a new Purpose.
                                        Anchor
                                        The Bound begin play with one Anchor. An Anchor can take the form of a place, object or person, and binds the geist to the world of the living. A geist is not damaged if her Anchor is, until the Anchor is completely destroyed. Every day that one of the Bound goes without an Anchor, she takes one point of Aggravated damage at dawn. This damage cannot be healed by any means and is cumulative, which gives geists anywhere from 5 days to two weeks (depending on their Health) to bind themselves to a new Anchor.
                                        The process of binding to an Anchor is a Ceremony that every geist begins knowing for free (see Anchor the Soul). Performing the Ceremony varies for every geist. It’s a unique process that reflects some aspect of the geist’s personality and/or past.
                                        As the Bound gains Psyche, she increases the amount of Anchors she needs. Every Anchor that is destroyed inflicts the point of Aggravated damage per day, so having multiple Anchors destroyed at once can be particularly devastating for a high-Psyche geist.
                                        A geist must remain within 10 miles per dot of Psyche from all their Anchors. Failure to do so inflicts 1 point of Aggravated damage at dawn for each day she remains too far from her Anchor.
                                        A geist with multiple Anchors may move between two of them while in Wraith form by spending a point of Willpower. She must be within 1 yard per point of Psyche of one of her Anchors in order to do so. She must still respect her Wraith form limitations.
                                        Geists may not bind themselves to new Anchors if they are already at their maximum for their Psyche. If they gain Psyche and it requires them to bind with a new Anchor, they must perform the Anchor the Soul Ceremony before the sun next rises or they begin to take Aggravated damage.


                                        Geist Physiology
                                        Geists have a +4 bonus to any rolls related to entering places of death (graveyards, mausoleums, morgues, murder scenes, places of recent/frequent death, etc.). This bonus applies to rolls of diverse nature, from picking a lock to breaking down a door to pushing through a defensive ward. Geists belong in places of death, and keeping one out of them is a difficult task indeed.
                                        Revenant Form
                                        Lifelike Appearance
                                        While in their Revenant form, geists are undead beings that can easily pretend to be alive. So long as the Bound has at least 1 point of Plasm in reserve, she looks alive and fools anything short of supernatural scrutiny. Even in this lifelike state, geists do not age or die of natural causes.
                                        She can still be poisoned, drugged, irradiated or fall ill, but she gains a +2 to all rolls to overcome those afflictions (the Stricken gain a +5 instead). She can still starve or die of thirst, but she gains a +2 to all rolls to overcome those afflictions (the Silent gain a +5 instead). She can still suffer exposure or the ill effects of weather and temperature variations, but she gains a +2 to all rolls to overcome those afflictions (the Prey gain a +5 instead). She can still suffer the damage of accidental physical impact (such as falling, explosives or objects crashing against her), but she gains a +2 to all rolls to resist or evade them (the Forgotten gain a +5 instead). She can still be deliberately harmed, but the Torn do not suffer wound penalties.
                                        Geists with Plasm in their reserves may spend it to ablate wounds as explained in the core book rules.
                                        True Mien
                                        When a geist runs out of Plasm in her Revenant form, she loses her lifelike appearance, revealing her true mien. She looks clearly undead, cold and lacking any vital signs, bearing the wounds or signs of whatever killed her (though the Phantasmal Key can disguise this). When this happens, however, she gains beneficial undead traits that increase her durability. She does not take wound penalties and is immune to suffocation, thirst, starvation, illness, radiation, pressure, vacuum, exposure, temperature variations (as long as they stay outside extreme temperatures), unconsciousness, and any form of mundane drugs or poison. She also takes Bashing damage from all weaponry (including firearms) and from physical impacts (such as falling, objects crashing against her, explosives, etc.). Extreme heat (including fire) and extreme cold deal Lethal damage as normal, as does any superheated or supercooled material.
                                        A geist with no Plasm in their reservoir may reflexively spend a point of Willpower to retain or regain their lifelike appearance for a scene. Doing this does not grant them the resilience of their undead form but allows them to hide their true mien in a pinch. Once this expenditure has been made, the Bound cannot lose the lifelike appearance until the scene ends.
                                        If one of the Bound was afflicted with a condition that they would be immune to if they ran out of Plasm (such as an illness or drug), it is cleansed from their bodies when they do so. If they later acquired Plasm or spent Willpower to regain their lifelike appearance, that condition would be gone.
                                        Whether by an ancient oath or by the very nature of their power, a Bound’s role in the world is to act unseen, save against those that matter to them personally. Geists that allow mortals to witness their true mien receive a -5 to all dice pools when attempting to affect that mortal with Manifestations or other supernatural effects. However, if the mortal is someone that is part of their Purpose (such as “the man that killed me” or “the child I swore to protect”), that penalty becomes a +3 bonus instead. This penalty/bonus lasts forever, unless someone finds a way to make the mortal forget what they saw. Being seen in their true mien by more than one person imposes a -1 to all rolls to unlock Manifestations, perform Ceremonies or do anything supernatural. This stacks per additional person, up to a -5. This penalty also stacks with the previous bonus/penalty, if applicable, and lasts for as long as the mortals are observing the geist’s true mien.
                                        Wraith Form
                                        Geists in their Wraith form do not exist in the material plane, but in Twilight, like ordinary ghosts. In that form, they are immune to all forms of damage, save for supernatural attacks that deal Aggravated damage (and only from attackers who can affect them in Twilight). However, Bound in Wraith form may manifest briefly if unlocking a Manifestation (or altering/ending its effects) requires them to make temporary physical contact with the world. Some ceremonies may (or must) be performed in Wraith form. If they require making physical contact with the world, the geist manifests briefly to do so.
                                        There is a Ceremony that allows a geist in Wraith form to manifest like a ghost.
                                        Geists in their Wraith form use their Psyche as extra Health. This takes the form of a visual effect around the Bound that fades as they take damage (some examples include an armour of shining steel, a sphere of fire, a swirl of rose petals or snowflakes, a ring of spinning blades, etc.). This extra Health lasts for as long as the Bound remains in Wraith form and, when spent to absorb damage, refreshes after a good night’s sleep.
                                        A geist in Wraith form cannot move further than 5 yards per point of Psyche from their corpse (though there is a Ceremony that affects this).
                                        A geist can only spend up to 1 hour per point of Psyche in Wraith form (though there is a Ceremony that affects this). Once her time’s up, she’s shunted back into her body.
                                        Switching Forms
                                        A geist may switch between Revenant and Wraith forms as an instant action. When one of the Bound shifts into Wraith form, her body goes unconscious. If her Plasm reserve is empty and she has not spent Willpower to make herself look lifelike, instead of going unconscious, the body simply collapses on the spot, looking like a fresh cadaver to any onlookers. A geist in Wraith form always looks freshly killed to any who can see into Twilight and is considered to be in her true mien automatically (though the Phantasmal Key can disguise this).
                                        Damage dealt to a geist’s body while she’s in Wraith form does not affect her until she returns to Revenant form. When she does, the damage that the body suffered is added to any Aggravated damage she may have taken while in Wraith form.
                                        Death Weakness
                                        All of the Bound have a specific source of Aggravated damage that is unique to each of them. The specific nature of their death is their weakness. If an enemy can emulate the way the geist died (attacking her with the same type of weapon that killed her, for example), they are able to inflict Aggravated damage with it. Geists that died in a non-weaponizable way (exposure, illness, starvation, accident) instead allow enemies to roll Manipulation + either Persuasion or Expression - the geist’s Composure as an instant action in order to revive the Bound’s moment of death with their words and deal Aggravated damage.
                                        This specific weakness deals Aggravated damage whether it’s inflicted to the geist on her Revenant or Wraith forms (if the enemy can see into Twilight, she may use a mundane weapon that does not affect ghosts, such as a common revolver, in order to deal Aggravated damage to one of the Bound in Wraith form, provided that weapon is the same type that killed her), but a geist can only be affected by her Death Weakness once per turn at most.
                                        Resonance Weakness
                                        Geists are vulnerable to blessed weaponry, just like ghosts are, but they are also vulnerable to particularly heinous, cursed artefacts. However, the Shroud manifestation protects them from such attacks, even if the blessing or curse says that it bypasses supernatural protections. The Shroud also protects against enchanted or ensorcelled weapons, even those designed to bypass supernatural defences. Only weapons crafted with death resonances designed to pierce the Shroud can ignore a Shroud’s protection.
                                        Death
                                        Geists do not die as easily as other creatures, and instead defy death as a second nature. When one of the Bound would die but remains tied to at least one Anchor, she is instead revived at the following dawn with a caul over her face, and an innocent has died in her place (as explained in the corebook, the innocent dies in a manner that falls within the geist’s Threshold, and the first thing that the Bound sees when she returns to life is the last few moments of that person’s life). This event is an sin against Anamnesis 6. The geist returns in Revenant form regardless of what form she was in when she died. If the body was completely destroyed, she reappears in the place where she originally died the very first time.
                                        If she was killed in the same way that she originally died, she suffers a major derangement (chosen between the Storyteller and the player) for one day per dot of Anamnesis below 7 (if the Bound has Anamnesis 7 or higher, the derangement still lasts for one day).
                                        If a geist is killed after having her Anchors destroyed (but she did not willingly choose to dissolve her connection to them), she does not return to life, and does not pass into the afterlife either. This event is referred to as vanquishing. A vanquished Bound becomes a faint echo in Twilight trapped in the area surrounding her, only audible by ghosts and other geists in Wraith form, and the Bound can only do so after succeeding a Wits + Psyche roll (attemptable only once per hour). If a Bound can hear this echo, she can only just barely communicate with the vanquished geist, though she must repeat her Wits + Psyche every so often (Storyteller’s discretion) to ensure she can properly hear what the echo wishes to tell her.
                                        A vanquished geist cannot use any of her powers, and influencing the world around her is nigh-impossible. She may attempt an extended roll of Presence + Psyche, Intelligence + Psyche or Strength + Psyche (whichever dice pool is highest) once per day (rolling once every hour) in order to cause slight supernatural manifestations in her surroundings (which depend on her personality and her current location). It takes at least 5 successes per day for a normal mortal to begin to suspect that their surroundings might be haunted. A mortal aware of the supernatural requires only 3 successes per day, and a fully supernatural creature requires only 1 success per day.
                                        If her surroundings are deserted, a vanquished geist may draw nearby people to her with an extended roll of Manipulation + Psyche, Dexterity + Psyche or Wits + Psyche (whichever dice pool is highest) once per day. She requires 1 success per mile away from her location to the nearest person. People that feel drawn to the area do not immediately drop what they’re doing and go; they may ignore the calling for several days, and the vanquished geist can only apply more pressure to the calling if she exceeds the previous day’s successes. If the vanquished geist exceeds the number of successes she needs by 5 or more, the calling becomes irresistible, and the person in question usually travels to the location within the next day. People summoned this way often stay around for a couple of days, which gives a vanquished geist a chance to alert them of her presence. This calling prioritises ordinary mortals over supernatural creatures.
                                        Once a person is aware of the vanquished geist’s presence, the Bound may roll Manipulation + Psyche in order to influence the person towards a certain course of action. This action needs to be simple and direct, such as ‘conduct a seance in this location’ or ‘get Rhea Wyatt to this place’. Vanquished geists are able to communicate with ghosts clearly, which gives them a potential ally.
                                        If a geist at Anamnesis 0 is vanquished, she does not leave an echo behind. To do so, she requires a sense of self, which the Wretched have lost completely. Vanquished Wretched are lost to oblivion, and most geists fear this fate more than anything else.
                                        The same Ceremony that can make a geist out of a fully supernatural creature also allows echoes of vanquished geists to return (see Ceremonies below). A vanquished geist that is returned to unlife via this ceremony begins at Psyche 1 and their previous Anamnesis or Anamnesis 5, whichever is lower.

                                        Shades are memories given a necrotic semblance of life. As a geist grows in power, so do their memories. A shade is an echo of a person important to the geist, that haunts them in their dreams and whenever they take Wraith form. Shades are not accurate in the slightest: they are distorted by the geist’s feelings towards that person, and by the deleterious effects of memory. Shades are uncontrollable, but they’re not actual people. Being slivers of the geist’s subconscious, they do not have minds or souls of their own and are therefore immune to any effects, supernatural or otherwise, that can affect people, spirits or creatures. Thankfully, shades give their Bound a reprieve while she’s in Revenant form.
                                        Gaining Plasm
                                        A geist may obtain Plasm in five ways:
                                        1) From Haunts, as described in the rulebook.
                                        2) Once per week, one of the Bound may regain anywhere from 1 to 10 Plasm (Storyteller’s discretion) from making progress towards her Purpose. Significant acts of Defiance or Obedience may also count instead, if the geist follows these philosophies.
                                        3) When a ghost or geist relinquishes their ties to their Anchors and passes on to the afterlife, they release a surge of Plasm that invigorates all nearby Bound. This completely fills a Bound’s Plasm reserves.
                                        4) Once per day, saving the life of an ordinary mortal (unaware of the supernatural) that would have died in the same manner as the geist’s Threshold, at a significant risk for the Bound, may refill a percentage of the geist’s Plasm reserves, from 10% to 100% (Storyteller’s discretion, should be proportional to effort involved). This does not trigger if the geist’s actions save lives indirectly. The Bound only gains this bonus if she directly intervenes at the moment of impending doom and saves that life through a significant effort.
                                        5) Each conclave has a specific form of regaining plasm (see Factions below).
                                        Factions
                                        Given that geists can be around for a long time if they find reasons to keep on (un)living, they have established over the centuries an eclectic form of society throughout the world. Factions within the Bound community are called conclaves, from their ancient custom of holding meetings behind locked doors.
                                        The Gravestone Conclave
                                        The oldest conclave still in existence, supposedly dating back to times before the Dark Ages, the Gravestone Conclave remains a pillar of geist society and the neutral faction to which others defer judgement to. Members of the Gravestone Conclave are called magistrates (though less-friendly appellations also exist), and they are instructed in the arts of diplomacy, patience and equanimity. Moderate to a fault, they are often criticised for being too slow to act, for apparent cowardice, and for not upholding the purpose of geists as agents of action. However, magistrates are quick to point out that they have acted at the times it counted and that they have remained a hale faction through the centuries for a reason.
                                        Their power in Bound society derives mostly from their financial and political wealth, rather than through numbers or combat prowess, though they are renown for their mastery over locations. Magistrates are usually aware of their surroundings and adept at channeling their powers far and wide. It is practically unheard of for a magistrate to lack a Haunt, for example. Magistrates tend to keep an eye on and control (or own) graveyards and other places of death. Given their financial prowess, ownership of land comes second nature to them.
                                        The conclave’s structure is hierarchical and bureaucratic, with each city or region having a High Magistrate in charge, reporting to a national Gray Magistrate, who in turn reports to one of the twelve Supreme Magistrates that form the Gravestone Council. The Gravestone Council arranges yearly meetings to debate the latest news and chart the future course of the conclave, which they then pass on to the Gray Magistrates for implementation.
                                        Admittance into the Gravestone Council involves a formal hearing with the local High Magistrate, who elaborates a report on the candidate and elevates it to the Gray Magistrate she answers to. If the Gray Magistrate agrees with the High Magistrate’s conclusion to admit the candidate in, she sends back her approval and the High Magistrate in question arranges a formal ceremony to welcome the new magistrate into the conclave. All promotions are decided exclusively by the Gravestone Council, though they take reports from other magistrates into account.
                                        Magistrates regain 1 to 5 Plasm when they align their virtue or vice to the conclave’s teachings in some significant way (for example, a Temperate magistrate negotiating a truce between two Pacts ready to tear each other apart, or a Prideful magistrate successfully arbitrates a difficult decision after claiming she was the only one capable of finding the right answer).
                                        Magistrates have a +1 bonus on Politics, Investigation, Persuasion and Academics.
                                        Magistrates may purchase dots in the Boneyard manifestation for New Dots x 5 rather than New Dots x 6.
                                        Crossovers: Supernatural creatures affiliated with this conclave include vampires and mages.
                                        The Conclave of Aether
                                        A somewhat old conclave arisen in Victorian times, the Conclave of Aether gathers the thinking minds of Bound society. More like a loosely affiliated club than a true power, the Conclave of Aether is more concerned with answering the age-old questions about death and discovering new forms of geist power than resolving mundane matters. Emulating scientific society, it has no hierarchy or leadership, and instead maintains libraries, laboratories and vaults around the world, as well as the Ethereal Network, a form of communication among members (which currently takes the form of an encrypted internet forum), where they share their findings away from prying eyes. Their power in Bound society derives mostly from their arcane lore, rare supernatural artefacts and information network. There’s little that this Conclave does not know.
                                        Members of the Conclave of Aether call themselves seekers and generally have periodic local gatherings in order to exchange useful information. Not all seekers are aloof and focused only on experimentation and abstract theories. Many seekers are active investigators, riddled with curiosity about their surroundings. The conclave as a whole has no agenda, and seekers are instead allowed to pursue their interests as they see fit. Admittance is decided by majority among the local seekers in an area. Though custom dictates the candidate present the conclave with some finding of interest, this is not strictly observed any longer. So long as her fellow seekers find her a worthy addition, a geist finds little impediment in gaining membership on this conclave. As such, there is no formal ceremony to welcome new seekers into the fold.
                                        Seekers regain 1 to 5 Plasm when they align their virtue or vice with the pursuit of truth in some significant way (for example, a Charitable seeker discovering a new use for her manifestation that allows her to aid the underprivileged in her neighbourhood, or an Envious seeker finding extremely important dirt on her enemy).
                                        Seekers have a +1 bonus in all Occult, Computer, Medicine and Science rolls.
                                        Seekers may purchase dots in the Oracle manifestation for New Dots x 5 rather than New Dots x 6.
                                        Crossovers: Supernatural creatures affiliated with this conclave include changelings and mages.
                                        The Sanguine Conclave
                                        The origins of the Sanguine Conclave are a matter of great debate in the Bound community. It’s not a new conclave by any means, but no one can truly confirm how old it is. The Sanguine Conclave exerts a strong influence in geist society, wielding a considerable amount of power. Its members call themselves reapers and see themselves as the armed forces of the Bound community. The history of the Sanguine Conclave is filled with attempts to establish hierarchies and ranks and to later standarise these ranks, as each country had its own take on them.
                                        Currently, reapers function as independent soldiers within a Pact, which report to a superior, or as Pacts of mostly reapers under the command of the most senior geist. The Sanguine Conclave has no ultimate authority outside a country. Each country has a hierarchy of reapers based on seniority, with the oldest reaper at the head. The ranks vary greatly from country to country, and even within states or provinces. One state may boast a proud military tradition and adopt the military rank, while another may adopt the ranks of the air force or the navy. Some areas have a blend of ranks from different parts of the armed forces, which causes reapers in other locations no end of frustration. Some entrepreneurial reapers have instead decided to fabricate a new hierarchy entirely, with the ambitious goal of standarising it globally.
                                        Admittance into the Sanguine Conclave is quite easy, and most reapers are out there recruiting. Reapers tend to be a tightly knit conclave where loyalty is paramount; and a reaper is always glad to train a new member in diverse forms of combat or help them develop their powers. Welcoming ceremonies vary from place to place. Reapers, unlike most other conclaves, remain in touch quite often, and they have a strong sense of tit for tat when it comes to reinforcements. A reaper may count with the aid of her brothers in arms, but she will be expected to drop whatever she’s doing the moment a fellow reaper needs her aid.
                                        Reapers regain 1 to 5 Plasm whenever their vice or virtue shines through in a difficult battle (for example, a Just reaper taking on a gang that has been terrorising her neighbourhood, or a Wrathful reaper letting her temper get the best of her and starting a fight with a powerful werewolf).
                                        Reapers have a +1 bonus in all Firearms, Weaponry, Brawl and Athletics rolls.
                                        Reapers may purchase dots in the Rage manifestation for New Dots x 5 rather than New Dots x 6.
                                        Crossovers: Supernatural creatures affiliated with this conclave include werewolves and hunters.
                                        The Conclave of the Unbroken Shield
                                        Dating back to the late Renaissance, the Conclave of the Unbroken Shield is a group that prefers to remain invisible until it is time to intervene. Its members call themselves wardens and have taken upon themselves the task of seeking out and exterminating large-scale threats to humanity. The conclave does not have a hierarchy, as it considers all members equal. Wardens are allowed to do as they please with their time, so long as they keep a watchful eye. When a member of the conclave spots something grave, it contacts all nearby wardens in the surrounding areas, and they organise a reconnaissance together. If a majority of the wardens agree that the threat qualifies as large-scale, they allow each warden to marshall whatever allies they believe they can trust (and they are expected to answer for their allies’ actions), and then they organise a swift and decisive strike.
                                        The shape of the threat may be anything from an ancient vampire to a mass-murdering werewolf to an abyssal horror to things so antithetical to life that they have no name. Wardens are generally a serious bunch, who value efficiency, patience and decisive action. Admittance to the conclave is not easy, as wardens are secretive and slow to trust. Candidates require a warden to vouch for them, and then they must earn the approval of at least the majority of the wardens in the region (what exactly constitutes as a region varies from country to country). They have no welcoming ceremony. Wardens rarely contact each other unless it’s an “official” matter.
                                        The Conclave of the Unbroken Shield holds significant power in the Bound community, though they are often underestimated. Though their numbers and combat training are inferior to the Sanguine Conclave’s, they excel in the area of strategy, and their prowess with defensive manifestations is second to none. Wardens often come into conflict with the Conclave of Aether, who seeks to treasure rare supernatural artefacts that the Conclave of the Unbroken Shield would rather see destroyed.
                                        Wardens gain 1 to 5 Plasm whenever their vice or virtue shines through in the protection of others (for example, a Fortitudinous warden enduring torture to buy time for her companions, or a Greedy warden committing some grand theft in order to draw attention to herself and provide a distraction for her allies).
                                        Wardens have a +1 bonus to Resolve, Composure and Stamina rolls made to resist supernatural effects.
                                        Wardens may purchase dots in the Shroud manifestation for New Dots x 5 rather than New Dots x 6.
                                        Crossovers: Supernatural creatures affiliated with this conclave include hunters and changelings.
                                        The Conclave of Second Chances
                                        The Conclave of Second Chances is a relatively modern group, dating back merely decades ago, to the sixties. Generally disregarded by older conclaves, it has been steadily increasing in numbers, until it currently stands as the most populated conclave in Bound society. Its members call themselves celebrants, and seek to use the second chance they have been given to make the most out of their (un)life. Much like the Conclave of Aether, it resembles more a loosely affiliated club than a proper organisation, and most of the other conclaves resent the power they wield in the Bound community simply for having such a plentiful membership.
                                        Admittance into the conclave is extremely easy and has no requirements. Celebrants throw lively parties to welcome any new member and they are rather open to outsiders. The Conclave of Second Chances believes in encouraging geists to avoid making the same mistakes they made in life and, in due time, to embrace the afterlife without regrets and a smile on one’s face. Celebrants can be travellers, hedonists, socialites or all of the above, and they often throw themselves fully at what they do, attempting to live without regrets and treasuring every joyful moment in their memory.
                                        Socially adroit and never shying away from trying new things, celebrants specialise in manipulation, and though other conclaves consider them unskilled, their ability to control and influence is unmatched. The joie de vivre that pulses inside a celebrant focuses their will, and their constant connection with their own feelings and desires allows them to sharpen their intentions. Combining both will and intention provides the celebrants with a strong force of personality.
                                        Celebrants gain 1 to 5 Plasm whenever their vice or virtue shines through a moment of important celebration (for example, a Hopeful celebrant throwing a party to lift her Pact’s spirits after a failure, or a Gluttonous celebrant indulging in a feast after taking out a great enemy).
                                        Celebrants have a +1 bonus on Empathy, Socialise, Expression and Intimidation rolls.
                                        Celebrants may purchase dots in the Marionette manifestation for New Dots x 5 rather than New Dots x 6.
                                        Crossovers: Supernatural creatures affiliated with this conclave include vampires and changelings.
                                        The Conclave of Life/Death’s Due
                                        The Conclave of Life (or the Conclave of Death’s Due, as it is also commonly known) is quite possibly one of the oldest of all conclaves, according to most sources, but is by far the least liked. While other conclaves are in general willing to work together for a common goal, the Conclave of Death’s Due is quite happy to scorn politics and demand a steep compensation for its services. Made up of the malcontent, the rebels, the spiteful and the outcasts, it’s a Conclave that lacks any substantial amount of goodwill towards humanity or their fellow geists.
                                        Its members used to call themselves advocates in olden times (and some still do), but they have been called bonepickers for long enough that most of its members have started adopting the moniker “ironically.” Admittance into the conclave is easy. All one must do is some act of sabotage or rebellion towards the other conclaves. The more spiteful and petty, the better. Welcome ceremonies are usually common and involve revelling in the mayhem caused.
                                        Bonepickers are disdained in Bound society, but their services are often worth bargaining for. The Conclave of Life is a very tightly-knit community and its members tend to find important items, information or supernatural power in their hands. While some advocates are too proud or stubborn to negotiate, most enjoy the power such bargain chips give them over the other conclaves.
                                        Bonepickers gain 1 to 5 Plasm whenever their vice or virtue shines through in a moment of spite (for example, a Faithful bonepicker avenging a minor sacrilege, or a Slothful advocate choosing to stay at home and watch TV when she knows someone she dislikes will be in trouble).
                                        Bonepickers have a +1 bonus on Wits, Dexterity or Manipulation rolls to acquire things they shouldn’t (such as information, items, lore, etc.).
                                        Advocates may purchase dots in the Curse manifestation for New Dots x 5 rather than New Dots x 6.
                                        Crossovers: Supernatural creatures affiliated with this conclave include werewolves, Prometheans and hunters.
                                        The Veiled Conclave
                                        Nobody truly knows when the Veiled Conclave was originally founded, but the most reputable sources place the first sign of their activity around the first World War, though they didn’t truly become a force to be reckoned with until the Cold War. Members of the Veiled Conclave call themselves gatekeepers, and they take upon themselves to perform the shadier actions that the Bound society needs done.
                                        Someone finds out confidential information for the Conclave of Aether. Someone watches the shadows while the celebrants enjoy themselves. Someone takes out a target that the Sanguine Conclave can’t assault directly. Someone tips the wardens off of a particular danger they miss. Someone keeps the Gravestone Conclave informed of the latest events in the Bound community. Someone bargains with the bonepickers. The gatekeepers do all this and more. Their power comes from how necessary they are for geist society to function as smoothly as it does, but it’s a fragile thing. While the Veiled Conclave may command power if it acts as a coordinated group, the opinion of individual gatekeepers does not hold much weight when compared to any of the other conclaves.
                                        To say that they have eyes and ears everywhere is a gross overstatement. The efficiency of their communication networks surpasses the seekers’, yes, but their numbers are far smaller than they’d like to admit. Gaining membership into the Veiled Conclave is almost as hard as getting into the Conclave of the Unbroken Shield, though the process is similar. A current gatekeeper in good standing must vouch for any new candidate, and she needs the full approval of all gatekeepers in the region to gain entrance into the conclave. Once she has been admitted, new gatekeepers are treated to a small welcome party. Gatekeepers may seem callous to outsiders, but they are quite different among each other or towards those they hold dear.
                                        Gatekeepers are spies, infiltrators, assassins, thieves, reconnaissance experts and information brokers. They are particularly good at achieving their goals without being seen and to cause problems without the cause being traced back to them. Their conclave-wide goals are murky, as they have no official leadership (gatekeepers defer to each other according to how powerful they are, and power in the Veiled Conclave can take many forms). Gatekeepers pursue their regular agendas, until it is time to act in the conclave’s name. The conclave has one motto, which every gatekeeper holds close to heart. “Do what they cannot do.” Of course, interpretation of this philosophy is varied, and is the main source of intra-group conflict for the conclave.
                                        Gatekeepers gain 1 to 5 Plasm whenever they express their vice or virtue through stealth or subterfuge, and in the pursuit of what they consider to be their duty (for example, a Prudent gatekeeper convincing her Pact to follow a more stealthy plan when engaging a mission, or a Lustful gatekeeper seducing a target in order to pry valuable secrets).
                                        Gatekeepers gain a +1 bonus in all Stealth, Larceny, Streetwise and Subterfuge rolls.
                                        Gatekeepers may purchase dots in the Caul manifestation for New Dots x 5 rather than New Dots x 6.
                                        Crossovers: Supernatural creatures affiliated with this conclave do not have a clear majority. All major types of supernatural creatures can be found in roughly equal measure.

                                            Keys and Manifestations
                                            General:
                                            Geists need to be in a specific form in order to use some manifestations.
                                            Wraith-form-only: Marionette and Curse.
                                            Revenant-form-only: Boneyard, Caul and Oracle.
                                            Either form: Rage and Shroud.
                                            When a Geist unlocks a Manifestation in Wraith-form, she becomes visible (but still intangible) until the beginning of her next turn.
                                            The Stygian Key and the Pit Manifestation will not be in use.
                                            Manifestations:
                                            Activating an Elemental Oracle manifestation is a sin against Anamnesis 9 even if it doesn’t deal Aggravated damage.
                                            When a Manifestation grants resistance or immunity to a certain type of damage (for example, the Pyre-Flame Shroud granting immunity to natural fire or the Industrial Shroud granting additional armour vs. man-made weapons), it does not apply if the Bound was killed that way. The weakness of a geist to whatever killed them bypasses all forms of protection save for one specific Ceremony.
                                            The Passion Caul has a slight change of flavour and mechanics. The geist changes his body to become more attractive, more imposing or more terrifying. He also becomes supernaturally good at dealing with people. The Passion Caul affects Presence instead of Intelligence at * and Social skills instead of Mental at **. At *****, the Passion Caul is reflavoured as giving the geist complete self-assurance and confidence.
                                            The Oracle Manifestation allows the geist to observe supernatural phenomena normally hidden from sight (like Mage Sight or Auspex), even at only one dot.
                                            Stigmata Oracle ****: Allows the geist to find the locations of imminent fatal bloodshed (within the next hour) using the same rules. Stigmata Oracle *****: The geist may project his soul into the Shadow by using the same roll.
                                            A geist cannot unlock the Rage manifestation on a corporeal target while she’s in Wraith form unless she’s manifesting or another power allows her to do so.
                                            Keys:
                                            Geists begin with one Key granted by their Threshold and then one Key that either fits their personality or reflects an important aspect of their death.
                                            Ceremonies
                                            Eliminations:
                                            Krewe Binding. Dedicate Charm. Fetter’s Binding. Sepulchral Gateway.
                                            Changes:
                                            All ceremonies may be altered flavour-wise so long as the mechanics are respected and the difficulty of the ceremony is maintained.
                                            Pass On: Instead of the Underworld, the ceremony must be performed in Twilight (by geists in Wraith form). May be used on geists. If they do so, the amount of successes are added to the geist’s Resolve + Anamnesis pool when dissolving Anchors. A geist can only benefit from one Pass On ceremony at a time (a Bound may attempt to redo the Ceremony if they want to grant a bigger bonus).
                                            Distant Voices, Listening to the Spectral Howl and Dead Voices on Air: All of these allows for reliable communication with vanquished geists.
                                            Plumbing the Depths: This is actually a moment of memory and self-introspection.
                                            Warding Circle and Warding the Household: Affects geists as well as ghosts.
                                            Bind Anchor: Does not affect geists. Instead, see Anchor the Soul.
                                            Create Deathly Passage: Still requires at least 4 participants, but only requires 2 different Thresholds (retains the bonus for additional Thresholds). Instead of creating a gate to the Underworld, it opens a passage to any currently uninhabited location within 2 miles per Psyche dot of the ritualist. The location needs to be associated with fresh death. A mortal must either have died 24 hours before, or be fated to die within the next 24 hours. The passage remains open for 1 hour per Psyche dot of the ritualist. Any number of people may pass through the passage while it remains open, but the passage closes if any non-geist lays eyes on its entrance or exit (blindfolded passage is recommended for non-geists). The ritualist chooses whether to open the gate to a valid location of his choosing or to open it to the nearest location within range that meets the requisites.
                                            Dumb Supper: Affects geists in Wraith form.
                                            Wings of the Moth: Affects vanquished geists. Also affects geists in Wraith form, though it’s rather wasteful/useless to do so outside extremely niche circumstances.
                                            Additions:

                                            Convoking the Bound (*): This ceremony sits at the heart of geist culture. It allows a group of Bound to found a new Pact and a geist to join an existing Pact. Dice Pool: Psyche (or Psyche + Presence). Action: Extended (target number of 6 to found a Pact or 3 to join an existing Pact). Time Increment: Once every 10 minutes if founding a Pact or once a turn if joining a Pact. Dramatic Failure: All successes are lost, and the rite is tainted. The geist can never join the particular Pact. Geists attempting to found a Pact must add, subtract, or replace a member before they can perform the ceremony again. Failure: No successes are gained. Success: The geists successfully found a Pact or the Bound successfully joins the Pact. See Founding a Pact for further information. Exceptional Success: No additional benefit. Suggested Modifiers: The geists are performing the ceremony in a location associated with new beginnings (+1). One or more of the individuals joining feels coerced into joining (-2). A geist joins or attempts to found a Pact for the express purpose of harming or betraying it (-5).
                                            Quiet Memory’s Echoes (***): This ceremony allows for a Bound to appease the shades that distract her while in Wraith form. To do so, the geist must spend some time alone while in Wraith form speaking to her shades, addressing each in turn and telling them what she thinks they want to hear. Dice Pool: Psyche + Manipulation. Action: Extended (4 successes). Time Increment: Once per 10 minutes. Dramatic Failure: All successes are lost. The shades grow restless. Their penalties are doubled for a day, and this ceremony may not be performed during that time. Failure: The shades are not listening. Success: Successes are gained. Once the target number has been met, the geist gains reprieve from the shades’ distracting effect. She does not take the penalty associated with shades for a full 24 hours since she finishes the Ceremony. Exceptional Success: Successes are gained. If the target number has been met, she doubles the duration of her reprieve. Suggested Modifiers: The geist is at peace with all her shades (+2). The geist is in a calm state of mind (+1). The geist has not made peace with most of her shades (-2). The geist is upset in some way (-2). The geist’s relationship with her shades while she was alive was particularly turbulent (-3).
                                            Fate’s True Defiance (*****): This ceremony allows for a Bound to make a geist out of a willing deceased person, including fully supernatural creatures (such as a ghost, werewolf, vampire, mage, etc.) and to re-Anchor and regenerate a vanquished geist. It requires the deceased’s corpse (not necessary for a vanquished geist) and the presence of a place, object or person to serve as a new Anchor. The prospective Anchor must be somehow related to the target. The ritual involves whispered callings to the deceased and drawing symbols with the geist’s own Plasm (spending 1 point per roll) over the prospective Anchor and, if present, the corpse. Dice Pool: Psyche + Resolve. Action: Extended (10 successes). Time Increment: Once per hour. Dramatic Failure: All successes are lost. The ritual goes horribly wrong. Something ancient, evil and not of this world enters the corpse or manifests in the location (Storyteller’s discretion). That specific geist may not perform this Ceremony on that specific target again. Failure: The ritual bears no fruit. Success: Successes are gained. Once the target number has been met, a new geist has been created at Psyche 1 and Anamnesis 5. Treat other characteristics as normal. Exceptional Success: Successes are gained. If the target number has been met, the geist is created at Psyche 2 and Anamnesis 7 instead. Suggested Modifiers: The prospective Anchor for the vanquished geist was actually one of his previous Anchors (+3). The target and the ritualist were dear friends or lovers (+2). Each day that has gone by since the target’s death (-1).
                                            Align the Dark Fates (*****): This ceremony allows for a geist to mark a mortal (who must be unaware of the supernatural) to become one of the Bound. The geist must have a depiction of the target (photograph, painting, sketch, etc.) and a small piece of their body (at least a thimble of whole blood, a small piece of flesh or skin, some fingernail clippings, a lock of hair, etc.). The geist then enters a trance and begins to visualise the target’s near future. She also begins narrating out loud the events she sees in her mind’s eye. When the target is in danger in the vision, the geist’s verbal narration changes from merely recounting the events to actually altering them. She speaks out loud the changes that lead the target to their death and sees in her mind’s eye how they will unfold. Her vision concludes as she watches her target die. Performing this Ceremony is a Sin against Anamnesis 3. Dice Pool: Psyche + Manipulation. Action: Extended (3 successes). Time Increment: Once per 10 minutes. Dramatic Failure: She misaligns fate horribly. The target from now on imposes a -4 to geists attempting to affect her with this Ceremony. Failure: The geist cannot change fate. Success: Successes are gained. When she reaches the target number, the mortal is fated to die and return as one of the Bound within one week’s time, as the geist has foreseen in her vision. His Threshold will be up to the new geist, depending on what part of his death was most impactful to him. Exceptional Success: The geist establishes a sympathetic connection with her victim. Once they have become a geist, she gains a +2 on rolls to attempt to persuade them to do as she suggests. Suggested Modifiers: The geist has uncovered deep personal information about her target (+3). The geist has more of the target’s body than she needs (+1). The parts of the body that the geist uses for the Ceremony are dirty or otherwise tainted (-1). The geist has no knowledge at all of her victim (-2).
                                            Anchor the Soul (****): Every geist is intimately familiar with this Ceremony, as it allows them to bind themselves to a new Anchor. Its performance varies from individual to individual. Dice Pool: Psyche + Anamnesis. Action: Extended (5 successes). Time Increment: Once per hour. Dramatic Failure: May not happen, as no negative modifiers can be applied. Failure: The geist does not bind to a new Anchor. Success: Successes are gained. Exceptional Success: Successes are gained. If the target number has been met, the geist’s sudden reconnection with unlife completely heals all damage and fully refills their Plasm. Suggested Modifiers: The Anchor is of huge significance to the geist (+2). The Anchor is of moderate significance to the geist (+1). The Anchor is somehow related to the geist’s Purpose (+2).
                                            Song of Triumph (*****): By singing a song of deep emotional meaning to the geist (which must be related to triumph in some way) out loud, the geist becomes so suffused with life that she temporarily ignores her weakness to whatever killed her. This, unfortunately, impedes her from adopting her resilient undead form. Even if she runs out of Plasm, she automatically spends Willpower to look lifelike. If she’s out of both Plasm and Willpower, she loses a Willpower dot to preserve the lifelike state. Dice Pool: Psyche + Presence. Action: Extended (8 successes). Time Increment: Once per minute. Dramatic Failure: All successes are lost. Instead of life triumphing over death, the opposite occurs. The geist loses her lifelike appearance and may not regain it for a full 24 hours. Failure: The geist does not triumph over death. Success: Successes are gained. Once the target number is reached, the geist no longer takes Aggravated damage from attacks/speeches that emulate the way she died. This protection lasts for a scene. Exceptional Success: Successes are gained. If the target number was reached, she automatically remains lifelike when she runs out of Plasm, without having to spend a Willpower point or dot. Suggested Modifiers: The song is explicitly about the triumph of life over death (+3). The song is upbeat, joyful, defiant, angry or courageous (+1). The song is sad, apathetic or depressing (-1). The geist’s mood does not match the song’s (-2).
                                            Ghostwalk (**): By ritualistically walking a full circle around her unconscious body and reciting a list of places she visited while she was alive, a geist in Wraith form may extend the time she may spend in Wraith form and the distance she may part from her body. Dice Pool: Psyche + Stamina. Action: Extended (4 successes). Time Increment: Once per minute. Dramatic Failure: The geist snaps back into her body. She returns to Revenant form and may not go into Wraith form for the rest of the scene. Failure: The geist fails to commence the ghostwalk. Success: Successes are gained. If the target number is reached, the geist begins to ghostwalk. She gains a special aura that trails a certain effect behind her, usually related to her personality or Threshold (such as flower petals, ash, dust, metal scrapes, snowflakes, water or blood droplets, etc.). She may remain in Wraith form for 2 hours per point of Psyche, and she may wander up to 100 yards per point of Psyche away from her body. The effects of the ceremony last for the rest of the scene. Exceptional Success: Successes are gained. If the target number is reached, her ghostwalk is particularly powerful, allowing her to remain in Wraith form for 3 hours per point of Psyche, and she may wander up to 200 yards per point of Psyche away from her body. Suggested Modifiers: Ceremony is performed in a cemetery (+3). The geist has an Elemental Key (+1). The geist is leaving her body in plain sight (-1). The geist’s body has been damaged while she was in Wraith form in the last 24 hours (-5).
                                            Veiled Touch (*): By gently caressing the bare skin (usually the face) of a living being who cannot see into Twilight (they may be supernatural being, all that matters is that they are not dead or undead), a Bound in Wraith form may manifest, much like a ghost. The geist may choose to be visible or invisible when she manifests, but regardless of her choice, this allows them to affect the material world while in Wraith form (which is normally impossible save for specific circumstances). In order to exert any significant pressure, the geist must make a Strength roll. Precise motions may require Dexterity rolls at the Storyteller’s discretion. Dice Pool: Psyche + Dexterity. Action: Extended (3 successes). Time Increment: Once per turn. Dramatic Failure: All successes are lost. The geist disturbs the ambient energies and her Twilight state is rattled. She may not attempt this Ceremony for the rest of the scene, and she takes a -2 to all rolls to unlock Manifestations or perform Ceremonies while in Wraith form. Failure: The geist fails to manifest. Success: Successes are gained. Once she attains the target number, she may manifest while in Wraith form at will for a scene. She may also return to Twilight when she pleases. Manifesting and going to Twilight are both reflexive actions. The effects of this Ceremony ends when she returns to Revenant form. Exceptional Success: Successes are gained. If she has reached the target number, she may return to Revenant form without ending the effects of this Ceremony, which allows her to switch back and forth between multiple forms and states at will, as is most convenient to her. Suggested Modifiers: The living being is of emotional significance to the geist (+2). The living being is vulnerable and unsuspecting (+1). The geist has the Phantasmal Key (+1). The living being is a supernatural creature (-1). For every additional living creature in the room beyond the target (-1).
                                            Other Rules
                                            The Underworld does not exist in these houserules (however, a Storyteller may choose to have the Underworld exist without much fuss). As such, Haunt Fluidity instead applies to rolls for conducting Ceremonies. Abmortals may or may not exist as the Storyteller wishes.
                                            Mementos do not exist in these houserules.
                                            Consuming a ghost’s corpus counts as providing a week’s worth of food and water sustenance for the geist.
                                            Haunts generate Plasm per night, not per week.
                                            Founding a Pact
                                            As per Founding a Krewe, but with the following modifications:

                                            Replace the word “krewe” for the word “Pact.” Any Pacts may use the Founding and Supernatural benefits of any tier regardless of their size. Instead of the geists being seen as mythical entities, the collective myth instead shapes ideals that the geists wish to aspire towards.
                                            Channeling Aspects and Avatars imposes a penalty on Anamnesis due to the geist temporarily forgetting their sense of self as they are caught up in the ideal they aspire towards.
                                            Sending Forth The Aspect instead allows for mental communication between both parties, it allows the target to use the founder’s Keys for any Manifestations they have in common (using all of the target’s characteristics), and it allows the founder to use Keys and Manifestations through the target.
                                            Revenance allows for founders to be resurrected without a loss of Psyche or Anamnesis (and avoiding the death of an innocent), but doing so requires the presence of all founders, a Haunt, a semi-intact body (as described in the book) and for the geists to collectively pour at least 100 total Plasm into the body of their soon-to-be resurrected companion. As per the original Revenance, this fails if the geist was killed the same way it originally died. This works on vanquished geists.
                                            Character Creation
                                            Ignore Archetypes. They have been replaced by Conclaves.
                                            All Geists begin with a point in the Oracle Manifestation for free. The Bound are haunted by dreams of life cut short, by the calls of the dead and by the urge to either defy or enforce Fate. All geists know how to part the veil between life and death and peer through it for answers.
