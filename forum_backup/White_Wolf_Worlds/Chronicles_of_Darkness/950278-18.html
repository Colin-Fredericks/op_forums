    Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
    The obvious follow up question is, in that case how would you define "mad scientist"?

    I mean that both thematically and in terms of how their powers work. As in, I'm not sure you could "double down on the science" and include the "Irish Wild Man" archetype at the same time. (assuming you want to double down on the science in the first place).

    Feel free to lecture away as much as you like.
    I think that mad science as a literary concept would be defined as opposition to the typical conception of what is possible and what is real. I don't have any problem with that approach in Genius. I think science is seen as mad until it isn't. When medicine was based on Humorism, that was science. When medicine progressed, continued study of the humors (a disproven or, depending on how you look at it, misinterpreted understanding of the way our bodies work) was "mad".

    My saying that, and Genius' saying that (if I understand correctly that that's what Genius says) is really a deconstruction of science as understood in scientific thought, which would claim that it doesn't matter that people once thought the world was flat, the world was still round, even then.

                                                I'm just going to add in that part of the reason I make a distinction between gurus/alchemists and mad scientists is that the former is a real world concept while that latter is a literary concept. I find it troubling to try and say that an alchemist is a mad scientists when real alchemists would not have defined themselves in anything close to those terms. At least with Mage the gnostic overtones are close enough in both cases to that an Awakened Mage is a reasonable approximatation. The Kings Raven , what you have defined to me is not the mad scientist, but the visionary. Those clearly overlap, but I take issue with using real world people and real world roles as the core premise for mad scientists when mad scientists are entirely a literary construct. Visionaries, be they religious, spiritual, political, or scientific, are all around us. It is a disservice to them and potentially an erasure of culture to equate them to an entirely fictional archetype. Especially one that opts not to explain the source of the Mad Scientist's power, becuase every Alchemist and Guru I've read about has entirely coherent and passionate belief in their metaphysics. The way Genius is currently set up, those metaphysics cannot possibly work or be adapted into the game's metaphysics becuase the answer boils down to "Lol! Wrong!" This is my opinion, but I think OP shares it with me. RPGs, especially those set in the modern day, should exult and celebrate the cultures of the world. That is not an easy thing to do, but it has been a continuous tone with 2e, and especially with Dark Eras. Without a metaphysical hook for why Geniuses can do what they do, it's downright impossible.

                                                Sorry if that was repetitive and rambling. The tl;dr is that I don't think conflating real and literary concepts in this instance is appropriate.
