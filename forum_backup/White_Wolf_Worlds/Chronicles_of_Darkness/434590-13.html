                            Speaking as someone who has been following Leviathan only sporadically and who doesn't have any personal investment...

                            I have to side with Vree and CalvaryKeter and the others expressing dissent with TKR's stance on Marduk.

                            I'm happy to play as a "cosmic abortion" of a PC.

                            But I don't see why that means there has to be some Mary Sue Technocracy rip-off running around in the setting.

                            Yes, humanity won, the Leviathan's lost, the Chaoskampf and all that.

                            But Marduk as it is? Bores the hell out of me. It feels far too much like some OWoD fanboyism mixed with the "Humanity, F**K YEAH" attitude of Hunter fanboys.

                            I would happily play in a game with Vree's take on Marduk, but I wouldn't want anything to do with a game that uses TKR's version. It is that freaking overblown and overhyped and generally offputting.

                            Lightning-manipulating science heroes makes a great Hunter Conspiracy. But they should NOT be "The Good Guys". NOBODY in the World of Darkness is "The Good Guys". That's the whole damn POINT of the setting.

                            I'll be honest here: I love the idea of "okay, so I'm a monster: what do I make of myself?" as a game concept. I love the idea of trying to define myself when I don't have a "place" other than that which I make for myself. It's one of the reasons I loved draft!Beast so much.

                            And contrasting Leviathan stagnation to humanity progression is great and all... but, still, it's far too limiting, the way it's being portrayed.

                            As was commented a page or two back, humanity is STILL, at heart, little more than an animal. We wear our clothes and use our tools and perform our rituals and tell ourselves that this makes us better than the beast. We shun the dark and foul impulses from our primeval self. But the Leviathans? They are US, stripped of all our trinkets and talismans. They are the beast within brought to hideous life, the living reminder of the bloody seas from which we dragged ourselves. They are our darkest impulses and most primeval natures garbed in hideous, ever-shifting flesh. We might try to deny them, we do so with great success... but we cannot eradicate them. And, perhaps, we never will.

                            Leviathans are scary because they represent the darkness humanity was born in, and that it still hasn't finished clawing its way out of. Worse, they represent the possibility that we may - or even that we will - lose our grip and be swallowed by it once again.

                            Their time has passed... and yet, their time may come again. That is not dead which can eternal lie. And with strange aeons, even death may die. What was once mundane is now unthinkable, and may yet, in time, become mundane again. The light may burn so brightly, but darkness is eternal; in the end, darkness will always swallow the light.

                            THAT, to me, is far more interesting a thematic backdrop than "you're just pale shadows, hiding from the Mighty Men of Marduk before they inevitably scour you from existence". It gives Leviathans an "anchor", and a source of hope. Promethean has attracted so much backlash for its bleakness, and yet, to me, Leviathan seems to make Promethean look downright super-happy-funtime. Prometheans at least have the hope of reaching the New Dawn. Leviathans have nothing to look forward to but death.

                            Humanity and Marduk have the strength of reason and their advanced technology. Good. But let Leviathans have their own strength, the old strength; muscle and blood and bone. Technology is always progressing, and so to is nature evolving. The stars are wrong, the blood thins, Leviathans have faded from relevance in the modern era. But that is the core of nature: Adapt or Die.

                            In my games, I want the options for Leviathans to Adapt. I want the option to tell of Leviathans winning shoals in which to wait for their time to come again. I want the option to let the stars tumble into the seas and the drowned cities to rise and for mankind to shudder as the veil of civilisation is torn away to reveal the screaming, bloody animal that has always been behind it all.

                            So why am I not getting those options!? That's Lovecraftian as hell, right there - the very essence of Cosmic Horror is how utterly insignificant humanity ultimately is, for all its culture and science and technology and civilization.

                            So why is the impression I am constantly getting from this project that I'm supposed to be signing up to play the "Super Cool and Ultra Righteous Marduk Organization, who hunts down the Things From Out of Time with the power of Science!", instead of the game's titular antediluvian horrors?

Unless a Leviathan explicitly forbids it, those who succumb to her mental presence will mentally devolve back to a more primal state. This then gives Leviathans a choice; do they try and preserve their minions by taking the responsibilities of godhood to reinforce Thou Shalt Not Steal, Murder, Covet Another's Wife, etc? Or do they simply let it happen, because it's easier, it makes the worship stronger and purer, and it makes the cult more useful - a cult that no longer suffers Integrity loss for murder or torture or theft will more viciously and effectively defend their god. Especially if, as I hinted at earlier, a "devolved" Cult grants increased benefits for worship.

                                    Secondly, part of the Leviathan's powers might include options for mental and even physical devolution in humans. A Leviathan, by its very nature, can tear through the veil of humanity and flick on that primordial lizard-brain, bringing out all the darkness humanity tries to deny resides in it. When a Leviathan focuses its psyche, madness, barbarity, degeneracy and decadence soon bloom. And once a mind has been torn down, perhaps a Leviathan can proceed to do the same to the body, allowing them to transform and warp humans in their own image once that "veil of lies" is stripped from them.

                                    Thirdly, I would add anti-tech powers to the Leviathan's arsenal. Technology is the physical manifestation of how humanity has managed to suppress its instincts, an embodiment of logic. As the living avatar of the primeval darkness of instinct, a Leviathan can subvert the very ideology that technology is symbolically presenting, making a lie of its existence. This also has the advantage of making Leviathans feel less "one wrong step from total extermination" against Marduk. Yes, Marduk tech is superior to anything humans have, and so it's more resistant to these powers, but the same ideological clash is still there. Leviathans can't build it better... but they CAN tear it down.

                                    In fact, a deleterious effect on higher technology might be an inherent aspect of higher Sheol. The most powerful Leviathans are Walking Techbanes, capable of destroying technology with their very presence. They can possibly suppress it, but it adds to that primeval god feel when electronics die (bad news for you if you have a pacemaker) and gunpowder just stops functioning.

                                    Fourthly, making virtues of "politically incorrect, yet primevally justifiable" actions helps enforce that "A Leviathan is the bloody beast of the past made manifest" feel. A Leviathan sees someone disabled and is immediately goaded to prey upon them; society calls that ableist, incompassionate, but to a Leviathan, the Weak Are Prey, The Sick Must Be Culled From The Tribe. It feels good, it feels [/b]right[/b] to slay them. This is the trickiest idea to convey without getting it wrong, so I'm hesitant to suggest it myself.

                                    Finally, Leviathans at higher Sheol could be "walking rifts" in the Gauntlet; their presence makes it easier for spirits to slip through, and those spirits that flock to them and join their Cults are always of the most primal natures - you cannot see Knowledge or Electronic Spirits in a Leviathan's wake, but Blood, Death, Birth, Murder, Joy, Hate, these all flock to them. Again, this references and reinforces the idea of "Leviathans are gods" (they summon and maybe can even control spirits, the most primeval form of worship-figures, as their "angels"), the idea of "Leviathans are inherently primordial, evil" (they drive away "higher" spirits and allow only the most basic and elemental of spirits to thrive in their presence), and the idea of "Leviathans are living manifestations humanity's darkness" (the spirits that flock to them are things of murder, sex, blood, violence, hatred, death).

                                            Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
                                            You're talking to one of the Princess developers that argument won't work on me.
                                            Speaking as another Princess developer - Princess is like a World of Darkness game because, while the Hopeful are unambiguously Good Guys, they have little power compared to the other supernatural factions and tend to get locked into conflict with most of them. As I said before, the Marduk Society as you conceive it is both good and powerful, and it has no real reason to oppose any splat but Leviathans; thus it just isn't compatible with the World of Darkness in general.

                                            Besides, look back through the thread. There's several people telling you that your Marduk makes for a poor antagonist. Is there anyone who thinks your Marduk is a good idea, other than you?

                                            I even question whether Leviathan needs a representative of human progress for thematic purposes. It seems to me that the implicit history of the Leviathans represents that theme adequately already. Back in the Bronze Age it took supernatural backing and self-sacrificing heroism for mortals to challenge a Leviathan and his cult; today, a unit of the National Guard can break a Leviathan's cult in a week and drive him to the ocean's depths to lick his wounds. There is human progress in a nutshell: you don't need a Tier 3 conspiracy with a fancy Endowment to ruin a Leviathan's day anymore.
                                            As it happens I think there is a more interesting take on Marduk that keeps a lot of their original flavor, without turning them into plaster saints. Make them into Randian heroes.

                                            Vree's mention of Captain Nemo is my starting point here. Nemo, you see, is a genius engineer (his submarine is his own design), an aristocrat and a scholar, and a generous man (he gives salvaged treasure to the poor and oppressed), but he's an enemy to the social order of his time (the European empires) and has removed himself from it, living in exile by sheer intellect, and controls his technology and subordinates with an iron hand. He is, thus, a precursor to John Galt - though Verne was more dubious about his character than Rand was about hers.

                                            Now let's suppose that the Marduk Society shares the general capabilities and outlook of Nemo or Galt. They retain the technology beyond the edge of what public science can do (superscience is central to the trope these characters embody) but they also withhold that technology from the world at large, because they want to control what is done with it. They firmly believe that their purposes are pure and righteous, so they can use these tools without fear, but the rest of humanity has not caught up to them, and therefore doesn't deserve to have them. And while they protect humanity from supernatural threats (especially Leviathans) they refuse to submit themselves to humanity's judgement, so they conceal themselves from the public. In short, they are Man in his pride: triumphant exemplars of Reason applied to master Nature ... and swollen in an unreflective hubris.

                                            You can even keep the part about eating Leviathan flesh with this mindset - not as an ancient practice, but as an example of their hubris. The Marduk Society are so much masters of themselves that they can steal immortality from their ancient enemies in the Tribe without being corrupted ... or so they think. Mermaid meat isn't the secret of a cabal of hidden masters, it's a perennial temptation that any Society member might succumb to.

                                            Now, that would make for a more interesting antagonist - and a thematic contrast with the Tribe.
