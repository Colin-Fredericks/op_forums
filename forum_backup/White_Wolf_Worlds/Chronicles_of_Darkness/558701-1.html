    A nerd could still need Social Manouvering because he can suspect that's a ruse, and it's Big Bullying Time coming.

    That put aside, there are situations where you DO want a roll, but not Social Manouvering. That's when the other guy isn't really put into that to really resist, but you NEED to know his reaction. Sometimes the great speech is just a first act to acquire what you want. But sometimes you have to know about the speech by itself, and how much hearts you conquered at that very moment.

    In the same way sometimes you can reduce combat to a single roll, because you can't either deny it's relative relevance, nor think it deserves an entire scene, sometimes you need simpler rolls. I do understand the rationalle that you shouldn't ask a roll if it doesn't have drama, but sometimes you have moments that do have drama, but don't have enough complexity for Social Manouvering.

    Lets think about the PCs trying to sneack into a building. One of them tries first to talk the guard into this with some lye. This IS a dramatic roll. If he succedes, he can buy a passage for the PCs into the building. But if he fails, the guard isn't likely to listen to another try. In fact, the guard would probably suspect and reinforce security. If the guard isn't impressed, it isn't good enough. He will probably call for help.

    Such situation could call a Social Manouvering, but would hardly be the best option. It would bring too much variables to a simple question: he believes? Yes or not. There will be enough risks inside the building to take so much numbercrunching time with a guard, but enough drama in this for a roll be called. That should be a moment for a simple social roll.

            Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
            Personally, I only call for rolls when two conditions are met:
            1. There is a reasonable chance of the roll failing. If you don't have to roll dice to see if you tie your shoes correctly, why would you need to roll to buy something from a shopkeeper?
            2. There are dramatic consequences for failure. Otherwise, why even bother rolling? Just adjudicate the results so we can move on to something more interesting.
            As it seems, we agree on major points. Maybe mine reading of English on morning was just low then and that I thought you do not agree with me.

            Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
            Now, I have seen quite a few people who don't use Social Maneuvering when the players need to convince a mark to do something (something worth rolling dice for) right away, because they feel the long intervals between rolls makes Social Maneuvering ill-suited to such a situation. To that I say, that's why the Impression and Forcing Doors rules exist. If you're in that much of a hurry to convince this character, you'd better make a damn good impression with him, or Force Doors to coerce him into it. 99% of the problems people have with the Social Maneuvering system can, in my opinion, be resolved by the ST being a bit more generous with Impressions, and the players being a lot more willing to Force Doors.
            Here is where i clearly have objection - for me, if Goal the player want to make is resolved in the same scene ( like "give me the Artifact you hold in hand!" ) using Social Maneuvering is too much and not totally counter-thematic to the character. You are not making prepared speeches and nice environment to slowly influence the Target - you are using your abilities more like fighting skills - you "shoot" with Persuasion or Intimidation for quick response. I could consider using the SM system to make NPC divarge from his long term Aspirations or basic characters goals ( like protecting the daughter or finally becoming Hierarch of the city ), but still would more think that decisions made "in the heat of the moment" need to use normal Social Skills roles, just like you would fire a gun or attack with your fists him. Also, it makes game much more dynamic than trying every single time count Doors and assume Impressions.
