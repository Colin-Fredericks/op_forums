    Originally posted by Razikale View Post


    I guess the first question should be: do you, as a Storyteller, want this to be an issue? If you have her job call her when she's working with her cabal, what do you hope to get out of it? Do you expect that her character will ditch work, or ditch her cabal? If you think she'll ditch her cabal, no questions asked ... is that desireable for the other players? If it would be, I'd ask the player why she's made a character that prioritises her mortal life above her magical life in all regards. And why does this character choose to do so? Is there a reason? What does the player want to get out of making a character like that? Can it lead to something that's enjoyable for everyone?

    If the player doesn't really have a great idea, maybe you could work something out. If she does prioritise her cabal, then her work-related issues could be something for the entire group to solve. Perhaps she needs the money for some magical project that will benefit the entire cabal, so perhaps her cabal can help her set up some scenario that proves how indispensible she is. Perhaps they can work to get her boss to be replaced. Or something along those lines.

    Or if no one is really interested in that particular aspect of the character's life, you could just choose not to call her in. If you need an in-game reason for it and not just Storyteller fiat, someone in the cabal could be casting Fate spells to have her not be called in. Or use Mind to have the boss just choose others first. Or just use mundane means to get into her boss's good graces. There's always bribery or extortion, as well.
    Being a nurse, she could just enjoy helping others.

    But if she is called away (always a good thing to keep in mind if a player has to miss a session), you can always rope the mundane occupation back into magical interest - perhaps someone is admitted to the hospital the has really strange wounds, or there are a pattern of incidents. Maybe the director is a pawn of another power (with emergency call or inconvenient scheduling being purposeful, or not).

    EDIT: Thinking more, an occupation as a Nurse would be of interest to a Guardian, regardless of personal feelings. It helps having someone on the scene to be aware of strange (and in need of covering up) medical issues. Other related professions like police, firefighter, report, or counselor are also good career choices to detect and contain weirdness before it develops too far.

        #6
        The basic problem I've encountered with Mages and jobs is similar to "vampires in mixed parties" - non employed PCs have much more "gameplay time" than employed ones, and it often has lead to frustrating situations where characters can't operate as a group for majority of the day, because X has a day work, while Y has a night shift, so they can only participate for 2 hours, which means that Z and C decide to investigate stuff on their own. This obviously works differently with jobs that operate under flexible schedules, or even better, where you are only bound by actually providing your product. So in the end, most gameplay-friendly occupations from my experience ended up being freelance jobs, "being an owner of something", while jobs that require you to spend 8 hours behind desk or doing something specific either got handwaved, or produced a lot of frustration, sometimes for GM, sometimes for a player, sometimes for other players.
        [In fact, one of my gamemasters got so annoyed - this wasn't a mage game, but it also was a supernatural wod game - that he ended up "faking death" of such characters to just. get. going! I wasn't amused, to say the least.]
        I had very similar problem with a group of six changelings that the only thing in common they had was the Bar they attended at nights, but were so disconnected about, well, they had nothing in common, that the game session was a mess. My fault as a novice GM of CoD, and your question made me remember that story, and find a solution.

        What do you think about the following?

        Tell the players to be in the same job or symbiotic jobs. Let them decide. Examples for 4 group players:
        - Police + Lawyers: an investigator, a forensic, a lawyer and his/her secretary.
        - School / High School / University: all working for the school, not to be all professors, for example there could be a lawn mower man, other the cooker, another the psychologist and another the librarian.
        - Hospital: a social assistant used to the slums, a nurse that makes home visits in those slums, a psychologist that gives advice and help to the young to not fall in the gangs, an ex-police or private investigator who is very street wise.
        - Detectives + Lawyers + Journals: an investigative journalist, two private detectives (the force and the mind, or the good cop and bad cop, for example,, a lawyer.

        Or you can propose your players to set up a business between the 4 (if they are 4 players), for example:
        - An underground clinic, where they attend people in the slums that cannot afford the medical bills
        - A law firm defendeing their clients not only with the law (mmm... I remembered Daredevil series here...)
        - A nightclub / concert hall: the chief of security, the bartender, the DJ/musicians recruiter, the accountant...

        It would be easier if players agree in taking jobs of the same "theme" or themes that can mix well with the story. You can also modify the story to fit well with their jobs. For example: a corpse is found in a backstreet:
        - Police / Detectives / Lawyers: the police has closed the case but the parents of the victim go for the players for help to investigate the case.
        - Hospital: the corpse is that of a patient that healed of a depression and was an example and a helper for other patients.
        - Nightclub: the corpse is that of a drug dealer, and was found in the backstreet of the club that the PCs owns...
        - Clinic: the corpse is that of a homeless child that was given food yesterday by one of the PCs...

        What do you think about it?

            This seems like a problem that would be solved by the group making their characters together. I've seen similar issues in other game systems when people make the character they want to play, and everyone does that on their own, and then people just start playing without talking about group dynamics. It typically helps a lot if the group either creates the characters together, or at the very least, if a part of the first session is spent on deciding how the group will be able to function. Make up bonds between the characters, give them reasons to trust each other, or at least work together. In a game like Mage it seems like occupations and such would be an important thing to consider. If everyone has a character whose job is incompatible with another character's schedule, to the point where you can never have the cabal together at the same time, then someone really has to change their characters somewhat. Several people, preferably.

                                                Razikale, if you are looking for a career that fits the character's skills, has a flexible schedule and has the potential to pay as much as needed, your character could work as a consultant, either freelance or working for a firm.                                                         In the game in which I played my House of Ariadne character, she was employed by an architecture and interior design firm that was run by mages. As far as I can recall, she was the only member of the firm that wasn't a member of the cabal that ran the business. This could have been a job-based Mystery Cult because each of the mages belonged to a Legacy where their Legacy's teaching helped them in the job that they do. My character's mentor was a member of the firm and my character worked as her personal assistant. There were Reality Stalkers and Geomancer's there, too. They did everything from feng shui to heists out of that firm.

                                                        That was my first "professional" mage. Other times, my mages have careers that lend themselves to the Mysterious lifestyle. They are bartenders, waitresses and students. They work odd or unpredictable hours and have jobs that allow them lots of freedom to miss work if necessary. Or, if I miss a scene, I can say, "I was working that night. What happened?"
