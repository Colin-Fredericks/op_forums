    What's your favorite Doomsday Cult?

    01-25-2016, 08:02 PM
    So, I'm working on a little project and I find myself in need of an antagonistic, cult-like group. Something in the style of the Cult of Cthulhu, or from NWoD, the Cult of the Doomsday Clock or the Red Word people or maybe Claire Redfield's Children of the Worm. The problem I am running into is that there is a bloody lot of these things, and I lack the patience to comb through all of the forums and books to find the best one, and I'm generally indecisive. So, I'll throw the question out to you, gentle readers.

    What is your favorite antagonistic cult? And why?

    For the purposes of this discussion, we'll count Bloodlines, Lodges, Legacies, and what have you, so long as they meet two criteria: They are broadly destructive/antagonistic, so that all but the most depraved character will figure "those guys suck" (so, Belial's Brood counts, the Guardians of the Veil do not), and secondly, they ought to have a rationale for why they are doing this stuff, beyond just "Yay Cthulhu!"

                      Let's see...

                      I always had a soft spot for the Red Word. I'm a big fan of Lovecraft and I appreciate that the Red Word is some sort of recostruction of the classic (some would say trite) concept of evil cult that, while mantaining the superficial aspects one would expect from such cults, evolves into something completely different. On one hand you have evil cultists and a sanity-breaking texts hanging around New England, while on the other you have time paradoxes and alternate realities that mix Dunsany and Hogdson with more modern influences. It's fresh, in a way. It manages to grow from its root into something completely new and unexpected. Something I have always liked about the Red Word is that defies some well-known conventions of the genre and manages to surprise even seasoned RPG players who, let's face it, have probably seen their fair share of evil cults. Other evil groups both in the CWoD and the CoD are more innovative and original than one would expect when first hearing about them, but I think the Red Word is the one that manages to pull this specific trick better than others.

                      I'm also quite fond of the Shuankhsen, even if some would object they don't exactly fit. I love the justaposition between their inner torment and sympathetic backstory against their vile state of being and the horror that is Ammut. They're tortured, doomed creatures forced into service of this unholy force of nature. Some are evil, some want vengeance and some others are just following the dictate of the curse inscribed on the remains of their shattered souls. I think the options they offer both under a narrative and gameplay viewpoint are simply too cool.

                      EDIT: If i may indulge in some shameless self-promotion, there's also my Brotherhood of the Locust, an expansion of a cult mentioned in Nightsbane. I took heavy inspiration from The Enlightened Society of the Weeping Moon, a Wyrm Cult detailed in the Wild West book Frontier Secret. The Enlightened Sociey had not much wordcount, but many agree they were one of the best part of the Wild West setting and one of my personal favorites from CWoD. I'm attached to the Brotherhood of the Locust because I wrote it and I think I did a decent job at giving them a sense and a motivation, but I'm not exactly unbiased here. I always had fun when I used those guys but, you know, that's me

                                                  I think the Cult of the Doomsday Clock is still my favorite one. Not that I know much others, but I like the Men In Black style they have and the fact that they are walking time paradoxes which makes you question if they can be still considered human beings.

                                                  They believe the same as most mages; “Reality is a cage” and they decided to go for the same goal as many have; “Let’s free everyone from this cage”. The problem is that they have decided to do it by blowing up the cage, which translates to destroying time (and by extension reality) as we know it.

                                                  It’s also interesting that it’s not clear how are they going to achieve their goal, which makes nearly impossible to predict their next move or to understand why they pick their targets.

                                                          I'm not sure if it exactly qualifies as a cult, there's also Valentine and his minions from Kingsman: The Secret Serice. For those who haven't seen the movie (and it's a really fun early Bond-style but R-rated movie) I'll spoiler the plot details:

                                                          Valentine becomes convinced that climate change would end humanity one way or the other unless something like 80-90% of humanity is "culled" ie killed. He uses a special signal routed through electronic devices to fill everyone near them will homicidal rage and reduce their inhibition to zero. Effectively everyone experiencing this thing tries to kill the nearest person until they're either completely alone or the signal stops. Note that skills, tactical training, etc are not lost in this homicidal fugue (a highly skilled super-spy simply kills with far greater frequency and efficiency due to his training and equipment). The only defense against this effect (aside from simply not being in range of the signal) was to be implanted with a special chip of Valentine's design that blocked the signal. This had its own drawback as Valentine (or someone hacking into his system) could trigger said those chips to cause the implanted person's head to explode to prevent them from spilling information to others. People coming out from under the influence of the signal had full memory of what they'd done under its influence and would likely suffer Integrity/Morality Breaking Point from the experience.
