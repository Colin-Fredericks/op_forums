          Originally posted by Zooroos View Post
          Hi! I'm looking for ideas and suggestions regarding the following: I'm trying to create a city were the vampire clans are weirder than usual, each clan is like a different species of blood-drinking predator that just happened to coexist in the All-Night Society. Folllowing the example of the proto-Mekhet and the posthumous Embrace, what sort of mechanical and flavor-wise ideas could I use to further differentiate each clan from each other?
          Going from the established material, vampires becoming Kindred is part of the larger phenomenon of the Blood tending to pool and run together from shared food and territory, so localized clans that are more differentiated from each other than Favored Attributes, Clan Banes, affinity Disciplines, and exclusive Merits are likely to have recent ancestry and/or activity further removed from interaction with Humanity.

          To this end, the city might have a relatively new or insular All Night Society whose clan representation comes from a small number of bloodline-bearing elders awakening from extended torpor (whose childer typically begin their Requiems already initiated into the bloodline), or there might be strict territorial enforcement and feeding restrictions between the clans such that even without the natural evolution of age and bloodlines some low-level speciation/specialization has occurred over the years. If none of the major covenants are present and Clan Status is more important to city politics this has more space to expand. Generally speaking, pulling further away from the base situation where fresh neonates are better off than revenants but not possessed of substantially more specialized powers means their sires are exerting a fairly high degree of control over their movements until they're more particularly developed.

          Alternately, you could lean into the game's natural mechanical space for unlimited granular development and have exclusive Merits and proprietary Devotions be more strongly represented among all the local clans; rather than an old and insular convention, this could be a fresher coup by a subset of the existing clans crowding out the less particular specimens in the area however many years ago as part of a specific agenda or suitability to some niche that is especially fruitful in this city, and this state of affairs hasn't existed long enough for time to start sanding off the hard edges of the clan's overall shape.

          Banes and ritualistic quirks below the level of a full Discipline are big ludonarrative arrows in your quiver for differentiation purposes, and if you want to tie them into the city specifically (in either causal direction) you might further link them with strange phenomena in the area. Maybe there's a gate to the Underworld that the local Haunts lair around that gives them all the ability to see and interact with ghosts, with their Clan Bane taking cues from Geist by gradually limiting their ability to communicate outside the context of fear with anybody but their Touchstones (or people who meet some other criterion). Maybe the local Serpents have adapted to literally feed on emotions and start to wither away if they don't have a Condition representing some strong sentiment. Maybe the local Savages begin their existence as a tiny animal that nests in their old body (like a rat or a large spider) and have to exert extra effort to control their human form before a certain point, but can extend that power to other human bodies. Maybe the local Lords are all geomancers that can use human sacrifice to exert gross control over their Havens and Feeding Grounds. Weird vampires can get real weird!

          Resident Lore-Hound
          Currently Consuming: Demon: the Descent 1e

                      Supernatural merits can go a long way to give a sense of "otherness" to a character, even if most of them DO come from the clanbook.

                      Of Rose & Thorn is like unlocking a whole additional discipline out of Animalism, one that can go even crazier if one decides to mess with Mandragora & Lacrima (ghoul pants and the vitae-like sap produced by those, both from the Ghouls book). Swarm Mind is freaky and can get even more so depending on what animals one can turn into. Those two plus Kindred Medium (from New Orleans: City of Damned) and Ghost-Eater (from Book of Dead) made my "Gangrel" (being someone risen from the dead through a pact with a strix-like black cat spirit made his identification as such...somewhat questionable) elder quite different from what many would expect one of the "Savages" to be like and something of a factor in his city's power dynamics and games.

                      So much so he became a primogen much to the distaste of the recently enthroned prince - and his own, but that is another story.

                      That some of these same gifts would also be manifested by his childer only served to create more of a sense of a bloodline and entrenched heritage of strangeness among them.
                      And the black cats - the dead and the damned cats (kanaima) could never leave them wholy alone. But that's what family is for, no?
