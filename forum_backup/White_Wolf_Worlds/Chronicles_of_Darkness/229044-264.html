            The Views From Without are pretty awesome.

            Also, having fully read the new text now, I have to say that overall it feels too rushed and doesn't mesh well with itself. There's pages of going on about Wisdom and teaching through fear being their purpose and such and then you get this:

            "Teaching a lesson through terror is, to most people, morally reprehensible...but
            Beasts are not people. They use lessons to give context and purpose to their feeding
            practices. If Beasts are honest, they admit they use the concept of “lessons” to make
            what they do more palatable.             Some Beasts rail at that notion, claiming divine
            purpose. Others try to make the lessons matter, to improve humanity — whether
            the Dark Mother charges them with it or not, it’s a noble pursuit. Still others shrug,
            cast off even the ideas of a higher calling, and feast."

            I think that Rater is being a bit more strident and interpreting things more narrowly than I would, but they've also raised some pretty valid points. Not every person who's said something to the effect of "I'm doing this for your own good" is an abuser, but nearly all abusive people have said or implied it at some point. The notions of kinship and family mesh badly with the idea of the Devouring, especially as it is presented in much of the text, and the mentions of self-realized Beasts definitely come across as almost certainly untrue.

            For that matter the original text had, and the updated version still has, a monster of a problem with consistency in re: to how it refers to the Soul/Horror (take your pick) and it's relation to the rest of the Beast. Depending on what paragraph you're reading it can be either the whole being is one unified constant, or it might treat the Soul as though it's a completely separate entity that just happens to be barely leashed to your hindbrain. The Lair suffers the same issues, being either an extended part of the Beast's Soul or just a hunk of the Primordial Dream that you liked and moved into.

            Overall, I think the game needs to take a step back and regroup. Trying to scramble something together to quickly patch the holes is only going to cause more issues, and ultimately I think they can do better than this.

                    Well, gone through the Chapter 1 and 2 revisions. I like much of what was added and expanded upon, like the additional material on the Primordial Dream. A lot of The View From Without sidebars are actually fantastic. I am relieved the nature of the Begotten hasn't really changed and that the Devouring has retained the spirit of the Homecoming for the most part, unlike what the revised Introduction seemed to suggest. But the devil (or the Horror, so to speak) is in the details.

                    I seriously do not follow how the most common way of undergoing the Devouring (that one may come face to face with their Horror and finally realize it is them) involves another Beast inflicting a nightmare on the Begotten-to-be that is not their own and feasting on what is left of their human soulstuff, rather than the Beast's own Horror performing that act, tormenting and killing the dreamer one final time as they become the monster. I'm aware many details are intentionally murky to allow interpretation and supposition, but I don't get why the event is so commonly induced by another Beast and their Horror as opposed to the nascent Beast's own.

                    The teaching through fear angle has an interesting place between intended ancient byproduct of how the predations of Beasts affect humans, and culturally encouraged prerogrative, giving a focus to their Hunger not to justify it, but to make something positive of it. And I do like it, particularly when it is explained in the context of what the Primordial Dream is and how important it was for the early survival and progression of mankind. But the topic feels a bit clumsy in how it's presented. I'm not even sure why, it's just the feeling I get from my reading.

                    The new Ugallu birthright is good though. I think it captures their role as nightmares of exposure better, and has more potential in play.
