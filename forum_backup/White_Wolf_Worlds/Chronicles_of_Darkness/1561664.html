    Protean - A Rant and some Homebrewed solutions? Looking for Opinions

    07-13-2026, 07:15 PM
    Hello all!

    Today I want to rant about my least favorite Discipline: Protean.

    But first, let's talk about what I like about the other Disciplines.

    In Requiem, most Disciplines kind of build upon the base layer as they go up. Auspex, Dominate, Majesty, Nightmare, and Obfuscate all feel like they progress naturally. Each level beyond the first feels like an improvement and an expansion of what it can do.

    Animalism is a bit weird, which happens to be my second least favorite Discipline. Levels 1 to 3 feel natural, then you get into Levels 4 and 5, which are cool on their own, but feel disconnected from the previous three levels. Anyway, Animalism doesn't cause me as much issue as Protean does, so I'll focus on that.

    I have two main issues: a thematic one and a mechanical one (with Protean 3).

    Thematic Issue
    Protean progression doesn’t feel natural at all and is all over the place. I get it, Protean is the ‘’Mythological power we couldn’t fit into another discipline’’ kind of discipline, but still.

    When you first learn the discipline, you learn to merge with soil, brick, or even concrete. How? why? It mentions that it creates a cocoon of soil around the user, but how does it work in a 5-inch brick wall? Or in the floor? One has to assume the vampire kinda melds somewhere. It’s, magical, I guess.

    Now the Second dot is cool, it’s IMO, what Protean is truly about; you learn to manifest animal traits. Very cool, but how you got there from earth melding is … certainly a stretch.

    Third dot and fourth dot make sense coming from the 2nd dot. Transforming into an animal after having learned the ability to manifest some of their trait, is both cool and make sense. The Fourth dot expands on the 2nd dot, which I also like.

    Then there’s the fifth dots; You become mist. Okay, what’s the link with all the rest? Not much to be honest. It has…some very minor connection to unmarked grave, if you ignore the fluff about creating a cocoon and imaging the vampire melding, but anyway.

    Mechanical Issues
    I have a few mechanical issues with some of the powers.

    Unmarked Grave: It's not interesting; it's flat-out boring. All other Disciplines have a cool one-dot ability.

    Physical Disciplines have useful activations as soon as the first dot is invested. Celerity 1 is awesome on its own and allows you to Clash of Wills against other uses of Celerity, which you flat-out can't do if you don't have one dot. Vigor 1 doubles your jump distance for free, which is pretty cool. Resilience is the least glamorous, but for one dot, two points of activatable armor can really save you from torpor or even staking.

    Even Nightmare, which I find kind of meh, still has some fun minor illusion effects you can pull off at one dot.

    With Protean 1, your power makes you better at sleeping. Yay.

    Beast's Skin: Protean 3 is the one I have the most mechanical gripes with. Since the user gains all of the animal's physical Attributes and Skills, it encourages Gangrel, a physical Clan, not to invest in physical Skills at all.

    Why build Stealth when you can shapeshift into a rat or a cat and have a pool of 8?

    Why build Survival when you can turn into a wolf and gain 4 dots?

    Why invest anything into combat when you can turn into a bear and have a grappling pool of 10 and a Health pool of 13 before any Vigor or Resilience is factored in?

    You might say, "Well, a cat isn't powerful enough for a surprise attack, and a bear isn't stealthy at all."

    Might I present you with the best of both worlds: the great cat!

    With a base Stealth pool of 7 and a fighting pool of 9 or 10 (with claws), it's only one shy of being as effective as a maxed-out fighter like the bear, but it also sports much better Defense at a base Defense of 8, before Celerity. Its main drawback is that it only has 8 Health boxes, but that's nothing Resilience, which happens to be a Clan Discipline, can't fix.

    Basically, you are discouraged from investing in any Physical Attribute, as well as Athletics, Brawl, Survival, and Stealth because not only do you gain access to all the animal's physical traits, but you also lose access to your own.

    Unnatural Aspect: I simply dislike Barbed Hands. Grappling is already strong; it doesn't need extra incentive. If you want to be the best at grappling, invest in Vigor, I don't know. It's yet another thing that stacks in favor of grappling, which combat almost always devolves into, in my experience.

    Primeval Miasma: I've read that since Kindred don't breathe, you can't steal Vitae from inside their lungs. If Strix can't steal breath from Kindred, this power shouldn't be able to do it either. If you can use it to steal Vitae from Kindred while inside them, I hate it. Otherwise, I don't have any mechanical issues with it, outside of the fact that it might be a bit of an auto-win against mortals, but to be fair, mortals never stood a chance anyway. Certainly more fair than Swarm form (which I also disallow).

    Solutions
    All right, I'm done ranting. I'm looking at solutions. Here's what I'm thinking.

    Unmarked Grave: Unmarked Grave becomes a Devotion using Protean and Resilience, probably one dot of each, as the power isn't that interesting on its own.

    If Unmarked grave is a devotion, Protean 1 becomes a minor version of Predatory Aspect that costs 0 Vitae but allows you to choose two adaptations, plus one per dot of Protean after the first. You can only manifest one at a time with a reflexive action. If you use Protean 2, you can manifest all of them at once.

    Beast's Skin: Transforming into an animal gives. You gain a number of transformations points equal to your protean + 3. Each points allows you to buy one of the following:
    • Increase Strength, Dexterity, Stamina or Wits by 1, up to Blood Potency Limit.
    • Increase size by 1, up to Protean + 3.
    • Increase speed by 2.
    • Increase : Athletics, Brawl, Intimidation, Stealth and Survival, up to Blood Potency Limit.
    • Your form has access to Flight or Swim. (Free if you already have access to aquatic(swim) or patagia (flight).
    • Reduce your size by any amount.
    • Gain a natural weapon (Free if you already have access to claws with Predatory aspect.)

    Those forms are fixed, and you can have up to protean form stored. You can change them as usual and they should represent predators.

    I don’t know if it’s too weak, but that way I feel like… It grants the versatility of having multiple animal shapes, while not completely discouraging investment into physical stats. If you want your Strength 1, Brawl 0 nerd to be good in combat, it can certainly increase your combat capability by +6 (By adding +3 STR and +3 brawl) at protean 3, which is very good, but it won’t turn you into a combat god either. If you’re already good, it might push you a bit over your limit, to truly make you scary, but it won’t make it mandatory for any fighter to invest too protean.

    Barbed Hands: I'm not sure whether I want to remove it or simply state that it doesn't stack with Vigor. Maybe it could add Protean as grapple damage on a successful grapple instead of adding to the grapple roll. Still useful, less mandatory.

    Primeval Miasma: I don't truly care, to be honest. Five-dot Disciplines are rare in most of my campaigns. Maybe it can stay. It makes little thematic sense with the rest of the Protean, but at that point, the vampire can already shapeshift into an amphibious, echolocating, wall-crawling bear with wings, so maybe turning into smoke isn't that far-fetched after all... Beside, mythological power, yada yada.

    I don't know what an appropriate power would be otherwise. Maybe a lesser version of the Force of Nature Devotion. Primeval Miasma could become a Protean 4 + Obfuscate 3 Devotion.


    Conclusion
    That was a long rant. Hopefully not too negative.

    Anyone had the similar issues? Are you already fine with Protean?
    Any critics about my half-assed solutions?
    Any other solutions to propose?

      2e's version of Protean 1 represents the culmination of the ability to merge with the earth moving forward in the Discipline's powerset from Masquerade to Requiem 1e to Requiem 2e, and it makes this the thematic base of the ladder by making it the means of refitting the kit provided by the middle dots (remaining a cheaper means of doing so than Primeval Miasma in most situations).

      Blood and Smoke made it a specific design point that the first dot of a Discipline is its gateway to monstrosity, with later powers ratcheting up the intensity. Unmarked Grave serves as the introduction to the vampiric ability to transform their bodies by allowing them to dispense with the specifics of their material form on one hand and become a preternaturally buried corpse on the other hand.

      This being the introductory power of the Gangrel's proprietary Discipline makes it very easy to explain how they maintain their reputation as nomads and wild folk around population centers despite a propensity for behavior that may put them on the outs in the All Night Society. It gives them a reason to keep soil and lower-Durability chunks of material handy even if they have a dedicated Haven.

      Yes, visually, manifesting animal traits is the classic power of Protean. Thematically, though, it's about manifesting the Beast, and literally every published version of Protean in both Vampire games so far puts concrete mobile shapechanging above the range of the first dot, and definitely doesn't fit the general 2e design space of starter powers being mostly free. "You are swallowed by the earth and can drink blood spilled upon it in comparative safety or stir the cauldron of your Beast's stronger manifestations" is a decent complementary ability to the kit of "be harder to damage" or "talk to an animal and give it simple commands."


      The raised issues with the Trait replacements of Beast's Skin cheerfully ignore the simple ludonarrative facts that this is a game where you are trying to maintain your Humanity, shapeshifting costs more Vitae than a starting character can spend in one turn when you need Vitae to be active, and things that aren't humanlike actors are going to give you less Vitae while humanlike actors are unlikely to react positively to the type of animals you can turn into, to say nothing of the loss of opposable thumbs and ability to speak. (And, not to put too fine a point on it, needing to find and kill a given animal first to add it to your limited library of shapes.)

      Mild similarity of mechanical framework notwithstanding, the Kindred are not mages and do not benefit tremendously from pursuing magical development over personal and political advancement. Other Disciplines have a hard limit on their ability to supplement the fixed traits of any given animal and animal forms have a limited ability to overcome, say, an elder who's spent decades honing their skill in unarmed combat to superhuman levels without needing to waste precious blood turning into a bear first.


      Speaking of comparing to mages, I had a thread some years ago asking about why Disciplines are so structures when the Blood is so variable and reflecting on that means my reaction to your proposed alternatives to Protean 1 and Protean 3 is a firm "Nah." The language has shifted away from the more "lifeless" manifestations of it over the years, but it remains a fact that vampires aren't vital — their powers are accumulated tendencies of the Beast in the blood, and that means the closer you get to fully freeform shapeshifting on the fly as a universal quality of the Discipline the further you're getting from individual vampires as (to borrow neutral language from a different subtopic) creatures of theme. Unmarked Grave and Primeval Miasma allowing a vampire to dream up a new Beastly loadout a day later is appropriately metered for undead shapeshifters without the roiling flesh of werewolves or the chaotic elemental sorcery of mages.

          I'm under the impression the issue is more table culture than the Discipline. Which isn't a problem necessarily, it just means the playstyle of the group isn't like most.

          Protean is not about animal shape change. It has never been. It's about the whole range of physical transmutation of the undead body and being adaptive. So, animal forms aren't a basis of it, they're just one more application.

          Unmarked Grave is a must-have to live in the wilds or otherwise as an undead survivalist. It's cool as a theme of a monster that haunts the very land. I don't just find it fitting, but hard to give up on.

          Other problem is that other Disciplines make a point of having all levels being useful. Not just build upon the theme, but they either add effect or alternative uses rather than just being "last, but better".               Both interesting answers that go in a similar direction, so I'll answer each point I can make out in general.

              Gaming Style
              My issues with Protean are definitely table culture issues.

              I tend to run Requiem as a sandbox-style political drama. Currently, I run my second chronicle in my homebrewed version of Tokyo.

              My game runs on a calendar system I made to track time. Basically, the game runs on a monthly basis, with four weeks plus one weekend representing Elysium night. Each week, players get:
              • 1 feeding action to reset their Hunger dice, representing how well they hunt during the week. (I homebrewed Hunger dice into the system.)
              • 1 personal action they can devote to... anything, really. It can be a Touchstone scene to regain Willpower, they can work on a plot kit, find rumors, etc. They must make a Rouse check before this action to see if they get hungrier.
              • 1 coterie action they must use in conjunction with their coterie to advance whatever the coterie wants to work on. They must also make a Rouse check before their action to see if they get hungrier.

              At the end of the month, Elysium happens, and since it's basically a big convention where a lot of Kindred meet, it's useful for making connections and sharing information without having to spend one of your precious actions on a meeting that could have been resolved with a five-minute conversation at Elysium.

              If someone does not want to participate in a coterie action, they can opt out to regain 1 Willpower by staying home and resting, but they don't get any extra scenes. (This is to avoid "spreading the party" too much and to keep the story moving.)

              Finally, I try to give each player at least one personal scene per month—something that happens specifically to their character. Sometimes more, during busier months.

              I make some room for personal horror, and Humanity has its place, but it's definitely not my sole focus. My experience with Humanity is that characters who aren't fighters tend to plateau around Humanity 5, while fighters usually hover around Humanity 3 or 4. Personally, I tend to like making characters who try to act morally and care about the consequences of their actions (which isn't quite the same thing as Humanity), but not all of my players care about that, so I try not to focus on it too much.

              Feeding scenes and Touchstone scenes can happen once in a while if I feel like I have a good idea for one, but I find that they tend to become repetitive. I usually reserve Touchstone scenes for when players ask for them. Players—not characters—generally have control over whether they risk their Touchstones.

              Combat, as weird as it sounds in a post ranting about Protean, is really not my focus either. It tends to be a last resort or plot-driven. Combat generally happens because the players want it to happen, as those who want to avoid it usually have the tools to do so.

              In my current chronicle, it has happened maybe three times over the course of twenty-one six-hour sessions.

              In the previous chronicle, combat happened a bit more frequently as a result of having an actual fighter, but even then, no more than once every three or four sessions.

              Physical characters in my chronicles usually have political weight in the sense that people who know they're dangerous are careful about how they approach or cross them.

              Anyway, I'll stop myself here. I mostly wanted to share some insight into how I run my games.

              Protean Theme & Discipline Progression
              My issue here is that Protean, and to some extent Animalism, are the only two Disciplines that stick out like sore thumbs among all the others. Every other Discipline increases in a way that feels like a natural progression from the previous dot. Protean doesn't. Clearly, that's not an issue everyone has, but I tend to prefer when things are a bit more organized.

              Might just be a me problem.

              Satchel, from what I understand from your other post, it seems like you'd go in a different direction regarding how Disciplines manifest themselves. I'm not sure I completely follow, but I feel like we have opposing views, which is perfectly fine.

              I'll also add that any references to other game lines have a high chance of flying over my head, as I haven't played any of them.

              I understand that Protean is not the "druidic shapeshifting" Discipline. Even if most of the Discipline is focused on manifesting animal traits, I don't see those traits as natural. They morph the user's body in ways that are unnatural and creepy, and I have no problem with that.

              The description of the Discipline mentions that it's about "[indulging] their Beast, reveling in the descent from an undead thing yearning for its grave to a force of savage power." Or, as Satchel aptly pointed out, a descent into monstrosity. But even in the book's own description, I see a narrative stretch: it's both "yearning for your grave" and "savage power." The "yearning for your grave" part only really comes into play with the first dot. The rest has very little to do with it, outside of the fact that future adaptations must be changed with Unmarked Grave. But if Unmarked Grave wasn't part of the Discipline, it wouldn't even be mentioned, and torpor would probably be the default way to change those adaptations.

              Which brings me back to my point: Protean, as a Discipline, feels like a collection of otherwise unrelated vampiric mythical powers linked together by the vague idea that they're all tied to shapeshifting.

              Why must every Protean user learn how to Earth Meld before they can manifest their bestial traits? Why does the first power of the Discipline have very little to do with manifesting your Beast and everything to do with hiding away?

              I just don't see it.

              It's most certainly a me problem.


              Unmarked Grave
              Unmarked Grave is certainly useful to have. It's an iconic Gangrel power that has carried over from multiple game lines.

              It can be used in a political campaign as an additional intimidation tool. After all, do you really want to enter a turf war with Gangrel when they can sleep anywhere? That's definitely a scary prospect.

              But that's about the extent of what the first dot offers: a backup plan and a safer nomadic lifestyle. If you really want to stalk the wilderness, there's always the possibility of carrying an opaque tent or something. Besides, not all Gangrel are going to be constantly on the move.

              This is why I'm proposing to make it a Protean 1 / Resilience 1 Devotion. It gives easy access to Gangrel who want to play nomads, but it doesn't force the Discipline on all Gangrel—or on any bloodline with access to Protean, for that matter.

              I'm aware that all my previous points are subjective, but the next one definitely is, so take it with a grain of salt:

              To me, Unmarked Grave commits the cardinal sin of being an incredibly boring first-dot Discipline power. It's up there with Resilience as something that only comes up once in a blue moon.

              I get that the genre of Vampire: the Requiem leans heavily into the "Woe is me, I'm a cursed supernatural being, vampirism sucks" theme, but nearly every other Discipline offers something awesome right from its very beginning, while Protean 1 doesn't. Let's look at those Disciplines:

              Animalism. You gain access to a whole new realm of possibilities. You can interact with animals in ways that even a mundane expert could never hope to. Incredibly useful, and it costs no Vitae.

              Auspex. Incredibly useful. It gives you valuable information at very little risk. It's also one of the few defenses against Obfuscate. The first use is free.
              Dominate. Probably the strongest first-dot Discipline. It lets you control someone else and even erase memories of a scene or implant simple ones. Again, no Vitae cost.

              Majesty. It eliminates nearly all situational social penalties caused by your actions or appearance, while also granting a +1 (or more) bonus to Presence. Incredibly useful, and free.

              Nightmare. Not quite as useful as the others, but the illusions are fun. The Intimidation bonus is nice, and preventing Willpower from being spent to bolster actions is also useful during a Clash of Wills.

              Obfuscate. Incredibly useful. You're now almost impossible to track, and you can approach almost anywhere with people present without being noticed. You could even beat someone up in the middle of the street, and anyone other than your victim might not even realize it happened. Again, no Vitae cost.

              Vigor. +1 Strength and doubling your jump distance isn't particularly powerful on its own, but it's certainly useful during a chase.

              Celerity. +1 Defense isn't particularly exciting on its own, but the active effect is so absurdly powerful that almost everyone wants at least one dot of Celerity just to have a chance during a Clash of Wills if their action gets interrupted by Celerity. It's useful for much more than combat. I've used it alongside Dominate so the user could effectively "win initiative," instantly lock eyes with a target who was intentionally avoiding eye contact, and gain just enough time to Mesmerize them. In a game where the initiator often wins, always acting first is extremely powerful.

              Resilience. Eh... this one is nice to have when you really need it, but it's also one I'm not particularly fond of. It's not bad, but it's not the Discipline I'd prioritize on a combat-focused character—which also happens to be Gangrel's physical Discipline.

              Here's why I proposed offering a Predatory Aspect at the first level: it's the fun part of Protean. It's also what the entire Discipline revolves around after the first dot: shapeshifting. (I'll come back to that later.)

              Every Discipline gets a cool power that's free or nearly free at its first dot. Why does Protean simply make you safer while you sleep?

              Maybe I'm just not imaginative enough. Maybe the real strength of Protean 1 is that it lets you extend your active hours by already being where you need to be the following night, or by allowing you to stay where you need to be until the very last minutes before sunrise. Eh... that's pretty anecdotal most of the time.

              Beast's Skin
              I think I made my case poorly and focused too much on the Strength Beast's Skin can grant, rather than my main issue: how it discourages investing in physical traits for one of the most physically oriented clans.

              Let's take three basic character concepts: the thug Gangrel, whom I'll call Marie Blackwood; the businessman Gangrel, whom I'll call Steven Cash; and the elder Gangrel, whom I'll call John Gangrel for his ridiculously high Attributes and Skills.

              Let's assume Marie and Steven both have a bit of extra Experience to spend on clan Disciplines. Let's also ignore Merits, as they don't affect my argument.

              Marie Blackwood, the Thug
              Attributes: Int 1, Wits 3, Resolve 3, Str 4, Dex 2, Stam 3, Cha 2, Man 2, Com 2
              Skills
              • Mental: Academics 1, Occult 1, Politics 2
              • Physical: Athletics 3, Brawl 2, Larceny 2, Stealth 2, Survival 2
              • Social: Animal Ken 1, Intimidation 3, Socialize 1, Streetwise 2
              Disciplines: Protean 3, Animalism 1, Resilience 1

              Steven Cash, the Gangrel Businessman
              Attributes: Int 2, Wits 3, Resolve 2, Str 1, Dex 3, Stam 2, Cha 3, Man 3, Com 3
              Skills
              • Mental: Academics 2, Investigation 2, Politics 3
              • Physical: Drive 2, Firearms 2
              • Social: Animal Ken 1, Empathy 1, Intimidation 1, Persuasion 3, Socialize 2, Streetwise 1, Subterfuge 2
              Disciplines: Protean 3, Animalism 2

              John Gangrel, the Blood Potency 7 Elder
              Attributes: Int 2, Wits 4, Resolve 3, Str 7, Dex 4, Stam 6, Cha 3, Man 1, Com 4
              Skills
              • Mental: Academics 2, Craft 3, Investigation 2, Occult 4, Politics 2
              • Physical: Athletics 4, Brawl 6, Stealth 4, Survival 5
              • Social: Animal Ken 4, Empathy 2, Intimidation 4, Socialize 2, Streetwise 1, Subterfuge 3
              Disciplines: Protean 5, Resilience 5, Animalism 4, Vigor 3, Celerity 2, Nightmare 2, Auspex 1

              Now let's look at what happens when each of them transforms, you can probably guess where i'm going with this. I'll use three forms: a battle form, a stealth form, and a versatile form.

              Bear – The Battle Form
              For this form, I'm looking at increases to combat and survivability. I'll ignore Stealth. The bear has Str 6, Dex 2, Stam 4, Defense 5, 11 Health boxes, Brawl 4, and Athletics 3 (Climbing 3). At Size 7, it also has 11 Health boxes, but there's no real way of calculating how much Experience that Size increase is worth, so I'll ignore it. I'll also ignore Stealth and Survival since they aren't relevant here.
              • Marie: +2 Str, +1 Stam, +3 Health boxes, +2 Brawl, and a Climbing specialty. That's a total of roughly 13 XP worth of combat ability. (Let's ignore Size since everyone gains the same benefit.)
              • Steven: +5 Str, -1 Dex, +2 Stam, +4 Health boxes, +2 Defense, +4 Brawl, +3 Athletics, and a Climbing specialty. That's roughly 39 XP worth of combat ability—three times as much as Marie, despite being the non-combat character.
              • John Gangrel: -1 Str, -2 Dex, -2 Stam, -3 Defense, -1 Athletics, -2 Brawl. That's roughly -26 XP. Shifting into the battle form somehow makes him weaker. In fact, every combat form granted by Protean 3 is weaker than his natural body.
              Domestic Cat – The Stealth Form
              For this form, I'll consider increases to Athletics, Stealth, and Dexterity. Strength and Brawl don't really matter. The cat has Dex 5, Athletics 4, and Stealth 3.
              • Marie: +3 Dex, +1 Athletics, +1 Stealth, for roughly 16 XP worth of stealth capabilities.
              • Steven: +2 Dex, +4 Athletics, +3 Stealth, for roughly 22 XP worth of stealth abilities—again ahead of Marie.
              • John Gangrel: +1 Dex, -1 Stealth. A whopping 2 XP gain, but his actual Stealth pool doesn't improve—it remains 8. Looking like a cat certainly helps narratively, but mechanically his stealth doesn't improve, and his athletic capabilities are simply worse across the board.
              Large Dog – The Versatile Form
              This form is useful all around. It's easy to obtain, reasonably capable in combat, and excellent at tracking. It also has the advantage of being a form you could realistically see almost anywhere, unlike a bear wandering through downtown.
              The dog has Str 4, Dex 4, Stam 3, Athletics 4 (Running), Brawl 3, Stealth 1, and Survival 3. Its Defense appears to be listed as 8, but it should probably be 7, and its bite pool is listed as 6 but should likely be 7. I'll assume both are meant to be 7.
              • Marie: +2 Dex, +1 Defense (capped at 6 by Wits 3), -1 Health box, +1 Athletics (Running), +1 Brawl, -1 Stealth, +1 Survival (Tracking). That's roughly 14 XP worth of improvements. Her combat ability increases slightly at the cost of one Health box, her Stealth pool increases by 1 if based on Dexterity, and she becomes a bit better at tracking.
              • Steven: +3 Str, +1 Dex, +1 Stam, +3 Defense (capped by Wits 3), +4 Athletics (Running), +3 Brawl, +1 Stealth, +3 Survival (Tracking). That's roughly 44 XP worth of abilities—a straight upgrade across the board.
              • John Gangrel: -2 Str, -3 Stam, -4 Health boxes, -3 Brawl, -3 Stealth, -2 Survival. As expected, another straight downgrade, losing roughly 36 XP worth of capability.
              Let's move away from comparisons and instead use an example from my own group's experience.

              I'll use the actual Gangrel character in my game. I'm perfectly happy with the character, and the player seems to be having fun too, so I'm not complaining. His character has no combat Skills, and his physical Attributes are fairly mediocre before Disciplines. He has access to the bear form.

              The Gangrel, along with the rest of his group, got into a fight with three revenants, partly because the idealistic Gangrel tried to stop them from feeding on a dying mortal and announced his presence beforehand. The fight began, he was grappled, and then shifted into bear form. (I allow the use of Disciplines while grappled. I'll get back to that later.)

              The revenants had Brawl 3, Strength 3, and Vigor 2. They weren't pushovers.

              It didn't matter in the end. The bear's 11-die grapple pool (including Vigor 1) was more than enough to overpower them.

              Conclusion
              The mechanics of Protean 3 actively discourage investing in an entire branch of Attributes and Skills.

              For characters who avoid physical investment, the deal is simply too good. For those who already invested heavily, the power ranges from underwhelming to outright detrimental.

              If we compare Marie and Steven, Marie might reasonably ask:

              Finding Animals
              This is entirely dependent on the territory, but bears are fairly easy to find throughout most northern and mountainous regions. Large cats are also fairly common in some areas.

              Humanity doesn't really factor into my criticism.

              Sure, spending 2 Vitae isn't free. I'll grant that. It's also not particularly expensive.

              It's true that you lose access to your equipment while transformed, but that's the price you pay for actually becoming an animal.

                                                  Beast’s Skin Rework
                                                  The human form is weak and unfit for a true predator. The Beast wants nothing else than to be let out of its cage. While it lurks in the dark corner of a vampire’s mind, the Beast dreams of new predatory forms to perfect its hunts, taking inspiration from all those predators the vampire has previously consumed. A vampire can unleash those forms, warping their flesh, growing extra muscle, or folding in on themselves until they closely resemble the shape of an animal predator — or something truly wicked, should they listen to the Beast’s every whim.

                                                  Cost: 2 Vitae
                                                  Dice Pool: None
                                                  Action: Instant
                                                  Duration: Infinite

                                                  When activated, the vampire’s flesh ripples and warps to take the form of an animal. They must have consumed the beast — killing it by feeding from it. They can only take the form of predatory animals, carrion-eaters, plague-carrying beasts, and parasites between Size 1 and 7. A vampire can keep a number of forms ready equal to their dots in Protean; to add or replace one, they must consume have consumed appropriate beast in the last lunar month, then slumber for a full day and night in their unmarked grave. (Or in torpor if I end up changing Unmarked Grave, but let’s ignore that for now.)

                                                  Each form is built using the following rules:
                                                  • Move around up to Protean dots' worth of Attributes between Strength, Dexterity, and Stamina at a rate of 1for 1. Normal Attribute maximums still apply.
                                                  • Add 2 extra dots to distribute Strength, Dexterity, Stamina, or Wits. Normal Attribute maximums still apply.
                                                  • Add 2 + Protean dots to distribute among the following Skills: Athletics, Brawl, Intimidation, Stealth, and Survival. These Skills cannot be raised past 4 this way. If you already have at least 4 in a Skill, you can invest 2 dots to gain 9-again, or 4 dots to gain 8-again while using that Skill.
                                                  • Set Size between 1 and 7. Size 6 inflicts a –1 penalty to Stealth. Size 7 inflicts a –2 Stealth penalty and increases the Strength limit to 6.
                                                  • Gain any of the animal's normal methods of movement, using the animal's base Speed factor to determine your Speed.
                                                  • Gain any of the animal's natural weapons.
                                                  • Lower any physical Attribute of your choice to a minimum of 1.

                                                  Once the form has been built, compare it to the animal's physical Attributes.
                                                  • If any of the form's stats are lower than the animal's stats, the animal shape might look a bit frail, sickly, or sluggish based on the lower Attribute, but it is otherwise normal enough to pass as an animal.
                                                  • You gain the Cryptid Condition for as long as you remain in your form if any of the following statements are true:
                                                    • Before taking Vigor into account, your form's Strength is higher than the animal's Size.
                                                    • Before taking physical Disciplines into account, your form has at least one physical Attribute that's 2 dots above the normal animal Attribute.
                                                    • Before taking physical Disciplines into account, all your form's physical Attributes are above the normal animal Attributes.
                                                    • If the animal's size is different that the normal animal's size.

                                                  Once transformed, a vampire is limited by their form's physiology, with all that entails. They may be unable to use some physical Merits at the Storyteller's discretion. If they lack the cryptid condition, they look like a normal animal of their species. Kindred still picks up on predatory aura, and without blush of life the animal shape remains undead to close inspection.


                                                  The vampire can remain transformed indefinitely, but they retain their kindred nature; they can use any kindred ability and discipline as normal, and they still feel the need for blood burning within them. Finally, sunlight still harms them.

                                                  Cryptid
                                                  You let your Beast loose and it exaggerated its predatory desire in a way that renders your animal form unnerving and wrong. A sick mockery of the lifeform it's trying to imitate.

                                                  A bear with too much Dexterity might look lanky and weird or have an extra pair of limbs. A dog with too much Strength might have a huge misshapen head with large fangs. A rat with too much Stamina might be bloated to the point that it looks like it's about to burst if you touch it.

                                                  For as long as you suffer from this Condition, any character who looks at you knows there is something unnatural about you.

                                                  Possible Source: Exceeding an animal's capabilities with Beast's Skin.
                                                  Resolution: Transforming back into your normal shape.
                                                  Beat: The character breaches the Masquerade in a way that creates a new cryptid legend. Be pursued for the sole purpose of proving your form is a real cryptid.

                                                  Conclusion

                                                  I originally made Steven to be the worst possible physical character; he has no dots in any significant animal Skill set. Marie benefits the most from respecting her clan Stereotype and John Gangrel gets to become the stuff of legends.

                                                  Yet Steven still gains significant benefits from transforming, just like an asocial Daeva would still benefit from Awe if they suddenly needed to become likable, or a soft-spoken Nosferatu would benefit from Nightmare if they needed to scare someone off. And that's even without mentioning the added benefit of looking like an animal.

                                                  I think the cryptid condition might actually be pretty fun, it allows a character that really want to go ham to burst through the normal animal limit at the risk of making the form very obvious. It makes finding a bear or tiger less mandatory for an optimized battle form, but still interesting if a bear would be appropriate for the region.

                                                  The Cryptid Condition giving Beats is entirely optional. I just thought it would be a fun way to add to cryptid legends and emphasize the dangerousness of looking like a weird, messed-up animal.

                                                  I think my proposition respects character Attribute and Skill investment, doesn't ignore the drawbacks of Protean, and isn't a straight nerf or a buff.

                                                      Making a (relatively) brief case for Humanity as a concern because this is one of those things about which the game is a little roundabout (the other being "the Predatory Aura being free to use on mortals and applying Conditions and bonuses that make it easier for you to get exceptional successes on certain types of behavior effectively counterbalances vampires not regaining Willpower with rest by giving them the Bestial Triad as a loose extra Vice by making it easier for you to gain the Inspired Condition from acting like a monster"):

                                                      Riding the wave is not just "practicing with the Beast." Regardless of how good your Resolve + Composure pool is, any failed roll on the set of rolls counts as a dramatic failure. Dramatic failure on a roll to resist frenzy means the frenzy cannot end until the character reaches a breaking point, which is going to be a more severe breaking point the lower the character's Humanity is. Messing up at riding the wave forces the issue re: vampires' ability to be nonviolent.

                                                      Any of the things on the sidebar of suggested modifiers for frenzy could potentially trigger a check for frenzy — a list that includes things like being injured, insulted, or hungry — and without powers and Merits to expand its scope the only way to ride the wave is to encounter a frenzy trigger and spend Willpower. Gangrel capping their rolls to resist frenzy (and specifically not their rolls to ride the wave) means they're less likely to exceptionally succeed at normal rolls to resist frenzy (and thus disincentivized to spend Willpower to hold it off and get a big bonus to the singular roll to resist because they're less likely to get it back all at once) and also less likely to normally succeed (thus minimizing the degree to which they have a mounting penalty from the Tempted Condition that they need to fall to frenzy, kill someone, or have a meaningful connection with a Touchstone to clear), and so their Clan Bane has them encouraged to respond to frenzy triggers by gambling on their ability to steer the Beast in a way that makes them more likely to kill someone the lower their Humanity is.

                                                      At the best of times, a Gangrel who e.g. unexpectedly has their outfit ruined or gets too close to a bloody wound has a reduced capacity to keep it together, and if they attempt to get the Beast to do something other than what it wants then they run the risk of escalating to murder — impassioned violence being a Humanity 4 breaking point means that's a concern until you drop to Humanity 3, and likewise with impassioned killing until Humanity 2, and removing those breaking points with banes doesn't make this problem go away (and in some ways makes it worse).

                                                      Frenzy triggers being strictly in the ST's hands as to when a given event counts means this is technically up in the air, as does any given table or character's decision on what turning into or spending time in a nonhuman shape does to a character's Humanity beyond the sample breaking points, but it's fairly observable that the mechanics of the game are built for vampires to have uncontrolled violence as a regular risk to manage and frenzy tilting characters toward being more effective at violence is the main pressure valve for Humanity, to the point where the Condition for dramatically failing a Detachment check shares a resolution criterion with the Condition for successfully bottling up the Beast.

                                                      The Gangrel's bane forces them to contend with this more often and the classical way in which the clan mitigates the issue is to retreat from territories and circumstances in which they are more likely to encounter triggers for frenzy — hard to get insulted by a stranger or encounter open wounds or large flames if you spend most of your time miles away from human activity, and keeping up appearances every couple of weeks is relatively simple.
