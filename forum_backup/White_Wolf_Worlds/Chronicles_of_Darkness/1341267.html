          I’m a big softy when it comes to politics as in I tend to build a safety net for my players when I come up with my city’s court. On the subject of hunters remember that hunters exist because a vampire messed up, they are people who know something but nobody gave them a copy of the rule book and they won’t just yell and attack. Play the hunters like you would run any of those DnD monsters that can change shape (succubus and doppelgänger come to mind) and don’t be afraid to let them make mistakes, if the hunters think someone is a vampire they should approach the situation accordingly and deal with the outcome as best they can.

              While not a VtR player, I can say that one of the big changes from DnD to CoD is that the former focuses on the triumphant use of force (magical or physical). CoD players can gain beats (and therefore experience) by accepting failures and complications that make the narrative more interesting, more dramatic. DnD tends to be tactical - you need to figure out which spells you should use to buff the fighters, and take out the traps or nuke the hordes. CoD has some of that, but there's a lot more strategy: who can you trust? Can you persuade your enemies to attack each other? How much of the safety of your friends are you willing to risk? How much of your self are you willing to sacrifice?

              As Second Chances wrote, unlike many DnD games, the narrative flows not from what characters can do, but what they value. Is the respect of other vampires important to them? Has the Prince given them a task that serves as a complicated political trap? They're not questing for the amulet of Yendor, but instead trying drive off Hunters or save the life of their brother.

              In designing DnD adventures I tended to focus on maps and monsters. In designing a CoD game it was more about a web or network of actors, from street level to midlevel to major actors. In DnD it's dungeon levels, in CoD it's levels of response. Taking out a street gang will provoke a response from higher up the power structure, causing an attack from an unanticipated direction.

                  Think: who is connected to whom and what is the nature of that connection? Who has more power in the connection? Is it formal or informal? What do they think of the other person?

                  Now: your PCs and NPCs will constantly pull and shift these connections--sometimes by mere presence, as if with gravity. How does that change the behavior of others such that they fall out of stasis and into action? The nature of the connections pulled, and the predilections of the character involved, will determine which action is taken.

                  Example: just because you're a newbie doesn't make you dog food among the more powerful. If they assume that you must be working for someone they will want to know who and what you are before taking action that would be otherwise reasonable and violent. That gives narrative breathing space to establish new characters as they slide through the uncertainty--the older, powerful entities are working to calibrate their sensors on the unknown blip... which gives the blip time to slide out of range or resolve into a "safe" state.

                  I use the above principle to let otherwise squishy but mobile mortals interact with very dangerous vampires. They could easily be neonates (and in fact are sometimes mistaken as such). Characters do not walk around with a nametag on that declares everything.

                          The Roman Colosseum was created to be a grand venue for events that brought together the high and politically-connected with the lowly servant-class, so they could establish commonality in appreciating the blood-sport provided. The high would sponsor these (expensive) proceedings and would be appreciated for their generosity as well as a certain democracy in the results: "fan favorites" might receive mercy or rewards, so there was an illusion of largess as well.

                          This is the "circuses" part of the phrase "bread and circuses". It's purpose was to manage the factions in Rome and enable them to be ruled more easily. Everyone jostled within their class during these events, while also observing the protocols that placed them firmly within their class. They were a great distraction and are closely analogous to our modern sporting events--especially professional ones.

                          The "bread" part of the phrase gets at feeding rights, and the parceling out of those right or resources such that everyone has something to eat. Those who are poor hunters are looked after, and those with larger areas may be asked to "contribute" to the ruler's provisions for the good of the community.

                          A Prince who accomplishes both bread and circuses will be stable until the jaded tastes or stomachs of the populace grow beyond his or her means to support. At that point some greater... discipline--an example or few at least... will be needed to illustrate who is in charge. Maybe a foreign enemy to serve as a distraction?

                                  As both CoD and D&D(-esque) Game Master, I advice you to start in CoD with clear chronicle setup. That means, focus on one, maybe two Factions/Covenants - and based story on that. It will be much more manageable and much clearer for you to focus games in particular atmosphere of the story. I will just break down to you basic 5 Covenants in Requiem:
                                  • Carthian Movement – revolution movies, like Matrix or V for Vendetta
                                  • Circle of the Crone – pagan vampires worshiping ‘Mother of Monsters’ – movies like Cult or many pagan horrors.
                                  • Invictus – modern/medieval political drama – stories like Game of Thrones ( without dragons, can work with ‘zombies’ / see Revenants in Requiem / ) or House of Cards
                                  • Lancea Sanctum - Damned Church, stories like Name of Roses, Interview with Vampire ( much more movie ) or Inquisition.
                                  • Ordo Dracul – secrets societies, alchemy, legacy of literal Dracula. ( Movie adaptation are cool – also Hellsing and Castlevania anime series. ) Also, most crossover friendly with other monster races of CoD, if you want later add them. ( I do not advise crossovers from start – it’s cool subgenere, but headache on the start. )
                                  That are my two cents for new CoD / Requiem fellow Storyteller.
