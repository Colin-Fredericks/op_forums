    nameless order/Worship Judges?

    01-16-2024, 05:00 PM
    Based on this question:
    Originally posted by KaiserAfini View Post
    When a mage gains the Renowned Artisan merit, what are the strings attached ? Let's say an alchemist wants to learn about the Mesen-Nebu, can they learn through their own research and would that trigger a Judge to target them ? Do they need to believe the Judges are the ultimate arbitrers of Dedwen ? Do they need to keep constantly working for an Arisen cult ?
    Has anyone written a nameless order/mystery cult based around a Mummy/worshipping the Judges?

    How would it work for awakened mages to attract other to a nameless order where joining meant also being in a Mummy's cult?

    Would a custom legacy be useful for interactions with the Judges (maybe with access to something akin to Exarchal dreams which Prelate's get, but instructions coming from the judges somehow).

    Would it be possible to just earn Mystery Cult Influence in a Mummy's cult without worshipping the Judges? (Or would this be incredibly unwise).

    How would the Pentacle/Seers look at a Nameless order/Awakened cult worshipping these unlikely gods?

          Sorta. I once wrote a legacy for an NPC that borrowed the philosophy of the Arisen Alchemists. I don't think I wrote any attainments, it was just a kind of vague outline.

          I'd use the prelacy mechanics. The Mummy core already uses the paradox mechanics to represent the wrath of the Judges.

          Would it be possible to just earn Mystery Cult Influence in a Mummy's cult without worshipping the Judges? (Or would this be incredibly unwise).
          Probably? Like, there are Arisen cults that go rogue and stop worshiping the Judges.

          How would the Pentacle/Seers look at a Nameless order/Awakened cult worshipping these unlikely gods?
          Contact with the Lower Depths is generally a crime under the Lex, whilst the Seers aren't supposed to worship anyone but the Exarchs.

          That said, a lot depends on how you construct your world. Like, how much do Mages know about the Arisen? Even if they don't know details, are they at least a know quantity?

              I could see an offshoot of the Sons of Imhotep (the geomancers from the Broken Diamond AP) being interested in the work of the Tef-Aabhi to draw on their knowledge to make more Supernally active locations, or even to engineer just the right configuration to create an Iris back into Imhotep's Emanation Realm. Some Attainments could include a Shifting Sands that sends you back up to a scene and uses conjunctional Space to Co-Locate the mage to where they are on the present for the duration, or using a version of Corridors of Time that teleports them where they are on the present, allowing them to maximize what they do in the past. They would look bizarre to the untrained, blinking from place to place in the present and using Istros' Knife to solve issues before they escalate.

                                  Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                  With regards to how Mummy metaphysics might interact with Mage ones (a subject I think is prompted when some crossover references go out of their way to describe mage ideas of what the magic of the Shan'iatu was like), I speculate that it's something like... when you look at a place with Mage Sight, the Supernal symbols you can perceive are the lens through which you recognise its meaning, the underlying emotional quality native to it. Sekhem is the actual aspect of the place that provides the basis for those symbols appearing. Any given Awakened may or may not believe that the Supernal and its symbols are the actual origin point of all that, but Sekhem as Mummies recognise and interact with it could probably be seen as its presence in the world as actually experienced. Think of it like how Mage Sight through the Matter Arcanum might tell you many and true things about a crystal of some salt, and you can even cast spells to affect it, but at the end of the day (barring certain philosophical abstractions) the crystal is still a thing that exists for itself rather than just in terms of the symbols it's seen through, and a chemist can have the expertise of that existence to do things with it that are distinct from Supernal magic. I'd say that if a Mummy well versed in their own metaphysics gained a good grounding in mage philosophy, they'd be of the opinion that Sekhem is a quality of the world that precedes any Supernal nature to it, and there can even be mages who would agree with them. Not all sects necessarily regard the relationship of Phenomenal and Supernal in the neo-Platonic terms of "the idealised Forms of which the Supernal is composed are the original template for all phenomena", and the core book describes some of the ambiguities of their cosmology from which comes a speculation that things might exist first in the Fallen World and then become rarefied to the point of becoming the pure symbols they perceive as transmitting reality (even before getting into the question of influences brought in by strange and alien dimensions).
                                  Makes me think of the Free Council, with their focus on human culture and "As Below, So Above".

                                  The use of cults as fairly common practice across the orders makes this seem perfectly reasonable, whether other Mages know that this particular cult has an actual Mummy at its centre or that is a cult secret, is secondary.

                                              Originally posted by orathaic View Post
                                              I think I meant secret from those other mages who might decide the cabal was being left-handed.
                                              I've yet to look at Contagion Chronicle stuff myself, but between that, some of the Dark Ages stuff, and even a couple of points in the likes of Signs of Sorcery, I think there's a reasonable case for Pentacle society to make some accommodations for the idea of mages who have intertwined themselves a bit with other supernaturals. Perhaps looked askance at a bit, but not necessarily accused of committing some crime or profanity against magic as a matter of course.

                                              Although the other side of that is my being personally inclined to assume that most cabals in a Consilium are pretty secretive from one another as a matter of course, even when they're all being perfectly orthodox.

                                              Originally posted by orathaic
                                              That seems reasonable, of course I could imagine a Mummy cult which either has one of its members awaken, or a newly awakened mages joining a cult before they meet with other mages (like wanting to find out more about magic and finding this esoteric cult first .. or being annoyed by the Pentacle orders approach because they don't like being told what to do and joining a mummy cult instead).
                                              I don't quite think that Mummy cults present very many avenues for entry to people who don't already possess some basis for being sponsored in. Like, if the cult is in the backbone of some corporation, they're mostly drawing in people who work for the corporation and can become personally associated with existing members. That being in cases where they don't outright form a community that brings people up from birth for membership.

                                              A new mage might have the sensory powers to discern something surrounding the group that seems worth following up on and presenting themselves with questions, but I think in a lot of cases the cultists are going to respond to that with suspicion and fear, even if the mage doesn't actively introduce themselves as having powers. Some follow up investigation to figure out how this stranger has sussed them out. If the mage can employ powers to thwart such methods, that just becomes more suspicious, and might even escalate to waking the Arisen.

                                              A sufficiently devious new mage might seek to infiltrate in more organic fashion, like joining the company in the usual manner and putting the desirable qualities in the line of sight of recruiters until they work their way in, but I think understanding of such methods will be uncommon across regular new Awakened. Even then, it ends up as a rather dedicated strategy for somebody who's supposed to be unsure of what they're looking for.

                                              Awakening from within the cult is not impossible, but I'd say there's a question of whether or not cult activities might interfere. Not even just in terms of what they mean for the person's supernatural familiarity that affects shocking confrontations with the Lie, but just... time to themselves. The kind of Awakening that doesn't take place totally within your mind tends to entail going about conducting some activity that you really need to be alone for, so that you can carry them to their conclusion without interruption.

                                              Hmm, I've seen people express scepticism about how many newly Awakened are supposed to embrace the Orders, and it occurs to me that an unspoken assumption of Awakening is that it might occur to people who are already a bit alienated from others. Otherwise, they might have people in their lives who would interfere when they start behaving very strangely, and possibly shake them out of the state of Lucidity.

                                              As for being told what to do, the Diamond Orders have fairly flat hierarchies and aren't really much about being commanding towards any of their members who aren't actively presenting themselves for such, and even then there's always the option of the Free Council (which presumably gets taken at the rate that makes it the largest Order).

                                              Originally posted by orathaic
                                              Either case they could end up by default ina subservient position without necessarily knowing all the details of the cult in advance.
                                              Perhaps, but the relationship between cult and Mummy can be complicated, more circular than just the Arisen being the one in command. They're at the centre and are a figure of reverence, but at the same time the cult will be educated in the rites that will resurrect the Mummy and endow it with purpose, that the raised being will be strongly inclined towards carrying out. These are often purposes that are directly beneficial to the cult. When that's not going on, the Mummy is often completely absent. Either way, not entirely a situation where membership puts you under the Arisen's thumb.

                                              Originally posted by orathaic
                                              Does being aligned to Duat mean there is no Supernal reflection of the practices? This is interesting to me, I guess if that is the case it would apply to lower Depths in general (how the Strix/Dis or the Luciferge/Inferno interact with mages/the Supernal).
                                              Not necessarily without Supernal connection as not the correct kinds of Supernal connection for Free Council knowledge to harness as a Yantra like that. It's like with any Order tool; masks clearly have a reflection, but Arrows can't use them the way Guardians can.

                                              Originally posted by orathaic
                                              I kinda considered Iremite culture to be part of sleeper culture until you suggested this. But i am happy to hear another take!
                                              Keep in mind that Irem isn't just realistic proto-dynastic Egypt that happened to be aware of deities in Irem. The Nameless Empire was founded and ruled by inhuman beings sent as emissaries by the Judges (and possibly higher gods than them, I'm not fully familiar with the lore) in what is implied or stated to be somewhere between the expression of some mysterious cosmic intrigue and a project of deliberately founding a society to serve as a mechanism for channelling Sekhem into Duat. My crude understanding of how the latter works is basically along the lines of "make a society subject to the law of the Judges, so that when its people inevitably break them, the Judges will have authority to condemn them to the Devourer (keeping her satisfied enough to remain at bay and not consume everything)".

                                              Plus some other stuff, Mummy lore runs deep and goes to some unsettling and difficult to parse places. The basic point stands that it wasn't just a normal human culture, even if it's supposed to provide a template that many other human cultures followed.

                                              That's before getting into stuff like how the inhuman entities attempted to harness its resources to rebel against their masters and elevate themselves to the level of gods, by means of sacrificing the entire population and even kind of breaking Irem's place in history (which is why Tome of the Pentacle described it as a closed uchronia). That's where Mummies actually come from, somewhere between a lynchpin in that great mystical working and the means by which the Judges sabotaged and co-opted it to provide themselves with more reliable emissaries.
