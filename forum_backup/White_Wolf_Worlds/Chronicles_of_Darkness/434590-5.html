        Well, i was kind of thinking about the Rift as a fight.

        Not only a battle of minds though, but a fight of gods, a clash of worlds. I'll explain. As i was reading through The World that Is (and Was), Tiamat gradually rose from an incredibly powerful monster, an inmense and utterly titanic god, to a pillar of the creation, a reality itself, a terrifying Gaia, (or more accuratedly, Angrboda), an idea, a concept in fact: Tiamat as embodiment istelf of the Old World.

        (Or more accuratedly, the young world, a world still full of violence and the elements themselves coiling and fighting each other, a world where the reminiscences of its own birth were still present because it hadn't been so far away, with primeval oceans and volcanoes bleeding lava and spitting fire and smoke to the young skies; a world full of potential and furious energy)

        And i started thinking. Tiamat, as this, destroyed by a human hero? No way. No way even if all the warriors of all the Tribes would have lead a charge against her, against it. But what about the collective impulse of Humanity and Time itself? The mere presence of humanity, the rising numbers of it and the progressive domestication of the World (a world no longer as elemental and vigorous as was before), lead to a world in which Tiamat itself was left behind, a World in which her/it, the concept of the Old World, had no place. And so Tiamat fell in torpor, a dream so deep that sinked itself through reality... creating the Rift.

        And so Tiamat dissapeared, dragging down with herself not only, well, herself, but also the most powerful -and therefore purest- of her incarnations/sons: the titanic Progenitors.

        The blood of Tiamat flowed still through the world, of course, and so new Leviathans rose among humanity itself: a pirric victory -but a victory nonetheless-. And in their dreams, the Leviathans would hear the deep call of Tiamat and the Progenitors, and those who would follow it, swimming into the Depths, would arrive into the Lost Dream-Mind-World of their Goddess. The Rift. They quickly realized that the Rift was a physical place as much as the memories of the Whole Tribe. Even more so, the Rift was also their own minds. And so (like the first Leviathan goes) the First Depth is forged of their collective minds, while the most you sink, the nearer you are to Tiamat. Tiamat would be at the other side of The Tempest (or so the legend goes), and the Tempest would be its own Call: so powerful and alien it is to these newborn from Humanity Leviathans that the Voice of their Goddess reveals itslef as pain and obstacles (exemplified as is done in the first edition).

        That's why The Rift is a triple fight: of the mind (the more human mind of the first Depths against the more primeval mind of the darkest Depths), of Worlds (as is the fight of this world against the world that was) and of Gods (of the God that each Leviathan has in his core against the Older, more powerful Leviathan-Gods who lurks in the ever sinking depths of the Rift).

        Thougts?

        PS. Following this direction, maybe the Cults would be not only sources of power and adoration for the Leviathan, but also ways of bringing back a little bit of the Old World into the actual one, as each Leviathan thinks of the Call and the Old World in his/her own way. They fail again and again, as they are fighting not only against humanity and its warriors (the Ahabs and the Marduks) but against Time, always going forward. That could be the explanation of the major theme of the game, stagnation.

                            So now for something a bit different. One of the minor templates tied to the tribe:

                            Primordial Theurgist

                            To the gods, performing miracles is as natural as breathing. A goddess of love touches the world every time two young things lock eyes, even when the goddess herself is giving her full attention to elaborate plots of revenge against her stepsister.

                            The Tribe are no different. Each Leviathan is, in some infinitesimal sense, a law of nature, and like any natural law they can be studied and manipulated, even against their own wishes.

                            There are of course drawbacks and risks. You can call it Newton's laws, though that is an oversimplification. Nevertheless, if you touch the Leviathan's mind then the Leviathan's mind, in all it's sanity rending eldrich glory, is touching you. Primordial Theurgists, mortals who wield Rituals and claim the Tribe's power as their own, are Beloved of the gods they seek to control. If they are not, then they will be before they call their first miracle.

                            Not all Theurgists wish to control their god of course. Plenty are priests and cult leaders who have been taught how to perform Rituals to enact their god's will while it's busy elsewhere. Others believe themselves to be loyal worshippers who've started experimenting in their deity's absence. Many Leviathans flee in horror when they first discover their presence creates Beloved, only to return years later when their abandoned cult's experimentation finally creates some horrific yet effective summoning ritual.

                            But other Theurgists do want to control Leviathans, or at least their divine power. Why wouldn't they? Leviathans are a broken shattered people, mankind has already scattered them upon Earth and the gods cast them down from the heavens. The Tribe are still gods, but defeated gods with little ability to defend their drop of divinity from mortals eager to tamper in the gods' domain.

                            For your sanity, a far cheaper price than a soul, a scholar can acquire power that's not merely occult or supernatural, but truly divine. It might not be more powerful than other forms of magic but it operates on a different level of reality. To an oculist who understands the difference, it can be the most important factor in which tradition they peruse.

                            Priests and cultists, whether they're doing their god's work or just believe they are, typically use a mixture of trial and error, insight from their own madness, and whatever their deity has taught them.

                            Occultists stealing the Tribe's power typically have a much more academic approach. They have their theories and maps of the heavens, charts of stars and tides, and tomes of research from their predecessors. Many draw on research that spreads beyond the Tribe to other gods, especially if they with to use the Tribe's power to influence other divine beings. (A risky prospect, Leviathans couldn't stand up to the gods when their powers weren't being controlled mortals, but if you're lucky the god will only lay his vengeance upon the Leviathan).

                            This research isn't just for figuring out the correct Ritual. A cultist rarely thinks about which Leviathan to petition, he has at most one Cohort and knows them all quite well. But to occultists the choice is paramount. The wrong Leviathan might notice and eat you or attempt to control you, and of course you have to pick a Leviathan who's Vestiges can prower the desired ritauls.

                            Occultists typically use Ophions to power their rituals, as an Ophion has no way of withholding his power save direct intervention; and trapped in the Rift many Ophions are unable to do so. The closest thing to coin among these Thurgists is lists of Ophions with their powers and the techniques they might use to influence their Beloved from wtihin the rift. No Thurgist wants to wake up one day and realise they're a cultist, or the guy who freed a monstrous god from the depths.

                                So this isn't quite finished. I feel in tails of a bit, so I need to think of some way of wrapping it up; but the core is there. Though on re-reading, I might split the bits about priests/cultists and the bit about academic occultists into separate sections.

                                When I think of Primordial Thurgists I always think of the occultist from Darkest Dungeon
                                I suspect that's because we're both thinking of Abdul Alhazred.


                                The actual rules for these guys will have to wait for the Rituals rules of course. But it shouldn't be much; an alternative to the cult merit; probably apprentices and Allies with advantages or disadvantages for each. Rituals should work identically to Leviathans; which is a bit unusual for me. Normally I make my mortals far weaker than full splats at ritual magic – but in this case the weakness is you go mad. It fits the theme of humanity being triamphant over the tribe.

                                One neat fact is that Leviathans are insanely good at Clashes of Will (Sheol + Depth; which means that an Occultist channelling an Opheon has a minimum dicepool of of 7). I quite like the fact that vampire sorcerers and Mages will have to admit that, no, these guys really are using divine power.

                                (It's a shame we don't have Morality any more, giving Benedictions Morality for Clash of Wills would have had the same effect)


                                Fun fact. Leviathans actually have no magical ability whatsoever. They have biological abilties and psychic abilities. Some of it is divine biology and divine psychic powers. But, in my WoD anyway, trying to draw a distinction between divine and not divine requires insanely advanced supernatural lore.

                                Two random people falling in love is divine in the sense that it's a subconscious thing at least one Love God is doing, but it's also purely mundane consequence of brain chemistry. Trying to figure out what's what is ridiculously complex and there isn't always a distinction.

                                So trying to say “that psychic power is divine” and “that one wasn't” isn't really productive. Same goes for a Mage's magic, plenty of gods of magic out there (Hectae, Isis...)

                                            Let me dig up the ol' notes.

                                            I was thinking that rituals will probably be based on the rules for Mage 2e. However I do have some concepts, mostly based on the Blood Sorcery book.

                                            So firstly Leviathan rituals require two things: Channels, Sacrafices and and a cult. The Themes/Arcana are: Awareness, Elements, Fecundity, Might, Predation, Sanctity and Vitality. You have three different Elements Channel, you have Arcana: Elements 3.

                                            For the cult, I'm thinking that Zeal will be moved to a purely Rituals thing. Zeal will work like Gnosis.

                                            One thing I am worrying about is that Channels go up to 7 (if you count the Birthright). Arcana obviously only goes up to 5 without Archmastery and I don't want to give Leviathans archmastery for a simple exp cost. So either Rituals will need to have more gradual progression from 1-7 or just break the Leviathan tradition of using the number 7 everywhere and make Rituals go up to 5.


                                            So next up: Sacrafices. Mage's get Yantra's to extend their Reach in a spell. Leviathans depend on moral evils, the more dots in the ritual the more evil the sacrifice must be. Maybe something like your Reach being equal to (1 + Zeal + Sacrifice - twice Rituals dots); and of course you need to have a Reach of at least one to make a Ritual work.

                                            You can also get bonus Reach for The Stars Being Right. But if the stars are wrong you get negative Reach. The nice thing here is that a Zeal 4 Cult can never perform a 5 dot Ritual unless the Stars are Right.


                                            Now some miscellaneous points:

                                            Rituals are really bad at helping people. Like lets say you want to give your followers superhuman toughness, they'll grow fish scales.

                                            Sympathy works in reverse. Instead of how much the Leviathan knows about you, it's how much you know about the Leviathan. (So don't read that tome of eldrich lore!). Fluff wise it's because gods are partly a concept. So Leviathans partly exist inside every mind or book that talks about them. Just like how Thor exists inside every thunderstorm.

                                            Rituals are technically a combination of divine and psychic phenomina. There's no actual magic involved. (This probably doesn't matter much.)

                                            When Mages get modifiers like area of effect; Rituals get more for the same success. Rituals focus on big things; a Leviathan can't throw a fireball at someone near him with a ritual (not that she needs too) but she can send a storm against an entire town.

                                            Aside from that it's probably going to look a lot like Mage 2.


                                            Best of luck, I look forward to seeing this
