    Ok, now that I've had the chance to watch the video, I actually really appreciate Martin's answer. I definitely don't agree with all of his assessments of the two IPs, but he expressed his viewpoint and his reasoning eloquently, and respectfully.

    Re: WoD having made a meaningful impact on people's lives and identities in a way that ChroD hasn't to the same extent, this just speaks to a bias towards a particular generation of gamers. Maybe that's true for people who were in high school or college in the 90s, but us millennials weren't playing Masquerade in our formative years, we were playing Requiem. To be fair, tabletop gaming was bigger in the 90s, so there probably is some truth to the idea that WoD may have had that kind of impact on a greater number of people's lives. And that may have been what he meant when he added "to the same extent".

    Re: the assertion that WoD was chosen as the IP White Wolf wanted to push because it was able to speak to real things going on in the here and now (or to be more accurate, that were going on in the here and now of 20 years ago) and the unspoken implication that WoD does not, this goes back to the "WoD is Punk, ChroD is Goth" thing. They are both very visceral responses to very real issues in the world. WoD just responds in an external, angry way, where ChroD responds in an internal, reflective way. Again, I think recognizing the impact of one and not the other just speaks to a generational bias.

    At any rate, It's clear he learned his lesson after the backlash from that interview I referred to earlier because this time he really seemed to take great care to explain himself without speaking negatively of ChroD. I also don't think he answered the question that was actually asked, but that's neither here nor there. The same cannot be said of Shane, who I think did try to answer the actual question, but did so without any regard for ChroD or ChroD fans, or even attempting to hide his disdain for ChroD. And it's not like it would have been hard to express that answer without insulting ChroD. He's not exactly wrong that ChroD makes tabletop gameplay a higher priority than WoD does, and that priority comes at the cost of other priorities. But the way he presents that props WoD up as a more believable world (which... huh?) and dismisses ChroD's quite extensive worldbuilding as "layering other stuff on top of (the gameplay)". Also, the "I hope that doesn't happen again" bit at the end was unnecessary and a cheap shot. The "how much time do you have?" could have gone either way, but in the context of the rest of his answer was also an unnecessary dig at ChroD.

                                                            I'm gonna be honest. I don't like this new direction White Wolf is taking under Paradox's control. They seem stuck in the past and desperate to cling onto oWoD nostalgia fans instead of helping nWoD/CofD grow as a brand. In all honesty, while the tabletop gaming market was smaller in 2004 than it was in 1991, what hurt Requiem and the other nWoD/CofD lines the most was the mismanagement of White Wolf as a whole under CCP. CCP neglected the brand and let WW die on the vine. So, initially I thought Paradox buying WW was going to be awesome until I found out more of what that actually entails.

                                                            ​Between the decision to resume the oWoD metaplot and scrap the metaplot-neutral nature of the 20th Anniversary editions, as well as include events such as 9/11 into said metaplot, combined with the decision to intentionally sabotage and diminish CofD as a brand so they can promote their "One World of Darkness" by pandering to those blinded by nostalgia and knee-jerk distaste of anything nWoD. I mean, I like Masquerade (sans the metaplot) but I prefer Requiem. Same goes for most of the CofD gamelines, with the noted exception of Demon, as I dislike both Fallen and Descent (nothing wrong with those games per se, but they aren't really my cup of tea)

                                                            And as a disenfranchised former WoD LARP'er, I can attest to that. So, I'll tell you a story about my experiences here in Virginia that is relevant to my point.

                                                            I'm a Requiem fan but the Roanoke VA LARP and White Wolf scene is dominated by hardcore oWoD fans who despise nWoD/CofD just because it's not the game they played back in 1996. For example, when Mind's Eye Society finally established a joint Roanoke/New River Valley-based chapter, the MES people from New River Valley (mostly college girls my age from the college towns of Blacksburg and Radford) initially wanted to do a nWoD Accord venue chronicle, even hosting a regional event for it in March of 2014 at a local Holiday Inn. I was in attendance and I played an Italian-American Daeva in the Circle of the Crone who was a devout Roman polytheist by the name of Tony Cardona, I even dressed up in-character for the occasion. But the Roanoke gamers, being the hardcore oWoD fans they were, threw a huge fit after the LARP, enraged that MES dared to run a nWoD game in the Roanoke Valley. So, while there is still a Mind's Eye Society chapter in Roanoke and the New River Valley, it is exclusively oWoD, only playing the Masquerade and Apocalypse venues. In our particular group, my fellow Millennials were outnumbered by bitter Gen X gamers blinded by nostalgia. So I decided not to officially register with MES, at least not in the Roanoke/New River Valley chapter. It's a shame because my character Tony Cardona was one of my favorite characters I ever made, and I only got to play as him once. Now, if I move to a new area that has a CofD/nWoD friendly Mind's Eye Society chapter, I may officially register with MES and join them. But between the hostility towards nWoD/CofD in my area's gaming scene combined with the fact that I personally dislike By Night Studios' new LARP rules for both mechanical and metaplot reasons, I am done with oWoD LARP, at least organized oWoD LARP.

                                                            ​The thing is, Martin Ericsson and his buddies at the new White Wolf are encouraging and supporting this kind of behavior in the White Wolf fandom. I mean, I'm glad they didn't totally cancel CofD and let Onyx Path continue developing it, but I also resent the fact that they treat Requiem and the like as a red-headed stepchild while promoting a franchise that simply isn't market viable in this day and age without having to rely on nostalgic pandering and attempting to be "edgy". What worked in the 1990's probably isn't going to work in the 2010's.

                                                            ​So, while I do play homebrewed oWoD games that lack metaplot, overall I prefer nWoD/CofD because I don't need to homebrew it nearly as much as I do oWoD in order for me to enjoy it. And some CofD games such as Hunter: The Vigil I don't feel the need to homebrew at all. I like telling my own story rather than the story White Wolf is pushing on me, so I naturally prefer CofD over oWoD.

                                                            ​CofD has a lot of potential as a brand precisely because it has no metaplot. Maybe not for Netflix TV shows or anything like that, but it would be great for video games and maybe even novels and comics as well. I mean Bloodlines was a great oWoD video game and as far as I can remember, it didn't really use the metaplot for anything beyond Easter Eggs here and there (well, except maybe the Great Leap Outward since Ming Xiao and the Kuei-Jin play a major role in the game, but that's really the extent of it). Hell, few if any of the metaplot characters from "Los Angeles by Night" are even mentioned in Bloodlines.
