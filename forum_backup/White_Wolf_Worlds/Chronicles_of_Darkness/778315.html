    Why do Ghost Wolves get the shaft so hard?

    12-02-2015, 10:50 AM
    To an extent, I get it. This is a game about being part of a pack, a larger community, and they want to encourage you to be part of that. But if you give me the option not to join a tribe, I'd at least like it to be a viable option. Especially when the book makes a point of saying that just because a guy doesn't join a tribe doesn't mean he's abandoned his duties or doesn't want to be part of the Uratha.

    Ghost Wolves get one less dot of renown than everyone else. They get one less gift. They can't start with the second dot of their moon gift. And for all of this they get... nothing. There's no trade-off. There's no benefit to it. If someone sees Ghost Wolves in the book and decides "That sounds kind of cool, I'd like to try that" then the book essentially slaps them in the face for seeing an option it presents them with an liking it.

    I get that werewolves join tribes for a reason. I get that in a game about being part of something larger than yourself, the guy who's standing off on his own is going to be missing out on something. But when you give me an option to play something in a game, I shouldn't just be punished for picking it.

    For contrast, in Masquerade, if you decided you wanted to play a Caitiff, there were some serious consequences to that, but there were a few fringe benefits. All your disciplines cost the same xp, more than an in-clan discipline, but less than for out-of clan, so you could play around with it. You didn't have a clan weakness, outside of being ostracized in vampire society. It was honestly not as good an option as picking a clan, but you got a few trade-offs for choosing the harder road.

    In Forsaken, you get the shaft.

      I suppose there's two different answers to this.

      The first is that in general in New World of Darkness games, not joining up is always presented as the worst possible option one can take. Being an unbound vampire or apostate mage is just as bad (and from the 2e spoilers, being a courtless changeling is practically a deathwish). As for why this is, one might make some guesses at some of the greater themes of NWoD, that the political game is one worth playing, the benefit of freedom is the freedom itself, etc.

      The second is that (and the Werewolf book isn't fully upfront with this) Tribal Bans are meant to be a huge deal to the Forsaken, and the big perk of being a Ghost Wolf is that you don't have to make a soul-binding oath to one of the Firstborn. You don't have to worry about paying each spirit in kind or honoring your territory in all things or such. Less chance of people banging down your door in the middle of the night as well, most likely. It's not much, but it's something. Maybe Secrets of the Tribes will have some fun stuff for Ghost Wolves.

                                      Things you get from a tribe in char gen: Gift affinity. Tribal merits/rites. Dot of Renown. Bonus Shadow gift.

                                      Gift affinities you get obviously from your association with the tribe and its totem.
                                      Tribe based merits/rites are obviously exclusive.
                                      Dot of renown you earn during your initiation trial.
                                      Gift you receive during the branding, one assumes - Or the elders take you on a hunt to teach you a gift in commemoration of your acceptance.

                                      The latter two are things that a werewolf could have as a Ghost wolf, but are not an intrinsic part of the creation of one. He is operating alone, without a millenia of tribal knowledge and a network of experienced tribemates and mentors. He is going to be at a disadvantage.

                                      That's part of the romance of playing the Ghost Wolf, as others have said. You are an underdog. You know the benefits of the system and you said fuck it anyway, I'm my own wolf. If a werewolf cared half as much as the OP does about the disadvantages, he'd have joined a tribe.

                                      I said before you can throw 3 xp to a player if he really feels disadvantaged, but it's not really necessary and generally doesn't make sense. All things being equal, a ghost wolf doesn't really gain more experience being alone than an uratha in a tribe does. He might have it a little tougher, but he's also missing out on the experience of tribemates. And honestly, he doesn't even really have it tougher when you consider tribal expectations toward prey and purpose.
