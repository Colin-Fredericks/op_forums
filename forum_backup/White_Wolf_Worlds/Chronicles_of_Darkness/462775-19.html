                        Everything has a spirit.

                        Evil has a spirit as well. If your antagonists can be the spirit of a river, the personification of anger over a football game, and the essence of corruption in politics, why not a vice?

                        It's unknown whether Maeljin are 'true' demons. Inferno says they are comparable to archdemons, but specifies it's only, 'for the purposes of this book,' while werewolf places them with spirits, more or less. All in all the books suggest they're a mix of both, like demon-corrupted spirits. Their interest is in the Hisil, expanding the Wounds within it until the entire Shadow is a bloody reflection of the world.

                        It's also possible they have a Firstborn on their side.

                        Due to their interests in the Hisil, it would make sense to reach out to Uratha to get things done, considering their abilities and renown. And in exchange, they offer rewards. Plus, considering the ease with which werewolves are tempted by vices, it's easy to fall into their service even if it's not your intention.

                        There's not much information about them in second edition yet so a lot may have changed. They may be some vestiges of Pangea, the same way werewolves are. Except rather than being remnants of the pillar of predators, they're remnants of the pillars of evil.

                        It's also worth noting that the Maeljin aren't exactly antagonists except in games of large scope. It's more likely you would confront Bale Hounds and Pure, than Maeljin and Pure Firstborn.

                                                Ignore that it's in the name for a moment, and just go with me - the Pure (or some Pure at least) refuse to believe Lunacy is anything to do with Luna.

                                                I mean, it can't be, right? Pure cause Lunacy and they've torn Luna's tumourous Auspice right out of themselves. What's left is wolf. Lunacy isn't about the moon - it's about human reaction to being faced with the most godawesome godawful terrifying predator they'll ever encounter, an inheritor of the Wolf itself. People aren't scared of the moon and it doesn't drive them into a desperate fight for survival - otherwise every time people looked at the sky it would be bam, there goes human civilisation. Put people in front of a wolf, though? Well they get fucking scared, or they get backed into a corner and fight cos there's nothing else to do, or they get crazy spiritual ideas about running with the wolves in their head after seeing the magnificence of the creatures.                                                             I keep going back and forth on it myself. I was originally thinking the Ivory Claws'd have them in their 'families,' perhaps at a diminished stature until they proved themselves, so they could keep from sullying their name by bringing in a wildcard. But then Elfive and Bunyip reminded me about the origins of Lunacy. But then Acrozatarim reminded me how it works in the current edition, which is supposed to be a supernaturally-bolstered sort of primal fear of predators like Mother Wolf, and I think he had a solid argument for the Pure. That's not counting in other werewolves who might take in new members they caused to change or who have no werewolves in their own families to help them through things.

                                                            Though Bunyip has a point, in werewolf and really the World of Darkness it feels 'right' when there's some groups with conflicting views. I originally asked because I could see wolves having a problem keeping their bloodlines alive solely through the old fashioned way, with all the wars popping up, but I do like how vampire has dynasties and in Masquerade there's pretty much a family tree connecting all the vampires. Which gets skewed if someone could just get 'chosen' and become one.

                                                            Either way I like that they're a part of it, especially with the Ivory Claws. It's amusing to think of someone having such a horrible time with their first encounter with werewolves that they have a dramatic failure on their Lunacy check, later end up turning into a werewolf, then have a more horrible time as the Ivory Claws decide they're now part of the family. And they take family seriously.

                                                            In the Autobiography of a Werewolf Hunter books, which I can't help but recommend,
                                                            they have something like Vampire the Masquerade, with an ancient progenitor werewolf, the Beast, who created Princes beneath him by sharing parts of his soul to 'mark' them, and they in turn marked others, and they marked others, all the way down the line. The problem being that they have one shared soul, an evil and powerful thing, but sharing it too much can stretch that power too thin. Which calls for occasional cullings by the higher ups. They're sort of like the Hosts, too, I thought, with the shared soul angle.


                                                            Anyways, thanks for the input and ideas. I like that one, Acrozatarim.
