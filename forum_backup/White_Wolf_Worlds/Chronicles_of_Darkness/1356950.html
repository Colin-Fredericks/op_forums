      I can run a Monte Carlo analysis later, but off the top of my head, it seems like it would increase the average number of successes and make big pools even better.

      My quick, informal reasoning: right now, on average, a 10 is worth 1.333… successes. In this system, on average, a 10 would be worth a bit less than 2 successes (since you sometimes don't have another 10 to pair it with). This would also give you an extra advantage to having a large pool, since having more dice increases the odds of a second 10 to pair it with.

                  The V5 method adds a completely unnecessary step in needing pairs. It's easier on the math and players to just have 10s count as two successes.

                  By itself, there isn't a huge problem, however you're massively skewing the statistical values of different options. The math of the CofD systems are built around 3 dice averaging 1 success, changing it to 2.5 dice averaging 1 success means everything's value changes. Dice adders give you more, exceptional successes become easier, systems that require specific numbers of successes (like inflicting Stun) are going to come up more, etc.

                  The biggest question is how much of the rest of the system are you willing to retool for this.

                              The difference between every 10 counting twice and V5 is...slight. Counting 10s twice, being more reliable, is more meaningful at smaller dice pools, but once they get big enough it's very small.

                              Why? From what I understand it's an artifact of the concept that lead to Messy Crits with the pairing of a Hunger 10 and a normal 10 for a special effect before things like how WP would work was settled on.

                              The level of variance... well.. depends. For basic rolls without Merits/powers and so on... it's not that big. But the more additional permutations that get applied the greater the variance becomes.

                                              Why not make it every 2 10's add 1 success? This makes it far closer to the normally expected 1.33 (ignoring rolling 10-again on a re-rolled 10) successes per 10. Since if you roll exactly even 10's it'll be 1.5 for those 10's, but if you roll an uneven number then the last 10 you roll essentially becomes just "1".

                                              So the average successes per 10 would depend on the amount of 10's you roll, and would be:
                                              (Amount of 10's : average successes/10)
                                              1: 1
                                              2: 1.5
                                              3: 1.33
                                              4: 1.5
                                              5: 1.4

                                              Across these 5 scenario's that would average 1.346... of course some of these scenario's will be more likely than others, but on the whole I don't think it skews the math enough to really matter on the global scale.
                                              So you could do this to make the gameplay slightly faster, or you can re-roll to have that re-roll excitement some players like.
