    Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
    First off, there's no exclusive territory on inspirations. Castlevania's Dracula can be both a Vampire and a Beast, as an example. Gamelines can share sources.

    Second, Has anyone here read Backup, Aftermath, Even Hand, or Bombshells?I've read the first three, and I'm running on assumptions based on that with the fourth.

    No one other than Dresden himself treats Dresden as just a person, a friend. His brother, his love, his enemy, his apprentice-they all speak of Dresden with a measure of dread. Sure, they know where he's benign-but they speak with horror of when Dresden lets loose, or even just how god-fucking creepy as shit it is when Dresden just draws answers from seemingly nowhere. People who have soulgazed him have consistently described him as terrifying.

    Since we read from Dresden's point of view, he's often humanized to us, but to outside perspectives, the man is this kinetic, inexorable force, a laughing, snide madman who flips off gods and demons and wins against them, with hands of wind and fire, haunted eyes that don't see you so much as take you all in-a tower, tall and dark, filled with secrets and schemes.

    So even Dresden's not outside of the sphere.

    Thirdly, Surely I can't say the same about Potter? Surely not. And yeah, maybe not by many of his peers or mentors standards-and yet, while it might cross over into Beast's territory of Hero-dread, consider Voldemort's position.

    Here is a child-a young man at best, perhaps, ultimately a child-marked by the hand of fate, a child who inexorably lives, this feigning, ill-begotten mark in the scheme of destiny, whose hands always burn, who's tools bind the mightiest wizard of an era down, who dives into danger after danger and emerges victorious again and again. He seems to wield, with utter innocence, this strange and impossible force, which he cannot beat time and time again regardless of how utterly simple and juvenille and sentimental it is. This Potter child bound in magics, forces, truths that the Dark Lord cannot comprehend, and they, to the bidding of the child, seem hell bent on destroying him.

    Voldemort blames everyone-anyone-he can for this-Dumbledore, the Potters, Grindelwald, Sirius Black, Death-whoever he can-because he's looking for a way to win, and he knows, as great as some of his foes might be, they operate on laws and logic he can understand. He can beat what he knows. Strip this child of all his support, and surely the terrifying forces at his beck and call will fade. And as Voldemort does just that, his fear grows, because this child, this young man, this occultist knows something he does not, and survives time and time again, an inexorable sun to end his dark night.

    And as for Harry's perspective, one need look no further than the moments when Dumbldore cuts lose, or how he views Voldemort himself-The minimum is awe, the greatest is terror.

    Yes, Potter and Dresden do a lot to mollify wizards, and like those narratives, people are too keen to look from the mage's point of view to see themselves, to be blinded by a certain point of view and fail to see-

    These witches and wizards twitch and the universe responds and they don't even seem to know how far their actions carry, how terrifying they are in their self-innocence.

    Even if Dresden were the strongest source for Mage(and it's not, Hellblazer's genes are much stronger), That would still clearly qualify them for their place in the pantheon of modern pulp horror. The fact that the most ardent and vocal fans for Mage often fail to see that just reinforces that there is nothing so terrifying as a powerful figure with little self-awareness.
    Excellent post. I would also add, as far as more obscure stuff goes, Peter Straub's Shadowlands novel as an excellent example of horror genre about people that practice magical traditions. What always surprises me the most I think when people attempt to bring up somewhat lighter aspects of magical people pop culture is that it almost always misses the point that well....people being able to manipulate reality is inherently horrific in and of itself. Obviously that falls at one end of a pretty wide spectrum, but it's as inherent to the idea as the idea of miraculous uses of magic.

        I think they were trying to get the Ascension crowd. The big three were all pretty clearly made full of attempts to get as far as possible away from the World of Darkness versions of the game while still keeping core elements.

        I really wish there was a Witchcraft source book, or some sort of blue book devoted to the occult and various beliefs that the common mortal can understand, or at least confront. The books are full of little pieces of fiction about random mortals who are somehow doing city-threatening things after stumbling upon an occult secret or forgotten lore but there's very little back-up for it in the books, and what there is is scattered across them and clearly not made with each other in mind, like the cults in Second Sight and the witches in Witch Finders.

        The structure for it is all there. There's cults, sorceries and rituals that are accessible to 'lesser' templates. There's been an Occult skill since the origin of the game but for the most part it's there to identify things. And the simple fact that Awakening is so clearly defined and set in its way gives you a lot of room to do other things that wouldn't infringe upon it.
