    Originally posted by Verlan View Post
    I think that the larger question then is why the books use the term soul to refer to an animating essence in the first place.
    Because the things that constitute a soul — the things the Subtle Arcana govern — include among them the consciousness, the animating essence, the capacity for emotional resonance, the predispositions of one's role in the world, and the parts of oneself that linger after death (including character flaws and moral failings).

    When removing the package deal that these things correlate to causes a character to be given over to their personal weaknesses, more susceptible to being overtaken by possessing entities, and cut off from their inner landscape, eventually losing their inclination to act on their own initiative, that tends to check a lot of people's boxes for "has this person Lost Something Spiritually Important?"

    In general, I feel like the established cosmology of the setting tends to overshadow the worldviews of individual mages.
    Because the books are mainly written to provide the established cosmology of the setting first and example characters second, since they're mostly tools for troupes to create characters and local settings for their own stories.

    The core rulebook exemplifies this pretty well. In the beginning, the book clearly differentiates mortal magic from Supernal magic, and yet the book also constantly references mortal religious traditions when it speaks of mages.
    Because many mortal religious traditions have magical traditions that inform mages' backgrounds and inform their use of magic the way literally anything else does. Religion is a symbol-dense subject and magic is the leveraging of symbolism to have concrete effects upon the world.

    It is distinctly possible I have misunderstood your meaning here, in which case I would ask you to explain what you mean by "constantly referencing mortal religious traditions" and "clearly differentiating mortal magic from Supernal magic."

    Also, Awakening does not really do a good job of establishing that what the game's mechanics refer to as a soul might not match philosophical models of the soul. For example, the whole philosophy of the Tremere in 2e is reliant upon the idea that they really do strip themselves of their souls, and are thus able to identify with a monistic world-soul. The Tremere very much interact with the soul presented by the game's mechanics. If anything, the Awakened should be very aware of the fact that what seem like distant, abstract questions are actually empirical in nature.
    The Tremere are the subject-matter experts in souls among the Awakened. They are also a bunch of evil bastards who socially poisoned the well when their unsavory activities were found out along with taking most of their findings in the divorce from the Diamond. The Tremere are so far underground that the fact they're still an organized group is not common knowledge. "The Awakened should know this hidden lore about how souls work" is absolutely not true in the modern setting as-presented specifically because the premier example of a Reaper in the world is "those monsters who were cannibalizing soul-related Legacies behind our backs when we worked with them centuries ago and who still show up every now and then making trouble by stealing people's souls, a thing any mage with a moderate amount of Death lore can also do."

    Separate from that, the Tremere are also attaching ideology to the fact that the Hollow where their soul used to be allows them to do magic and physically interact with souls without specific Arcana in ways that gel with their disdain for the inhuman. They don't have any insight on how a hypothetical afterlife works that any other mage couldn't stumble upon, nor what normally happens to the soul after death; they don't know if werewolves go to Hell or if there's any actual benefits to high Integrity beyond those evidenced in life, and the distinction between "an individual human soul is a discrete object that happens to be internally connected to every other human soul through the Astral" and "the Astral demonstrates that every individual soul is a branch of the world-soul" is so fine as to be philosophical.

    Again, it is possible I have misunderstood your meaning, in which case I would ask for examples of these "distant, abstract questions" that are supposedly actually empirical.

                                            In the matter of ghosts being souls, page 97 of the Mage 2ed core book states that a person’s soul is not their mind, spirit, or the source of their morality. It also states it is not made of ephemera, which ghosts are. Mage Sight (active and focused) draw a clear distinction between ghosts and souls.

                                            In page 185 of the Geist 2ed core book, there is a tab that specifically mentions ghosts are not souls. Ghosts are created when the powerful death resonance of an event leaves an imprint in Twilight. A minor imprint (Rank 1) is stuck in a loop, a stronger one is closer to the person that was imprinted (Rank 2) and anything beyond are beings affected by the Underworld's Resonance (Geist 2ed core book page 187). The existance of multiple Dopplegangers while a person is alive provides further evidence to differentiate the person from the ghosts they create (Geist 2ed page 196).

                                            In the subject of the Awakened being always right, page 239 of the Mage 2ed core book has a side tab that points out the opposite. Ultimately the occult is hidden from mundane eyes and no one has a perfect picture, with the different game lines providing infrastructure to build stories on. Exploring how it relates to a religious traditions and how it affects a character is a possible direction for a chronicle. It all depends to what suit the the mood and themes of your chronicles, as well as what your table is comfortable with.

Awakening itself presents its own very specific image of the wizard, and uses terminology from a variety of traditions in order to present it.
                                                My dude, one of the primary antagonist groups in this setting serves the Demiurges who want humanity to remain trapped in spiritual infancy. There is a great gulf of ignorance separating the Truth from experiential reality. Mind/body dualism is more strongly encoded into the standard model for Awakened magic than for many other monsters and the primary counterpoint to sensual pleasures being a distraction from higher pursuits is in ecstatic practices. The world is set up to prevent people from finding out the Truth, to the specific benefit of the bastards that started and sustain this state of affairs. Chronicles and particularly Awakening is a Gnostic setting.

                                                Furthermore, Awakening has presented ghosts in their classic magico-religious contexts. Ghosts have commonly been held to remember things from their life, and they have often been thought to possess supernatural perceptions that render communication with them practically useful.
                                                Yes, this is the thing that I just said by way of explaining how being able to talk to ghosts does not mean mages have proof of an afterlife that is not part of the subtranscendent Realms Invisible.

                                                At the end of the day, Awakening draws from real-world traditions in an erratic manner in order to manifest its setting, and within that setting, presents such traditions as misguided. Instead, the cosmologies of the Awakened are presented as superior. It would not really matter if the authors were clear that that was their intent, and that they intended no disrespect to the religions that they mined for content, but that is not the case.
                                                This would be the least bit relevant if Mage: the Awakening made any pretense of being a game specifically about being a practitioner of real-world magical traditions rendered supernaturally efficacious, if the lines released before it were not similarly clear that their take on vampires and werewolves had pronounced differences from at least some subset of folkloric or pop-cultural vampires and werewolves, and if Chronicles as a whole were an All Occult Beliefs Are Unconditionally True setting instead of, y'know, running the spectrum from horror/mystery-flavored urban fantasy to your various subtypes of horror where "alienation" is a theme or subtheme in at least half the gamelines.

                                                In contrast to Ascension's "everyone starts with a paradigm and as they rise in Arete the training wheels start to come off," Awakening runs on "everyone can leverage any symbolism they can recognize and that means career wizards are magical magpies who favor the tools they're best at using." The Diamond is an organization that was created to better understand the magical world as the Awakened experience it, which is not the primary purpose behind the esoteric doctrines of e.g. Mormonism. Mages who encounter a contradiction in terminology between reality and their religious beliefs either synthesize a new understanding of the two or let one fall by the wayside, but either way their own powers are beholden to their own wills and perspectives, which shape the uses they put them to but do not have causative influence on the reality they interact with.

                                                "Mage doesn't canonize real-world magical traditions as true" is a statement on the same level as "Werewolf isn't a game about playing The Wolf Man" or "Changeling isn't a game about playing the fairy that was left in the crib" or "Demon isn't a game about playing an escapee from Hell." Chronicles of Darkness games are often built toward the end of playing beings with only partial relation to the common archetype of a given sort of monster, to the point where the two gamelines whose characters are the trickiest to describe are the ones with an intrinsic connection to a specific branch of the setting's cosmology; "the game about investigating all sorts of magical weirdness and Fortean phenomena privileges the perspective of magical researchers powered by the transcendent force that they use to describe reality and the evidence of their magical eyes over the teachings of their nonmagical upbringing that they broke from to become a wizard" doesn't rate.
