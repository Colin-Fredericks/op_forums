    Originally posted by PenDragon View Post
    Maybe it would it help if I explained my reasoning for the assorted changes:
    • Cost: This one was pure preference. I like my Mages casting, but not all the damn time, and wanted to emphasize the importance of mana and hollows. I deemed that, given the boost in power, Rotes could use to be cut back somewhere, Cost was the obvious choice. As an added benefit, along with the removal of Ruling Arcana, this lets Praxis shine as the only spells Mages can cast freely. My players used their Praxis non-stop, which further focused and differentiated the characters.
    • Reach: This change was for two reasons. First and foremost, with everyone having access to Rotes I needed to differentiate between characters who were actually good at the Arcana and those that merely dabbled. Sure Arcana is already in the dice pool, but 1-5 dice seemed too little, especially given that they could be offset with Yantra easily enough. Reach was the other clear option, and from experience the hack does a good job of making it clear who the real masters are. Speaking of masters, point two I wanted there to be some Reach based benefit to learning a Rote as a master. Making it possible for 5 dot spells to be cast safely by making them Rotes fit the bill.
    • Prerequisites: You already hit this nail on the head, the removal of prereqs gives the Orders a much clearer reason to covet their Rotes, and lets players (and storytellers) give mages interesting powers without having to give them, say, five dots of Time and everything that comes with it.
    I can say, with confidence, that all these changes went over really well. My players were eager to invest in Rotes and prefered casting Rotes over relying on standard Creative Thaumatugy whenever possible. They are just a better value for the mana. The above Sun example also showcased why novices shouldn't be given five dot Rotes, as when the spell was used he had nowhere near enough Reach to hit the scale he was aiming for and ended up nearly killing himself to soak the Paradox.

    Edit: Hot damn, I forgot to add my Paradox hack to the doc. How the hell did I forget that?
    Well, that definitely gives me a better idea on how to handle my hacks.

    I might let Rotes cost 1 Mana, at minimum, and apply the reduction once a Mage meets the prerequisite Arcana. I might go a different direction, though, with differentiating Praxes. Especially since I'm very receptive to the idea of a very skilled Mage being able to synergize a spell he knows, by heart, with the benefits afforded by a Rote's encoded workings.

                    Similarly, if you don't do the cost change, Praxis would need a different direction.
                    Hmm. Well, I have tentative notes put together. Allow me to post them here for easier comparison....

                    -=-

                    Praxes:
                    * Consider all magical tools as Dedicated (actual Dedicated Tools double their benefits).
                    * Cost one less Mana to cast, unless the Mage does not meet the prerequisite Arcana.
                    * Do not suffer from Paradoxical Casting.
                    * Any extra Reach in excess of the Mage's Wisdom does not add +1 to Paradox rolls.
                    * Score an Exceptional Success at three successes.


                    Rotes:
                    * Add dots in an associated Skill as a Yantra to the dice pool.
                    * Cost one less Mana to cast, unless the Mage does not meet the prerequisite Arcana.
                    * If a Rote's Skill is a specialty of the Mage's Order, add bonus dice equal to half the Mage's dots in Order Status (rounded up).
                    * Reduces Paradox dice by half the Rote's associated Skill dots (rounded up).
                    * The Signature Nimbus of the caster is indistinct, hiding the caster’s identity unless another Mage fully Scrutinizes the spell.

                                    Little Mage hack update:

                                    After some consideration on the words of DaveB regarding the Withstood mechanic I've opted to drop my Withstand hacks (which allowed characters to use Supernal advantage) in place of a universal Clash mechanic. Mages can use Nimbus to clash with supernatural effects that would warp their mind, body or soul. Similarly, all other splats have some means of actively opposing supernatural effects. Active being the operative word here, and the reason for the change, since I find active countermeasures more rewarding to the players and less punishing than just saying "nope, you don't beat his Withstand rating". It's also cooler to have supernaturals flashing their true nature when they start to butt heads.

                                                    You have seen the cycles of change that govern everything from beasts and men to empires and gods, and now you know the Truth. No one ever steps into the same river twice, the hero of today is the villain of tomorrow and life can come from ashes. To insist on permanence is futile, and to understand the movements of the cosmic wheel is paramount.

                                                    Possible Sources: Moros or Thyrsus Awakening

                                                    Resolution: None

                                                    Beat: You contribute to changing people and places with a focus on the long run, even if you have to disrupt their more immediate plans. This applies to yourself as well.
