    Chapter 9 – Firestorm

    The throng raced through the dust, stumbling almost blind in search of a way to escape their pursuer. They could hear the Pandoran on their trail, its heavy breathing echoing over even the howling winds of the Black Blizzard. They never laid sight on it, but they never lost the sound. The desperate, scrambling minutes dragged on, pushing through the storm, dust filling their eyes and noses and mouths. A massive shape loomed out of the storm and they shied away instinctively before they realised that it was a just another dead tree. They paused there and realised they could no longer hear the breathing. Riley dropped from Terah’s saddle, giving the exhausted horse a reprieve from carrying the weight of two Prometheans, and checked on Abram. The Tammuz was clutching his wound, but he didn’t appear to be in any immediate danger.

    Then the dust erupted around them, and the beast was on top of them. Terah screamed. Elsie reacted the fasted. She reached up and thrust Baby into Abram’s arms. Riley struck Terah, and the horse took off carrying Abram away. The beast snarled, but it made no move to chase the horse. The two cornered Prometheans were the more tempting prey.

    It moved slowly now, confident that it had won. Its head drifted between Elsie and Riley. This close, they could make out the traces of humanity they had not had time to notice when it first attacked. Despite its huge size and insect-like mandibles, its head followed the curvature of a human skull. They could see cheekbones and traces of other features, but they fixated on the eyes. It had two human eyes, far too small for its head, squinting at them.

    The Pandoran sniffed, looked at Riley closely, and growled out, “Are you ill, little one? Ill or wounded? Come to Typhoeus. He will make it better.”

    Riley shivered. They made no move forward.

    This seemed to frustrate Typhoeus. His mandibles clicked. A few tense seconds passed, and then, “I apologise for before. I did not mean to hurt any of you. But I was burned dry. I could not control myself.”

    Riley glared up at him. “What do you want?”

    Typhoeus’s head withdrew. He fixed his gaze on Elsie instead, and declared that he wanted a child. Not some budding from his own flesh, but a child of his own blood. Kin to himself, Prometheans like them. He asked them to stay, to be his children, for him to care for and stroke and pet.

    Elsie stepped forward. The movement took Riley by surprise, they were too late to grab her and hold her back. She walked right up to Typhoeus and reached out a hand to stroke the chitin of his closest limb. The massive Pandoran leaned in to the gesture, curling its body around her to shield her from the wind and dust of the blizzard. In that small bubble Elsie smiled her most brilliant smile and told Typhoeus exactly what he wanted to hear. She admired his strength, his raw power, his dominion over this land. His physical form was something she thought should be admired. The beast made a sound in its throat that could almost have been a ghastly purr. Then, collecting itself, it asked her if she would stay with him.

    He never got an answer. Riley had pulled the revolver they had found in Malik’s camp from their jacket, and aimed it into the air. They pulled the trigger. The world exploded in fire and lightning.

    Amidst Typhoeus’s screams, the roar of the storm, the roll of thunder, the rage of the Firestorm, Riley grabbed Elsie and dragged her away from the thrashing Pandoran. They ran as fast as they could, their clothes beginning to smoulder. The bone-dry trees of the Bone Fields had ignited. They could see them through the storm, pillars of fire blazing like beacons. The wind whipped flame into a chaotic maelstrom of flying streams and eddies.

    Typhoeus rose behind them, fire streaming across his armoured body. His head pulled back, his maw yawned open, and a stream of bile jetted out at Riley. They ducked, the corrosive liquid raining droplets on them, but they were unharmed. Then the fire roared up in front of them, a wall of flame without mercy. Riley stared at it, looked at Elsie, and back at Typhoeus. With no other choice, the Prometheans stepped through it together.

    They emerged out the other side. Riley was screaming and clutching their left arm, the flames burning right to the bone. Their Pyros-infused flesh was burning no less fiercely than the dead trees all around them. To their right, Elsie was staring straight ahead, her face blank. Her hair and neck burned, but she paid it little mind, as though something had snapped inside. She ran forward, continuing to flee, mechanically and without a glance at Riley. Gritting their teeth, Riley rushed to catch up.

    Typhoeus was slowed by the same wall of fire. In the chaos and confusion they soon lost sight of him. Lightning strikes struck all around them in waves, one bolt lancing straight into Riley’s chest. Their fingers and toes curled as the energies coursed through them, reinvigorating them, healing the remains of their wounds from Sioc. It gave them enough energy to attempt one last attempt to evade their pursuer. Riley poured their remaining Pyros into igniting an alchemical transmutation within their body. They grew larger, dropping to four legs. In moments they had mimicked Terah’s form. Elsie clambered on and Riley called on newfound strength.

    The firestorm burned out after only a few seconds, but Typhoeus was not so easily shaken. They rode through the blizzard for what felt like hours, the massive Pandoran keeping pace with Riley at times, at others lagging behind, but always on their trail. At last Riley broke free from the storm, the dust clearing from the air so suddenly it was as if a knife had been used to mark the storm’s edge. Up ahead they could see a line of silver running straight across the horizon. A railway line.

    They raced the storm. It roiled and curled behind them, pursuing them, Typhoeus hidden somewhere within it. A train came into view, coming from the north. Riley could see Abram and Terah well ahead of them, angling towards the train as well. As they drew level, a door in the side of one of the train’s containers was hauled back. Terah seemed to require no urging, leaping inside.

    Riley could almost feel Typhoeus’s breath on their neck. They lengthened their stride for one final burst. They followed in Terah’s footsteps, aiming for the same carriage hauled by the long freight train. The door was still open. As they drew closer, they could see a figure standing in the opening. They recognised him. A weathered man, his age indeterminate, they had last seen on the night Malik left them. The man that had driven them apart.

    Elsie, for her part, also recognised the man. He had sold her a radio a few weeks ago, something she still carried with her. He had said his name was John Shepherd. They were close enough to touch. She stood up, keeping her balance on the back of a galloping horse with astonishing grace, and held out a hand. Shepherd took it, and she stepped daintily from the horse into the darkness of the carriage. She had to step quickly to one side, as a few seconds later Riley leapt aboard.

    The carriage door was hauled shut. The storm hit. The interior of the carriage went completely dark.

    Elsie found her way to Baby and nursed him as she listened to the Black Blizzard rage. Only the leading edge of the storm caught them before they left it behind, and it could not reach them inside. She settled down, listening as the others moved around. She called out to Shepherd. He spoke from behind her, and the two sat in the dark and talked. She prompted him to hold Baby, which he appeared to find intensely uncomfortable, and then asked him if he knew what had chased them. He said he did. It was a monster born in the mines to the west by a ritual gone awry. Its birthplace had been filled with Prometheans, and it had gorged, grown strong, and made its way to the Dust Bowl in search of food.

    When light began to return to the carriage, John Shepherd was gone. Riley had holed up in a corner, as far from where Elsie and Shepherd had been sitting as possible. There were several other occupants in the carriage, hobos from the north. They had pushed the crates in the half-empty carriage up against the walls and made a nice little camp for themselves. Elsie chatted with a man who introduced himself as Damien Sharp, who became very friendly when she mentioned that she was friends with John Shepherd. “Any friend of John’s is a friend of ours,” he told her.

    They found that the train was heading to Sour Springs. The hobos hoped to find work there or in Wakesfield, further down the line. Riley drifted off to sleep, but Elsie wasn’t able to relax in the rattling, clanking carriage. When they arrived at Sour Springs the hobos disembarked quickly, before they could be found, and split up once they hit the streets. The throng made its way through Sour Springs, finding it a quiet town with many empty, abandoned buildings. They found a shop not too far from the centre of town that was visibly empty, and chose it as a place to stay while they were in town.

    The empty shelves and a pile of blankets in one corner of the room showed that this place had been used by squatters before, but not recently to judge by the dust. Here, at last, Elsie was able to get some sleep. Riley, a small measure of Pyros returned by the recent sunrise, peered into Twilight after noticing that their spirit companion was fixated on something in the corner of the room. They looked and found a small, grub-like creature about the size of a cat. It stirred under their gaze and looked up at them with the face of a child but weathered and lined like an old man’s. The dirt caking its face was parted by tear trails running down its cheeks.

    The creature didn’t appear to understand English. It certainly made no attempt to reply to Riley’s words. Their spirit companion didn’t seem threatened by the creature, just alert. In the end they decided to wait out the day. They were all injured, Riley had no interest in speaking to any human and wanted time to mull over the appearance of John Shepherd, and it seemed safest to stay hidden just for now. They drifted off to sleep later that night, one by one.

    Riley opened their eyes. They were lying where they had been when they fell asleep. It felt like only moments had passed, but they didn’t feel tired anymore. They looked around the room and could see Abram and Elsie there, but something had certainly changed. The windows of the store were now shattered, but there was no glass on the floor. Riley stepped outside.

    It could almost be Sour Springs, if they turned their head to the side and squinted. The shattered windows and broken doors of the houses gave them the look of gruesome, damaged faces. Some of the buildings were a lot larger than they had been in daylight, and Riley realised that it was as if the buildings were moving in the corner of their eye, crawling on one another, feeding. Above it all loomed the Town Hall Clocktower. It had been a squat, simple structure in the daylight. Now it was this immense gothic monolith, adorned in spires and gargoyles. Riley rushed back inside to find the store’s shelves were now full, each shelf stocking a different human bone.

    They knew they were dreaming but, somehow, they also knew with unerring certainty that Elsie and Abram were in this dream with them. There was no sign of Baby, Terah, or Riley’s spirit companion. They went outside, together, and looked up at the tower looming over them all.

        Chapter 10 – The Nightmare of Sour Springs

        Abram called out, his voice echoing through the empty streets. Riley hushed him hurriedly, but it was too late. They heard howls in response, seeming to come from every direction at once. The three Prometheans set off towards the tower at once, jogging down the middle of the road, when they heard a low voice hiss at them from a broken doorway. They saw a young woman holding an axe in front of her, beckoning them urgently.

        Up close the woman had coarse, blunt features and was dressed in little more than rags. The house they were standing in was in ruins, every piece of furniture had been smashed to pieces. Once they were all inside, she asked them what the hell they were doing. It came out that it was their first night in Sour Springs. The woman introduced herself as Marcy. In the background Elsie busied herself collecting scraps of cloth and cushion fragments from among the debris. Then they heard the footsteps.

        They all took cover at Marcy’s urging. The beast’s smell came first, a thick musk and the stench of wet fur. It was a large, heavyset creature, bigger than Abram. Its furred body was like that of an enormous ape, though its head put them more in mind of a toad. Its broad mouth hung open as it walked, giving Riley, lurking in the doorway, a view of its wide, red gullet ringed with row after row of teeth.

        Once it had passed the throng turned to Marcy and demanded to know about the creature. They found out that it and two others like it haunted the dream, hunting the people of Sour Springs night after night. Dying in the dream didn’t kill you, but you had to endure a very lifelike death and you always woke sore and feeling bruised from the experience. That description sounded like a challenge to Abram. He had refused to shift from his Soldier Role since completing it, and felt like his failure to stand up to Typhoeus needed to be rectified. He offered to help Marcy with the town’s problem, and asked if anyone had tried to kill the beasts. She told him that the prisoners and guards from the town lock-up formed a mob each night and tried to bring the beast down. Sometimes they even succeeded, but most nights they just died in droves. It didn’t seem to be achieving anything.

        The Prometheans had fixated on the immense clocktower that the town hall had become. With the coast clear, they left Marcy in her hiding spot and made their way towards it. Abram collected some of the black bile from his still-oozing wounds and used it to start painting on the wall of the clocktower, once they found there was no visible entrance. Then they took up hiding spots in the nearby buildings and waited for a beast to show itself.

        The creature that appeared was closer to a humanoid wolf. Its crocodilian muzzle was grey with age, and it moved with a grace the thunderous creature from before had been lacking. It reached Abram’s artwork and sniffed, clearly picking up his scent. Then Riley shot it in the back. It howled, and the others rushed from hiding. Abram struck it with his pickaxe, sinking it deep. Elsie threw herself onto the beast, trying to find its neck through the fur. The two writhed together, the swirling mass of fur and flesh making it impossible for Riley to get a clear bead on the beast for a second shot. They charged instead and threw themselves into the scrum.

        It was to no avail. The creature’s fur began to crackle with energy, rising into a shimmering corona of Pyros-infused lightning. The blast swept out from it, striking Abram and sending him convulsing to the ground, the light dying from his eyes. It twisted and seized Elsie’s arm in its jaws, clamping down and tearing it free from her body. As she crumpled it flung Riley from its back, pinning them to the ground. The last thing Riley saw was its jaws closing in.

        They woke in the early hours of the morning, aching and sore. After some discussion they decided further heroics would need to wait until their wounds had healed. Instead they went to visit the town hall in daylight. The plain, wooden building with its short clocktower sported no sign of the painting Abram had made in the dream, to his disappointment. Sadly, they were unable to get past the clerk in the lobby. He refused to let them in without an appointment, and refused to let them make an appointment. The best they were able to get was a set of directions to the office. They passed a morbidly obese man struggling up the stairs as they left, but paid him no mind.

        Days passed, as they waited for the burns they had suffered in the Firestorm to healed. They stayed inside their abandoned building in their waking hours and in their sleep. Every night the dream appeared to reset, and they rebuilt hasty fortifications to keep the monsters out night after night. During daylight Riley would make a show of reading the Bible, hoping to provoke Abram into asking to learn. Abram, for his part, got a glimmer of Riley’s intent through their shared brand. He relented, and asked for some lessons.

        That night, when their eyes opened into the nightmare of Sour Springs, they felt ready. They stepped out into the street together, eager for another chance at the beasts.

                            Chapter 11 - Everything is fine

                            Riley stood in the shadows of a doorway across the street from their shelter in Sour Springs. They were in the nightmare, taking their first steps outside in several weeks. Elsie was standing on the street corner, idly looking at the sky.

                            A tile smashed onto the street. The two Prometheans turned, and looked up. The distorted silhouette of the closest building became a roaring, leaping blur of teeth and fur. Elsie stepped neatly out of the way just before it reached her and the large, greying wolf-like beast slammed into the pavement, cracking it with the force of its impact.

                            They had a brief moment where they recognised the beast as the same creature they had fought before, at the base of the tower. It was the older of the two beasts they had seen, lean with a greying muzzle. It turned towards Elsie, rising to shoulder height even on all fours. She stretched out a hand as if to stroke its head. The beast lunged straight at the proffered limb, tearing it away, before it pinned her to the ground and crushed her skull between its teeth.

                            Riley was running before the beast's jaws snapped shut. They burst into the building and raced through the ruined interior, making for the back door. They slammed into it, but the door appeared to be jammed. Then, behind them, they heard the terrible sound of wood snapping and breaking as the beast forced its way through the wall. The house began to come apart. Riley pressed their back against the wall, just out of reach of the beast's flailing arms, and fired two shots into the creature's chest. The bullets made a strange sound as they struck home, and for a moment they could glimpse something like an armoured shell beneath the fur.

                            Then a second massive, corpulent arm reached in through the ruin of the house's facade. It pulled away more of the roof and wall, and the bloated beast Riley had seen when they hid with Marcy on their first night in the nightmare pulled itself into view. Its arms reached for them, seized Riley before they could get out of the way, and pulled them into a rib-snapping embrace. This seemed to anger the first, older beast. It lashed out, tearing chunks from the larger beast's face. Riley's face was spattered with a clear, viscous liquid that flew from the wound. They struggled, to no success, unable to get free. They felt their hair rise to stand on end and smelt the tell-tale trace of ozone as the older beast's fur began to crackle with lightning. The blast swept outward, and Riley was knocked free to die, shuddering, on the floor.

                            Elsie and Riley woke not long after, feeling sore and bruised. The injuries faded quickly, but it was a couple of hours before dawn started to creep in through the window and either of them felt like moving again. In the meantime they talked over their plans. Riley was resolved to staying in Sour Springs for some time, at least until they could pick up Malik's trail once again. In the meantime they wanted to help the people here. Dealing with the pig spirit had inspired them, they wanted to create a service for the people of Sour Springs where people could come to them for help with supernatural problems. Elsie offered to help by gathering together some supplies to make posters. She tore strips of wallpaper from the walls to serve as paper, but no one in the group had anything to write with. So she set off to get a pen.

                            She returned about an hour later with the pen.

                            Nothing bad happened.

                            Everything is fine.

                            Honest.

                                    Chapter 12 - Meanwhile...

                                    For once, Elsie had not taken Baby with her when she left the building. She had just left her wrapped up in the middle of the floor of their little hovel. Abram and Riley were engaged in the middle of a writing lesson when they heard her start to cry. Calling on some long unused instincts of his human body, Abram was able to calm the child while Riley hauled out any vegetables they had that weren't too rotten and set about boiling and mashing them.

                                    Together, they managed to feed Baby. Riley started asking Abram probing questions about how babies were made, and somehow the conversation ended up on prostitution. Riley ended up forcing an Elpis Vision, trying to divine if their future held any of these fascinating subjects. The cavalcade of vivid images did reveal to them an upcoming milestone that would require an extended period of time spent in close company with humans. The process infused the surrounding air with their humour, however, and the throng felt a Wasteland fester into being.

                                    Abram kept Baby with them for the next few hours, until Elsie returned. She was at once overjoyed to see Abram taking an interest in her child, and struggled to keep her jealousy at the attention from boiling over. She took Baby back, soothed her when she cried, and happily turned over the pen to Riley. When Riley tried to ask her how she'd gotten one, since she hadn't taken any money with her, she dodged the question. Poorly. She retreated with Baby to a corner of the room as the skeptical Riley noted that one of her shoes was damp. Between them, Riley and Abram agreed that Elsie must have certainly stolen the pen.

                                    Nevertheless, the throng put their suspicions aside and they all began to work on advertising posters. Elsie stripped wallpaper from the walls, Abram decorated them, and Riley wrote down details. Between the three of them they soon had a small pile of flyers and posters. The posters promised to help anyone who asked for help with their nightmares, and gave directions on how to contact the throng.

                                    It was starting to grow late again, and Abram and Riley had been discussing the Pyros-linked nature of the monsters in the dream. They figured that since fire worked particularly well on their own flesh, it may be similarly effective against the beasts. Since it seemed that they needed to have items in the real world to carry them over to the dream world, they decided to get their hands on some alcohol so they could make some flammable projectiles for the night ahead.

                                    Unfortunately, this proved more difficult than expected. A town the size of Sour Springs should surely have had a speakeasy or similar, but Abram was unable to persuade anyone to tell him where he could find it. They tried the General Store, but found it cordoned off and surrounded by a small crowd of horrified and distressed looking people. Rather than inquire they headed back to their lodgings, when they spotted the filthy-looking barbershop across the street. Aftershave could work in a pinch, Abram figured.

                                    The grimy shop turned out to be even worse on the inside. The door had been propped open, as if to invite in customers, but in doing so had allowed dust to blow in from the street. Dust, water, and hair had combined to make a thick sludge that pooled in the corners of the room, beneath the counter, and in any other moderately hard to reach spots. The barber, a thin, middle-aged man wearing stained clothes and smelling strongly of old sweat, got slowly to his feet and eyed Riley.

                                    "It'll be extra for that one," he said, his voice low and hoarse.

                                    The Prometheans exchanged a look, not sure if he was referring to Riley's race or their long, thick hair. Abram carefully explained that they had come to buy some aftershave. The barber was quiet for far too long, before finally agreeing to go and get some for them. He shuffled off, returned with three bottles, and demanded a price far beyond that which Abram was willing to pay, certainly far more than the aftershave was worth. A warning hand from Riley was the only thing that stopped the scene from escalating to violence. Instead, Abram merely threatened the barber, grabbed the aftershave, and left behind a fraction of the asking price. For the barber's part he didn't put up a fight, merely shrinking away from the Prometheans and hiding his face.

                                    That night, as they dreamed, the Prometheans felt that they were at last prepared to take on the beasts. Abram was still feeling vindictive, so he made his way across to the dream-version of the barbershop and started to lay about with his pickaxe. He smashed open the rear wall of the store, where glistening scalps lay in place of hair, and stepped out into a new street where he could hear the sounds of fighting. The throng raced towards the noise and walked straight into the midst of a slaughter. Men, some manacled and clad in prisoner uniforms, others wearing the outfits of local deputies, were fighting a beast none of them had seen before. It was smaller than the other two, with paler fur. It was cornered against the wall of one building, but it was still lashing out with its claws at any man who got too close. The street was littered with dismembered bodies.

                                    The Prometheans wasted no time gawping. Abram hurled the first of their makeshift explosives at the beast, setting its fur ablaze. As it howled in pain, Riley hurled the second... but their aim went wide and flames quickly spread among the human mob. In the chaos Elsie quickly climbed up the side of a nearby building and hurled herself onto the beast's back, but she was unable to get a purchase and was thrown into a wall.

                                    The beast's jaws opened wide and a thick, grey fog rolled out. As it swept across the humans their faces fell and they cast their weapons away, throwing themselves to their knee. Riley breathed in the fog and felt the hopelessness of their situation. The beast was growing larger, visibly enraged but unharmed from their attacks. They knew this fight would never work. The flames roared over them as they stood there, unmoving, giving up all hope.

                                    Abram and Elsie were made of sterner stuff. The fire was roaring out of control, but they charged through the fire into the beast's open arms. The beast tore open Abram's chest, moments before he brought his pickaxe down on its head. Its skull split open and it collapsed, even as the fire consumed Abram's tendons and muscles. His limbs gave way and he collapsed to the street.

                                    Elsie alone saw the change that spread across the deputies as their quarry died. They threw their heads back, howled and picked up their weapons once more. She followed them as they charged through the streets, a savage pack looking for their next prey. Then they stumbled out onto the main street and found the elder beast waiting for them. Lightning crackled across its skin, and Elsie's last sight before she too succumbed was of the electricity dancing from man to man, downing each in turn.

                                        Chapter 13 - The cracks are showing

                                        The characters woke from their disturbed, confused nightmares in the early hours of the morning. They spoke among themselves and resolved to locate the local speakeasy or equivalent gathering spot and try to find someone interested in hiring them on as supernatural investigators. Then they had to pass a few hours until the sun had risen. Abram whittled a ball from a lump of wood and played a simplistic game of catch with Riley, but the whole time he clearly had something on his mind. Tensions between him and Elsie over the disregard with which she treated Baby boiled over into a full-blown row. Elsie was more than happy to yell insults and recriminations back at Abram, though her attacks left him rather confused. Her every response to his criticisms of her parenting styles was to attack his, or Riley's, appearance. Riley, wanting no part of the argument, ran out to be with Terah. Eventually, Abram too left, leaving Elsie alone with Baby.

                                        Crooning over the child, Elsie resolved to make sure that the growing bond between Baby and Abram went no further. She activated her Weaponise Transmutation to force Disquiet into Baby's system, knowing that the attraction her Disquiet inspired would keep Baby close.

                                        Meanwhile, Abram conducted an early search of the town and found some men who informed him circumspectly that the place to get a drink locally was Mike's Roadhouse. The throng gathered together, a little reluctantly, to check it out and advertise their new supernatural problem solving business.

                                        They waited until midday before heading over. They found the roadhouse to be clean and almost entirely empty. The desk was unmanned. They struck the bell, and it took several minutes before a big, greasy man came through the back door. He sized them up, identified Abram as the leader, snorted, ran a hand through his hair, and went in for a handshake. He introduced himself as Mike, and asked if they were after rooms. Abram quickly gathered from the comments Mike made that the roadhouse itself must have no, or almost no, clients.

                                        It took a little bit of persuading to allow three strangers inside, but Abram was able to convince Mike of his credibility. They ended up through the back door and into the basement. A simple speakeasy-style arrangement had been set up there, with the walls decorated by sepia pictures of Sour Springs. There were two men at a table in the corner, a half-finished drink showing that Mike had clearly been with them.

                                        Riley and Elsie set up in a corner while Abram talked to the men. He wanted to find out if any of the men were interested in help with their dreams, but Mike seemed to interpret the questions as Abram looking for mob work and turned him down. When Abram clarified, he was laughed off.

                                        Over the next few hours, as men finished their working shifts, the roadhouse began to fill. Elsie took the opportunity to leave Baby with Riley and to start flirting with Mike. She was quickly the centre of attention, much to Abram's disapproval. She found out that Mike had a car, which was going to be used for an 8 o'clock delivery, and he promised to take her for a drive afterwards.

                                        In the midst of this one of the local labourers came up to Riley's table. He didn't introduce himself, and halfheartedly made fun of the idea of supernatural investigators. Then, in a lower voice, he asked to meet them the next day somewhere more private to discuss the matter.

                                        The Prometheans had realised by this point that the men in the room, and they were practically all men, were drinking slowly but steadily. It was a room of men who were not eager to return to their homes, who all seemed intent on dragging the night out to the early hours. No one wanted to go to their beds.

                                        Abram, unable to tolerate Elsie flirting with half the occupants of the room, left Riley alone in the bar. Not long after that, Mike led Elsie from the room to see his car, leaving Riley alone with the baby. Elsie saw three men, two young, one much older and distinguished who was clearly supervising, unloading barrels from the back of the car. Once they were done they went inside. She got into the car with Mike and they drove off, passing Abram on the way out of town and kicking dust into his face. He hurried after, arriving on the edge of town, but by then he'd lost them. He did, however, see something that sent him sprinting back to town to find Riley.

                                        Mike pulled to a stop a short drive from Sour Springs. Elsie, by this point, was busy evaluating him as prospective suitor material, and was insistently trying to suss out his opinion on children. When he seemed to dislike the idea she asked, "Well, what about my children?" Mike seemed to take that as an invitation, and leaned in to kiss her.

                                        Riley and Abram (and Baby) were staring at the horizon. It was hard to make out in the darkness, now that the sun had set, but there was a dust cloud to the south-east, in the direction of Joseph's Cross. The storm wasn't moving, which was concerning enough in its own right. They remembered the beast Typhoeus from the Bone Fields, and the storm that had followed it. When it was clear that there was nothing they could do about it this night, they walked back into town. Riley could tell that something else was bothering Abram. They pried, gently, but Abram's responses weren't exactly clear. Something about Elsie. Together with their connection as a Branded Throng, however, it was enough for Riley to work out what the issue was. Abram was jealous of Elsie, jealous of the way she attracted men, and at a loss on how to reconcile that jealousy with his role in the group.

                                                            Session 14 - We're in business!

                                                            The Prometheans retired to their makeshift home for the night. When they entered the nightmare Riley, desperate for a calm night's sleep to recover some of their energy, chose to run and hide. Abram just nursed Baby, making no particular effort to hide himself, until the front of the building was torn away by the elder beast. Abram promptly dropped Baby, callously deciding that the child would be fine in the end no matter what injury it suffered in dreams, and raced to snatch up the carefully prepared molotovs. As the beast clawed at him he began to hurl the molotovs at the floor, burning down the building with himself and Baby inside. Riley, hidden in an alcove further down the street, watched in horror as the flames spread from building to building. Humans were forced out of hiding and into the street, where the elder beast and its rival, the bloated, fat toad-like monster, fell upon the helpless victims.

                                                            Riley survived the night, waking in the morning as Elsie burst through the door in the early hours of the morning. She refused to tell anyone how her date with Mike had gone, and instead she and Abram quickly got into another argument over parenting. Riley left before it escalated too badly, taking Terah for an early morning ride. They noted that the dust cloud appeared to have shrunk from the night before, just a discoloured splotch on the horizon.

                                                            When she got back, both Abram and Elsie appeared to have decided they had won their argument. They decided that they wanted more alcohol to prepare more weapons to fight the monsters, and sent Elsie out to procure some. She found the roadhouse unmanned and, rather than ring the bell, just proceeded straight down to the speakeasy. She knocked on the door and it was opened by the old man she'd seen supervising the unloading of Mike's car the night before. He smiled at her, opened the door to let her see it was him and Mike inside. The man introduced himself as Carlisle West, and asked what she'd come for.

                                                            Elsie was tired, hadn't slept, and couldn't be bothered to dissemble and persuade the men to give her alcohol. She bluntly told the man that she wanted to burn some buildings down, then began to burn her Pyros on the Weaponse and Redirect Alembics to turn Carlisle into a furious, raving lunatic who launched himself at Mike. As the two men brawled in the background she scooped up a few bottles of liquor and walked out, burning the last of her Pyros for good measure to turn the speakeasy into a Wasteland.

                                                            The throng then waited until midday to meet with their potential client out the front of the barbershop. Riley was eager to be there first, so made sure to be outside a full hour before the agreed upon time. Abram spent the time standing next to them, staring through the dirty window of the barbershop at the barber inside. He noted that the man spent the entire time just staring at his own shoes, not moving. Elsie rejoined them, and their client arrived. He introduced himself as Peter Jones, and he and Riley agreed upon terms. He was poor, saving money to move to California where he hoped to find work. Instead of paying them, he would try and find out what he could about the nightmare. He told them that it had only been happening for a few months, so they asked him to find out if anyone new had come to town around then. In return they would protect him each night in the nightmare.

                                                            This completed the Hermit role for Riley. They decided to move on from Cuprum, instead joining Elsie as a Plumbum in the Pilgrim role. They were determined to get Abram a date.

                                                            The throng spent their afternoon visiting the sheriff at the county lock-up, hoping to learn more about why the deputies and prisoners were teaming up to fight the beasts in the nightmare. The sheriff flatly refused to speak about the nightmare though, telling them (in a rather patronising fashion) that while their dreams might seem real, they couldn't hurt them. Riley's protests that the dreams were obviously unnatural fell on deaf, or unwilling, ears.

                                                            Dejected, Riley and Abram decided to go for a simple walk about town. Riley still hoped to pick up the trail of Malik, but so far it appeared to have gone cold. Stretching and testing the limits of their new Refinement, they used the Plumb the Fathoms Alembic to find out if there were any still-living Prometheans who had passed through Sour Springs. The Azothic Memory's response was that there were two.

                                                            Almost on cue, the two Prometheans paused as they both sensed the same thing. An Azothic Radiance, coming from somewhere close. They had just walked past the Green Plains Bank, a local bank showing signs that it had been targeted by thrown projectiles in recent days. Abram suggested that perhaps they might be sensing the presence of an Athanor in the Bank's vaults. Stricken with indecision, the two of them stared up at the Bank's scarred facade, not sure what to do.
