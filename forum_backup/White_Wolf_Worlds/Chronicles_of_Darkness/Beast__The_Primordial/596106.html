    Fixing Beast: how would you have done it?

    07-19-2015, 05:57 PM
    The Beast controversy has been ongoing on these forums - and the RPG.net forums, from what little I know - for a while. The initial draft release attracted lots of debate, the official rewrite attracted even more.

    But I was wondering: for those of you who had problems with Beast, either before or after the revamp, how would you have fixed them?

    Myself? Well, I haven't been able to memorise the thread here, nevermind the RPG.net equivalent, but still...

    #1: Horrors wrapped in human flesh
    One of the things I liked about draft!Beast was the presentation of Beasts as literally born monsters. This is something touched on only be three other splats in the NWoD; Werewolves, Prometheans and Demons. Of those three, werewolves are often conflicted about whether or not they are happier now, demons have to feign humanity as best they can to survive, and the whole point of prometheans is to become human. Beasts, as they were presented, were something unique: monsters from birth who were actually happier when they accepted the truth.

    "I am a monster who dreamed he was a man. But the dream is over. And the monster is awake."

    That's not something integral to the other lines. It was in Beast, and that was a perspective I liked for its uniqueness. I don't mind using the term "Horror" to refer to a Beast's inner self, because that actually a good emphasis of the theme; humans have souls, but Beasts are horrors wearing a human suit. However, the term "Homecoming" is essential to me; it emphasizes that a Beast is closer to a Mage or a Werewolf than to a Vampire or a Changeling. No exterior agency acted on them to make them into a Beast; it was just who they always were, and they finally accepted it.


    #2: Eat to live, don't live to eat
    Beasts need to feed. That's just how it is. Everything needs sustenance. Beasts just happen to feed on fear and horror. Something all Beasts must learn to live with is that, no matter what humans think, they are still nothing more than meat, singing to itself in the darkness. But that doesn't mean a Beast shouldn't exercise restraint. A Beast must learn how to balance its hunger with the world around them; do they try to be humane and feed "reasonably", or do they embrace their dark side and gorge themselves, heedless of the destruction they cause?

    It's alright for Beasts to want to put a higher meaning on why they feed or how they feed, but as more than one complainer has noted, the revision's "Beasts terrify humanity to teach lessons" comes off as foolish and pretentious. At best, it's worth a social Z-axis foundation, but I don't think it's strong enough to be the underlying purpose of the game.


    #3: Heroes only in their own mind
    The biggest problem with draft!Beast was the very use of the term "Heroes" in my opinion. No matter the higher cause behind it, the basic fact is that people associate "hero" with "good guy" - it's our cultural indoctrination. And if the other side are the Heroes, then, by simple logic, we Beasts must be the Villains. This simple fact, more than anything, incited readers to sympathize with the Heroes and look more harshly upon the Beasts.

    Personally, I would call such people "Slayers". It's a much less loaded term that emphasizes who and what they are: they are Killers of Beasts. Morality has nothing to do with it, except maybe as the excuse they use when called upon it. They just want to kill Beasts. That's all that matters to them.


    #4: Slayers are born, not made
    The other big problem with Heroes, from my perspective, is that they were given a mechanical creation basis. This fueled the sympathetic interpretation of them and caused the schism between Hero supporters and Beast supporters.

    I say that if a mortal rises up against a Beast because of that Beast's depredations, that's a Hunter, more tahn anything else. It's the universal thread that underlies all supernatural splats in the NWoD: Actions Have Consequences. Bully mortals too much, and humans will fight back. It's simple Cause and Effect.

    I think Slayers should just arise. There's no need for malignancy on a Beast's part. A Slayer-to-be sees a Beast, and she knows that they are a monster. The Beast does nothing to them; she could just be passing by in the street, buying a coffee and a donut at a local stall, sitting on the bus, and it still doesn't matter. When a Slayer sees a Beast, they undergo their own twisted version of the Homecoming, finally realizing their place in life.

    In fact, ideally, I think a Slayer potential should be immune to the Feasting of a hungry Horror. This would emphasize how shallow and selfish their goals are. They don't suffer from the Beast's presence, they don't experience anything to push them into accepting their role, they just open their arms and embrace it, as willingly as a Beast does.


    These are the things I think of when I consider what I would have done to make Beast the best I think it could be. How would you have fixed whatever problems you see or saw in Beast?

      Honestly, I found both of the drafts to be relatively good. Not as good as the other 2nd ed games but good nonetheless. The core idea of Beast is unique. I'm not certain that I'd change the things you've mentioned too much. I'd just like the existing material to be expanded a bit more. I do think the lesson teaching should be more egotistical (think Smaug) rather than "wisdom", but that's hardly a deal-breaker.

      The notion of Family and the cross-over parts of the game are the most underdeveloped (indeed in some places they almost make the game into the Care Bears cartoon), so I'm hoping that the final draft fleshes them out more. Likewise the spirit rules were simply copy and pasted into the drafts without any meaningful reason for being there. I'm hoping that the final draft replaces them with a "spirit" section that is better aligned to the game's themes. Expanding on astral / primordial dream denizens would seem appropriate.

          I like most of the stuff in the revision.

          It's just, the Beast Cultural Focus and the Homecoming being replaced with the Devouring are both awful.

          I'm bolding my actual fixes, so if you dont ant to read a rant, skip to the bold

          I continue to insist that "Scare or hurt mortals to teach them lessons" is a horribly position for the culture of an entire splat. All of the complaining about Beasts being abusive assholes becomes more valid in that context. Furthermore, it's far too restricting when it comes to character concepts.

          If an entire culture is based around teaching mortals lessons, then a Beast who does not teach mortals lessons is going to be looked down on by other Beasts. So, if you dont want to be a beast that teaches lessons, then you're automatically an outcast from society.

          I now will note that New Beasts cultural thematic is most similar to the Lancea et Sanctum(scare mortals and punish sin, for as the children of Longinus, we are the monsters that scare humanity back into the embrace of YHWH/Jehovah/Christ/Allah/Other Name for Abrahamic God or Similar being) and the Scarecrow Ministry(Scare the fuck out of mortals with Hoaxes so that they'll either stay the hell away from this gate to the hedge or be too busy investigating the hoax to do something about it) Both of which are sub splats.

          If I understand correctly, this focus was added so that Beasts would have more to do on their own, instead of in crossover games.

          I say that that could have been accomplished by say, something similar to the dread power "build a monster" rules from hunter. an Appendix of unique critters with ties to the Dark Mother for beasts to investigate.

          Alternatively, The Presence of Z-Splats could have provided that focus, in which case "Scare the hell out of humans to teach them a lesson" would fit better there, among other options

          The Devouring has all kinds of problems.

          Before, a nascent beast was always a monster, and by coming home she discovered her true family.

          Now, it's a person who had a sleep disorder has their soul hollowed out and a monster climb inside so that they can get super powers and join a club.

          That's... Not the game we were promised. We were promised a game about family. This is not family.

          When you're born human and become a monster, then you do not belong to the first race of monsters. You're not the primordial progenitor, you're the upstart mortal who's apping everybody elses shit, then claiming that makes you their big brother..

          In addition to this, the fact that you now become a beast because another Beast ate your soul to make room for your Horror is... Supposedly it's to give the character more agency, except. "You one day realize that the monster in your nightmare is and always was you" is much more agency than "some other guy decided to invite you to the club and offered you a choice to join... except sometimes he coerces you or just eats your soul without telling you what's going on."

          The first is something you do, the second is something someone else does. Net loss in agency.

          And it's really inconsistent with the fiction, and is just jarring when you look at all of the "Family" stuff.

          Also, anyone who willfully joins a culture where super powered individuals use violence or scare tactics to force their own views on morality or teach "lessons" to mortals is probably an asshole. Even if he only hurts their assholes, he's still an asshole.

          Now, there's the sidebar on homecomings instead of devourings, but it's inconsistent with how the devouring is depicted and really feels most like a half assed appeasement than an actual address at the potential complaint.

          If I understand correctly, this major change was made to justify a scenario in which an elder beast is there to provide and introduction on Beast culture...Except, that could have been done much more easily with a "Beasts can tell when a nascent sibling is about to undergo their homecoming" power, and a mention that some older beasts tend to get maternal or paternal feelings about younger beasts and tend to approach them to help them adjust to realizing what they really are.

          Everything else about the revisions, the changes to heroes, the rewritten stereotypes, and the one family's new boon, all of that's good, keep that.

          It's just, two major unnecessary changes kind of ruin the game we were promised.

          I'm an aspergic individual. This means that I can't imagine how others will react to situations. This has led to me accidentally offending someone, and has also led to me being less clear in statements than I thought I was. I'd like to apologize, and ask that others don't get mad, but just ask me to reword or clarify. Thank you

                  I think the best idea I ever saw about fixing Beast was that Heroes create Beasts. Somebody feels an overwhelming need to be a hero for one reason or another, so they point to somebody and say "look he's a Beast, I shall slay him".

                                  How about we drop this until the game is finished? I'm not happy with some of the changes either but that doesn't make them bad. Yes I prefer Homecoming & Soul, rather than Devouring & Horror; that doesn't mean the game needs 'fixing'. What it does mean is that I prefer certain stylistic and thematic elements that have been de-emphasized.

I have no problem with a setting 'hack' or 'shard' being written by the fans to adjust the tone of the game. However that isn't what is happening here and is premature anyway.

                                  While there has been a good case made for why some people want these changes above the 'common tone' of them, (meaning how the tone of the post can be taken with both the context of this thread and the fewest assumptions based on what is outside of it), is rather...aggressive.
