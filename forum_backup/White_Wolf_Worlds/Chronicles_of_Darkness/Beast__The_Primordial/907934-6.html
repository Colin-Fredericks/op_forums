                                If it helps as a reference, it would be somewhere between Mage and Promethean in how things tend to go. As Satchel covered, there's a lot of investigation of the strange any mysterious to explore kinship with other monsters, which will end up a lot like Mage in terms of the mystery angle. However unlike Mage where there's no real "end goal" to this, like Promethean you're on a journey to a different state of being. You're not seeking to become human, but you and your fellow Beasts are looking to figure out your place in the world and what you'll become as you grow in power. Also similar to both is the consequences matter. There's stuff like what Satchel said, but Heroes fill a similar game play aspect as Paradox and Disquiet to tie specific narrative obstacles into the experience.

                                                TheStranjer Calling something "SJW" is childish. It's barely even true, and if you're running a game where Beasts would be allowed that isn't a Beast game, "banning" people from playing that is silly.
                                                Niknokk That at it's heart is why Beast is a game that needs to be sold to someone. It's not really got any focus. It's got less reason for you to do any particular thing than Geist, and also has plenty of mechanics that get in the way of any sort of narrative. If you want to focus on scaring people (as the game wants you to), well, much like with Vampire and Changeling, that's treated as a pretty solitary thing. Even in the examples, your Brood or whatever they call it tends to wait outside while you spook your victim. If you want to focus on building up your secret base mechanic, you're not really doing anything besides playing WoD: Animal Crossing. If you go try and meet new monsters or do the crossover the game is intended for, Heroes get in the way. If you ignore Heroes, you've just cut out a portion of the game.

                                                It simultaneously wants to do too much and too little at the same time, so it doesn't really have any niche to hang it's hat on. A lot of the themes feel like they come right from Masquerade, others are from Changeling, and the most interesting part of the setting is tacked onto a side-realm from Mage, which is a thing the rest of the WoD tries to avoid so much that Changeling dreamworld and Mage dreamworld are different. On top of that, I feel like "no social splats" in a WoD game is something they tried with Geist and failed at. "There's no reason to do anything" is true of both that and Beast.

                                                So, if I were going to, how would I "sell" someone on the concept? If your group loves the Hunter and Mage style of proactive "go find a problem and mess with it" style of gameplay over more political focused "the Elders make you do a thing" type games, then chances are you'll be able to enjoy Beast as a game with Thing of the Week stories. I only really looked over Tokyo, but there are plenty of things for your group to poke their noses in, even if the only reason to do so is "might be neat".

                                                    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                                    OK; so if I'm reading this right:

                                                    a Beast is a dragon, kraken, gorgon, or other mythical monster — but only in her dreams.
                                                    . . . Sort of.

                                                    Did you ever see Wes Craven's New Nightmare? The one that got really meta? In it, he explained that Freddy Krueger was never a "real" person. Rather, it was a supernatural entity that humans started thinking of as a badly burnt dream predator (named Freddy Krueger). In time, though, it came to think of itself as Freddy Krueger, to the point that it took that shape, and even played by the fictional character's rules.

                                                    A Horror is a lot like that. Its a super-goetic demon, a gestalt entity born from one of the shared fears of humanity. As it congeals into a discrete entity, it starts to take on the shape and personality of a monster that embodies that fear for humanity. Something that makes intuitive sense to a human - it is the spawn of the human subconscious, after all ("form follows function")

                                                    For example, Anakim are Horrors born of the fear of helplessness, of being weak and powerless. By the time an Anakim Horror is ready to evolve into a Beast, it has taken on a form that personifies brute force and strength. Ogres, giants, minotaurs . . . An Anakim is big enough to crush a human underfoot like a bug, or has impenetrable iron skin that no human could hope to scratch. Everything about it screams physical strength and brutality - the oldest, most primitive form of power.

                                                    Now, there are some "repeats". In becoming a unique entity, Horrors often do end up as "mythic monsters" - or even "real" ones. But that's because they've been shaped by the human subconscious (and those forms make sense to humanity). Each one is still a unique entity, and colored by their nightmarish birth. So a "werewolf" Horror will be some twisted monstrosity, with razor sharp claws, blood-stained fur, a necklace of human skulls . . . its a nightmare wearing the shape of a werewolf, not an actual werewolf out of Forsaken.

                                                    A Beast is what happens when a Horror gets itself a human body & mind, evolving into something that can think beyond the simplistic purity of the Hunger. If you strip away all the fluff & color of the game, a Beast is basically a psychic vampire that feeds by forcing people to experience fear (particularly deep seated or repressed fear).

                                                    However, that's not really the vibe the game's going for. Rather, Beasts are supposed to be the "monsters" of ghost stories and slasher movies. Seemingly human characters that scare people on a very fundamental level. One of the sample characters, for example, is basically the Hook-hand Killer - you know, the one who went after the two teens parked at Make Out Point? Meanwhile "Skeleton Key", a starting power, allows them to pull a "Here's Johnny" (from the Shining), while a Promethean-inspired Nightmare allows a Beast to do the whole invulnerable Michael Myers thing.

                                                    When she's asleep, she becomes the Horror (which, in Mage terms, is basically a monstrous Daemon) and roams the Primordial Dream, invading peoples' dreams and giving them nightmares in order to sate her Hunger. When she's awake, the Horror lurks in its Lair (analogous to a mage's Oneiros) and she walks the material world as a mostly-normal human with a limited ability to manifest powers based on her Horror (Atavisms) and Lair (by inflicting appropriate Environmental Tilts) and an ability to mess with peoples' minds (Nightmares). In theory, the Beast does what it does to teach people caution, humiity, and other “school of hard knocks” lessons; in practice, Beasts often ignore that and feed solely to sate their Hunger.
                                                    Again, sort of.

                                                    A Beast is simultaneously both a human in the waking world, and the monstrous Horror lurking in its Lair. As long as the human half is keeping it fed, the Horror is generally content to stay in its Lair . . . Doing whatever animalistic nightmare creatures do, in their spare time (although it will generally respect the human half's decisions - i.e. no eating the invited guests).

                                                    If the human half doesn't keep it fed, though, the Horror will start hunting. Breaking into the dreams of others, and twisting those dreams into nightmares. Its hungry, after all.

                                                    However, while the Horror normally can't come to earth, the human half can usually enter the Lair at will. All it takes is finding a place in the waking world that mirrors conditions in the Lair, and the Beast can withdraw to the Primordial Dream - and even take other people with them. That's often a combat tactic, actually. When their psychic powers aren't enough, Beasts retreat to their Lair, and (temporarily) merge with their Horror - which often has impressive combat abilities.

                                                    . . . Kinship is part of the game's subversion of other games' themes, in that the Beast's view is that their monstrous natures are the best part of them while what's left of their human natures is an impediment.
                                                    Depends on the Beast. During the Devouring/Homecoming, the latent Beast's soul is destroyed, and the Horror's nature floods in to replace it. (Some Beasts find this a huge relief, by the way, particularly those that had a tight bond with their Horror even before the Devouring).

                                                    The result is a being that's as smart as human . . . but doesn't think like one anymore. The new Beast has the same memory, personalities, quirks . . . but it now looks at the world from the perspective of a nightmare creature. As much as it might want to, a Beast will never see things quite the same, ever again. And many revel in their new, darker nature - for many, the Horror fills a hole in their life they never knew existed.

                                                    At the same time, though, the new Beast often finds new appreciation for the positive aspects of human existence. A Beast can't feed on hope, friendship, kindness . . . but being permanently joined to a being from the darkest depths of the collected subconscious shows them just how valuable those things actually are.

                                                    The balance a Beast strikes between its urge to revel in its inner darkness, versus their desire to hold on to those few glimpses of light, generally define whether the Beast ends up a good guy or a bad guy. Relatively speaking, of course.

                                                    (Incidentally, Reapers - mages who steal souls - can try to take a Beast's Horror. That's incredibly dangerous, though, since the Horror - unlike a human soul - can and will fight back.)

                                                    Complementing this is the Beast's relationship with deranged self-described Heroes who see Beasts as little more than creatures to kill in their quest for glory. Beast admits that there are Heroes who are sane and who serve a purpose that's complementary to the Beast's supposed “drill sergeant from hell” role — the carrot to the Beast's stick — but for the most part, it concentrates on those Heroes who slay Beasts.
                                                    Pretty much. Technically, a Beast's "job" is just to force humans to admit there's a problem. Its the responsibility of Heroes to help humanity grow strong, and fix the problem.

                                                    But that was before the average Hero degenerated into little more than a rampaging murder hobo. And thanks to their own evolution, most Beasts want to do more than just terrorize and destroy. They want to make a difference. Such Beasts often adopt the idea of teaching lessons, or work on a pet cause (protect an orphanage, track down war criminals, etc) that allows them to feed in a "constructive" manner
                                                    Last edited by One Vorlon; 06-11-2016, 03:44 AM.

                                                        Originally posted by Niknokk View Post
                                                        As someone who missed the kickstarter and is catching up, what would you be doing in a typical Beast chronicle? What I've heard so far is pretty... sparse. There's the Beast and their Hunger, the people they hurt to feed it, and the Heroes who oppose them. Is that it? Vampires have their political games, Prometheans have their journey to humanity, Demons have their guerilla rebellion, etc. Playing Magic Dexter who messes with normal people and fights the occasional Hero seems like a pretty thin Chronicle if that's really all there is.
                                                        Feeding your Hunger is actually a great story element. For a vampire, a pint of blood is a pint of blood. There's no practical difference between drinking one pint from ten people, or draining one person dry.

                                                        That's not true for Beasts. If they want a big feed, they have to do something big. A Collector can squeak by through buying gold from a local jeweler, or ordering first editions through eBay, it they're willing to live on the metaphysical equivalent of bread and water, and don't want to use their powers. But if they want to gorge themselves sick, and actually recharge their power pool, they have to do something equally huge. Steal the cure for AIDS from a corrupt pharmaceutical company. Recover a Brahmastra (Atlantean superweapon) from a forgotten temple. "Liberate" the only known map to Irem from an ancient vampire's library.

                                                        If you tweak the mythos a bit, Magneto's arc from X-men: First Class works as an excellent example. In pursuit of the Mysteries, Sebastian Shaw (an evil mage), forces him to undergo the Devouring young, and in a very horrific manner. Shaw later throws him away, and forgets about the young Beast.

                                                        Magneto doesn't forget Shaw, though. A Nemesis (a Beast with a Hunger for Punishment), the adult Magneto starts by tracking down a Swiss banker, forces information from him, and then punishes him for laundering blood money. Its a serviceable meal for his Hunger, and sends him to Argentina. There, he finds a couple minor Nazi fugitives, who tell him how to find Shaw . . . and who make an even bigger and better meal.

                                                        Working with Xavier, Magneto is able to track down Shaw, and build a team to stop him. Using Mother's Kiss, they boost the team's power, and Xavier is able to help Magneto recover some of his favorite (and purely human) memories of lighting the menorah.

                                                        So of course Xavier is completely shocked when Magneto kills Shaw. He assumed that helping Magneto remember his humanity would make him a better person. But even though Magneto treasures those recovered memories, he's not human - he's a Beast now. Showing mercy to Shaw isn't even on the table. Killing him is not merely personally satisfying to Magneto, but a massive feed. After all, is there anyone more evil and deserving of punishment than a high ranking Nazi butcher who just tried to kill all humanity in a plot for personal power?

                                                        Saving the world, getting personal revenge, attracting followers, and recharging your batteries to full power - sounds like a pretty successful campaign, doesn't it?


                                                        Edit: In a different direction, you can also use most campaigns designed for other lines as a Beast game. Between the protean nature of their Horrors, and ability to mimic the powers of others, Beasts can substitute for . . . practically anything. For example, if the Beast community is well-established, with a powerful Incarnate as Apex (basically Beast king), and a lot of intrigue between broods, then you can swap them in for the vampires in a Requiem campaign. If the Apex actively hates them, and tries to persecute them, you've got a mock-Demon game . . .
