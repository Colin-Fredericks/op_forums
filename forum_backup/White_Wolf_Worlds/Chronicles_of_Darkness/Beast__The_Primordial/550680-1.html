        Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
        So, the difference in the stories is just that one's bigger than the other?

        ....so it's just the same story?
        To an extent. You can't exactly have a normal person tackle the same things that Capt.America, Thor, etc. But, yes, its the same Hero's Journey structure, the mono-myth applies to both.

        And I like tragedies, but I don't see why the tragedy has to begin a story all the time. Going to Captain America*, he has tragedy, and they shape him as a character, but his story doesn't start with him going through a tragedy as an impetus for him to be the hero.
        Correct me if I'm wrong, but wasn't his "pre-hero tragedy" being the target of bullies himself? Mind you, its not on par with Wayne's parents being killed, but its still there.

        Now, let me be clear. I think that the same stories are getting old. They do contain a lot of problematic material - hells, a lot of the old stories use "the dangers of female sexuality" as a moral or challenge. I think using Women in the Fridge trope is disgusting as a character development. There's a lot involved that's overdone.

        That said, when we're writing continual stories, such as Capt. America, we need to have a lot of material to work with. There's a LOT of writing involved. If anything, I would say that the problem comes down to the fact that the Hero's Journey itself is the problem. There's this idea that the Hero is the Chosen One. Everything revolves around them. Its a tired old plot that's been done to death, and is heavily focused on Manly Man type stories. Its about solving problems with violence, seeing the world as challenges to overcome.

        I think that the Hero's Journey is why we see so many games (role playing like D&D, video games) and comics that revolve so heavily around violence. Its the "default" that we need to start writing away from, I think.

                                                        All the criticism I saw, including mine, was essentially reader interpretation not matching writer intent. I mean that in a way less contradictory (and logic defying) than it sounds. I can wait with an open mind until they are clarified so that I can truly understand them.

                                                            My interpretation of writer intent was that the Hero vs Beast conflict isn't so much Good vs. Evil or even Evil versus good, but rather Black and White vs Gray. The Hero sees the world only in absolutes, and the Beast sees only in relativistic terms, and both are colored by self-interest. Maybe the reason it appears inconsistent is because this could result in a wide array of different situations. Sometimes the Beast really is an awful monster that has to be put down. Sometimes the Hero is a sociopath. You get all these different combinations of sympathetic vs. unsympathetic, sympathetic vs. sympathetic, unsympathetic vs. unsympathetic, and everything in between. No neat little boxes, right?

                                                            I was recently thinking that a good contemporary example of a Beast and his Hero was" Breaking Bad." Walter White fears the powerlessness he feels in the face of his impending death and being unable to provide security for his family, so he gradually embraces the darker part of himself (Heisenberg) to, presumably, take care of his family. We gradually realize, however, that what he is really doing is satiating his Hunger for power. He never wants to feel powerless again. Enter the Hero, Hank. At first he comes off as a kind of a jerk and the audience is firmly on Walter/Heisenberg's side, but that gradually shifts and Hank becomes a sympathetic protagonist while Heisenburg becomes a genuine monster.

                                                            Is the Hero the good guy and the Beast the villain, or is the Beast the good buy and the Hero the villain? I think the most accurate answer, to the consternation of many, is "Yes."

                                                            In any case, this is probably the most excited I've been for any RPG game line, and I've been a WoD fan for at least a decade.
