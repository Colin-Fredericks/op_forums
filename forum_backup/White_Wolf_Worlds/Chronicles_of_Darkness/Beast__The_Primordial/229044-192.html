    Originally posted by Leetsepeak View Post
    Losing Humanity is mechanically encouraged by 2nd edition of Vampire, because any Conditions gained and resolved in service of the story grants Beats. The story is about stuff like falling humanity and managing the Conditions that come along with it.

    While Beast is due for some focusing based on Matt's post, it's pretty clear that fluctuating Hunger is very much a part of the game, so Conditions reflecting levels of Satiety and the resolutions that accompany them seems pretty clearly to be a desired state. Your Hunger is very, very important for a game. Satisfying that hunger, expending Satiety, they want you to do that, hence Conditions which incentivize it.

    If you think these games encourage you to be a bad person solely by granting Beats, you're missing the point. You get Beats when you lose your Integrity, you get them when you keep it. Demons gain Conditions when they risk their Cover and when they lose it. Beats are rewards for playing into and resolving Conditions because doing so adds drama to the story. In all reality, it is the story most of the time.

    If you feel your players are "gaming" the system by playing the game, you can see how that's a little wonky, can't you?

    I really, really hate to play this card, but I think you're thinking "simulationist" when the 2nd edition trends towards "narrativist." That is, the bad things that happen to characters like Losing Humanity or Integrity or Cover are encouraged because they make story, drama and the game. If you were never losing any of these things, then you're probably not playing the game at all, because the act of going out and doing things necessitates risking all of them. It's considered a good thing, because you're trying to tell a story, and story results from stuff happening, from these things being risked and lost, from the up and down. Beast seems very keen on this, to the degree that Matt said early on that Satiety is likely fluctuating regularly in a given session.

    If you just don't like that, it's fine, but don't make the mistake of thinking it's some kind of exploit or loophole for players to get Teh Sick Beatz.(tm) It's part of the game. If you feel like your players are "gaming" hungers and not actually developing story, then don't give them the Beats.
    If I could make a suggestion for keeping that kind of play under control, I'd suggest that the game give better rewards for novel behavior, and diminishing returns for habitual acts, in the form of growing penalties to Feeding rolls.

                            Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                            Watching my Roommate play Overlord, I couldn't help but notice parallels. What should be a black and white game, with you on the black side, actually turned out to be quite muddled.

                            Also, Freemind, your treatise on Satiety is fucking awesome.

                            I actually gave my screen a high five. Thanks.

                            Don't get me wrong. I think beast needs some cleaning up. I think that if they clarified the themes, and the nature of the beasts roles in the WoD, the game would be great. But I do think Satiety is one of the stronger elements of the game and fits quite well.

                            Things I think need that clarification are:
                            • What is the driving element of the Beast and Heroes origins? A lot of people viewed the story as the beasts going "whoa is us, why does everyone want to kill us" while also being "I kill puppies for the lulz", and their hatred of the Heroes that hunt them despite knowing they created them. The theme seemed "Eat and don't get killed" and "make friends with the other splats", with a smattering of other themes at different points.
                              • I could have been wrong in my reading, but one of the themes that seemed to be present was the idea that the Beasts are cast in a role by the Primordial Dream, the classic heroes tale. They are the monster, the manifestation of humanities fears, their lair the setting and their role is to be the villain killed by the hero. The tragedy is that they are aware that this is their role, and yet even if they don't play along, every other part of the setting, even their Soul, wants to follow through with the story. So they essentially either have to give in, fight their role and possibly die for their efforts, curl up and surrender to it, or overcome the myth and change the story to make them the protagonist.

                                Emphasizing this wouldn't change the setting, the need for satiety, or even the end game. What it would achieve though is giving a constant theme beyond the hunger and struggle to both sate it and control it. It would add the nuance of being the only one who realized they are trapped in a story and who doesn't like how it is supposed to end for them.
                                • Whats more, it would help make heroes cleaner while both keeping their traits, creation and even role as victims
                                  • Heroes are those who was cast as the Hero in the story around the Beast.
                                    • The reason they are cast is because their soul (their onieros) was exposed to the Beasts soul while it fed in the Astral, and due to their lack of personal identity, their identity gets subsumed into the myth by the primordial dream. This forms a bond with the primordial dream and the story, and as they follow the path the story set out for them, it follows the traditions of the myth. Hence why their powers grow, or their ability to define the Beasts weaknesses with Anathema: it follows the conventions of the mythology that is centered around the Beast.
                                      • So they are victims just as the Beasts: people drawn in to the role in a living myth by the Soul of the Beast. They just lack an awareness of what this means
                                      • Whats more, their lack of awareness and certainty of their role in the story irks beasts, as these heroes seem to be people who are killing them because the plot demands it, and the heroes are too weak willed to realize this
                                  • It would also explain their "all about me" attitude: it is, at least in some sense. They are part of a story, and they are at least innately aware of their role in it as "the hero". They just don't have much more insight because they are being defined fully by the role, and it controls them as they lack enough spiritual fortitude to realize what they are.
                                  • It would also explain their lack of characterization. They are people with poor self-identities who are being driven by an epic narrative driven by a realm based on the world of concepts. It is like the gentry almost, turning a human into the role needed. He is a hero, he hunts the monsters because it is what heroes do. The fact that this role is what gave him the drive and unifying focus means that it is the central core of his being, and unless he gains enough integrity to rise above the role and embrace his role as a human being, he basically becomes the hero prince from a story...which is fine in literature and entertainment, but would be unpleasant to deal with in real life.
                              • Then again, I could be full of shit.
                            • Clarify what is the beast cultures opinions and what are cold hard facts.
                              • Some of this was unclear. I know I read parts where I couldn't tell if I was being told "this is what the beasts believe" and "this is how it is".
                            • Make the crossovers less "easy".
                              • This game is a crossover game, so people being upset by that are missing the point. But the idea of alliances should be something that are not common nor are they something that the cultures on the other side are okay with collectively, if even aware of it. It also should make clear how much insight the Beasts have into the other splats, and how much is their own mythos.
                            • The fact that the Beasts are "bad guys"
                              • I think this is mostly personal taste
                              • That said, I do think the book should clarify that their examples represent the gamut of the types of beasts, from those who use their powers to minimize their harm while others revel in their roles as monsters. And make the point that Beasts are like people: Some are psychopaths, and others are decent people. They just have to deal with a monster whose hunger pushes them towards being the psychopath, meaning there are more of those than in the general human population.
                            • Heroes
                              • Already touched on this above, but maybe give a less "this is how Beasts see them" and "this is what they are, and the beasts see them as monsters because the Beasts are being killed by these people".

                            (also, I was trapped in clinic without any patients...I had to do something)

                                I took the time to give Beast a strong reading, and for the most part I definitely enjoy it. 7.8 out of 10.
                                Some thoughts:

                                While I can see some members of the LGBT community identifying with some of the situations Beast brings up, it is clear to me that this was an accidental parallel because they happen to share some bad circumstances. Not an intentional effort by the writers to generate controversy on that front.

                                Beasts seem to lack direction beyond just surviving from day to day. Vampires have their politics, Werewolves have their wars, Mages have their exploration, Changelings have their Fetch and their paranoia and their coping / recovery and their courts.

                                The relationship with other supernaturals seems very forced and sometimes inappropriate, especially regarding Changelings. The Dark Mother metaphor feels like it should be much more obviously unreliable narrator.

                                Some of the powers feel like they really step on the toes of other splats. Primordial Pathways and Skeleton Keys are the biggest example, since any starting Beast can go to the Hedge or one of the Towers if they have some time on their hands to align resonance. This was a really big deal for the respective splats. It's less noticeable for other splats, but I was able to make a Werewolf themed beast that was somewhat better at combat and tracking than a Werewolf.

                                    I know I'm late to the party, and I'll keep my thoughts brief:

                                    I like Beast as a standalone game. I think Beast can support a single non-Beast Supernatural if that splat's player tailors their character to the game.

                                    I feel the best Beast in X xsplat is either Vampire or Hunter. The themes are fairly close with Vampire and Beasts have to manage Satiety in a way similar to Vampires managing Vitae. More human-friendly Beasts could help Hunters in hunting "evil" Supernaturals in a unique way.

                                    I feel the Changeling xsplat is workable. Both can influence dreams, both can use each other to feed, and Beasts can consciously choose to be less abusive rather than more to allay the Lost's fears. Beast's can also empathize with the inner them and the outer them not matching. Both also are bound up in the ancient narrative thing.

                                    I don't think that Beasts are capable of successfully cross-splating with:
                                    • Sin-eaters - too much of the Sin-eater's job is interacting with Ghosts, something Beats have no ability to do. They're also pretty limited in their ability to enter the Underworld. What's a Krewe going to do with one of the Begotten?
                                    • Mummies - difference in power levels, difference in goals, the whole Mummy managing memories thing.
                                    • Werewolves - same issue as with Sin-eaters, how is the Beast supposed to cross over in the Hisil? Interact with Spirits, especially those that haven't manifested? This one isn't too bad, though, because at least the Beast can select a Hunger that can be sated while hunting with Werewolves.
                                    • Mages - zero ability for Beasts to interact with the Supernal. Zero ability to protect themselves against spells. No real way to help a Cabal investigate a Mystery other than by being a guinea pig. Same power issues that come up with Mummy, but worse because versatility. The least workable, IMO, unless all the Mage players cater to the Beast... in which case why play a Mage game?

                                    The easiest fix for most of these is to give Beasts some way (Atavism?) of interacting with things like Spirits and Ghosts and crossing over into the Shadow or Underworld. Perhaps Nightmares that cause Power Incontinence in Mages and Mummies?

Sure, the final results are known (Merger, Incarnate and...the other one) but not how the Beasts view their inheritances nor their different takes on their responsibilities as beasts. This means you could add the role of morality as a part of one particular "clans" code, while another thinks that all that matters is being at peace with the Soul, no matter how disgusting it is to achieve said peace. And one could think that Beasts should accept their roles as monsters in the story and enjoy being the villain, another to fight the role, and the third who caves and wants the story to just end already before they hurt anyone else.
