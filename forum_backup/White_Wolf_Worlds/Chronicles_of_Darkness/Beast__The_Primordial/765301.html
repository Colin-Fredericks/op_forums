    Begotten and Wicked: Children of the Dark Mother

    11-13-2015, 10:55 PM
    I'm pretty sure I'm not the first person to ask this question. But for those who used the fan supplement Leviathan: The Tempest in their games, and those who are incorporating Beast: The Primordial as well, how exactly do you write off the (possible) relation between them? The Wicked Tribe were described in their write-up as the spawn of Tiamat, a name which happens to be one of several to refer to the Begotten's Dark Mother. Thoughts, anyone?

          Leviathan's main point of distinction lies in that the monsters they are tend to be focused on cataclysm-these things destroy towns at the low end and end worlds on the high, and so reining them in is a big deal. Beasts are inclined to look at the closeness of them and might declare the first group to branch off, when mankind started becoming advanced enough to worry not just about their own death, but the death of the species. Nightmares of Extinction, if you will.

          They might consider them kin, but Leviathans put Beasts in a weird spot. Beasts are very much about accepting their monstrous state, embracing it and living it up. If Leviathans do that though, it invites calamity on a scale even Ravagars might not be comfortable with. Leviathans are the fragile or disabled kids in the family tree, and the Beast, bursting with energy and a need of play, just don't know quite what to do with them. They want to help, but it's apparent that they might make things worse from the word go.

          That's the best reading I can offer, anyways.

                              No, I have a few gripes with that.

                              1) It's been acknowledged, several times now, that the thematic, iconic, and (in game and out of game) spiritual similarity of Beasts and Leviathan is obscenely close. Failing remembrance of Leviathan's mechanics be damned, they're so close that asserting it doesn't work doesn't make a lick of sense.

                              2)Speaking of, it's not a psychic phenomenon. Beast's powers are all spiritually based, so it's a spiritual effect.

                              3) This just smacks of the post-Imperial Mysteries mega-template debacle, or everyone making super vampires and changelings and stuff who's first order of power was to make it so Archmages couldn't do their shit. Which was stupid, because

                              4) A gameline saying another template can't do it's thing on another template kills narrative potential(I hear you on Demon, I'll get there in a second). Not only is it dis-empowering, it's boring. A Leviathan player misses out the joy of mystery and manipulation and emotionally based conflict by saying that Beast can't do what Beasts do. It treats a story hook like a curse, and that's the last thing you want to do.

                              Now, what about Demon, you say?

                              First off, that's Beast saying Beast can't do a thing rather than Demon saying Beast's can't do a thing. A gameline limiting itself is fine, narrative parameters create interest, and it works when everything else except humanity works. Secondly, Matt's super involved with Demon on top of deving Beast, so again, it feels legitimate. Third, Demon as a game of divine espionage has a lot of reasons to emphasize they are apart from the rest of the World of Darkness. Beast's rejection of them just strengthens Demon's narrative firmly places Demon as the Outsider, the stranger, the alien, the other, the apart. Leviathan doesn't have enough of that sort of framework to justify it.

                              I mean, if you can justify it the way mages do, go for it, but even with my failed memory of Leviathan's mechanics, it's so clearly out of place it's...well, stupid.

                                      Just a heads up, I don't look at the Beast forum that often so if you're going to ask Leviathan questions here it's best to shoot me a PM. I only noticed this because someone quoted me and that goes into my inbox.

                                      Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                      The thing about Leviathan being immune to the First Impression trait is straight up stupid.
                                      It's a natural consequence of Leviathan having strong family themes that are completely incompatible with Beast: the Primordial's themes and a core principle that Leviathans don't get healthy relationships - giving a Leviathan a healthy relationship is taking away a Vampire's need for blood.

                                      In Beast the meaning of "family" comes from a modern western culture where family love and support each other. In Leviathan family is a bronze age political unit. As an example, in the Bible Laban sells his daughters in marriage in exchange for seven years of labour. That's the theme of "family" that Leviathan works off, and IMO it's completely incompatible with any interpretation of "family" that Changelings are happy to be a part of.

                                      Edit: A better example. In the origonal myths Tiamat gave birth to monsters who's only purpose was to try and kill her first set of children. I think Beasts would generally frown on using family as disposable cannon fodder. 1E Leviathan has a power for doing exactly that.

                                      Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                      1) It's been acknowledged, several times now, that the thematic, iconic, and (in game and out of game) spiritual similarity of Beasts and Leviathan is obscenely close. Failing remembrance of Leviathan's mechanics be damned, they're so close that asserting it doesn't work doesn't make a lick of sense.
                                      Leviathan is about playing embodiments of mankind's savage, superstitious and destructive nature, struggling to exist in a world where civilisation has made you obsolete, and most probably destroying yourself and all of Somalia through your own flaws.

                                      I haven't actually figured out what Beast's core theme is supposed to be, but I'm pretty sure it's not supposed to be “Biblical Tragedy”

                                      Honestly from what I've seen of Beast it seems like the only thing it has in common with Leviathan is: The ability to have a cult. The fact Kraken is a valid character concept. Some similarities between Heroes and Ahabs. Honestly I'd say Leviathan is closer to Genius than Beast.

                                      Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                      2)Speaking of, it's not a psychic phenomenon. Beast's powers are all spiritually based, so it's a spiritual effect.
                                      The Leviathan mental defence mechanic applies to everything that affects their mind or emotions.

                                      Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                      3) This just smacks of the post-Imperial Mysteries mega-template debacle, or everyone making super vampires and changelings and stuff who's first order of power was to make it so Archmages couldn't do their shit. Which was stupid, because
                                      See my first section. This wasn't some reaction I came up with after Beast. It's a natural consiquence of pre-existing mecahnics and themes: Leviathan's incompatible interpreation of family and Leviathan's having mega psychic defences were both in 1E and thus predated Beast by years (In 1E it's Eye of the Eternal Storm / Citadel of the Doldrums, in 2E it's innate).

                                      Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                      4) A gameline saying another template can't do it's thing on another template kills narrative potentia
                                      Sure, but I have to choose (and since the power is directly tied to the theme of family I do have to choose) between respecting another template or respecting the integrity of Leviathan's themes I'm going to go for Leviathan's themes every time; like all non-Beast WoD games I'm optimising for single splat play.

                                      If you want to play crossover you're priorities will be different and some adjustments will be needed. This is not the biggest issue, I do not envy whatever storyteller tries to balance a combat encounter for a mixed splat party including a Leviathan.
