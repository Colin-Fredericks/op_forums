                                            Mr. McFarland has made an anouncement!

                                            Originally posted by BlackHat_Matt
                                            I sent a long email to Rich, Rose, and Michelle (the editor of Beast) tonight. I'm not going to go into the contents (yet) because I need to actually have some feedback from folks, but basically it was a proposed batch of changes/fixes to the material, based in large part on the discussions happening here and elsewhere.

                                            Now, I could be flip and say that these discussions might have happened anyway, or I could be contrite and say that we really want all y'all to love the game so we're changing it, but neither of those things is precisely true. The truth of the matter is: This game is generating discussion. It's generating controversy. It's challenging people. I think that's good...but I also think that some of the criticism that's come up is entirely valid. Beasts do need a more defined struggle and culture. They do need specific things to do. Their relationship with Heroes does require some more scrutiny.

                                            So: To the people who have read the game and made their opinions clear without being hostile, to the people who have shared what they feel works as well as what doesn't, to to the people who have been constructive with their criticism: Thank you. I tell my authors all the time, "kill your darlings," but sometimes it's hard to know which darlings to kill without some outside perspective. I won't say that this discussion hasn't stung; it has. I'm a writer and I'm as sensitive as any other writer. But at the same time, as I was scribbling notes in my li'l red notebook last night, I also found myself really getting excited to run Beast (starting up a chronicle when I get back from vacation).

                                            I think the changes are going to strengthen the game, and I'll be talking with the rest of the team about how best to communicate those changes to the backers and the readers so you'll get a quick sense of what we're doing.

                                                Originally posted by Rater202 View Post
                                                I've seen like, four valid criticisms for the book vs a whole bunch that can only have come from misunderstanding and/or not paying attention to the relevant passages
                                                I don't want to sound rude or aggressive, but if I need to cautiously read 400 pages cover to cover in order to evaluate this game's potential and/or the feelings coming from it, then something's wrong. Something should be adressed so that I don't read 399 pages of things that make me cringe to finally read the last one and understand that this wasn't all that bad after all.

                                                Also, as far as mechanics go, I don't like the Hunger states to be represented by Conditions. It will drive players to constantly go from satiety 3 to 5 and back again. Non stop. They'll craft the story around the idea that they must find a way to use Atavisms, and then make an easy feed. I know it's not every players that will, but since the Condition system was built to encourage players to resolve them, well then the easiest way to do so is to jump between Satiety 2 and 5 as many times as possible.

                                                Also, for some odd reason, Satiety is stated to represent the connection that the Beast has with the physical world, with his human self (page 92, first sentence), which honestly makes no sense and is referenced nowhere else. Then there's the Life and Legend (Virtue and Vice) which are supposed to represent the Human and the Monster, but which really don't. Life is the sole mechanical "morality" trait, and it's really not.

                                                I think what I'm trying to say here is that people (including myself) complain about Beast's Evil-Only tendencies and the counter-argument is that all monsters in the WoD are amoral monsters.

                                                My point, then, is that this is completelly true, but not mechanically encouraged in their respective games. Sociopath murdering monsters all face consequences both mechanically and in a role-play sense.

                                                -Vampires lose Humanity and risk Masquerade Breach
                                                -Werewolves risk unbalancing themselves and getting the ire of their Tribe
                                                -Wanton Mages lose Wisdom, causing their Nimbus to grow and become stronger
                                                -Prometheans lose Humanity, getting farther from their goal
                                                -Changelings risk Clarity and getting banned from their Court
                                                -Sin-Eaters risk their sanity whenever they kill someone not deserving
                                                -Hunters lose their Integrity in a downward spiral into madness
                                                -Demons must follow the code of their Cover of risk being targeted by the Eye Of Sauron
                                                -Beasts....get bonus XP

                                                I think that's really the point that many people dislike. Not the fact that it's dark or grim or an endless cycle of abuse. Simply the fact that trying to be good has nothing to it. At least in Vampire, if you only feed on animals or from blood banks, using your powers as a vigilante, you'll probably be able to go out in the sun, to chat with an old friend and to look at the other players while saying "My Humanity is at 8!". Sure they get to use their powers more often, can do horrible things without consequences (thanks to Banes) and are more in tune with the Danse Macabre, but the Good-Guy-Vampire still has a feeling of success from a mechanical standpoint.

                                                        Losing Humanity is mechanically encouraged by 2nd edition of Vampire, because any Conditions gained and resolved in service of the story grants Beats. The story is about stuff like falling humanity and managing the Conditions that come along with it.

                                                        While Beast is due for some focusing based on Matt's post, it's pretty clear that fluctuating Hunger is very much a part of the game, so Conditions reflecting levels of Satiety and the resolutions that accompany them seems pretty clearly to be a desired state. Your Hunger is very, very important for a game. Satisfying that hunger, expending Satiety, they want you to do that, hence Conditions which incentivize it.

                                                        If you think these games encourage you to be a bad person solely by granting Beats, you're missing the point. You get Beats when you lose your Integrity, you get them when you keep it. Demons gain Conditions when they risk their Cover and when they lose it. Beats are rewards for playing into and resolving Conditions because doing so adds drama to the story. In all reality, it is the story most of the time.

                                                        If you feel your players are "gaming" the system by playing the game, you can see how that's a little wonky, can't you?

                                                        I really, really hate to play this card, but I think you're thinking "simulationist" when the 2nd edition trends towards "narrativist." That is, the bad things that happen to characters like Losing Humanity or Integrity or Cover are encouraged because they make story, drama and the game. If you were never losing any of these things, then you're probably not playing the game at all, because the act of going out and doing things necessitates risking all of them. It's considered a good thing, because you're trying to tell a story, and story results from stuff happening, from these things being risked and lost, from the up and down. Beast seems very keen on this, to the degree that Matt said early on that Satiety is likely fluctuating regularly in a given session.

                                                        If you just don't like that, it's fine, but don't make the mistake of thinking it's some kind of exploit or loophole for players to get Teh Sick Beatz.(tm) It's part of the game. If you feel like your players are "gaming" hungers and not actually developing story, then don't give them the Beats.
