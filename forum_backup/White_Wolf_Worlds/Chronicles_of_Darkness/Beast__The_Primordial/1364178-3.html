        A simple (and possibly overpowered?) fix would be to let Gifts work against all sorts of monsters, not just Beasts; and then retool Kinslayer to be less about making Gifts useful at all against non-Beasts and more about giving some special edge when Family Ties are involved.

        Kinslayer might still be the only way to use Heroic Tracking and Heroic Stalking against someone who isn't innately tied to the Primordial Dream (e.g., Beasts and Dreamborn). Also, maybe Kinslayer could allow the Hero to place an Anathema on those with Family Ties; but for them, the Anathema always manifests as its Low-Satiety effect (so they're generally not as effective), and the Anathema is temporary, going away at the end of the scene (or maybe chapter? I'm spitballing here).

        That would maintain the desired feel that becoming Kin to a Beast makes you especially vulnerable to a Kinslayer Hero, while greatly opening up the possibilities available to all Heroes. That said, I wouldn't let a Kinslayer gain new Gifts by killing Kin. That would remain a Beast-only thing; not even most Dreamborn would count, although larval Horrors might — they are, after all, nascent Beasts.

        And making “kill a Beast” the only way to gain Gifts makes things needlessly difficult for the likes of Sleeping Beauty; there should be other ways to gain them. I'm not sure what those other ways might be; only that they ought to be harder to accomplish than killing a Beast is, and/or not the sort of thing that the usual Hero would think of. I'm perfectly fine with the Hero-as-foil thinking that the only way to grow more powerful is to kill Beasts. But Sleeping Beauty ought to be able to grow without having to slaughter Beasts in order to do so.

        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                        Anyways, Sane Heroes.

                        Assuming a Hero doesn't transcend their condition somehow, the most sane Hero I can think of is one who steps up primarily to a Beast who's being right jackass and tries to get them back onto the program-and making that clear, that means acknowledging that both are icons that guide the masses in terms of aspiration and rejection, and therefore not killing all but the most egrigious of Beasts and otherwise smacking them around a bit to soften the ego, and then working to impart some wisdom to Beasts so that they may be better wisdom dispensers themselves. Violence may be a part of their lives, but like the better Beasts in their community, good Heroes understand that they have the power to impart strong values to others through their actions, and iron can sharpen iron without breaking the other. THe pagentry of the conflict between Beasts and Heroes can be very powerful and beneficial stuff, but it works best when it's not done to fill a hole in their lives and under assumption of the worst of their opponents. Heroes build both regular humanity and Beasts (and maybe even other monsters) in the same way a truly transcendent Beast will do for all of the above.

                        Both Beasts and Heroes are, ultimately, meant to be metaphorical guides for the masses, and they are at their best when they strip the ego, work to really understand everyone, and are willing to make themselves object lessons without murdering each other for it.

                                    I suppose my question is, what's the gameplay goal from playable Heroes? What do you want a session of "Beast: The Primordial, but y'all can be Heroes if you want" to look like?

                                                Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                                I will say I have myself complained that while Beasts work well in crossovers, Heroes themselves have a limited crossover capacity with other games, and a lot of this ties into that.

                                                The center of their conflict should always be with Beasts, but Some Diversification is not a bad thing. A Hero should be a decently viable sideways antagonist to throw in most games.
                                                As a random thought, I feel that to a Hero, all supernatural beings that step up to him are Beasts, and he engages them as such, as hero of his tale. He can’t be the aggressor in this story, as that wouldn’t make him the hero, but if he’s in a vampire’s territory and it decides to stalk him, it’s a beast. If he’s with his family and werewolves begin hassling them, they’re beasts and suffer the weight of the anathema he brings to bear.

                                                But these are only lesser minions sent to trouble the Hero while he hunts his true foe, the Beast itself. The Hero can hunt the Beast as that’s the story. But these lesser creatures only make themselves his enemies when they stand in his way. Of course, smart Heroes figure this out and can twist the narrative to be deliberately provocative, essentially forcing other creatures to notice him, and he feels the invigoration of anathema power as those beings decide to hunt.
