                    The big question about how any society reacts to a Beast is largely dependent on what an individual Beast does to garner the attention of that society. THe broad stereotypes provided by each template about Beasts is a good measuring stick, a decent way to start brainstorming ideas, but the fundamental problem at hand is that the thing you're asking about is something that is best answered in game. A freehold is going to respond to a Beast who gained their attention by blowing up a strip mall differently from a factional krewe who met the Beast when she was being the life of the party at the last Wake. Similarly, a traditional Mesen-Nebu guildmaster, who will even sort of applaud those who steal from them, is going to respond differently to a Collector than they would to a Ravager, the former with favor and the latter with disdain(maybe). A less traditional Alchemist is more likely to be pissed at either one, but might respond favorably to the workings of a Tyrant.

                    This is a thing that can be talked about in later supplement, but it's largely not useful to hypothesize about how society reacts when it quite frankly comes down to each person's game.

                                For me, personally, I find Beast to be too...happy.

                                The main grievance I have is that the Beasts don't feel like they have anything to worry about. They realize that they are Beasts and that they have been forced into this role for some grand narrative, locked into some story in which they must be the villain. And they're pretty okay with it. Sure, there are some crazy people who want to play Hero and attempt to slay them, but it's all good to them.

                                Granted, I am simplifying the concepts of Beast into what I feel it all relates to, but that is what I see when I think of how Beast currently is. Tonally, it's pretty feel-good. Two of the Beasts in the story are gay and in a relationship (I have no issue with that, being a gay male myself who likes seeing little things such as that in games), but given that Beast plays up the idea that there will be people who will hate you for what you are, it kinda hit the nail on the head a little too hard.

                                For me, I feel that Beast, or at least the inspiration for the tone of Beast, should have shifted away from Beauty and the Beast, where it focuses on the idea that Beast is a good person despite his outward appearance, and should have taken a bit from The Tiger's Bride. For those of you not familiar, The Tiger's Bride is a short story written by Angela Carter that is based on the classic story of Beauty and the Beast but reverses the idea by having the Beast be a literal tiger who hides his nature and masquerades as a human. The Beauty of the story, feeling a kinship with the Beast due to her being treated similarly to an animal due to her status as a young woman, realizes her own nature and allows the Beast to lick the skin off her body, revealing that she is also a tiger. I would have loved for Beast to have incorporated that idea for the actual Beasts. Granted, Beasts start off as humans unaware of their true nature, but there doesn't feel like there is any conflict between who they are/were as humans and who they are in reality. Being seen as human by mortals isn't something that is truly awkward or conflicting for them; it's more of a convenience so they can go munching on fear when the lights go out without anyone suspecting a thing.

                                As for the treatment of Heroes, I would have preferred to interpret their relationship as, rather than a mortal wielding a stick against a nightmare-ish creature who can crush them like a grape, to something more modern and even, like Batman going against his Rogues Gallery. Bruce Wayne became Batman when something unfortunate occurred, causing him to fight back in order to keep Gotham safe. This, in turn, inadvertently meant that the many individuals who he faced were, in some way, influenced by his presence. Granted, they were all a bit loony before he came to exist, but his constant existence molded the way they reacted. (Note: I'm basically taking this from the TvTropes website, the Trope "Create Your Own Villain", and the Batman section. I'm not a devout follower of Batman nor do I feel that I have a strong understanding of the Batman series, but I do know that some of Batman's reoccurring antagonists were influenced by Batman himself) Each villain as some sort of rhyme and reason to do what they feel should be done and Batman responds by preventing them from doing what they want to do. Is Poison Ivy wrong for being an environmentalist and wanting people to take care of the world around them? No, not exactly. Is she wrong for inspiring people with fear with her man-eating plants and mind-influencing pollen? That is a more relativistic question, where Poison Ivy feels that she is in the right and Batman (and, presumably, most of Gotham) feels she is in the wrong. I feel the same should be reflected in Beast. Is a Tyrant who wants to invert the power structure of some societal belief wrong for doing so in some vicious manner? Possibly no, but there will be someone who feels that it should not change and rise up to prevent said Tyrant from doing so. Maybe to fill some void within themselves, sure, but also because, in some way, that is what they feel is right.

                                Of course, that is all my own opinion. Beast, despite whatever grievances people have with it, is a fine game where it stands now. Some people may be confused and want clarification, and that's fine. Some people may want there to be changes in order to better represent what they believe Beast should represent, and that's also fine. Objectively, I think Beast is a wonderful addition to the World of Darkness and allows players to be something that has not been well represented in the other game lines. Subjectively, I feel that Beast may not be thematically tasty for my palate.

                                But what do I know? I may have interpreted things incorrectly and may have created my own inferences with no basis whatsoever. Feel free to comment or discuss so that I, and maybe others, will be able to understand.

                                                        Originally posted by MCN View Post
                                                        Its happy when, by nature, you're someone that voyeuristically feeds on people's loss and defeat? I guess I can't reconsile happy with effectively playing a sadist.
                                                        Further to this, you can feed reasonably harmlessly, but it's only small meals you have to eat often, and you risk your inner monster going out to feed itself (in fairness, though, that only seems to cause vivid nightmares). You want a good meal? It has to be elaborate, specific or heinous. You want a really good meal? It has to be all of those. The fuller you get, the less of a general danger you represent to people, but the worse you have to be to get there. Unlike a vampire, your feeding can't be hidden behind an enforced sense of bliss. You could restrict yourself to people who deserve, but they don't come along every day, so you have to deal with the temptation to let deserve slide into "deserve." Finding a balance you can live with doesn't seem happy to me, it seems like a grind. (Unless you're playing a straight-up bad guy of course).


                                                        For me, though, Beast doesn't particularly need fixing. The only thing I'd like to see is the adoption of a philosophical splat (I kind of think of Family and Hunger as two X's, despite it not being impossible for Hunger to change). In this case, the teaching deal would be one such philosophy. Another would take Family as an imperative: that is, the purpose of Beasts is to seek out other supernaturals and become middle men and social glue for them. One would follow their humanity, and live relatively ordinary lives, balanced by their new powers and concerns (something I think Beast is oddly suited to, given its outré protagonists). There might be one that takes feeding their Hunger as a righteous imperative (which might either replace any conscience they have or uneasily coexist with it).
