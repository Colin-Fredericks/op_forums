            Savage Beast (ooo)

            Effects: Your Horror is a creature of blind instinct and ferocity who cares little for the civilities of Man. Leave the thinking to the egg heads, you just want to smash your enemy and move on to the next. Such brutality makes it difficult to think straight at times, but that doesn't really matter as long as the job gets done.             Cunning Beast (ooo)

            Effects: Your Horror is a creature of surgical precision and strategical calculation. Its not one for grandiose displays of power, especially when a coordinated strike is enough get the job done. While some monsters like to bray and shout about how mighty they are, you watch and wait until victory is assured before springing into action. When performing an Intelligence or Dexterity check, you gain an exceptional success on 3 instead of 5. However, when rolling a Strength or Stamina check, all rolls suffer a -2 die pool.

                So I did this sometime back
                Loup-Garou (ooo)
                Prerequisites: Beast, Relentless Hunter, Family Ties: Werewolves
                The Beast's Kinship with the Hunters left a mark on it - it's Horror is invigorated by their vitality, and heals more quickly.
                Effect: The Normal Effect of Relentless Hunter is doubled - it heals 2 Bashing per turn, 1 Lethal every 15 minutes and 1 Aggravated every other day.
                The Low Satiety Effect is increased to damage equal to 3 times its Lair dots at the end of any scene in which he was hurt get downgraded. Satiety Expenditure remains unchanged.

                Drawback: The Horror is used to being around a Pack - being deprived of such interaction for more than 5 days, it grows lethargic, and instead of doubling the effect of Relentless Hunter, it is diminished to 1 Bashing every 2 Turns, 1 Lethal every hour and 1 Aggravated every week. The Low Satiety Effect is reduced to an amount of damage equal to Lair dots getting reduced.
                , which got me thinking: How would other Kinships impact Atavisms?

                So, here I try:

                Second Wind (oo)
                Prerequisites: Beast, Relentless Hunter, Family Ties: Thyrsus Mage
                The Beast's Kinship with the Shamans left a mark on it - his Horror can seemingly come back from the brink of Death.
                Effect: The Satiety Expenditure Effect of Relentless Hunter is magnified - In addition to its normal effect, physical tilts like Drugged and Arm Wrack are removed, as the Horror's nature knits and mends muscles, tendons, skin and bone and discharges any harmful substances, burning away the last traces of it.

                Hunter in the Night (ooo)
                Prerequisites: Beast, Relentless Hunter, Family Ties: Vampire
                The Beast's Kinship with the city's Kindred left a mark on it - his Horror becomes a creature of the night.
                Effect: Once per scene, the Beast may spent a turn to heal up to half her Lair rounded up in Lethal, or up to her Lair in Bashing.

                Drawback: This benefit only applies at night.

                Deadened Flesh (ooo)
                Prerequisites: Beast, Unbreakable, Family Ties: Vampire
                The Beast's Kinship with the city's Kindred left a mark on it - his Horror mimics their toughness as its flesh becomes less and less lifelike.
                Effect: Whenever the Beast would suffer Lethal damage, up to her Lair points of damage get reduced to Bashing.
                Drawback: The Beast looks somehow off when the Horror is hungry- Low Satiety applies a -2 penalty to all social rolls (except to intimidate).

                Unstoppable Drive(oo)
                Prerequisites: Beast, Unbreakable , Family Ties: Promethean
                The Beast's Kinship with a Created left a mark on it - his Horror becomes more resilient.
                Effect: In addition to the normal and low Satiety Effects, the Beast ignores Wound Penalties.
                Drawback: The proximity of Fire temporarily disables this benefit, and causes the wound penalty to double. If the Beast is immune to Fire, she cannot take this Merit.

                Impervious Skin (ooo)
                Prerequisites: Beast, Unbreakable, Family Ties: Gangrel with high Resilience
                The Beast's Kinship with the Hunters left a mark on it - his Horror shrugs of attacks that would fell others.
                Effect: The Beast gains half its lair rounded down in Armor in addition to the other effects. (+1/0 at 2, +2/0 at 4, +3/0 at 6, +4/0 at 8, +5/0 at 10).
                Drawback: When he does recieve Lethal, the Beast must roll Resolve + Composure. On a failed roll, he freaks out and falls in a faux-frenzy (no mechanical benefits) for [Lair] turns.

                Display of Might (ooo)
                Prerequisites: Beast, Cyclopean Strength, Family Ties: Titan Promethean
                The Beast's Kinship with a Titan left a mark on it - his Horror bursts with prowess in the face of adversity.
                Effect: The Horror may spend one willpower once per day to add her Lair to any Athletics dicepools for the remainder of the scene



                Feedback and comments much appreciated.

                    Originally posted by Wormwood View Post
                    So I did this sometime back , which got me thinking: How would other Kinships impact Atavisms?
                    […]
                    Feedback and comments much appreciated.
                    The first thing that comes to mind is that I don't feel Family Ties are an appropriate prerequisite for Merits. These aren't like Werewolf's Code of Honor, where the associated Trait can change but naturally justifies itself toward one end of the scale over the other with sufficient instability; Conditions can be long-lasting or even nigh-on permanent, but their relation to Merits doesn't work in that direction and it especially doesn't work that way when Family Ties is a Condition that other characters have — if Display of Might remains an element of your character after the Titan changes Refinements or reaches the New Dawn or perishes or severs ties with you, then there is no mechanical point to the prerequisite.

                    The only things in the extended Begotten template that require Family Ties concern themselves with either the ability to develop a new power or the ongoing ability to pass casual scrutiny as the same sort of monster, and Atavisms exist at the opposite end of the Satiety scale from where those powers are relevant to the natural trajectory of its resident Beast; relying on the overtly shocking power of Atavisms generally means opportunities for Kinship are more fraught and isolating.*

                    A Merit to boost an Atavism should therefore lean on more permanent elements of the character or their Hunger. For example:

                    Child of the Dark (•-•••)
                    Prerequisite:                     Survival dots equal to rating.
                    Your Horror is practically synonymous with a particular aspect of its Lair, and more readily exerts its power in such places.
                    Effect: When you take this Merit, choose a Lair Trait and an Atavism. When in the presence of that Lair Trait, you may add your dots in this Merit to 3 for the purposes of permitting the use of the linked Atavism's Low Satiety effect. This Merit may be purchased multiple times, with each purchase reflecting a different Atavism; connecting a different Atavism to the same Lair Trait applies the full rating of the Merit associated with that Lair Trait to the new Atavism for the cost of one Merit dot. You may connect the same Atavism to different Lair Traits, but doing so adds two dots to the Merit cost.
                    Drawback: Using the presence of one of your Lair Traits to apply another to a location costs an additional dot of Willpower if the linked Trait is not one of the Traits you are applying. Subtract your dots in this Merit from rolls to open a Primordial Pathway to a place without the linked Lair Trait. Subtract your dots in this Merit from your Lair rating for the purposes of determining how many times per scene you may use the presence of a Lair Trait to use the linked Atavism's Satiety Expenditure effect by spending Willpower instead of Satiety for any Lair Trait other than the linked Trait.

                    Riddling Presence (•••)
                    Prerequisites: Trained Observer 3, We Know All Your Secrets
                    Your time among keen-eyed and sharp-eared monsters has stirred dormant aspects of your Horror to better leverage its senses.
                    Effect: Once per chapter, when your character uses an Atavism effect that provides them with knowledge or sensory information about a person in your presence (such as Needs Must, Mimir's Wisdom, or some applications of Shadowed Soul or Heart of the Ocean) you may spend a point of Willpower and roll Presence + Intelligence + Lair to treat them as a Contact in a relevant sphere of your choice for the rest of the scene.

                    Home Ground (•••••)
                    Prerequisites:                     You Cannot Kill It, Occult 3
                    Your time among hardy and resilient monsters has given you insight into their persistence that grants you a conditional form of their invulnerability.
                    Effect: While in the presence of one of your Lair Traits or in the location of one of your Chambers, your character gains all the benefits of the Relentless Hunter and Unbreakable Atavisms even if they do not have it. If they do have that Atavism, they recover a point of Willpower at the end of any scene in which they use their Satiety Expenditure effects.
                    Drawback: When you take this Merit, choose three Lair Traits you do not have. The presence of any of those Lair Traits prevents you from gaining the benefits of the Relentless Hunter or Unbreakable Atavisms even if you have them normally.

                    * Tangentially related, a weird side avenue:

                    Storied Advocate (•••)
                    Prerequisites:                     Fame (Advanced), Any Kinship Nightmare
                    You use your place in the collective unconscious as a means to more comprehensively spread fear of particular monsters.
                    Effect: You may apply your Advanced Fame dots as a bonus to any Nightmare that you can reasonably connect to a character who has the Family Ties Condition tagged to your character. In addition, you may use a Kinship Nightmare you do not know on a human character once per chapter as long as it can be reasonably derived from a character who has the Family Ties Condition for you.
                    Drawback: Characters who have the Family Ties Condition for your character suffer the drawback of a Fame Merit equal to your character's Advanced Fame dots during any story in which you use this Merit.

                        Tiny Terror (o to oo)

                        Effects: The sound of rapid pitter patter, accompanied by high pitched giggling as you dart in and out of your victims peripheral. Your Horror is deceptively small for such a dangerous creature. It might take the form of a possessed doll, or a blood thirsty child, or any other number of small framed monsters. While in the form of your Horror, you gain +2 on Dexterity and Stealth rolls at first rank, and an additional +1 at the second rank. But as a drawback, you suffer a -1 and -2 Size penalty respectively.



                        Pervasive Horror (ooo)

                        Prerequisites: Presence (oo), Manipulation (oo)

                        Effects: Not all horrors cause humanity to flee their presence at the first opportunity. Some have a more subtle approach that takes longer to notice, but is just as terrible as any city smashing giant or ship sinking leviathan. Your Horror is adept at twisting society to its will, convincing their victims to bring about their own downfall while feeding upon their despair. For the purpose of using this Merit, you receive a +2 bonus on any Manipulation rolls that do not involve overtly intimidating your target.

                                            Originally posted by Wormwood View Post
                                            Sorry, I don't quite follow: establishing Kinship and using that as grounds to purchase a Nightmare (thus modyfing the supernatural makeup of the character) is okay, but not ok for merits? Is that what you mean?
                                            Merits generally have stable prerequisites that often factor into their continued usability — Area of Expertise becomes completely useless if you lose the Skill that makes it work, but it takes extraordinary circumstances to lose Skill dots in general — whereas Kinship Nightmares have a gateway circumstance that can be completely unintentional but serves to let the character grow their supernatural power in a sort of digestive manner — once you've internalized the fear of an implacable enemy that your Promethean sister represents by learning You Cannot Kill It, that Nightmare stays learned, and the only prerequisite to its usability is that you have any Satiety at all.

                                            Applying Family Ties serves a similar function to having Connected, in that it represents a bond that goes away when bridges are burned or one party no longer exists, but Connected is not a prerequisite for having a given Social Merit.

                                            If you mean "Why make it a requirement in the first place if you can keep the ability even when the Kinship ends?", my answer is simply: because it is more interesting, it provides a different kind of expression for the character, and having it end would be perfectly fine with me too, honestly. (Sanctity of Merits is a thing, so this would be narratively interesting without mechanically disadvantaging the player).
                                            One of the principal tensions in Beast is that your Kinship with other monsters only really gives you connections with individual monsters for as long as that connection isn't severed, and there are legitimate reasons for characters (your own or others) to want to sever that connection; powerful Beasts who want to maintain a Kinship with a particular type of monster despite the notional obligations of family and the reduced feeding pool and the pressures of low Satiety to hurt the monsters closest to them often wind up isolating and/or consuming their charges.

                                            If there were to be a consistent prerequisite for a Beast Merit based on a Kinship, I feel that a more coherent choice would be having a Social Merit tagged to a given monster or group of monsters, or having knowledge of a particular Kinship Nightmare like I did above; Family Ties has the awkward distinction of not just being a Condition, but being a Condition that another character has, which itself makes having it as a dependent prerequisite for a Merit's use by the Beast serve as an abandonment-detector in a way that doesn't track too well.

                                            You could make a Merit whose effect depends on having a given active Kinship, maybe, but that seems more in the vein of a variable Merit than a set of specific bonuses. For instance:

                                            Your Mother's Eye (•-•••••)
                                            Prerequisites: Beast, Occult or Socialize 2
                                            You mirror the abilities of your kin at a more inconstant level than the slow adaptation of your Horror.
                                            Effect: At the start of any chapter, you may distribute your dots in this Merit between any Merits or Skill rating possessed by a character who is related to your character by blood or who has the Family Ties Condition tagged to your character; borrowing a Skill that you do not possess any dots in costs a point of Willpower per roll, but having dots in a Skill reduces the dot cost of benefiting from your kin's rating to the difference between the two (i.e. if you have three dots in this Merit and two dots of Socialize, borrowing your mother's three dots of Socialize would only take one of your three dots in this Merit).
                                            Drawback: At the end of any chapter in which you benefit from the effects of this Merit, roll your Lair rating. On a success, lose one point of Satiety.

                                                    That there is a certain redundancy written in the formatting of those Merits that happens to span into the Prerequisites section doesn't really change the fact that "This Merit works on any animal of the appropriate type that you share the Bonded Condition with and have had an appropriate amount of time to train" is closer to "This Merit works with these types of weapon and doesn't work without one of them" than "You can't practically have this Merit without currently having an instance of this Condition."

                                                    Maybe this is more of a hack to you, but a Kinship-based system that does for Atavisms a bit of what Kinship does for Nightmares is something I personally would just do with something like Merits, since there should be some sort of xp-cost to it. I would like to make more use of Kinship in my games, and just 'fitting in' is not always enough to translate this sense of Beast's Family.
                                                    You could also make a Merit that modifies Passing Resemblance or Family Dinner or lets you "save" a number of Kinship bonds based on Lair; there's a number of potential workarounds to the thing, but the main dichotomy I keep going back to is that Atavisms are very direct and incontrovertible expressions of the Begotten nature through lasting adaptations of the Horror, and that this contrasts against Kinship's greater focus on letting your environment do your job for you by empowering monstrous proxies or bringing the broader framework of Nightmares to bear on a subject or drawing your Chambers to more loosely-related areas and blending into the background as a mundane human or a different sort of monster.

                                                    Accordingly, I feel like changes to Atavisms that are related to Kinship in a sense more involved than narrative justification for buying a new Atavism should either filter those relationships through the Hunger-weighted side of the scale or bring more of the complications of Kinship to the use of the Atavism — if you're binding one of the truest expressions of your power to an external source, you should either have a greater incentive to internalize that source (maybe take the Addicted Condition tied to their presence or some shared ritual between the pair of you) or become more susceptible to that source's influence (e.g. take a Persistent form of Swooning for the source of the Merit).

                                                    Outside the context of learning Nightmares, Kinships key into a whole narrative framework of support and vulnerability that doesn't quite mesh with a straightforward "buff the powers that mark me as specifically a Beast" trick — even within the context of Kinship Nightmares, exhibiting an uncommon power implies a referent from which the Begotten developed that ability, which suggests either the relationship still exists or something happened to it that hints at the power of the Beast or the existence of something stronger than them that they no longer keep in touch with; "I learned this from my time in Boston courting an unkillable terror" is still a story to tell even if you killed that terror or parted with them on unfriendly terms or never remember to write home and ask about their kids, and that relationship doesn't call for the same upkeep as befriending a walking forest to disguise yourself as a tree.

                                                    You got me thinking, though: I also could just take those Merits above, and make them conditions, instead. Conditions with prerequisites and very powerful benefits, so I'd want to put on some harsh additional requirements. But sure, that might work. Hm.
