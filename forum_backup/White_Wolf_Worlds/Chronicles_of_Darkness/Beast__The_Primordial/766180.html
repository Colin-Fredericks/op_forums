      The Lair is a narratively active place, so the short answer is: if that's what your character wants. As we lack much of the information on the Astral Realms, it's hard to definitively rule on how that'd work mechanically, but Kinship would likely be a requirement, particularly when dealing with Astral Entities or other Ephemeral Beings in the Lair. As to material beings (Supernatural or otherwise), some will be harder to bring in then others.

          According to word of god during playtest/spoilers, lairs can be inhabited. Or rather seem to be inhabited- a castle can have servants, a prison might have prisoners and guards, an ocean might have sealife, etc, but they aren't real, more like NPCs in a videogame.

                      Originally posted by Dreaminggod View Post
                      So... what happens to animal who are brought into a Beast's lair? Do any other creatures, Astral or not, inhabit a Beast's Lair? If a Beast has a sprawling jungle as a Lair is it devoid of life?
                      You could make an interesting argument that "real" animals brought to the Lair should slowly change, developing odd traits . . . possibly even becoming the Primordial equivalent of cryptids:
                      • You know they're there. You hear them scuttling behind you, and through the darkness. From the sounds, there must be tens of thousands of them by now. But no matter how fast you turn your head, or get the lights on, they're always gone. Set something down, though, and look away . . . and you may never see it again. Why, oh why, didn't you kill that cockroach when you had a chance?
                      • Its just a parrot. You keep telling yourself that. But after years of living in a Namtaru's undersea cave . . . It spends its time singing disturbing songs about long lost ships, and making cryptic prophecies about the future. And why does it keep looking at you like that?!?
                      • The snake eggs are starting to hatch! How cool is that? They actually hatched for you! The little snake sticks its head out of the egg . . .and then a second head . . and a third . . .
                      • Maggie was just a mangy old hound you found out on the street. Thin, and stringy, with a matted coat and big chocolate eyes . . . how could you leave her there? After a couple of months, though, she was happy and healthy again. The other day, though, you found her gnawing a bunch of bones . . . bones you didn't give her. Bones that look distinctly human. And is it just you, or have her eyes turned red?
                      After all, a lot of the old stories have "lesser" monsters woven into the narrative. Things that weren't famous enough, or striking enough (one guy had a flock of man-eating sheep, for example), to get their own myth. They weren't necessarily allied to the main monster . . . but they were there. And it does make a certain degree of sense that exposure to the Dream would slowly turn man's best friend into monster's . . .

                      My suggestion would be to keep any such changes thematically appropriate to both the Beast's Lair and Legend. Worms released into a desert chamber might go Tremors (maybe even Dune or Beetlejuice). A cockatrice might hatch from a chicken's egg, if the egg hatches in the Lair of a medieval Beast.


                      Edit: And, of course, if they're actually helpful to (or under the control of) the Beast, they need to be paid for, just like any other advantage, merit, or Lair trait

                                      Originally posted by Hexmeister Vic View Post

                                      Our new cliché Beast: a highwayman with the soul of a Hydra and an unusually large pet Hermit Crab.
                                      Hmm. . . I could get behind that - particularly for a short story. A Beast breaks the arms & legs of a hunter (or Hero), and tosses them into a pit Chamber (or on a deserted beach Chamber) to rot. At first, he thinks he's trapped there all alone, with the bones of other unfortunates. Then one of the skulls starts to stir . . .and the hunter realizes a Dream-mutated hermit crab has claimed the skull as it's shell. Other hermit crabs, clearly in need of shells, start crawling out of the dark and the water, and start tapping him with their claws . . .



                                      Edit: I could also see a Dream-twisted crab using the entire corpse as sort of a zombie-shell. Personally, though, I think I'd prefer using roaches or maggots as the basis for such a creature. That way, you get the lurching motion, and it sheds maggots/roaches as it stumbles towards you, trying to talk . . .
