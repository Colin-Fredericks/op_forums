    I think the themes of fear, hunger and kinship have a strong focus throughout the text and were presented in an intriguing way. I think the characters are presented with a different tone in each half of the book. Beasts/Heroes are clarified in the end, but in the beginning they come across to me as unrelatable with alien mindsets. I view them now as truly amoral creatures with some human characteristics. I think that is still playable.

    The first half was well written and an interesting read. I just didn't find it appealing as a game, so if I was trying to convince someone to get into Beast I would tell them to read Chapter 5 earlier. I might put it before Chapter 3 or concurrent with Chapter 2. I think that would eliminate much confusion. But I'm not one that cares if Chapter 3 covers X because that is the way it has been done. It requires a hard sell to convince someone to make it through 300-400 pages before making a decision on whether you actually like the characters.

    Overall, I think this game could manage without additions, but the points that are being looked at by Matt wouldn't be hurt by some additional work. I also give a nod to the writers.

    Lingering Questions

    The sample hero Mariann appears to go from high integrity to hero overnight. What happened there? A segue might be in order.
    Beasts seem a tad listless. I'm not sure what they do aside from feeding and dodging Heroes. I'm assuming there is more to them.
    There was early talk of Beasts searching the occult in the beginning of the text and I don't know what happened with that. Missing element?
    Do Souls use Satiety to fuel Atavisms?

                For the most part, I have to nth the voices unimpressed by the preview text of Beast. My main issues with it are:

                - The subtext is questionable at best, particularly when you consider that the "outcast" and "hunted" Beasts are actually in a position of personal and social power that they use to torment the people around them and are distinctly more powerful than their main antagonists.

                - The way the crossover stuff is done leaves a bad taste in my mouth—it feels like a total Poochie thing, with the totally coolrad character suddenly inserted into the scene and treated as the best thing ever by everyone.

                - Beasts are, frankly, kind of boring. If you cross off the crossover stuff, there's just not much there: do unpleasant things to people to charge up your power meter (and be totally justified in it because you are special and those normal humans are just suckers who deserve everything they get!), and kill Heroes (and be totally justified in it because you are special and those Heroes are just egomaniacs who deserve everything they get!).

                    After reading through a large section of this thread, not the entirety but over 150 pages mostly starting around page 35-40ish, and having read through the Kickstarter document I'm in agreement with some of what I'm seeing. I feel the tone needs to be tightened up. I agree with some of what's been said that Beasts aren't really sympathetic so being an anti hero feels harder. Fluff is thankfully easy to change at a whim, but the Mechanics back up basically being a dick on some level. I'm more of a mechanics guy than fluff guy as I can just change whatever fluff I don't like.

                    I made a comment on kickstarter about the Satiety conditions and how Satiety is our power pool and also our "integrity/morality" stat. I feel that the extremes of Ravenous and Slumbering are suitably terrible conditions to ever get and you'll want to avoid them. My issue is that the other 3 conditions are just more crapsack. The only winner out of the 5 is Starving. It doesn't feel right that Starving is kind of your badass I want to live here state of existence. At Starving: Anathema penalties are lessened, Atavisms are stronger, Your condition penalty is a minor penalty to open Pathways, and while Starving your soul will accept a whole lot of things to feed your hunger.

                    By comparison Sated isn't too bad, it's the window of opportunity for Heroes to apply Anathemas. No bonuses, but no real penalties besides Anathemas at this level. So still a crapsack but it's a story driven crapsack in that Anathemas have to have a window or they don't really matter.

                    Gorged on the other hand blows so many chunks why would you really want to be there? You get a +2 to open Pathways, stronger Nightmares, oh and lose Lair to your supernatural defense vs mind affecting things. So because I'm feeling refreshed from eating and not starving myself I become more easily influenced by mind buggery magic? It clashes with my image of a Dragon fresh off the feast immense in his power, secure in his home, but because he's not hungry he's no harder to Dominate than a Mortal. The real disconnect comes from the fact that these Conditions all still apply to the Beast Incarnate.

                    If you become an Incarnate, the pinnacle endgame of beast, you can no longer hit Ravenous or Slumbering. You can still be Gorged, Sated, and Starving. Why is an Incarnate at Satiety 7-10 losing their bonus Supernatural Tolerance to mindbuggery? These are supposed to be the badass monsters that other Beasts are wary of and look up to. When they're fed though, easier to slap you with a Nightmare! Not exactly thrill/fear inducing.

                    The players at my table will unfortunately look at things the same way, as cool as Nightmares are being a Dragon is more about breathing Fire and wrecking shit to them. And since you get beats from these Conditions I see them feeding and dropping back to 3 fast as they can just spending satiety to drop, resolving the conditions and making a Beat farm happen.

                    I've only read through Atavisms and Nightmares once so far as I spent more time working to understand Lair, Hero/Beast stuff, Crossover themes, and then Satiety and it's associated Conditions. From what I've read they're pretty cool and flavorful, some are more open to re-skinning between the Families than others, but that's the nature of the beast. I think a little more time in development could flesh out some of the hiccups in the text. The overall tone, coverage of crossover material could use a little more meat in that section, some defining direction for the game beyond "the crossover splat", a revisit of Satiety conditions and their balance against the system and each other, and some work on Heroes and other antagonists.
