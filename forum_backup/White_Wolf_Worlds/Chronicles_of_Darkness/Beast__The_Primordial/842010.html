    Beasts overpowered? Try underpowered.

    03-04-2016, 02:14 PM
    This started off as an aside in a werewolf thread where someone objected to how a Clash of Wills example made Beasts seem overpowered. Personally, I find it mind boggling that people consider Beasts to be that powerful. And here I'm going to examine why I consider Beast to be one of the less "powerful" of supernaturals. In the course of examination, I will be comparing the Begotten to vampires, werewolves, mages, and changelings. I won't be using Demon, given that the balancing feature of the Unchained are Compromises, making the effectiveness of the demons highly subjective. As well, I will be using the following areas of comparison, found primarily in the CofD core book:

    * Investigation / Tracking
    * Research
    * Social Maneuvering / Social Merits
    * Chases
    * Stealth
    * Crafting
    * Combat

    These five areas cover what I feel to be the overwhelming majority of situations that will come up in a game in some shape or another, and provides a wide basis of understanding situations where and how Beasts shine, and where they faulter.

    Also, keep in mind that Beast, Mage, and Changeling, while we have enough information to do an analysis, aren't in a finalized edition yet. So, until I get a hard copy, some things may be subject to change.

    Investigation / Tracking
    Five supernaturals all living together. The Beast has a mortal-but-psychic brother, whom the vampire was dating, the wolf adopted into the pack, changeling pledged, and the mage occasionally worked magic with him as a Sleepwalker. One day, this mortal died, brutally murdered. All five supernaturals rush to the scene of the crime.

    Vampires have access to Auspex 1, 2, and 3, all relevant for tracking. Vampires have a keen nose for blood as well. Further, there are a few notable Theban and Cruac divination rituals that can be used to outright point you in the direction of the killer. Call up an animal to help with tracking. Mehket have a 1 dot vision merit as well. There's a 4 dot Prophesy ritual that you could bribe a Theban Sorcerer to cast to simply magically force the killer to fall into your lap! Low level Cruac rituals provide even more visions and benefits.

    Werewolves can pick up the scent of the killer, and start tracking immediately. Impossible Spoor even lets them track a getaway car. Call up spirits for help. Lick blood under fingernails for tracking bonus, assuming defensive wounds. Knowledge Gifts are a thing as well.

    Mages? Information gathering is their forte. Anything the other supernaturals can do in terms of gathering info? A mage can do better. Post-cognition, asking spirits, following sympathetic connections between the dead body and the killer, scrying, Fate magic to create a fate of meeting once again.

    Changelings. Almost as flexible as mages. There are several post-cognition powers. You could create a custom Goblin Vow. In 1e, there was Oath and Punishment. Reflections of the Past Fairest aspect lets you name a specific person to scry on (the killer); hells, the various mirror magics let you scry on the killer, then step immediately out of a nearby reflection right next to them! Changeling hours could arguably rewind time and restore the mortal from death!

    Beasts. The problem with beasts is that, for the most part, Atavisms (Needs Must/Heart of the Ocean) that let you track only focus on 1) people you met before, or 2) something resonating with your Hunger in the immediate area. Nightmares can't track people, only manipulate them. So, the relevant abilities here are Mirmir’s Wisdom, Needs Must and Direction Sense (advanced and epic). Mirmir's Wisdom seems pretty cool at first glance - Eidetic Memory, ehanced mental attributes, ability to tell lies, rote quality, Leverage, and temporary skill dots. The problem? All this is effectively mundane talent. Sure, you seem really good compared to a mortal. But, meanwhile, everyone else has already bypassed the need for doing things as a mortal. Mimir's Wisdom also lets you ask the ST a question to shed light on a subject. However, this question must be impersonal and cannot identify someone. Even Vampires can get visions that identify your mortal killers. Werewolves, mages, and changelings all have powers that do. The Beast cannot with Mimir's Wisdom. Which brings us to Needs Must / Heart of the Ocean. Needs Must, as said above, requires you to track someone you've already met in some way. You can also sense your Hunger fulfillment, but that not only relies on having an appropriate Hunger, but the killer to be nearby and no other options that will feed you. Lastly, there is Direction Sense. Advanced lets you track someone you've met before. Again. Still not helpful. Epic Direction Sense gives you post cognition. More helpful in knowing what happened, but still doesn't help you find your killer - you still haven't marked or directly sensed the killer, which all other abilities rely on.

    What does that leave us with? Beasts generally floundering at roughly a mortal pace. Generally speaking, Auspex and the Mehket Dream merit can do everything that Mimir's Wisdom and Direction Sense can when tracking an unknown person. And then, we have rituals that can do better. And vampires are generally the worst information gatherers of the early games. And all of this before we touch on the ability of mages and changelings to sense magic pretty much anywhere, giving them a huge leg up on any investigative tasks.

    Research
    Research is important when trying to identify weaknesses of supernatural spiritual enemies - ephemerals, strix, etc. Even demons suffer from being researched.

    Mimir's Wisdom is pretty badass when it comes to doing research. Mental boost, access to libraries in your head, free questions. Vampires, Changelings and Mages, meanwhile, actually have the actual Libraries to do research - its a key part of their society. They can get the same rote quality and very likely teamwork to help. And that's not touching on their supernatural abilities. The sad state of affairs is that Mimir's Wisdom mainly replicates mundane skill and equipment that everyone else has access to. Sure, you are likely to be better than the others at gathering the same information a mortal can. But its not taking into account the numerous divination powers others have access to that give information raw research can't.

    Meanwhile, lets look at Werewolves. They generally don't have Libraries - its not as important to their society like it is others. However, they don't need to do research - the Itheur is literally built to know everything that others need to research with their Aupsice abilities. Bane, ban, abilities, motives, drives? All Auspice powers. This does require a specific build, admittedly. But that's how the game is designed - you need to have a spread out pack to cover all threats.

    If we're talking doing research about physical opponents (ala Investigation rules), then again, we're looking at Mimir's Wisdom granting Leverage versus all the Auspice abilities, dream merit, Knowledge Gifts, divination Arcana, and various changeling divinations. One ability that works roughly at mortal speed versus supernatural insight that goes beyond the mortal is generally going to favor the supernatural insight. That one question of Mimir's Wisdom is pretty restrictive in a way no one else is.

    Social Maneuvering / Merits
    I consider this to be two separate areas that are closely related, though they can often come with different builds. For example, take Werewolf. Cahalith and Irraka both have social maneuvering abilties, but the Elodoth has the power to manipulate social merits.

    So, lets talk about social merits first. Vampire is pretty much defined by having supernatural Status everywhere. The Invictus and Carthians specialize in favor trading to get more and more social merit abilities. Elodoth can manipulate them through their Gifts. Mages have their Mystery Cults that come with their Order Status, which I'm sure comes with some Allies and Contacts when we start looking at the GotV and SL. With Changeling, it depends on the Court - Spring did in the past, and I can't vouch for all the new ones having access to some magical way to manipulate social merits and be the whole spider thing. Even then, though, you generally can call upon the help of your fellow Court mates. Beasts, meanwhile? It just doesn't come up. Its not a thing at any point. Even social Tyrants have to progress up the ladder like a mortal. Admittedly a mortal with magical benefits, but still at mortal speed. The Begotten have no innate social support or ties beyond the brood. That leaves them at a disadvantage compared to everyone else. Not really that big of a surprise, though - Beast is basically the anti-Hunter game, and social support is one of the strengths of that game. Why else would Profession and Conspiracies be that huge of a thing?

    Anyways, moving on to Social Maneuvering. Now, Beasts are looking pretty good with Alien Allure, Siren's Trecherous Song, and You Must Obey. You can even use Nightmares to help when Forcing Doors. Alien Allure and Siren give massive massive dice pools; we're talking like 2x Lair + extra striking looks benefit + fake Status. On the surface, we're looking pretty good. That is until you realize that large dice pools are pretty meaningless when opening Doors normally. Exceptional success gives you an extra door. Woo! You can magically boost Impression up from average to Good to maybe even Excellent with all the merits and Atavisms.

    Vampires bypass the need to open Doors entirely with Majesty and Dominate. Mages can open all Doors with a simple Mind spell. Irraka and Cahalith both have ways of opening doors for free. Changelings' Mask of Superiority opens 3 doors automatically; that's twice as effective as a simple Exceptional Success. I know for a fact that vampires, werewolves, and mages have ways of creating Perfect Impressions magically for when you want a different approach; I don't see anything with Changeling at the moment, but I could be missing something. When it comes to Maneuvering, its really not the size that matters, its how you use it. Or, rather, how well you can manipulate Impressions and auto-opening Doors. Rolling one giant dice pool once an hour isn't nearly as good as rolling five times in a single minute with an average dice pool. And even then, both pale before simply opening all of a mark's Doors at once. Using regular methods, a Beast simply lacks the same ability that all other splats have for playing nice.

    Speaking of which, Beasts do have a way of opening all doors at once. You Force them. In fact, the giant dice pool is very, very good for that. You even get an implied threat (and thus Hard Leverage) with the Satiety expendature of Alien Allure. Beasts are literally designed to excell at Forcing Doors. And sure, a Low Satiety AA can even repair damage the next day. That assumes that there is a next day, that your target hasn't become a Hero, and that your target hasn't used the burned bridges to sabotage you before that next day comes around. Which can very much be a problem, leaving enemies in your wake.

    Lets take a moment for looking at mind control. Nightmares, for the most part, all sort of control minds in some way. The problem is that a lot of them are combat abilities. Robbing your defense, direct damage, making someone run away in fear, inflicting poison. And the ones that aren't combat? Curses to make someone weaker in some way. Sure, that can make a target worse at socializing than you. But its not making you better. You can socially isolate someone unless they spend willpower, you can strip their Striking Looks, you can inflict penalties and Conditions. Which is good against a mortal, but doesn't stop another supernatural from using their above abilities to still be better at Social Maneuvering than you.

    You Must Obey is pretty much the signature mind control ability of the game. Which, unfortunately, is still highly restrictive. Compared to Dominate, you can't force someone to forget, you can't enforce complete compliance as they only operate out of fear, and once out of sight, they stop doign what you say. Dominate breaks when the target is struck, making it worthless in combat. YMO generally fails against threat of bodily harm or another Integrity breaking point. Vainglory and the Mind Arcana can rewrite people's memories as well as make them do things they normally wouldn't do. The Beast has nothing comparable. Werewolf lacks a lot of mind control powers, but they can craft fetishes that can have a lot of emotional manipulation and Urging someone to do things with spirits. Given everything, I'd say the two games are roughly comparable for last-place-on-mind-control.

    Chases
    Under the new rules, Urhan werewolves flat out dominate chases. No if, and, or but about it. And there are ways to make a victim stop being chased as well as Gifts to further boost the innate superiority in Chases. That said, while I will maintain that werewolves are hands down the best here, Beasts are pretty good. Not only can they run pretty fast with Wings of the Raptor, but this is a situation where Nightmares are handy, assuming you have enough Satiety to use them. Or the target doesn't use magical abilities to teleport away. Khaibit vamps, changelings, and mages all have teleport abilities.

    In any case, the point is that this isn't actually an area where the Beasts are underpowered. However, considering they come in second to wolves, they definitely aren't overpowered either.

    Stealth
    Vampires - Obfuscate, you can't see me! You don't notice me making sound or doing practically anything. You don't notice other people I make you ignore.

    Werewolf - super dice bonuses and rote quality against Clash of Wills. Sure, its mundane Stealth rolls, and you have to act stealthy, but there's so many bonuses that we're practically talking about assured success against anyone opposing you. Mages with Spirit Sight (rote quality to notice werewolves) are the only ones I'd give a chance of success, and that's pretty niche since they have to know to look in the first place. You can cross the Gauntlet for another method of stealth.

    Mage - take your pick of method. Creative Thaum at its best.

    Changeling - Phantom Glory, Light Shy, Nevertread... there's some very good methods here. Honestly, there's so many

    Beast - This is pretty bad, actually. There are two abilities - From the Shadows and Shadowed Soul. In the first case, its like Obfuscate lite. The Normal and Low Satiety are roughly equivalent to Obfuscate 1. Worse, because even passive magical senses break this with no contest. Passive mage sight, kenning, possibly even predator's aura. You have to spend Satiety to get the potential for Clash of Wills. And it breaks with an attack. Obfuscate 3 lets you pull others in to your magic to attack while still cloaked, blowing it out of the water. I don't think mage invisiblity is restricted to not attacking. Definitely one of the worst options.

    Now, as for Shadowed Soul. This is kind of cool. The normal ability lets you merge with shadows - no form to detect. You can listen through the shadows, and you can teleport to shadows. The problem is that you can't leave the shadows, or the effect ends. You can't do anything other than observe, or the effect ends. That's a very severe restriction. Its very telling that Beast, with spending two Satiety and using every potential stealth ability at their disposal, is arguably worse than a vampire with Obfuscate 1 and a certain devotion. And the vampire only gets better from there. And mages can pretty much do what a vampire can do with some preparation. Phantom Glory from Changeling can turn you into an undetectable ghost or gust of wind, and you can pull others into your state of being, just as vamps and mages can.

    I suppose a better comparison might be against Werewolf, who get an odd selection of abilities scattered between Human form, New Moon Gifts, Evasion Gifts, and Stealth Gifts. In short, Irraka are really good at blending into crowds, people not paying attention or remembering them, and crossing the gauntlet to spy/move in from there. Stealth Gifts are good at being hidden, making sure pack mates keep stealth as well. Evesion lets a wolf stick their nose back into hiding when someone does notice them, both physically and socially. They can ignore alarms and other technology when Beasts can't. Breaking down the From the Shadow abilities again, the Normal effect is like the human form "can't pick me out!" crowd effect. Low Satiey effect is like the Stealth Gift that gives Rote quality. Satiety Expendature is the only area where Beasts can do something that Werewolves can't do just as well - turn invisible. But the werewolf has a host of other stealthy abilities that let them blend in and, more importantly, be unremembered. Beasts don't want to be forgotten- they want to stand out boldly and inflict nightmares on people, because that feeds their Horror. Spying from the Shadow and from Shadowed Form seem roughly equivalent to me. Beasts can't make technology ignore them like a wolf can. Beasts can't bring others with them. So, the question becomes - who's better, wolf or beast, at hiding? Depends on how much you value pure invisibility.

    Crafting
    Now, this is pretty simple. Neither Vampire nor Beast have magic items of the current 2e gamelines, while everyone else does. Niether have powers that give bonuses to working on mundane items, while everyone else does. Most 1e games did as well. And, even then? Vampire technically does get a few limited "magic items" that they can make through Theban / Cruac rituals, as well as arguably some Ordo stuff. Blessed weapons and gargoyles come immediately to mind, as does mandrake gardens and alchemical solutions. In terms of building mundane equipment, Vampire and Beast are roughly equal. Beast can buff up mental skills, and Vampire can beef up social and physical dice pools easily. Still, because of the magical equipment angle, I must give Vampire the advantage here, leaving BtP the worst when it comes to crafting in the whole of the CofD.

    Combat
    Now, we come to the last category. Combat. There's several things we need to cover before we even talk in any depth about the matter. First, we need to assume that the Beast is going to have Low Satiety. Not only are the majority of combat powers Atavisms, and thus more effective, but High Satiety characters lose their Lair as a bonus to resist magical effects. That leaves them widely open to any sort of power to shut them down. While its possible to use combat Nightmares to good effect, its much more like a D&D wizard - in other words, glass cannon combined with Save or Suck effects. Whomever goes first would win. Everyone has rocket-tag options. Except that most supernaturals aren't glass cannons like High Satiety Beasts.

    Next, lets touch on tactics. Lots of people also have insect-swarm forms, retainer / familar swarms, long ranged gunners, elemental magic control, and more. Combat is highly fluid situation where a wide variety of tactics, builds, styles, and more. There's two I want to focus on - front line warrior and magic user. Why? Because the former is actually rather notable for real life simulated situations - gladiators. There are competitions with both of them that can easily be translated to the CofD. At least for me - I am not shy about using the Midnight Games in my campaign. And the latter, because, well, magic. I'm not using a long range gunner option because 1) its heavily stealth and movement based, of which Beast really doesn't have any powers along that line, and 2) we already covered stealth.

    So, gladiator style. Daeva with Vigor 3, a Rahu with Purity 2, Glory 1, and a Giant with Cyclopean Strength and Limb From Limb (assume starting Satiety 5, and spend to immediately get Low Satiety and activate both Atavisms to fullest). Starting characters. Assume 3 in each relevant stat for simplicity, and Supernatural Resistance of 2.

    The Daeva and Rahu get to deal additional damage from their supernatural powers on top of their weapons / claws. The Daeva deals +3 damage on top of a +2 or +3 lethal weapon, plus riding the wave bonuses, plus spending vitae. The Rahu is dealing +4L between his Gifts and claw. With Lair 2, the Giant is dealing +4 damage with his claws as well, but as agg. The problem? While it seems that the agg dealing Giant is going to win, there's a small problem. Both the Daeva and Rahu are far better grapplers at this point of the game, getting at least +4 benefits. And the Giant needs the agg to bypass the vamp and wolf's innante healing. Meanwhile, the Rahu is getting +Purity dots to his health, meaning that he can soak at least one turn's worth of agg before establishing the grapple. And maintain it. Next turn? Efficent Killer for lots of damage. The Daeva is at more risk from the agg claws. However, with a fireaxe and Vigor, that's a minimum of 7 damage on the first turn with just one success; the daeva also has a better dice pool thanks to Vigor, averaging 3 successes with vitae and willpower, for a total of roughly 9 damage. The beast is only getting automatic successes, meaning that our base dice pool is going to be 3 + 3 - (defense of 6), so we need to spend willpower just to get a roll, and average 1 success. The vamp has enough to put down our giant, but not the giant our Daeva.

    I don't know how mages work, so they get a pass. Changelings? Interestingly, they don't have an option for getting more automatic successes on a hit. Not with the Contracts we see. Just a Strength (and other general dice) boost, armor boosts and Wyrd/2 unarmed attacks. Generally, you're better off with a +3L fire axe. Elegant Protection makes it harder to hit the Changeling, though. Meanwhile, the Giant gets auto successes, but few boosts to actual dice pools. If we're in a situation where we can't hit the changeling because of chance dice rolling, and the changeling can hit the Beast for small damage? Beast doesn't get mundane armor against a Render kith. As well, there's the Cloak of Elements - autodamage from being too close to the 'ling.

    Right now, I want to point out that it seems that everyone is beating the Beast in a gladiator style situation. How can that be? Beasts don't start with good innate abilities for combat. Vampires and werewolves, both supernatural predators, start with some very good abilities - healing, damage reduction, physical boosts. With Changeling, I get to start with a little bit of magical combat ability thanks to my choice of kith. Beasts get no supernatural combat ability outside Atavisms. Where Beasts have an advantage is that they are highly XP efficent. Changelings need to buy up Contract as well as Wyrd dots, vampires pick between several different Disciplines, werewolves raise up five Renown scores. Beasts just raise up Lair. Eventually, they will surpass a combat wolf or vamp because of this.

    Now, for magical strength. There's a couple of Nightmares that can be rather strong, but again, we're talking about being rather defenseless in the meantime. But where Beasts actually shine? Elemental magic. Between the immunities from the Lairs, actually calling down their Lairs to cause environmental damaging effects, and some Atavisms like Storm Lashed, there's some potent abilities that come on command. Vampires really kind of suck at this part, no pun intended. Werewolves have a few elemental Gifts, but I don't believe they have protection against the elements. Certainly not against lightning or ice. Changelings are just as competative as the Beasts, though they can't do city-wide storms. Mages can do spells, but that requires time and preparation, when Beasts do it naturally. Mass scale elemental powers and immunities is where Beasts really do excel at. But, then again? Massive scale effects generally are a Bad Idea. You don't want to raise a bunch of Heroes by being too flashy and scary. In a world that wants you to be hidden, flashy isn't the best option. YMMV, but I consider it a mark against them. Another mark is that, while they're good at elemental magic for combat, its less useful for beneficial uses of that same magic.


    Satiety Issues
    When making these comparisons, I assumed that a Beast can always use their Satiety effects. That generally doesn't happen though, at least not in my experience. Satiety expendature is when crap hits the fan. Vampires can casually spend vitae, because its easy to replenish and plentiful. If they lose all of it, they just need to eat. Their abilities are stable throughout their entire track. Beasts rely on either being high Satiety (and thus fragile), or Low (and thus around Satiety 4, limiting their powerful abilities). Like, take the gladiator system. A vampire can easily replenish their vitae and fight on. Same with a werewolf and esssence and a changelign with their glamour. A Beast needs to devote an entire scene to feeding. And that brings them up to 4 or so. On a bad roll, less. Most vampire powers and werewolf Gifts I mentioned don't even require fuel, and they're a match for a notable number of Satiety effects.


    Where Beasts Shine
    Its not all doom and gloom. Beasts, because of how Lair and Satiety are the only two big things you need to worry about, are extremely XP efficent. That means that, while other supernaturals rely on pack and society, each Beast is a walking jack-of-all-trades. They're good at a wide variety of subjects and abilities. This makes them extremely flexible. How else would they jump from a vampire party, to help a mage chase their Obsession, to hanging out with a werewolf on the Hunt? Beasts don't specialize as well as other splats do. Or, at least not when it comes to the above areas of the game I mentioned.

    Beasts also have a tendacy to collect Immunities like candy. Environmental Tilts, personal combat Tilts, etc.

    Beast: the Primordial isn't a Social, Physical, or Mental game. Its not a game of Second Chances, about Madness, or about Stolen Lives. Its a game about dabbling in all of that. Jack of All Trades, Master of None. You are never going to be a better socialite than an Invictus Ventrue, a better hunter than a werewolf, or a better investigator than a mage. But you can hold your own, and if being a workable Mario is overpowered? That's one thing. But, given that what prompted this little rant was people saying that Beasts outshone other supernaturals in the field that supernatural is supposed to specialize in? I have no reason to believe that, and every reason to disagree.

    High Lair Beasts are still scary, because you are never sure what their weakness is. If any - Anathema are unpredictable without a Hero. And the Lair means that there's a good chance that the Beast has access to whatever weakness you have. There's a good chance of a wide variety of defenses, and they can hurt striking back in a wide variety of measures. But, unlike any other supernatural, you can't nail down what their flaws are. Where you can exploit them. And that's the scary part. Well, that, and the fact that they come built in with lots of fear powers. But there are sharp limits to what they can know and do. And those restrictions are what make me feel that Beasts aren't even close to overpowered.
