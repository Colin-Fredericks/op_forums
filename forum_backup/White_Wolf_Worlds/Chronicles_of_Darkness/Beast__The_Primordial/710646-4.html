                                                    I'm about halfway through, and I'm noticing a lot of repetition. The same ideas, sentences, and phrases keep turning up over and over, sometimes even on the same page. If that's deliberate, that's one thing - but that's also something one can fall into by accident.

                                                    For example, one of the more subtle examples involves the nightmare imagery. The giant, the tentacles pulling the subject into the water, the pursuit of a giant aerial predator . . . from the introduction to the back cover, those three keep coming up, over and over again.

                                                    Now, as they're very iconic nightmare imagery, and are featured prominently in the Kickstarter pitch, I understand using a fair amount. But the three together, and neglecting other nightmare icons . . .

                                                    p. 251
                                                    True, the edges have been nibbled away by strip centers and suburbs, . . .
                                                    Maybe this is a regional dialect issue, but "strip centers"? I'm familiar with "strip malls", but have not encountered the phrase "strip center" before.

                                                    Oh, and the whole "Darkness in the Trees" section is another example of (possible accidental) repetition. Is it really necessary to keep pointing out the pine trees, every two-three sentences?

                                                    p. 252

                                                    A short drive from a number of major cities, the Pine Barrens could well draw any number of Beasts to poke around its isolate areas.
                                                    Possible typo - shouldn't that be "isolated areas"?

                                                    She knows more about the Pine Barrens than any living creature, including the final resting place of a significant proportion of those who have gone missing in the Pines and lord knows what other tidbits picked up during a decades of riddling and strangling in the World of Darkness.
                                                    Two apparent typos. Isn't "Lord" (in "lord knows") usually capitalized? And "during a decades of riddling" has an extra "a" - since Edna's been there for decades, it should probably read "during decades of riddling".

                                                    One concern, though. Edna's undergone the Merger. How does she have enough mind left to come up with riddles, or swap secrets?

                                                    During the 18th and 19th centuries the Pine Barrens were a major center of American iron production, bog iron deposited by waters bubbling up from the depths of the earth.
                                                    Probably should be a comma after "centuries". And shouldn't "centuries" be capitalized, since its a proper name (a la the Eighteenth Century, etc.)?

                                                    (As a point of interest, World of Darkness: Urban Legends presents a different take on the Jersey Devil, but there’s no reason that book’s version and Edna couldn’t co-exist.)
                                                    Given that its already pretty clear that Edna isn't the Jersey Devil, this line seems . . . redundant. Might want to consider switching it out for a cross-promotional one ("For more secrets of the Pine Barrens, check out Urban Legends today!")
