                            So, I have two big problems with You are Meat. They don’t really have anything to do with balance or clarity of the rules’, and I don’t want to get these confused with the points I have been making about those aspects. Which is why I am posting them here, not on rpg.net, where I have been posting more about the balance side of things.

                            So, we have already been told that the Doylist reason for why You are Meat doesn’t work on Beasts is that it would just outright kill Beasts. Which is an okay reason, I can buy that. But then they the same isn’t true for any other splat!? What? For some reason the Beast book decided for all other splats that stealing the closest equivalent for a soul that they have doesn’t kill them? Prometheans, Sin-Eaters, Deceived/Mummies, probably some others. There are at least several splats that should be just as dead as Beasts (or at least demoted to ST controlled NPCs in the case of Sin-Eaters and Deceived) if they got the closest equivalent to their soul stolen. Take Prometheans for example. The closest equivalent to a soul they have is the fire animating them. You take that away, and you get a pile of stitched together body parts lying on the ground.
                            So sorry, no. Not only doesn’t that make sense, it’s also a statement about the metaphysics of other splats that Beast should not be allowed to make. If You are Meat can handwave away soul stealing killing, say, Prometheans, then it can do the same for Beasts. And if the book wants Beasts to stay immune, then it should accept that it shouldn’t work on some other splats either.

                            And then you get the Watsonian explanation for You are Meat not working on Beasts: “Beasts are immune to the effect, as their “souls” (Horrors) are far too powerful and vicious to be thus affected”. Again, what? The book goes ahead and uses rules text to make Horrors the canon most powerful and vicious ‘soul equivalents in the entire setting?? Well, that one hell of a no-sell right there. No buildup, no textual support, not even the slightest attempt to make this really over the top statement believable to the reader. By now we have a truckload of books for the WoD, we the readers have read about beings and weird shit in the WoD that, by comparison, make even the most horrific descriptions of Horrors that the Beast book has to offer look like fluffy cuddle-kittens meowing to get some attention. Horrors lack any support at all for this statement outside that one sentence in the power's description. And somehow this explanation, just like the Doylist one, tries to make a statement about the metaphysics of other splats in an absolute, 3rd-person rules voice that the Beast book shouldn’t make. That’s just not a statement it should get to make about other splats outside in-character opinion pieces.

                            These two are exactly the kind of stuff that makes people dislike the way Beast treats crossover. Because it likes to use rules text for Beast powers to say stuff about other splats that it has no business ruling on.

                                    Read through the revised Chapter 3 and the edits to Chapters 1 and 2. Have belated thoughts to share.

                                    What I like:
                                    • The change to Expanding The Lair that was already pointed out. It's a neat addition that encourages investigating old horrors and delving into old supernatural history. Really good in that it's a mechanical and flavorful incentive for Beasts to explore the World Of Darkness.
                                    • The tweaks made to the Devouring. Matt's second take on it, in which the event happening on its own is just as common as it being facilited by another Beast, makes the whole thing far more palatable. The text is just noticeably more accepting and inclusive of both perspectives now, and I can appreciate that. The clarified wording of what happens exactly also makes me much less hung up on the matter of another Beast's involvement in the process (because it's essentially giving a push on an inevitable requirement for the moment of revelation).

                                    What I don't like:
                                    • The change of how Atavisms work for the Horror. I think it significantly takes away from its rightful oomph that the Horror on its own doesn't have free access to the Satiety Expenditure effect, and while merged with the Beast atavisms work entirely as normal. Paraphrasing Matt's explanation from the kickstarter comments, the logic for the latter is that you are essentially burdening your Horror with your human body/nature when you merge with it, which works but feels a bit restrictive. The way I would do it is: While merged with the Horror you always have access to Low Satiety effects but must expend Satiety for the last effect; and when it's by itself it just uses every effect for free (working cooldown times applied as appropriate).
                                    • The softening in a few examples of Beasts through Chapters 1, 2 and 3. Some of the text might've benefitted from a less antagonistic or monstrous bent on the portrayal of Beasts, but I think there was more than one case that really didn't needed to be changed.

                                    What I'm not sure about:
                                    • I'm curious about how the workings of Heroes might have changed with the revised fluff, because it appears feeding through dreams now draws Heroes in and attracts the notice of any in the area rather than creating them; on a first read of the revised chapters 1 and 2, I still had the impression Beasts created Heroes, or were implied to. I understand the reasoning behind editing that out, since as it was it probably leaned too much on the abuser/abused angle for some people. And drawing every Hero in the vicinity to them rather than creating one might actually be more dangerous (and more danger is good). We might be missing context on this since Heroes are elaborated on in Chapter 5, so for now I'mma wait on this topic. Slightly concerned about how the apparent removal of "Beasts unwittingly create Heroes" might affect the conflict between them, but I may be worrying for nothing.

                                    (On a side-note, I kinda want to run a side by side comparison of the old and new sterotypes in the Families, because some felt better than others.)

                                            Oh, mechanically I understand it. But flavor-wise it feels off. I'm not as bothered by Atavisms working normally when you merge compared to the Horror on its own lacking the unrestrained access to the entirety of its power.

                                            Should a thundering, looming roc in its stormy mountain perch that is in fact a hungering nightmare one cannot overtake not be able to fly without concern in that dream realm? Should a shapeless, amorphous stalker of tearing darkness not be able to meld and travel through the shadows at the blink of an eye? Should a titan of stone armor not be in truth as tough and impervious as it has every right to be, an unstoppable force of nature chasing its victims?

                                            My concern is not really a mechanical one. It's a narrative one. It's that the Horror feels offputtingly smaller than it should by all rights be in the revised framework.

                                            (When you consider Lair Traits, this shrinking of the Horror also doesn't really do much for the "Heroes need to be a threat" argument, since fighting in the Lair on your own continues to be at best very difficult and exceedingly impractical, and at worst suicidal. If the idea there was balancing the danger Heroes pose by giving them more of a chance inside the Lair, there are better ways to do it. A Gift that grants resistance to Lair Traits. A Gift that allows them to collapse Chambers much faster. A Gift that grants bonuses to the Hero's every action when facing the heart of fear itself. Some inherent characteristic or mechanic that gives Heroes an advantage when invading the Lair.)

                                                    Well, we've been waiting on it for a while. The chapter on Heroes is now in the Drive folder and we are free to sate our hunger!

                                                    EDIT: Wow, we knew it was coming, but big changes none the less to the chapter right off the bat in terms of tone. This is a good very early on:

                                                    Originally posted by Kickstarter
                                                    The Good Hero

                                                    What, then, about the Hero that listens to reason? What about the Hero that chooses to follow her better angels, as it were, trying to soothe the Primordial Dream by providing therapy to troubled individuals, by hunting down violent or destructive supernatural creatures, or by taking on an advisory role such as clergy? Surely these Heroes exist? They do, but Beasts don’t tend to cross paths with them, because they resist the urge to hunt down the Children, or because, for whatever reason, they never find any of the Children. In game terms, the Heroes that stalk Beasts tend to have low Integrity ratings — their souls are weak and ill-defined. Being a Hero is, like being a Beast, something that a person is born with. Also like being a Beast, being a Hero is what one makes of it. The Hero’s narrative role in a Beast chronicle is discussed in more detailed in Chapter Seven. Heroes discussed in this chapter, however, are the Heroes that react with violence and hatred to the Children. Other Heroes exist, yes, but we’re not talking about them right now.
                                                    I like that a lot. The negative sanity implications of Heroes are removed, there is now a lot more design space for Heroes, and you still get to keep your obsessive style Hero. Tough to please everyone, but that's about as close as I can see anyone coming for this particular antagonist.

                                                    EDIT 2: I skipped ahead for another reason, but I just checked out Melanie's entry. The same basic idea is there, but its been greatly reworked and all of the uncertain implications have been removed. I actually like it much, much better now. Melanie is much more unique and becomes that much more of living plothook. I'd be very interested to see a campaign were a group of Beasts actually help her and she becomes one of the "wise" Heroes instead of the obsessive ones.
