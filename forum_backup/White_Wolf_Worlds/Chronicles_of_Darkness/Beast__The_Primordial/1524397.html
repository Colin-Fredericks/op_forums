    Hunters Pass, Beasts Fail?

    10-22-2023, 04:32 PM
    So there I was, running late with my day in general, trying to get things I needed done today, when all of a sudden a thought intruded and demanded I give attention, so I'm doing that. Let's start by elaborating on that provocative title!

    There's been an argument, not as old as others for Beast, but kind of old, in that part of what makes Beast: the Primordial a turn off for people is that their Satiation of Hunger has no fantastic filter-the actions that a Beast commits to are all real world shocks and horrors, from throwing a brick through a window or beating someone up to destroying a person's home or ripping out a hamstring or two.

    Now this can be a understandable issue-a lot of that can be hard to think about, nevermind viscerally describing or hearing it described to you-but we're in a horror game, where other monsters can and do rip the limbs off of people and beat them up with it, manipulate and gaslight others into doing and thinking what they want them to think and do, etc., etc. But to be fair on that front, and going into the complaint, some of that exists in the realm of cartoonish excess or fantastical implementation. While I think it's delusional to imagine players characters never just go "No, that didn't happen it, you imagined it" in addition to their emotion manipulating powers or what not, you can conceivably argue that all of the horrific action of the game can feasibly be only performed through the side of the supernatural angle.

    But then you get to Hunter.

    Hunter, as a gameline, is predominantly not a game where the violence of it's forms is expressed through a supernatural veneer. Sure, the Conspiracies have their Endowments, and nothing says that hunters can't also have supernatural merits, but the game primarily markets itself where you deal with someone you don't approve of by running their face through a chainsaw blade. There is a lot of sense of the mundanity in the physicality and psychology being used to harm, manipulate, denigrate, and otherwise impose unwanted conditions upon other people. If your average hunter wants to make someone do something they don't want to do, they probably do it through lies, deception, using a person's perception and memory against them, and even drug and/or other substance abuse.

    THe immediate counter is that in these cases, the scenario is flipped-the actions may be real world transgressions, but the victims are fantastical entities, and are often the bad guys-but that falls short in a couple ways for me. Humans often find ways to be cast as antagonists, even villains, in other games readily enough (hell, Hunter regularly fills in for those roles alone, but as a quick rundown of other options, Forsaken has humans as Prey and have things like RD-13 and Lupus Et Fidelis in their portfolio, Prometheans regularly have humanity as opponents even when they aren't alchemists, demiurges, or clone-scientists, changelings have every family and friend member who gets onto the fact that there is an alien trying to pass among them, Sin-eaters often have humans intruding on their quest to resolve their Burdens even without necromancers and Eaters of the Dead, the Arisen have Last Dynasty International, Demons have the Deva Corporation, and Deviant's main opponents are Baselines most of the time, and even the Awakened can find the structures of humanity and the people who support, engineer and manipulate them as being in the way of their own work), and just as often the humans you get cast against are victims you probably don't have context on as much as they are monsters in their own right. For every Loyalist trying to trick a mortal into the Hedge there is, there's a Lost desperately trying to get someone important to them to finally get what's going on-and hunters don't have a privilege to see a difference between the two just because we as players know the difference.

    The only other major difference I can see at the moment is that Hunter's are, directly or indirectly, working to such an end that is ostensibly altruistic-reclaim the night for humanity, however that's interpreted-where as Beast's are all self-interested and self-involved, but that equally ignores both that hunters often chase that altruistic end, of saying No More to monstrous predation and exploitation, because they are chasing an emotional fix to having been hurt or violated, that their motives can be very selfish and the good they are doing is a perk or cover up for it, and likewise that a Beast can bend their Hungers towards ends with an eye for improving their community and the lives in it. They can go at it from different directions, but it doesn't take much to see how both avarice and altruism can work in either of the splats in regards to what they do, nevermind the ways they can commit to both with it.

    Sooooooooooooooooooooooooooo

    Square the circle-how is a Hunter's Vigil meaningfully different from a Beast's Hunger in terms of the violence it commits?

      Hunters aren't obligate predators; Beasts are. When a Hunter resorts to violence, it's a deliberate decision, and usually a response to violence from a monster. When a Beast turns violent, it's because he needs to fill his Satiety meter.

      The compacts and conspiracies who don't hunt monsters because they're threats - Ashwood Abbey, who do it for fun; the Cheiron Group, who do it for money - get the same disgusted response as Beasts. The thought "I do this only because my targets deserve to suffer" is apparently necessary, when the supernatural veneer is absent.

                      Ultimately, I don't think the "no fantasy filter" issue is rational, so expecting it to apply evenly is going to work even with people considering things with maximal charity and good faith intention.

                      There are some people that don't mind clowns. There are other people that hate clowns and all clowns are a hard nope for them. But there are also people where they need to take each character on a case-by-case basis because what exactly makes them dislike clowns is too variable to come up with a simple solution (and some of them will avoid clowns because the time and energy of separating out the ones they can enjoy and the creep-inducing ones isn't worth the effort). While people might be able to articulate specific reasons clowns creep them out; it's ultimately an emotional response that can't be addressed by pointing to how androids usually get a pass (unless the creators are purposefully pushing the Uncanny Valley as hard as possible).

                      While I find the... uncritical... analysis of Beast endless frustrating, the illogical application of the fantasy-filter is something I can sympathize with because it isn't about addressing a purely logical issue. If someone wants to sit down and really dig into why they kinda let a bunch of other game-lines do stuff, but hold Beast to a higher standard? That's a conversation I think is worth engaging in, but I think it's ultimately a personal one. Getting into an Internet debate on how people give the Joker the Clown Prince of Crime a pass as a watchable clown but not Art the Clown isn't really going to be productive.

                      To me the end result was predictably exactly what we're already seeing as some people are going to continue rationalizing not liking Beast as logical instead of preferential by shifting to a different axis of comparison. When a pod of orcas (and that would apply to Uratha if you strip away the fantasy filter when you remember what things like Hunter Aspects do) rather brutally harass prey, we accept that obligate carnivores have to hunt and eat, and accept that obligate predators are going to do things to optimize their survival strategies rather than "hunt fair." Humans, who aren't obligate predators, performing many of the same hunting tactics we observe in orcas (even on prey animals, not even other humans) would be branded as cruel and inhumane precisely because that's a choice. But people are arguing that Beasts are bad because they're obligate predators instead of ones by choices; like hunters are. It doesn't really strike me as trying to defend a logical position, it strikes me as trying (poorly) to justify something that doesn't need justifications beyond preference.
