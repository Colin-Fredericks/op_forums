        Also, I want to touch on something that keeps bugging me (especially since I just gave a lecture on this topic): Victimization is not Justification. Specifically, citing that the Heroes have a right to kill beasts as they are victims or that the Beasts are harmful is not okay.

        Being a victim is a seperate and isolated experience from the means of coping and acts that follow after. Even if the acts are directly linked to the act, if unhealthy or inappropriate they should not be tolerated. At the same time, you have to recognize that the event was traumatic and that the person is a victim.

        For example, I had a an adolescent patient who attempted to kill his father, who had been physically and verbally abused by his father since he was 3. His mother had also suffered abuse and had been afraid to come forward or protect her son because of fear of reprisal. This father was a horrible person, had a criminal and substance abuse history, and is as far from a sympathetic. Yet the son's response is still not the appropriate response, no matter what. As much as the Dad might seemed to have deserved it, the response was not an effective coping mechanism, nor would it be beneficial in the long run. What's more, it was the cusp of other violent tendencies that the son had begun to manifest as his outlet for frustrations, especially towards autority figures, and he likely would have gone in to hurt others too.

        With him, the whole thing was seperating his response from him being a victim. Which is hard. You felt for this kid. But while I can sympathize, and did acknowledge his suffering, I also had to rebuild his world view to not being defined by his anger and fear of his father.

        And there are myriad other examples of this. One of the biggest pedictors violent and predatory behavior in adults was being victimized as a child or adolescent. This is due to the fact that their identities are still forming and thus they are susceptible to having such acts lead to inappropriate development, especially if they had co-morbid conditions that make then more susceptible to having poor coping mechanisms Even adults have these breaks, though it is less common as their identities and coping mechanisms are more fixed and defined. But in all cases, you do not allow them to justify their actions through their nature as victims, as it validates that their response is appropriate. You acknowledge their suffering and the evils of what was done to them, but then must make it clear that said events do not make their current actions or thoughts okay.

        This is the nature of the heroes. They are victims of the Beasts. As much as I like the idea of them being puppets in the Mythic Arc that surrounds the Beasts (just as the Beast is) they are still victims even in that scenario, as they susceptible humans who were cast in a role after having their soul attacked by a Beast's Soul. To pretend that the heroes aren't victims or deserved it is wrong, as it is ignoring the fact that their soul was ravaged, likely more then once. But one also needs to acknowledge their coping mechanism and response is excessive and unhealthy, especially since their quest doesn't end with the death of their tormentor, and will usually leas them to their own destruction. Their delusions and single minded focus on their hatred of the monsters can be explained by the Beasts attack on them, and it is unfortunate that it happened, but these thoughts and the resulting actions are not healthy nor good things to have.

        To parrallel it with an extreme example, a hero is akin to a rape victim who has decided to kill off all men to prevent anyone ever from being raped again*. Rape is never ever okay, the perpetrators are (in my mind) some of the worst people on earth, but the single minded and excessive response is not okay either. To target not only the perpetrator but all who are just associated with the ability to be such perpetrators as needing to die is not a healthy nor justified response.

        TL;DNR (like half the Medical Students in my lecture): Beasts can be violators. Heroes are the victims of those Beasts who are. This part is tragic. The heroes actions after , however, are not justified nor healthy.

        *note, this isn't hyperbole: I had a colleague who was called in to treat a girl who tried to rundown several boys crossing the street who cited this as her reasoning to do so.
