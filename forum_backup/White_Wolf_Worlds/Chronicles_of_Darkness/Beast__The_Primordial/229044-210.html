    Also, there should be non-Hero mortals who fight beasts, right? But with better judgments and without shiny Hero-powers?

    I think it would be good if the devs added even a paragraph about non-Hero mortal antagonists, because at the moment the definition of Hero is a little bit narrow and strawman-ish, and those eventually lead them to being unimpressive antagonists.

    Like... if we take Beast/Mortal mirror narrative, Hunters would be at the narrow end of the spectrum opposite of which lies the meanest, the most brutal beasts lie, and I don't think that's a good way to handle the major antagonists for a line. I mean, sure, people like Banishers and VII are like that, but these are only (kind of more flat) part of many antagonists that the lines provide.


    I don't know... If I were a dev, I could have made things like... having one single, special, designated Hero against each Beast, who grows in power and magnitude with the Beast, whose quest is pursuing that particular PC beast if it meant his life. Then give the PC to subvert or deconstruct the heroic epic, either by defeatinging him, 'rehabilitating' him, befriending him, or even ignoring him. (i.e., anything but letting him kill you, but a good story could even be made out of that.) I think it might be better solution in the lategame than making heroes 'mere footnotes.' (actual words)

    It... could give Beasts things to do, as well? I don't know. I think as much as the story of Beowulf IS about Grendel, the subversion of the story of Grendel should not just be.. Grendel killing heroes and hanging out with other monsters. If the mechanics are given to Heroes, I think they deserve better narrative.
