        In looking at the original and the new intro, I've got a couple thoughts:

        1. The fiction that precedes the intro now makes very little sense due to the Embracey details of turning into a Beast.
        2. Most of the overall Family motif in the book doesn't work very well if being a Beast isn't an inherent condition IMO. (and the Big Brother/Sister thing is somewhere between cheesy and creepy IMO)
        3. I like the general idea of needing/wanting to behave as a corrective mechanism, but I'm uncertain this is the best way to phrase things.

        For my own games I'm probably going to treat them a bit like a mixture of C:tD and the Devil-Tiger Dharma from Kindred of the East. (The following is offered as both a suggestion to other Storytellers and potentially to Onyx Path as well. Feel free to use all or part of this however you like, I'm interested in improving the game, not fighting over IP rights.)

        A Beast is what they've always been, rather than something they are made. While some go through a Homecoming on their own, others are catalyzed similarly to a Hero when one of their cousins bumps up against their dreams and the recognition of kinship with the horror they see wakes them with a revelation of their true selves.

        The Hungers then, are half satisfying the monster you are beneath the skin and half about karma and forced perspective for the wicked. You are not merely a monster born from the collective depths of the most basic of human fears. A cursory glance might make it appear that way, but it is not so simple. More than simple fear is mixed into the Primordial Dream, there are other primal drives as well.

        For every person who has screamed in the grasp of a giant or been dragged beneath inky waves in their dreams, there is another who has toiled thanklessly under the lash of a pitiless master or been cast adrift by treachery or felt irreparably soiled by the touch of something repulsive lurking in a fairer guise. Someone who dreams of vengeance. Whose fantasies, waking or asleep, are filled with the terror of those who have wronged them. Their minds craft a champion of sorts, a monster who Hungers not for the powerless, but for the flesh and fear of the lesser monsters who have preyed on the weak.

        The Beasts are driven by their Hunger, an endless gnawing need that must be fed. When they are starving, even the smallest wrong is enough to satisfy. A small flicker of greed tugs at their mind, guiding them to the meager wealth of a man whose only crime was ignoring a panhandler that morning or blasting someone with the Nightmares of swimming through a sea of salty glass for having shouted at their children that morning. When fat on the pain of the deserving, only the choicest morsels will do... blood diamonds stolen from the private vaults of a remorseless magnate, stalking and tormenting a wealthy serial rapist until their terror forces a confession even their overpaid lawyers can't get them out of.

        Not all Beasts agree on what punishment fits a crime, not all agree on what even constitutes a crime. Most don't even consciously view their Hunger in the light crime and punishment anymore than the average person analyzes the exact reason they like oranges more than grapes, they simply prefer a certain flavor of fear and know how to get it. The billion scattered fears and hopes of humanity are hardly a unanimous chorus, and each Beast feels their Primal urges through the lens of their life before the Homecoming. Some are even so broken themselves, or formed from such distorted imaginings, that they Hunger simply for blood and shrieks, caring little for the lesson it might teach or the example to the rest of the herd.


        Heroes are their cousins in a sense, not born, but called into being when the currents of the Primordial Dream draw too near and break into their souls like a sudden unexpected wave. A Hero forms when a mind that cries out for vengeance, for agency, for the power to matter again is exposed to the wake of a monster who hungers for the wrongs the Hero suffered. In their scattered dreaming the Hero sees not an ally or an answer to their wish for justice, but a wrong given flesh. A young man sees in a sudden shock of what he mistakes for clarity, not the desire to batter and rend and despoil the uncle who molested him, but the embodiment of his pain. Not just an abuser but Abuse itself, a slithering hateful thing that sullies the world with its every breath. Helpless, hopeless, for too long the Hero knows what must be done. What THEY must do, because no other will see the thing for what it is until he has torn out it's heart to mend his own. Their connection to the Primordial allows them to strike out and reshape a vulnerable Beast to better match their personal narrative, rather than it's own. Their need guides them toward the weakness the Beast must have, as surely all foul things do. Nothing so vile could be perfect, could it? Anathema exist because if they did not, there would be little hope.

        Pain is a private thing, and so Heroes rarely work well together, but they frequently have the power to convince and gather others who have suffered similar fates to their own. An alcoholic might convince his AA group that perhaps there ought to be a new first step in their program. A recently jobless steel worker who has recently seen Greed far too close at hand speaks to his friends on their way to the unemployment lines. The homeless see Exposure, or Indifference, or Poverty and draw together the other forgotten to take back a chance at their lives.

        The threat of a Hero is a serious one, because humanity has always found it's strength in numbers and in certainty. Alone their powers are potentially dangerous but of little use against an entire Warren. With the aid of others though... weapons are easy to come by in the modern World of Darkness, and when they cannot be purchased or stolen, fire and stones have served mankind well since they first left the trees. A Beast may have the power to scour them with flaming breath or crush their minds in Nightmares, they find it sits uneasily with them at best.

        Whatever their true family, Beasts lived as humans once, and still live among them. Better by far to duel the Hero and what followers they may have found, to battle them closely and carefully, striving not to slay the would-be slayer, but to guide them back to sanity. To remind them that no matter how much blood they spill, there is no simple answer, no single foe to slay that will quiet the call of the bottle or the memories that make their skin crawl at the touch of a lover. Some are too far gone to hear reason, some simply develop a stronger grudge against the nemesis that will not obligingly die before their righteous assault... but others can be calmed and saved from a life of obsession and bloodshed.

        (Possible rules sidebar here: Only by defeating and convincing the Hero of their error can a Beast remove an Anathema placed on them. Killing the one who saddled them with the new weakness will make it a permanent part of the Beast, cementing the role of the defeated Hero in their legend as someone who once wounded them, paving the way for a later champion to succeed.)

            Re: the above, I'm planning to frame the God-Machine and it's minions as something of an opposing force to the Beasts. It seems to me like the God-Machine has a vested interest in keeping the bulk of humanity toiling along on the fumes of hope and imagination it allows, conforming to it's grand design (whatever it is) but never stepping out of their place. It relies on the abusers and despoilers of the world to function as part of it's Infrastructure, a guiding mechanism to ensure the rest stays in line. Shaking that pattern isn't good for its plans, nor is something whose very existence is meant to rattle the bars of the cage it is making.

            Beasts and their cousins are bursts of color and strangeness in the grey oatmeal mundanity that is meant to drown the world, and they cannot be tolerated.
