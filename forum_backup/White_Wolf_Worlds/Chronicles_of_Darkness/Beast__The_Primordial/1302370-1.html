                Originally posted by Konradleijon View Post
                Ugh I don’t think Pain and fear is a Sustainable teaching method it just breaks people. And stops them from experimenting in Life.
                As someone who's gone through that sort of teaching mechanism a few times, I'd like to provide you with some insight:

                It works. You wake up before the established time every morning because you know it's coming. You have a bed ready for inspection. Your heart rate goes up when the music plays because the pain and fear and confusion are coming. The fear and pain lessen with time, in part because you adapt to avoid it and in part because you grow acclimated. You see the instructor and you leap to the proper courtesy. And they lighten up. As long as you do the things you must do, they relax the punishment.

                It does break you down, but it also makes you rebuild yourself. A new you in a new paradigm. And you know the pain and fear isn't forever. For you it will pass in weeks or months. You may experience again briefly once in a while, but you've evolved. You can take the pain and set it aside. You can handle the fear and push it down. The training set you up to take the stress of normalcy and then some.

                And then it happens to someone else. Over and over again people *volunteer* to go through it. We've used it for centuries. You say it isn't sustainable, but it is. We've changed it, fine tuned how far we can push, but it's still there. Inducing stress to make trainees more resilient.

                Heck even on a small scale it works. My daughter is five and cut her finger on an open can once. It's been two years and she still is very careful with sharp objects now. Because the pain of the cut on her finger and the fear on seeing that rush of red?

                Tldr: Yeah. Pain and fear is sustainable. It's just a matter of how you apply pain, fear, and shock and the frequency of application.

                    Originally posted by Konradleijon View Post
                    Ugh I don’t think Pain and fear is a Sustainable teaching method it just breaks people. And stops them from experimenting in Life.
                    I mean, regardless of whether it is or not, the original question still stands for Beasts. They can just eat, but a Beast basically has to be okay with eating anyone and everyone regardless of who or what they are or what they are going through and how they can or can't afford your Satiation. Because the instant a Beast put in any sort of logic, any sort of reason, any sort of justification to chewing on people, they've created a Lesson-and most of them are going to be really bad ones done that way. "I ate you for your [Hunger] because of X" invariably happens.

                    Take, for instance, death to the bourgeoisie. I think you're pretty comfortable with a Beast Sating themselves on them, in which okay, but why? If you only feed on them isn't that, in itself, a message? Isn't that, in fact, a lesson you're trying to teach? Death to the Bourgeoisie, for whatever reason you deem that correct? At a minimum, because people need to understand you'll do what you want to these people, fuck off?

                    And I'm not even saying that's wrong, as specific examples go, but it is also what it is-a biased feeding that is a Beast using what they were going to do anyway to send a message to everyone else. And that's if you're lucky and manage to keep yourself only eating the bourgeoisie. Eat a member of the proletariot while sending that message, and you got some 'splaining to do, Lucy. And, if you don't have plans for meals, the likelihood of you eating someone you don't want to increases, and the more you've got to ask yourself what you're doing and why.

                    Lessons help to keep your Hunger tamed to your will. It helps to keep you on schedule and having regularly planned meals. It acknowledges that feeding is already an act of messaging and that what you say will eventually come back around to you. Feeding changes the world, and that includes changing yourself, as you discovered when you ascended by Devouring yourself. If you want to own your Legend, own your Life, own your Myth, You Have To Control What The Story Is. That's what Beast is about, and Lessons are just a tool in the box that further facilitates that.

                    Besides, there are positives. A lot of people have thoughts about Power, Hoard, Prey, Punishment, Ruin, Secrets, Transgressions that keep them from being better people, and it is possible to be gentle enough with a well taught feeding that the things they think they want are getting in the way of what they need, that the way they think is getting in the way they could be. A Beast with an eye on these things could do a lot to make a whole host of better people. If you don't believe me, consider how many people have learned to be better human beings to one another through horror, through things like The Twilight Zone or the darker episodes of Mushi-shi, xxxHolic and the like.

                    But at the end of the day, while it can be about the ones feasted upon (And I do hope that there are always Beasts characters who attempt the art of compassionate guidance through feeding), really it comes back to the Beast owning themselves, rather than letting anyone or anything dictate who they are and what they mean.

                            To add a bit to Arc's post, something that probably doesn't get enough highlighting in the books, but something my experience has seen a few times:

                            The Lesson isn't always for the Beast's direct victim. One of the more interesting ways I've seen a balance struck between Lessons and more vicious in feeding Beasts, is who they're trying to help. It's one thing to be a Beast that feeds viciously on horrible people and assuages themselves that it's OK because of who their victims were. It's another to go beyond that and use the death of an abuser to insert yourself into your victims' victims lives and help them come out stronger. It's not fantastic, it's manipulative and creepy, but it's at least trying to pull a net good out of it.

                            While not a new take or anything, consider how much better a place like Gotham would be if Bruce Wayne put more of his resources into fighting poverty, homelessness, and food insecurity, than into punching criminals as Batman. Not that he needs to stop being Batman and getting his emotional need for revenge out on whatever criminals need punching tonight, but actually put his vast resources to fighting crime instead of fighting criminals.

                            It feels like a place that's narratively ripe for Beasts to find compromise even if their normal feeding strategies aren't great for more compassionate lessons.
