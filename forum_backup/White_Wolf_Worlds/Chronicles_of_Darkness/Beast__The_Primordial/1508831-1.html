                                    Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
                                    So, if it’s based on Density, would the Beast’s discomfort reasonably not be applicable to dreams? I think Changeling says that dreams are mostly Prop, with a few Actors. Seems less likely to make Beast feel claustrophobic.
                                    In the Temenos, the Realms are made up of the dialogue of shared narratives. "People" are paying attention, but only to the extent that it's more risky/nerve-wracking to do stuff in front of an audience.

                                    The Oneiros is a place of self-reflection. Everything interacting with a visitor is doing so under the assumption that they're the person whose thoughts created them if they engage from the tableau at all. You're being scrutinized because the Oneiros is where one goes to scrutinize themselves.

                                    Regular dreams cycle back around to not being as intense by default — things can be unimportant in oneiromancy based on whether they represent something from the dreamer's mind or are just there for set dressing — but needing to play a role to accomplish anything in oneiromancy is a pseudo-social thing; you have to steer the scene on its own terms, and that means presenting as something that aligns with what's there already. You have to pose as a visiting archaeologist or a private investigator or a cat burglar to convince the dream to give you information and, without a paradigm shift, you won't get further results by acting outside of the role you've taken up, because the dream won't play along any more than your sports team would if you started acting like a producer for The Bachelor in the middle of a big game — that's not the story being told by you and the dream at the moment, and the sudden twist hasn't been narratively earned.

                                    The Primordial Dream is thin. The one consistent unseen observer is (probably) the Dark Mother, in contrast to the press of humanity in the Temenos or the mirrored fixation of the Oneiros; those bits of the Dark Dream that aren't the dreamscape itself carry bits of how the Bright Dream works into the prosaic landscape of the Astral view of the material world as divorced from the directed attention of the world itself and humanity as a whole, with Burrows working the way connections between Bright Realms work, Lairs evoking memories of events that the present has tried to move on from.

                                    Dreams aren't the inside of the soul. There's no density to worry about there, because you can't walk out of the inside of the soul and getting to regular dreams takes a separate route where intrinsic degrees of scrutiny aren't a metaphysical concern.

                                            Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
                                            Ok, so a dream has a narrative. And to mess with it, one has to play along. So what exactly happens when a Horror Pathways inside? I’m guessing it doesn’t have to play along with the existing narrative, it can rampage all it wants or otherwise do it’s thing. Is it doing that by changing what going to happen the the narrative? Or is it overriding the narrative? I guess a better way to phrase it would be: does the Horror technically have a role, when it engages with the dream? If so, a Beast would likely have a similar role if they try to Pathway in. If not, then the Beast is free to engage with the dream on their own terms.
                                            Assuming that Inflicting Nightmares works along the same narrative lines as a larval Horror with streamlined mechanics, the Horror opens a Primordial Pathway in response to dreamed situations that will allow it to feed, probably including the presence of Lair Traits in the scene as well, which means that the narrative of the dream had room for "a monster arrives and asserts dominance/punishes a transgression/steals something/makes you aware that one of your secrets is out/etc," which the Horror will likely progress the story towards at its preferred pace.

                                            It's kind of a moot point whether or not a Beast or their Horror "has" a role in a dream, because oneiromancy is not an ability any character who is in a dream is assumed to have — per the Player's Guide to the Contagion Chronicle, a changeling and a Horror in the same dream use their respective systems for interacting with the dream — and "playing a role" is Changeling's non-dice-based prerequisite action of "getting stuff out of dreams with oneiromancy requires some give-and-take," akin to how Astral Realms (pages 40-45) talks about Dream Actors and Affecting Scenes:

                                            When the dreamer acts out of character, it confuses dream actors. They’re part of a memory where the dreamer is nine and carefree and don’t understand when he says, “I can’t stay here. I’ve gotta find the night Jenny died!” Such events slide from dream actors’ consciousnesses with little impact — Cousin Jack pauses and might ask who Jenny is, then goes back to talking about getting the Frisbee.​
                                            A person can affect an Oneiros not her own, if she is clever. The right word in the dreamer’s ear, a recognized smell or a certain sound can all bring certain memories to the fore, just as manipulation of the dreamer’s psyche can dull old mental wounds and make the unconscious gentler. When able to influence the dreamer, either because he is astral and right beside her or because she has accomplices in real space, the character attempting to shift the subconscious needs an exceptional success on a Manipulation + Persuasion, Intimidation or Expression roll.
                                            The narrative in a dream does not have a "going to happen" to change except in the sense that some dreams involve revisiting events that have already happened — the narrative a role exists in is based on established "facts" about the setting of the dream. (You're at a birthday party. You've presented yourself as a police officer. Acting like the birthday clown at this point is just going to confuse people the same as it would if you tried it in real life.) Some actors native to the dream will have supernatural powers enforcing their role in the narrative, but oneiropomps and other visitors are definitionally not those actors — Changeling 2e has playing roles and the nature of truth as a social construct as recurring parts of its metaphysical activity.

                                            The need to play a role in oneiromancy is a Watsonian and Doylist justification for why you can't just make the dream do what you want as an abstract exercise, not a script strictly straitjacketing your activity, and a Horror having an impact on the dream by definition has established its role for that purpose, which it does through the systems that Beast uses for interacting with dreams, i.e. normal dice rolls, because Beasts and their Horrors do not use oneiromancy as mechanically presented in Changeling as a tool for changelings, Fae, Huntsmen, and certain fae-touched.

                                                    Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
                                                    Given that Shift Conditions require a paradigm shift, which requires oneiromancy, is it true that Beasts cannot acquire them, no matter how far they warp the dream they are in? Aka, if a Beast decides to say, kill all of the eidolon/dream actors in order to feed, they won't gain a Shift Condition.
                                                    That is my understanding, yes.

                                                    Operating from the Bastion itself rather than the inside of the soul means their actions would still be penalized by the dreamer's Composure despite their lack of ability to spend Glamour to effect shifts, but the extent to which the dream will play along is limited to the ways it "thinks" the situation would respond to those actions, rather than your choice of effects from the table.

                                                    Functionally this would be the dreamweaving equivalent of trying to get results in the Hedge solely through prompting it to Hedgespin itself in reaction to what you're doing; Shift Conditions represent you drawing unnatural attention to yourself, as the dream recognizes you're trying to change or extract something below the surface with Glamour and moves to protect the deeper contents of the person.

                                                    I don't believe killing eidolons would allow you to feed any more than you could from smashing a wall in the dreamscape — they're not real people with a persistent existence outside the dream, so "if someone eats, something gets eaten" doesn't really have stakes in that case — and killing a dreamer in their own Bastion likewise doesn't even inflict a Condition.

                                                    Basically, a Beast acting in a Bastion through the channels changelings use to get into dreams doesn't really have any especial means of affecting the dreamer more than showing them a dream where the Beast does whatever they're doing; it doesn't serve as a shortcut for them to do stuff like they would in the Oneiros except maybe in the same sense that opening a Primordial Pathway lets you deliberately target you Horror's feeding.
