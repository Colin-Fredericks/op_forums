    After a one-shot cross-over game, I've concluded:

    -Most Major Templates have some form of offensive and defensive innate powers: Vampires are resilient, can heal, and increase their strength, Werewolves regenerate and shapeshift, etc...
    Beasts instead of those universal abilities, get Atavisms that are like Demons' Form Abilities (Beasts innate ablities are either not useful in a fight of too situational to be mentioned here).
    Except Beasts only get 2 Atavisms at Chargen, while Demons get a truckload of them, although more problematic to access. So, chargen Beasts lag behind most other Supernatural there.

    -Beasts also only get 2 Nightmares, while a Vampire will start with 3 Disciplines, a Demon with 4 Embeds+Exploits, etc...
    We had an Eshmaki with Kinship with a Forsaken. She had Relentless Hunter, Needs Must, Behold, my True Form, and Your Tools Betray You. Mimicking some werewolf innate powers cost her her 2 Atavisms, and she was kinda forced to take those 2 Nightmares specifically, because she needed some offensive capability and a Kinship Nightmare, and didn't have the time to make a custom one. It felt a bit like she had to play a werewolf with a single Gift. The player still had lots of fun, though.

    So in short, Beasts start weak but it's nothing that won't be solved by gaining XP.


    When will you rage?

                        I would, generally speaking, use the following as the "core" to a combat-oriented Beast build.

                        Attributes:
                        Intelligence 2, Wits 3, Resolve 2
                        Strength 2, Dexterity 1, Stamina 5 -OR- Strength 1, Dexterity 3, Stamina 4
                        Presence 2, Manipulation 1, Composure 3

                        Horror Attributes
                        Power 2, Finesse 5, Resistance 5 -OR- Power 2, Finesse 5, Resistance 4

                        Skills:
                        Occult 2, Medicine 1, Athletics 2, Brawl [Grapple] 5, Subterfuge 2, Streetwise 2,
                        Strongly consider Stealth [Ambushing]
                        Consider Firearms if you aren't taking Dragonfire

                        Merits:
                        2 Iron Skin
                        2 Iron Skin (Advanced)
                        1 Combat Defense [Brawl]

                        From here look at:
                        2 Iron Skin (Epic)
                        2 Fist of Nightmares
                        3 Killer Instinct + 1 Killer Instinct (Advanced)
                        5 Professional Training
                        1 Area of Expertise [Grapple]
                        X More Fighting Styles (Close Quarters Combat, Streetfighting, Relentless Assault, Chokehold,Cheap Shot, and Grappling Style are all solid buys. If you went Strength 1, Dexterity 3 you can get Martial Arts and Defensive Combat Style)

                        What and how much depend a lot on how many Merit Dots you are willing to dedicate to Combat, what your spare Atavism is, and whether or not you start with one less chamber in exchange for +2 EXP (it is generally a good idea to do so). Make sure to talk to your ST before taking Professional Training. Rote on combat actions can very quickly get out of hand. On the other hand, there's an atavism to give Rote at the cost of 1 Saity and Willpower is more valuable to Beasts than other Splats so... ymmv.

                        Atavisms:
                        Unbreakable
                        1 Other of Choice

                        Nightmare:
                        Behold My True Form
                        1 Other of Choice

                        Notes:
                        Attributes can be manipulated a little bit. Wits 3, Stamina 4 or 5 and Composure 3 are the important numbers. Strength 2 grants access to Grappling Style and Dexterity 3 grants access to Martial Arts and Defensive Combat Style. This also influences what dicepools your character is better at.

                        The skills presented focus on meeting the minimums for merits and traits we might want to take later. I deliberately left out a lot of dots to give leeway to a large variety of concepts. These are skills for an unarmed combatant. I generally proffer unarmed to other forms of combat, but Brawl can be swapped out with Weaponry or Firearms just as easily. Depending on your Atavisms and playstyle, Athletics, Occult, and/or Expression may also replace Brawl. Always have as many dots in your skill that grants Defense as your concept will allow.

                        Iron Skin is hands down the best merit to take if you are concerned about combat. Combat Defense keeps your defense up, but is ultimately skipable if you are willing to boost Athletics for whatever reason.

                        Unbreakable is my go-to starting atavism because it's hard to pass up immunity to several tilts and passive 3/2 armor (+1/1 on Low Saity, combine with Reinforced Clothing for another +1/0 armor) for purposes of defense. It elevates your combat beast beyond the realm of mere mortals and it just feels awesome and empowering to shrug off blows like that.

                        Behold My True Form is a stopgap measure. It ensures you almost always have a source of damage (say, vs targets that can turn into swarms or turn into mist or whatever) and it helps against targets with extremely high defense. This also combines very well with Fist of Nightmares.

                        It's important at this moment to take a look at the build's strengths and weaknesses. It is very defensively slanted. In terms of offense you are only inflicting 0B or 0L damage, compensated for slightly by having access to grappling. Your attack dicepools are terrible, clocking in at a mere 7 (8 during a grapple) to attack, the Defense any serious combatant is going to reduce your dicepool to somewhere between 0 and 2. This means that with the optional choices available to this build, we are going to want to address a lack of damage and a lack of reliability. It's also worth noting that while you have an "okay" Grapple dicepool, it isn't amazing enough to win grapple checks against other types of supernaturals who specialize.

                        Your other choice of Atavism should probably be made around now, and you'll want something offensive. Relentless pursuit is amazing and you would be well served to pick it up as your first Experience buy, but right now what you need is more offense. I'm going to recommend the following as first-order optimal for each family:

                        Anakim: Looming Presence, Intimidate 3+ (with relevant specialty, ideally)
                        This takes care of your damage issue. The low Satiety effect means that onany successful attack, you have a big chance to inflict Beaten Down. After doing that, you just need to wait out their willpower. Don't bother with Killing Instinct if you plan on relying on this.

                        Eshmaki: From the Shadows or Dragonfire; Dexterity 3; Stealth 3+ or Athletics 3+, respectively
                        The advantage of Dragonfire is that it gives you a ranged (potentially multi-targeting) option that runs off of Athletics, potentially saving you the need for the defensive combat merit. Agg damage is a nice touch, but pretty situational. The real winner with Dragonfire is that it lets you use the fire Lair trait at its maximum value with impunity.

                        From the Shadows takes a different rout. You rely on stealth (adding that dynamic to the character) and surprise attacks to negate defense. I would consider using a weapon and/or firearms with From the Shadows to perform hit and run tactics. The idea is high sudden damage, ideally enough to stun the target.

                        Makara: Monster from the Deep or Siren's Song; Expression 3+ and more Presence/Manipulation if you use Siren's Song
                        Monster from the Deep is the real winner here. Not only does it lower the target's defense, but it can increase your unarmed damage and grant you an aoe attack that ignores defense entirely. This is probably the most straightforward option for those who want to be the best unarmed combatant.

                        Siren's Song is a worthy mention. If it could harm people after making them deaf, it'd be amazing. At the moment it's a combat opener, though a pretty good one because it ignores all forms of defenses. It's more for a social / fighting hybrid, though. If you use Siren's Song I wouldn't bother with Killer Instinct.

                        Namtaru: Infestation with Fist of Nightmares and/ or Killer Instinct; Basilisk's Touch with 3 Dexterity
                        It's worth noting that since Unbreakable is a Namtaru Atavism, they can use any of the other family's starting options freely.

                        Infestation is fantastic against mortals with a Satiety expenditure. However, it simply deals too little damage by itself to be able to rely on it by itself. It's too easy to negate 1 Bashing or 1 Lethal entirely. The defenses, while great, are too situational to replace Unbreakable entirely. The answer to these problems are Fist of Nightmares and Killer Instinct 3 + Killer Instinct (Advanced) 1. The former lets you apply damage or other effects to your swarm cloud, while the latter lets you ignore armor and convert your AoE 1 Bashing damage to an AoE 1 Lethal damage.

                        Basilisk's Touch is probably on par with Monster from the Deep. In some ways it's better. A simple touch (Dexterity + Brawl +2, helping your dicepools) can inflict poison and/or the drugged tilt. Combine poison with inflicting Insensate and the fight is over, the opponent just doesn't know it yet.

                        Ugallu: Storm Lashed + Occult 3+ or Eye of Heaven
                        Storm Lashed offers a variety of special effects and a special means of attack, all of which are useful. Nabbing a second point of Lair so that you can blind someone for multiple turns becomes important, though probably not enough to bother starting with it. Make sure to boost your lightning dicepool if you plan on relying on that.

                        Eye of Heaven is situational and requires you to be the one taking initaitive, but is pretty nice. Note that its Satiety effect can apply to things like Nightmares or anything else classified as an "attack." Personally I think there are better options, but Eye of Heaven offers enough utility to be worth considering.

                        On Cyclopian Strength, Limb from Limb, etc.
                        Things that deal damage based on Lair are pretty good, but IMO not worth starting out with. Limb from Limb is a great Atavism, but this build suffers from a lack of damage, and you need damage to make Limb from Limb work properly.

                        Summary
                        Looming Presence, Monster from the Deep, and Basilisk's Touch are probably the best option.

                                        In the original printing of Professional Training, in Hunter: the Vigil, there was a Sidebar explaining how Applying the Rote quality to Combat Actions worked and what that represented in real world terms, and what the appropriate drawbacks were to doing so, namely that all of your focus is on acting in accordance with your training rather than on reacting to external threats and the realities of a combat situation. I'll double check Demon and the GMC, but I believe that that's still a balancing effort on the part of the dev team.

                                        UPDATE: neither of the newer books include the Combat by Rote sidebar (Hunter: the Vigil, pp. 69) with their versions of Professional Training, however not imposing the loss of Defense limitation would make the merit as a whole an auto-take for the vast majority of combat builds, as it would make the All-out Attack option, and abilities that utilize the similar mechanics, redundant.
