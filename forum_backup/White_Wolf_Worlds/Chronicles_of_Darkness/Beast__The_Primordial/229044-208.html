    I find that there is one major difference between Heroes and whatever analogy people want to compare them with on a given day:

    Heroes have no other recourse. There is no legal system, mortal or supernatural, that they can turn to. There is no way to get protection. There is no way to non-violently prevent a Beast from hurting them again, such as jail or rehabilitation.

    The only choice any victim of a Beast has is to either roll over and take it or fight back. Self-defense is not immoral.

    Of course, then people bring up how they attack even the Beasts that didn't make them. I say it's important to realize that people in the World of Darkness, Hero or otherwise, do not have the benefit of reading the books. They only have what they see. And they see primordial monsters masquerading as human beings who abuse others. They see it because that's what made them. Beasts look like people, but Heroes aren't going after people. They're going after Beasts.

    This goes double for Heroes who regularly see Beasts in their Lairs, like the infamous Sleeping Beauty. If they look like the monsters of legend, act like the monsters of legend, and live in lairs made of nightmares, and you don't have the benefit of getting the full picture from the book, what else can you do? You can't report these monsters to the police. If you're one of the few people in the world that knows this threat even exists, and you know you're capable of stopping it, don't you have a moral obligation to do what you can to stop it? Beasts may not all be evil, but given what Heroes know and have experienced and how they don't have any other way to go about it, I feel the only moral option they have is to fight.

    The only time I'd consider otherwise is if a Beast went out of their way to show the Hero that they aren't evil, but even then a Hero has every reason to believe they're just hiding it to get the upper hand. You have to look at this from an in-universe perspective. Heroes don't have the book. Heroes have no other way. I do think they're justified.

    Which is why I'm glad Matt is open to clarifying things and seems intent on changing it. Which I suppose makes a lot of the debate about a lot of things moot until we see what the changes are.

    EDIT: Also, Morality isn't Integrity. They each do and represent different things. That's where the Heroes = Slashers mentality falls apart completely.

                            Welp, I just want to throw a comment in, then run off.

                            "Beasts have nothing to do" and "Beasts need more antagonists" thoughts - I think that they do, both from the same source. Crossover. Beast: the Primordial isn't quite a game I'm comfortable doing a Superfriends style game, where everyone is a different supernatural. What I am comfortable with, however, is having the Beasts driven to seek out other supernaturals to interact with, and that cause drama.

                            Beasts, to my mind, are like Overlords, in that they're building up an Army of Darkness. Culturing contacts and support among other supernaturals, and making their enemies yours, or failing and getting into battles, building a grand Dungeon/Lair connected that's a home for all of them. The central drive is to build a Family. You're like Dracula building up his Castlevania fortress. Its like playing the game Overlord, or Dungeon Keeper, and their spiritual successors. It literally nourishes your soul to do this. And, like those games, you can be as evil or good as you like, though your powers and methods are still dark.

                            In a way, Beast is a lot like Mage and Hunter. The Awakened go out to find mysteries to study, which can include supernaturals (though, more often. its just the realms and the ephemeral beings). Hunters seek out supernaturals because they're the Enemy - you're standing up to the night. Both those games revolve around exploring things from the other game lines. Beast takes that tendency, and runs with it as the main story hooks.

                            Beast isn't a crossover game because you're expected to play Beast x Mage in the same party. Its a crossover game because dealing with other supernaturals is as critical to the game as the hunt is to a werewolf. At least, that's my opinion. So, if you ignore or diminish that crossover element, of course there's less for Beasts to do.
