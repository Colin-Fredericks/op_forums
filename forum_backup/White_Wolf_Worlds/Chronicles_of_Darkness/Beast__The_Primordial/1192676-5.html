                    As a rule, I found the BPG an excellent, top-quality sourcebook, as important to the game as the corebook itself, in almost all its components. Some stuff may look rather more valid and useful than a few other items that look mostly useless or redundant, but this is only natural. The only material that I'm really unhappy about is the Satiety management guidelines that imply Gorged is the only optimal Satiety condition for a Beast to be in (I strongly disagree, IMO Starving is at least just as optimal in its own way) and the Talassii curse for its rapey implications.

                    Not because I have any squeamishness objections to using rape in fiction, quite the contrary, IMO it has a valid place in appropriate circumstances for drama or as an erotic fantasy, but because I absolutely despise the prudish, PC meme that sex is a sacred thing that should only happen with romantic love in a perfect relationship and hence rape is a "special kind of evil", worse than killing and more so any other non-sexual violence, abuse, or coercion. In my opinion, sex is an important but vanilla part of life that people can legitimately and gainfully indulge in for all kinds of non-romantic reasons in all kinds of less than ideal situations, from erotic pleasure with no strings attached to personal experimentation or gratification to a transactional exchange. Sexual abuse does not warrant any special status, it is just the equivalent of non-sexual assault, blackmail, harassment, or torture (as the case may be, according to circumstances) that just happens to involve sexual stuff, nothing more, nothing less, and should be treated no differently.

                    From this PoV of mine, in Beast terms I find way distasteful, arbitrary, and hypocrite to burden a Family with a special mystical curse since the entrapment fear archetype that birthed them may have some rapey implications, when Beasts of all kinds and Families often do nightmarish things to victims for feeding that are just as bad as or worse than sexual violation. Just think what a typical Tyrant, Predator, or Ravager can do to victims if they don't really care about the consequences of their actions, all the way to murder or absolutely ruining lives, that Nemeses might easily stoop down to excruciating torture, and stuff. Hence I've decided to ignore the Talassii's 'curse' at my table as prudish/PC nonsense and surgically cut it away from the setting. Last but not least, I have my serious doubts a single Hero could or should be able to burden an entire Family with a lasting curse. That kind of event should at least involve the intervention of a godlike entity (say, that hypothetical uber-Hero should at least have been to Heroes what an Incarnate is to Beasts).

                    And I cling to my notion that while Sated and moreso Ravenous and Slumbering are dangerous and unpleasant situations that should usually be avoided barring special circumstances, Starving and Gorged at least are equally advantageous and useful conditions for a Beast to be in, each in their own way (I have my own reasons to prefer Starving, but I can see the reasons to choose Gorged instead), at least this side of the Incarnation Inheritance.

                                                    The only other thing I'm unhappy with in the BPG is the implication achievement of Inheritance is necessarily going to be an endgame for the character. Now, most kinds of Inheritance admittedly make the character unplayable in a Beast chronicle, either because they turn animalistic (Merger, Retreat in most cases) or they effectively regress into a Muggle (Divergence, Erasure). But I cannot see any valid reason why an Incarnate cannot stay a playable character indefinitedly in a suitably high-powered chronicle, more or less under the same terms one can play an Archmage or vampire elder PC (stuff we have entire sourcebooks about). Incarnate Inheritance is basically the same kind of playable, trascendental supernatural condition as Archmagedom, if with less phenomenal cosmic power and more viscerally physical superpowers. Inversion is also entirely playable in theory, even if practice I expect it is going to cut short the character's lifespan.
