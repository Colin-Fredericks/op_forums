            Originally posted by Jacob View Post
            Just asking and answering questions like -- If I hunger for things (collector) what do I get out of feeding from other supernaturals' feedings? -- is immensely helpful. The game doesn't need to answer all of the metaphysical / mythological questions but it should handle the essential logical cause-effect ones. If it wants to be a cross-over game then it should be, but what it presents right now is still only half-baked and thematically weak. It's a collection of mechanical hacks with very little narrative reason for existing beyond the deus ex machina of because...Crossover.

            Honestly, I don't understand why people are being so defensive. It isn't like the game is not going to happen. It is going to happen. That being the case, there's no reason it can't be the best game possible. It should definitely try to outdo Werewolf (which has so many built crossover hooks it's impossible to count them all; literally everything can spin towards needing to temporarily or permanently add other supernaturals as allies or packmates).
            Disagreeing with you isn't exactly the same thing as being defensive. For instance, you get the impression, from reading the book, that Beasts are very much defined by their own particular hunger/obsession, and therefore it doesn't make sense to you that they can be sated by taking part in other supernaturals sating their own hunger. Others may have a different perspective; for instance, that all beasts essentially serve the same Hunger, but have adapted to it in different ways. This allows for the possibility that Beasts can share certain feeding methods in common, such as being present for another supernatural being's feeding. Expressing that perspective and asking that you consider it isn't defensiveness, it's discussion.

            Incidentally, one perspective that I'm having a hard time understanding is your view of Werewolf as a super crossover-happy game. Sure, there are plot hooks and comments on how some werewolf powers might interact with other supernaturals, but they're just little bits and pieces scattered through the book, and I don't really think there's anything at all that Werewolves "need" from other supernaturals. Plus, it's really no different from Vampire in this respect; Blood and Smoke has all the same sidebars and plot hooks for how kindred may interact with werewolves or whatnot.

                Belated response but wanted to answer anyways.

                Originally posted by Jacob View Post
                (...)
                I disagree.

                Maybe you think otherwise, maybe you don't, but to me the theme of Family in Beast The Primordial is very much about acceptance and support. Beasts can find those not only among kin, but with every other monster as well. With vampires, or werewolves or changelings or whatever, they may find understanding and companionship. And in the same way, other supernaturals can find understanding and aid in the Beggoten.They're pretty much equipped for it.

                Of course this won't be the case with every supernatural. It most certainly won't be the case even just among every single Beast. Vampires and werewolves argue internally about territory and socio-cultural matters. By the logic of finding Beasts more interesting as lab rats, you could certainly say the same for most other supernaturals, which is making dangerous assumptions about the methods of all mages. Mummies that care for their individual followers wouldn't tolerate other things taking from their cultists either. Supernaturals come into conflict with different splats, or even among those of the same kind, for the same variety of reasons they may come into conflict with Beasts. When it comes down to the nitty gritty, it depends a lot on individual expectations and feelings. But hence choosing your family.

                I think the book actually does a good job presenting all these paradigms. Also, Demon is one of my favorite books. Mummy is another of my favorite books. I love them both for various reasons, and I think I love Beast just as much for different reasons. And I find the writing on all of them quite fantastic and of comparable quality (even if they are at their best in different places, and Demon is more polished mechanics wise than Mummy or Beast right now).

                You can have your opinion, I won't say it's wrong. But consider that it's a lot more subjective than what you're making it sound like.
