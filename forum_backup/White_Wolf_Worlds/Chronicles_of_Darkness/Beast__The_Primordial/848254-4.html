    Not at all.

    The standard Beast-Hero story is one where the two battle to the death. Life and death battles have an inherent way of grabbing the dramatic spotlight, if a Beast tries to bring a family together and breaks it apart I want the Beast to deal with that consequence directly; rather than substitute it for a different combat heavy conflict.

    In addition thematically you're between Scylla and Charybdis. If turning into a Hero makes the mortal into a significantly worse person as it typically does then the thematics become dangerously close to victim blaming - yes the Beast ruined her life, but she's in the wrong for not forgiving the Beast. (It may have been an accident on the Beast's part, but when you play with fire you can't just claim it's an accident when something gets burnt.)

    On the other hand if she becomes genuinely Heroic and a better person because of it then it's another story about a Beast tearing out the dross. Which is of course a perfectly appropriate story, for example: Batman's origin, but it is an example of a Beast's lesson going right (for a certain definition of right) and doesn't fill the niche about a Beast's lessons going wrong.

            Technically speaking, a Hero isn't necessarily turned into a Beast-hunting obsessive. They need to be Integrity 4 or lower to become obsessed with hunting Beasts, and with the egotistical ideas of the Monomyth protagonist. Before that, a Hero is a person who can sense disturbances in the Primordial Dream, a person who can feel fear as a palpable force. They are predisposed to look for Beasts, since Beasts share a similar intrinsic connection to the Primordial Dream, but that doesn't stop them from looking for the true source of the fear and trying to address it.

            Taking your 'failed Lesson' examples from the last page, let's throw an Integrity 5+ Hero into the mix.

            The Hero finds the scapegoat and works to restore the family relationship, and find what was the source of the family's fears that made them project them onto the scapegoat.

            The Hero tracks down the artist and acts as their rock, helping them struggle with their depression.

            The Hero tracks down the scarred addict and tries to help them get clean.

            This is what I think Heroes did before everything went wrong. The Beast introduced the Lesson, but it was the Hero who drew the information from it, it was the Hero who helped dredge wisdom from primal panic. The Hero would help the person consumed by fear overcome it, and in doing so learn something themselves- a Lesson they could take and carry with them, without the need for the primordial fear. Basically, the Hero would put others before themselves, and genuinely try to help them. This isn't to say a High-Integrity Hero won't hunt Beasts. If the Beast goes too far, if they become a menace rather than just problematic, then the Hero steps in and defeats the monster at their door. The Low-Integrity Hero doesn't care about the victims, they think it's all about them. They refuse to learn anything, and the kill becomes everything to them. The glory of hunting down the monster and killing it.

            I think a High-Integrity Hero can be a real force for good, but I'm also of the opinion that a High-Integrity Hero isn't likely to go looking for Beasts, or other supernatural creatures, and instead tackles other sources of fear. All they know is that they don't like seeing people afraid, and that they want to help. A High-Integrity Hero who accidentally stumbles across a Beast in the course of the Hero's career as a psychotherapist would be interesting.

                    Am I really the only person who asked about what happens when Beasts lessons go horribly wrong? I would have thought that is an exceptionally obvious question to ask.

                    That said, I do have a lot of sympathy for the people who think the only responsible way to eat is to hunt horrible people; and even then my natural instinct is to respond two ways don't make a right. I did mean what I said about the importance of consent in the last page. Even if it does all work out for the better, it's still immoral if you don't get consent first, and if I'm honest I'd give it 50/50 whether you'd find a Beast teaching racists to be tolerant or teaching tolerant people to be racist. Where does racism come from if not the darkest and most primordial parts of the human psyche?

                    But I agree with you about what's actually a problem to address. Firstly Beast is now published, and I doubt anyone would want to rewrite it a third time even if it wasn't. Secondly like most WoD games it's about playing monsters. Vampire would be pretty boring if everyone used the immortality to set up compound interest schemes, only brought blood with money, and spent their time doing something more ethical than backstabbing each other in an eternal dense of treacherous politics. Beast would be boring if everyone taught lessons by becoming a licensed therapist and paying strict attention to professional ethics while avoiding any feeding save Family Dinner via watching Werewolves hunting wild deer for dinner.


                    Beast is what Beast is. If you don't think Beasts feeding is moral, tell stories about immoral Beasts. They're supposed to be monsters, or use them as antagonists for Hunter (to be fair, that's what I do. I think my Beast opposed Compact is possibly my best).
