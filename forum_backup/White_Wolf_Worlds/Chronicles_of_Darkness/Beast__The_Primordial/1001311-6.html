    Originally posted by Paradim View Post

    I think it might be a little erroneous to think of the use of the Lair as a "fast-travel system". From what I'm understanding of what ArcaneArts' point, is that the Lair exists as a narrative that fits your Horror. The Lair Traits are expressions of "These are the dangers that are where these monsters lurk". Yetis in blizzards, krakens during turbulent seas, dragons during thunderstorms. As darkness falls in the forest to a pitch black, monstrous wolves roam and hunt, so close your doors and huddle up to your fires.

    Your Lair doesn't actually exist as a real location. There is no actual correspondence between a Lair and a geographical point on Earth. The Primordial Dream just exists and is everywhere and nowhere at the same time. Due to this, it's the resonance of your Lair, the evocative expression of the dangers your Horror represents and has as it's calling cards (rumbling cracks of thunder, the fall of blinding darkness, the silence that falls and all you hear is your own heartbeat getting faster) that let you touch your Lair to real world geography, because those evocative elements are the herald to your Horror's arrival that people dread.

    Functionally, to your Beast... It can be a fast-travel system. But to the Beast's Horror, it's not really, because to the Yeti Horror, any place that's being covered with a howling blizzard is the outskirts of it's Lair, whether thats in the Himalaya's or in North Dakota.

    Did I make sense there?
    Elegant and eloquent explanation. Alliteration intended.

            A random to jump to a place you've never been is silly-archmages don't get that much leeway- and given the nature of doing as Beasts, places you can jump to are probably places you've been feeding in.
            I'm not seeing how it's silly. The section for Primordial Pathways provides no consideration for distance. Considerations given are "somewhere random", "within a specified world" and "but still resonant". That can be a location practically anywhere on Earth.

            Archmages don't function like Beasts. They have different scopes to their power and different considerations for when their power is applicable (such as having sympathy or resonance). Beasts are a lot more restricted than Archmages in what they can do, but in their area of capability, Beasts are quite powerful. An Archmage that doesn't know the Death Arcanum isn't going to have any luck using magic to open an Avernian Gate to the Underworld, but ANY Beast can try if they know it's there.

            Now, for practical purposes of running a game, the ST can just have a random location be near the usual stomping grounds for the campaign if there's no interest in doing world exploring but that's a matter about what helps the campaign and story move forward. But this very much fits the theme of exploration that Beast has. Being able to go to a random location that fits the resonance of your Lair is an opportunity to explore and discover new things, whether that random location is a section of the city you call home that you haven't visited much or whether that random location is somewhere more exotic and unknown. The Beast doesn't get to decide, that doesn't mean the ST and the Beast's Player can't share ideas and decide what sounds more interesting.

                        Taking cues from Arc's playbook here and just straight-up writing a pseudo-essay.

                        Why It Is Important That Chamber Resonance Applies To Primordial Pathway Rolls: A Micro-Dissertation

                        Going through the key issues in no particular order…

                        1. If It Doesn't Apply, Other Beast Template Features Are Made Inferior and/or Redundant.
                        If you can open a Pathway to or from anywhere (or even "just" anywhere with a Lair Trait belonging to you or your broodmates), Skeleton Key and Under the Bed are absurdly involved, restrictive, and/or expensive to use — why bother seeking out supernatural gateways when jumping to your Lair or the Shadow of your current location is as simple as opening a Pathway and maybe switching Chambers?
                        If you can open a Pathway anywhere, you can overlap your Lair with anyplace — and your Lair offers all the benefits that imposing Lair Traits does and more without having to spend Satiety. (Imposing Lair Traits from scratch is already slightly lessened in utility by the ability to cause some of them [and thus become able to invoke the rest without a roll] with Atavisms or basic preparation like setting off the sprinklers or turning out the lights.)

                        2. If It Doesn't Apply, Half the Benefits of Incarnation Are A Booby Prize.
                        If you can open a Primordial Pathway from anywhere, that means you can enter your Lair and assume a monstrous form from anywhere — and that's a description of two of the more interpersonally relevant traits the Beast Incarnate universally possesses (shaving off half the downsides of Satiety is desirable, yes, but it doesn't do anybody else any good unless they're also seeking Incarnation) without the drawback of Destabilization and the potential geographic constraint of staying the Apex of a given hive to worry about.

                        3. If It Doesn't Apply, Multiple Example Characters Have To Be Supremely Unimaginative and/or Idiots.
                        The Eastside Pyro is starving and in prison. Boleslav is an Incarnate who rules over a small village. The vast majority of Beast's inspirational critters and example characters are quite regionally particular (the hodag is largely unheard of outside of Wisconsin, for example), and yet somehow the vast majority of Beasts do not simply pull up stakes and vanish to another district/city/state/country through the Lair when their activities come back to them. Speaking of which…

                        4. If It Doesn't Apply, There's No Reason to Eat at Home.
                        It's not hard for Beasts to figure out how to scare people — they tend to embody and/or dwell in the kinds of things that terrify regardless of regional specifics, so foreign cultures and the language barrier don't usually stop them from feeding.
                        So if you can just open a Pathway to anywhere on Earth and it's not significantly harder to reach a place that you haven't personally sowed nightmares in, what actually stops you as a young Beast from disappearing to a faraway land and eating random strangers without any repercussions for your personal life?

                        5. If It Doesn't Apply, Slumbering is Narratively Toothless and Your Lair is the Most Insecure Secure Location Around.
                        If your Heart's lack of correspondence to a material location has no interaction with the rules for Chamber resonance, you should by definition be able to open a Pathway straight to your Horror at Satiety 10 and inflict the requisite scratch damage without having to collapse a Chamber, put yourself near death, or brave the hazards of your Lair first.
                        "Another Beast's Lair" is part of the subset of the cosmology that it is unambiguously the easiest to open a Primordial Pathway to — there is no "except for the Heart" clause there, so any Beast who knows you're a Beast can harry you at the core of your stronghold without any extra difficulty whatsoever under this interpretation.

                        6. You're Playing a Sympathetic Monster Even if the Word "Sympathy" Isn't Directly Used, and That Matters.
                        The random-location option works on the basis that the Primordial Dream touches every place in the perceivable cosmos through resonance. Resonance is sympathy — the mystical/emotional/physical principle of like-attracts-like is one of the most basic organizing principles of supernatural ecology, echoed everywhere from the Shadow to the convergent evolution of the Kindred, and Astral domains like the Primordial Dream embody it most strongly thanks to not being constrained by material geography — and sympathy is variable in ways that have to do with more than just what world you're in — whether you agree about their applicability to Pathways or not, that's why the penalties from Chamber resonance apply to attempts to invoke Lair Traits from nothing.
                        The Primordial Dream does not have a substantially stronger connection to "everywhere" than "the immanent magical center of all things" and its denizens claim relation to a number of monsters that do engage with sympathy, so the idea that they arbitrarily don't make use of sympathetic connections in more than the absolute broadest sense of shared-phenomenon doesn't have a lot of heft to it.
                        The random-location option's social analogue is Thicker Than Water — in the absence of repulsive circumstances, every Beast can potentially get along with any monster they encounter and eventually develop Family Ties with them, but mere universal Kinship (and Kinship is also a form of sympathy) doesn't give a Beast Family Ties with every monster in existence, much less draw them all to you from across the pond. You have to build up the connection to be able to reach the point where that monster is better at helping you and vice versa than other monsters, and if Chamber resonance doesn't apply to travel then it becomes substantially less likely that the sort of circumstances that lend support to "You don't choose your family" as a theme come up.

                        7. Exploration Does Not Exist in a Vacuum.
                        Most explorers in common thought do not blink from island to island and jigsaw their maps together as they stumble across common elements. Most explorers travel some unknown direction in relation to defined routes and mark the terrain they pass through to assemble a more cohesive view of the world. (Sometimes they also plant flags, which are usually more helpful if you can reasonably expect your enemies and countrymen to reach the same place as you without your guidance.)
                        I find it really hard to play a convincing explorer when I have Farthest Shores Landing Prana.

                        8. Other Games Equip Their Characters For Dives Into Foreign Parts When It's Appropriate.
                        a. Born With It
                        Werewolves and Sin-Eaters both have inherent workarounds for potential language barriers in their respective chunks of the cosmos (speaking the native tongue and having a universal translator for the dead, respectively) and possess innate means of keyless entry through those domains' entrances. Any werewolf can talk with any spirit in any part of the world, regardless of whether the spirit has learned any of the Uratha's human languages or indeed has ever been outside of the peak of an ancient mountain on the outskirts of rural India, and the Bound don't need to worry about the fact that a ghost from the age of the Crusades only speaks the great-grandparent of a living tongue and dwells behind a secret gate in the basement of an office building in Istanbul. Neither of these character types would be capable of handling the other's situation with the same ease, because their gamelines do not assume that werewolves will routinely need to deal with ghosts in the Deep Dominions or that Sin-Eaters will have much of any truck with the Shadow of a secluded mountain wilderness.
                        Demons and Mummies both have reason to find themselves far away from their last known location and needing to integrate with mortal society on short notice, and they have linguistic shortcuts to facilitate this capacity to be in four wildly different countries in the course of a decade. Demons have perfect memories and total control of normally unconscious tics and processes, the ability to steal or fabricate mortal identities and shed them in an evidence-shredding flash of brilliance, and native fluency in pretty much every language you'd encounter on the street in any part of the world; seeing as they're fugitives hiding from God under Its own nose, they need to be able to bug out fast and deflect suspicion in short order. Mummies are served by cults whose existences essentially parallel the development of civilization, they wake up with only a small amount of self-knowledge at the beginning of a given descent, and they could well have been inactive for over a century depending on the circumstances; fortunately, even though their most common task requires very little in the way of communications, they can pick up new languages over the course of a lunch break and most of their cults have at least one member who speaks their ancient language.

                        b. Work For It
                        Mages and Prometheans both kind of have to build for dives into faraway countries or other dimensions, but they have some shortcuts where it's relevant. Prometheans switch Refinements with some regularity, and the one that deals with human interaction as a focus needs no special consideration to start following and includes a universal translator effect in one of its signature Alembics, which can be retained after leaving the Refinement with a small investment of resources only a bit more involved than learning new languages the long way (and there's an Alembic that can theoretically shortcut that in two other Refinements' sets, including the one focused on interaction with other supernatural beings) — the Created are encouraged to wander from place to place, and if one of those places happens to pose a language barrier, they can adapt themselves to surmount it. Meanwhile, mages have "speak with [native ephemeral being]" spells become available before "open gateway to [supernatural dimension]" spells in the Arcana relating to those worlds by virtue of the basic progression of spell knowledge (the same level of expertise in another Subtle Arcanum allows you to be understood by anybody as long as you're telling the truth), any one of them can convey meaning to anyone who isn't a vanilla mortal even without formal training, and the Path with the most talent for regular worldly teleportation also has a natural affinity for telepathy and universal linguistic translation — the Awakened are consummate investigators whose reach sometimes exceeds their grasp, and the structure of their powers is such that dealing with the expected outcome of a given journey is usually within their ability by the simple virtue of their being powerful enough to make that journey.
                        Changelings and Vampires are mentally and metaphysically incentivized by their lost humanity to either keep to their established haunts in and around the section of the mortal world they live in or specialize their (un)lifestyles toward being nomadic, largely on the basis of known threats being more manageable than unknown threats and sudden surprises being detrimental to their grasp of humanity and current events. Nothing stops them from learning new languages or acquire mortal or mystical interpreters, and they have a great deal of extra time to do so, but the niche their default society operates under is typically very local on account of it offering better security and not needing to lean too much on supernatural powers to stop people from asking about the new stranger in their midst and/or where all the thumbtacks went.

                        c. The Takeaway
                        Beasts fall conceptually closest to changelings and vampires in terms of innate cultural integration skills and their extradimensional nexus-point corresponds to their location and experience most strongly; the vast majority of them aren't on the run from angels or Huntsmen or Disquiet, without outside interference they're living beings who experience time as a daily procession of occurrences between periods of sleep, and generally speaking the Devouring doesn't grant them any heightened ability to understand what people from different worlds and eras are saying, so either Beasts are supposed to be able to make contact with complete strangers without preamble and vanish just as quickly with no meaningful entanglement resulting or the game is set up in such a manner that the expected playstyle uses good old-fashioned legwork and local guides as the principle means of exploration from the known into the unknown.

                        That's about two and a half hours of rambling type in points of of wildly varying length that still doesn't go into a whole lot of detail, but there it is. I'll clarify particulars if asked, but as it stands I have a substantial backlog of media I just spent two hours not catching up on, so up the listicle goes with special emphasis given to Points 1-4.

                        Resident Lore-Hound

Nothing stops them from learning new languages or acquire mortal or mystical interpreters, and they have a great deal of extra time to do so, but the niche their default society operates under is typically very local on account of it offering better security and not needing to lean too much on supernatural powers to stop people from asking about the new stranger in their midst and/or where all the thumbtacks went.

                                        c. The Takeaway
                                        Beasts fall conceptually closest to changelings and vampires in terms of innate cultural integration skills and their extradimensional nexus-point corresponds to their location and experience most strongly; the vast majority of them aren't on the run from angels or Huntsmen or Disquiet, without outside interference they're living beings who experience time as a daily procession of occurrences between periods of sleep, and generally speaking the Devouring doesn't grant them any heightened ability to understand what people from different worlds and eras are saying, so either Beasts are supposed to be able to make contact with complete strangers without preamble and vanish just as quickly with no meaningful entanglement resulting or the game is set up in such a manner that the expected playstyle uses good old-fashioned legwork and local guides as the principle means of exploration from the known into the unknown.
