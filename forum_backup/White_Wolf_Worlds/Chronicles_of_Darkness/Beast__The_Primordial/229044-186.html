                                    Originally posted by branford View Post
                                    I mentioned this in another forum concerning one of my criticisms of Beast.

                                    I believe the crossover-heavy nature of Beast, with included mechanics of varying quality, was intended to replace the importance of social and political factions which characterize every other major and beloved nWOD game line. Beasts were supposed to piggy-back and insinuate themselves within Covenants, Consilia, Freeholds, Protectorates, etc.

                                    It was a bold design decision that sadly may have failed to achieve its purpose. The lack of these species-specific groups feels like a void in the Beast setting, creates ambiguity in Beasts' motivations and overall goals, and creates other problems such as the hyper-focus on Heroes (which has been discussed here in detail). It also may unfairly presume a substantial knowledge about the other nWOD splats and an actual desire by most player groups and ST's for crossover.
                                    I think a lot can be learned from other splats that don't treat the Y-axis (or in the cases of Demon, Promethean and, I think, Geist, the X-axis) of the template as another avenue of power. In most of these splats, interactions with other members of those axes is not exactly essential to character growth the way it is for other templates, so you don't end up with the kinds of axial monoculture that other games (particularly Requiem, and Forsaken 1.0) either fall prey to or rely on in order to maintain the suspension of disbelief.

                                    Also, it's incumbent upon the Storyteller to challenge any notions of Setting Mastery that may arise and embrace the possibility of a crossover in whichever method they see fit. Preconceptions are fine, so long as you are willing to let them be challenged, and a Beast holding a position of power within an established Supernatural power structure should always be the start of a story or Chronicle, not the end. Look at the Vancouver section of the Beasts in the World chapter. The stranglehold that the Leviathans have over the region is an opportunity to subvert core assumptions about how Supernaturals interact with each other, and gives the troupe something to deal with besides the standard threats posed by mortals and non-Begotten adversaries.

                                    Crunch isn't a hobby; it's a calling.
