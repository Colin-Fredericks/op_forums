    Help me understand a few things

    03-09-2018, 08:50 PM
    So i am having a few lingering hangups about Beast. Elements i'm still struggling to understand.

    First fair warning (?) i started with a very negative view about Beast and i have mostly come around. I'm afraid i am still not 100% behind the game. Some things that, if i run a game, i would either avoid/ignore or maybe outright change. But i am making effort to ... get the game because i do believe there is something there that i want to play.
    All that said, lets get to it.

    The 3 points i dont quite understand.

    1- Relation between hunger and choice.
    Okay lets see if i can explain that well.
    From what i understand there is 2 parts to feeding. What i call the core (the hunger), like a hunger for ruin and what i call the scheme, that is the particular way you feed.
    So from what i understand the core (the hunger) is innate to the horror the Beast gets. But what exactly determines the scheme?
    Say you have two Beast with a hunger for ruins what determines that one needs to destroy kneecaps and the other needs to destroy expectations? Is it a choice? Can it be changed?

    2- I don't get what a makara is.
    Its stupid i know but ... is it really just about being an aquatic monster? For monsters representing types of fear, you can have an aquatic monster from every other families.
    Anakim: 2 things here fits. Either the giant whate/leviathan. So huge that you are helpless against it. Or i feel that the fear of the vastness of the ocean is very fitting as an anakim concept.
    Eshmaki: Water obscure vision very very well. Especially at night. Say your somehow reading this while in the ocean at night, there could be a shark literally next to your leg and you wouldnt know it. That feels very eshmaki.
    Namtaru: Just google Blobfish. Try not do it while eating.
    Ugallu: There is no cover on the ocean. You are quite literally exposed to everything that swims there. And swimming is relatively kind of like flying... i guess. (okay ugallu might be my weakest one here).
    So what is left for the makara to represent?

    3- The family aspect in Beast.
    That one makes me feel a bit stupid as the book keeps talking about Beast being a family and i just dont see it. Like at all. I'll admit i dont even know where to begin with that one. I get with werewolf the concept of the pack as a foster family. I even get how the concept work with vampire. But with Beast. It flies right over me.

    Even if i dont solve all of these, i'll still be very satisfied seeing your thoughts on the subjects.

    As always, thx for the help.

          As to your first question: The specific nuance of a Hunger is left up to the player to decide. In universe it's usually presented as some mixture of Horrors being picky eaters when they can afford to be and Beasts not always being comfortable with the more exacting demands. So a Horror may favor one very specific type of destruction, but that's an ethical line the Beast would rather not cross and so they end up picking an alternative method that both can live with.

          For the second: One of the nice things about Beast is that it's conceptually broad enough to represent most any monster a number of different ways. Makara can represent a hostile environment humans aren't adapted to, crushing pressure either literal or figurative, being overwhelmed with tasks you aren't equipped to handle.

          And for the third: Being a Beast can be a hard, scary, lonely experience. Finding somebody else like you, somebody who understands what you're going through is a Big Deal. I think that theme runs pretty true throughout CoD in general. Monsters gravitate toward other similar monsters because being an outsider is difficult and lonely and while they may not be fully human most of them are still subject to that aspect of the human condition. Seriously, you have this problem that sets you apart from your old friends and family, puts them at risk, makes it so you can never safely let any of them get truly close to you. Might end in a lunatic murdering you just for existing. Learning you don't have to face that alone is huge.

              ​Okay, I'll give this a try and hope I help clear up some problems:

              1)
              ​A Beasts Hunger is inherent to the Beast (for more specifically, the Horror that is the Beast's soul). How a Beast feeds a particular Hunger is however the Beast's choice. Using Ruin as an example, a Beast is compelled to feed by destroying something, what they destroy is up to the Beast, not the Horror. Meaning that a Beast could just feed by breaking the kneecaps of a athletic hopeful (thus destroying his hopes and dreams), by posting spoilers all over the net (thus destroying expectations), or by throwing bricks through rich people's windows (thus destroying their sense of security). What a Beast Hungers for is set in stone, how they feed that hunger is entirely their choice.

              2)
              ​With Makara, their fear is drowning, whether drowning in literally in water or drowning in debt, despair, or pressure. For Makara, the deep can be symbolic or literal. The other families don't represent the fear of unfathomable depths, crushing pressure, and the siren call of the abyss quite as well as Makara.

              3)
              ​The Family aspect comes from the idea that Beasts are all related, at least spiritually, to each other and to the other monsters of the Chronicles of Darkness (Demons and Instatiables excluded). The fact that they can all relate the feeling of having a ravenous monster living in their soul that compels them to hurt people and constantly being attacked by psychos declaring, "Die monster, you don't belong in this world!"

              ​For Beasts, they have incentives to gather together, much as Werewolves, Vampires, Mages, Changelings, Demons, and Hunters. For defense, to pool resources, and emotional support.

              ​I hope some of what I said might have been helpful Maitrecorbo

                  Let me see if I can help a bit.
                  1) The player's guide actually addresses this and the answer ends up with, "We don't know." It's one of the major mysteries for Beast's that look at it as they all understand the Hunger is just an inherent part of being one of the Beghotten, but they don't actually know why one Beast revels in chasing down the prey and ripping their heart out of their chest and another collects wedding rings. Let along the odd divides that come from why one nemesis will only punish etiquette blunders and another a chamber in Quebec because punishing smugglers keeps making them the central point on cartel wars.

                  So not really a choice, at least not as far as anyone in canon is aware. If you walk the path of hunger on the way to incarnation you'll probably come to your own conclusions and understanding about it for your game. Change is noted as a thing that does happen, it just isn't common. Sometimes it's a minor change you know a ravager changes what they need to ruin and other times its more extreme and a tyrant becomes an enabler. Again by no means common, but not unheard of for it to happen.

                  2) Water and the ocean is the most literal aspect of it. It tends to come out more as being overwhelmed, things you can struggle against and still lose to because it just keeps coming and eventually it'll either overtake you or you'll make a mistake and then there will be no coming back. The main difference between the Anakim and the Makara in this respect would be how futile the struggle is. An Anakim is a tidal wave there is no fighting a tidal there is only escape or being destroyed by it. The Makara are holes in the boat fighting isn't impossible, it may not even be a bad idea if you act quickly enough you can plug it. If there are too many, if the patch doesn't hold, if you don't act quickly enough then it can be just as dooming as that tidal wave.

                  It is also the fear of the depths, thing that are buried and gone away. Think of it like this a Makara whisperer drags out those old secrets secrets and nightmares that you were sure were long gone.

                  3) Beasts are predominatly looking for places to belong, for others to watch their backs and help with their goals. I kind of think what Beasts tend to be looking for is closer to the nakama concept as you see it in a bunch of anime. So less all monsters are my family, or even the garou are my family and more, "So I know this glass walker, goes by the handle Mokie online, she's solid. Rest of her pack are kind of douchebags to me, but Mokie and I are tight and in a pinch I think we're both sure the other will come through."

                                  1 - I believe the choice is a result of personal preference, rather than having anything to do with being a Beast. The core book makes a point that the Horror doesn't actually care how you feed, only that you continue to do so. That being said, there is a difference between a sirloin steak and a cheese burger. Sure, they're both made of beef and will fill you up if you eat them. But one is a high class meal, while the other is a greasy sandwich.


                                  2 - The Makara are descended from sea monsters, but that's not all that they are. Some have migrated to dry land, just like some fish crawled out of the sea and evolved legs. The way I interpret Makara is that they are the fear of being in a hostile environment, one in which you are ill equipped to survive in. If you were to pluck a human from New York City and drop them in the heart of the Amazon Rainforest with nothing but what they immediately had on them at the time, then chances are they would be dead before the end of the week. Same thing as dropping them in the middle of the ocean, or an abandoned mine shaft. And it doesn't have to be physical depths, either. Your Makara could immerse people in social-political situations that they were unprepared for, plunging them into a metaphorical kind of depths.


                                  3 - As for the family aspect, the best I can tell you is that there are an awful lot of cultures who believe in a mythical progenitor of all monsters. Whatever name you wish to go by, they share a core theme of being the mother / father of ALL monsters. It seems like Beast decides to incorporate this mythology into its setting, so that vampires, werewolves, faeries, est are all literally family. If they go back far enough, they can trace their origins back to a single progenitor, and it is that progenitor that the Beasts know to be the Dark Mother.

                                  Whether or not the other splats acknowledge the validity of this story is up to the individual, rather than society as a whole.
