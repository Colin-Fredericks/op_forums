                                                I smell another brainstorm situation coming on!:

                                                The Vicar's Lair: The Temple of Sleep

                                                It's not a loud place, the Reverend Grebert's soul and home. In fact, it seems to eat sound, ambient indoor light, the sensation of motion (nobody actually moves any slower, but it feels like they are); it's actually fittingly sleepy, and laying on its many stones will reveal that it's actually quite comfy, too; the stones are flat as boards and smooth as skin, and the grass that seems to sit everywhere cradles the body. One could very easily have the best rest of their lives here, if one could ignore the ghosts and the deathly Resonance that permeates the entire edifice and its grounds. Naturally, its lord is one of them; the Son of Charon finds that he's been long spoiled by his home. He no longers sleeps in his flesh-side house, no; his bedroom is vestigial except as a portal to here, where the bony, cloaked priest dozes upon an altar of asphodel and slate, a sleeping statue overlooking his precious Avernian Gate and the abandoned anchors of those he has helped move past the Gate's other side.

                                                Traits; Lethargic, Unquiet, Open, Sacral

                                                Lethargic:                                                 The Temple is almost a physical lullaby. Simply looking at the pedals dancing in the quiet breeze is almost hypnotic, and what sound the breezes make sounds like a mother's cooing. It's hard for even its master to think quickly here, and should one upset the peace, it turns its full power on the heretical swine who dare disturb its tranquility. Meditation rolls gain a +2 bonus, and anyone who initiates a fight in a location under the influence of the Lethargic tilt is suddenly overcome with the desire to rest and sleep, suffering a -3 to all actions until they willingly surrender (at which point they instantly fall asleep). As a side note, changeling may use the Temple as a way to enter the dreams of others, much like the Hedge, although Grebert doesn't know this.

                                                Unquiet: The dead here, while as desperate and eccentric as usual, are not hostile, not most of them. In fact, were it not for their translucent and obvious signs of death, the ghosts seem...normal. Something about the Temple turns ghosts sane, and returns much of their living personality. On one hand, this allows them to approach the difficulty of moving on rationally and accept help more readily...and on the other hand, it makes them more dangerous, since a sane ghost is also a ghost who knows exactly how their Numina and other powers work. A ghost who is in (or has an anchor which is in) a location affected by Unquiet has normal Virtues and Vices (they may regain one Willpower by indulging their vice and all Willpower by acting in accordance with their Virtue), and a +2 bonus to any rolls involving ghostly Manifestations or Numina.

                                                Open: The Temple welcomes all. All ghosts may treat the Temple as Open for their Manifestations.

                                                Sacral: Some Children revel in their seemingly demonic nature and willfully Ruin churches, Punish holy people, and Hoard the Satanic. The Vicar thinks these Beasts are still stuck in a teenager mentality and probably lashing out against a far more mortal enemy than God. No, he and the other Begotten are the bloodline of the two creations of God, men and angels, and its his job to take up the latter's slack when it comes to escorting people to their eternal reward. The Temple agrees with him; it counts as holy ground for all purposes, and holy powers meant to harm him or his charges automatically fail, with the added insult that the triggering individual of their weakness is suddenly overcome with a sense of incredible shame and guilt (this has no game effect, it's a plot hook).
