    The Lie, the Law, The Monomyth, and Other Such Things

    11-20-2022, 02:00 AM
    "Tyrannical gods, the world itself a Lie? Distant Cousin, do not deny all that is dark as some external force of evil. It is as natural as the light. And the limits you overcome every day mean nothing without it"- a Beast to a mage
    So a short little while ago, this particular idea was floated in the Boast thread and it stuck in my craw for reasons unknown for me. I assumed, at the time, that my issue with it was that it was hard to grok that Beasts would deny the Exarchs, but that made me run into the question of why they would accept them. I realize now that my problem is not with that, but rather the way it casually misses a point with Beasts-but that by itself is not worth coming back with findings on. Fortunately, I did also come up with my answer to that dilemma, and I am here to share that wisdom with you all, because interrupting the Simple Questions thread with something that's going to be a bit seemed counter to the point of that thread, so here we are. I will be putting my actual problem with the quote in spoilers at the bottom, because it is good and relevant detailery, but not the more useful point.

    (Also, I sanded the poster's name on it because it's not about them, it's about the quote. Anyways)

    So, Do Beasts have any reason, beyond the word of their kin, to believe in things like the Lie or the Law of Suffering, and in their architects (The Exarchs and Judges, accordingly) given their distance with reality?

    The short answer: Oh yeah, plenty.

    Beasts benefit from their breadth of view for seeing recurring patterns across the board, the sort of invisible machinery and alignment that can move away from the specific goings on and instead pay attention to the "themes" of reality, and more importantly where repetitious occurrences occur even against expectation-and it's not hard to see that tyranny and oppression aren't just the outcomes of power, particularly where the human psyche is involved, but something seemingly baked into the fabric of the supernatural itself, and with that reality itself. The Arisen and Awakened have the most direct names and arguments for this force, but even beyond the machinations of the Judges and Exarchs, there is something in the concurrency of the God-Machine and the True Fae, and how even human affairs tend to warp towards conspiracies that utilize the ephemeral and spiritual to sit on top of others. The Underworld being as broken and consumptive as it is makes a perverse kind of sense in a layered cosmology (Which the Begotten know is not strictly true, but can find convenient for some arguments), that with so much of the top side being loaded with that sort of outlook embedded in the metaphysic, that being the bottom of the world and holding the rest of it up, that it would be that messed up. There's something in the Disquiet, Wasteland, and Torment of the Create that, for all that it makes sense as the consequence of having a force of change constantly around that cannot always be directed because of that constancy, it never the less carries the stink of that theme. Even the spirits and curses that occupy the werewolves and vampires neck of the woods has that kind of creeping feel to it, to a degree that, with enough evidence, it all feels off kilter. The Masquerade, Lunacy, Paradox and Quiescence, the suite of Promethean problems, The Mask, Sybaris-hell, just the hostile nature of magic to the human psyche, all of them have place in in this extended shadow on reality.

    More importantly, though, Beast experience similar phenomenon themselves, and even have a name for it. I've mentioned before that, from a food web angle, Heroes are awfully screwy, beings who, even with their advantages, shouldn't be such a requisite force as to have shaped the course of human history and defined the language of all cultures and times-and yet, they win, they do it again and again, despite all the odds-and with that, crush nuance and myriad forms down into sharp and narrow pillars. Beasts recognize this slight in Heroic favor as the Monomyth, and they can see how the many other forces in the world play to similar books. They also refer to all other such forms as the Monomyth-not so much out of thinking they are all exactly alike, but because it's a easy short-hand to group the recurring forces they see and the similar effects they have over reality.

    For me, this is actually kind of a useful realization-Cinder has always leaned more into Monomyth than I have, because for me, while the idea is intellectually interesting, it didn't really have a practical benefits on the ground of playing the game, whether utilizing it in the original Campbellian sense or just as the generic "Heroes kill Beasts, and all that is wrought is rendered naught except Heroic glory" that is often used. But by recognizing that it's a seeming force exerted on reality, that has actors behind it (like the Exarchs and Judges) makes it click a lot more and creates a cornerstone of Beast Culture with utility, and can even feed into hooks about the Dark Mother* and any other major figures that might be introduced down the line**. It also gives me an excuse to continue making a point of Beasts, as a whole, being entities who sit on that line between the Lie and the Truth, the Law of Suffering and the Way of Will, who employ the tools of monsters but know the way towards enlightenment through them.

    Which is a good time to have that spoiler covered rant. In the mean time, yeah, this was my big dumb brainwave that I had to make a big dumb point about.

    So, my actual problem with the quote at the head.

    Number 1: It's not a boast. I realize that's a trivial and petty thing to get at, but it must be said, that is not a boast, which is counter to the thread it was posted in. Moving on.

    More to the point, though, it feels like apologism from the Beast, which I kind of bristle at.

    See, the thing of the matter is, having otherwise established that Beasts have good reason to believe in things like Exarchs and the Lie, the particular 'boast' here makes it sound like the Begotten is trying to dismiss the concerns of the mage, which is just not really how the Begotten roll, even when they're being assholes-they're not ones to deny the ugly and broken side of things. Yes, the Begotten have grasped wisdom and psychological wholeness by coming to terms with their Fears and Hunger, but it doesn't take a genius to see how the very things they are can be manipulated into things like the Lie and the Law of Suffering, and in fact they probably have reason to suspect that it does-a lot.

    Borrowing from my shit again, an Insatiable Beast is likely to just shrug and call that the price of doing business, a Heroic Beast is likely to take it that the principle, if not the ends, of their mentality has merit, but Beastly Beasts (I see the problem now) are more likely to respond to actors on behalf of those forces with their own sort of visceral anger-"You TWISTED me", on account of being, to some degree, those Fears and Hungers directly. But they would all own that the Lie exists, and that they can even inadvertently be actors for it at times.

    So, if I were to revise this statement-well, first' I'd probably make it a Boast about how the Sleep takes even the most Wakeful of sorts, but putting that context out of the way, I would have them acknowledge the problem first, before going "But there is more to these things than just the tyranny"-the Begotten are the sorts to appreciate the thing that seperates the Adamantine Arrow's and Praetorian's Violence, the Guardians of the Veil and Panopticon's Surveillance, the Mysterium's and Paternoster's Religion***, and the Silver Ladder's and Hegemony's Nationalism*** is that the former uses them, ostensibly, for more just, more merciful, and more compassionate ends, where as the latter use them for tyranny-but the tools remain the same. They are of the same.

    ***I would have to argue, though, since while I can see ways Religion and Nationalism can contribute some good, they are ultimately net evils, but that's for a different conversation.


    *Oh, sure, Mother of Monsters, but Mummy Dearest doesn't always have your best interest at hear, and what's the point of having a really cool background character like the Dark Mother if you can't get conspiratorial with her.
    **The Storm Hero, for my own work on possible supplements and second editions.
