                                        Introduction:

                                        On pages eleven and twelve, fourteen and fifteen . . . Clearly "Devouring" & "Horror" have won out, over "Homecoming" and "Soul". It shifts some interpretations, but I think most of us can live with that. However, whomever did the re-writes in this section is pushing a radically different vision of Beasts. They're basically describing Beasts as proto-Dreamspeakers who sold their souls (and any chance of Awakening) in exchange for getting rid of the visions & nightmares, plus the power of a Horror.

                                        Coupled with a Beast's need to feed their Hunger. . . That pretty much changes Beasts into psychic vampires. Ones who sold out to the darkness, and agreed to prey on their former species. And frankly, that makes them bad guys. Not shades of grey - just outright traitors and monsters. If this view sticks, Heroes are right to pursue and kill Beasts.

                                        So its a very problematic reinterpretation - one that, frankly, the other chapters don't support at all. Heck, even the Q&A box on page 12

                                        Beasts aren’t human: True. Beasts are born human, though most of them experience deep, primal nightmares throughout their lives — an intrinsic connection to the Primordial Dream. At some point, another of the Children finds the incipient Beast and offers to change her through Devouring. From that point on, the person is no longer fully human, having lost her soul and replaced it with a Horror.

                                        Some Beasts claim that they have never been human, however, and that the Devouring merely cleared away detritus. Instead of replacing a human soul with a Horror, they say, the Devouring clarifies the connection, allowing the Horror that was always present to come forth. Indeed, some Beasts claim that the Devouring was spontaneous and that no other Beast was involved. In either case, whether another Beast instigated the Devouring or it happened on its own — whether a Beast was human or just believed she was — the Children are not human.
                                        Which points out - at the very beginning - that Beast aren't human. This new introduction provides no support whatsoever for the second type of Beast (never human, or self-Devoured) . . . and that's really the kind that sold most people on Beast. Simultaneously being a primal fear coping with the fact they have "friends" and "family" now, and a person who finds out the reason the never fit in with other humans, is because they weren't human?

                                        And, frankly, wouldn't it be more fun to spend some of that word count on talking about how, while feeding on humans, a Beast will sometimes find one who's NOT PREY. How, when they try to feed on the person, a gigantic hand slaps them away or tentacles wrap possessively around their attempted meal (in the Dream, of course)? How, when they gaze into the NOT PREY's eyes, they see the same darkness from which they were born?

                                        Plus, the interpretation being pushed by this intro raises other problems. Maybe its addressed later in the book (still working on the second half), but what stops a Beast from randomly devouring people's souls, allowing a Horror to forcibly claim the person? Or why don't Horrors start bonding with other people who've been de-souled by other means?



                                        Edit: Hmm . . . on a related note, would it be possible to add "Homecoming" to the vocabulary list on page 19 & 20? There seems to be enough white space to accommodate it. Plus, it makes sense that the never-humans and self-devoured would have their own term for what happens to them when they come into their nature.

                                                Page 51

                                                A Horror is a nightmare monster, a creature from the dreams of a terrified human being. Modern Beasts theorize that every time a person dreams of monsters, that monster either already exists somewhere in the Primordial Dream (and the dream calls on ancestral memory) or is created at that moment as a Horror (if the dreamer is one of the special few whose dreams go so deep). In any case, the Horror drifts through the Dream, eventually reaching one of the outer Chambers. If it finds one it considers habitable, it enters it, settles down, and bonds with it. The Chamber becomes a Lair.
                                                The second to last line feels awkward. At the very least, it contains excess "it"s. Even a slight tweak would help, something like:

                                                "If it finds one it considers habitable, it enters, settles down, and bonds with it."

                                                Horrors, on their own, rarely touch the minds of human beings deeply enough to have a greater effect than a particularly intense nightmare; it might be a cause for discussion, even analysis, but doesn’t do any lasting harm to a person. Only when Lairs are close to the waking world do the nightmares have the power to cause real harm. The primary way this happens, of course, is when a human being becomes a Beast.
                                                Kind of a run-on. Consider re-editing, maybe along the lines of:

                                                "On their own, Horrors rarely cause anything more than a particularly intense nightmare. One that might be cause for discussion, even analysis, but without inflicting any lasting harm. Only when a Lair drifts close to the waking world does a Horror gain the power to cause real harm. And nothing draws a Lair closer than the birth of a Beast . . .

                                                Growing up estranged or even bullied, the dreamer has every reason to hate her tormentors, but does that make the human race her enemy? What about her family? That one teacher who spoke up for her? The boy next door who had a crush on her even though she was "weird?" These people whom she recognizes as people and who recognize her in turn, these are family even if they aren’t kin. Is she, then, really a part of this community? If so, then why can’t the rest of them recognize it?
                                                There's a typo here. Either it should be "Those people whom" or "These are people whom". Also might want to consider adding a comma after "as people", and breaking the sentence in two (with a period after "in turn").

                                                    Really, really liking the art. Kudos both to the artists, and whomever hired them.

                                                    p. 53

                                                    The Beast looks at the world through her Horror’s eyes. To others, an Eshmaki is just a sleek young woman with sun-darkened skin and the kind of lean muscle that does not come from a gym. Her true self may be a serpentine dragon, but she is confined by the physical reality of her human self, at least in the waking world.
                                                    Although I like the first line, it doesn't really seem to fit the rest of the paragraph.

                                                    Aspects of her Lair bleed into the environment as the Beast concentrates. Heat shimmer fills the air. The sky grows sullen with the glow of distant fires and ash blows on the wind like snow. Shifting her perceptions only takes a modicum of effort, but seeing truly is harder when Hunger gnaws at her.

                                                    The Beast can draw upon her Horror and aspects of her Lair — its heat, its darkness — to perform supernatural feats in the waking world known as Atavisms. These powers become more potent in locations that echo her Lair in some way, consciously infecting those around her with her fear. When a Beast uses an Atavism, any nearby being with supernatural awareness — be it one of the Beast’s inhuman cousins like a vampire, or simply a mortal with some psychic affinity — catches a clear glimpse of the Horror in all its monstrous glory.
                                                    The first line of the second paragraph seems extraneous - just repeating information from the first one.

                                                    And getting a little nitpicky:

                                                    * The first line of the first paragraph is fine as is. But consider adding a bit of flair. For example, perhaps instead of "as the Beast concentrates", how about something like "when the Beast calls" or 'when the Horror stirs"?

                                                    * Like "consciously infecting" (nice imagery there), but that sentence could be a bit neater, and it kind of makes it sound like the Beast herself is afraid.

                                                    * Not wild "any nearby being" - even "any being nearby" is better

                                                    p. 54
                                                    THE VIEW FROM WITHOUT:
                                                    JULIA PHOENIX, GALATEID
                                                    I saw her surrounded by men. The men carried bats and boards and knives, and I though, for a moment, she’s like me. They hate her so. But I did not feel her inner fire, and I thought that something was happening that I did not understand. So I watched, but I promised myself if the men started to hurt her, I would intervene.
                                                    Not wild about "men. The men carried". I’d suggest something more like
                                                    "I saw her surround by men. Men who carried bats and boards and knives"

                                                    There’s also a missing "t" in "thought"
                                                    She fought them. She threw one against a wall so hard he bled. She punched one in the chest and he dropped, coughing, spitting red. But then one moved in behind her with a knife and I yelled a warning. The woman turned and exhaled fire. And I saw — I promise, I saw — the dragon, superimposed on her, mighty and terrible and resplendent and beautiful. The man ran, burning, only to collapse. The other men stood and staggered away.
                                                    Are you sure you want to start three sentences in a row with "She"?
                                                    I climbed down from the fire escape into the alley. I did not know what to say to her, this beauty, this woman with a dragon in her. I could not feel her inner fire the way I would with another like me, but I had, beyond doubt, seen it.
                                                    Possible typo - shouldn’t it be "beyond a doubt"?

                                                    p.56

                                                    Sometimes that means tough love, of course; a Beast who can’t or won’t curb her predatory impulses attracts attention from Heroes or extended family, and that endangers all of the Children.
                                                    I recommend tweaking this sentence, and (definitely) breaking it into two. Something like:

                                                    "Sometimes that meant tough love, of course. A Beast who can't (or won't) curb her predatory impulses attracts attention from Heroes or extended family, and that endangers everyone."

                                                    And this might be nitpicky, but:

                                                    Beasts sense each other in a general way. They instinctively know someone is in their city and will eventually cross paths in the Primordial Dream.
                                                    I'd be tempted to italicize "their", to emphasize that the Beast considers that city as their private hunting grounds.
