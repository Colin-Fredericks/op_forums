    If a Beast with a Hunger for Power is a professional Dominatrix or into the BDSM scene, would that feed the Horror's Hunger? Would safewords and healthy practices render/impose a penalty to the Satiety roll? Could Satiety even be gained this way at all? For clarity, I am not interested in a Beast with a Hunger for Power expressing their hunger through sexual predation.

          In a healthy/safe BDSM relationship, the Dom/me has very little of the actual power in the relationship, because what does and does not happen in a given scene is pretty much down to what their sub is into. Without making a huge BDSM 101 post, the short version is that I would say it's impossible to feed the Hunger for Power this way. Look to the guiding principle in the book: for someone to eat, something gets eaten. If you aren't harming someone, you aren't getting anything out of it.

          That said, there's an unfortunate number of unhealthy BDSM relationships out there, and the more abusive and generally screwed up it gets the closer to feeding you are. Christian 50-Shades-of-terrible-book Grey would be able to feed the Hunger for Power just fine, because his relationship would be like a blood doll without fangs. Obviously every group is comfortable with different things, but for a situation bad enough that I'd consider it feeding-worthy it would be well past the limit of what I'd be comfortable with in most cases.

              Yeah, BDSM may involve power play, but it's just that, playing. When done safely, all participants should be equal partners, just like in any healthy relationship or sexual encounter. If there is a genuine power imbalance, it's probably not healthy and may be abusive. A Tyrant certainly could feed their hunger for power through unhealthy power dynamics in sexual relationships - there are, unfortunately, a lot of abusers who prey on inexperienced people looking to get involved in the BDSM community and don't know the abusive behavior isn't normal. This problem is made worse by things like the above mentioned terrible book depicting abuse and treating it like it's not only normal behavior in the BDSM community, but that it's somehow romantic. But it sounds like you're more interested in a character who practices healthy, responsible BDSM, which involves full consent and safety measures. If it's being done right, I would say it's not going to satisfy a hunger for power.

                                          Well, I'de say it depends on whom the character has found to practise it with.
                                          If your character has found a really disturbed person, who enjoys actually being seriously and violently abused, well beyond the point of normal BDSM, I'de think it could feed you, with a slight penalty or something.
                                          I'de also think that that kind of behavior might be a slippery slope for your characters moral compass. Maybe one day he doesn't give a shit about 'safewords' anymore? "Nothing is 'safe' from the BEAST!! *Commence Eldritch tantrum* "

                                          I think being "ok" with depicting rape and rape-ey circumstances depend largely on how its done. I'm part of a few games in which the PC's often engage in horrific actions, but the thrust of those games are that we're all very well aware the PC's aren't the "good guys" or "the heroes". None of us are under any illusion that those actions are laudable or excuseable. Those games have a sort of "for the greater good" narrative going, which allows us to explore the rationale behind those horrific actions, and how they might concievably be justified, to an abselute lunatic.
                                          Its all about frameing, when depicting that kind of horrific actions. You want to have a reason why the character is acting the way he is, and then explore that reason. Cudos to you, if you can make a downright sympathetic rapist, although I doubt it
                                          That said, you might not wanna do this, if you're playing with somebody who has experienced sexual assault firsthand. Or else, check with that person first.
