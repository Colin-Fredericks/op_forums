    Similarities between Beast and Geist

    01-26-2016, 02:34 PM
    How is Beast: The Primordial different from Geist: The Sin-Eaters?
    Fundamentally, they seem very similar in their Lore. The PCs have a catalytic event (Death/Beast-inspired Nightmare), and are offered a chance to become more than what they are (The Bargain/Devouring). They've always had a connection to the unseen (Ephemera/Primordial Dream). Their 'reawakening' is accompanied by a connection to a powerful entity, connected to archetypes base in human mythology (Geist/Horror). These ephemeral entities push the Sin-Eater/Beast towards goals unique to their archetype, acting as a disparate entity who can at best be described as amoral.
    Anyone can become these, provided they are not already supernaturally tainted by a Major Template.

    The major difference that I see is the Hero pseudo-mechanic (which I may start to incorporate into my other WoD works alongside Chambers), and the fact that Beasts as a whole have more direction than a 'motley Krewe' of Sin-Eaters. Leaving aside that they have differences in ability, I hold that their powers are less evocative in a storytelling game than their themes; I see a marked similarity in these themes, and that is what I am addressing.

    One could say that the above are near-tropes available in all CWoD and CofD lines, though I would posit that these themes are more pronounced (and done so in more similar ways) than in other titles. Changeling and Werewolf are stories about identity, living with a foot in two worlds; they are reflections of each other in many ways, yet different enough that I've even seen people refuse to let them mix. Beast and Geist seem too close together, with a piece of a chronicle-seed floating around in my head that Horrors may just be sufficiently powerful Geists anchored not to one keystone and person, but to many Lairs and Children.
    I'm still very excited to use Beast, and reading it through has re-invigorated my interest in the American supernatural Gary Stu show, Grimm.
    What do you think?

      From what I can tell, both Geist and Beast delve into the human soul, but in two different areas; what happens to it once death comes, and what the effects of fear may do to it. It's even specifically stated (at least in the pre-kickstarter edition) that Beasts don't really have governance over death, and that making a kinship with a Sin Eater opens up those kinds of possibilities for them. Sin Eaters, while utilizing fear, are able to approach things from a variety of different angles, whereas Beasts are more inclined for the terror aspect.

      Furthermore, Sin Eaters tend to have a lot more utility to their powers than Beasts do, at least by default. Ceremonies by and large are able to do things that Nightmares cannot do, generally beneficial things as well as negative, and though Beasts are able to empower other supernaturals they don't have the same capabilities. Manifestations, while maybe not as powerful as Atavisms, likewise have a lot of different utility to them that Sin Eaters can dial up or down depending on what they want to do. Atavisms, meanwhile, while having different levels of power they can utilize are much more situational in terms of benefits others.

      There's also the way that the Geist/Horror interacts with their host that differs. For all intents and purposes, a Beast's Horror is their soul - it shapes their intents and behavior when their satiety goes low, and barring trying to kill/starve it I can't think of a way to degrade relations with one's Horror. Geists, on the other hand, are a separate entity (somewhat), which relations must be kept up with in order to have a functioning relationship. Furthermore, Geists don't need sating to stay in tandem with them - sure, you can do things that'll piss them off, but they don't require as much constant maintenance as a Horror does, nor are they (generally) as dangerous to others if relations with them degrade.

          I wouldn't be opposed to making a Beast whose Horror had some kind of connection to death or oblivion. Fear of ending or Nothingness are each primal archetypes in the collective psyche, and so it makes sense that the Primordial Dream would contain something that hungers for those fears; by proxy creating Beasts that inspire lessons based upon them.

          I agree that the utility of Sin-Eaters would be a tier or two above those of Beasts, partially by route of how Beast was designed. They are very much the "lesser" siblings and amalgams of each of their supernatural kin. I very much enjoy the 'cousin of the Strange' concept, and hope to play with that dynamic more.
          I disagree with your interpretation of the relationship between Beast and Horror (first and foremost that it seems rather plain in what I am reading that there is one Horror for multiple Beasts, and so is not replacing a Devoured soul with its entirety), but admit that with no mechanical expy (like unto Synergy and Discord with one's Geist) there is little to say that the Horror's whispers are much more than a superfluous social/mental pressure.

          It would lay on the Storyteller to decide whether a Horror could take offense to the actions of a single Beast they've connected to. I'll probably marry the concepts of required feeding (Satiation/Vitae) with a little bit more required digging into esoteric mystery to unlock the full potential of your connection to a Horror (by way of Sin-Eaters/Mage).

                          While some of this might be subject to change with a 2e Geist book:

                          I think the difference is most pronounced looking at a simple comparison. Synergy vs. Satiety.

                          Synergy is about two separate beings working together. A high Synergy Sin-Eater represents a Bound and Geist closely aligned in actions. Dropping to being one of the Wretched means the two have become inseparable but completely disconnected as they battle over control of a single body with no cohesion between different states. Maintaining a relatively high Synergy is purely a good thing for the dual-entity to exist.

                          Satiety is simply about keeping a dark hunger within a Beast fed. The connection between Beast and Horror doesn't change as it goes up and down. How well fed the Horror is simple dictates how a Beast interacts with their Hunger. There is no "good" level of Satiety. Do you want to be hungry, but sharp and full of raw power from the drive to hunt? Do you want to try to balance the Hunger to keep it easy to feed but the Horror sleepy and weak? Do you want to sate the Horror for a more subtle form of power in freedom and selective hunting, but risk putting the Horror to sleep and being weakened for it?

                          For all the other differences, I think these traits are highly representative of the core reasons why these end up as very different games.

                              Wait, what? OP, you appear to have a fundamental misunderstanding of the nature of Beasts. Or at least of the nature of the Horror. This is not some external entity with which you have a relationship. It isn't controlling you, and it doesn't talk to you; it's YOU. It's your own monstrous self. Your Id. If your Horror has a mythic identity, the person who remembers said mythic identity is YOU. If you want to, you can constantly be seeing through your Horror's eyes within your Lair. If you want to, you can also ignore your human body and just wander about in your Lair as your Horror by using instant actions to control its dream-body directly. It doesn't direct or control you except in the sense that your own feelings of hunger and thirst direct and control you. You can't make it unhappy except in the sense that you can make yourself miserable by not eating.
