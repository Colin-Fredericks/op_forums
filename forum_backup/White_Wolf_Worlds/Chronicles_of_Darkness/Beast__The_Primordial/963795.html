    Heroes and Conspiracies

    09-05-2016, 01:57 PM
    The book describes Heroes as seeing Hunters as basically canon fodder, while Hunters think that Heroes are twisted versions of what they could become (or akin to slashers so to speak).

    I'm curious to know what the forum thinks of what the major conspiracies might think of Heroes from their own perspectives and directives. For Example--

    Task Force Valkyrie has a standing order to observe
    but not engage with any independent Heroes they encounter.
    Heroes pose too great a risk to equipment and personnel just
    by jumping into a fight they have no way of winning (that the
    hunters see). -Page 204-
    Ashwood Abbey in particular seeks Heroes out to
    find quarries for their legendary hunting parties, treating them
    like the proverbial hound after the fox. -Page 204-
    Members of the Union frequently end up in Hero-inspired mobs. Conversely, mortals
    find their way to the Union or other compacts and conspiracies
    as a hunter after a Hero leads their normal, everyday life into
    the deep, dark places they never knew existed before. -Page 204-
    Thoughts?

      Well, my personal take is that, if a hunter and a hero ever met? The hunter would simply assume that the hero was merely another hunter instead of something different. After all, the overwhelming majority of Hero abilities really aren't anything extrodinary - the flashiest of which maybe a "relic" weapon for killing monsters, or knowing the trick to opening the gateway into the shadow-realm where monsters hide, neither of which are exactly unheard of by hunters. Certainly, heroes are less obviously magical than a human psychic, let alone an Endowment.

      So, I imagine that the major conspiracies would look at Heroes as simply rival conspiracies in the same line of work with little difference. Maybe a bit obsessed with one type of supernatural, but that's pretty common with a number of Conspiracies, like the Cannite Heresy or the one that punches demons with magical tats.


      Task Force Valkyrie: "Bunch of crazy vigilantee thrill seekers. We take up the Vigil to protect American citizens from the nasties that go bump in the night. These guys are in it just for the glory. Like the government would let them get recognition for murdering something that's not supposed to exist. Still, they seem to have a sixth sense for tracking down hives of extra-human criminal activity, making them excellent informants."

      Aegis Kai Doru: "They often claim to have magical talismans and relics that were recovered from the homes of these slain monsters, but all efforts to retrieve these relics for study have ended in failure. Further study is recommended."

      Ascending Ones: "Diplomacy is a key strategy of ours, while these so-called heroes are more interested in fighting. Good for watching your back when shit hits the fan, but not so good to keep the street wars to a minimum."

      Lucifuge: "Most of their targets seem to be living productive lives in the same way we do, using dark powers to protect people. I even met one that called me sister. He was cut down simply for being born different. Watch your back, unless these heroes decide we're part of it."

      Malleus Malificorum: "God helps those who help themselves. These warriors choose to fight against the darkness, driven by dark fears they have trouble bringing up even in confession. Lend a helping hand when possible, and seek to bring them into the Light, for they follow a holy Mission, even if they know it not."

      Cherion Group: "Excerpt from the Cherion Employee Handbook: Don't be a hero. Killing the specimen costs thousands of dollars, while a single live specimen can help save hundreds of lives. As well as earning a nice bonus."

                  I think you should draw a line between the theological elements of the AKD and practical information.

                  Their secret history and things like that should be buried under layers of secrecy and only given to members who are "worthy". But practical information like which Relic sword works best against a Vampire, or which monsters produce usable Relics and which ones produce things that only look like a Relic should be commonly available to the front line Hunters.

                  Knowing that Hero's don't produce Relics is fairly basic information and, well, Conspiracy Tier hunters should have more information than and play fundamentally differently than Cell Tier Hunters. I mean, if they play the same what's the point of having two different types of Hunter? Even more importantly my personal interpretation of the AKD is strongly flavoured by Indiana Jones and The Liberian: Ancient conspiracies, occult relics, archaeologists. It's all there - and it's even the first sample Hunter in the core book.

                  So to me one of the core reasons the AKD exists is so your PCs can be trained in enough occult archaeology to solve the clues left behind by whatever ancient order has hidden this story's McGuffin. But if the AKD can provide it's members with enough knowledge to solve clues left behind by an long dead order then surely they can also give you basic information like Hero's "Relics" are just ordinary weapons.
