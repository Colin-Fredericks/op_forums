    After reading through the full Family writeups, I've decided that my Slenderman Beast is definitely an Eshmaki, not an Ugallu.

    EDIT: Isaac is an Eshmaki Nemesis. His Soul has declared that the forest is not the domain of man, and anyone who trespasses on it cannot truly return from it until they have been punished for their transgressions. People who wander off the path in his territory find themselves lost, the way back having disappeared behind them. Even if they manage to physically leave the forest, he ensures that the places they once regarded as safe become labyrinthine and unfamiliar. Doors no longer lead where they should, paths turn back on themselves, and their memories of their movements become obscured. And then, as they desperately attempt to trace their own passage, they become aware of a figure in the background, a silhouette thin enough to be mistaken for the shadow of a dead tree, only noticeable in retrospect. Watching. Only when they surrender to the directionless expanse of the forest is his Soul sated.

                                I think that the Primordial Dream offers a lot of opportunity to take campaigns beyond satisfying individual hungers.

                                EDIT: I haven't fully read the crossover material, but your view seems a little pessimistic, ata. You can essentially draw on any of the themes of the other game lines, any of their goals, and come up with a way to incorporate Beasts. Does the BtP corebook not flesh this out enough? Even so, with a little creativity and ingenuity I'd imagine the possibilities are pretty limitless, which to me is a very exciting idea.

                                The Beasts think they are the original monsters. What happens when this clashes with the views of the other splats? nWoD was always about "this is one take on how things are, and this is another," and BtP offers a personification of that conflict (dialectic?). What your Beast "is" is completely up to the player. They themselves gets to decide what kind of monster they are in a much more open-ended way than any of the other splats IMO.
