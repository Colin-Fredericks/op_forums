    I don't understand why there's such a strong resistance to believing that the Atavism does exactly what it says.

    You can do whatever you want, and it will explicitly be forgiven. The victim will explicitly have a good impression of you afterwards. All of this is spelled out in the Atavism. It's not complicated.

    Then it doesn't feel like you're trying very hard.

    First, the book has some of the worst editing of any CoD book I've read, and I've read all of them. It contradicts itself constantly, and this is especially true with Alien Allure where the mechanics do not support the claim that 'all is forgiven' in the Normal effect. You don't even get to ignore any social penalties unless you spent a point of Satiety, and if we're going to continue to use Vampire as precedent, egregious past actions are measured through penalties to your social roll. Difficulty of what you are asking for is measured by your doors. The only thing Alien Allure does is force the other party to hear you out, instead of stone walling with Hostile. Oh, right. It also makes you prettier and appear more impressive than you actually are.

    Players do not like when a form of social manipulation exists that does not have a potential resistance, and affects how their character feels about someone or something (potentially another player. For those of us who ST large moderated chat games? Almost always another player.) Majesty 1 toes this line. The saving grace is that other vampires can buck it, and that all of the following powers have rolls that can be contested and resisted. Forcing a feeling or conclusion onto a player without them having a say or a roll is bad design space.

    Happily, Alien Allure's mechanics don't support the silly flowery language added at the end of the sentence. All it does is force the person to hear you out. Impressions don't give you a positive bonus to your actual social roll, they don't lower the number of doors you have to open, and it doesn't mind control the person into doing whatever you want.

    So, for all of those reasons, I don't understand why we're so hellbent on making an Atavism bend over backwards into negative design space over a single sentence that doesn't reflect the mechanics in a terribly flawed book.
