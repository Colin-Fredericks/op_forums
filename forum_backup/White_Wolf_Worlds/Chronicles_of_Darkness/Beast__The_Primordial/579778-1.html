                            Mother's Kiss: I might have gone hyperbolic with this one, but let's just say that buffs usually don't go as far as "Here's a buff to your VERY EXISTENCE". They usually stay in a nice little box, such as Combat (D&D Bards and Cahalith) or Stealth, or Social, or whatever. It's not a buff that states that Obfuscate 1 is now perfect invisibility and the next turn to get a +2L to your attack just because a Beast blew a kiss.

                            I think the main of my concern is not with each individual powers, but from the fact that ALL Beasts have them built in, unlike Vampires or Mages or whatever you're throwing at me. I never argued that You Are Meat is too strong or that a Makara should not be a giant fish, I argued that innate abilities of a whole splat basically swat away a lot of powers that other people paid for in XP or built-in with their characters.

                            I played a Witch in Pathfinder, basically a buff character. I played an Aes Promethean. I played buff-mages in a lot of LARP, but I've never had such a wrong impression of a buff skill as Mother's Kiss. Mind, maybe it's because it seems so trivial for a Beast, whereas a Bard needs to sing for turns on end and a Mage needs to cast and bring forth arcane knowledge and Prometheans need to surge the Pyros through their very being, transmuting chaos in order. A Beast "blows a kiss" and they have a better result.

                            Take any movie or cut-scenes from a video game. If the character is faced with a terrible opponent and in the shadows you see another character simply snap his fingers and then the main character defeats his opponent in a few strikes before we see the shadowy figure grin, who has the spotlight? The audience is led to believe that this success had nothing to do with the main character. That is the way I feel about this power. It doesn't turn the protagonist's sword ablaze. It doesn't imbue him with courage. It doesn't make him see the weak spot in his opponent's defense. It simply...Fate victory because reasons and family and stuff.

                            The comment about Mage cosmology overwriting everything else is this really old and tired idea that doesn't have any basis in reality. The idea that Beasts believe in a thing called the Dark Mother and have some kind of sympathetic connection to other monsters in no way invalidates any other game line.
                            You're right. If we forget that their mythos basically states that Werewolves and Prometheans are brothers, or that the Judges are in fact the sons and daughters of Tiamat. Same goes for Father Wolf, The Principle, The Exarchs, the whole underworld, the entirety of the Shadow, all the Gentries and Slashers (but not Hunters, because they're just humans, though Cursed humans are descendants).

                            Going from there the players either accept that the Dark Mother is real and it overrides their own Mythos, or they flip the bird to the Beast and no one talks about it anymore (which is not better in my oppinion).

                            Family Resemblance: It goes beyond Auspex. Auspex tells you if he is or not a Super. FR tells you what monster he is and what he's good at (with an exceptional) and is totally passive. Auspex is once per scene, a single question, binary answer, active, resistable...and is not innate.


                            Final Disclaimer (again): I certainly didn't feel threatened or aggressed in any way in the replies. Sure, some had some punch to them or some sass, but I did too at some points (mainly using hyperboles) and as such think that it's only fair I'm answered in the same way. I'll try and stay in a civil state of communication for as long as possible, and leave if I feel like this discussion is going nowhere fast, or getting too personnal. I am not perfect, we all argue about stuff, and I may be biased toward not liking the splat.

                            Then again a lot of people are biased towards liking it.

                                        I don't have a direct answer for you, there. I imagine it has to do with the artificial nature of the God-Machine and its creation. If you'll notice, a theme in the metaphysics of the game (particularly in the Neolithic Mage setting that's upcoming) it seems like civilization and people and the state they're in affects the Supernatural. Father Wolf seems to grow weaker and more haggard as Humanity moves away from our previous hunting lifestyle to an agricultural one. I imagine the move into civilization affected people's relationship with the Primordial Dream and the Lessons.

                                        I mean, there's also the bit about how the Primordial Dream is, well, primordial and seems diametrically imposed to the incredibly complex systems of human civilization, as it's a primal thing that touches us in the lizard brain and all that. The God-Machine is representative of incredibly complex systems that we create and consequently lose control of, because we know longer understand them. I'm certain someone else can phrase it better, but the sort of concept of the Primordial Dream is based firmly in primal, basic, essentially human things, and the God-Machine seems in many ways to be an opposing force to that.

                                        I imagine that's something they're tapping into with Demons not being kin. Honestly, it sets them up with a sort of unique hook that makes them more interesting when engaged in a Beast game than if they were just considered another source of Kin, and being that they have the connection to the God-Machine, it just makes sense that of all the splats you'd pick to be the exception, it'd be Demons.


                                        All that said, I'll say I'm not a huge fan of Beast or its crossover, but I think this reason is a very silly one to be critical of it for, because it's based on the same kind of misconceptions that have been bandied about on other websites about Mage, and it's like a red herring of criticism when there are other much more legitimate critiques to discuss. So that's where I'm coming from with this. I think it's totally fair to not like Beasts for crossover, but the reasons you're bringing to the table seem pretty silly.
