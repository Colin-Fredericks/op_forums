    So it's been a month plus change since this first gave me a headache, but I feel I can't have mentioned that headache without eventually commenting on it, so:

    1) Thank you, by the way, for the default female pronoun. That's probably going to become the mainstay here soon. Might become the mainstay all over the board of life, but that's a different subject, but odds are my online self is going to just be female.

    2) So. Like. Here's the thing about any rewrite about Beast:

    With possibly one exception, and that's one I bat back and forth on a lot, and frequently bat with Cinder on, the main thing to understand about Beast is that....all the materials for making a great Beast are there. You don't have to change much about the fundamental elements of Beast. What needs changing is the presentation and the order of operations. Beast is a game about Building Your Lair (your presence in the psycho-spiritual landscape of humanity in a meaningful way) which you do by sating your Hunger that build your rumors and whispers, bind the Chambers of tragedy and the particular mythos of your fellows through Kinship to your image, and fight off the Heroes (and Insatiable) who challenge your transcendent image (here read as "personal values and means of self-identification as communicated in the medium of horror").

    Lair=Hunger+Chambers+Kinship+Opposition. That's the game in a nutshell, and it's Darkly Scion With Weirdness and Transmittable Mutability is damnedably strong on it's own.

    Putting it bluntly and shortly, the main failing of Beast right now is that Matthew McFarland fucked up* taking this really simple formula and delivering in a really cool, cohesive, and positively controversial way. The mice will play whatever they want when the cat was away, and in this case the cat was clearly away at a bunch of different houses.

    Beast has everything it needs to be awesome. It just needs to be lined up and presented the right way, with the correct unity of vision on the material, to be properly seen. Supplements from there just need to build outward in a way that is progressingly fun to play, terrifying to consider, and weird to comprehend.

    I'll leave the "exception" unexplained as an interesting batting experiment.

    *Okay, maybe not fucked up, I think Matt did enough good in fixing things up and directing it, and the writers inserted enough diverse and interesting text(even to it's detriment) that there's a clear reason Beast has it's fanbase. But for those of us who got into Beast, once we got away from the feeling that was right, that spoke to us, it's not hard to see how the text is a fairly sloppy stitched together animal with weeping wounds.

        Arcane speaks truth.

        Thinking on what I wrote, the two major rules changes I would make are "Oracles are in the core" and "there's a social z-axis."

        For the first, I'd have it explicit a Hero is a berserk Oracle. They haven't all gone out, probably not the majority of Oracles are Heroes. However, society encourages Oracles to become Heroes and helps Heroes get stuck in an emotional rut. What really makes a Hero a Hero isn't some inborn impulse to hunt Beasts, but believing that their own Dream sense is telling them to destroy it rather than study it. An Oracle who knows, and more importantly accepts, what her power is telling her can still turn on Beasts, but that's because the Beasts in question pissed her off. More than a few of the most dangerous Heroes in the world are actually Oracles who work as a team with hunters, and regularly seek and destroy Beasts who they feel are polluting the Dream with toxic nightmares of abuse and not controlling themselves.

        For the second, that gives both a social aspect to the whole thing and also puts the Beast Player's Guide social groups in the core, which is a good thing.

            Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
            Lair=Hunger+Chambers+Kinship+Opposition. That's the game in a nutshell, and it's Darkly Scion With Weirdness and Transmittable Mutability is damnedably strong on it's own.
            Arc's whole post is a gem, but I'll quote this part since I feel to elaborate a little, because why not.

            This formula here is on point and it's pretty much the key to unlock the magic. As Arc said, that's the game in a nutshell, which allows for a huge range of possibilities & stories. A range, I dare to say, which belongs to Beast first and foremost and other games don't cover in the same way.

            (Which is alright, Beast can't do as well things other gamelines do easily and each one should have its strengths. To think otherwise...is not the best thing)

            Beast, with that in mind, just is this weird game about fears, hopes, actions, and consequences, with its own gnarly language to talk about all of those. No matter the scale of the story it's always deeply intimate.

            You can have a sorta-Pulp chronicle where you travel across the world looking for artifacts and tracks of the Dark Mother.

            You can have a game where the monsters from a book start to haunt the town because some kids opened it on Halloween night and the Brood must clean up the mess.

            You can have the kind of crossover game which requires my weight in books, with allies & foes from all gamelines woven together into the Beast's legend.

            You can have a game of mythical resonance, with heroes and horrors who fight one against each other, each strike reverberating in the Astral.

            You can have a Tier 4 game where old gods awaken, the Dark Mother opens the gates, and Gaia stirs in her slumber.

            And you can have your Tier 0 game, where flawed individuals fight to tell their own story, one day at a time. With blood, pain, and no easy answers.

            The only thing we need is to take steps to see that more clearly. Supplement after supplement, with a strong guiding vision behind. There's lots of stuff we need to be able to see better. The vast world connected through Lairs, the mysteries of the Dark Mother & the Primordial condition, the kind of stories one can tell, the actual immensity of the Begotten's crooked family tree...the list goes on.

            Also, Beast is the Astral gameline. The Begotten are living stories and have a role in our world no other Astral-related phenomenon has. They're the children of Fear. Let's see how cool that can be, let's explore the facets of that universe, let's have astral gods and Dreamborn show up to try and have their role in the story, whether the Begotten would like it or not.

            The supplements after the core are doing a great job. Conquering Heroes and the Player's Guide were awesome, and I say this with renewed vigor because I just reread them. The Geryo connection in Shunned by the Moon is cool as fuck and ripe with story fodder. The Dark Eras 2 Beast sections are solid, useful, and good.

            The fire is there and it's alive. It just needs to be presented better, so it can breathe, grow, and burn brighter. Hopefully we'll get there.

            God, I love this game. This dark mixture of mythology, psychology, empathy, hunger, folklore, acceptance, conflict, growth, mistakes, regrets, and aspirations.

            I know this reply is kinda superfluous, but I was in the right mood for it.

            P.S. That one exception does need some batting, because it's an important element. But, by Nightmare Mom, I'm not giving up on that. Also, Arc knows what she says and I'm thankful I get to chat with her about Beast.

                                Your Mother needs you.

                                Beyond the abuser thing, one thing that's struck me about Beast is its lack of an "epic struggle" concept. Almost every other "splat" has an epic side to it along with the individual, day-to-day struggles.

                                The Forsaken are the inheritors of Father Wolf, forced to take up his task after killing him for the good of reality, even against Lovecraftian nightmares like the Idigam.

                                Mages have the task of closing the Abyss and Awakening the whole of humanity.

                                Prometheans spend their existence struggling to becoming something that fits into a Natural Order that constantly rejects them.

                                Changelings live with the constant fear that the True Fae, the monsters who abducted, abused and enslaved them will drag them back to their special hells in Arcadia.

                                Hunters are people realizing that they live in a world of darkness and monsters.

                                Sin-eaters ultimately want to reboot the Afterlife.

                                Demons are trying avoid/resist the God who is a Machine.

                                Mummies are torn between the screaming demands or otherworldly Judges as they try to understand who they were and wish to be.


                                Even Vampires who are in some ways a lot like Beasts in terms of feeding a demanding Hunger, face a kind of epic struggle in terms of the vampire vs. The System. Every vampire from the newest Neonate to the most ancient of Elders is forced by Kindred society to fit into places that that society defines, to play a game whose rules were set (and rigged against you) millennia ago.

                                With Beasts... they need to feed their Hungers. For Ruin. For Prey For other things. They use Nightmares and Atavisms. They build their Lairs. So they can better feed their Hungers. Some grow potent enough to became their Legends Incarnate... so they can feed their Hungers. And... that seems to be it. Sure Heroes crop up and the Insatiable are problematic, to say the least. Others in the world aren't necessarily friendly, but over all there doesn't seem to be much of a calling for Beasts as a whole beyond, than get fed, but not too well fed.

                                Looked at in some ways, Beast is basically the "darker, edgier" version of Monsters, Inc. with the PCs being a bunch of Sullys going out to make sure their personal "power plants" don't run out of juice.

                                So let's consider changing that up a little.


                                Because the time is coming...

                                We have Lilith, the Dark Mother. Except that Lilith seems mostly irrelevant. Father Wolf casts a much longer shadow over the Forsaken than Lilith does over the Begotten despite him being dead for thousands of years. So, Lilith, the Dark Mother. If there's a Mother, is there a Father. And for my purposes the answer is Yes. Oh yes. And things are so much worse than you might think.

                                ...when He will wake up.

                                So, let's spin the myth a little.
                                '

                                Two They were. He and She.

                                They made the world or found the world or were the world.

                                They loved and made and played

                                Until the Machine Thing came from Elsewhere.

                                It tried to take their world for a Purpose of its own.

                                They fought It. They damaged it but He was wounded.

                                Wounded. Poisoned. Maddened. He fell.

                                Into an Abyss. He was the Abyss.

                                She could not help Him and had to flee.

                                To retreat into the Great Priomordial Dream.

                                The Machine forgot Her and Him. Forgot its Purpose.

                                The Poison did not kill Him.

                                And He dreamed. Terrible Things.

                                Time passed. She watched the world change.

                                She watched the monkeys lose their fur and grow their minds.

                                And She loved them in her way.

                                And She feared for them. Feared for Her world.

                                Because He slept, but only slept.

                                In His dreams He came to Her and they made...

                                children together. And She loved the children,

                                Horrors though they were.

                                But She did not sleep. Could not rest

                                Even while the addled Machine did its Purposeless work

                                She whispered to some of the Humans, letting them see Beyond

                                        I feel like the "epicifying" of Beast misses a few things-partly in how local all of the games actually are how that epic angle misses that, and also Not All Mages Care About the Abyss(Arguably, just the Guardians and the Mysterium make it a Big Ass Deal, everyone else is like "Yeah, okay, but EXARCHS"-

                                        But mostly it also does hit on something that is true about Beast....even if it doesn't need all of ^that.

                                        Because Again, Beast is a Darkly Scion game. It's a game about Building Your Legend, which by the way eventually births a new world as that Lair grows. The Astral is the place where you will actually find your mythologies come to life, and is thus loaded with your Sets and your Odins and your Tezcatlipocas (although archetypalism and formalism remains king, so it's confronting Sage and Mountain and hey did anyone else realize that Beast is the second game to be most keenly interested in Pangaeans in this way?)-the game is loaded with Astral Gods and Demons to confront and get involved in. This is a game where it is appropriate to say you become a deity at it's higher levels, and that's not accounting for the ways Inheritances are psychospiritually huge ass deals.

                                        It's mostly self-directed, mind-the game doesn't implore you pit yourself against anything directly in the way the other games do-but on the other hand, it really does, because it basically asks you to become bigger than the world itself, and even gives you the tools for it. Beast is a game where as you rise, you should feel it, can even see it-all the world, turning in your direction. Beast is the game where you get to answer the question "I ask of thee!- Art thou humanity?" with "Nay! I am the world-the world inside the gourd." It's a game of chaoskampf against forces all interested in building their own worlds, and it absolutely ratchets up to that point.

                                        Now a lot more of that can be shined up and bought to the forefront in the text and even supported mechanically (Looking at you, supplements), but it is there. It just has to actually be understood that way.

                                        But no, Typhon is fine on his throne.
