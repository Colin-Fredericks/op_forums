    Beast musings.

    06-03-2015, 02:37 PM
    Can a Nightmare form for something that does not exist?

    See, I was thinking. The Beast preview is out, and I've been enjoying it, but it left me with this unpleasant aftertaste in a few ways.

    1: It felt like the 1st Edition of nWoD Mage. Too many answers. Too many objective statements. Not enough information presented as an IC perspective.

    2: The Beast crossover mechanics felt somewhat... insulting I guess? It felt like Beast was taking the role of the smarter, more socially adept older sibling. This might be okay in a Beast only game, but speaking for myself at least, it would certainly rub me the wrong way as the player of a different splat.

    3: It felt like Beast was pulling in two directions. They are creatures of Legend (literally even. Hah). They are the creatures of our darkest Nightmares. But the are also the incomprehensible progenitor. One of these three felt really boring to me. Here's a hint. It's the one that isn't tied to a Beast mechanics.

    So, just for the hell of it, I'm posting my musings on the subject. 'Cause why not?



    What came first, Fenris, or Fenris-Ur? Humans knew. On some level, they knew. The Wolf was out there. It was mighty, It was implacable. It could not be stopped, could not be killed, could not even be beaten. So we turned to dreams.We created Fenris. We chained him, buried him, and tried to forget him. Tried. It's the way we deal with things. Lock them up and dream them safe and secure. Then we tell stories. And in the stories, Heroes win. The beasts are defeated, and humanity is saved. And these stories build and develop, and form patterns and take on lives of their own. By now, the fear that motivated them is hardly even noticeable on the subconscious level. It's there all right, but it's real cause is long forgotten.

    And then time passes.We slowly become aware of other things. There are hints, here and there, scattered around the periphery of our existence of the other. Vampires, Werewolves, Mages. The Lost, the Created, even the Dead and Not-Quite-Dead. They're all out there, hidden. Waiting, just like it was in the old days. So our mind searches for answers. has been searching for answers. And there, nestled away in the darkest recesses, it finds the Wolf. The Thing in the Darkness. The Presence Above, The Unstoppable Force. The Dreaming Deeps. The Unspeakable Horror. All built long, long ago to explain why the world was as it was. It finds the Beast. And the Beast is waiting.



    What did I hope to express with that? I guess it's that Beast clicked the most for me when it was dealing with their involvement with the patterns of myth. When it didn't really explain anything. So, to cut from the purple prose. In this view, Beasts aren't really the progenitor monster. They are what we imagine the progenitor monster as being, so we don't need to deal with what they truly were/are. Are they still "related" to all the other splats? Sure! Humans want answers. They want things to follow patterns and have explanations. It just makes sense that they want there to be a point of descent. So while Beasts aren't actually the aforementioned point of descent. they still have the connections, and the connections are very real.

    In this view, the Beast is not:

    1: The Missing Link. The Dark Mother may or may not be real, but it didn't make the Beast's, at least not directly. That would be too convenient, and in my experience, in a good WoD game, convenience is only for stories or lies.

    2: The Elder-Cousin. After all, they aren't from the Dark Mother. And honestly, they have no idea if anyone else is either. That doesn't mean they don't think they are of course. Because that's what they are in the stories, and they are, after all: the monsters in the stories.

    In this view, the Beast is:

    1: The Human Explanation. It's not like Beasts lose their crossover mechanics, But Beasts don't have said mechanics because they came first, they have them because that;s what they story said they should have. What the Nightmare demanded they be capable of.

    2: Our Own Creation. We made the Beasts. To explain and label our fears. To be the Monster in the Story. Sure, they weren't really the real monster, but they were everything we feared the real monster was.

    3: Always and Ultimately Human. After all, we made them. They are a product of the Temenos, of the Primordial Dream. They are how we understand our deepest, oldest fears, as well as how we shape and understand our new terrors. And as such, they are ultimately of us. It is in this central core of humanity that their enmity from the God Machine arises. We can fear falling, but not gravity. We can fear the Gears, but not the Infrastructure.


    Does this solve all my problems? Not really. But it feels a bit cooler. It answers fewer questions. And it feels like (at least to me) it makes Beast's connections with other splats a celebration of them rather then a somewhat demeaning pat on the back.

    It's also probably incredibly conceited, but this is the internet, and I'm posting my opinion, so that's bound to happen to some level or another. Thoughts? Concerns? Explanations of how I'm wrong? All are appreciated! Particularly that last one actually.

                                  Beasts don't have facts. They have beliefs. What they can observe is an ability to suss out certain supernaturals. What they can observe is a special connection with some beings, via the idea of Kinship and powers they can develop from it. What they can observe is that they know there's something in the Primordial Dream which connects to Beasts, and they believe these sympathetic connections between themselves and monsters may have something to do with that something in the Primordial Dream.

                                  They believe that this something can be called the Dark Mother, and they believe that this connection with other monsters is a sign that they are all 'related.' however distantly. It's super important to keep in mind the difference between literal and conceptual relation, and that we're dealing with primordial creatures of fear which seem to come from a part of the collective unconsciousness in the nWoD. The supernatural is a lot more complicated than literal lines of descent, and we're getting beliefs here, not facts.
