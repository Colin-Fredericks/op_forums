    Packs of Same-Species of Begotten

    10-22-2022, 05:05 AM
    It seems I have not found any discussion (so far) about a Family, or at least a Pack, of the same species of Beasts from Beast the Primordial that would hunt and feed together aside from the Supernaturals outside of the splatbook. I find that idea very intriguing. We see other Supernaturals like so many Vampires together in Clans, myriads of Changelings in Courts, and Werewolves with their Packs, for example. You could have several players get together at the table playing Werewolves in Werewolf the Forsaken and Vampires in Vampire the Requiem and even if they all come from different tribes and clans, they are still the same species respectively. I think the problem is that when players get together to play Beast, they all want to play different things. Nothing wrong with that at all and no need to force them to play the same things. I think it's fun to see different Beasts playing together. I suppose the same could be said about Changelings with hundreds of kiths, but they are fairies, which counts as a species of Supernatural, doesn't it? With crossover (or even the Contagion Chronicles,) you could mash-up all the splatbooks and that diversifies them even further. But what I'm trying to say is that there should be at least worth a mention of large packs of same-species of Beasts running around somewhere. I think it would be much more realistic. After all, if all the other Supernaturals like Vampires and Werewolves came from the Dark Mother, then why would each Beast in Beast the Primordial have no same-species pack of their very own aside from a Family of diverse Beasts together? Imagine a pack of Sea Monsters for example, with their own 5 tribes of sub-species of the same kind with 5 different factions and 5s of whatever. That makes Beast able to potentially come up with sub-splatbooks in a way for each monster.

          Originally posted by OvercastGhost View Post
          But what I'm trying to say is that there should be at least worth a mention of large packs of same-species of Beasts running around somewhere.
          I must confess some confusion over this statement, because one of the corebook's sample settings (Vancouver) literally opens its history with discussion of a vendetta between two Families, but also I think it's important to define "large."

          Beasts are rare enough that the line explicitly states they're outnumbered by mages, and part of the reason Mage 2e made it plain that the Orders include Sleepers and Sleepwalkers in their organizational structure is to allow their being global conspiracies with substantial reach to make sense without there being millions of reality-warping wizards around every street corner. Hives tend to concentrate Beasts the same as any other sort of focusing circumstance gathers monsters who favor it, but the impression is that thirty to fifty Beasts in a large population center is about right for urban-legend monsters, and not all of them will necessarily get along long-term.

          Beasts' Horrors tend to be individuals — Family is a sorting category more than a lineage, and its interpersonal effects on Beasts are comparatively minor in terms of mechanics — but places with certain Lair Traits will tend to attract Beasts who share those Lair Traits, and Family is a fairly common predictor of those Lair Traits. A place by the sea will get a lot of people who are scared of the ocean to attract or create Leviathans, a place with disgusting or unsettling stimuli will see its share of Namtaru, and so on. Hunger and personal circumstances will affect whether those individuals can share turf or want to keep it for themselves, Beasts feeding normally are likely to be doing something some people would consider contentious, and Thicker Than Water explicitly doesn't undo bad impressions once you've made them.

          I think it would be much more realistic. After all, if all the other Supernaturals like Vampires and Werewolves came from the Dark Mother, then why would each Beast in Beast the Primordial have no same-species pack of their very own aside from a Family of diverse Beasts together? Imagine a pack of Sea Monsters for example, with their own 5 tribes of sub-species of the same kind with 5 different factions and 5s of whatever. That makes Beast able to potentially come up with sub-splatbooks in a way for each monster.
          The trouble is that a brood is an interpersonal bond between individual monsters who are each effectively the equivalent of a pack totem in terms of spiritual role, and the way brood Lairs work means the Horrors can sort of blend into each other through shared environment. A bunch of different Giants hanging out together will gradually begin to resemble one another just the same as a Lurker-and-Raptor crew starts to get wing-y and sneak-y, but there's nothing particularly special about that besides the debatable novelty of happening to have gotten a bunch of different Beasts with the same Family in the same places with personalities compatible enough for them to collaborate.

          Beasts are sorting machines for monsters who are also trying to self-determine by association, and while some of them might lean into figuring out what the archetypes of their Family mean for them, by no means will that be particularly common compared to figuring out their general place in the world.

              I never figured out an in setting reason for it, but I did try this out once. One of the broods called itself the Dragonflight. It had a member of each of the five major families, each representing an archetypal dragon. Ie, the Ugallu was a Wyvern, the Makara was a sea serpent/lung style. And honestly, it felt wasted in NPCs. I think it wouldve been really interesting to explore if I had players taking up the roles and defining themselves as dragons. Itdve been super cool to see how players wouldve designed their lair and grown their legends. As NPCs, the Dragonflight represented the old ans established money of the Metropolis, as I wanted that Smaug quality, an ancient and wealthy wyrm.

              Slightly less cool but in the same vein, I made another one-type Brood that I called the Jotun. Each was a different sort of elemental giant. Storm giant, fire giant, ice giant, mountain giant, and sea giant. Unlike the Dragonflight, the Jotuns were all newly Devoured, and thus starter characters. Each had Looming Presence + one other Atavism to define them. That one was.. interesting. I always forget that little whelpling beasts only get the two atavisms.

              It was actually very fun to do one-"race" broods. It does become tricky to explain it in setting though. For my Dragonflight, it was an ecclectic brood that joined together due to aesthetic and mythological similarity. The Jotun, however, were formed because the Horrors that made them up all "escaped the Mother's Land" at the same time.

              Of course... now, in retrospect, I probably would remake the Jotuns as Insatiables. Rather than being plucky rebels trying to overthrow a long established tyranny, it would now by apocalyptic monsters of chaos trying to overthrow an established tyranny.
