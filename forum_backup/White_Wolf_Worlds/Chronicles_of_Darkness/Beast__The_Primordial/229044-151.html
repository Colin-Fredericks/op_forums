    Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
    The bit on Hercules in the Heroes section made me laugh out loud. Listing "having many children from his heterosexual relationships" among his litany of 'crimes.'

    There's a definite current of "Heroes represent th heteronormative patriarchy!" without much mind paid to the fact that such an assumption makes oppressed identities out to be flesh-eating incarnate nightmares.
    This was pretty much my view until a few hours ago. Something just clicked for me.

    Beasts aren't being mapped to "The Other." Think about it. They're predatory, have incredible power that is inherited from ancient times, have connections to nearly every group of shadowy monsters in the WOD, and their actions generate monsters personified by the worst of humanity.

    They're the 1%. The deck is stacked in their favor from the start and they still don't feel like its enough. Actions against them are treated as irrational and villainous no matter how justified they may be.

    Heroes aren't meant to be seen as oppressors, they're the products of a corrupt system. They're the PIC/Tea Party/etc. While they have the power to harm Beasts, they're largely too unstable and disorganized to do so. Meanwhile the masses are either blind to the true nature of their predators or too scared/overwhelmed to make any serious moves against them.

    Its a bit too depressing for me, but it makes a lot more sense in this light. Also explains why Unchained and Begotten don't get along. Unchained are revolutionaries.

                            Heroes are deeply unstable people who, upon having a nightmare about a monster, decided that the monster was both real, and had to die just because it was a monster.

                            Meaning the Average Hero will be hunting Beasts regardless of if a Beast is hurting people.

                            since the nightmares are otherwise no more harmful than regular nightmares, and nightmares happen most often when the beast is hungry, then a beast is most likely to create a hero when he is not feeding enough.

                            Meaning when he is doing the least harm.

                            Compare this to the description of heroes, which notes that 1: Repeated exposure to the Supernatural does not usually lower Integrity low enough for someone to be able to become a hero, and two that Bullies and Abusers are both in the category of people who are likely to have the requisite low integrity to become Heroes.

                            Two people have urges that are negative and harmful. One of them is aware of the nature of the urges, can try to control them, to limit the damage he causes, and ultimately try and direct his damage towards positive ends. The other is either gleefully unaware of the harm his urges cause or just doen'st care because he's so important.

                            A Beast could be either of those people. a Hero is always the second person. That's what I'm getting from it.

                            The fact that the crossover section mentions that hunters are likely to compare Heroes to Slashers(and if the Slasher Morality Rules carry over to Integrity, then they both have to be at 4 to become what they are, it's not a stretch) tells me that Hunters, whatever they were, are not good people. At best, they are Byronic Heroes-deeply, deeply flawed individuals doomed to a terrible fate.

                            And finally: "You're a monster, you hurt people" is a good reason to want to kill a beast. sometimes, it might even be both completely true and the beast in question is gleeful about it. but That's not the reason the Hero is hunting the Beast, even if that's what he's telling himself and others. He's hunting the Beast because the Beast is a Beast and therefore deserves to die, and he, the Hero, is the one who deserves to kill them.

                            Just becuase a rationalization is a valid reason in it's own right does not mean it's not a rationalization. A serial Killer who rationalizes his killings by only victimizing criminals is still a serial killer. Especial when not every crime is worthy of death.
