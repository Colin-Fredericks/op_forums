    Too address the points of both Shadowflame and Xenu's Paradox have raised.

    On Xenu's point about being able to play morally questionable (or repugnant) psychopathic characters -

    I think this is a pretty interesting viewpoint. Its the viewpoint that brought us Slasher for example. Which is one of my favourite splat books for the NWoD.

    Its also a viewpoint that the 1st Edition NWoD did not really support all that well as a PC option. A lot of Supernatural morality traits were basically just ratified takes on Morality (a system which itself was pretty damn flawed and modelling anything resembling real world morality). There were options that could get you a more monstrous PC, playing Brood in Vampire or Pure in Werewolf for example. However, these were options fleshed out later on in the gamelines lives when the need to diversify and expand the antagonists of the respective lines became a priority.

    One of the real strengths of 2nd Edition NWoD is that its increasingly moving away from the idea of a default morality for everyone. Integrity for humans it outright a measure of stability rather then morality. The new take on Humanity is all about mimicking human behavior more then its about being "moral". Likewise, the bane system in Vamp 2nd Ed allows you to be pretty monstrous and not devolve into an unplayable monster. Demon goes the whole hog and just removes a hierarchy of breaking points all together and has Integrity style breaking points for acting "out of character" for whoever your cover identity is. Its utterly subjective and that in my opinion is a very good thing.

    Beast seems to be going the whole hog as well. Its "morality" stat is all about how well fed you are. I'm hoping that a Beast can be exactly as moral or monstrous as they choose to be and are not punished for either choice. Some beasts might be kind protectors of small communities. Others might be the monster in the woods that snatches away children. Both are valid monster story archetypes after all.

    On Shadowflame's point about Beasts wanting to fit in, are misunderstood or just want to be left alone -

    I think its fine that an individual Beast character can have these motivations. I don't think its a default presumption of Beast that either is the case.

    From all this talk of Family I get the impression Beast is about relationships. Particularly familial relationships. While this could be seen as an aspect of "fitting in" I can't say its the most prominent take on the material I've seen.

    Likewise, all the talk of Heroes makes me think there is an obvious element of persecution. However, we have to remember that Beasts create Heroes, they create their own worst enemies. Its in their nature. Maybe we could see this as a metaphor for a self destructive or self hating aspect. Personally I see it as more tying into the narrative idea of Beasts. Every monster in every story is always challenged/defeated by a Hero, usually a Hero who has had to come deal with the monster because of the monsters actions. We know that Beast is subverting this narrative trope but that does not make the central idea that the monster "created" the Hero any less true.

    One thing I think we can say about Beasts is that they are "monsters" and as such are a varied bunch. Some are friendly (see "good monsters" in Japanese mythology), some can be bartered or parlayed with (the Djinn of middle-eastern folklore), some can be avoided (every story about a monster not seeing you if you don't look at it) and some come to end the world as people know it (Smaug burning down Laketown).

    For my part I've made the mistake of reading in what I WANT a game to be rather then what a game IS before. It made me waste money getting a copy of said game and ultimately made me have a very, very bad experience running said game. Followed by a continued love/hate relationship with said game. So I just wanted to hand down the advice of not reading too much into the tidbits of info we have on Beast so far and presuming what type of character does and does not work right now. Might harm your enjoyment of the game in the long run.

            From all I've seen and heard and read about Beast, I find myself feeling potential but uncertain if it's been/being realized yet. I don't know if what I'm going to say has been said before - it's quite likely - but I find myself wanting to say it.

            A lot of White Wolf/Onyx Path games approach their characters from a humanistic/simply human perspective; being human is special. There's some ineffable awesomeness about being human that, while not necessarily bringing you happiness, is still fundamentally there. This is a default assumption most game lines approach from, because becoming inhuman often simply removes characters from play and/or turns them into monsters. Vampires need to hold onto their humanity to avoid becoming savage, mindless beasts; Werewolves need to hold onto their humanity to stop from losing all hold on their flesh side; Mages need to hold onto their humanity to keep from consuming themselves with hubris; Changelings need to hold onto their humanity to keep from being solipsistic nightmares; Prometheans seek humanity because it's all that gives them purpose; Mummies try to reclaim it because it gives them their liberation. I hammer on this excessively just to point out how critical humanity is to the thought process of so many World of Darkness lines.

            Beast, it feels to me, should ignore this completely.

            Beasts aren't human. Beasts are Beasts; they are things spawned from the Primordial Dream, and their wearing a human skin should be seen as convenient at best and horrendous at worst. The nature of the Beast is the thing in their Lair; their true self; their Soul self; that Hungry, inhuman nightmare that seeks to slake its thirsts on others. This inhumanity should be a central conceit of the game line. Shedding humanity should be offered as a major potential goal that is eminently attainable, just as Mages have Archmastery or Mummies have their liberation from the Judges. Inheritance should be a major path possibility and something that Beast offers a path towards.

            Likewise, this non-humanity should have its impacts. A possible mythos/background for the setting that's wandered through my mind is the idea that Beasts and the Primordial Dream descend from a time before Man; before humanity ever walked the Earth. They are the ur; the thing which existed before anything else. For millions of years, they ruled this planet. They were that which held dominion, drawing their strength from ancient and primal forces of the cosmos. Then, humanity appeared, and this upstart race - weak and small but many in number - arose to fight back the Beasts. Humanity's dawn was a time when monsters lay at every horizon and gods sat on every mountain peak, and humanity rose up to smite them all. In this cosmic war, the Beasts thoroughly lost.

            This would provide a resultant basis for the setting. The reason Beasts appear - initially - human is because they thoroughly lost this war for the world and now must exist in hiding. Their birth by some distant mother-force is concealed to save them from instant death when they are at their weakest and most vulnerable. Likewise, Heroes (and this is probably the most controversial thing I'm going to say here) would be something akin to humanity's unconscious defense mechanism; a tribal, unthinking backlash that sees the Other as a threat and feels it must be destroyed at any cost. Mind you, Beasts are, by their nature, somewhat antithetical to humanity, so they're not without reason. A Beast is a victimizer by its very nature. This is why, despite often being ruthless and sliiightly obsessive, people still adore Heroes: They're slaying monsters that feed upon them.

            This sort of background gives characters motive (reach Inheritance before Heroes murder you), adds extra character focus to Beasts ("What is this thing you call hoo-mah-nih-tee?"), and provides both them and their antagonists with a strong niche. We already have a game line about bestial predators given over to unholy urges that see them victimize people to slake their unnatural hunger, and it's awesome. Beast needs something to call its own.

            This would also let the writers play with some Lovecraftian themes, too. Mummy introduced those but I never felt quite like it capitalized on them fully (it has some shining sparks, mind you, but there's holes in it). We could see Beasts drawing the souls of sleeping mortals into their Lair, to become a part of their symbolism; turning their waking forms into mad cultists who seek to bring the Beast into the waking world (perhaps allowing cult rituals to bring the Beast's true form into a location; binding a path to their Lair into the waking world?). Or perhaps constructing strange monoliths that amplify the Beast's power, allowing them to feed on more people/over greater distances through their dreams; or to summon down aspects of some mighty over-being that they can draw power from? It'd be fantastic to play a Beast who bargains with or otherwise pleases Shub-Niggurath to gain the power to produce their own dark spawn; or performing the appropriate rituals to Yog-Sothoth to let them see the future. Not literally these beings, of course, but you get the idea.

            So... Yeah. I might be rambling and raving in ignorance here (kinda apropos, honestly) but, if I'm not... How am I doing?

                                        Fair enough point here Shadowflame.

                                        I'm more on board with the Axelgear point of view. Mainly because I would like another game in the NWoD besides Demon that engages with the idea of "inhumanity as the norm".

                                        On a more personal tonal point, I would like the NWoD move away from having a supernatural that agonizes over either their lot in life or the lot of reality as they view it. I mean lets have a look at some of the previous NWoD games on the issue of "having a bad time" of being supernatural:

                                        Vampire pretty much perfectly captures the "post-human" state of being "blessed with suck" (namely being a monster that looks like a human) and having to deal with that. You have all your human memories and you still walk the same streets but you look at the people around you as food now on some instinctual level. Commence head-fuck and angst. Great game for slippery slopes, moral compromise and a lot of self-discover/hatred.

                                        Mage has people having a good time of being supernatural for the most part, but pretty much makes them view reality as a place inherently broken, degraded and fallen. Which is all the fault of human hubris that got access to too much supernatural power. So we could say its existentially angsty rather then personally angsty like Vampire.

                                        Promethean does an excellent job of getting across that being a a supernatural is not a fun thing that makes you better then humanity. It also does an excellent job of selling a humanist message and making an argument for "humans are special". It's getting across its themes well and I love the humanist message in that games scope. However, it gets a double bite of the angst apple for having both personal and existential issues.

                                        Changeling has a lot of the same issues as vampire. Though less "I'm a predatory monster" and more "I'm a broken inhuman freak" and even more of an emphasis on the "post-human" aspect of the game. I'm not going to even go into the many ways and facets that Changeling deals with personal pain, angst and general depressing things. Needless to say they are many and central to the game.

                                        Given all the above, I could do without Beast being yet another take on the "woah is me" angle of being something that is inhuman or post-human. We have games that explore those themes pretty well and cover the majority of the characterful stuff you can get out of them (as detailed above). The last thing I want is to imagine a Beast sitting in his/her Lair moping/crying/lamenting about their "lost humanity". I want Beast to get on with the business of being a monster, of being something that was human but is now inhuman (by which I mean something that has dropped a human viewpoint or reference), not post-human (by which I mean something that retains a human viewpoint via memory but has now had its current perceptions warped by its new nature) and is exploring that state.

                                        Some Beasts might explore it with trepidation, not even sure if they are cut out for this business of embodying fears but muddling their way through. Others might throw them selves into their new role in life, finding it natural and liberating from their "laval quasi-human stage" that they always felt uncomfortable in.

                                        If a Beast is being protective of humans or aiding a Promethean to become human that's all cool by me. I just want the reasons they are doing this stuff to not be immediately obvious as human motivations. I want a least a little bit of an alien mindset and motivation for these actions. Maybe the Beast is protecting these people because they fit some arcane category that makes them part of its hoard? It cares about them as property to be protected not because it hold their lives to have intrinsic meaning beyond belonging to it. Maybe the Beast is helping the Promethean because the Promethean asked it too in a particular way the means the Beast can't even contemplate refusing such a request, its just not a done thing. It could care less about the Promethean in questions eventual humanity or the alleviation of its personal and metaphysical issues. It's just got to help because its got to "the rules" that Beasts follow (if they have such rules) say so.

                                        In reality, I don't actually expect Beast to engage with inhuman themes as much as I want it too. That's not to say we can't both have our cake and eat it too. If Beasts can get frighteningly human (which the preview stuff seems to imply) and have a bit of disconnect between their human form and beast soul (also implied) it would be nice to have the short-sharp snap between the two. A hungry Beast is not thinking like a human it just wants to feed, but its still intelligent and a person. A sated Beast is almost completely human in mindset and likely having trouble understanding or rationalizing (or possibly even remembering) its actions while hungry.

                                        Basically, if Beast can at least manage a good Jekyll and Hyde style internal conflict depending on the Beasts Satiety rating, I'll be mostly happy.

                                        Time will tell I guess.
