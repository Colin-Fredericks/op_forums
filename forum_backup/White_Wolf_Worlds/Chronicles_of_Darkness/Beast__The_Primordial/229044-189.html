                        The weight of Hunger and what it means to a Beast are absolutely explicit and align, if not entirely then in at least in part, with the points Freemind brought up. At least in my reading of it.

                        I will say that, far as I'm concerned, explanations in the genre do not need to be explicit to this degree of detail - existential horror is best served with a mixture of subtle and obvious, but should avoid being 'in your face' lest it lose the impact. It should evoke terror to see and when you ponder on it, not tell you to feel terror.

                        Exploring the implications on your own is in great part where the feeling dawns for me, and in that respect Beast offers the essential information in the writing and elaborates adequately to support that type of reading without overextending itself; it gives just enough to immediately understand, but not so much that it eliminates the potential for major exploration.

                        In that sense, you could argue the amount of discussion BtP has prompted so far about Heroes and the position of its protagonists (leaving aside the toxicity some developed into) speaks wonders of how accomplished the text is in the regard above.

                        The book may have its flaws, but I think highly of it and its difficult themes (the cycle of violence, justification and true monstruosity, who you are versus what you are, what we hate to see in mirrors, the subversion of the narrative, etc), and I believe it is sufficiently transparent about its intended tones that it should not invite the amount of objection and controversy we're seeing.

                        That said, I understand not everyone feels that way, and that's fine, because different strokes for different people and there would not be legitimate concerns if parts of the book weren't troublesome from at least one valid perspective. Ultimately, if adjusting the final product so it is more faithful or clearer to the intention of the team alleviates these issues, then I'll be alright with that and OPP has all of my support. This whole thing is probably quite hard on them, and they have to worry about things like sales as well.


                        I really didn't mean to turn that into a rant but whelp! Okay, I got it out my system.

                            Originally posted by Freemind View Post
                            I would point out: has anyone here ever had to struggle with being hungry? Or to stay on a regimented diet?

                            Starvation leads your brain to focus on good. While your cognitive function doesn't decline (otherwise, how would human, without tooth or claw, get food). But it does distract you from tasks that are not centered on food. They have shown starvation is akin to withdrawal, while food triggers similar pathways to recreational drugs (and some suspect obesity to be due to these pathways firing too much, creating literal addictions).

                            And then you have diets, where you are willingly starving yourself and depriing yourself of things that give you pleasure. Therr is a reason most people fail their diet regimines, and it is because you are essentially pitting your individual discipline against millions of years of evolutionary instinct. To top it off, even the discipline can undermine you, as flawed thoughts: "I can't eat this" is more likely to lead you to cheat, as such a denial leads to part of your brain rebelling against the part telling it it can't do something (it is like when you tell a 6 year old he can't have a cookie... he will then want it even more).

                            And the obvious inverse is over eating and poor consumption control, when you keep eating when full or to often. I mean, the obesity crisis is there in part because a lot of processed foods trigger the reward process too well (I still remember my button class in Medical School showing an FMRI of someone on coke and someone who was both overweight and eating some form of processed desert (name wasnt given)...the overlap was scary). Point being, not eating too much is only a bit easier than keeping yourself from eating, sue to the same pathways.

                            Now imagine that it isn't something physical, but metaphysical. Your soul literally thirsts for something, and is starving. And it feels good to sate it , just like a cheeseburger would taste like nirvana after being in the desert for a week without food. It isn't something you can fully control, because if you don't feed it, it will hijack the dreams of others and feed itself. And feeding it to the point of it literally falling into a metaphysical "food coma" is nearly as dangerous as letting it starve. And the issue is, you have to let it be hungry unless you are willing to act in the dark manners to fulfill it, harming mankind (even if it is a non-persistent manner to only those who "deserve it") in someway to instill the fear you need to feed.

                            Beasts have an inner struggle, just like anyone on a diet. There diet simply is "not instilling terror into humanity".

                            (Sorry, the MD in me slipped out a bit there. But trust me, when working with both morbidly obese 14 year Olds and anorexics, you learn how much the hunger/reward response can mess things up).
                            I deal with this every day, and it's hard. This is part of why Beast appeals to me, this resonance with my lived experiences.

                            Crunch isn't a hobby; it's a calling.

                                                        Originally posted by Wasteful View Post

                                                        But is that an explicit thing in Beast, or merely an angle we can easily imagine?
                                                        Considering that the fluff makes a point about how different levels of hunger shape the beasts actions, and that the system imparts conditions on the Beast depending on the Satiety, which in turn act as a means for Beats (i.e. XP) in the current system... I don't see an issue with it. I mean, rhe fact that both ends and the middle reflect different levels of rhw beasts actions and your susceptibility to Anathema's all point to reasons to have a mechanic (Low means the Soul ravages dreams and drives you, 5 is most susceptible to Heroes, and High means the Beast leaves you be but you have difficulty sating it, and it may not respond). Plus, satiety is also your power pool, so it is essential mechanically in that respect as well.

                                                        And here is the thing: the central theme reflecting the core conflict of each splats setting. I mean, you have:
                                                        • Mortals/hunters have Integrity (holding onto your sense of self in the face of the horror of the world, some if with you perpetrated or experienced)
                                                        • Werewolves and Harmony (balance the world's of flesh and spirit)
                                                        • Mage has Wisdom (Not abusing your semi-phenomenal, nearly cosmic power)
                                                        • Sineaters and Synergy (don't piss off that ancient ghost that brought you back, but stay human (ish))
                                                        • Humanity (cling to what you were-Vampire/Become what you want to be-Promethean)
                                                        • Changelings have clarity (hold on to reality after being the play thing of beings who defined both it and you before you eacaped)
                                                        (If I am forgetting anyone, I apologize.)

                                                        I mean, you could make the argument "but do we really need a gauge to determine that? Why not just role play it? " But i could make that point for everything in nWoD. Do you need Harmony or Synergy, Humanity or Integrity? No, nor do you need to play with them, as they all could be reflected by game play and the PCs thoughts and actions. For example, I dropped the morality aspect from my 1e mage games for the most part, focusing more on how you used vulgar magic and let the abyss into the world. But the games do have those systems for a reason in 2e beyond moral judgement, which is to have mechanical factors that attempt to reflect the central theme of conflict of each line and reward players both for optimizing those tracks and for playing their characters failure on them (via Beats and conditions).

                                                        Sure, you can act hungry IC, but with the current system, you get a condition that reflects it that grants you XP for both acknowledging as you play and when it resolves, thus offering a reward for playing your character. It also offers guide posts so the player, other PCs and the ST has an understanding of where characters are on the scale and what actions might be appropriate.

                                                        As for why satiety, it fits the conflict best. I mean, other lines have "hungers", but those aren't the central theme, or at least not the one that drives conflict. Mages thirst for knowledge, power and Mystery, but the conflict comes from how they use that knowledge and power, not it's acquisition (unless acquired via said misuse). Vampires have their Hunger, but the conflict isn't fighting it so much as trying to cling to being human when you are not one any longer (and I would argue that since their soul is still human, if scarred and twisted, that they face a dissonance that beasts don't). Werewolves have the need to hunt, but their conflict is the balance of the spirit vs. the flesh, and how that shapes their hunt (not the need hunt itself). And so on.

                                                        The point is, the central theme of Beast's conflict is that you have the soul of a powerful, hungry, and totally inhuman monster that is a part of you. The Souls hunger is your hunger, and it is the conflict of the setting because if how you need to fill it: harming humans to create the appropriate type of fear or loss. And you are still human enough to recognize those things as both harmful and necessary for your existence, and must balance sating yourself and sparing those around you.

                                                        The Beasts are driven by and must fight their hunger, balancing it against everything else. And that hunger shapes both how far they are willing to go for their principles and a reason to forgo them. For example, to compare it to a human: cannibalism as even a concept is appalling to even think about while you sit in a cafe while down the street from the supermarket (at least, I would hope it is), but when trapped on a snowy mountain with no resources but the frozen guy next to you, you find your principles challenged as you starve to death. Beasts are beings that literally have a hunger that resides in the Core of their being, that transcends the physical needs of their body as their very Soul demands sustenance. They potentially face the conundrum of forsaking their principles to eat every day, not just when trapped on a snowy maintain, because holding to the principle of not harming humans (even if only metaphysically) is what starves them. Some might be able to go and maintain hunger strikes to the point of being willing to die for their principles, though the beast won't let that happen (and will ravage humanity in their dreams anyway). Others give in to their hunger, and gorge themselves, guilt setting in as they realize what they did. And others try to funnel it into morally justifiable acts, while others don't care and accept being monsters. The thing is, the Hunger is what drives their conflict with the world and in themselves. The fact that denying your Hunger leads to the Mythic Arc initiating the creation of a Hero, and thus drives the games central external conflict, also makes it appropriate.

                                                        Tl;DNR: Mechanically, Satiety is important as both the power pool for abilities and as a source of conflict for beats, as well as defining your susceptibility to Anathemas. It also fits thematically as the core driver of the Beasts conflict, both internally and with the world, as it reflects the state of the Soul and it's influence on the Beast as well as the Soul's actions in the Astral (which in turn leads to the creation of Heroes, the one of the key elements of the games external conflict).
