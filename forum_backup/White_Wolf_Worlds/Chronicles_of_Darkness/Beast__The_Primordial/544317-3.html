                From what I've read so far it seems like Heroes are ordinary people (for the World of Darkness anyway) who were already on the edge thanks to their own shortcomings or unfortunate circumstances, who were just given the opportunity to go full long into the plunge. In that regard they are the ultimate embodyment of Transferance (think that's the right term anyways), flawed people who could use a sholder to lean on who glimps true supernatural horror and splendor in the form of the Beast's Soul and turn that into a symbol for all of their grief, hate, fear, and desire. This trapps them in a cycle of violence that will eventually erode away the last bits of Integrity they have untless they are slain or begin to climb out of the pit they've thrown themselves into, and all because they refused to see that they made the Beasts the target of their obsession. For a Hero isn't magically controlled or influenced by the Beast, they simple get a taste of it's power but see it as a Call to Action instead of what it really is: an escape from their own life.

                I feel the best example of this comes from the primary difference between Hunters and Heroes. Hunters take on the Vigil primarily because the discovery of the Supernatural and realize that their lives will never be the same again after such a revelation and choose to either make the most of the situation they find themselves in (and become stupid-rich in the process) or because they truly wish to protect others from the same horros of the world that forever changed their lives. On the other hand a Hero activly takes on the mantel as either a way to make their lives better (either through returning things to an hypothetical bettter time or by destroying the imagined cause of all of their grief) or to give meaning to a life they believed to be empty and meaningless. There will of course be some overlap in certain circumstances because both are unquestionably human, but generally a Hero is a Hero because they now refuse to be anything less. Because to do so would be to go back to what they were before that fatefull nightmare, just a scared human being.

                        In a way, Beast: the Primordial and Hunter: the Vigil are opposites.

                        In HtV, you play a human that hunts the monsters that creep in the dark to protect humanity from their evil and terror. The game reduces the other supernaturals to a point where all are treated as a large extended family of monsters (usually via the Inferno, the collective sins of humanity).

                        In BtP, you play the monster that creeps in the dark and inflicts terror on humanity, and are hunted by a human that wants to slay evil. The game pulls in all other supernaturals to the point where everyone is the same family through the collective fears and dark urges of humanity.

                        The two games are hitting similar themes on different sides. So, in a way, Heroes really are just a variant hunter template, but with a very specific tie to Beast, and super-emphasizes all the different mental issues that comes with being a hunter. Its unsurprising that the Heroes and Hunters would be mirror reflections of each other as well. Both are profoundly changed by their exposure to the supernatural. One difference, of course, is that a hero is, by definition, kind of insane. Heroes can't tell the difference between reality and their own illusions. They can't see the harm they're causing.

                        That said, there is another side to the whole hero-beast aspects that we haven't looked at, or talked about, in any detail. People look at heroes as "jerks," "dicks," or "zealots." Ironically, the description is actually more appropriate for the Beasts themselves, who are basically the embodiment of all of humanity's sadistic tendencies. Heroes aren't necessarily jerks - in fact, I'd say they generally aren't. They're primarily Knight Templars. They're the fundamentalists that think they're good, but can't see shades of grey. They're the Moral Guardians.

                        The idea here is that, where Beasts are the dark aspects of humanity, the dark buried swarms of the id buried below the subconcious level, the Heroes are the super ego that is the seat of all that is "light," the moral imperatives. And the funny thing about the freudian super ego is that it loves judging others. The thing to remember about Freudian ego, super ego, and id is that good isn't light, but rather, good is the Ego balancing the light and dark in a way that allows empathy and compassion. The two extremes of Hero and Beast are both dangerous. Its also notable that Freud considered the Super Ego to be a completely artificial construct who's purpose was to just restrict the Id. Ironically, that's the same role that Heroes have.

                        Heroes are the super ego, the Soul is the id, and the actual human part of the Beast is the Ego that mediates between the morality and the primal drives. And, yes, the Astral does contain all of that as well. Those of you familiar with the Astral in Mage should be familiar with the idea that there's the single daimon that controls and orders the mind, while there are a multitude of goetic demons running around. The daimon is the super ego, the goetic demons are the id. And the conscious mind mediates between these two urges. The strife between a "shoulder angel" and "shoulder devil" is real in the WoD, and that's played out in the battle between Hero and Beast, the embodiments of the human parts of the Astral and human psyche.

                        From this perspective, the Heroes being the super ego, a lot of their description makes actual sense. What they do, how they do it. Heroes are as much "non-humans" as the Beast is. They're tapping into a Super Ego Dream, for lack of a better term. The unfortunate thing about the super ego, though, is that its actually not powerful. All the drive, ambition, arousal, energy, and power that humans have come from the id. All the super ego can do is punish through feelings of remorse and guilt. So, from a Fruedian standpoint as much as a mythological one, giving Heroes power equal to the Beasts they "punish" doesn't make sense.

                        The problem, I think, is that people are used to thinking of the "shoulder angel" as good, and being equal in strength to the "shoulder devil." I also think that people want to see the Heroes as a kind of major power on their own.

                        Yes, its true that you need at least Morality 4 (remember, 1e rules) in order to become a Slasher. What could this mean?

                        Not sure. Low Integrity doesn't really reflect what it means to be a Slasher anymore. Slashers and the Inferno are strongly tied to the idea of objective evil forces in the world. I'm not really sure that Integrity is appropriate to reflect what either of those represent. Low Integrity means you're mentally unstable, and Heroes and Beasts are definitely unbalanced, especially since we're talking about them as inflated super egos and ids.

                        Slashers do tend to be insane or broken people, so they're likely low Integrity, but their powers aren't linked to the psyche like a Hero's is. Heroes stop being heroes when their id, ego, and super ego go back into balance (represented by raising Integrity). I don't think that applies to Slashers - they're more of a supernatural monster with an alternative Integrity system that's not really detailed.
