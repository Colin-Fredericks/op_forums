    Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
    If we must compare Vampires to Beasts, there is a metric to remember, a drum to beat in determining which is better:

    1) Those who are clever about their advantages,
    2) Those who are not.

    Beasts have all sorts of tools that can wreck merry hell on a vampire, and vampires have all sorts of tools that can wreck merry hell on Beasts. Depends on how you play it-but I will note a bias here and point out that Beasts inherently have a wider toolbox than vampires.
    I wider toolbox than the versatile Auspex, Dominate, Nightmare/Majesty and Physical Disciplines?
    A vampire with Celerity straight up trumps Beast in a stand up fight, the beast simply cannot keep up either perception wise (everything is a surprise attack for the vampire) or flat out can't do anything (first initiative uncontested, interrupts) and every power the vampire has is like that - controlling and versatile.

    Beast's nightmares seem cool but they don't scale to higher power games too well imo. Their atavisms look even cooler but are typically more limited in scope or raw power.
    The birth-rights are nice perks but a Satiety per attempt to pierce an Obfuscate that can just be immediately restored for 1/10-75 vitae is rough, to say nothing of actually trying to hit the kindred.

    Likewise with Werewolves and their gifts, I imagine.

    That being said, I'm sure I'll have fun trying to play the underdog, the one who can only look in their direction and "make friends" short of bringing them into their Lair.

        Originally posted by Arduras View Post
        I wider toolbox than the versatile Auspex, Dominate, Nightmare/Majesty and Physical Disciplines?
        Literally no Discipline or Devotion will let you generate sunlight, and learning powers outside your clan's Disciplines or the common five requires proprietary membership or becoming more vulnerable to another vampire's influence. Their greater abilities come at the price of precious Vitae or social leverage or territorial limitation. The active social/mental Disciplines all rely on a buildup of effects to be used at the higher levels, Auspex takes multiple scenes to use effectively and freely, Celerity's interrupt effect is both not free and not unlimited in use, and Obfuscate's interaction with surprise is wholly counteracted by simple dice bonuses.

        Meanwhile, literally every Beast can develop any Lair Trait, Atavism, or Nightmare with the only real investment being legwork and time, and the associated vulnerabilities that could commonly come up are two-way and muted by the splat's incentives toward resilience in general. Beasts grow rapidly under pressure and synchronize well with other monsters, some of their supernatural powers are just better versions of mundane Merits, and their Atavisms include multiple sources of "you just know these things."

        A character that can regenerate health, have an innate Armor rating of 3, beat the Initiative of anyone who fails a surprise attack against them, know when their mind or perception is being altered, and/or unerringly track anyone they've ever made contact with in addition to potentially being able to summon disasters or breathe fire for free is inherently in a more flexible place than a similarly randomly-chosen vampire.

                        So here's a thing. What's dangerous about a vampire is not that she's fast or that she's unnaturally charming or that she can turn into a coyote. What makes a vampire dangerous is that she's hungry. All the powers, all the influence, all the elaborate social games... they're all just there so the vampire can cover her most basic need.

                        And that's why so many other monsters get fucked over or fucked up by vampires. Because they don't get the desperation. They don't get the need. They don't get the why.

                        But a Beast? A Beast gets it. A Beast knows what it's like to be hungry. A Beast knows you can turn hunger, the strongest motivation, into weakness. A Beast understands cutting off supports and starving the enemy.

                        And the vampire knows the same thing.

                        A face to face, tooth to neck, rotten breath in each other's faces kind of confrontation can be fun. But when Beasts and vampires clash, it's as two predators who understand each other at the very core. So they'll strike at the supports and the territory and all of the things around each other. And more often than not, rather than turn into two hungry animals circling each other, desperate for the kill... they'll have to strike a bargain. Because each knows the other, and knows how bad it'll get.

                                                    Beasts are worse than what splat in what area, and at what EXP? Because the answer to who is better depends a lot on the circumstances, goal, and exp. Vampires are about control and bypassing systems by sending in their Beast.

                                                    Social Challenge, get someone to do a thing
                                                    Mortal: Fast-Talking, Sympathetic, and Pusher a Mortal can open 5 Doors on a single roll, which will be enough for all but the most stubborn of people. They also have access to Allies, Contacts, Mentor, and Status, which are amazing at getting people to do things. On defense, Etiquette 1 and Closed Book allow them to lie like a rug and have 10 Doors with 6 xp + A skill at 5. Mortals can stop incoming violence with that merit in Hurt Locker, even from frenzying creatures.

                                                    Vampire:
                                                    Innately, Vampires gain the ability to Lash Out to achieve their goals, which nearly bypasses the social maneuvering system entirely for short term gain. They also gain Blood Bonds, allowing them to enslave others to their will. They also get the ability to ignore mental coercion by Frenzying.

                                                    Social power beyond that is really exclusive to particular clans and covenants. Dominate 1-3 allows you issues commands and have them followed against someone's will, while also handling short term memories. Majesty 1-4 lets you enter ever more into someone's confidence and control their emotions. Nightmare 1-3 allows you to terrify people and give them Delusions. Auspex 1-4 allows you to glean more information from people which, while not directly applicable, can be used by a clever mind. The different Covenants have access to merits that make your social merits more efficient and/or more effective.

                                                    Werewolf:
                                                    Innately, Werewolves gain Lunacy (forget the monster), Hunter's Mark (penalties that make prey easier to hunt), and a penalty against incoming mental powers while in a Frenzy.

                                                    All werewolves can purchase Residential Area, which is a bit of an omni-merit and they gain access to a Totem, which can grant them exp-efficient benefits. They can gain perfect impression for a short term social gain with a gift. Some werewolves have access to additional dice on social rolls, generally up to +5. They have a couple more social gifts that grant conditions to people, and Half-Moons with high Honor can spawn social merits out of thin air. This doesn't include what they can do with spirits. They have several gifts related to gathering information. Half Moons can also bind magical oaths.

                                                    Beast:
                                                    Alien Allure ensures good impressions, ensures people forgive you for your wrongdoing, grants a bonus to your social rolls, spawns Fame/Status dots out of thin air, and forces people to trust you and do you favors when you win a social contest -in addition- to the normal effects. Advanced Striking Looks is going to give you rote on Social Rolls. Mimir's Wisdom keeps you from being lied to. The Siren Atavism whose name I forget gives you yet more bonus social dice and makes you good at luring people, or attacking them with a sonic attack.

                                                    Then you have Nightmares. Nightmares can control people (though not as well as Dominate), they can instill strange beliefs in people, or otherwise manipulate them to do what you want so long as you can contextualize it as being motivated by horror/trauma. With Mummy Kinship (and probably Ventrue Kinship) you can even alter memories. This i on top of their innate ability to start with a Good Impression with all supernaturals.

                                                    Conclusion:
                                                    (Kindred = Beast) > Werewolf > Mortal is how I see it, but note that all of those can reach the point of "one roll = you do a thing," and have a good chance of passing that roll. Kindred pull ahead because of their ability to have long term gains to their powerbase through their powers, while Beasts have some fringe benefits (immunity to lying, autoforgiveness) that really stand out. Both can affect memories. Notably, each is best in their own way...
                                                    - Kindred is best at aquiring and exorcising control
                                                    - Werewolf is good at setting up the hunt.
                                                    - Beast is good at being an abuser and breaking people down until they do what you want, while they welcome you with open arms because it'll totally be different this time.
