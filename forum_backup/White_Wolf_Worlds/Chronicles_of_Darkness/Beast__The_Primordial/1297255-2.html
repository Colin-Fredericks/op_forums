        Originally posted by Cinder View Post
        I can't see this end well, Camilla.

        For starters, reading the OP makes me groan for how much it willfully ignores what even Beast detractors admit the books say. No need to like the book nor the game because, it's worth repeating, not all gamelines are for everyone, but to dislike something, with its flaws and weak points, is different than to dislike something that only exists to you.

        But the main reason I doubt you'll have an easy road ahead is there's a lapse of logic between what you feel, what you want to do, and what you're asking. You find Beasts repulsive and yet want to make them even worse so everyone would be well within their rights to slay them without a thought? That makes things worse and only serves to confirm your own bias. Also, a game about heroes killing monsters, with them being the protagonists of the events and the focus of their consequences? That's hardly anything new, either as a game or a topic discussed around here.

        First, the book allows that, straight from the core. The only difference is that you would made their enemies so horrible, any conflict in there would be gone. I get that you don't like it, but there's this tightrope Beast walks on between the mythical ideal of Heroes and Beasts as forces of Good & Darkness and how it manifests in less than absolute ways in the setting reality which is a rather big deal for the game themes, one of its central conflicts and reasons to be.

        Long story short, if you subvert a subversion and ignore everything that made it interesting in the first place, you'll have something emptier than both the original trope, its deconstruction and even what one good reconstruction would do. I'm an advocate for Beast & Heroes to always feel and confront with tensions towards a clearer, more platonic and "simple" life, but a source of struggle more than anything else.

        So I don't think there's much potential for this idea as something more than your own personal hack for the game. Aspects of it are there, others build upon foundations that don't exist and overall it offers something that it's either done better elsewhere or I doubt would pique the interest of those who like the game for the unusual narrative avenues it offers.

        Can't speak for others, naturally, but I personally see no way this sort of fan game can gain much traction in this specific context. Sorry about that.

        If it helps, I'm mainly intending this idea for personal use in my own chronicles. I may post a variant of it for others to use, but I am aiming for personal use first.

                                            It's also worth noting that the Beast fan community here is... kinda touchy? We get a lot of people that openly admit to not even reading the whole final core book (let alone the supplements or actually trying the game itself), that come here to 'fix' the game by posting ideas that essentially strip Beast of what makes it a unique and interesting game in a very crowded RPG market for something literally dozens of other games already do (many of them other WoD/CofD games at that). Camilla has been very vocal about their dislike of the game before, and the "this isn't mean to be an attack on the fans" refrain is difficult to treat innocently if someone has to keep repeating it... they know it's not going to be well received in the first place. It's like the classic, "I don't mean to be racist, but..." disclaimer. Either people aren't going to care because it was offensive anyway, or you're just priming people to consider your statement in that light. If you feel like you have to qualify it that way, you either need to consider not saying it, or rephrasing it because that's a giant red flag you're saying it wrong. This is no different in construction. If you have to say, "this isn't meant to be an attack on Beast fans," you're probably going to be interpreted as doing so anyway, or you're phrasing things in a fashion unlikely to get your point across to/get positive responses from Beast fans.

                                            If you don't like Beast.... why post a fan-game that basically has nothing to do with what Beast is about in the Beast subforum? Just head on down to the general CofD and start a fan-game about what you want to play if the existing games aren't scratching an itch you have. Esp. if you know this is a reoccurring issue around here.

                                            Also, however much it might suck to hear? If you ask for criticism, sometimes the feedback you get from your chosen sample group is going to be, "nope, this sucks." It's not sad that happens. You're hearing something worth considering before moving forward. That's a good and useful thing once you get past any frustration with it. It's pretty important to consider that the time and energy for Camilla to turn BtP into what they want out of a game, compared to turning HtV into what they want from a game, is actually a waste of time that isn't going to get better results for the investment.

                                                        Originally posted by The Throne View Post
                                                        Nobody want help you with your demand: this is the real answer, i guess.

                                                        That is profoundly untrue. Those are just a few examples that point out things they see as problematic or give suggestions. Furthermore, telling someone "Hey, that thing you wanna do with x? You can easily get that with y!" as a few here did, IS helping someone achieve their goal, albeit in different way. I'd also like to point out that you yourself didn't provide any sort of support for his vision in favour of an uncalled-for comment against the other posters, which I too consider rather rude. That this is your only comment in this thread doesn't really help matters, because to me, that makes it feel like you just came here to complain about this perceived lack of help.

                                                        Camilla
                                                        I'll try not to repeat what others said too much.
                                                        Being as clear as possible in the OP helps, especially if you were boycotting the gameline so far and the other stuff. The written word is an instrument that isn't optimal for communication, because it leaves a lot open to interpretation. Reading the same e-mail while happy or angry or sad would yield different interpretations of what you consider the intended message to be. And people tend to base their interpretations on their available knowledge. Had you included "I am considering getting the core for this fansplat to research Hero abilities etc.", the tone of some messages might've been quite a bit different. (This is my personal interpretation of the posts of this thread, obviously.)

                                                        I think playing a Hero-focused game is more problematic than making up a Hunter conspiracy. Not that we don't have sympathetic Heroes (and the Core explicitly states that it tends to focus on low-Integrity varieties, because those are the ones Beasts tend to clash with; high-Integrity ones get little wordcount due to being not too relevant), so you'd have something you could build on even on the core as is. But the lack of information on high-integrity Heroes and their goals/tasks (What do Heroes do when they do their job right? How do they perceive the world/their own spot in it?) would make that a bit work intense, and the themes would be a bit hard to pull off. Plus the fact that this 'subversion of a subversion' might end up quite tasteless, which would be a shame: if you invest time, you'd like to have something to show for it, I assume.
                                                        As others already mentioned, in addition to problems like you'd have the lack of conflict, and conflict drives a story, you'd also have a PC or several who basically play on easy: Heroes are made to combat Beasts, their abilities help them a lot, the "Kill = Powerup" would escalate the PC's power quickly, and then you'd have the problem of reduced stakes: unlike e.g. Hunters, Heroes don't bleed to death, so the danger of confrontation is somewhat lessened.
                                                        If you take Hunter and go from there, you'll get your result much quicker and cleaner, and can save money (you weren't very keen on getting the Core, after all, and getting it for just a fan game is a tad much, I'd think, even if that might make you appreciate the game more than you do now).
                                                        Being the Hero that hunts monsters also would basically require you to make Beasts more sympathetic if you don't want to drift into a clearcut black-and-white game (which gets boring quickly imo). To me, what makes these games the most fun is the different layers: is a Beast Ravager that throws bricks into rich peoples houses worse than a Kindred that feeds on their blood? A Promethean Cathar that studies the underworld by taking part in drug-deals and robberies? Why/why not? Not the answer, but the reasoning of the people making those answers is what makes this interesting.
                                                        One example: PC's come to find the bloody trails of a Beast in their city. They meet with Hunters to get some information.
                                                        One Hunter cell considers Beasts the worst, because they put themselves on a pedestal, 'teaching' through pain and fear, robbing people of their agency and scaring them for life.
                                                        Another thinks they're pretty much harmless, because they don't cause as much 'harm': they don't tend to turn people into Vitae-junkies, they don't tend to accidentally kill people (as much as Kindred for example), and they don't suddenly lose control and murder someone because they were surprised by them lighting a cigarette or something - all they do is give people some fright, maybe some bruises (I exaggerate, this is an IC point of view). Both valid opinions.
                                                        Now you have an Eshmaki that feeds by beating people up - but he focuses on people brutalizing other people, so mobsters, human traffikers, pimps and the occasional Kindred.
                                                        Cell 1 wants to see the Eshmaki dead.
                                                        Cell 2 thinks he's a piece of shit, but a useful one. Is there a clearcut answer to this scenario? I don't think so. That's what would make your characters' decision interesting and fun.
                                                        A Hunter's answers could range from 'It is a supernatural abomination and thus must die!' (sounds kinda speciesist, though), to "It harms fellow humans, so it is a threat, who knows what it'll do when it runs out of prey/finds easier targets?" to "Let's see if we can find some understanding..." or even "Hell, keeps some scum off my back AND makes the area a nicer place for the kids? Let 'im do his thing!', depending on what brought them to the Hunt in the first place, their lives, etc.
                                                        A Hero on a quest to kill Beasts doesn't have as much wiggle room here, and might end up with more simplistic answers because "it's their role": "he probably also does worse things we don't know about", "they all do worse sometime", "It's regrettable, but it must be done" or somesuch (the last of which at least carries some potential for an interesting chronicle imo). He is less likely to consider the impact of the Beast on this society, because 'Heroes bring wisdom and glory' after facing the monster, the monster doesn't bring safety and happiness. He is less likely to consider the Beast trying to make the best out of a shitty situation (and having to break people's boney or uncontrollably torment your loved ones with nightmares IS a shitty situation, especially if the guy isn't really suited to fighting and basically only makes it out of those thanks to spending Satiety on Relentless Hunter, forcing him to feed more often). A Hero is less likely to decide Cell 2 has a point and just leave the guy alone, or meet them, size them up, and establish a common understanding or something. Because they have something akin to a God-given mandate to hunt those beings. They're called to do so. This is slightly coloured by my perception of low-Integrity Heroes, so take it with a grain of salt.


                                                        tl;dr: Imho, the thematic clashes would be less interesting than what you'd get using Hunter is what I'm saying.
