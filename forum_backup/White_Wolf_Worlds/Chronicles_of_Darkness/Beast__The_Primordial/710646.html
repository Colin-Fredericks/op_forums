          References to the Homecoming on p.79-80 and the Chart Quick Reference on p.337, which should probably be replaced with Devouring in accordance with the change in terminology.

          p.20-1: "The three Inheritance states are Incarnate, Unfettered, and Rampant." "Merger: The process by which the Beast joins with her Horror, creating a near-mindless monster unable to access the Primordial Dream, thus achieving the Unleashed Inheritance." "Unleashed Inheritance: The Inheritance gained when a Beast undergoes Merger." All references outside the Lexicon refer to the physical-world Inheritance as "Rampant," but this is also the name given to the Lucifuge-analogues in the Tokyo setting. Needs disentangling.

          p.47: "Beyond the fence, though darkness not even Tanya’s keen eyes could pierce, there was something waiting." Should be "through".

          p.97: "Opening a Pathway to the exact location from which the Beast created a Chamber is easiest, but desperate Beasts and cunning would-be intruders can use similar locations by spending a point of Willpower during the attempt and incurring an additional dice penalty based on how well the location matches." Phrasing suggests this is an additional cost, but subsequent roll description includes "Cost: 1 Willpower" as though this is a baseline facet of opening a Primordial Pathway anywhere. Suggest removing the Cost line or the "by spending a point of Willpower during the attempt" if opening a Pathway without an Exact similarity is not meant to cost 2 Willpower.

          p.99: "Visitors or intruders using Primordial Pathways from anything other than the Primordial Dream are physically present, while characters whose nightmares touch the Primordial Dream (Beasts who have yet to undergo the Devouring, for example) are present in a dream state." Should probably be "anything other than the Astral [Realms]," else this is a severe and bizarre complication for, say, mages engaging in highly inadvisable meditation.

          p162. "If using Social maneuvering on a character that doesn’t have a Vice (like a vampire or a Beast), a temptation that plays to a similar trait, like Life or Mask, can have the same effect at the Storyteller’s discretion." Per Second Edition Vampire, the Kindred substitute for Vice is Dirge, not Mask; analogously, it is possible that the Vice-equivalent for Beasts should be Legend rather than Life.

          p.227: "The curse of the Created, however, is in humanity’s reaction to them. Prometheans who stay in one place for too long and use their alchemical powers too much engender “Disquiet,” which to Beasts all-too-closely resembles the creation of Heroes." References the pre-revision version of how Heroes worked, should probably be altered or removed.

          p.236: "Lurkers who Hunger to hoard knowledge find this ability useful for information gathering." "Some Lurkers have managed to find ways — either through the Dream, or in the physical realm — to enter these realms, exploring them for their own reasons." Obsolete terminology that now solely refers to the Eshmaki. Suggest replacing with "Unfettered."

          p.249: "I feel larger, fuller, and so much than the skin, muscle, and bone that contains me." Should probably be "so much more than".

                                  I'm seeing some punctuation issues in "Tales of the Dark Mother: Sabia". For example:

                                  Sabia falls in an ugly, graceless kind of way as Isolde, the Hero wrenches the sword back.
                                  Need a comma after "the Hero"

                                  She’s six-years-old with sleep paralysis, being weighed down by a nothing in the dark, calling out without a word for her mother to Please help! Please help!
                                  Shouldn't there be quote marks around "Please help!"? I'd also be tempted to cut "being", and capitalize "nothing".

                                  Do you want to go back to sleep, baby?

                                  "I’m scared."

                                  Do you want to stay up with me?

                                  "I don’t know."

                                  Her mother kisses her forehead and sings a lullaby she doesn’t remember. It has no notes. It’s the color of ice and sweet like corpses. It’s the most wonderful thing Sabia has ever felt. It makes her think of pain. Of the nothing in the dark. That fear is her friend.

                                  Do you want to go back to sleep?
                                  Like the imagery, but I'm tempted to play with the font of the mother's lines. In this situation, it makes sense not to use quote marks (after all, who says the Dark Mother "speaks" as humans understand the concept?) However, it might be worth experimenting with various font tricks to set her words off - italicizing or bolding her lines, or doing them in a visibly different font . . . Something to put that special emphasis on them, and show the omission of standard quote marks is deliberate.
