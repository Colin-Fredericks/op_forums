    Originally posted by Xenesis View Post

    I think I finally get why the game isn't hooking me or is just making me uncomfortable with liking it.

    I can't get over the dissonance between the glorification of the Beasts and the sheer contempt for Heroes. So much victim blaming. And then there's the Heroes on the Beast dynamic and it just becomes a mess. The fact that the portrayal of the Beasts throughout the entire text is really not that self-aware gives it that extra squick factor that Other Terrible Splats (e.g. Vampires, Demons, et al) avoid by virtue of owning what they are.

    The whole game just gives me an uncomfortable "no, I don't want to go there" feeling.


    I think the contempt for heroes is that while Beasts may be dangerous and terrifying, they at least have the decency to admit this and they don't hold any significant grudge towards Heroes as they can understand the reason behind their reaction to Beasts existing. Even if they won't lay down and die for them, they at least admit its natural to retaliate.

    Heroes don't have the same level of respect or decency. They are right and you are wrong. You're with them or against them. They can do whatever the hell they want because "its for the greater good."

    Heroes are self righteous, arrogant, merciless and vain. When a Hunter kills, they do it to protect humanity. When a Hero kills, they do it for self gratification, bragging rights and glory. Hunters fear becoming Heroes because that would mean they start caring more about themselves than the people they serve.

                                                            Originally posted by Shadowflame View Post
                                                            The way things are written now it posits the opposite, but the reverse can happen just as easily. So this goes from "Beast the Primordial, to "Blame Game the Victimization."
                                                            While I'm not saying I haven't explored aspects of what you're talking about, I think you're taking this a little too far. Granted, I get that what Beasts do hits really close for you,and I will freely admit, this is not a game for everyone.

                                                            But may I remind you that this game is designed by Matthew McFarland? The Black Hat does a lot of dark stuff, but he seriously doesn't tolerate that sort of crap. You should have seen his response to someone making a dog-fighting joke, it was one of the firmest shutdowns I've ever seen.

                                                            Putting that aside, because you don't get a copy of Black Hat Matt(Cloning stretch goal maybe?) with the game, THe problem is not the victim, the problem is the victim becoming the victimizer, and that's also what this game is heavily about. Neither Hero-to-Be nor Beast is responsible for their lot in life, it happens, but they have control over their actions from there. A Nemesis can be a policeman and dispense punishment readily that way. A Tyrant can rise through a corrupt organization and clean up it's practices as he goes along. A Ravager can remind dictators and oppressors that they aren't untouchable.

                                                            It's a little harder for Heroes/-to-be, because of the entire "Hunger infection/lack of soul infrastructure/consolidation with roots in survival instinct" thing, but considering that Hungers have root in a Mage thing, is it too much to expect that like calls to like? A Nemesis Hero-to-be usually has, buried beneath their fear, a desire to be the one who decides what deserves punishment, to be the moral judge so they don't have to be guilty. A Tyrant Hero-To-Be wants to master the hierarchy like his Beast, but is too afraid of it. A Ravager Hero wants to be self-sufficient, but has misplaced his view of how to be that and is afraid of moving away from the things he's used so far. When the hero-response comes, they consolidate, and the result is to brutally take what they think they deserve. Heroes don't just come from people who are traumatized-they come from people who are just a few steps away from being the Beast themselves.

                                                            And in fact, that's something. Beasts exist to allow people to confront their fears. An experience with a Beast can be freeing, because the change in perspective can lead to appreciation of what one has, growth as an individual, recognition of hamartia, and so on. A beast can shake up their lives for a bit, but humanity can come out stronger for...without a need for violence. Beast, as a game, is about the, say it with me, the cycle of violence, and it condemns it quite readily.

                                                            Again, No one's really at fault, but the people involved aren't squeaky clean either.

                                                            Originally posted by Shadowflame
                                                            *shrugs* I was looking for entertainment, an escape from reality...A game where victimization is a large part of the story isn't either of those. Nuance be damned.
                                                            Holy shit, and you thought the World of Darkness was a good place to get that? A gameline about exploring dark themes from a company that asks of its' fans to take simple gaming and push it into an artform?

                                                            Man, I make it a policy not to question people's play styles, but I feel I must suggest you're in the wrong neighborhood regardless. Kudos if it works for you, but still seems really backwards to me.

                                                            If you want to play a monster that embodies hope and wonder, then you can. But that depends on you being the monster who inspires. The game won't do that for you, because it comes to the character to be those things.
