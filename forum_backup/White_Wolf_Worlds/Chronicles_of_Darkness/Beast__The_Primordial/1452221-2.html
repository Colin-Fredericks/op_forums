                The Insatiable are actually fairly simple, and once I get a Hunger write up to explain my thought process going into Chapter 1 of the Beast book, you'll see what I mean. For now, I'll say I dunno how the Insatiable ended up feeling kinda messy and janky when the obvious role and purpose of the Insatiable was staring everyone in the face-but this may be because I keep my Beast-as-commentary-on-nihilism glasses at the ready.

                The Incarnates are something I need to get around to really kicking around, though properly squaring Heroes and the Astral and expanding Kinship will probably answer a lot of that. In the short, thematically, the Incarnates are tuned correctly*, we don't need to course correct the ideas that make them up. Where Incarnates become interesting is regarding the larger scale issue of Beast's power scale and relationship to the rest of the Chronicles, what that should be, what's "gated" behind the Incarnate Inheritance, and how that should play between the themes.

                I suppose a simpler way to put it is that if we take the phrase "A god is just a monster you kneel to" as one of those vitally important concepts for looking at Beast, then what does it mean when the Incarnate are still a "step up" from Beasthood? In my conception of future versions of Beast having a lot more involvement with Astral Gods, what does it mean when I immediately say the Incarnates are among them, but waffle on whether or not that means High Lair Beasts aren't?

                THe framework for answering that problem is, like a lot of things in Beast, inherently there-I just haven't gotten around to firming it up into realization. And it's something I could probably rant a fair bit about.

                *It's a metaphor for suicide and a parallel to heroism (though not in the vein of way we're approaching Heroes with the Forsworn, and that's fine), mythic apotheosis that leaves nothing but the most self-satisfied monster, and that's not strictly a good thing.

                                    2)I'd peg that Family idea more as the Inguma, Inguma as Fear of the Other extending beyond basic stranger danger and into the genuinely disquieting notion that other people know you better than you do and represent what you should be.                                     but hell, maybe you do, and you just have trouble admitting that to yourself.

                                    That said, the Fear the Makara CAN represent with that, though, is when a person realizes that what seemed like a simple and easy world they entered into is a lot more terrifying complex.....and oh gee, the surface is so far away. They could lose themselves and drown in this world, which might mean dying, or becoming a person they don't understand at all but feel they can no longer escape.

                                    Beast has always had some thin lines to cut and transition over-look at the way people have trouble reconciling the difference between the Anakim and the Talasii, or how there was once an argument that the Anakim represented every other family, or the confusion about the Eshmaki and the Makara in these threads. A lot of the time, it comes down to what, exactly, a person is really reacting to when they encounter something, and that can be hard to cut the difference between. It takes introspection and a lot of careful parsing out, the sort of things Beasts go through with their Devouring into enlighenment.

                                    EDIT: on the Flood, because I was really wanting to talk about that but missed it:

                                    It depends on the reaction to the Flood. if the fear is in trying to figure out how you're going ot get around town, where you're getting food from ,where you'll sleep, thinking you might be able swim across, but what about differing currents, or objects being swept along-like, you know you can hypothetically survive the flood because others have but you feel overwhelmed trying to figure out how you're going to do it, that's the Makara. If instead you're just crushed by the inability to do anything as the waters weep through and destroy your homes and hang out, carries away your pets faster than you can ever hope to swim and catch up, the certainty that you can't swim across that rapid-that there's nothing you can do about the flood, and your life is in the hands of more competent strangers and the water itself, that's a watery Anakim.

                                                            I think I have mentioned it before, but I don't think I've ever spelled it out in full.

                                                            So, The world's a scary place, I think we've established that. All that is frightening in the world is, by it's very nature, scary and intimidating to confront, understand, and come to terms with, and no one expects anyone to just nail harmonization and equanimity with fear out of the case, to turn it into empathy and care and community just like that. It's a process, one that, by it's nature, requires one to pull back from time to time, to have a barrier between you and it. More over, not all incarnations of fear are harmless-indeed, many of them will chew you up and leave you for dead on the rocks. Heroes aren't wrong when they question the sanity of walking naked into a sandstorm or why you don't just turn on a light to get through the dark. Our basic survival is often met with food and drink, clothing, housing, and fuel for warmth, but when it comes to security in a world full of monsters, at some point, we all need our safety blankets beyond the basics.

                                                            Hungers are those safety blankets-the hunger is for safety and security and comfort that comes from accruing power or collecting stuff or knowing all the secrets, being able to predate upon on others or know that you can ruin anything and burn any bridge, to rely upon a system of punishments or deftly transgress against such.

                                                            Spend enough time divorced from fear, these Hungers mark a transition from simple desire for security into desire for itself, a thing that is collected because it fills you, it satisfies you, maybe even makes you happy, a thing that spirals into itself and shells outward into homes, communities, cities, nations-civilization itself. Go through any place in the world you would like, you can see how the stones of each are laid in pursuit of those Hungers-wise civilizations even understand that transgression is a needful thing, as law and order can be cloying and rotting things, and that a little freedom and challenge in the populace releases pressures that might otherwise stress, strain, and break a society (look at civilization's long and sometimes complicated relationship with alcohol).

                                                            As a rule, as a general thing, it is understood as a good and meet thing to sate Hunger-we all need a measure of these things to be whole and secure in our own right, and to be wielded to foster and build it up for others. But it's one thing to sate, quite another to glut. If/When we do things like the family write ups for Hunger-if the key to Families is and that's all right, for Hunger it's and that is all well and good.....up to a point. Some will accrue their Hunger to an excessive amount, til they are divorced from genuine fear and their humanity, some will starve others of their Hungers. Beasts will sometimes pick targets because their abundance of Satiety is leading them to mute their psychospiritual senses and connections, sometimes they'll go after someone because they're being an asshole in pursuit or success with their Hunger.....or sometimes because they just want it.

                                                            The Three Main Sections I'd have in a new core would be something like To Take And Waste (since at a minimum every Beast must take the representation of someone's Hunger to sate their own, and the closest kin to that is to destroy it), To Keep and Build (since a lot of Beasts will of course incorproate their gains into their Lives and Lairs, and build from there), and To Return and Gift (because sometimes, the point is that someone just needs to remember what it was like without the blanket, and thus a Beast can give it back after the fact, or otherwise gift it to worthier, wiser people-or just whoever they want to).

                                                            The Insatiable have the clearest relationship to Hunger, namely in that in their over-satiation, they demonstrate the folly of trying to divorce away from fear, and with it the world and the animal self-that no amount of Satiation can actually make you safe, secure, comfortable. Heroes contrast that, and almost prove the Insatiable's point-in that, since they have such an abundance of Hunger and it's Satiation, how could any think they're afraid, putting the cart before the horse and presenting persona as self.

                                                            But yeah, the tl;dr of it all is "If Family is Fear, Hunger is Security". Hunger is the reason Beasts have just as much in common with the Spring Court as they do the Autumn Court.
