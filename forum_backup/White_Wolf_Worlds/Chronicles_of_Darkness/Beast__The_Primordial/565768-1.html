    Originally posted by MCN View Post
    Maybe people are having problems with this question because they misunderstand what deconstruction means?

    Deconstruction means to nothing more than an in depth analysis of the story. Naturally, since this is a game, we analyze the myth through playing it. We play it by having a reinterpretation of the story as the setting. In this way, its a deconstruction, no matter if it was a subversion, reconstruction, or modern interpretation, simply because the analysis is a natural consequence of playing the game. That's all there is to it. We're looking at the different parts of the monomyth as they come up in play.

    Its not like we're talking about opposite terms here. Deconstruct, modern interpretation, subversion, and reconstruction are not opposites here, they're not mutually exclusive terms. Honestly, they're all part of the same thing.

    I really hate reducing things down to dictionary definitions, but that's what it sounds like to me. A misunderstanding over the terms used. We can't really have a discussion if we're all misunderstanding what each other say.
    I think you are conflating experience of a thing with analysis of a thing, and that's led to a weird conclusion where constructing a structure is somehow the same as deconstructing a structure. I agree that we aren't talking about opposite or necessarily mutually exclusive terms, but we are talking about terms with different meanings. To deconstruct a work is to reveal the ways it is not complete on its own, that it is still attached to the umbilical of the transcendental signifier, and that it is standing on corpses it claims aren't there. If it was merely to recognize that there are parts to the whole, Derrida wouldn't have a problem with Saussure. He does however, because the Professor hadn't even realized how he privileged the spoken word over the written in his work on the signifier/signified relationship.

                        I think Beast is not about fantasy racism, but could be. The big problem is Hunger, which enforces modes of monstrous behavior. There is room for nuance in how you address that psychological craving for hording/dominance/whatever, but that desire is still there.

                        That's part of why I find your discussion of Changeling as being about fulfilling archetypes fascinating, because I have the exact opposite view. Changelings are forced into roles to the point where it changes their nature, but the moment they escape Arcadia, they don't have to do that any more. That the Spring Court kind of imitates the weird fairy tale masquerade lifestyle of the Keepers concerns the other Courts, even though the Spring courtiers would argue they are just trying to take control of that part of their life. The Winter Court could arguably be called "The Court of Not Paying Any Attention To That Time".

                        Beasts, on the other hand, chose to take on their roles (though some comments suggest that might be changing a bit in the revision). They can certainly grow disillusioned with that decision, but they can't really escape it. Even if a Beast joins a monastery, they will still have that Hunger. It's part of them forever. Even in the Apex (which most directly resembles the Atonement with the Father->Apotheosis->Return format of the Campbellian monomyth), it's still there. It just doesn't have as much power (I would say because they've gone through the Temptress stage, but whatever).

                        Even worse, the Beast within kind of reinforces that behavior. Don't feed enough, and it goes hunting itself... Which at least in the original draft was the primary way Heroes formed. Is it a coincidence that a starving Beast creates entities which remind the Beast of its distance from humanity?
