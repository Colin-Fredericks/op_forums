    Not Inherently Evil??

    02-15-2016, 01:52 AM
    Ok, at the behest of some people's comments in another thread I tracked down the Preview PDF that is available to the public to read the Heroes information. I figured I would read the whole thing just to get a feel for how it works an- HOLY CRAP WHAT THE F*CK IS WRONG WITH YOU PEOPLE?!?!

    As a survivor of mental and emotional abuse from a predator pretending to be a loving family member, what I read horrified me to my core. It seems that, if I was reading it right, Beasts *must* inflict some kind of pain on their neighbors to feed their "special souls". In essence Beasts are abusers but they seem to justify it by targeting people who 'deserve' it. Oh, god, that is not going to sound familiar to abuse survivors AT ALL.

    Now, I have been playing RPG's since the early-80's. Odds are if a game has come out in the last 30-years or so I've played it. I've played lot of Horror RPG's of various types, I've even played some of the oWoD Black Dog label games and worse. But this game practically requires the players to learn how to and play out doing deep psychological damage and long term mental scarring as part of the basic game mechanic.

    I understand that the authors/moderators (at least the two I have interacted with so far) have a rather non "black and white" sense of morality, but a game where you are tormenting people with your fantastical dragon soul and if people object to it then they are evil bigots who don't understand you seems to be beyond "playing a monster" and possibly reaching "Inherently Evil". This certainly seems to be beyond where the other "monster" game lines go to. When vampires skew evil, for example, they typically see themselves as apex predators; detached predators. If you look at the Invictus or the Sanctum they have opinions about human masses and not individual people. We still can acknowledge that as an evil perspective but it's sensible. I think it's the fact that Beasts tend to judge their prey on a very personal, often petty scale that would make even a vampire raise an eyebrow.

    As TVTropes puts it, "Beasts cannot avoid their need to feed. Even the weakest of Beasts, those who cling most strongly to their human origins, have a choice: either deliberately find someone and scare them, as appropriate for their Hunger, once per week, or else cause nightmares in random people, which will potentially create some loony who will happily maim, kill and burn innocent bystanders to find and kill them. The "best" Beasts are those who accept their Hunger and find a way to sate it that minimizes the harm they cause, as several sample characters mentioned in the corebook show."

    Now you can try to justify the seeding of fear and apprehension as a good thing - giving people something to overcome and better themselves, etc - but THAT IS LITERALLY THEY TYPE OF THING THAT REAL-LIFE ABUSERS SAY TO JUSTIFY THEIR ATROCITIES. Even the powers, with names like "Everyone Hates You," "You Deserve This," and "Everything You Do is Worthless" read like the a laundry-list of psychosis-causing phraseology. The players know from the very beginning that they are truly evil creatures intended to cause horror, regardless of how they try to justify it or minimize their harm. Heroes, for all their fanaticism and flaws, are trying to stop a self-admitted monster from spreading nightmares across... well.. everywhere but somehow Beasts have the moral high ground?

    At least one poster in the other threat suggested that Beasts, while monsters, could be heroes. Having now read the book I literally do not see how that is possible. I may be missing something but this might as well have been called Sadist: the Abuser instead. Now, I will admit that, given my background, I am not exactly coming to this game with no baggage. SO...

    Explain to me how sounds like a fun experience which will totally not bring the wrong kind of attention.
    Last edited by Feonorx; 02-15-2016, 01:57 AM.

    Yes, yes, yes... I know it is a horror game.

          You're pretty clearly looking at the preview draft, which lacks some of the revisions that adjusted things — Heroes are no longer created by Beasts, for instance — but for brevity's sake (and in the interest of not losing too much sleep on the night before an early shift) I'm going to just quote the Themes section of the backer copy of the full book:

          Originally posted by No Neat Little Boxes
          Beasts are not “good guys.” They terrify people in order to feed their Horrors. Although their culture teaches that they do so in order to impart important lessons, their peculiar form of pedagogy is entirely optional. A Beast is capable of being a force for wisdom, even for “good” in the World of Darkness, but doing so is entirely up to the Beast. Likewise, a Beast primary enemy, the Hero, believes that killing the Beast is the right, objectively moral thing to do. The Beast disagrees. Neither of them is necessarily wrong.

          A kind of dichotomy — the Beast and the Human — is an integral part of Beast. That isn’t to suggest any kind of reconciliation; a Beast can never be fully human. Perhaps, though, Humanity is just one more type of monster; the fact that any human can become a Beast is an important part of that. That applies to many of the other types of supernatural being (anyone can theoretically become a vampire, mage, changeling, or Sin-Eater, too, since those conditions are all foisted upon people from the outside), but for a Beast, the potential to become the monster is right there, in the deepest, darkest part of humanity’s nightmares, locked into their very genetic code. The characters in Beast are not the most evil parts of humanity. They’re the scariest parts of humanity, because they are the stuff of nightmares.
          Originally posted by You Don't Choose Your Family
          Monsters don’t choose to be monsters, but, looking at Greek myths, one often finds an element of fatalism or determinism. Medusa and her sisters are sometimes listed as children of Echidna, but there are also legends that Athena cursed Medusa for sleeping with Poseidon. That notion that monsters are responsible for their own nature plays into the rather childish, but pervasive, notion that everyone and everything faces the same choices. On a sociopolitical level, we see it when people blame the poor for being poor, as though it was a choice (and a moral failing). In Beast, Heroes see the Begotten as irredeemable because of what they are — but the Beasts cannot be otherwise. Even Beasts that “choose” to be Beasts because another of their kind offered the Devouring aren’t really making a choice; they know what they are. The Devouring just confirms it.
          Originally posted by To Thine Own Self Be True
          Beasts are primal, elemental, and ultimately simple, right? The Horror is, at least. An individual Beast might have a job, friends, family, lovers, children, and all the while they are also a powerful and hungry being from the nightmares of early humanity. They cannot ignore the Horror, or it runs wild through local dreams and attracts the notice of heroes. They cannot ignore the human part of their lives, or they slowly lose touch with the world and become cruel and savage. Beasts have to be true to themselves, but being true to themselves doesn’t excuse them from the consequences.

                      As an aside, I remember when the game came out and a lot of people took it as a metaphor for sexuality. Now it's getting compared to abuse dynamics. Both are interesting angles.

                      I think OP's not too far off the mark - I mean, yes, horrors NEED to be fed, and fear is their bread and butter. But there are a couple of nuances which I think are important:
                      - Abusers use made up excuses. A Beat's one is real. This does not make the consequences less disgusting - but the Beast HAS to feed.
                      - Feeding on the same victim again and again is discouraged. I think there is a difference between long term abuse and a once off burst of fear inflicted on (in all likelihood) a stranger.
                      - Beasts have absolutely no requirement to like who they are or what they do. Indeed, two of the inheritances are what I see as escape routes, killing off the human bit to make the horror either a purely spiritual nightmare, or an actual monster.
                      - 'Heroes' are 'wrong', in that their desire to kill beasts is lead by a form of megalomania. But regular people just defending themselves - nothing wrong there, or right either.
                      - There - are - worst things than Beasts out there, which are worse the hard lessons they teach. Being frightened off by a horror once might be worth it if it prevents one from becoming addicted to being fed on by a vampire, or torn to pieces by a werewolf on her territory. The World of Darkness is a generally shitty place for mortals - so while Beasts certainly aren't good news, they're not necessarily the worst option.
                      - The whole "lesson teaching" aspect, as presented in the final PDF, is a way for Beast to make the most out of a bad situation. It clearly states the Horror doesn't care about the lesson - but Beasts themselves try to make themselves into something at least marginally better.
                      - The nightmares' horrible names are just that - they're terrifying because they're relevant psychological fear. But by definition they're inflicted through supernatural means, and temporary.

                      To conclude, here are a couple of feeding examples from my campaign which, while not necessarily heroic, are at least different from abuse in my mind:
                      - Threatening a tyrannical manager with a lawsuit, to stop her from tormenting an employee (Power)
                      - Creating a victim free incident at an oil drill, to have it shut down and protect the local eco-system (Ruin)
                      - Buying a coin from a salesman who didn't realize its true value (Hoard)
                      - Hunting down a werewolf hunter who was about to shoot a pack and their immediate family/friends (Predator)
                      - Punishing a ghoul who was trying to sneak on the brood's home base to murder them (Punishment)

                                          Nope, not doing this again, not if you start with that tone and accusations. I hated it in the Kickstarter, I hated it every single time it happened again and I hate it now. I understand emotional baggage and all but that's just counterproductive.

                                          Bullet points:

                                          - You're not forced to enjoy the effect you have on people, and never were
                                          - The thing about "I do this to people but it's ok because I say they deserved it?". It's a possible rationale but it's not the ultimate excuse, nor the only one, nor an absolute that's objectively valid and again, it NEVER was
                                          - The Horror is a monster, the Beast does not have to
                                          - The fact that a Beast has to do unpleasant thing to survive does not mean that those things have no consequences nor that Beasts are excusable
                                          - The types of action you need to feed are not only variables of torture, psychological or not. There's plenty of ways, not to mention the fact that you can tag along supernaturals that do their thing and feed by merely being there.
                                          - Tv Tropes, as much as it can be useful, is made by all sorts of people with no limitations and it's not the final judge nor impartially correct by default on anything.
                                          - The revised book offers indeed a more positive role for Beasts in the world if they choose to take it and deals with many of the most problematic points (or at least those who were dangerously open to negative, and I guess legitimate, interpretations)
                                          - The point, and I mean the main source of drama of the whole game, is to find a balance with your horrifying needs and your own morale. It can be difficult, it can be easy, but it's that.

                                          The Beasts I've enjoyed more making are those who try to do good no matter what their monstrous nature demands. And the fact is, they're not oddities: they're the actual norm. Merging with a Horror does not make your morality disappear. Beasts are people, with alle the negatives and positives meanings of the term, and that means they can as bad or as good as you want them.

                                          The people who love this game are not assholes, nor sociopaths nor abuse justifiers. And every single time we have to justify ourselves in front of someone who comes around and begins by making a certain type of assumption seasoned with a variable amount of bile, I cannot help but to think that they've read the book (yes, even the preview, which I completely agree had to be revised to solve many problems) while already having made up their minds about the game and, perhaps unconsciously, overlooked many things that went against this interpretation.

                                          At least you show a degree of willingness to reapproach the game with an open mind, which is more than many people did and is a commendable effort. But I'm getting tired of that.

                                                          Beast deals with some pretty heavy themes, it's true, but while Beasts certainly can be vicious abusers they really aren't forced to.

                                                          Probably the single biggest mechanic that you (and a lot of other people) overlook is Family Dinner. A Beast can tag along with any other supernatural as they replenish their magical fuel and gain Satiety from it. My Anakim character follows his buddies in the Summer Court to political protests and similar events and gets Satiety as they absorb Glamour. It's zero impact feeding, these people were already angry, and arguably doing something productive with that anger. The Changelings get their Glamour, Drake gets his Satiety, and nobody gets hurt because of it.

                                                          Even barring that ability, low Satiety feeding doesn't require you to inflict much if any in the way of lasting mental trauma. The hunger for prey specifically says animals count for low (and only low) Satiety, and the others generally have low impact examples.

                                                          Also, there are a lot of other monsters stalking the night and it's entirely possible to find a role feeding your hunger by protecting people. Drake feeds his hunger for power by fighting the freehold's enemies. Border Reavers, Huntsmen, Charlatans, Loyalists, it doesn't much matter, Drake is prepared to run them off or die trying.

                                                          Another good example from the same setting is Reaper, the city's Apex. She works as an EMT, and she feeds her hunger for prey on the people she saves. Their lives are in her hands, and she uses every bit of her training to save them all, but she gives them just enough of a scare to encourage them to be more careful and appreciate life a little more in the future. Life is precious and fleeting, and second chances are in short supply. When she's not doing that she works with the Autumn Court to patrol the bad parts of town at night, making sure the alleys are safe by scaring away the actual dangers that lurk there. So yeah, she scares a lot of people, which is why she's the Apex, but all of that fear is directed toward raising the average life expectancy of her city.
