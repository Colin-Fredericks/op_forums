        Being afraid of what having no inhibitions can do. Holding yourself back out of concern you may be capable of too much. Hiding away from making choices of your own will because you can't deal with the responsibility. Cowering at the implications that having nothing to tell you what to do also means you have no one or anything to rely on when you're unsure of what to do. The way people always create and flock to hierarchies, unable to persist in meaningful cohabitation without someone to enforce rules, regulations, and everything else that is necessary for society, but restricts one's actions.

        This is brainspew, but the idea is that Beasts are primal fears. And this sounds like a very doable primal fear ingrained into humanity's psyche to me.

                                                I just want to throw in that fear of freedom is actually pretty common, just less with a notion to personal freedom and more to the idea of universal freedom. People tend to react to the idea of anarchy with horror and revulsion, because a world without restrictions is one with a large bunch of bastards willing and able to do what they want.

                                                A Beast that represents something with no fear of retribution is something that's scary; the usual defenses of civilization don't work against it. It reminds you that there's nothing in the world stopping it - or anyone else - from killing you; no invisible force field made by the police or what have you. A king's army never slays the dragon, after all...
