                        Before I pick up the pdf, what are the new Families and Hungers like?
                        WELL.

                        (as a heads up, this is my impressions from the splat write-ups only and not the expanded "how to play [Family/Hunger]" section before it)

                        Families

                        Inguma: Nightmares of the Other

                        Called as the youngest Family, the Outsiders preys on the fears of the people who don't fit in. Who are anyone who don't fit the cultural norm of a community. This Beast could be someone who deliberately disregards common dress or custom, or the guy that does, on the surface, fit into the community, until people realize that no one knows who this guy is. Some of the cultural monsters that belong to this family include the Haitian Mètminwi, the Japanese noppera-bo, and the Albanian Catalan.

                        So yeah, pretty straight-forward all considering. Fear of Other is an easily understandable and relatable fear that one could do a lot with. I can picture depicting them as Lovecraft horrors like Deep Ones or even modern stuff like Slenderman...but not much else.

                        One major criticism from me is the Lives section and how...odd the different Begotten write-ups are in it:
                        • The first one with the flight attendant and the way her thing is described (reminding the passengers of a plane that she might be "the last person I ever see on the ground"), I have no idea how it connects to "fear of the other".
                        • The second write-up involves a homeless man, panning for change. And it has the specific mention of "tech hipsters" avoiding the homeless man and avoid tipping him, because they fear of how "one bad business decision could turn them into him"...but then go on to say that the bankers flip him a coin for good luck before their big meeting. I'm...not sure what this write-up is trying to say.
                        • The third one is the most uncomfortable: it's about a guy that's been watching/stalking a woman who he deduces has been stood-up by a date. That the guy is contemplating swooping him to charm her, that he'll: "throw in a few attraction spikes, initiate kino a couple times, and she’ll be thinking of him all night. Whether she wants to or not." Uh...yeaaaahhh...even then, still have no idea what this does on relating back to "Fear of the Other". Also, I have no idea what PUA training, or "initiate kino" is.
                        • The fourth involves a consultant to a company giving a presentation. And how her presentation involves...cutting down on the number of employees in the HR department.


                        So...yeah. Lives section leaves me confused. Rest of the write-up is fine though.

                        Additionally, I believe a while ago, atamajakki expressed a concern about the Inguma write-up getting into uncomfortable territory of having fear of the Other be equivalent to fear of oppressed minorities...no, the splat write-up avoids calling attention to any specific demographic. The only thing that comes remotely close is the mention of a woman clutching her purse and having to walk through the "bad" part of town...but that's about it. It does not call attention to specific racial or ethnic demographic. And, well, broadly speaking, most major urban areas in any part of the world has at least *a* bad part of town. So just mentioning it doesn't really raise any red flags, at least to me.

                        Talassii: Nightmares of Confinement

                        The Captors are hinted at being the rarest and also the most misunderstood by the other Families. Mostly due to an unfortunate stigma left from a Hero labeling them as kidnappers and rapists. Something that the Captors have been working against all these years later, retraining themselves from ever laying a hand on their prey and indulging in any unnecessarily cruel and unusual punishments. That it's all about capturing and holding their targets, and depriving them of their home and safe place. Cultural monsters for this Family include the Central African eloko, the Slavic leshy, and the Brazilian encantado.

                        When I first heard about this Family I was...initially kind of confused. I thought that "Confinement" seemed like an overly specific and niche type of fear. The kind that really didn't provide enough plot and story hooks to work with and was a paper-thin character concept.

                        Of course, my mind was changed when I actually read the splat write-up.

                        The write-up frames it as being the loss of freedom and control. Of taking away the familiar and comforting environment of the prey and having them deal with the new and unfamiliar surroundings to them. So...yeah, that sold the Family to me, and opens a lot more story hooks then I initially thought.

                        Gonna save a different post for Hungers, since this one is getting long already.

                                    Originally posted by Beriorn View Post
                                    Are the Heralds, cults and Horrorspawn any fun? Or are they too cumbersome to use in regular play?
                                    Nope, they're pretty awesome. A Cult is mechanically just a Staff with a specialized function and some potential extra Merits (and risk of becoming Heroes if you abuse them), a Herald is a guy with some Beast abilities who is completely playable. Horrorspawn are more complex, but if you can make a Horror using the notebook, you can make them; only real bookkeeping is keeping track of the Satiety investment and preventing the Horror from reabsorbing them.
