                          I think my main issue with Beast is that it seems to be treading a lot of the same water that Vampire does; "I'm a hungry monster who teaches mortals with fear" has been the schtick of the Lancea Sanctum for over a decade now, and vampires are a lot easier to grok than incarnate fears of humanity who may have always been you or maybe ate your soul? Even ignoring the first draft debacle, it remains that the game largely feels kind of directionless (do Beasts just go out and fuck with people until they need to fight the inexplicably-coded-as-evil Heroes?) and that the crossover element seems less baked in and more there because the dev said so. Hell, Mage feels more like a crossover game than Beast does.

                          I'd love to hear some non-confrontational pitches from folks, because I have a hard time seeing the appeal of Beast and am still very much haunted by the initial draft.

                              They say not to shoot the messenger, but banshees still get a bad rep. When your legend overshadows your life for too long, you become a particularly unsubtle siren warning of a particular flavor of The World Is Not Safe. Even if you're not starving yourself, people will pick up on something from the big and little bite marks you and yours make at the edges of their social circles.

                              They called Satan the dragon and his fellows great beasts; you're the Adversary. You live in the Id Vortex. You're a de facto potent chunk of the narrative-driven collective human unconscious trying to find its nuances in how other monsters shake the mortal soul, and one of the easiest ways to accomplish that is to learn by doing.

                              They say that life is suffering; subject or object, you are Suffering. Your ties to other supernatural beings manifest in the ability to refine grains of terror from the fears they express and inhabit — not just the things that make them scary, but the things that make them scared. Experiencing a monster's powers firsthand is one of the easiest ways to learn what makes them scary. All of them save the most patient (and as an etymology nerd I implore you to examine the root of that adjective) will eventually be driven away unless they have nowhere else to go.

                              They say monsters are selfish, but the self is a deep road with many turnings. You offer self-knowledge of the fundamental sort: what are the people you interact with as a monster like at their cores, in their hearts-of-hearts? What are they like in the dark? You are uniquely well-placed to find out.

                              They always describe the old monsters in terms of other creatures. The chimera doesn't have three chimera heads, it has the heads of a lion and a snake and a goat just like Cousin Ferdinand has the same eyes you got from Grandma Maggie. The dragon keeps many beasts inside its skin, and it partakes of those qualities that make it strongest and most terrifying.

                              tl;dr Beasts as a whole are hooked up to almost every supernatural actor in the setting at the level of theme and motif; making and testing personal connections does triple duty as a course of self-discovery, a means of gaining supernatural power, and a process of exploring the innermost depths of the universal human psyche; and the stories that can be spun from having to balance hungers that threaten to drive a wedge between you and human society with the common need for a place to belong are potent, if heavy, stuff.

                                  Alright, like nofather, I'm not exactly a professional at this, so forgive any missteps.

                                  The monster haunted your dreams for so long. Maybe you confronted it on your own, facing down the thing that haunted you only to see that it was just a potential part of yourself waiting for you to embrace it. Maybe another aided you in doing so. Whatever the case you chose to be Devoured, and in doing so were reborn anew.

                                  Now your Soul is a mighty beast, an incarnation of the fears within the souls of man. This gives you power, from inhuman strength or beauty or intellect to more supernatural gifts like a flaming breath or the form of a swarm of insects. You also have more subtle gifts, psychic scalpels born from the fault-lines of the human and abhuman mind.

                                  But at the same time you bear a terrible Hunger, for it is the nature of monsters to be driven by such wants. You hoard or hunt or ruin because your nature requires it, like your human peers require water. The other monsters speak of teaching through fear, an imperfect mission that never the less gives a purpose to what would otherwise be mindless feeding.

                                  Of course, not all agree with this. Many would argue that you are a blight that should not exist. Maybe they're right. Some among these are brave humans who hold a candle to light the darkness you call home. Others, the stories say, were once your partners in a great dance of hope and fear that made mankind stronger; now they come to you mad-eyed and bearing power ripped from the corpses of your fallen brothers and sisters, seeking validation in murder to assuage the holes in their soul.

                                  But not all being in this world are you foes. Many of the creatures normal people fear you call Kin, whether from a single progenitor or through the primordial ooze of your nature. Not all are things you would join with, and many you might find abhorrent. Others are like you, once-men who struggle to find a place between survival and morality. Your souls touch each other, and you may grant them strength even as they grant you succor and unknowingly teach you the fears of their soul.

                                  So what will you do, little Timori, one both fearsome and fearful? Will you feed and feed and feed until the light fades from your eyes and the Heroes are made righteous merely through your slaying? Will you seek to make yourself useful through fear, punishing the wicked or trying to teach what others need to learn? Will you immerse yourself in your Kin, giving them strength so you can be a net gain to someone? Or will you seek your Inheritance, throwing away your humanity in hopes it will numb your pain?*

                                  No matter what, you are Begotten. Either find a way to live with that, or do not live at all.

                                  Beast the Primordial,as I see it, is a game of monstrous Hungers and finding a purpose, of defining yourself and making a niche, whether in the monsters you call family or trying to benefit the world or even just being a jerk because doing so means you don't have to be human enough to care anymore. But no matter what, you must find a path to walk down, a way to be. Otherwise, why not listen to the calls of the slayers that name themselves Heroes and save the world from your existance?

                                  Sorry if that's not a good pitch.

                                      This is the way that I look at Beast. You are someone with a problem, a hunger that will cause bad things to happen if you feed it and bad things to happen if you don't feed it. It's a shitty situation, but you didn't choose it and there's no way to undo it. What matters are the choices you make to deal with it. You can try to starve it, feeding that hunger just enough that the monster doesn't go out on its own rampage, but you'll pretty much be throwing away any hope of having a normal life. Some would say that's a worthy sacrifice, others won't accept that. You can try to keep it fed as best you can, making the monster pliant and docile, so you can have something resembling an actual human existence. Feeding is perilous though, how do you do it? Do you try to find a method of feeding that will inflict the least harm, living conservatively enough that you don't have to feed more than once in a while? Do you try to embrace the whole "teaching through fear" thing? Do you try to use the normal life it gives you to do good in the world to make up for the harm you cause by feeding? Its all up to you. Alternatively you can flip the whole thing on its side, cultivating kinship with the other supernatural denizens and feeding vicariously through them, a method that is kind of a separate game from the rest of Beast but one that some might consider to be the least objectionable option presented to you.

                                      Dealing with Heroes is very much the same way. How self conscious you want to be about the way Heroes get made and how they're also stuck with a raw deal they didn't ask for is entirely in your court. Meaningful reflection on the topic is made more difficult by the fact that they're still trying to kill you most of the time and their particular neuroses can make them hard to sympathize with, but who said the right thing was always the easy thing?

                                              To be honest, I don't like a lot of the changes they made. The original draft may have been controversial, but it was controversial in a cool, thought-provoking way. The finished draft is still controversial, but more over accusations of being watered down and needlessly PC. However, here are some of the key changes (for better or worse):
                                              • The "ugly duckling" theme has been removed. Rather than being born a monster, Beasts are now closer to a Dreamspeaker (Awakening-style) gone wrong, one that's fused with a goetic demon
                                              • Many of the examples have been toned down. Rather than slaughter people, a number of sample Beasts now only scare people. And examples formerly involving children (such as Mrs. Winters preying on the neighborhood bully) have had the ages changed, or been dropped entirely
                                              • Heroes have been beefed up, apparently with an eye to making them available as PCs in a later supplement. They're no longer obsessed psycophaths provoked by Beast hunts - such characters still exist, but now they're just insane/degenerate members of the Hero splat.
                                              • New rules for merging Lairs, along with the introduction of the Apex - the supernatural creature that most terrifies the locals, and whose existence colors all the Lairs in the region
                                              And yet, there are still a lot of good ideas, and memorable color. Satchel and Zealous Christian24 have very good pitches, but here's an excerpt from the book, where a werewolf encounters a Beast for the first time:
