    Originally posted by Magnum Opus View Post
    I think the main of my concern is not with each individual powers, but from the fact that ALL Beasts have them built in, unlike Vampires or Mages or whatever you're throwing at me. I never argued that You Are Meat is too strong or that a Makara should not be a giant fish, I argued that innate abilities of a whole splat basically swat away a lot of powers that other people paid for in XP or built-in with their characters.
    Mages and Changelings need to pay for abilities that let them enter a berserker-like state, while vamps and wolves get it for free. Vamps and wolves need to pay for the ability to sense other supernaturals or magic, but mages get Mage Sight stuff innately. Changelings can make pledges, while mages need to invest in Fate and vamps need to use Invictus Oaths for an equivalent. Don't get me started on innate versus purchased healing abilities.

    All this is just part of every different game's niches. Each of the supernaturals are better at some things than other supernaturals. One of Beast: the Primordial's niches is "making crossover friends." Furthermore, that's pretty much the extent of a Beast's innate abilities - facilitating crossover play. Their only innate ability that somehow doesn't relate to dealing with other supers? Invoking Lair traits. That's it.

    Vampires are all about the physical and mind controls. Werewolves hunt. Mages study. Prometheans survive, changelings have wishes and illusions, demons are the perfect spies. Every game has its niche with a wealth of innate abilities to support that niche, and you are begrudging the Beast their niche.


    Take any movie or cut-scenes from a video game. If the character is faced with a terrible opponent and in the shadows you see another character simply snap his fingers and then the main character defeats his opponent in a few strikes before we see the shadowy figure grin, who has the spotlight?
    Actually, its the main character who gets the spotlight. There's a lot of dismissive attitudes for support characters. I mean, really. Do you think that the person who makes all the hero's magical equipment gets any attention? Or the holy one who blesses the main character's weapons to smite evil? Of course not. The focus is always on the main character and their skill at using the supporter's magic, and the supporter fades into the background for the final battle as the main character flexes their new muscles. I really have no idea where you get the idea its anything but this - I've never a cut scene, movie, anime, or show where support characters are given heavy spotlight. Its the one who actually takes direct action that gets the recognition.


    You're right. If we forget that their mythos basically states that Werewolves and Prometheans are brothers, or that the Judges are in fact the sons and daughters of Tiamat. Same goes for Father Wolf, The Principle, The Exarchs, the whole underworld, the entirety of the Shadow, all the Gentries and Slashers (but not Hunters, because they're just humans, though Cursed humans are descendants).
    Actually, you really need to read far more in depth. Others have covered this topic in more depth.

    Family Resemblance: It goes beyond Auspex.
    But not Mage Sight. Furthermore, Auspex can pierce supernatural concealment, Family Resemblance can't.

    And why do you insist on comparing apples to oranges? Why does it matter if vampires need to buy a power? Beasts are built to fascilitate crossover play, vampires are not. Vampires are built with the ability to bloodbond people, be immortal, and have strong physical and healing abilities, while Beasts can't.

    I am not perfect, we all argue about stuff, and I may be biased toward not liking the splat.

    Then again a lot of people are biased towards liking it.
    A bit out of context, but let me say that this is about your conclusions being in a rather false direction, and not bias. You are making sweeping generalities that simply don't make sense. Its fine if you don't like the game. But your conclusions are just so wildly off base that it just doesn't make logical sense, and you are misquoting how the rules work.

Thicker Than Water helps to prevent you from appearing instantly threatening to an otherwise neutral party, it won't do a bit of good if the person you're approaching is prone to being paranoid toward everyone. Similarly, if someone is a raging supernatural xenophobe, their default behavior for non-(vampire, werebeast, sentient potato chip) is still 'AAAAAKILLITWITHFIRE'. A Pure werewolf isn't going to immediately think you're a Forsaken ally without reason, but if they just saw you BBQing with the local pack then the best you can hope for is an explanation of why they're gutting you.

                    To use a real-world example, think of it as a particularly affable black guy starting a conversation with a hardcore white supremacist. The Klansman type's internal response is going to be something along the lines of 'well, he seems like he might be a decent enough person if he weren't a (insert slur of choice here)'.

                    As far as Thicker Than Water registering as a supernatural ability, I would say that it's just an innate part of who and what the Beast is, and doesn't show up as distinct from their just standing around. It isn't a power you actively use, after all. I doubt that the Beasts are even aware that Thicker Than Water exists as a discrete ability, and most would probably be confused if someone accused them of using some kind of magic or other influence.

                    If I were going to define it in in-character terms (because I'm the kind of person who usually does) I'd say that what's probably happening is that on a subconscious level, the other critter is sensing the Beast's presence in the Primordial Nightmare and the sliver of recognition is enough to prevent them from feeling completely like a stranger. They are fears given flesh, how unfamiliar can they really be? Who has never been afraid of powerlessness, of the unknown, of judgement or pain or disfigurement?

                        Originally posted by SunlessNick View Post
                        I like the crossover stuff. But I also like the idea of it as an extension to the way (player character) mortals also dig into the dark corners of their world.
                        Crossover isn't really part of mortal chronicles. It is, however, a major part of Beast chronicles. Its the difference between adding flavor by sprinkling in spices, and having those spices being part of the recipe to begin with.

                        Originally posted by Magnum Opus View Post
                        Vampires without the All-Night Society is a game similar to Interview with a Vampire, a game of dark descent into inhumanity.
                        Werewolves without the hunt is a game of loss of control, of exploring a dark and twisted world and of making sure your friends and family don't get hurt by your very nature
                        Mages without a Mystery is a game of god-like hubris and power.
                        Changelings without the Courts is a game of trauma, trying to reassemble the pieces of your reality and getting a real life back.

                        I don't see why these concepts are wrong, so why would Beast be wrong without Crossover? It would simply be a game of Endless Hunger...as stated on the cover of the book.
                        First, let me say that Interview with a Vampire DID have a vampire society that the main characters interacted with. So that's actually a very bad example. Courts aren't the defining aspects of CtL - the defining feature is getting your life back from your abuser. Your counterpoint should be about changelings that don't care about reclaiming their lives. Mages kinda need mysteries, because without them, there is no God-Like power to reach. One comes with the other.

                        Next, let me say that there's nothing wrong, per say, but its not the way the default. There's a reason I said it was like Vampire without All Night Society. The politics is the default, but its possible to do other things. When I'm sitting down to a game of Vampire, I'm going to assume politics and covenants are in; that's kind of the general understanding of the game. Same thing with BtP.

                        Beasts seeking out other supernaturals is the default plot of the game. You can run without the other supers, but that's like cutting huge chunks out of the game to do it. Crossover isn't filling gaps in. They're the bricks and stones that make up the house, and you're making gaps by pulling it out. So, it is viable, but you're missing a huge chunk of the game that people should. If you invite people to play a game of Beast, they should be sitting down with the expectation that crossover will be there.
