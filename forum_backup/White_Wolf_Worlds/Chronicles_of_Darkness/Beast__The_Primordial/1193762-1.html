        A Beast's Lair is practically a part of their soul. When a Brood connects their lairs, they basically connect their souls.
        Beasts are family, they share similarities, recognize each other, and can relate to each other better than anyone else in most regards. On the other hand, family squabbles and fights, and family fights can be the nastiest. True for beasts, too. They can even invade each others souls, and hurt each other in ways most others cannot precise because of that inherent understanding. Just like family.
        That doesn't mean others cant understand the beast better than Begotten in some cases- Predators and uratha will find a lot of common ground, and vampire and tyrants probably share more common ground than they do with collectors.
        Just like you may have some more things in common with friends than with family.
        in essence, Beasts share common roots, and they feel that pull as anyone else does when they meet their family.

                Not really, due to the nature in which Prometheans work. The other splats are all about finding a way to cope with existence as a monster, Prometheans are about finding a way to cure it. They want to become humans, and their story revolves around the quest for a soul of their own. They also have a supernatural force that compels people to hate them, despite their best efforts. The other splats are feared and hated, sure, but that's more due to their behavioral consequences, rather than some eldritch power making people react to them.


                One aspect of family is that Beasts have the unique ability to evolve their powers through their bonds with each other. The more time they spend around other supernatural creatures, the more Nightmares they can discover and add to their list of powers. The core book encourages players to create their own Nightmares and Atavisms, offering a wider variety than what is presented in canon.

                    Also be mindful that the Horror's and the Lair's shape and features are based on dream logic, hence eminently malleable. When the Beast learns new tricks in terms of Atavisms, including out-of-family ones, the Horror and/or the Lair may transform to reflect that. When the Ugallu learns Heart of the Ocean, their roc Horror learns to dive in the depths and takes a little bit of the leviathan aspect, or perhaps their Lair manifested a watery feature. If the Makara learns Wings of the Raptor, the opposite happens to the Horror, or perhaps their Lair developed some windy aspect. Moreover, several Horror archetypes are conceptually and mystically vast and flexible enough to encompass most or almost all Atavisms in their thematic purview and potentially belong in many Families in the first place. Dragons are the most iconic example of such a catch-all Horror, but others exist.

                        Originally posted by Maitrecorbo View Post
                        So what if i have the concept of an eshmaki that is aquatic? Am i just hosed? Cause all the aquatic atavism are in the makara line.
                        Say a crocodilian monster with the lair being a murky river that the horror hides in.
                        So, the big thing to remember is that Eshmaki are not nightmares of being hunted, or nightmares of predators. The Lurkers are nightmares of darkness, so when coming up with your concept, it needs to be sure to take that into account. They're the fear of things being out there, hidden, things that can kill you without a thought. Eshmaki stalk and strike while remaining out of their quarry's sight. That's the important part: attacking before the enemy can see you. Whether that's done through stealth, moving so fast the enemy can't react or being so innocuous that the prey doesn't realize what you are until it's too late...

                        In this case, I think a monstrous crocodile is a fantastic idea for a Horror, and it totally can work with Eshmaki! You definitely want to emphasize the aspects that play up that fear of being attacked out of nowhere. A crocodile is good for that, because they're known for floating or sitting motionless, appearing to be harmless logs, or being juuuuust under the surface of the water. When their prey gets too close, they snap their jaws far faster than you would expect and then go back to being "just part of the scenery."

                        You describe your Lair as a murky river, which works well! That would probably be the Flooded Trait, representing the Chamber having a river running through it that intruders must contend with. Have you thought of other traits? (You get two to start with!) Downpour might be a fun one; it represents constant heavy rain, not strong enough to do damage, but that can make it hard to hear and see things. That would work well with your crocodile Horror; while crocs aren't exactly stealthy normally, it would help create those moments where the Heroes stop to rest next to a big fallen tree...only to realize the tree is you. The important part is that they don't see you until the violence begins.

                        As for Atavisms, representing your Horror's physical abilities, I think there's several options you can go with. One of the two starting ones must be from the Eshmaki's family trait list; the other one can be anything. I'd suggest Heart of the Ocean for the first one; while it's from the Makara list, it gives you a strong set of abilities related to swimming and hunting in the water. For the second, you could either go with Limb from Limb, representing your toothy maw, or a new option from the Beast Player's Guide called Ravenous Maw, which allows you to open your jaws wiiide and swallow anything, no matter how dangerous (or alive...) Both of those would fulfill your Family Atavism. Future additions could be Unbreakable (to represent a scaly, impenetrable hide) and Monster from the Deep (in your case, a mighty sweep of your tail)!

                        Of course, all of this is just suggestions. One of the beautiful things about Beast is that it's versatile and that there are so many options! Hope this helps, or at least sparks your own ideas about stuff you can do.

                                                            Originally posted by Maitrecorbo View Post
                                                            Yeah i was more imagining a hunger for prey. Tho i dont know what particular scheme he'd use. Or is lurking in the water for the unsuspecting to lunge at and drag in water be enough for a feeding scheme?
                                                            Alternatively i could see a hunger for ruin working by him disliking when humans use boats on his river and wrecking them.
                                                            Well, one thing to keep in mind is that most feeding will probably done in "human" form; that is, you don't need to feed in your Lair, as your Horror unless that's specifically what you want. This goes back to the "Beast is figurative" point I made earlier; you're feeding your monster soul by essentially re-enacting the sort of thing it would do, but in the "real world."

                                                            Example: An Eshmaki Predator (a Nightmare of Darkness with the Hunger for Prey) feeds by hunting down hunters. For this session, she follows a deer hunter out of town to the woods where he's planning to set up camp. That night, when he's set up in a tree to wait for the deer to wander by, she stalks around him, making just enough noise to let him know something is out there with him, but without letting him see her. (she can do this through both mundane stealth and her supernatural powers)

                                                            Eventually, he gets freaked out enough to come down and try and make it back to vehicle — only to find that she hot-wired the truck and moved it without him noticing. He starts panicking, firing into the darkness, and she hits him with a Nightmare right before knocking him unconscious. As he fades into blackness, she whispers, "There's always a better hunter..." (this is her Lesson)

                                                            Of course, the method Mad_Maudlin suggested also works really well! There are merits to both approaches. Staying human is simpler, and keeps people away from your Lair, where you're most vulnerable. (remember, the more you open Primordial Pathways to a Chamber, the easier it is for others to do the same thing) Taking things into the Primordial Dream lets you take on your Horror form, granting you a lot more power, and gives you total control over the environment. (you can let Tilts do a lot of the work) You also avoid the whole "forensic evidence" thing, especially if your Hunger is the kind that leaves bodies, rather than scared, but potentially wiser, prey.
