    To Kneel Before the Maw Final Mix^-The World Tree of Beast Society

    06-22-2022, 11:26 PM
    I really struggle to play Beast because there's no cohesiveness in the game, nothing that contextualizes my character's existence as a Beast; there's no Beast society; there's no Beast laws; there's no Beast traditions, and so on.
    “I saw the world I had walked since my birth and I understood how fragile it was, that the reality was a thin layer of icing on a great dark birthday cake writhing with grubs and nightmares and hunger.”
    --Neil Gaiman, The Ocean at the End of the Lane

    "I ask of thee! Art thou mankind?"
    "Nay! I am the world-The world inside the gourd."
    -Gakuto Mikumo, The Mystic Archives of Dantalian

    "A god is just a monster you kneel to."
    -Rasmus Berggreen, Fall of Gods

    So if this manages to go up, bad news everyone-we're not doing Kneel Before the Maw Part 2. It's scraped.

    In part, that's because we've discussed the pieces that make it up in the....................jesus fuck, too much time since that was a concept, but mostly because the FIRST quote here has been scratching at my brain for a bit here and it's only real solution to that is to basically spell out those two parts as a comprehensive whole.

    So, what exactly does that mean, particularly for those who have no idea what I'm talking about? Well, this essay is going to talk about what the internal world of Beast: the Primordial looks like, and address some of the hows and whys it functions that particular way. The direction of this exploration is going to start at the bottom with the individual Beasts and work it's way upwards towards the Dark Mother-though it's worth noting that that particular flow of ideal, power, and influence goes down the other way too, and is part of why some struggles and conflicts emerge from this way of looking at things. The specifics of that exploration is gonna start with Beasts, where their Devourings point them to go, and three broad mindsets/philosophies that they can fall into. From there, we're gonna build into Broods, the main form of society for the Begotten at all scopes of Chronicles, how they organize, grow, and both how they can attach other supernatural societies or act as an alternative for those supernaturals. We're going to then contextualize the pre-eminent forces that Broods build around and against, namely the immediate impact of Apexes in a setting and the larger background influence of Astral Legends and Gods, including those (more directly) within and without (or less directly within, if you'd prefer) of the family, Inheritors (particularly the Incarnate), and other Astrally Potent Entities, and how those shape or conflict in the shaping Broods. Finally, we're gonna end on the Dark Mother, the role she has in not-/directing the Begotten, and re-emphasizing the implication her various Children leave on who and how to be for Beasts at the ground level.

    Because the topic of "how much of this is in the book actually?" is a related topic to the quote that sparked all this, it bears disclosure on some of the make up of this, because while, yes, a lot of this is in the books, just buried under delivery, this overlook also includes results of a psuedo-developer mind drawing logical conclusions from what may be inferred by relevant non-explored spaces, themes, interactions, etc. Laying it out there, the majority of that stuff is that which is related to the Astral and features we hold adjacent to that, and the last bit is taking the three Children of the Dark Mother (being the Insatiable, Beasts, and Heroes, under some of the other psuedo-developer angle of revising Heroes) and allowing the philosophical and psychological profiles to be defined by each set of Children but not limited to them as attitudes on life. The reasons why are, for the former, that at this time we don't have a Storyteller's Guide that probably would have explored Beast's relationship to the Astral and the Dreamborn in that way but reasonably would have, and for the latter because honestly early on after the revisions, there was a lot of bitching that we could now boil down to "But what if I want my Beast to act like an Insatiable?" that I think could reasonably be addressed with a hearty "Sure, you can act like your natural antagonists! Good luck with how that turns out?. It's also a handy soft way of removing that sense of limitation that came with some of the mid-KS changes while also reinforcing the themes and conflicts of family. Everything else, though, is the result of critically reading the existing text-a whole fuck ton of critically reading and re-reading the existing text. Because perseverance against adversity.

    I'll leave a spoiler that basically gives a top-to-bottom look of influence to semi-counter the opposite angle we're taking and to otherwise provide an easy track of influence (and also clearly list where I'm extrapolating rather than just simply working from the text), but without further ado, let's stuff our faces with this.

    From Chrysalis to Storm Strewn Sky-Who The Devouring Leaves Behind and What They Become
    Let's start at the beginning-what do you do as Beasts? Well, You Build Your Lair. Okay, but why? And normally I would go into some psychological literary theory that mangles Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs, and don't you worry about that, I will, but let's take a different path today.

    First off, a reminder of what Lair is-Lair is a Beast's* psychospiritual presence in the Astral/Dreaming, the degree and depth of their reputation settles into the reality of a place and the memory of the people's in that place. As Goetia/weird-ass Dreamborn, Beasts cause ripples and leave scars with their actions (and, to a lesser extent, their intention) in the Astral, and the Dreaming and adjacent elements to it like, say, humanity remember it as a Beast grows as a symbol and collections of meanings derived from their being. (Mage has some adjacency to this, albeit with a game-ified accountabilty instead of just regular old accountability.)

    So to answer that question, the base answer to "Why Build Lair" fills the same occupational niche as "why drink blood" or "why wolf must hunt"-and that answer is "Because it's gonna happen anyways." If you were to create the most here-to-make-a-point-and-otherwise-break-an-argument non-character and established a Beast who won't commit suicide/Inheritance (particularly the Life Inheritances of the BPG) but otherwise did everything in their power to not engage with either the world as an actual person or with the game as a character, the resultant prowling of their Horror and their stark refusal to to apply direction or take accountability would still add up to build Lair over time, and it would probably develop into an incredibly bad one that leaves the asshole wondering why no one helps him when he finally goes to seek help for the Hero trying to kill him. The general point is that the act of being is an act that generates and builds Lair, although admittedly certain actions do it more than others, both more potently and more directly.

    And of course, that efficiency is the same road that leads one angsty vampire to having a blood doll on arm with any given call.. It doesn't take long for a Beast to realize there are benefits to courting deliberate work into their Lair for the power-or perhaps a little more directed, the arete, the excellence that comes from Lair bleeding into their Atavisms, merits, connections, and more in general. Since Lair is also related to one's "reputation", one's "legend", a cultivation of Lair also leads to other tangential benefits. First and foremost is against Heroes and Insatiable-it's easier to fight a wannabe storm-hero or a sense-destroying primitivistic-nihilist when people are more willing to close ranks with the Beast than join the cult of personality or crumble along with the systems in place before an increasingly present non-stability of the world. It's also pretty useful for all the reasons you might think-having yourself walk before you makes it easier to grab that snack when you need it, casts the shadow that makes it easier to score a big meal, makes like calling to like easier and lets you meet more of the people who like the cut of your jib, and otherwise opens doors into opportunities and weirdnesses that emerge. It's hard to argue with the benefit of meeting someone new and having them immediately grok that there's something about you that brings winters, the awareness that some people want to suck down your marrow, the comfort from lying in the dark. You can imagine the lot of specific, but making a place for yourself is just inherently beneficial.

    And finally, because as people who have accomplished a major act of individuation/actualization, who has fulfilled Maslow's missive that "What a man can be, he must be" and emerged the otherside a memetic thing of teeth, Lair introduces a new hunger from the psychospiritual satisfaction, a need to make a world, neither cruel no kind, out of themselves, to allow for their being, dark and harsh as it may be, to be contributive. Beasts recognize that they are id-shadow symbols that other people can project their own fear and hunger onto with them, and some utilize that to turn Satiation into a mini-Devouring for others, allowing them to recognize what fears they've built on and how their hungers have contributed or distracted from that. It's not like a Beast doesn't benefit from it either-by making Satiation a part of their own heroic cycle, fully embraced as such, they can recognize their potential to have had the same sort of superegos and personas as others, the same potential for frailty and weakness, and incorporate all of those into their own increasing legend. Not all Beasts get down and dirty with it, and of course not all Satiation can facilitate psychological and spiritual revelations, growth, and potentiality-sometimes, you just gotta grab a snack-but it's an ambition plenty of Beasts inevtiably do come to.

    Of course, that's one of the ways you can think about Lair. As Beasts go through the world and meet and conflict with other Beasts and the other Children, a Beast might not adhere to the rough mentalities that led govern their own transcendence. Beasts (and the other Children, though not as frequently) tend to find that, over time, most tend to adopt what seems to be the main driving thought behind the Three Children, that the Insatiable, Beasts, and Heroes have given rise to a the trains of being Insatiable, Beastly, or Heroic.
    • To be Insatiable is to accept Lair as natural consequence, and don't see much of a point in overthinking the rest. Eat, sleep, fuck, and be merry, and do it all again tomorrow. If someone comes after you, either you're strong enough and smart enough to take the fuckers, or the fuckers take you. Connections come from people recognizing the virtues of your strengths-the weak give of their flesh and soul in the hopes you'll direct your gaze against other predators after your herd, the equals will use you as much as you use them, and the strong will either chew you up or dominate you, and the only concern is if you're okay with that or if you want eventually rip the fucker's throat out. Reject the meaning that has burdened and confused the bonobos into making civilization-you've realized the bullshit for what it is, and you know those that get by in the world are those who embrace that we're all just animals, the whole of the law the song of the lizard brain and the ever hungry jungle.
    • To be Heroic is to accept Lair as overt dominion, to be the one who defines what and how and why the world is without being subject to it. They believe that they have become Nietzsche's ubermensch, to have arrived at the point where they can trans-value their values, but are also above such things-the lawmaker is not bound by their laws, for they can change the laws of reality to say so. The problems of this world aren't with them, really-it's everyone else who doesn't understand, who has not seen the wisdom and forms that naturally emerge from the world and refine themselves into their final answers. You have descended down into the darkest depths of your primordial psyche and have found in tangled roots of the spiral gardens of the world the an unoccupied with a godly throne that you, of all the scattered stars, were always meant to occupy. Those who have power over you may have grasped a greater transcendence, but the self is not fixed, and one throne can interlay over another. THose under you, in the meantime, need to accept that it's your bones that form the world, your blood the churn the seas-your will that invents-and names-lies and virtues in kind.
    • To be Beastly is to accept Lair as cyclical conversation, that understand that you get what you give and what goes around comes around. The power of a Beast kicks the wheel, but what the world takes from that and sends back is equal in it's own measure and beyond control, and so Beasts take care in how they make themselves in their transcendence. No self is singular, the fears and hungers they are is multitudinous, named legion, and those strangers who are also them can just as easily come back to chew on their leg or lift them out of the ditch-it falls on Beasts to to figure out how to best ensure one or the other. They are in and of the world, and the world is in and of them-to pretend they that fact, or that it does not come with what they themselves and others who to get out of the world, is to court disaster and spin the cycle in useless ways. They already know how to take-they will always take-so now it predominantly comes to how, why, and what to give back. Both low instinct and high philosophy have their predators, real and imagined, and it falls to Beasts to find out how to keep the circle going without crushing themselves under it.
    Beasts, of course, don't need to stick strictly to one of those all the time, or possibly instead embrace more niche outlooks-but as a general rule, a Beast needs to know what they are about as they get out and about, connecting to others and devouring their mythos for their own.

    These Monsters Like Me, These Bones Like the Earth-Broods and their Extended Mythologies
    Beasts, early on, form Broods out of a mixture of answering magical sympathy, convenience, and the fondness for some rather than others. Beasts know they aren't alone out their, and between the emergence of a new Beast casting ripples through the Primordial Dream attracting the other Children by way of their omens and a Beast recognizing and affiliating with other monsters, early Broods quickly conglomerate in that need to get and be together, for immediate survival and comfort(read: long time survival), and beneath those motives the recognition that monsters build their own Legends a Beast can hop into and use to feed their own Lair. Younger Broods tend to be a little more subject to be extensions of the issues and affairs of the monsters they get in contact with because of the haphazard mix of people they'll meet, and how individuals who join might have more immediate shit going on the currently actualized-and-starting-pretty-well-on-life-as-a-monster (comparatively), as opposed to a changeling might need to figure out what the hell they're going to do for a home, a vampire whose got to figure out how to climb the Invictus without getting dragged, or an elder Beast who has regular bitch-fights with Pain and needs someone to watch the fort while they go to try redefine reality again.**

    Regardless of how much they get into someone else's society, stay centered in Beast's usual set up, or define a new boundary wound/state of affairs, at some point a Brood*** starts jiving together and getting a real sense of who and what they are. They coordinate their Satiations in such a way as to not only make each one easier, but to allow for one to act as a stage for the next. Chambers are chosen, or made, to communicate the sort of monsters who dwell there, and what they value. Certain oppositions remain just opponents, but some take on a level of sacredness or profanity. As the Beasts in a Brood settle in, they begin to develop their own mythology, their own sense of what sort of monsters they want to be and how that works in relationship to each other. Beasts and Kin may break off when this happens due to a mismatch of personalities-rendered-as-worlds, but by this point, others may just as easily join in.

    Cults tend to have formed under a Beast around this time, at a minimum because the spreading of whispers makes it clear that the Beast is an alternative means of answers and that answer is not always "get chomped, scrub"-a Beast can get a landlord to shut up for a bit, findout what's going to give a child nightmares by going to the source, connect them to people who can buy the groceries after a check bounces, stuff like that-and at it's maximum because they embrace the mythology and beliefs about the way the world is that comes with that. Kin tend to join into the Brood for the same reasons, with an explicit acknowledgement that it also tends to work for them when either a) their own structures aren't providing enough from their end of the bargain, b) their own internal structures just aren't big or present enough to take immediacy over broods, or, most critically for Beast's to think about, c) when they want to do something that their own structures won't facilitate or tolerate, like werewolves and sin-eaters cannibalizing humans and ghosts, or mages and demons flirting with entities that make everyone else squirm. That last one is where it's important to know what any given Brood's lines are, and how they'll figure that out, because most often the first people to jump into an alternative powerful enough to keep pace with everything else are the ones who were too toxic for any former organization's tastes, and while some Broods are going to be all for "Yeah, fuck those assholes and their good taste and humanity", assumedly the majority is going to have a little more philosophical questioning to do.

    Of course, Older Broods may have their own idea of how monsters should be and do, and siblings in a family will interact how siblings in a family do-sometimes there's the deference to expertise, sometimes youth and brilliance walk hand in hand, sometimes the kids talk it out, and other times they're breaking each other's backs across the swing set. We'll talk in a bit about the broader forces that can lead to some organization and strata to the culture of a given Hive, but the universal ideal of "They're your family, treat them like it" is otherwise subject to what a given Brood's idea of handling family relations is supposed to look like and how much they're going encourage/enforce it beyond their own practice. At it's most extreme, multiple broods might come together to handle something big in particular-guarding access to a dangerous or sensitive occult site, harrying down and feeding the hell off of something, trying to rein in the other kids, whatever.**** It's not too common, though-for all the community building that is aspected in the way Beasts are wired, the end of their Lair is an individual basis, and you can only intertwine so many people into each other's lives like that before it begins to detract away from it. Beyond that, Broods tend to handle each other much like werewolf packs or Bound krewes do for their own.

    Okay, so that covers how the islands of the Beast archipelago***** form and deal with other on the small scale, but that's generally not helpful for people looking to create a complex Beast society, or on a more grounded level, what are the sort of forces and ideas that Broods form around-and that's because that's a subject with a lot of individual responses, but there are polestars to build around.

    Who is Like Unto The Beast? Who is Able to Make War with Him?-The Apex and Astral Gods as Centers of Society
    The most important mythopolitical factor in any given setting for Beasts is the Apex. At it's most basic level, this is because the Apex is currently the embodiment of the generic ideal Beasts go after-powerful while relevant for being directly plugged into the human ecosystem, the sort of being that no one can pin but everyone knows something about, the urban legend you tell your buddies while working the late shift because it's interesting and also because no, really, you need to understand you don't walk down 5th after midnight wearing blue. This is the sort of thing that a Beast can plug into and assume to bolster and fill out their own Lair, even simply by playing copycat-the world is wide enough for two Hook Handed Men, and that's a whole bunch of psychospiritual landscape that's easy to latch onto yourself if you're willing to play the game. As a general rule, few Apexes are islands unto themselves anyways-they usually operate in some circle or greater soceity, and their actions often need someone providing support underneath and to the side. All of this creates a tree a Beast can graft on to and grow mighty on the side, at least until they become mighty enough to set roots of their own and build up as their own variation on their own turf. Trickle down monstrosity at it's finest, Beast's relationship to the Apex can be as transactional as we've laid it out to keeping the Apex as a primordial figure, one that, sure, the Brood probably eventually plans on supplanting, but more transfer of mantle over time than active fighting.

    Of course, before we go too far into "So wait, Beasts are just the bootlicking toadies for the biggest, baddest, monster in the area?" territory, it's worth noting that that's just the most immediate and easy thing to tap into. Just as easy to recognize, though possibly less supported by surrounding elements, is to take one look at the Apex, say "Oh, FUCK that guy," and get your Misspent Youth on. Opposition is just as fertile a ground to build on, and it has a versatility to it too, based on why any given Brood is pitting themselves against the Apex, and what they think an Apex should look like. In any given mythology, the Apex can be a sideways figure to the mythos and ethos of the Brood to being it's dominant antagonist, the profane to the Children's sacred. The contrast of such a figure can be it's own fertile ground to become a nightmare legend to people for, with, and over, the antagonism giving it's own form and shape of who they are and how they predate. Noteworthy about this approach is that, while Broods who build around and along with the Apex tend to also seek and even accomplish integration with other societies, oppositional organizations tend to be more aggressive about who they bring into the Brood as an expression of a Brood's ideology-one Brood opposing a mage Apex might deliberately court deviants and changelings as a pointed argument about how the Awakened don't really use their power to prevent abuses to humanity******, while another Brood might deliberately court Predator Kings and Strix as a pointed opposition to their pretense of civilization and humanity over simply being and using power. Of course, some of them might still go toady, building around and underneath other candidates they'd rather elevate to the Apex position-but it's less likely than the former.

    Almost inevitably, this issue of thesis and antithesis regarding the Apex leads to various forms of synthesis, whether a Brood does get powerful enough that it can't keep going in the shadow of the Apex and needs to seperate into it's own thing, an oppositional force finally cools it and accepts some aspects of the Apex into their own being, or a Brood literally starts out that way trying to act as a peacekeepr and arbiter between the two. Synthesis Broods are more likely to emerge as their own dominant pillar of the supernatural community in an area, though again they could graft onto other powers and forces acting against either the Apex or one of their oppositionals. It's a complicated space to build in, but it has a lot that a Beast could iconographically draw from, both in terms of being and fighting against, and as a general rule, the formation of synthesis Broods comes from a setting that's probably ready to have someone and stop the cycle the emerges from the Apex's wake.

    But regardless of whether Broods form out of thesis, antithesis, or synthesis regarding the Apex, the biggest point is that the politics are often most firmly established by who the Apex is, what and why they believe about themselves, monsters, humanity, and the relationship between the two, and the wheres, whens, and hows they act on that and how it ripples out into the larger community. Apexes aren't empty characters, and being in the position often means being in a crossover capacity just because being the most powerful still-connected monster in the setting means that they probably interfere with mutliple spheres just on principle. A Prince murders some new punk Gangrel for fucking with his Elysium's equanmity, only to find out oops, that was a Changeling Beast-but still stands by it because of the risk it posed to the people he's supposed to not-kill and their safe operation in their city*******. In another city, The Sovereign of Clear Blue Skies and the Radiant Summer Sun takes their court to war against those vanishing people, and when it turns out that it's the local Pentacle in cooperation with mortal Conspiracies, their fury does not swerve from their judgment. And that's assuming any isolation-the more a setting has mosnters recognize each other as allies and resources in the night, the more involved an Apex is going to be with multiple societies activities, nevermind just their sheer impact on humanity and the way everyone responds to that.

    Of course, there are some sideways considerations to a Brood-after all, they may hew towards Local and Regional settings in the waking world, but the Astral/Dreaming is an inherently Global and Cosmic affair, and Beasts have an innate conenction to that. In that larger picture, all manner of Dreaming Gods, Divine Beasts, Sovereign Archetypes, Ascendant Horrors, and Inheritors (and more********) could take interests in Beasts, and visa versa. Broods that are a little more proactive might go searching for the Dreamborn who best embody the sort of monsters they want to be and the world they want to believe in, whether as it is or as it could be-but even if they don't, the world of the Dreaming Divine is just as likely to interfere with Beasts, as one of the most significant actors between the real and the ideal, and to offer patronage and guidance that helps faciltiate Lair as easily-you can see this in the idea of the Cults of the Incarnate In fact all of these basically just bring the principle of following the Apex out of the locality and into the starry reaches of the Astral. Nevertheless, an Astrally guided Brood can serve as a sideways force to the usual politics of the Children in any given setting, as they work to devour and become the legend of their Astral guides by working to their ends.

    Certainly the most prevalent of these are Beasts who decide to embody particular legends in their Family trees-after all, who knows better how to be the Minotaur than the first Horror to be the Minotaur? Beast can and do comb through the Astral to connect to their Progenitors to bolster their own Lair-after all, in a certain sense, any given Giant and the Progenitor-Beast of the Anakim are the same monsters, speaking from the point of Astral Sympathy, so by developing deeper connections with them, they take on their mantle and degree of Lair, same as when they take on the Lair of Kin they connect with.

    All of that comes back down to how and why Broods operate on the street, the weird idiosyncracies that can emerge from a given Brood all layering on top of each other, but it all comes back to that development of lair, of what it means to be an Apex Monster in the world that is devoured by both it's own meaning and it's constant risk of loss of relevance. And that cycle, as it climbs up and down the tree like Ratatosk, eventually reaches the top of the family, the parents of the monstrous family. It reaches the Progenitor-Beasts.

    It reaches the Dark Mother.

    Cry, Little Daughter, Love is with Your Mother-The Dark Mother and Her Presence in All Settings
    Other supernatural progenitors are distant. If Luna wants a pack to do something or be something, they send a Lune. If the Judges are cross, they burn Sekhem, or perhaps burn Emanations into reality with their gaze. If the Principle applies, it's qashmallim point the way. The God-Machine is ever-present but silent except by way of Messenger, the True Fae are more often than not on the other side of reality, the Cthonic Gods may not even be there. The Curses are manifold and systemic, the Watchtowers only shine-you get the idea. Sure, any of them can create business(read: usually problems, rarely help) for any given organization of monsters, but it's at an arm's length away.

    The Dark Mother is not that. The Dark Mother is with you, always. The Dark Mother is you. She may have already devoured you. You might be nothing more than the still dancing dream of the Dark Mother.

    Whatever the case, the general point is that unlike other big actors, the Dark Mother finds ways to get involved into her Children's lives*********, and that's a very pointy point of distinction. The Matriach of Monstrosity is in every Beast's ear, heart, and soul, and can give guidance or reproach whenever she wants. If She has a chore for a Brood to do, they'll know about it. If She needs a doctor, she can get a kid to be a doctor. She has a conspiracy, she has a whole family who listen close. The Apex of Apexes, the Legend Above All Legends, She Of A Thousand Dread Names And A Million Dark Deeds-she's in every Brood, every Beast, in every form of Monstrosity there is. If she whispered an order, perched as she is on her branch at the top of the tree, the worms at the roots would shiver and act on it.

    So with that, it's worth noting that her deal seems to be one of people breaching the chasm of becoming and being-which mostly makes sense. Dreamborn embody and are the things they are, so a goddess who is perpetually breaching the chasm of becoming and being in her own right would encourage more of the same in her Children. It's telling when some of her stories are things like getting one Beast to stop going after drugs and getting another one to do what they were going to do in the first place, but with the correct frame of mind. She swoops in when things are bad, sometimes, and helps her Children find themselves, sometimes by giving them the chance they need, sometimes helping them die the way they want. The Dark Mother makes a mission out of her Children's need to be, and provides holy wisdom, thick and dark like blood, to vivify and enliven her Children.

    But let's not have that mix up the issue-she does tell her Children to do things, to be what she wants them to be. The culture and society of Beasts is always being tossed in the currents of her will and want, her essence. If it's important for kids to know that there will be no killing, she will tell them. If it needs to be a blood bath, she will say "Let there be blood." Beasts don't have to follow her dictates, of course-what parent, even a good parent, has their kids listen to them a hundred percent of the time? who here would say the Dark Mother is always a good parent, if ever?-but what she says informs every Beast in a Hive, an underline of direction to the currents of a setting that rocks all the boats there. And like with any parent, those rules can change as a Beast matures and ages. Momma has an idea who who all Beasts should be, and those ideas will inform not only that Beast, but those around, and while some will accept that it's all individual ,some will hear Momma give a rule and think everyone should follow it. And others will, of course, rebel.

    And it's worth noting that, while the Dark Mother mostly has that goal of guiding Beasts into the Lair they were always going to dwell in, it's seemingly not her own only agenda, and she's not above her kids killing each other for it (she's not above it in her normal circumstances). She'll happily tell one child to kill another one so their blood an be used to complete a key to an unfathomable door. She assumedly still has her own Hungers, still thrives on boundaries to breach, taboos to enact, securities to break, desires to chase. As the bird that carries the sun and the leviathan that'll drown it, her Children exist in the wake of her movements and are often called to hold her hand and follow into dark, darker, darker still abysses. Such journeys leave no one unchanged, often anyone can see that.

    And it's not just her. The Progenitor Beasts, the grandparents in the attic, the serpents in the branches, the old and original monsters, the thoughtforms that have lingered since the plow first wounded the earth-they speak to and mingle with their family. They bring their squabbles to the table, ancient grudges that will never be fed, and convince Children of their rightness and wrongness all the time. They speak the folk wisdom only they can know, only they could conceive, and hope that Beasts understand it as common sense. They go to Broods and direct their mythologies, or try anyways. Any given Hive is bound to have collected stories of that time that Grandpa Thunder threw a hissy fit against Mountain, and the way that fucked with Colorado Springs for the better part of a week.

    Any given Hive is bound to have a collection of wisdoms from the "adults" of the monstrous family, particularly from the Dark Mother, and this often forms a soft set of commandments in the setting-and every Brood in particular is going to have their own recollections and stories of such that will inform who they are and how they interact with other monsters going forward. As any story with Beasts goes on, it becomes collectively harder to not see all the goings on of a setting as possibly some kind of statement, or story, or dream-a dream of Monstrosity, the Dark Mother.

    I'm Picturing A Jacob's Ladder Made of Nightmares-Conclusion
    So much like when I wrote up the To Kneel Before the Maw First Go, if I had to bring this all around to a point, it's that Beast not only has society, culture, traditions and such, but that it's also pretty well present in the books, if not really well explained-the books present Beast society as the collection of Broods co-ordianting and conflicting with each other about how to be, while higher powers up to the Dark Mother give direction and advice to the betterment or detriment of those Broods.

    The biggest problem that comes with grokking that is that it relies both on the individualistic definition of self in relationship to community and the concept of family, both of which are highly fluid and highly personalized aspects to the particular setting. There are universals, such as "Try and be your own person, but recognize your liability to end up like others before you" or "don't shit where you eat" or "You cannot eat the Prince until you understand why the Prince is there in the first place"(Chesterton's All Night Society), but a lot of it comes down to the particular wisdoms that permeate your setting. The game has a theme of To Thine Own Self, Be True as an expression of sheer, existential Hunger, and throws the truth and value of that against the equally trying and challenging truth and value of You Don't Choose Your Family as an expression of Kinship, and draws a lot of it's conflict from the question of what we owe each other and how we deliver on that. Beast is a simple enough game, but it's not easy, and it doesn't hold anyone's hand while doing so. Being true to yourself (or your fictional character's self) is hard work-it's to live as an alien, to accept your own will and no longer be a foreigner in the lands of your spirit. It'd be easier to be something concrete, something someone told you to be, like a warrior or a sage or a thief-easier still if there are active benefits and resources given to you for it. A Beast can do that, a Beast can be someone else. They'll have to be, in some ways, along the way, though hopefully never forever. Or they could surrender and Just be Fenrir, Just be the Minotaur, Just be The Monster of Choice. Some communities are looking for that. Some parents have that as their only desire. All it costs is being themselves-something they already fought tooth and nail against with the Devouring.

    Now, does the book explain this well? No, and I honestly wouldn't mind having a source book to go over it. Hell, this entire essay can be considered a rough outline for the first three chapters of that book in it's own way (Blatantly ripping off the Pack as it is, the fourth chapter would be settings). It's not like I haven't considered some ideas for how to firm that up a little bit (and we might talk about them a little bit after this is up). But I think it's important to understand that this didn't come out of nowhere. This conception is mostly in play, and everything I inferred is basically just a logical leap away from this. Beast provides the tools to figure out who you are, with the understanding that you're going to figure that out instead of just filling in some boxes, to be, rather than to simply play as.

    It's freedom we're not oft afforded in reality. I wouldn't take it away.

    So, uh, yeah. I scratched at an itch, and this is how I bled from it.

                                  All right, it's been over a week, now it's time to get hypothetical.

                                  The above is an exploration of things either already in the books, albeit poorly explained in them, or what I would call reasonable extrapolation that you'd reasonably expect to see in material going forward. What we're going into here are three ideas of varying enthusiasm that probably aren't inherently intuitive. So, without further ado:

                                  1) Family Rules-You Want a Oath of the Moon, Fine.

                                  The most recent idea and the one I am least enthused about, nevertheless it creates a sort of interesting idea-can you create a set of rules that feels useful while being completely at odds with itself?

                                  Okay, hear me out. If Beast has a hard rule to obey, it's that it doesn't really hold your hand in telling you who to be, because being yourself is a thing that should be determined by, you know, yourself. That said, good direction on Beast wants to leave players with the impression that you, for all the ways you can and are expected to be a dick, play a character who is not ever so phenomenally a dick, that while you are playing a monster, you are also a person of influence in the world and that that influence can be and, to some extent to be figured out by player and character, should be used for betterment of the world. Handling that could be tricky, though.

                                  So it was that while I was rereading the Diasporic Code from Mummy: the Curse(Guildhalls of the Deathless, to be specific) that an idea emerged of creating a sort of Family Rules set, where in, on top of the three main bits of defined Beast Culture we have from the core (We Are All Family, We Are Allowed to Be What We Are, Don't Shit Where You EatEat to Live, Don't Live to Eat), each Family would have a Rule that is basically just a really effective guideline for that Family, that is cobbled together and sometimes presented as the whole of how Beast Culture works.

                                  The important bit of this, however, is that these particular Family Rules contradict each other pretty openly. Sure, they may all be good ideas on their own, but if you try and implement them all together, you're going to effectively just sitting there diddling yourself until death takes you. They should all point a way to being not only an effective Beast but a monster who integrates well with their community, but they should very obviously not be a list of commandments on how and what to be. This, I feel, is an effective way to build a sort family camraderie and traditionalism that points the way for some of the more confused readers, but also points towards the schisms that also occur in families as each child is handled according to their own needs and issues, and ultimately reinforces that who you are is not dependent on your family, even if you cannot be divorced from it.

                                  I have no idea what any of these would look like, but it would also very definitely be setting only shit-you might see some of the logic as it relates to the mechanics of the Families, but they themselves have no mechanical impact or import.

                                  Okay, enough of that, next.

                                  2) The Dark Mother's Presence(s)-Not as Much of an Alan Wake Reference as You Might Imagine.

                                  One part Totem, one part Krewe Archetype, all* 100% ripping off the Ryuujin from Ryuutama, Presences are the oldest concept I've had for shoring up Beast Society to provide direction for players while also keeping things Beastly, and in fact comprises everything I ever thought up for Beastly Kinship for those of you who remember my musing on the subject (wherein I imagined up that Kinship could and should be expanded from just the relationship to other monsters to a set of four forms of Kinship, being Beastly, Familial, Monstrous, and Human, which is all a different subject all together).

                                  By and large, the idea was expanding on the fact that the Dark Mother is closer to her Children than other splat progenitors are to their respective splats, and occupied a spot between Guidance and Communion for individual Beasts and Obcasus Rites as the Actively Engaging With The Dark Mother option, Presences emerged from when Beasts formed Kinship with each other based on their focus and approach to reality. It really is ripping off the Ryuujin from Ryuutama, in that it a) provides a semi-Storyteller surrogate character with ideas of the Dark Mother's Presences powers taking cues from Artefacts, Benedictions, and Reveils, and b) primarily a genre of play for the group, and honestly I'd keep it to the way that it's presented in Ryuutama, being Urban Horror takes on (Green) Adventure, (Blue) Emotional Drama, (Red) War/Violence, and (Black) Shadowy Political Intrigue, with a fifth one of Survival Horror as the White option added in for good measure, mild adherence to the rule of 5, and also getting in the whole DnD Tiamat reference. It would hone the idea of how to play down distinctively, provide a pretty clear and naked way in which Broods differ in world view from one another, and provide another avenue for the Dark Mother to feel like a present and active entity in the world. Also, it's an excuse to provide more epithets to the Dark Mother, which I am always for.

                                  If I have a motive against doing this, it's that it's too much of a rip off of Ryuutama, and even if we divorced it from that, I feel like it would also still get judged nakedly as Totem-meets-Krewe Archetype, and it deals with all of that while still not telling people what to do in Beast-again, I'm ardently against that, but the problem with any sort of this sort of splat is that it very much is where players go to get their answers on what to do and who to be, and by presenting a thing that narrows it down but doesn't answer it is that it becomes a bit of a cockteasing half-answer, or at least it feels that way to me. But mostly it's the blatant ripping off of Ryuutama.

                                  3) Branches-Yes, We Remembered Z-Splats

                                  THis one is an odd one. Of these three, it's the one I actually sort of see being both really useful and really playable, but at the same time Cinder didn't like it all that much, and time and tide has made me go "Wait, did I take someone's really shitty attempt to fix Beast and turn it into a subsystem?" I don't think I have, I do think what I have just said, but it may be that I am not particularly aimed at the idea right to make it worthwhile.

                                  Here's the Idea: Branches are a Z-Splat like Bloodlines, Lodges, Legacies, and the like that is rooted in the X-Splat of Families, and represent particular Branches of that Family tree, which consist of Dreaming Gods and Horrors, Inheritors, Particularly Powerful and Astrally Connected Monsters, and of course Lots of WeirdnessesTM. Connecting to these members of the Family allow you more directly take on their Lair(Legend) as your own, which could result in Beast's having situations where their Lair is effectively higher when conditions are met, alternate means of Satiating, access to Merits or such, unique powers, or such of the like. The major downside is that by surrendering more of the self to the Branch, the more like that Branch they act like. This could result in things like Bans or Anathema, Permanently having an Aspiration set to their Branch's, other such things. It's tapping into that notion that Astral Dreamborn are effectively the thigns they represent, and so in that way that a Beast is the fear and hunger in the people around them, a older monster is that Beast as well as others. It's like establishing a connection to a Legendary Horror Merit after the fact, but more divorced as it were.

                                  The main virtue of this idea, I reckon, is that it answers that all important question that people seem to have trouble with, which is "what do I do in this game?" by answering "Be That." It also gets into Kinship vs Hunger in it's function as the community vs the invididual theme, by literally presenting a path for power that involves sacrificing of self to a higher, larger, older, more powerful archetype. That said, as an idea, anything that particularly starts really hamstringing player agency also needs serious consideration-a few restraints are good, but too much ruins the the fun. THere's also a bit of risk in that by providing legendary things to ape, it takes away from the self-determination of player characters, that full on freedom to be your own kind of legendary monster. Yes, Beast is a game about cyclical violence and perpetuating chains of cultural attitudes, but make it too "fit yourself into this grandfather shaped hole" and it becomes blind repetition emptily parroting the cycle, not actually getting into the heart and soul of how and why these things happens.

                                  So yeah, I'm not sold on any of these, but I have thought about them and decided this was a good time to see what they looked like outside of my head. Also this thread is not getting trafficked as much as I thought it would be, so let's stir some shit.

                                      Gonna sound off on Z-splats, partially because the topic is something I've talked on before and partially because I need something to drag me away from Vampire Survivors for a bit:

                                      There's kind of a reason that the Big Three lines are the ones that have enshrined Z-splats, the three other lines that had them redefined them in 2e, and every other line from Demon forward (arguably Mummy forward) has been structurally designed against them, Beast included. Going down the list…

                                      As I've mentioned in different contexts over the last several months, the Kindred's world is one where their mystical and social influence is indirectly otherworldly and interconnected, and they carve out a niche to sustain themselves. Clans and covenants serve as modifiers to that end, and bloodlines can get the most specific about it by virtue of how they're started. It makes sense for vampires to have a refinement of their magical and societal niche significant enough to warrant mechanical impact.

                                      As 1e makes clear in aggregate and 2e states explicitly, lodges are connective tissue for werewolf society; packs are typically bound to territory and tribes don't take a personal interest in every member's local situation, but the Uratha link up both across the world and into spiritual ecology with the broad-scope directives presented by the bonds of an extra totem. A pack can become the basis for a lodge, a lodge can become the basis for a tribe. Spirits interacting with werewolves in this way gain power separate from what they would gain from their fellows, and werewolves serving in a lodge benefit from its secrets. There's valuable middle ground in the continuum of werewolf society that these groups occupy.

                                      Legacies are a little closer to bloodlines than lodges, but they fill multiple important roles for the Awakened that kind of fit in between the two. Mage global-scale organization is definitely closer to vampires than werewolves, but the Nimbus doesn't spread like the Predatory Aura does and wizards aren't by default as desperate for their fix as the Kindred. Legacies aren't necessarily needed as go-betweens among Consilia, but they do provide unique benefits the Awakened have a concrete interest in, and it's been stated that it's relatively uncommon for a mage to go too long into their career without joining one. And this gets into the heart of the matter: it tends to go unconversed-upon, but Mage is a game where the assumption is that your player-characters are not entirely self-taught. You are students with mentors and possibly students of your own; even before getting into the abstractions of Order membership and requisitioning materials from the Lorehouse, you had at least one teacher and likely a couple of peers, and Legacies most directly facilitate this mode of engagement even among the Nameless. That and soul-borne Mysterious Nonmagical Powers™ provide ample grounds for their inclusion as societal widgets for the Awakened with a mechanical basis.

                                      Athanors were barely a Z-splat in 1e Promethean; they gave you a minor power that translated into a different minor benefit if you managed to become human, which was an extra incentive of sorts to become human, but not much else considering that becoming human is usually the end of the character's narrative arc. 2e gave the name to artifacts hooked into Azothic Memory as the new shared Created headspace, and while they can be used to cheat your way around the way Transmutations now work at dramatically lower Vitriol cost, the biggest thing about them is easily the fact that they're Pyros-resonant objects with history to them along with a spread of potential helpful or troublesome effects. Big useful change, but it does also mean that the Created no longer have Z-splats, because they don't really need them — their society is barely in the triple digits globally at the best of times and their nature is fairly incompatible with finding a niche in the world before they become a part of it.

                                      Entitlements changed from being a middle step between Courts and motleys, forged with a pledge and sufficient Wyrd, to being singular inheritable identity-mantles, taken up through fateful circumstances or more rarely forged wholesale. This shift likewise means they went from something like lodges to being something like personal bloodlines or Legacies; the material focus of the blazon differentiates them further from the individual tweaks of vampires and mages, allowing changelings to take their self-image into their own hands a bit at the cost of added duty, rigidity and necessary vigilance. Even in 1e, changelings were the closest to globally-organized of the non-Big Three splat, and now it's not hard to see how a role passed from changeling to changeling fits into Lost society even beyond the local scale — the way the Hedge works, if you need the Baron of the Lesser Ones, finding out where she's hung her hat is tricky but far from impossible even if she's been conducting all her affairs on Earth.

                                      Krewes were already a player character group that could be expanded to a personal Z-splat, and now they're a shared collective character with a power stat of their own that 2e gives you the tools and incentive to raise. The organization mechanics are serving all the function a Z-splat would have now, and like the Created, the Bound are temporary (in addition to having the same issue the Unchained have, discussed anon); they don't have a separate interconnected society to tag into and their default unit of social cohesion already does what lodge-style groups would do for them. All that on top of having their plates full with unfinished business both personal and inherited means there's not much call to add yet another axis of character differentiation into the mix.

                                      Demons are collectively so societally fucked that the equivalent of a freehold or Consilium is looked upon with suspicion, and for good reason on account of being in enemy territory under Moscow Rules. On top of that, catalysts and the Cipher mean the Unchained are dealing with a diversity of deeply personal agendas such that the capital-A Agendas are as large-scale organized as most demons ever get and figuring out unique magical powers is largely a matter of poking around the God-Machine and its creations for long enough to find the signposts for your end goal. Most of the other stuff you would get from a social group with specific extra benefits in this context would be more sensibly handled through mundane organization rules and additional mechanics for world-weirdness, Infrastructural or otherwise).

                                      Deviants are laser-focused on their Touchstones, have highly individualized powers, and can already join and/or subvert conspiracies to gain their rule-bending Icons and other benefits, and therefore hit on similar deals to demons and Sin-Eaters. I don't think I've yet seen anyone saying the Remade need a Z-splat for these and other reasons.

                                      Which brings us back to Beasts.

                                      Old arguments about Primordial Pathways notwithstanding, the Begotten are very clearly themed to be local cryptids, and they're at least uncommon enough to be explicitly outnumbered by mages. Hives spring up around population centers, but the Apex can be a non-Beast (and there's kind of an incentive for Beasts to make it so, too) and the Begotten are diverse enough that the unaware might not even connect the dots between members of the same Family. So we're back at the recurring pattern in this list of "too uncommon and disconnected from each other to warrant the thing a Z-splat represents," and I remarked some time ago on the arguably more interesting angle of Beasts being the sort of phenomena that inspires other monsters to develop a Z-splat.

                                      Tapping into an external identity feels simultaneously too close to where Changeling 2e has gone with Entitlements and too much leaning into the half of Begotten power that isn't where they actually come from. The Bright Dream may be able to change a Beast temporarily, but Hunger is the part of their nature that has gravity on its side; Legend is a human invention, and part of the advantage of how Satiety currently works is that if you're being taken advantage of by someone who is keeping you well-fed then you only need to go hungry for a bit to make trouble for them (and that bit gets shorter the stronger you are). Being technically part-shapeshifter born from the chaos of the cosmic oversoul means the Begotten probably shouldn't have something more involved than letting an Anathema stick as far as things that ignore "adapt or die" as a truism.

                                      As with the others, I think engaging with the organization rules and unique weirdnesses would serve the function of character differentiation better than an enshrined Z-splat mechanic, but you knew that about me already.

                                          My thoughts are based on a quick read-through, and should be treated as such. Ideally, I would go through your posts with a fine comb, but I'm dealing with too many distractions.

                                          Family Rules leave me utterly cold. I understand that they're meant to make Beasts less offputting, but that problem can instead be solved by making them face consequences of their actions. I do want the option to play a remorseless monster, if I feel like it, as long as the game doesn't let me get away with murder. All choices a fictional character makes result in a karmic debt (or credit) of sorts. The audience wants the story to remember that score. Hopefully, it's repaid until the finale, but even if the moral of the story is that the world is unfair, the story must progress with that concept in mind. Also, as someone enamoured with the "No neat boxes" approach, I want each PC and NPC to craft their own rules, based on their own experience.

                                          To understand the Presences, I need to familiarise myself with Ryuutama, which I don't have the time for. Still, all ideas were used before in some form, so don't let preexisting works discourage you from implementing a useful thing (Assuming it is useful, which I don't know).
