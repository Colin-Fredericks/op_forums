        There is a low-hanging fruit in the L-scheme. I'd say it's License.

        All myths are examples and justifications - something has happened before, and it's way less scary to use what you've been told happened in the past as a yardstick to guide your choices than to face the Great Wide Unknown. Growing your Lair means you accept to become that example, that precedent.

        That can be through your Legend, as in the case above of a Nemesis toppling a tyrant boss: you gave his employees the proof that a) the boss himself is fallible, and b) all seats of authority might be similarly flawed. It's not a moral lesson, not a ruling: it's not forcing, or even directly allowing them to take action. But... there's something there, something they didn't do because "nobody does that", or "nobody normal would do that", or "nobody would back me up if I did that", and now there's a voice in the back of their head saying "welp, seems like somebody just done did that, and who was there to stop them?"

        But that could also be through your Life: not taking the boss' shit and quitting, or cooperating with him to find his good side. Proving that sometimes, in the situations when human society expects and valorizes behaving like an Apex Predator, it's okay (and even more rebellious) to say "screw that, screw that game, I'm not playing it".

        And this kind of breakthroughs obviously consolidate your role as a Beast. The humans or kin that imitate realize they can, like you, exert a level of agency that makes them dangerous. And that gives them moral responsibility, whatever they end up doing with it. It pairs up well with the core themes of the homebrew Lineages write-ups. You're afraid, and your fears are - at least partially - justified, and there's no guidebook in the world that can teach you the perfect way to face them because there's no perfect. And That's Okay.

        But obviously, that is also why Heroes hunt you down. This dangerous freedom and chaos you bring, predicated on the idea that we should all behave as if we were still naked and afraid in the wild, despite the clothes and the street lights and the concrete ? That's counter to all values. Even the not-Zealot Heroes who are not completely convinced their way is the natural right way worry about what happens if your example spreads beyond you, emboldens mortals or supernatural creatures to become accepting of their monstrosity. The underlings of the boss you've beat might take it as License to become bullies of their own. Each of your accomplishments can be construed into a License to put you down, before the spread of your ideas damage society beyond repair.

        And the Insatiable whittling them down ? Well, as the Titans of this dark-Scion, they're Nature, cold uncaring alien nature. The very hard-to-argue-against notion that for the handful of individuals for which your example matters, there's a vastly wider world who doesn't give a single care.

        I can understand if the word is too administrative in English to make it work as IG lingo. In French, licence is often used in poetry used when an author or creator takes a departure from the usual rules (e.g. spelling) when it serves their artwork better - that probably helps in making it so compelling to me.

        If we privilege the aspect of mythology and recurrence, I'd say Leitmotiv. If we drop the L-scheme, I would probably call it a Beast's Wake. Because it wakes up people in their wake, obviously.

            Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
            As I mentioned, I'm not comfortable with passively allowing humanity's beliefs to change a person's metaphysical mark-just because humanity believes something doesn't make them right-but there is something in the angle of imposition that's a big deal of Heroes that I can get behind. Passively believing someone is a monster won't do it, but I think when we get around to hammering out how people can manipulate the Dreaming, both deliberately and accidentally, as well as how the Dreaming can intrude as a result of actions, the "Force you to be Kin" thing can be viable. But again, it can't just be passive belief, there has to be something where someone(s) take that passive belief and use it like a hammer or a spear. And of course, I'm also open to Heroes having the ability to, at cost, brand someone as a monster with their metaphysical authority (Probably 3 Satiety).

            Asides from coming off as "Kin, but it feels wrong" for Beasts, it also creates some fun where there's an ongoing risk that such shit can result in a person being a beacon for supernatural forces, and an ongoing risk that someone becomes a monster as a result. And that fits in with one of the other things I want to see going forward, being the involvement of Astral Gods and mechanics for them making monsters out of people-having humanity en masse be able to do similar is appealing.
            Personally I'm not too into the idea of Beast presenting a mode by which the Bright Dream can explicitly turn people into monsters without leveraging Primordial power to do so, for reasons complementary to stuff I've gone over about Heroes — the Temenos mostly doesn't work along that axis of supernatural power, and in my view the avenues where it does should either produce results analogous to the circumstances that produce Beasts (whether normal specimens or the products of Inheritances) or not be explicitly tied to that process at all a la "did rumors turn this guy into a monster or predict something that was going to happen anyway?" ambiguity that can feed into the splat's grappling with fatalism and sort of feels Persona-adjacent.

            The way Begotten's sense for the supernatural works means that they'll be able to tell if they're one of their fellow Children (optimally-stabilized or otherwise), and if they're not then they'll be able to identify another specimen of that type of monstrosity if it recurs, but the Bright Dream's responsibility for monsters in the metaphysics as it stands is most analogous to the reveal about why a given modern Persona game's ultimate antagonist showed up, i.e. collective humanity gave a demon a name to answer to and it came when they called for it.

            Some of this does kind of run afoul of Werewolf's issues with presenting mortals as a danger before bringing in Shadow occultists and the like, in that it necessarily makes the bulk of their danger a passive and unknowing one, but it's worth remembering that most of the Temenos's established ability to directly mess with individual people relies on those people being famous and possessed by the avatars of their fame. I personally wouldn't connect the Bright Dream to deliberately establishing any degree of monstrosity greater than that presented by gaining Supernatural Merits — if it out-monsters a Herald, it should probably be a Beast (with or without an Inheritance modifying the final outcome, because in my view that's half the point of those Inheritances), an Insatiable, or something else that a Dreamer could nevertheless see coming through the Dark Dream's nightmare filter.

                        I'll try to go through your arguments one by one, not because I'm confident in my position, but because I have little to offer if I don't commit.

                        Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                        1) From a purely practical standpoint, the bookkeeping of Kinship can be a pain in the ass if Beast's have to go and ask "Do they count as Kin so I can use a major portion of my tool kit?" Keeping it simple, maybe even making it simpler, let's the game just play.

                        2) Given the role of semiotics in Beast, the icon of monster is inclined to have more housed in it than not, and it would become increasingly incoherent to create more variation on what does and does not count based on the metaphysic Kinship works from.
                        If anything, letting Humans be Kin would simplify the matter. You don't need to figure out which edge case gets Kinship benefits and which one doesn't. If that seems like too much, you've already suggested Kinship as a tiered Condition - ordinary Humans (and animals) could be simply confined to lower levels, and only literal Monsters could get the whole metaphysical oomph.

                        ​
                        Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                        3) Also, from a thematic point, the world of Chronicles is not pre-disposed to lean towards subjective influence-the world has more objective forms and powers and nature, and public perception only rarely has affect. Now Beast is a good place to play around with that, because as you mention in a bit, Beast is a game of managing your brand-but there's a floor to how much I'd go with that.

                        4) On a human care perspective that relates to the humanism of the series, I'm not hyper comfortable with the metaphysical label of monster applying to people, particularly the disadvantaged of our world, in the same certifiable way one actually applies it to the actual monsters in the series. I think there's a line between a Namtaru who toys with the fear of the scarred and disabled and people who are just scarred or disabled that crossing would only do bad things for. The metaphorical and supernatural is one thing, but the instant we say "You are monster because people think you are" in world with actual monsters gets...again, it gets uncomfortable.

                        I'd rather keep the qualifications of Kin simple and finite for entry. In terms of expanding it, I'd rather make it deeper and more relationship work rather than murkier, harder to work with, and somewhat unfortunate.
                        Sensitive writing is the most important objection you raised. Especially for a gameline which borked that before. Having said that, I think the right framing could make this work. I'll start with a lesser point:

                        The Astral contains all concepts shared between multiple people, therefore it must contain a realm that equates "undesireables" with monsters. The Bright Dream would contain conscious narratives and excuses, but The Primordial Dream would connect to instincts and gut reactions from which conscious responses flow. That doesn't make such demonisation morally of factually correct. It would just mean that sheer pressure of human perception would push victims into Beasts' metaphysical backyard.

                        My major point: Let's depict it as profoundly messed up. An average Beast would resonate both with literal (likely harmful) Monsters, and Humans who experienced some of the worst Humanity had to offer. This has the added benefit of having the game not needing other monsters to work - the human drama offers plenty material already (especially for players who do want to tackle heavy subjects in a safe environment). How the Begotten respond to that situation would tell a lot about their character. Do they accept them as Family in an us-versus-the-world mindset (this would be a mixed blessing at best, coming from living Nightmares)? Do they consider this state a malfunction of society, requiring some strategically deployed terror to make the community rethink its priorities? Do they consider them a sacrifice by Humanity to the Mother (for her hunger or her love). A potential book could include a Human NPC in just such a situation to drive the point home, that this is not a good situation to be in.

                        ​
                        Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                        An interesting notion that leans also into Satchel's suggestion, but I run into the issue of "What does that look like?"
                        I'm in the same camp as TempleBuilder . In fact, I once had a silly idea of a character's Horror becoming such a prominent urban legend, it started being used as a Cereal mascot without the Beast's consent. A cutesy version begun entrenching itself in the Dream and overwriting the legendry. Would a Beast choose violence to get her reputation on the right track, or use that as a springboard to a constricted but wide infamy. Another bit of brand management would be that sometimes, needs of the brand and needs of the individual are at odds. Perhaps you worked hard to be seen as a fair but firmly cruel tempter, but the Hero endangering your family is not interested in playing along. He needs to die ASAP, but the witnesses and, God forbid, the rumour mill can't connect you to his death, or your brand will shift in unpredictable directions.

                        I don't know how to approach this mechanically. Perhaps Scion has a useful mechanic that could be repurposed (I don't have the game)? Perhaps reusing Deviant's Conspiracy actions to govern reputations? Lair Chambers would be not just useful infrastructure, but could hold and anchor fragments of reputation: The Namtaru wants reduce opiates addiction in the community. She befouls the house of an unscrupulous doctor, forms a Chamber there and writes "The Slug Swarm befouls the homes of corrupt medics (+2 to relevant dice rolls within the Hive)" into the Primordial Dream. She must now fight to stay on top of that narrative - the doctor must struggle in vain to clean up his house, but can't rethink is life, because he would no longer be corrupt.​

                            Seems weird to assign dot ratings or equivalent to each instance of Family Ties, and probably won't yield much to justify the extra book-keeping. I think that Family Ties should remain a mostly narrative consideration, with particulars like "how much do you care about this relationship?" and "are you rivals or friends?" being handled via roleplay. If you want some kind of "dot rating" for Family Ties to determine how easy it is to develop corresponding Kinship Merits, I'm pretty sure making it more of a two-way mechanic would be sufficient. The Kin gets Beats for neglecting other aspects of their lives to help the Beast, and in turn the Beast gets Beats for enabling the more monstrous side of the Kin in one way or another.

                            If your Beast spends more time on maintaining and expanding this two-way dynamic, he gets more Beats and thus can afford higher-rated Kinship Merits more quickly. THAT right there is you effective "dot rating" associated with Family Ties, IMO. Besides, assigning objective values of psycho-spiritual importance to a list of Kin seems weird, and might also result in arguments with the ST about whether or not Family Ties Level 3 has been arbitrarily reached, or if you are still at Family Ties Level 1 because the ST doesn't agree that your interactions with the Kin were meaningful enough. How many people IRL consciously give grades to their relationships and make quantitative comparisons between those grades?

                                            Originally posted by Teatime View Post
                                            Sensitive writing is the most important objection you raised. Especially for a gameline which borked that before. Having said that, I think the right framing could make this work.
                                            On this particular vein, my biggest problem is judging the line where it crosses...

                                            Actually, for my articulation, i'm going to start at the opposite end of the spectrum. There's this idea in games design and criticism where it doesn't matter if a game comments negatively on a thing if the game allows you do the thing anyways, even if it somehow renders playing onward in a negative way-a good short way to explain it is "It doesn't matter if you lose points for killing hookers in your game, the game still allows you to kill hookers and therefore contributes to the overarching problem of accepting violence against hookers as normal." It doesn't matter if the content is condemned on the other side of the action-if there is content beyond the action, then it is an accepted part of the game.

                                            Now, TTRPG's have a weird relationship to this, and games that deal with transgressive ideas and roleplay get even weirder, and Beast as a game that has Transgression as a major element of it's DNA is going to be a place that requires maturity about it. As a general rule, I lean into Beast's relationship to transgression as a positive for a mature roleplaying game expected for a mature roleplaying group, which is a flirtation with danger, but arguably not particularly above any of the other game lines.

                                            On this point though, I worry. It's a situation where I have to ask myself "Does it matter if I make it clear that this is a kind of really fucked up thing to do, (particularly by tying it into the most assholish element about Heroes as my beginning baseline, as I followed up on), if at the end of the day I still put in content that effectively lets someone create scenarios where people, particularly the disadvantaged in society, are read as monsters in a world with actual monsters?"

                                            There's a lot of things I'm willing to flirt with on Beast, and I get the appeal of this sort of mechanic, but on the other hand, whenever I really think about it, I keep arriving at the only correct thing to do is to not suggest content on the other side of that action.

                                            That's not a hard no, just-I really want to think about this, even longer than I already have, and this has come up a few times already.

                                                            Originally posted by Teatime View Post
                                                            The Astral contains all concepts shared between multiple people, therefore it must contain a realm that equates "undesireables" with monsters. The Bright Dream would contain conscious narratives and excuses, but The Primordial Dream would connect to instincts and gut reactions from which conscious responses flow. That doesn't make such demonisation morally of factually correct. It would just mean that sheer pressure of human perception would push victims into Beasts' metaphysical backyard.
                                                            The connective tissue for the Primordial Dream is Lair Traits and loss of Integrity. There are Bright Realms whose Themes can match with a Beast's Lair Traits, but the Astral is all unconscious — the Dark Dream doesn't need to get involved at the level of conflating a class of person with a monster when it can just absorb a dream that resonates with fear of undesirables that a larval Horror can come to live in.​

                                                            Originally posted by GibberingEloquence View Post
                                                            Seems weird to assign dot ratings or equivalent to each instance of Family Ties, and probably won't yield much to justify the extra book-keeping. I think that Family Ties should remain a mostly narrative consideration, with particulars like "how much do you care about this relationship?" and "are you rivals or friends?" being handled via roleplay. If you want some kind of "dot rating" for Family Ties to determine how easy it is to develop corresponding Kinship Merits, I'm pretty sure making it more of a two-way mechanic would be sufficient. The Kin gets Beats for neglecting other aspects of their lives to help the Beast, and in turn the Beast gets Beats for enabling the more monstrous side of the Kin in one way or another.
                                                            Arc is explicitly riffing on the Connection mechanic from Golden Sky Stories, which serves as the source of the primary resources in that game — you get the resource that lets you use supernatural powers based on the strength of your connections to others, and you get the resource that lets you raise or lower your stats to adjust the degree to which you succeed at an action (GSS is a diceless game) based on the strength of others' connections to you, and either of these can also be used for the game's main means of not drawing attention to yourself as a magical nonhuman. (Notably, the Town the game is set in counts as a character for these purposes.)

                                                            The third resource in the game is used for strengthening Connections; you can only spend that resource on characters you were in a scene with, and you can only strengthen your own Connections toward other characters (the narrator counts as a character for this purpose, which matters because getting a Connection above its third level is increasingly costly, while you can get Connections with a strength of 1 or 2 from mutually creating one with a character, though doing so will almost certainly require you to spend the stat-raising resource unless you're leaning heavily on a particular base stat, and which stat you use for the check affects the starting context of the Connection)

                                                            I played a Rabbit in a game of Golden Sky Stories that Arc ran, and the default powers those get are heavily focused on Connections — every Rabbit, as a free power, gives a cost break for other characters to strengthen Connections toward them, and can spend their magical resources to bring a character they have a Connection with into the scene; two of their optional powers let them directly increase the strength of Connections other people make toward them, while a third gives them the same cost break they apply toward others for strengthening their own outgoing Connections, and a fourth lets them spend Connection-strengthening resources with characters who aren't in the scene as long as they also aren't in the scene; and the attached weaknesses that come with those particular optional powers (four of the six!) all limit your ability to act or mess with the context of your Connections in ways that aren't hard to cross-apply to habitually-Gorged Begotten who attend a lot of Family Dinners.

                                                            On the other end of things, Dogs are very heavily built around the strength of their outgoing Connections — two of their universal powers scale based on how strongly you feel about the target, two of their optional powers give them a fairly strong Connection from the outset, and the attached weaknesses for two other optional powers both mess with different ends of the process for creating new Connections but explicitly doesn't apply to characters you had a Connection with in a previous story; the power attached to one of these is another one that scales based on your own feelings. I have joked that this dynamic means Dogs in GSS get infinite free magic, but it's not difficult to imagine a version of Family Ties for which this sort of thing could be used to make Mother's Kiss more effective.

                                                            I can see what Arc is going for, is what I'm getting at here.
