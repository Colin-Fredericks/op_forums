                                            Sorrows

                                            "Well you do enough talk
                                            My little hawk, why do you cry?
                                            Tell me what did you learn from the Tillamook Burn
                                            Or the Fourth of July?
                                            We're all gonna die."
                                            --Sufjan Stevens, Fourth of July

                                            Fear is hardly the only emotion humans feel. Though terror dominates the Primordial Dream, manifestations of other sentiments can occasionally be found flitting through the ethereal space of unconsciousness. Though still quite rare compared to Horrors, the most common of these anomalies might be the creatures known as Sorrows.

                                            There are two competing theories about the origin of Sorrows: Either they are spawned wholecloth in the Primordial Dream from nightmares of loss, despair, and regret, or they are hapless Horrors who strayed a little too close to the Bright Dream, becoming infected with one of humanity's more "refined" sentiments. Sadness can be seen as noble, after all, in ways fear never would be.

                                            No matter their genesis, Sorrows act much in the same way as Horrors; they stalk the Primordial Dream for dreaming prey on which to fill their bellies. Inflicting sorrow is often less a single shock, however, and more a drawn-out game. The Sorrow hunkers down in one dreamer's mindscape, and waits, subtly and gradually sowing the seeds of regret and despair.

                                            Appearance: The forms of Sorrows are usually less overtly monstrous than those of Horrors, a little less primal in their awfulness. A number have humanoid shapes, albeit strange and forbidding miens still. Even more are hazy and indistinct, as if viewed through a thick veil of tears. Many are cold to the touch. When they do take the form of mythological monsters, Sorrows tend to focus on the secondary characteristics ascribed to these creatures beyond what can kill a man: religious iconography, an aura of incense, large, mournful eyes.

                                            Traits: Sorrows are built similarly to the Unfettered. They are ephemeral entities with no connection to the flesh of the world. There is no memory or record of a Sorrow ever devouring the soul of a human and creating a Beast; one might imagine the results would be messy and unfortunate.

                                            If the Dark Mother has any opinion on these half-children of grief, she has not made her feelings known.

                                            Hunger: Like Horrors, each Sorrow pursues a hunger for power, hoard, prey, punishment, or ruin. The end goal is to inflict sadness rather than fear, and so feeding methods diverge.

                                            Collectors always look for objects of great sentimental value. Nothing quite stings like losing an irreplaceable memento. They take love letters, wedding rings, urns filled with the ashes of dead relatives, and family pets. This isn't to say that they overlook monetary value in their ministrations: In some dreams, the month's rent check goes missing, or the Sorrow mugs the dreamer of last ten dollars to her name. When the victim wakes, she may find herself clutching her possessions ever closer, or she may decide that material attachments are meaningless in the face of the great void that awaits her.

                                            Nemeses feed off shame and guilt. They follow the dreamer around, constantly reminding him of what he did, and why he should feel bad about it. Some take on the shapes of someone the dreamer hurt. Others look like judges on high, ready to mete out harsh sentencing for crimes real or imagined. In the aftermath of a devastating feeding, a dreamer may turn himself over to the authorities out of self-loathing and the belief that he was going to get caught anyway, so why waste everyone's much more valuable time?

                                            Predators of Sorrow are master manipulators. Unlike Horrors, they can't feed from directly hurting their prey; that's not how one feels sadness. Instead, they have to get the prey to hurt themselves. They get into the prey's head, show him how worthless his life is, how he's already peaked and everything is a downward slide from here. There's only one rational thing he can do, and the Sorrow is more than happy to provide the means to the unfortunate dreamer. Feeding doesn't always involve tackling the only valid philosophical problem, though. Some convince dreamers to neglect their bodies, relationships, or pursuits by making them feel hollow and meaningless. The aftermath of a successful feeding of this kind can be brutal: the Soul Shocked are not the most emotionally stable of individuals. A rash of suicides in Seattle in the nineties was traced back to a high-Rank ravenous Sorrow that required the efforts of the entire Hive to dispatch (though not before it forced the city's Apex to hang herself in shame at one she had allowed to come to pass).

                                            Ravagers have a relatively easy time of things: They set up a dream where someone the dreamer cares about dies, and then wait. Some fast-forward to the funeral, and inflict their victim with the realization that their loved ones won't be around forever, and neither will the dreamer. Everyone she cares about will die, and then she will die. Why fight it? Why care at all? Ravagers who target the non-living take a similar, relentless approach. The dream begins with the victim's house blowing down in a violent storm. She rebuilds it, only for the storm to destroy it again, and again, over and over until the dreamer finally gives up and lies down in the cold. The malaise that follows in the wake of these dreams can last for days when executed right.

                                            Tyrants must, as one would expect, dominate the dreamer in some way, though they rule not through fear of what they'll do to their victim, but by making the victim feel like he has no other choice but to obey. They make him feel small, unintelligent, powerless. Listen to me, you can't do this on your own. It's only logical that you obey me. You're only resisting because of your own, petty ego. Why are you so selfish? After waking, victims of these Sorrows tend to be plagued with self-doubt, especially those in positions of authority.

                                            SOME SAMPLE SORROWS

                                            The Glacial King
                                            "Do not fight it. You cannot fight it. You are so small compared to the glacier, so weak compared to the winter winds. Lie down. Give up now."

                                            Description: The Glacial King takes the form of a tall, solidly-built old man with frostbitten gray skin, a stomach-length white beard, and gnarled fingernails, wrapped in a deep blue silk robe and topped with a crown of iron and ice. He is enshrouded in a permanent mantle of freezing air and bitter wind. He exists to make dreamers feel weak and inadequate in the face of uncaring reality, convincing them to give up on everything and embrace the cold.

In the center of its body lies a swollen, red eye that constantly twitches back and forth as it looks every which way.                                             The Jilter
                                            "I know it's been a while. Come to bed. Take off your clothes first, I want to see you naked again. Oh, hmm."

                                            Description: While floating through the Primordial Dream, the Jilter is a blank, white humanoid shape whose edges are ragged and undulant. When it steps into a victim's dream, however, it instinctively takes on the form of a former lover, or sometimes an unrequited crush, either way someone who caused the dreamer romantic anguish, and who maybe he eventually got over, but toward whom he still harbors some secret longing and misery. The Jilter takes full advantage of this, tugging at the dreamer's heartstrings, reminding him why things didn't work out between him and his beloved, or hinting that maybe now, things will be different. The Sorrow brings back the pain of rejection, and the realization that even after all this time, his old object of affection still holds his heart in the palm of their hands. Well, it's the Jilter's heart now, to do with as it pleases.

                                            Bane: Proof that the dreamer has moved on from the lover the Jilter represents

                                                        Seems like today is a productive day for this thread. Here's a phenomena. For the next one, I'll probably get back on creatures.



                                                        Entry #2: The Nightmare Maelstrom



                                                        The cold, hard truth about the Primordial Dream is that nobody can truly predict its influence. It answers to rules and laws that are so buried within the darkest corners of the collective human experience and so removed from the reality of today's life that hoping to understand it would be a treacherous endeavour at best. While its language is something made of pure and unbridled strength that still refuses to waver even in the modern world, the Primordial Dream speaks to the atavistic and primeval parts of our souls. Even Beasts, born as they are with an innate connection to the nightmarish dimension, have a hard time at grasping the patterns of the Primordial Dream as a whole. They sure are the most fitting for the task, but obtaining a truly complete understanding of the oniric structure of the Primordial Dream, supposing that such structure does indeed exist, might be a feat that belongs more to the Dark Mother than her children. This means that, whether they like it or not, the Primordial Dream sometimes holds a few surprises for the Begotten.

                                                        One of such phenomena is the so called Nightmare Maelstrom. Nightmare Maelstroms are the Primordial Dream's equivalent of a natural disaster. Nobody is really sure about the causes behind them, but Maelstroms happen cyclically and if not properly contained they can cause terrible damages. Maelstroms are thankfully rare, there's no more than one or two for each generation and they happen everywhere in the world, but the tales of those who went through them across the ages circulated and became a recurrent, if somewhat obscure, cautionary tale within Beast's culture.

                                                        During a Maelstrom, an unlucky person becomes the fulcrum of all the dreams and nightmares of a given area. It begins slowly, but a Maelstrom steadily draws towards himself all the oniric streams of a region and grows in power and strenght with time. It breaches the walls that separate the minds of the dreamers, human and supernaturals alike, and brings them together in a unique chaotic nightmare, each night stronger. Eventually, a Maelstrom's presence exerts so much pressure on the texture of reality that the Primordial Dream erupts violently into our world, leaving only destruction behind. All those trapped within it abruptly die of fear, screaming and crying, their collective deathcry resonating for years in the astral.

                                                        Only Beasts can hope to survive their aftermath. Their connection to the Primordial Dream and profound understanding of fear protects them from this certain death, but are not immune from the other consequences and are often let traumatized and damaged by the experience. A Beast's Lair is not safe from the Maelstrom and, even if they survive, going through it is often excruciating. The reason why stories of Maelstroms work so well as a scare tactic is that, for what concerns Beast, those disaster happen when a Devouring goes wrong.

                                                        For reasons unknown, a would-be Maelstrom can remain quiescent for all its life. Since their birth they show traces of a bond with the Primordial Dream like Beast and Heroes do, but their oniric identity is always shifting. They suffer from nightmares, but the Horror that stalks them is never the same. Sometimes they fight monsters, something they're the monster itself. Sometimes they dream someone else's dream. It all pretty vivid, but most potential Maelstroms go through all their life without questioning the matter. The problem happens when a Beast proceeds to put one of them through a Devouring. Maelstroms have something that resembles a Horror within themselves, but it's a fake, ever-changing construct that's more a shade of a Horror than a true one. Beasts don't know that. Most importantly, Beast cannot know that. For all they know, they're in front of a potential Begotten. If they proceed with the Devouring, the Maelstrom awakens.

                                                        There's no Final Nightmare for a Maelstrom. No epiphany, no neat powers, no Hunger. They're not Beasts and they never were. But after the fake Devouring, things do get weird. Suddenly, they're able to spot monsters and monsters are able to spot them on sight (and are inexplicably drawn to them). Each night they go trough an extremely vivid crucible were they wander around the astral dreamscape and enter the dreams of the sleepers.

                                                        Beasts are tricked by the fact that the Maelstrom appears like Family. To be more precise, it seems one of the Begotten, Horror, Lair and all. It's easy to see while many Beasts don't notice its true nature until things take a turn for the worse. For those who know where to look, though, there are signs. First of all, the Maelstrom's “Horror” changes. Each night it is a different one with a different Lair. It's odd, to say the least. The don't have Atavism or Nightmares, nor all the supernatural abilities of a Beast. It's just a facade that only Beasts can notice. In fact, at first Heroes can't even track a Maelstrom. They sense its disturbance over the Primordial Dream, but this only leads them to look after real Beasts. The most glaring difference though, is that Maelstroms are not aware of their condition. They know that something bad it's going on, but they have no control over it: they're victims as well.

                                                        As time goes on, the mere presence of Maelstroms alters the dreams of others, changing the narrative and the events. They'll eventually become able to interact directly with those dreams, with even stronger consequences. When the other dreamers begin to be forced into the Maelstrom's own dream, the event is reaching its final stages. On the last day, every single person in the area aside from Beasts and Heroes cannot wake up and are forced to take part to the oniric apocalypse that's about to happen. The barriers between their dreams crumble as they all become part of the Maelstrom. The Primordial Dream floods all their minds and the barrier that keeps it separate from the real world weakens. The Horrors and their Lairs feast on the screaming crowd and gain enough power to enter the material plane. At the culmination of this, the Maelstrom's soul detonates and brings all the dreamers with it. Some say the Dark Mother herself appears to take the Maelstrom's soul. The Maelstrom itself survives, soulless, traumatized and forever cut away from the Primordial Dream, but alive. Beasts are left to pick up the pieces.

                                                        What can Beast do when a Maelstrom happens? Once they've identified it, there's only a solution: they have to kill it. Not in their dreams, a Maelstrom's astral presence is so strong that it reforms each day if slained or devoured: they have to put an end to its life. Pure and simple. This, obviously, can be a complex feat for a number of reason, the moral one first and foremost. But when left with the choice between saving one life or saving thousands, the dilemma acquires weight. If you consider that more often than not Beast have socialized with a Maelstrom while mistaking it for one of them, it all becomes even more complicated.

                                                        Killing someone you don't know is already hard. If that person is a friend, a brother or a lover, the choice becomes even less obvious.

                                                        Mechanics:

                                                        Since the Devouring, the Maelstrom goes trough six different phases as it gains power. Each phases lasts for five days, except for the final one which lasts one. This means every Maelstrom lasts for exactly twenty-six days. During those days, the Maelstrom's influence on dreams resonates thematically with a combination of Beast's Families and Hungers, every day a new one. Its fake Horror changes accordingly. One day it might be a Namtaru Tyrant, the following one a Makara Collector and so on. Everyday a different Horror, a different Lair, a different Hunger. There's no precise scheme for this evolution, some spend a entire phase with a given Family or Hunger, some do not. All sleepers in the area are affected by the current shape of the Maelstrom's “Horror”. It speaks great volume that Maelstroms have to inflict all the possible facets of the Beast's condition over the population as their power grows. The last day, though, there's no new Horror: it belongs to the Dark Mother.

                                                        The specific rules for each phase are the following:


                                                        Phase 1 (Days 1 – 5)
                                                        - It counts as having Lair 1 when a roll is required
                                                        - The Maelstrom becomes the Apex. It cannot loose this position until the end.
                                                        - The Maelstrom gains Thicker Than Water and Family Resemblance
                                                        - The Maelstrom gains a fake Horror and Lair as specified above. It cannot enter this Lair and has no immunities, though other Beasts can.
                                                        - The Maelstrom becomes immune to the Soul Shocked Condition and its soul cannot be removed in any way.
                                                        -At this stage, the Maelstrom merely wanders around dreams. Almost no influence from the fake Horror
                                                        - Rolls to open a Primordial Pathway get a +1 bonus

                                                        Phase 2 (Days 6 – 10)
                                                        - It counts as having Lair 3 when a roll is required
                                                        - At this stage, dreamers begin to notice the Maelstrom. Dreams change accordingly to the current nature of the fake Horror
                                                        - Rolls to open a Primordial Pathway get a +2 bonus

                                                        Phase 3 (Days 11-15)
                                                        - It counts as having Lair 5 when a roll is required
                                                        -At this stage, the current nature of the fake Horror alters the dreams completely. The Maelstrom is able to visit more dreams at once.
                                                        - Rolls to open a Primordial Pathway get a +3 bonus

                                                        Phase 4 (Days 16-20)
                                                        - It counts as having Lair 7 when a roll is required
                                                        -At this stage, each dreamer enters the dream of Maelstrom instead and they are swept away from the fake Horror's current nature, but each dream is still separate from the others. People no longer regain Willpower from sleep
                                                        - Rolls to open a Primordial Pathway get a +4 bonus
                                                        - Rolls to use Nightmares get a + 1 Bonus

                                                        Phase 5 (Days 21-25)
                                                        - It counts as having Lair 9 when a roll is required
                                                        - Heroes can track and stalk the Maelstrom like a Beast. Whatever they do with it or if they notice they are not in front of a Beast, it's up to them
                                                        -At this stage, there's no such thing as a single dream. Everyone shares a immense, fragmented nightmare with the Maelstrom at its core. Many gain a Condition such as Delusional, Fatigue, Obsession, Paranoid or Soul-Shocked
                                                        - Rolls to open a Primordial Pathway get a +5 bonus
                                                        - Rolls to use Nightmares get a + 2 Bonus
                                                        - Rolls to use Atavisms get a + 1 Bonus

                                                        Phase 6 (Day 26)
                                                        - It counts as having Lair 10 when a roll is required.
                                                        - Every sentient being in the area, except Beast and Heroes, fall asleep and cannot wake up. Supernaturals can roll Resolve + Composure + Supernatural Trait VS the Maelstrom's Resolve + Presence + Lair to resist this effect, but they have to roll each hour unless they leave the area.
                                                        - Primordial Pathways open around Beasts whether they like it or not, their mere presence enough to alter their surroundings.
                                                        - Rolls to use Nightmares get a + 3 Bonus
                                                        - Rolls to use Atavisms get a + 3 Bonus
                                                        - If a Beast sleeps during the last day, her Horror materializes in the real world. It returns to the Lair as soon as the Beast wakes up, but its free to act as it wants in the meantime
                                                        - At the end of the 24 hours everyone caught in the Maelstrom, except Beasts, Heroes and the Maelstrom itself, dies. Mummies are forced into Duat as if slain and Demons' current Covers are shattered and reduced to 0.
                                                        - If a Maelstrom survives the day, it turns back to normal and gains the Fatigued, Fugue and Soulless Condition
