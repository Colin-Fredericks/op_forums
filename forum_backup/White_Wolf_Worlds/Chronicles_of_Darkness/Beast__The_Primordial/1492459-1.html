            Added sketches of new supernatural abilities to my Playable Hero Template based on the writings of ArcaneArts and Satchel. I have no clue if it's any good, or all that similar to their respective initial visions, but I'm open to critiques on how to improve my execution.

            Journeying:

            Rather than having Lairs, Heroes can call forth the Bright Dream to manifest storytelling tropes in baseline reality and enforce the validity of their worldviews and identities as they pertain to the Monomyth. These are called Journey Traits, and Heroes start with 3 of them: 1 based on Forswearing, 1 based on Legend, and 1 based on Hunger. They are classified into Minor and Major, and at least 1 must be Minor.

            Each 1 dot of Destiny after the 1st allows the player to pick +1 Journey Trait, which must be linked to the Forswearing, Legend or Hunger, with each of these able to receive up to 3 Journey Traits. Journeying requires 1 Willpower Point and a roll of [Attribute + Skill], with the bonuses and penalties being based on the feasibility of the story-logic that the Hero is attempting to actively manifest. If the player lets the ST choose the details of how the Journey Trait manifests, the cost of 1 Willpower Point is waived.

            Roll Results [Journeying]:

            • Dramatic Failure: The Journeying goes terribly wrong and the corpse of the Horror that the Hero partially slayed during his Conquering returns to life for the scene. Said Horror has a Rank equal to the Hero’s Destiny and can open Primordial Pathways into baseline reality, but automatically returns to the Hero’s mind and becomes comatose again at the end of the scene.

            The Horror will do everything it can to make the Hero succumb to the type of fear that was rejected during the Hero’s Conquering, and thus potentially cause the beginning of a Devouring to transform the Hero into a Beast of the Family that corresponds to his Forswearing, though the Hero’s Hunger might change due to the drastic shift.

            • Failure: The Journeying fails to manifest but the roll can be retried with a cumulative -1 penalty. Repeated attempts increase the chances of nearby Beasts noticing the presence of the Hero via Family Resemblance or equivalent supernatural ability. Spent Willpower Points are not regained.

            • Success: The Journeying manifests, and has a pool of points equal to [Successes] + [Destiny], which can impose bonuses and penalties for specific types of rolls on a 1 to 1 ratio. Besides dice modifiers, the Hero can also introduce narrative declarations modeled as free-form Conditions, and each such Condition can be worth 1, 2 or 3 Journeying points, depending on its usefulness for the Hero. The effects must be mundane and last for the scene, or until the Hero dismisses them as a reflexive action. Increase the cost of Journeying points by +2 if the Condition is Persistent, which allows its effects to last beyond this scene. However, a Beast can potentially circumvent or undo such effects with his Atavisms, Nightmares and Lairs Traits, and the same goes for the supernatural powers of other monsters.

            • Exceptional Success: As per Success, but the Hero can also introduce narrative declarations and/or dice modifiers whose effects are supernatural or otherwise extreme, due to the Bright Dream significantly bridging the gap between reality and the imaginary. The Hero imposes a Clash Of Wills consisting of [Highest Resistance Attribute + Destiny] against supernatural attempts to circumvent or undo the effects of his Journeying. The Hero gains a dice trick if he has placed an thematically fitting Anathema on a Beast, based on the latter’s Satiety Level as defined by Anathemas: Low Satiety = 9 Again, Middle Satiety = 8 Again, High Satiety = Rote Quality. This extends to Kin who have Family Ties to the Beast suffering from the Anathema.
            ​
            Example Of Journeying:

            Consider a Hero who has the Forswearing of Invincible, Legend of Judgmental, and Hunger of Whisperer. His initial Journey Traits could work like this:

            • Forswearing [Major]: Nobody suffers the fear of Exposure embodied by the Ugallu, even if it would be rational to do so. Those affected would walk naked into the street and glibly share all their secrets, which can be modeled as the Spooked Condition, but re-skinned as a Careless Condition that compels them to lower their suspicions and inhibitions around anyone who seems even minimally trustworthy.

            • Legend [Minor]: Individuals that weaponize the fear of judgment get marked in some way, such as a mortal journalist getting their car vandalized, or an Ugallu Tyrant finding himself caught in the middle of an angry mob of protesters. This can be modeled as bonus dice for relevant rolls (e.g. Wits + Investigation to decipher the message carved onto the mortal journalist’s car, Manipulation + Subterfuge to provoke rage in the Ugallu Tyrant via accusations disguised as questions).

            • Hunger [Minor or Major]: Those who are predisposed to revealing others’ secrets are compelled to do so and are presented with viable opportunities, thus outing them as monsters. This in turn attracts people who are scared of having their secrets revealed (e.g. people with the Embarrassing Secret Condition), which can contribute to them potentially becoming Hunters, Heroes or even Beasts.

            This can be modeled similarly to the "We Know All You Secrets" Nightmare, with Normal Satiety if the Journey Trait is Minor, or with High Satiety if the Journey Trait is Major, and scoring the same number of Successes as the Journeying. If the player waived the cost of 1 Willpower Point to let the ST decide the details of the Journeying, but spends 1 Willpower Point regardless, then the effective activation of the “We Know All Your Secrets” Nightmare is considered to benefit from Satiety Expenditure.

            Alternatively, this Journey Trait imposes a pseudo-Lair Trait based on the Hunger for Secrets. Perhaps the Minor version of the Journey Trait makes it harder to communicate sensitive information without being overheard (i.e. Echoing), while the Major version of the Journey Trait unlocks doors and windows that lead to places full of incriminating evidence (i.e. Exposed). ​If the player waived the cost of 1 Willpower Point to let the ST decide the details of the Journeying, but spends 1 Willpower Point regardless, then this pseudo-Lair Trait can introduce narrative declarations in the form of Persistent Conditions without the additional cost of 2 Journeying points.


            Additional examples of Journeying:

            A Hero who has the Forswearing of Victorious, Legend of Competitive and Hunger for Power could be a boxing champion who got bored of fighting humans and decided to revitalize his dwindling career by fighting monsters. He could have a Major Journey Trait linked to his Legend with the name of "I Always Find A Weapon Lying Around", such as a bottle of wine on a shelf that he can break to use as an improvised shiv-like weapon, or a rusty lead pipe poking out of a dumpster he can pull out and use as an improvised club-like weapon. This is modeled similarly to the Razored Major Lair Trait of Beasts. Alternatively, it could be modeled similarly to the You Must Obey Nightmare, which compels nearby mortals to hand him objects he can use as weapons, akin to handing a folding metal chair to a wrestling champion.

            A Hero who has the Forswearing of Immortal, Legend of Vicious and Hunger for Ruin could be a domestic terrorist who was judged and persecuted long after he stopped working as a drug dealer, and decided to use chemical weapons to destroy things that represent the ignored ugliness of society in his worldview. He could have a Major Journey Trait linked to his Hunger with the name of "I Always Find Ingredients For Bombs", such as mixing hygiene and kitchen products to make chlorine gas, or mixing hospital garbage and garden fertilizer to make Molotov cocktails. This is mechanically modeled similarly to the Toxic Major Lair Trait of Beasts. Alternatively, it could be modeled as the Run Away Nightmare, which provokes a similar kind of abject panic and flight response when the Hero's enemies are exposed to his chemical bombs.
