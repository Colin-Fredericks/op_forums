                        The BPG did offer a really great vehicle for Beast society that will probably get more stress in a 2e: Primordial cults. Cults let a Beast formalize their concept of lessons into the cult's practices, and any new Beast fostered within the cult, or that simply comes in and takes over a cult that lost its Beast, would learn about that particular view of lessons simply by engaging with those humans. There's also some fun narratives to explore around accepting the role of another Beast and if you can find a path towards Inheritance on someone else's coattails, or struggling to rework a cult in your image, etc.

                                Satchel actually ignored a lot of my post and just focused on the examples, nitpicking them, while not addressing anything else I said. It's why I don't want to argue with them. It doesn't help. Like saying "They" is in reference to other Beasts? Well, no shit. So WHO are these other Beasts? Is it in reference to ALL Beasts? ALL Beasts do this? ALL Beasts talk about Lessons? It's this completely universal thing for an entire supernatural species? What social teaching do ALL Werewolves have on how to behave? What about ALL Mages? Or ALL Vampires?

                                It doesn't fit. Anyways...

                                On Beasts getting more Satiety through acts that can be considered a lesson... Where is that from? I'm looking at the Feeding modifiers on page 108 and I'm not seeing any modifiers based on "teaching a lesson" or something like that... I'm open to missing something there and thought I initially did, but... still missing it. ^^;

                                And thank you for admitting to seeing the point, regarding Beast society. I'm not trying to argue for one way or another on this. Beasts don't NEED to have a strict society, and I can see how a lack of society for them can help push them to interact with the societies of other supernatural groups. but when you add a societal push for "Lessons"... It falls apart for me, because there is no noted structure for that to be pushed through, and even worse, it completely misses opportunities for differences of opinion amongst Beasts in order to push a single agenda. And that is tragic, because we don't need a single agenda pushed, we need multiple agendas pushed.

                                That's conflict.

                                The topic of Lessons for Beasts? Is an amazing area where Beasts can come into conflict with each other through opposing viewpoints. But we have none of that, because the concept of Lessons was shoehorned into a universal "understanding" that we're just told all Beasts somehow understand, and somehow all Beasts tell each other "Lessons are important yo", when the books flat out tell us that the Horror/Soul just doesn't give a crap about Lessons repeatedly.

                                And... I'm just disappointed. Because conflict for Beasts is something the game really struggles with. I really would have loved to see the philosophical differences between Beasts, in what it means for them to feed, in ways that drive them into conflict with one another, and maybe even see how these philosophical differences inform Beasts on why Heroes go after them. Like, consider this idea...

                                What if Beasts can't agree on WHY Heroes go after them? What if some Beasts think that the reason Heroes target Beasts is because they're feeding in the wrong way or for the wrong reasons?

                                Ultimately, Heroes don't, because there's something off with Heroes themselves. But Beasts don't know this. So what if they're trying to come up with new philosophies of what they're meant to do with their feeding, because they wonder if they've veered off the "proper path"? How do Beasts react to each other when they come to different conclusions as to what the "proper path" is?

                                That's conflict! And conflict is necessary for stories. Now, this is just off the top of my head speculation and is greatly unrefined, so take this all with a big grain of salt. Think of how you could use this for your own games.

                                                    consider this. the hunger for prey gives you satiety for hunting down creatures and giving them fear. doing stuff like running over a guy would give you satiety, not much but still. now consider this: a mob boss feared by everyone and always kept safe in an anonymous location and with guards armed to the teeth. if a beast were to strike fear into HIM, he would gain a lot more satiety. this can be seen as a lesson. no matter much power you have, no matter how far your reach, there is always a bigger fish ready to eat you up. this is where lessons come from. that same beast could indiscriminately feed, not gaining as much satiety but pissing off a LOT of people. Lessons in this way can less be about teaching people, but be a catch all term for how a beast focuses his hunger to get better feedings.
