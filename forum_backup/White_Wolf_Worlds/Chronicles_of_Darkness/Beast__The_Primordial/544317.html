    [Beast] Heroes: Necessarily Huge Dicks, or Just Probably?

    06-07-2015, 06:12 PM
    A fair amount of people in the main Beast thread have requested that discussion of Hero generation and personality be moved elsewhere, as it threatens to become toxic. Well, this is kind of elsewhere, so let's go. Are Heroes necessarily gentlefolk of a highly disagreeable nature before their... Awakening? Exaltation? Hero-Homecoming?

    A question I'd personally like answered: Where does the bit about Slashers and Integrity 4 come from? I know Slashers says pre-Slashers need to be Morality 4 or lower to become magical serial killers, but I can't find a statement saying the Integrity threshold is the same. Given the difference between the Morality and Integrity systems I'd rather not make an assumption, so what's the story here?

      Inferring from the sample Heroes, those in the sample scenario, and the explicit description, there's already a degree of inadequacy or frustration with mundane life that seems to habitually characterize the kinds of people that become Heroes. However you or I may feel about that, I feel like that at least is very unambiguous from the text: You can see very clearly what the writers typify as a Hero and what they're like.

      Integrity is closer to a measure of mental stability than it is morality or lack of morality. I think that comes with the caveat that while it seems to measure a degree of mental and emotional welfare, having high Integrity doesn't necessarily mean you're a good or bad person. The three things that seem to cause Breaking Points are: Traumatic experiences, the supernatural, and violations of broad (but important) social moral codes and personal moral codes. So for a person who thinks stealing is the worst thing ever, it will totally be a breaking point for him, while it won't be for a person who cares nothing about that sort of thing. Both will probably experience breaking points if they encounter the supernatural, are tortured or do the torturing.

      The popular assessment seems to be that the low integrity is more likely to be from them being victims of trauma or the supernatural, lending itself to the troubling narrative that Heroes are in fact survivors rather than total jerks. I think it's more likely that the writers and developer were looking at imagining them as failing personal moral codes, like expectations of themselves, or being frustrated by perceived inadequacy and stuff like that, which would lead them to have the low Integrity that lends itself to their being Heroes.

      I would drop the line of thinking about Slashers, Morality and Integrity don't measure the same thing, and the number values mean something differently in the system.

      I'm not endorsing any of the above suggestions, I just think these are the things we can observe from the text. How you feel about it is up to you.

          From pgs 399 - 400 of the preview on the kickstarter; you can download the preview directly from the page if you would like to read it yourself. As this is a preview that is already open to the general public I don't see any problems with pasting from the text but if this is a no no just let me know and I'll erase it.

          "In Chapters Two and Five, we mention that in order for a person to become a Hero, his Integrity rating can be no greater than four. For purposes of creating Hero characters, that’s all you need to know, but it’s important to unpack that idea so that you understand why that’s the case and what it means for Heroes.

          Integrity, as explained in Chapter Four, is a game trait measuring the health of a person’s soul and self-image. Integrity does not, by itself, measure whether a person is “good” or not. It is not a measure of kindness or compassion. Certainly people with high Integrity traits tend to be strong of character, and strength of character leads to empathy and compassion, but it is possible to have a high Integrity rating and be harsh, cruel, or even violent. It simply means that the character is well aware of who and what he is.

          Incipient Heroes, though, lack that awareness. Their souls are weak, whether by nature or by difficult lives whittling away their Integrity. They tend to be shortsighted, self-centered (but not self-aware), and unfocused. A person can fall to this level of Integrity in any number of ways; when you are designing Heroes for your Beast chronicle, it’s worthwhile to consider how it happened. You don’t need to design breaking points for the character (though you can), nor do you need to detail how any given dot of Integrity was lost, but just a general sense of what happened to the character is enough. Did the character avoid talking to people out of belief that he was better? Did the character suffer some hardship and look for someone else to blame? Or is the answer more basic — is the character an abuser or a bully?

          As noted in Chapter Four, it’s possible to lose Integrity from exposure to the supernatural, but this by itself doesn’t usually reduce the trait low enough to qualify a person for becoming a Hero.Thematically, too, it’s not appropriate for someone whose only “crime” was bearing witness to the supernatural to become a Hero. Since Heroes are Storyteller-controlled character, you as Storyteller dictate why the Hero is the way he is, so make choices that allow the Hero to fulfill the appropriate role in the story. If the Hero is a sympathetic character, driven to hunt monstersby the relentless attacks of the supernatural, then you might be better served checking out Hunter: The Vigil (and perhaps using Beasts as antagonists). If, however, the Hero has deliberately shunned other people, defining himself by what he is not, what he hates, or the wrongs done to him, that’s a perfect candidate."

          Personally, I don't feel that lends itself to the assessment of the Heroes being survivors and more to the what I suspect is the intended reading of the Heroes being people who were already prone to blaming and/or hating others for his or her failings as well as any life difficulties and in addition having already been in possession of a narcissistic world view before their encounter with a Beast.

                  It IS a matter of fiat, because the ST determines the Integrity of the NPCs. Using that Integrity as a guideline is one of the meeting points of mechanics and narrative. It tells you something about a Hero, and offers a mechanical conception of the difference between a person who can become/is a hero, and who cannot/is no longer a hero.

                  The stats of any NPC are technically a matter of ST fiat, it's really about what the stat communicates either to player types who can somehow perceive Integrity in-character, or to the Storyteller who uses those mechanics to help foster a narrative.

Continued abuse and/or bullying is likely to be in the same boat, especially as abusers an d bullies are both called out as potential heroes.

                      Reading some people's complaints, I can't help but think that people are trying to turn a game about light gray-gray and dark gray-black morality into a game that's solid black and white. And then getting cuaght up on two details and using it to dress the heroes up in leather pants while branding all the Beasts with dark marks.

                      Are there Beasts who are unrepentant Monsters that deserve to die? Yes. As there are with Vampires, werewolves, MAges, Hunters, Changelings, Prometheans, Sin-Eaters, Demons, Mummies, and Mortals.

                      Are they the majority? Hell no. The book is very clear that Beasts, despite knowing and accepting that they are monsters, still have a human conscience and sometimes hate what they are, even f they don't want to go back to being human. There are numerous examples of Beastial feeding that causes no harm to anyone(in Particular the Tyrant Health Inspector is doing a great service in keeping filthy restaurants closed and reporting bribe attempts)

                      Are there Heroes who are at low integrity from continued abuse or bullying? Probably.

                      Are they the majority? Probably not.

                      Are there Heroes who are right about the Beast who generated the nightmare that created them? Tes. But it's a broken clock scenario, to quote Matt. In General, the Beasts who are creating the Heroes are not the ones.who are hurting people frequently or even at all.

                      Does occasionally being right justify the Hero's actions? No. No it does not. Because that's not why they're hunting the Beast, it's just a rationalization* The real reason they're hunting the Beast is because they blame all of their life's problems on the Beast, or because they think killing it will bring them glory. Or just because it's there. And besides, once you get to the point where you're willing to blow up a daycare with the kids inside because the caretaker is a Predator who hunts the spirits that feed off the children's nightmares during naptime, you're not a Hero, you're a lunatic.

                      *Rationalizations that would be valid points on their own are still rationalizations. Charles Bronson's character from Death Wish is still a serial killer even though he only kills criminals and is officially motivated by revenge for the rapes of his wife and daughter.
