                Read the two revised chapters. The good thing is that despite the changes it's still possible to play most concepts possible with the 1st version. That was a relief. Unfortunately, the lesson part still feel tacked on and not completely integrated with the rest, and the whole message at the moment of the Devouring feels wrong (who would like a message from Longinus at the time of the Embrace?), but it can be ignored without mechanical repercussions, so it's not too bad. The revised text sugest that the Devouring doesn't have to be catalyzed by another Beast; it would be nice if this was more firmly established.
                The revised Beasts are much more human than they were in the 1st draft: practicality, responsibility and morality seem much more prevalent themes now. So, Beast mentality basically shows an evolution from callous amorality, to a kind of dharmic immorality.
                It kind of makes the stories focus more on the Beast's relatonships with their victims as people, rather than on the Beasts themselves, and the Begotten have mostly moved from amoral predators with an alien mindset and a vague goal of learning from and interacting with the WoD's denizens, to maladjusted people who seek to transcend their Horrors' hungers with a version of the 'righteous devil' brand of evil. It's like having 2 different and competing paths of enlightenment.
                One thing I prefer in the revised version is Eshmaki as Nightmares of Darkness. That was a pleasant surprise.

                It's kind of funny how the Hero part says that 'Justification miss the point of having a hunger in the first place'. It's perhaps just my perception but the Lessons feel a lot like justifications.

                Heroes feel more like people and less like mindless Beast-seeking missiles but IMHO they still lack something. The text makes a parallel between Beast and Heroes, but I feel Heroic motivation lacks a similar visceral component, a counterpart to the Beasts' Hunger for te analogy to work. I do hope Heroes don'tt suffer anymore for not hunting. If they are to feel more like antagonists and less like justified victims their actions need to be fueled more by the desire for a kind of reward than the fear of unfortunate consequences.

                All in all, I believe things seem far from being as bad as the revised intro made me think they'd be. Or perhaps I just calmed down.

Mummies following Ker-Uru the Face of Dread are a possible source of conflict. Mummies that follow Keraf-Het could be pointed out as being the most likely to take time and investigate the truth behind a Beast's or Heroe's actions/claims. Mummies following Neheb-Nefert, Ser-Kheru, Hetch-Abhu could all have reasons to clash with Beasts or Heroes, depending on their actions. Mummies in service to Hepet-Khet, Neb-Heru, Ser-Tih and Neheb-Ka are especially likely to clash with Heroes.
