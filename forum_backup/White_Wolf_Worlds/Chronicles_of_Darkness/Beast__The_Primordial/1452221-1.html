                                    No fault or statement on you, but I feel I must point out an irony, that this makes me ruminate on, that exists on this thread that doesn't exist in the other two, one that is delicious and bitter:

                                    It's not that simple.

                                    The Plain stick primarily to what they know and fight in advocation for it. And in the modern political climate, with ultranationalistic authoritarianism on the rise globablly and some very violent reactions in response societally (Like ISIS)*, it's very easy to point at the Plain (and a lot of the other Heroic Forswearings) as being the sort of the people who are the problem(and some of them are more than others-coming around to the Immortal in a pre-post-Covid-19 world is interesting)-and there's a lot of truth there, let's not sugarcoat it. And it would be wonderful if we could keep it that reductive at all points and times as it goes. Wouldn't it be great if we could just say the Plain are awful?

                                    And not responsible for some eye opening things, like, say, Shakespeare in the Bush?

                                    (For context, Shakespeare in the Bush is an article about how people removed from the wider constructs that Shakespeare relies on have a wildly different take, and actually teach how Shakespeare doesn't work in all circumstances-which in and of itself speaks to a new and fuller understanding of what it takes to construct the human. It's a wonderful argument, and the revelations that emerges from one reductive stance challenging another is phenomenal, and one the Makara could not ever make.)

                                    When colonialist powers roll into an area and attempt to stamp out a culture, the Plain are the ones who fight to preserve that culture. When capitalist powers try to turn everything into a complicated argument how things will benefit everyone in the long term, it's the Plain who drag the argument to how industrial attitudes hurt the people here and now. When assholes try to drag in truly harmful deviant sexual identities like pedophilia into fights about sexual social liberty, it's the Plain who cut the line between actual harm and the flimsier understandings of what harm is-the Makara can help explain finer nuances of it all, but Plain make the point.

                                    THe Makara embody alien worlds that exist under the surface, and I can point towards both sex, sexuality, and gender orientation as one point where it's scary to dive in and where some people will just say "No, it's simpler than you're trying to make it.", and to live-action roleplaying, how many variations of LARPing exist and just how many people actually engage with it-and I have actually scared people off who were interested by letting my overcomplicated mind try and explain that, much to my regret.

                                    By contrast, the Plain are tradition keepers and culture greeters. They're the sort of people for whom the cutting of the Gordian is not some grandiose philosophical statement of Occam's Razor but just how you deal with troublesome knots. They're the sort of people who think you'll get line dancing or the Awa Dance Festival if you would just shut up about cultural value and fucking do it.

                                    Like, they are the "common clay of the west, you know, morons", but they are also the sort of people who write diaries about their time because one day it'll disappear and people need to know about it. The fact that these future makers are wrong has no bearing on the fact that we would still be so much more greatly enriched if the Irish or the Norse had fucking written down their fucking mythologies before Christianization.**

                                    Like, I don't ever want to lose sight of the fact that rejecting fear is toxic as fucking hell, don't get me wrong, but these guys are Heroes for unironic reasons as much as there are ironic ones. They can, and often do contribute to many good things, things we in our modern era would fight for.

                                    Beast should flip the script and point out the problems with the average mentality, but it would be a shallow game if Beasts just made good points and Heroes didn't make good points, or have their mindsets contribute to their societies. Beast is a game about terrible people making good points that can either justify why they're there or cover for the fact that they're terrible.

                                    The Makara are the embodiment that nothing's easy to understand*** and that there's a whole fucking lot to understand-borrowing a little bit from an earlier comment, the Makara are the fear of just how much there is to learn and how it can feeling drowning, how it can feel like a fight for air to comprehend the self and the world-but the Plain are valuable because they allow for foundations to learn from.

                                    Don't ever assume things are easy when discussing the depths-it's always a little bit deeper than you think. Unless, of course, things really are that simple. Take the dive, but don't go in head-first.

                                    *in a way that makes the Hegemony Ministry cackle.
                                    **I am bitter, yes.
                                    ***and listen, as someone who has made some of the dive to realize myself as a gender fluid, pre-trans-op feminine sexed, and kind of still figuring out what the fuck my sexuality is, compared to the boy started from being a Mormon, a religious sect with heavily codified understandings of sex, gender, and sexuality, I want people to understand I really understand both where the Makara and the Plain are coming from.

                                                Originally posted by evtrax View Post
                                                Hmm. I guess I was overgeneralizing.
                                                TO be perfectly fair, it's a thing to happen.

                                                We're human. We're not omniscient and omnicomprehescient entities. We rely on consolidating and confining information in order to make sense of things. This is why the Makara are terrifying to us-because it presents a whole and terrifying alien world to understand, where we struggle to breathe. It's why the Plain are comforting-we can always rely on bringing the complicated down to simple principles.

                                                But again, Beast is a game that challenges our comforts. It points out how the Plain can become suffocating in their own right, particularly when allowed to be extreme. It can be transcendantal in helping us realize that learning more and growing our boxes, through the Makara, can not only help us handle more, but subject us to whole new delights and ways to be, that we can be alien in our own ways.

                                                Beast is....actually, shockingly simple in this way. Not comfortably so, but principle-y so. It's good guidelines to go and explore, challenge, learn, grow, and return--or-deviate as neccesary.

                                                THis game is about Transgression, and it's about Compassion. it is applied to self and to other, ands asks the weird question of what happens when a thing is both.
