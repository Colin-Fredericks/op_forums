        A major element that we haven't really addressed yet is the dungeon building aspect. If you like "Boss Monster", or other games that involve building a trap-filled labyrinth to crush any interlopers, that's definitely present in Beast.

        A Beast's Lair starts as a single chamber (room/cavern/field) in the Primordial Dream, one that's thematically appropriate to the Horror. But there are lots of places that have echoes in the Primordial Dream - often caused by brushes with the supernatural. By finding the real world counterpart to such a place (and spending a mix of XP and Satiety), a Beast can annex it, adding it on to his Lair. And since most brushes end badly for the human, these new chambers often bring their own nightmarish powers that can be used to defend the Lair.

        If that's an aspect you enjoy, your group can have a lot of fun building unique Lairs - find the right spot, and the sky is literally the limit (got to love airplanes) for what can be added. Arctic, undersea, underground, jungles, riots, temples, prisons, strip malls . . . Beasts can even combine their Lairs, pooling their defenses and powers.

        And as an added benefit, those new chambers also double as warp points back to the original site (that generated them), allowing a well-travelled Beast to (de facto) teleport around the globe, as easily as you or I walk next door.

            It's worth noting that the Horror replaces the Beast's soul, and souls in CoD are far from the sum total of personality. Losing your soul doesn't immediately change your personality, it gradually erodes your mental fortitude. You can even replace it with a different soul and return to a largely unchanged self. A Beast is something more and less than fully human, but they aren't totally inhuman until they reach Inheritance. Hell, Inheritance is pretty specifically something for Beasts who can't take the strain between their monstrous and human selves anymore.

            Now, as to what makes this conflict compelling to me, why I find this game so personally important.

            This is painful to talk about, but I have a strong sadistic streak and I HATE it. I have intrusive thoughts a lot and for a long, long time I didn't know that was a thing that happens to lots of people. It's terrifying to get those destructive impulses, and I couldn't tell anyone about it because I couldn't let them know that I was a monster. That I thought about doing really bad things, a lot. Not because I wanted to, but because I couldn't control my thoughts. I wanted to be a trustworthy person so badly, but it felt like a deception. It was a constant battle, not just to resist the intrusive thoughts (relatively easy, although disgusting and scary) but also to never let slip a hint that I was struggling with them (exhausting and demoralizing). It was, quite literally, killing me. I have high blood pressure and I spent years refusing to treat it even when I was getting turned away from blood drives due to critical hypertension, because I wanted to die. I couldn't commit an obvious act of suicide because there are way too many people who would blame themselves for not doing something, but if I succumbed to a freak illness? Well, he was just so stubborn. He was young and he thought he was invincible. I got help and I'm on medication to control my BP now, but it took a long time slowly revealing this shit to some trusted friends and learning that sadism and intrusive thoughts are fairly common and do not make me a monster.

            So when I started reading Beast it really clicked for me. These are people who have a monster in their head driving them to commit horrible acts, but unlike me they don't have the option of telling it to shut the fuck up. If they do it eventually rampages on its own, so something has to give. Maybe it's their conscience, they find it's just easier to wallow in their monstrosity. Maybe it's their humanity, they kill off that part of themselves to achieve Inheritance. Or maybe, just maybe they find a way to put it to constructive ends. They join a Freehold of Changelings and sate their hungers on the servants of the Gentry, supplementing themselves with Family Dinner when the Changelings harvest Glamour. Or they join a Pack of Werewolves and help hunt the hostile spirits and Pure, feeding that way. There are options. None of them are harmless, but some are pretty close, and maybe you can live with that.

            I get Heroes too. I know what it's like to see a monster nobody else sees, and to think: This thing needs to die. I know what it's like to want to be the good guy so very badly, and to think there can be no compromise with monsters, even when they fake humanity and have a family that cares about them. It's terrifying to be the focus of that kind of rage, and on the other end to finally realize just what you almost did.

            And that's why family is so important. Other Beasts, and more broadly other supernaturals. People to show you how to be a monster while still being a person, people to help you manage the unmanageable, people who will stick up for you when the rest of the world says "you don't belong." I might not be here if not for several friends who showed me I wasn't alone in dealing with these things. Beasts can teach you how to be a better Beast, more judicious, more responsible (or not), but other supernats have a whole lot to offer in the way of helping to find more community friendly ways of feeding, which is why I find Beast works much better as part of a crossover. Start thinking about what your favorite line has to offer Beasts, and what Beasts can offer in return, that's where the game really shines.

                    That seems a pretty extreme reading. There's no reason to read that as even trying to imply that Beasts are stupid and unthinking in their actions.

                    Beasts might be a bit "out of touch" with their fellow monsters, but they gain power by understanding them; not being Goofy. Forming Kinship bonds is a matter of actually understanding and no longer being out of touch; not a constant state of being the old dog that can't learn new tricks (since, well, you explicitly learn new tricks from Kinship). Beasts aren't (inherently) stupid. You don't have to constantly worry about Monkey's Paw results whenever you ask one for a favor. You might have to worry about them doing things that benefit them instead of you, but that's true for non-Beasts as well. A vampire trying to turn a Beast loose on the Belial's Brood is more far likely to run into the Beast deciding that the Brood are just as interesting to bond with as other vampires, than the vampire is going to need to worry about the Beast being an idiot and killing lots of people in a public space.

                    A Beast is only a "Goofy" if you make them one. As written, they're far more likely to be a Littlefinger when things go poorly for the relationship.

                                                    Originally posted by Aspel View Post
                                                    I'm pretty sure both Act and Rule Utilitarianism would be against putting someone's face on the sidewalk ledge and stepping on the back of their head until their teeth and jaws shatter.
                                                    Besides the fact that Act and Rule are not the only two forms of utilitarian thought, not really. Utilitarianism doesn't prohibit specific actions in a vacuum; that's sort of the whole point. While I meant my use of the phrase curb stomp figuratively over literally, it would still have to be seen in the light of an analysis of utility. Act utilitarianism is actually pretty easy to use to justify such things (part of some of its major criticisms), because it argues that each act is individual and you don't have to worry about creating moral rules that stem from how you apply utility to a given situation.

                                                    You're also ignoring how Hunger would interplay with utilitarian thought. Since sating Hunger is generally better than starving until bad stuff happens on its own, any situation that allows you to sate your Hunger with out tipping the scales of utility into overall harm would be a net positive.

                                                    I mean, I agree that you can be a vigilante in Beast, but you're encouraged to ignore "let the punishment fit the crime".
                                                    Not really.

                                                    First off, the idea that you "ignore" this only applies to sating the Hunger for Punishment. And you don't have to ignore just punishment options, it just isn't what matters to sating the Hunger. Punishing transgressions is what matters, and the more punishing (even out of proportion for the crime) the more potential to sate.

                                                    Second, encouraged isn't really present. The book is stating what actually sates the different Hungers, not what the characters should or shouldn't do.

                                                    If I remember, the Feeding system still encourages you to go overboard and kill.
                                                    Killing is a bonus to the roll.

                                                    But that's not inherently a good/encouraged thing. Overfeeding in Beast is a pretty bad thing because it means getting stuck with the Slumbering Condition.

                                                    If you have Low Satiety and you need a fast meal, killing can help a lot. If you have time to plan and considering your feeding, there are other options that are easily equal to killing (and can make it so that killing doesn't have any additional mechanical impact).

                                                    It'd be nice if the Lessons and the Feeding parts were more tied together.
                                                    No, it wouldn't. If Feeding and teaching lessons were more tied together, the lessons aspect is no longer a philosophic choice by the Beast, but a optimization choice by the player. Leaving them separate is important because it stresses that trying to be "good" is not something a Beast has to do to maintain their existence. It's something they choose to do for their own personal reasons.

                                                    As for this being "tacked on," remember that Beasts have Legends and Lives. A Beast's Life is always part of the character even if these sorts of conversations tend to skip over it. Lessons are a balancing point between Legend and Life, not a Feeding modifier.

                                                    The issue comes up that it's encouraged to feed as abusively as possible.
                                                    Again, this isn't accurate. You get the most dice on your Feeding roll for indulging your Hunger as much as possible. For some Hungers this is more likely to be abusive than others, but the point is never actually abuse itself.
