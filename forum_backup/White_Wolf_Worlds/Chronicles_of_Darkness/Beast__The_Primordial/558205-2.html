                                            That sounds less like a problem with the named experiences benchmarks and more of a problem with Beats assignment.

                                            I don't think people get that a baseline, 0 XP World Of Darkness character is already in many ways a very competent and above-average individual. A single Experience is, in a narrative context, already a noticeable measure of personal advancement.

                                            Mind you, I like that. I like that PCs are, unless you make very thin-spread decisions at character creation, hardly among the most average of janes and joes*. But see, there's a dissonance when you take in the Experience gain that can't really be eliminated in any game of this nature, between the speed of character progression from a mechanical standpoint and that of a real world standpoint.

                                            That dissonance can sometimes make things strange. The greatest example I've experienced was in my group's Vampire The Requiem chronicle. We started with some additional XP because all of our characters were Ancillae. But that's exactly what they were. Ancillae. And since it was our first time using the GMC update rules (though we started with the classic rules and switched shortly into the game), we didn't really have much of an instinctive understanding of 'how many Beats is too many Beats'.

                                            And then at one point we looked back on our sheets, and whelp. We just accidentally elders.

                                            Far as I'm concerned, NWoD player characters are supposed to be exceptional individuals. But there's a certain awareness of how exceptional that is, that players and Storytellers alike need** to understand things like the significance of the mechanical requirements of the Incarnate Inheritance; an effort of comprehension necessary to put into context how impressive some achievements are supposed to be, and to digest and manage the group's advancement in a way that fits the world and the story.

                                            *Have you ever tried playing a game where you and your group stat yourselves as accurately as possible and completely disregard the experience progression system in alternative of "when it makes sense"? Yeah. The difference is really noticeable.

                                            **Of course, how each group treats the dissonance mentioned above and establishes the standard for advancement and the exceptional nature of the player characters is up to them. From what I know, rarely will two groups (or indeed even two people) agree on every instance when discussing the scale of individual aspects of the story or setting. But the books do somewhat define the weight things have in perspective, although in an open ended manner.
