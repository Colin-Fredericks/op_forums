    A Few Questions About Chambers

    11-20-2015, 03:36 AM
    How exactly do Chambers work?

    The rulebook says that the Chamber has to resonate with both your Legend and the Primordial Dream. Okay. Right. It then specifies exactly how a chamber qualifies to be included, which depends entirely on its connection to the Primordial Dream and, as far as I can tell, has nothing to do with your legend. Unless it having at least one element that matches one of your Lair traits counts? But given that you can create said element pretty easily (e.g.: for Darkness, go in at night and turn off the lights, block the windows if necessary), this doesn't seem like much of a limitation. But whatever.

    Then there's a Wits + Occult + Lair roll which... can tell you more about the potential Chamber? Except you don't appear to need to succeed on the roll to add the Chamber to your Lair. You just spent a point of Satiety and if it meets the relevant requirements, it's yours. And there's a chart with dice mods that gives you more dice for certain conditions, among which are "if you actually investigate what happened to cause the Chamber and find the people involved in the incident that created the Chamber." So assuming none of the other bonuses apply, if you've actually done your homework enough to get the dice bonus from doing your homework, the dice bonus is worthless because you've already done the homework you need to be able to add the place to your Lair. If you haven't done your research the dice bonus for having met the mortal who lost integrity or the supernatural creature that made them lose it would be very helpful, but you can only GET that dice bonus by doing the things you would need to do in order to be able to add the place to your lair anyways. Which makes me wonder why they are even present as modifiers on the chart.

    Next question: Are Chambers basically just dream-versions of the actual physical locations they are based on? Can you customize them?
    If a mortal lost integrity in a spooky graveyard while you were there and it has an environmental commonality with one of your Lair traits and you add it to your Lair as a Chamber, is that Chamber then just a copy of the Graveyard modified as necessary by your other Lair traits? Or could you choose to represent that graveyard as, for example, a black marble path lined by gravestones through the middle of a vast grove of bone-white skeletal trees with grey-scale leaves that rise like gnarled claws out of the earth, each one shackled to the ground with heavy iron chains, with still more chains strung between the trees in a vast iron web and the only sounds the forlorn sighing of the wind rustling the colorless leaves, and the the slight jangling of the web of chains, with your Lair traits being represented as some natural aspect of the environment?
    (Toxic, for example, could be represented in this environment as "touching the trees causes every part of them you touched with your flesh to crumble like ash, and dozens of tiny white spiders swarm out of the interior in response to the attack...", at which point the Toxic tilt starts hitting the intruders in the form of horrible spider bites that necrotize the flesh.)

          If I had a clear and unambiguous run-in with a monster that could not be explained away as a person in a costume or something similar, then there are basically two possibilities: one, I hallucinated. Vivid hallucinations are probably a bad sign, and I should immediately go to the doctor and make sure nothing is extremely wrong with me. Depending on the nature of the hallucination, it could be a sign of anything from the relatively benign if frightening sleep paralysis to full blown psychosis. Either way, I want to know. Sure, I could end up in an institution in a worst case scenario, but if I'm suffering from a mental illness bad enough to make me a danger to myself and others, then I probably belong in one. Or it could be the second possibility: monsters are real. Of the two possibilities, "monsters are real" has the burden of proof. Assuming it can meet that burden (which will require tangible evidence beyond the inherently unreliable system of human memory, else we are back to "I probably hallucinated"), then I'm going to be trying to find out everything I can about those monsters. Which will likely involve talking to people about my experience. ... which, given the setup of the World of Darkness means I probably end up dead or worse, but hey, if I knew enough to know that going in, I wouldn't be in a position of having to find out about monsters.
