            All right, I have some complicated thoughts on the issue of Beast and Otherness, so bear with me as I try to untangle them. I don't think it was ever intended to be a metaphor for one specific marginalized group, but it was absolutely meant to strike that general chord of "Dominant forces of society portray you as a monster and tell stories about how the world would be better if you and your kind didn't exist." Of course people were going to see their experiences reflected and think "hey this is about me!" or see whichever marginalized group is currently in the headlines and draw a connection. Those perceptions aren't wrong, the game is about those experiences. But then people took that metaphor to be specific where it isn't and literal where it shouldn't be and it gets ugly because the fears people (in setting) have about Beasts are pretty well grounded.

            I think with more editing and a clearer idea of what the book as a whole wanted to say it could have walked the line a lot better, but from the start it picked a complicated and painful angle to attempt. There's a lot to be said for a narrative of "You say I'm a monster? My very existence makes you uncomfortable? Fine, I'm going to own that! I'm going to exist very visibly! I'm not going to let you be the only voice in this narrative!" But that only works if the fear-mongering isn't grounded in facts, and then Beast goes and muddles it up by devoting so much of its word count to actual monstrous behavior. That's not without a certain amount of real world parallel. Somebody goes on tv and says X group is rapists and inevitably a few of them actually are, and those few validate a view about the rest even though it's not true for the vast majority. That's how people are; you have terrible individuals in every group, and whether they get held up as proof of inherent badness or quietly excused as 'troubled loners' depends on whether the group is marginalized or dominant. And for those that are marginalized it stings every fucking time someone else gets added to the narrative that they're struggling to redefine. Beast tried to have it both ways without drawing a clear faction line between those who choose a path of trying to use their abilities for good and those who lean in to whole hearted monstrosity, and in trying to speak with both voices it undercut the message of both.

I'm trying to reach for a word and the best I can think of is "whitewashing". I mean that, pretending something to be pure with what has never been pure.

As a previous video posted above goes into regarding queercoding for villains and monsters, you can see there is a depth of material here that is very familiar with the very people you are trying to support. I am a black man born and raised in Detroit who saw time and again my own relatives and friends and neighbors fail and fall into bad places in life because of their own mistakes, only to blame it on anything but themselves, be it the white man, the police or just some other 'hater' when in fact it was their own damn fault. Nobody made these people be drug dealers or violent criminals. They weren't denied education or opportunity, they weren't in any way disadvantaged in a primarily black city. They failed because they failed. Yet my entire life i heard these same failures bemoan the injustice of it all and even actively work to keep each other down. Anybody who did well for themselves was stuck-up or 'acting white'. Anybody who decided not to play into the ghetto life was basically a race traitor. So when I turned eighteen after watching my people implode via stupidity for my entire life, I did the thing that would for sure get me away from there permanently and joined the military, only to find that there were enclaves or people who did the exact same thing within that institution as well. They formed cliques along racial lines or ideologies and played the victim, then used it as an excuse to circle the wagons and actively hold down anyone who wouldn't or couldn't play their game. They let their pasts and their ingrained beliefs color everything they saw.

They were not friends. I had always wondered why this particular First Sergeant always treated me so unreasonably shittily; i figured he just was doing the typical military leadership thing, or maybe it was something about me personally and paid it no mind; leadership changes and duty stations change. I just happened to be talking to my dad one day when I mentioned my first sergeant's last name and that he was black and also from Detroit, and my dad - who never forgets anyone - described him perfectly and asked me if that was him. That was when I found out that this guy was basically as close as it gets to a mortal enemy of my dads, and when I straight-up asked him (my 1SGT) he admitted he knew who I was the moment he saw me and just hated my ass because my father had kicked his repeatedly. He hated me for something I had no responsibility in and actively worked to make my life miserable until the day i left that unit. Hell, he tried harder once he realized that i knew. Over 25 years later, he still held enough of a grudge against a man hard enough to take it out on his child who was completely blameless for anything that had happened to him. That's the kind of people we have in this world - vengeful, petty, and utterly incapable of taking responsibility for their parts in what happened to them or letting it the fuck go. So that's why i don't give much shrift to 'past experiences' and people campaigning against anything that vaguely reminds them of a bad past experience or something that could maybe be obliquely symbolic of those experiences if you turn it sideways and squint.

                        The past is the past, and if you go out of your way to see ill intent or coded messages in everything you'll most assuredly find what you are looking for. It colors your viewpoint, your thinking, everything about how you see the world and in my experience people like that are never better for it, nor do they make anything better for anyone else by stirring up paranoia. You can't let perception - yours or anyone elses, no matter how awful or traumatic - dictate reality just for the sake of comfort. Be offended, be upset, crusade for justice - but don't cram your traumas down everyone else's throats.

                            Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                            Archetypal (and not stereotypical) power. Also employment of the uncanny and the novel.

                            Also it has to do with reshaping yourself as much as you are creating something for others. This is "turning the Axe of Sutek on Self" sort of shit.
                            Okay, so let's talk Archetypes vs Stereotypes, and a healthy amount of discussion on coding since there is an undeniable amount of minority representation in monsters media, Chronicles, and, to the consternation of all of us after the fact, Beast the Primordial (and not for the reasons you think). Let's start by creating some bases to work from.

                            Starting with the word everyone is most familiar with, a stereotype is an over-generalized belief about a person, role, class, archetype, etc.(to the point of caricature) that is often widely distributed in a society. The best way to understand a stereotype is as an image without original meaning, or perhaps more accurately as an image with original meaning replaced by dominant narrative, as something you can point to and say that "All X [insert action type here] Y" without explaining why or how it doesn't happen. A good example of what I am talking about here might be the fried chicken and watermelon stereotypes about black people-it's something that, quite frankly, everyone in the South actually eats, so by itself the fact of the stereotype existing ignores the fact that these are just staples of that regions diets regardless, and more so it's called attention to with black people since both are conventionally meals you eat with your hands, and eating with your hands is dirtier than a lot of other means of eating out there, so by strongly associating black people with eating with their hands, it conveys the sense that black people are dirtier than white people, who eat in a variety of ways and are therefore cleaner.

                            It's not always that loaded, but the idea is that the stereotype, the image without original meaning at a minimum, often gets bandied about just as the way things are. Moving away from race and into school archetypes, for example, the jock is always a good looking meathead who is either always a bully or a dude with a heart of gold, while the preppy girl is a vain, catty, try hard who wants to be seen as the queen of the school, and all them prep girls are cutthroat with one another and anyone who gets in their way to become the vainglorious ruler of the school. If you've ever wondered how the phrase "strong, independent woman" came to be a joke in some circles, consider that the jokes Hark! A Vagrant made used to actually played straight in movie after movie after movie for, hell, decades. Shonen (Jump) Protagonist? I know Naruto popped up somewhere, and I'm curious how you compare him to, say, Kenshin or Light, who are both also Shonen (Jump) Protagonists.

                            The main thing about stereotypes is that they are delivered without context and almost always used to brush someone's individuality under the rug. If you've ever been offended because someone said "All white men are trash," well, congratulations, you've gotten a little taste of what minorities deal with on a regular basis. As for the context thing, it's important to note that, Yes Smartass In The Back Row Of The Class, stereotypes do come from somewhere-but again, going to our Black People Eating Things example, the stereotype is often presented in a false way.

                            Using one from our discussion, in Orthodox Judaism, beards basically aren't cut as a response to the commandment in Leviticus 19:27, "You shall not round off the side-growth on your head, or destroy the side-growth of your beard." Since Jews started getting all the financial hoarding crap pinned on them as Arms described, you might not be as surprised from here on to realize just how many greedy bankers and moneyhandlers in fiction happen to have these very prominent beards (Go check out Mary Poppins to ruin your childhood.)

                            Which leads me to my next subject, coding, which is basically when someone isn't out and out X but clearly demonstrate the stereotypes of X. Dwarves as Jews is basically this in a nutshell with particular applications of death of the author (Because whether Tolkien admitted to it or not[and he did, let's not deny that], his dwarves come across as hella Jewish Stereotypes), as are Gay-But-Not-Really Villains (often more tied into fops and such) and Gay Sidekicks (For quick examples, see a shit ton of Disney, particularly a lot of the Renaissance material and DEFINITELY in Ratcliffe from Pocahantas, also I gotta mention Rattigan from Great Mouse Detective because him and Basil are def ex-boyfriends, just saying). Basically, coding is using the stereotypes to convey something about the subject without making them directly the source of the stereotype. THis is kind of a more neutral tool since this can actually be used to create representation in circumstances where limitations might otherwise prevent you, but also be used to basically perpetuate harmful stereotypes while also hiding behind a "But I'm not actually not talking about X now, am I?" statement, or just the ignorance of the masses who don't even realize that such representation is a harmful thing.

                            Coding is complicated because of this, and it's examples go very, very far depending on a direction you wanted to go in, including, for once, dominant narrative coding (though don't get too excited, white cis-hetero some-kind-of-Christian males who feel maligned, because the main way this comes up is when someone of a minority or underprivileged group is basically coded like someone who is the represented in the dominant narrative to convey the sense that the way to get ahead is to abandon their identity and start doing what they can to be like the dominant narrative, and casually withholding that such will always be impossible, and is all in all what academics call "a dick move"). But it's important to note that there's a couple of scales here, namely deliberate to accidental, and....let's call it useful to harmful. The main reason this comes up is that a lot of monster media is often initially coded against minorities, but as time has gone on, a lot of that narrative has been embraced, subverted, and put to use in such ways that they are powerful without going straight to damning by strict association, because, say it with me now

                            EFFECTIVE METAPHORS ARE NOT A STRICT ONE-TO-ONE MATTER.

                            We'll come back around to this with Beast. But now, finally, the big distinguishing point. Let's talk Archetypes.

                            An archetype is...a little complicated, but it's basically a fundamental form, a thematic function or vessel for certain themes. The main reason is that while stereotypes are fundamentally a purely sociological construct, archetypes blur the line between sociological and psychological constructs that are often broader than any one society, and may in fact, depending on who you are listening to on the subject, may in fact be inherent to all humanity. In case you were wondering, this is the notion that Jospeh Campbell's The Hero of a Thousand Faces and the entire concept of Monomyth was trying and failed really badly to argue-but it's not completely without precedence-but we're getting ahead of ourselves.

                            The examples here are things like The Mentor, The Hero, The Divine Woman and the Beastly Man, The Journey, the Initiation, The Journey to the Underworld, the Fire Bringer and Their Punishment, The Small Town, The Ocean, The Bully, stuff like that-stuff that is intrinsic to basically many of the narratives and therefore the viewpoints of major cultural basis-not just American or Japanese or Monster Media, but more Western Civilization, Eastern Civilization. We might modernize the words, but there's always been a form of the archetype, and it is held by broad, loose lines. You can see this in the Hero-sure, there have always been heroes, but there are Warrior Heroes and Trickster Heroes and Wise heroes and many more, and many combinations thereof, and that's before distinguishing between Bilbo Baggins, Kirk, Ash Williams, Captain Marvel, John Wick, Shaft, Zorro, The Prince, Prince, Shaq, etc.

                            Now before Smartass Student In The Back Row of The Class injects, let me acknowledge that there's overlap between the archetype and stereotype, because both are effectively methods of categorization on roles, grouping, similar structures, etc. HOWEVER, the main difference between the two is that an Archetype is a beginning point where a Stereotype is an ending point. An Archetype is an original form, a thing that we deviate from, flesh out, bring life to, subverted, deconstruct-it is a thing that needs to be contextualized. However, archetypes are powerful because they speak so broadly and often carry the legacy of their meta-narrative with them and further strengthen their better constructed versions by association while further weakening poorly constructed versions. By contrast, stereotypes exist primarily to not only answer the question, but to end the questioning. "Why are they like they? Well, they're Black/Gay/The Hero" is a common form of that.

                            Now let's bring this back around to Beast.

                            Beast, as a game, is concerned with Primordial Archetypes, and how, even in their stories as the antagonists, are acknowledged and even near-reverenced for imparting crucial truths to those who participate in them even as they die, and then asks if such a tradeoff is fair, just, equal, or even simply an act of denial as much as it is one of revelation. Yes, there is a normal set of trends to expect from Any Giant, but the Anakim represent a wide variety of entities regardless, and they all end up saying something about it all anyways while saying something quite unique within that umbrella. However, in the zeal of our current day and age, the writers of Beast took the ways those archetypal forms related to the specific issues of minorities, particularly LBGT people, and coded the game heavily in that way deliberately in a way that is equal parts empowering and harmful, with both effects being invariably intertwined with each other while being ruthlessly bound tighter than it should have been the archetypes at work than it should have been. This then mixed in with the cloud of stereotypes that already existed with the game before initial release based on who and what was involved with it, formed new stereotypes from a wide variety of readings that are shallow in a lot of really interesting ways (Suffice it to say, there's a lot of "read the first part", but there's also people looking for the vertical slice, the skimmers, the explicit "fuck this game" readers, etc, etc), and those stereotypes got thrown into the blender with the discussion that followed that got further exacerbated by the second draft and then further exacerbated by the scandals surrounding the lead developer, leading to people who have dealt with it's deeper turths and perspectives underneath the coding and stereotypes....and people who just don't want to handle anything beyond the stereotypes.

                            ALL OF THIS around a game that is about seeking Archetypal Truths and their particular applications in their given, particular situations, whose primary enemies are people who decided to kill them because of their Stereotypes about both themselves and the people they deem as prey.

                            Class dismissed.

People talking about BtP tend to forget that what defines Anakim isn't just "they're big," or Makara "are from the sea." It's about the hopelessness the teenagers feel as they set Jason Voorhees on fire, and he just keeps walking. Or treading water far out of sight of land, and something touches your leg.
                                                        • Every Chamber of the Lair is a "scene" from the Beast's "movie." The broken down train yard with the Low Light and Maze traits isn't just like a room of a house, it evokes a movie scene where the monster chases lost children. The Heart starts very direct, and then the Beast "collects" other Chambers by "acting out" horror scenes in the real world using their powers. That's why Chambers are formed from extreme mortal fear- to encourage the Beast to create those scenes by acting as a horror movie monster.
                                                        • Nightmares and Atavisms are you allowing a little of the film to "bleed" into the non-film reality. Humans don't breathe fire; dragons do. And when you use your Atavism to set the Hero's car on fire, you're basically taking a street with a human and a car and superimposing a clip of Reign of Fire onto it. A Nightmare is a meme you implant in your victim's head, forcing them to act out a part, even if the conditions otherwise weren't conducive. Someone effected by Run Away is "the guy running frantically away from the monster," even if they were just sitting in a Starbucks and sipping coffee.
                                                        • This is why Feeding works at all. Stuff like vitae and essence are real... well, "real" things that the vampire or werewolf is consuming. A Beast is just looking at someone feeling an emotion, one caused by the Beast themself. And that's because feeding is just acting out a script, one where the monster acts like a monster, whether it's a dragon sleeping on its hoard or a roc carrying off a villager in its talons. When you take a nap in your room of stolen drivers licenses after watching someone get a ticket because of your latest find, you are pretending to be that dragon. And when you kidnap someone and drive them to the edge of town, before letting them go unharmed, you are pretending to be that roc. And that theater is what satisfies the Horror.
                                                        • Heroes occur for the same reason as the above stuff. The monster always has the run of the place, until the very last minute. Then some random human manages to figure out (either through luck or Chekov's Gun) how to stop them/get away. So, Heroes can impose weaknesses on Beasts the same way Beasts use Atavisms/Nightmares; by superimposing their movie onto the world. The one where they win.
