                    I have only quickly read the previous posts, so my suggestions may be redundant, but here they are:

                    -Rules for creating new Atavisms, and the possibility of new Atavisms being inspired by other monsters, like Nightmares.
                    -More examples of feeding and lessons.
                    -More feeding options: Options to feed on symbolism and/or narrative significance as well as emotion; or feeding on awe as well as fear.
                    -An equivalent to Vampires' bloodlines or Changelings' entitlements. Archetypes?
                    -Options encouraging less directly predatory contacts with mortals: perhaps a way to become the guardian/protector/symbol of a territory or community, in a mystically significant way.
                    -Treasure or trophies. Specifically, objects/body parts created/owned by Beasts that other creatures might covet, like a naga's pearl, a vouivre's escarboucle, a kitsune's ball, an unicorn's horn...
                    -More exploration of non-atagonistic relationships between both Broods and individual Beasts.
                    -Beast organizations/societies; Cults of the Dark Mother, monster creating corporations, etc...
                    -More info on the Primordial Dream and whatever (or whoever) can be found there.
                    -More Lair customization options.

                            Originally posted by Claire Redfield View Post
                            The only thing I can think of right off-hand that my group really wants to see is an ability to assume monstrous form. Bonus points if it's customizable like a demon or similar. That gives us a big set of options for "monstrous shapeshifter" types, which would be great for me!
                            I disagree with this one. I see one of the strengths of Beast as the fact that they don't have a monstrous form. (And looking at some dev comments from Dark Eras, never did.) They're creatures of nightmare, they inhabit the deepest fears of your darkest nights, they linger in half-imagined forms and bring their power through in (mostly) unseen ways that terrify the victim in what they didn't see, not what they did. Victims can't rationalise what happens because they can't see anything. Their mind fills in the gaps from the dark places or irrational fear.

                            If Beasts had a 'monstrous shapeshifter' form, that would be the antithesis of Beast's elegance, dilute their fear, and just lead to a blunt 'monster' splat, not a Beast.

                                    Originally posted by Claire Redfield View Post
                                    I accept most of your points as preferences, but this one I think is untrue.
                                    And I disagree. Which is fine.

                                    I like that Beasts don't transform. I like that there's no apotheosis where the Beast's mastery of the art of fear becomes as crude as 'shapeshift into a giant monster'. Beasts master fear and become one with their horror by understanding that the most terrifying thing in the human psychology is that which you invent yourself. To paraphrase Alfred Hitchcock, you can build suspense and tension by implying and not showing. You stretch it out as the audience fills in the gaps through their own worst, darkest, secret thoughts. Once you show the surprise, it's done. People quickly move on.

                                    Beasts have maintained the fear by not being the visible surprise. They're tension and suspense and fear.

                                    Now putting 'shapeshifting monster' into the PG is a mistake, IMO. Many groups may want it, people may lament it. Adding it codifies it into Beast, takes the lesser 'jump scare' path, and diminishes what I think makes the game a masterpiece.

                                    YMMV. Yours obviously does. Repeating above, this is fine. This is a thread for suggestions and requests. You've done the later, I've made the former.

                                        The original Gojira was very much a horror movie, much as many folks think of the campy '60s-'70s Godzilla movies.
