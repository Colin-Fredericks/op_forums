        Yeah, I'm sorry, but that is hardly something that's unique to the Jewish community.
        How unique a prejudice is doesn't really matter does it? The application of it does. Europeans have a rather scarily long history of using "they're going to steal/rape our women!" as a reason to persecute minorities; Jews included. It just never really stuck as an anti-Semitic stereotype, and lacks the cultural resonance it has for, say, black people in the USA.

        This has particular resonance with Jews, because it's been used against us for centuries by Christians. This goes back to early Christian law prohibiting usury, but many Christian nations allowing Jews to be money lenders since they weren't Christians. Which was great right up until someone decided it was easier to kill off a bunch of Jews than pay them back. Thus painting Jews are particularly greedy, swindlers, and cheats, became a standard practice to influence people into accepting attacking them after they got too successful doing services Christians needed someone to be doing (or steal their business and make usury legal). This was carried forward rather harshly into the Reformation after Luther felt spurned by the Jews not all falling to their knees and converting after seeing him 'fix' Christianity. And was revived a few magnitudes over by the Nazis, and is now major feature of modern anti-Semitic conspiracy theories.

        People seem to forget that the Nazis talked about the Final Solution... because it was the last step in a series of escalating oppression. The first step was reviving this stereotype and destroying Jewish economic stability.

                                                    I'm a notorious iconoclast when it comes to WoD and CoD and I get a lot of flak here (often rightfully so, I will admit)

                                                    But is it wrong that I would much rather play a Hero than a Beast? I started a thread on it earlier, but I admit I worded the opening post poorly and the thread eventually derailed.

                                                    I'm a marginalized person. Yes, I may be a cis white male BUT I am bisexual, disabled, mentally ill, impoverished, and a religious minority (I am a pagan and prior to that, was an atheist) so while one could say I may be privileged in a few areas (and I won't argue that) it is very evident that I am marginalized in many other areas. This ties into why I dislike the concept of Beast.

                                                    As a social outcast my whole life and as someone who is LGBT (bisexual male) I just didn't like the stuff that was announced about Beast. The fact that several Heroes in the corebook are little more than shallow right-wing caricatures was extremely off-putting, especially given the political views of Matt and several others who worked on the game. More on that in a minute...

                                                    Now, as for the proverbial elephant in the room....I have a deep-seated dislike of Black Hat Matt that predates the revelations about his behavior by several years. He was exceptionally mean to me on the old WW Forums and RPG.net and in many ways, his attitude shaped me into the annoying, spiteful, and toxic iconoclast that I am today and have only been struggling to get away from very recently. My dislike of Beast has nearly nothing to do with Matt's reprehensible and abusive behavior.

                                                    My dislike of Beast is partially in its seemingly contrarian subversion of monomyth and its attempt to be the "anti-D&D" mixed with its preachy political angles in a lot of the preview materials and the corebook to a lesser extent.

                                                    The way people described Beast (including several of its fans) it felt less like a game that could be fun and enjoyable and more like a propaganda manifesto with dice mechanics.

                                                    My dislike of Matt aside, I also strongly disagree with Olivia Hill. I don't have a personal dislike of her like I do with Matt, but I find her political views to be abhorrent (and I'd prefer if we left it at that and agree to disagree) and much more importantly, I strongly dislike her stances on thematic purism and how WoD/CoD "should be played" but I am sure most of you know that by now. However, I mention it because it is very germane to the main point I am trying to convey

                                                    Now, back to my life as an LGBT individual and how that relates to Beast...

                                                    If Beast really was intended to be an allegory for LGBT people and other marginalized groups, then that just disgusts me on many levels, Not just because of Beasts being portrayed as abusive monsters (though I do have issue with that) or because Matt himself turned out to be a predatory abuser, because as Olivia Hill and countless others pointed out, a lot of people contributed to Beast, with Olivia herself doing a lot of the actual heavy work.

                                                    Which brings me to my main point as why I cannot stand Beast as it is written. Matt and Hill are known for being very vocal about their political views, especially as how it relates to our hobby and often times, the politically outspoken on all sides of the political spectrum tend to make sweeping generalizations about entire groups, especially if they are marginalized in our society

                                                    As a marginalized person I don't like being generalized and used in a work that I feel is a blatant propaganda piece, regardless of intent or political affiliation. Now, I know a lot of people here are also LGBT or part of another marginalized group and if Beast resonates with you, that is fine with me and I hope you get an enjoyable and fulfilling experience playing it.

                                                    But it did not resonate with me and regardless of their intentions, I don't want Matt MacFarland, Olivia Hill, or anyone else at Onyx Path speaking for me and my thoughts and experiences when I can speak for myself.

                                                    TL;DR-Just because I am marginalized doesn't mean I want to be generalized. I'm an individual human being, not just a label
