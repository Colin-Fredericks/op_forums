                                    I don't really find it persuasive. I don't see the text supporting their position, nor the thematic or logical analysis of it. Let me make my case.

                                    Primordial Pathways are a really key power to Beasts. It's truthfully their most impressive capability. Their aptitude is so strong for opening these Pathways that the very definition of Primordial Pathway cites, "The paths to access the Primordial Dream. Beasts can do it fairly easily, and can open them for other beings with Kinship." (Glossary). Page 91 starts off, "As the Masters of the Primordial Pathways, Beasts enjoy a nearly unmatched freedom to wander the different planes of existence." On the same page, the power Skeleton Key states that its due to this instinctive connection to Primordial Pathways that they're able to force open other mystical gateways. Under the Bed allows a Beast to rip open a Primordial Pathway utilizing the nightmares of a mortal.

                                    What the section of opening Primordial Pathways states that is very important here, is that "As with many aspects of their Lair, it's the resonance, or similarity, between the Lair and somewhere else that affords access." Its interesting that it's specifically referring to the Lair itself, and not specifically Chambers. Page 93 points out that Chambers are a defined "setting" of a Lair. It's Traits which are called out as actually defining what the Lairs resonance is. Traits of a Lair are not defined by Chambers at all (although they're good inspiration for such). So when the section Primordial Pathways states that a Beast who doesn't know where they want to go, they can feel around and open a Pathway to somewhere that is resonant to them, that's not referring to a similar Chamber, but a similar environment with their Lair Traits, because that is what defines the resonance of a Lair.

                                    The Primordial Pathway section states that its easiest for a Beast to open a Pathway at a physical location the one of their Chambers is based on. But says they can do without that, with "an additional dice penalty" (singular), along with spending a Willpower point. The willpower point is a real cost there. Further, there's only a single penalty in the list for a material world location that isn't based on a Chamber. -3 This is still affected by any bonus dice the Beast gets for having any applicable Lair Traits active in the area, whether natural or bled through from his Lair, and this fits because the bonus you get is based on how resonant the location is with your Lair, and its Lair Traits that define a Lair's resonance.

                                    Regarding the Chambers and Lair Traits section... Remember that Chambers are just "setting", they're not resonance. What this section is pertaining to is how to place the resonance of your Lair into the location by calling on a Lair Traits. The more divergent from a Chamber to exemplify to build your resonance, the more difficult it is. Each of the given levels of similarity are in direct follow up with a stated mechanic, "To impose Traits into a substitute location like this, the player must succeed a Wits + Resolve roll, with a dice penalty based on how dissimilar the location used is." Note that its only Exact which mentions that using the exact location for a Chamber does not incur a dice penalty to opening a Primordial Pathway. And this is true! You actually get a dice bonus for doing so!

                                    However, for the other three examples, even though each makes a statement of INTENT to open a Primordial Pathway, the citation of a dice penalty is alone as a separate sentence. That's because the dice penalty is explicitly for the above mechanic for rolling to impose a Trait. The Beasts in the three examples are looking to impose Traits in order to make opening a Primordial Pathway easier.

                                    Therefore, these two sections are separate and there is no editing error here (though perhaps not worded most clearly). This means that the worst penalty a Beast will likely encounter for opening a Primordial Pathway will be -6 from their Lair to a random location that opens in the material world, where the location will still be resonant with the Beast's Lair.

                                    This is a powerful ability, but time and time again the book hammers on that Beasts have an instinctual grasp of Primordial Pathways and that its their Forte to wander from plane to plane as they wish. This is perhaps their greatest strength.

                                    The book just provides zero support for Primordial Pathways to be something that Beasts find difficult. I've provided citations from the book to support my argument and I hope it's found persuasive. If you can see any flaws in my reasoning, or something from the book I've missed, please let me know. I would like to take it into consideration.

                                            All the world's a stage, but let's not mistake the props for the story.

                                            The Horrors of Beasts are ancient trends of fear and security born into a face scarred into being from the confluence of a moment in a place, when those ancient trends takes on a particular manifestation in a moment-an seasoned actor taking on a new mask, if you will. The Chamber that forms the Heart sets the standards, it creates the mood, the atmosphere, defines the sort of characters that may be acted out, but the it's tenor of realization as presence that enters into form, that becomes the roc, the dragon, the griffin, the character who defines the role. There are certain roles you give to the Rock and certain roles you give to Sir Ian McKellan, and while there may be some points you consider both for, they are very different actors with different considerations. So think of the relationship from Family/Hunger, Horror, and Lair as being a collision between the two extreme-the Lair is born from specific contexts that the more archetypal forces of Family and Hunger form the character of the Horror.

                                            The addition of Chambers is something of a moment of actualization, a crosspoints of reaffirming the core dimensions of the Horror's main themes and the realization and acceptance of varations on that theme, On recognizing the wider, deeper, more nuanced complexities of the core essence that makes the primal attitudes of man kind relevant in this day and age.

                                            The Primordial Pathway, as a gaming tool, is not intended to be only be a quick transit tool-in fact, it's really more a tool of establishing the set for the story of your character. It's a way to step up from merely bring in in your lair traits in full, it's escalating-it's bringing the poor unfortunate soul into you lair, it's ensuring that you are where you are needed to be to keep kids from fucking way too young.

                                            Now, I know what you're thinking-"but Arc, if the point is to ensure that the Beast is acting on a stage appropriate for it's own story, then shouldn't it be good and right that they have easy access to any stage that merits their presence?"

                                            Well, no. Because there's masks, and there's masks that are just for you/the play, darling.

                                            The thing about Chambers and Chamber analgoues(the latter being the basis for the argument) is that theoretically any place, even any Chamber, can form an archetypal(read empty) enough place to facilitate any sort of archetypal play. An wheat field can be a place for a Giant or a Leviathan, a Predator or a Collector, depending on how you want to play it. But Horrors aren't merely archetypal, being the child of a deep context and fresh face, ie, specific context, ie, the nature of their Heart. Any place is merely a mask anyone can wear, so devoid of detail that a Beast can't connect their Heart to it, and really evoke the performance needed. Moving beyond masks to stages, it's like trying to compare a blank stage to one that at least has some set design work done, or is hosted at a place that is haunted or cited for romance or something like that.

                                            As a TL;DR version, consider this-Beasts are powered by their Horror,s and Horrors are defined by their doing rather than their being. Having a place they fit matters, but having a place that is set for the story they tell is far more imporant. So having a place they know and have influence over the 'stage direction' of means that that place is more of who they are then someplace that is not.

                                            Now this is something of a moot point, considering most of that is focused on bringing Lair Traits into a place that doesn't have it rather than actually utilizing the Primordial Pathways, but from what I can tell, the most pertinent fact of here are the World Penalties, which includes metaphysical distance, the Lair Trait Bonuses, which ensures the stage is more and more set for the Beast, and the Imprecise Location penalty, which frankly is just sense-when any theater will do, an expert on the subject isn't going to bother helping you find the place to perform, so at that point it really is darts. The max is -6 from two -3, and it seems avoidable by just keeping your story on point.

                                            Another factor is to consider the idea of the Beast as a wanderer and explorer of the worlds, that they, in their primordial nature, are archetypally more inclined to wandering and journeying than merely "jumping" from place to place, to actually travel, to experience in awe. Dragons are always found at mountains, but they still fly too and fro. The point of Lair is for your mountain to always be realtively close-but you still need to fly from place to place, and not every mountain is yours-some belong to the dwarves, until you take it.

                                            But the point is this-while a transport tool, and a damn cool one, the Primordial Pathways (and Imposing Lair traits roll) is really more of a way of evoking the narrative of your character and their Horror, and it plays out to the purpose of keeping your narrative, and Beast's broader narratives, on track

As darkness falls in the forest to a pitch black, monstrous wolves roam and hunt, so close your doors and huddle up to your fires.

                                                        Your Lair doesn't actually exist as a real location. There is no actual correspondence between a Lair and a geographical point on Earth. The Primordial Dream just exists and is everywhere and nowhere at the same time. Due to this, it's the resonance of your Lair, the evocative expression of the dangers your Horror represents and has as it's calling cards (rumbling cracks of thunder, the fall of blinding darkness, the silence that falls and all you hear is your own heartbeat getting faster) that let you touch your Lair to real world geography, because those evocative elements are the herald to your Horror's arrival that people dread.

                                                        Functionally, to your Beast... It can be a fast-travel system. But to the Beast's Horror, it's not really, because to the Yeti Horror, any place that's being covered with a howling blizzard is the outskirts of it's Lair, whether thats in the Himalaya's or in North Dakota.
