    Originally posted by ZealousChristian24 View Post
    Another idea is that the Begotten are born to unify the creatures of darkness, bringing their Kin under one banner, whether to protect themselves or to claim the world as their own,
    This would have been great. As it stands, there's a reason for Beasts to go find other player splats, but not much reason for other monsters to bother with Beasts or treat them as anything other than a dangerous nosy mystery. If Beast had something going on that the other player splats would be concerned about, to flesh out a reason for a bunch of random folks from different gamelines to pull together, it could have been THE crossover Chronicles of Darkness gameline.

    As for Monsters in Black... well, the tricky part there is that Beasts NEED people to "misbehave" if they're going to take that route. The last lesson they'll ever teach is "here's how to cooperate with each other to find and kill me".

    The most compelling idea I've been able to find for Beast is that it's about alienation. Prometheans are alienated but they have a way back "in"; Beasts don't. Beasts aren't just the monster out beyond the campfire; they're also the monster that gets chased down and triumphantly killed. They are Not On Humanity's Side. Their Horror knows that and so do the humans (represented by the heroes), it's the Beast themself that needs to grapple with that; whether that means finding a new family they can live with, throwing themself into predation, or trying to mediate with the enemy and probably screw it up.

        Mother's Kiss and Family Ties both offer reasonably potent benefits for other supernats. Beasts can force open passages to other planes of reality, useful for escape as well as invasion. Beyond that, Beasts are just powerful monsters with a versatile tool kit of powers available to them. It's a dangerous world and everybody has enemies, that alone is reason enough to consider Beasts as ally potential. Don't forget companionship either. Supernatural types are isolated from the vast bulk of humanity by their otherness, and their own kinds tend to be few in number. This is more true of some splats than others, but finding somebody who understands you, the part of you that you can't show to most of the world, can be a pretty powerful motivator.

                            I think that when it comes to Begotten Philosophy that to some degree it all should come down to defining what aspects of a nightmare the Children should exhibit.

                            The reason why I love the inclusion of lessons in the final version of Beast is because it's a steppingstone to explore the nature of nightmares and how they have supernatural depth and substance in the world of Chronicles of Darkness. It's the reason why we love horror stories, and what we take away from such tales even when there might not be any intended morals. At it's core, the subversive themes of Beast gives us as players a lens to dissect and examine what elements we love most about good horror and most importantly why we like them.

                            For example, one familiar option and three less so:

                            Visionaries, Nightmares as Lessons: Humanity sometimes forgets. They forget the importance of family, friends, health, dreams, and the value of their own short lives. It's not always their fault, life has a way of getting away from oneself sometimes. They blink and they're old, and all that's left is regret for the roads not taken. You'll make sure that doesn't happen to them. Tonight they'll remember what it's like to run like the first time they knew fear. Tonight they'll struggle and cry like they haven't since adulthood stripped away their imagination of the unknown. Tonight they will fall and bleed and pray to powers they had given up believing in when the world became a place without meaning. But, they will wake up. They will survive. They will see the snow covered grave and cry out "Is this a vision of what will be? Or what may be?!?" And you will feed. They will not take their life for granted for a very long time.

                            Emissaries, Nightmares as Mirrors: They see something different. Every single one of them. The human sees just another human, ordinary if not perculiar in their individual ways. Look to close into their eyes or whisper just the right word, and they'll see the monster beneath. The monster that any human could become under the right circumstance. The supernatural creatures see themselves too, except for the particularly canny. It's never a perfect reflection though. The vampire sees a dead thing like themselves, always thirsty and able to blend in among the prey. But the thing inside isn't the slightest bit human, maybe never was. The witch sees the arcane aura similar to their own, and recognizes the desire for knowledge and understanding. But the aura unnerves them in it's hunger in a way they don't yet understand, like a truth that is until it isn't. What about themselves? What does the Beast see when it looks deep within itself? Well, it sees the broken reflections of the waking world. A dark mirror of a dark world. Beautiful in it's cruelty, terrifying in it's scope. Horror is in the eye of the beholder, and the reflection is endlessly hungry for something to give it form and substance.

                            Pragmatists, Nightmares as Truths: There are a few simple truths in this world. They aren't pretty. They aren't kind. But they are true, and whether you like it or not one of those truths are "Everyone's got to eat." That doesn't necessarily make you cruel. Nore does it mean you have to be gluttonous. It also doesn't mean you have to use proper table manners either. Nightmares and dreams are one and the same, the only difference is who's getting devoured and who's the one chowing down. Why not use your powers to make life comfortable? Why not use it to seize power? Under any other circumstance the prey would use it to do the same to you...well ok, that's not necessarily true. Everyone does have to eat though. Nothing's going to change that. Why not embrace that truth rather than suffering in humane martyrdom. That's a lifestyle choice. Hunger isn't.

                            Celebrants, Nightmares as Legacy: Here's the thing about dreams and nightmares, they're fleeting. Try remembering the last dream you had. Last nightmare? No? That's not a suprise. Dreams are fleeting because life is fleeting. We're born, live our lives for as long as we can, and then die. It doesn't have to be that way though. A part of one's self can survive beyond the veil of death. Shape the lives of countless humans and more. The dream need not die with the dreamer, nor the nightmare. The Legend holds the seeds of immortality. The Primordial Dream is shaped by the ancient shadows of monsters and fiends long gone, but who's actions and deeds still haunt the dreams of mortal minds. The hunger pushes one towards apotheosis, to a state of divinity impossible to obtain through mortal means. Follow the footsteps of those who devoured before you. Seek to become the Incarnant Beast. Become Myth, else all you are pass into eternity with the rising sun.

                                    Originally posted by Niknokk View Post
                                    Beast is the only gameline with that "we're all family!" thing going on, and I don't get the insistence that the other organized groups would naturally play along with that.
                                    Just on the face of it, there is no reason why they necessarily should. A Beast who takes family with supernaturals as their philosophical foundation - at least a sensible Beast who does so - won't be blind to that, and consider it their responsibility to provide reasons for other supernaturals to accept them and one another. Maybe they'll fail, but it's certainly a viable thing to try.
                                    My own approach to a "we are family" philosophy is generally to act as a mediator between factions.

                                    Others...

                                    One of the things that struck me about the first draft (a bit less about the second, but not entirely gone) was that despite its outre protagonists, Beast was good for a game where supernaturals continued to live very mortal lives, and engage with the world in very mortal ways - partly because feeding, at least in small meals, can often be carried out via behaviours that wouldn't considered monstrous in humans (and yet since they're of a continuum with more horrific feeding, good for looking at the strands of power and abuse that are accepted parts of human life).
                                    Thus, I like the idea of an Isolate philosophy, who consider their human life (and Life) to remain their "real" life, and continue living it as much as possible. They might still dig into the supernatural world around them, but in the ways mortals might, and perhaps more focussed on phenomena than beings. Not rejecting their supernature, just not using their supernature as a reason to reject their former lives.

                                    I also considered a Ravener faction - who might be disparagingly called Gluttons - who respond to the "is this all there is" question with "maybe, maybe not, but either way, it's good and true and right." Feeding their Hungers is a right thing, and they do so without guilt, shame, or remorse. But not necessarily without conscience or restraint - feeding might be a right thing, but it doesn't have to be the only right thing. I guess these would be the ones who take to heart that Dresden Files quote above, or a similar one from the earlier Nightbreed - but part of that power and choice is that they're not even forced to abandon love or morals - unless they want to.
                                    And they focus on their Legends the way the Isolates focus on their Lives. Some of them would pursue Incarnation as their ultimate goal.

                                    I hadn't really considered something like Heavy Arms's Meals, but I like the idea. Beasts are Mouths, and they exist to keep the Dark Mother fed. As well as big meals, they might also try to vary their feedings as much as possible - metaphorically make sure all her vitamins and cravings are satisfied.

                                        My thinking was that rather than "teaching lessons," both Beasts and Heroes are basically actors in reenactments of ancient, memetic stories. The struggle for Beasts and Heroes would be trying to balance their human sensibilities, and acting out their roles in the larger story. Beasts and Heroes aren't supposed to teach lessons on their own- rather, they tell stories together, to those who watch their interactions. Of course, some Beasts and Heroes don't understand that.

                                            Running off of a notion I've been chewing over for the last few days:

                                            The Night Parade take something of a pragmatic view to supernatural life. Not for nothing, they reason, are the most legendary monsters among their kin also the most well-traveled. The territoriality of the Horror is a trap; the Lair is all a Beast should need as far as a place to call their own, and the Apex is a role too pinned down by geography to be worth claiming.

                                            The family is their territory, they say, unbound by state or sea or shadow, and so the Harbingers aim to be the most gregarious of the Begotten, forming wandering broods and hunting across the myriad lands and worlds of the Chronicles of Darkness in the pursuit of new sympathies with which to grow the social network through which they feed and meet new people as they live the lives of Strangers in strange lands.

                                            Most of all, say the eldest among them, it is important that the night move on with the dawn. The Children are the scions of a dynasty on which the sun never need rise; it's always midnight somewhere, after all.

                                                    It occurs to me that one aspect I don't think I've yet seen is Beasts As Reflection. The text is so focused on family with all the other monsters that it seems to forget that humans can be the biggest monsters of all. Maybe most people will never have a direct connection to a Horror, but they don't need that to do many of the objectionable things Beasts do. The Horror makes it easier, sure, but it was always possible. I can see Beasts saying "I'm you, just with more power and less restraint, and deep down you know it."

                                                    That's sort of a theme of the CoD as a whole, looking at human problems and shortcomings from a perspective just outside human, but as the crossover splat I think Beast has potential to really dig into it. The Kinship nightmares are a start, looking into what human fear each monster specifically represents.
