    A Hunger to Conquer the World-Heroic Hunger and Satiety

    02-01-2023, 11:14 PM
    "The king must be greedier than any other. He must laugh louder and rage harder. He must exemplify the extreme of all things, good and evil. That is why his retainers envy and adore him. And why the flames of aspiration, to be as the king is, can burn within his people."
    -The Rider Iskandar Zeus-Ammon of Macedon, Fate/Zero

    “You were a hero round these parts. That's what they call you when you kill so many people the word murderer falls short.”
    ― Joe Abercrombie, Best Served Cold​






    Okay, we've had a couple of Family and Forsworn threads since the last Hero mechanic, I think it's time to revisit the way the Forsworn act on the world.

    Last time, we discussed Heroic Lair and the overall concepts at work behind the Forsworn Heroes model. This time, so we're going to exposit a little bit about the role of Satiety for the Heroic narrative and it's overarching impact on the narrative of the Three Children of Primordial before laying out the model-but before that, we should probably do a little backrooms conversation about some of how NPC's work in general before getting into that.

    Stare and I Disappear, Blink and I'm Here to Kill You-NPC's Relationship to Off Screen Narratives

    I'm going to say something super controversial-the Storyteller's characters don't exist when they're not in a scene.

    Okay, probably not the stunner you were hoping for-not even the stunner I wanted to deliver-but it's important to note that NPC's aren't really doing anything or having a narrative when the Storyteller isn't attending to them. Their actions off screen are more mechanical and skeletal in the affect-sure, a ST can go and really think about what they were doing where and when, why and with who and how, but it's more often dropped down to the results that come into play when they next appear on screen, or when the player characters come into something affected by them. Because of this, NPC's mechanics may seem similar to how PC's operate, but actually behave in a fairly different way.

    As a good demonstration of this, consider Willpower and Anchor traits in CHronicles. For players, the relationship of these two often forms a backbone of the general flow of gameplay and scene structure- in one scene, the PC's go to try and get something done, conflict ensues, and the PC's spend willpower trying to get it done, and in the next, licking their wounds and either making use of their success or planning what to do about their defeat, they also take some downtime action where they'll indulge in their Vice or commit to their Virtue (or Life and Legend, as it goes in Beast) to restore their Willpower, which will often also give them space to process their emotions, review what's happened, come up with plans and then decide on one. From there, they'll go and try to do something, and the cycle continues.

    But for NPC's, that causal relationship is looser and not even really connected. Using an antagonist for example, a Storyteller usually gives them as many Willpowers as they need dramatic twists and turns in a scene, and in fact probably don't give them a direct number so much as weigh on the feel of the scene and what they want out, allowing them moments of punching harder and taking the advantage until it no longer feels appropriate. By similar token, while a NPC's anchors can explain what they go to do to restore their strength of conviction and dedication, an antagonist's Virtue and Vice (or, again. Life and Legend) are more there for player character's to have a way to contend with them in social contentions, either posing weaknesses that allow players to manipulate them by, or as lines where they are adamant and unflappable in keeping. Sure, the argument can be made that these things are appelaing to antagonists because it restores their Willpower, but the general reality is that a ST can say a NPC was off doing a bunch of stuff that moves the plot along that never once brushes up against their Anchors, come back into play with a new amount of Willpower, and players will generally not even question it. Or, to bring this back around, the antagonist doesn't really exist and have to do things like an existent actor so long as they aren't on screen.

    Of course, this can get tricky when a NPC's offscreen actions aren't just intended to setup their next bit of personal conflict but have a meaningful impact on the setting and advancement of their own agendas and goals. Again, no one really cares if an antagonist bought themselves groceries, made steak sandwiches, enjoyed them while watching True Detective Season 1 again, and then got up, put on their Dick Dastardly cape, and go to snicker at the player characters, but they probably do care if they happen to kill their favorite gas station attendant on the way there, and will definitely care if they blow up the group's secret base. The first and always good piece of general advice is to try and get the players at any scene where meaningful action is being had, but as time and tide has revealed with gaming has evolved that that's not always feasible, particularly because it can bend the versimilitude of the game to have either the player's goals and actions, the NPC's goals and actions, or both twist to convulition to the end goal of getting players to witness and interact with anything that happens. There have been answers for how to handle it, with one very popular answer being to develop a count that increases as the players do certain things to demarcate the advancing of a meaningful plot events to their completion, keeping players as the driver of consequences while still maintaining a sense of time-based consequences and NPC agency being put to use (and I should mention here I am a fan of this model, as it can be seen in things like Blades in the Dark or in Polaris games like The Starlit Kingdom). Still, other options can lean into or embrace the non-causal relationship of NPC's to their actions off screen, sometimes trying to keep an NPC's actions limited to a precocneived notion of what can be done in the time, maybe arbitrarily applying the PC's logic to them, or sometimes allowing for bad things to happen with nothing that can be done about it. There are preferable answers, but how to manage NPC's when they aren't on screen is one of those subtle arguments with a lot of design and guidance conversations going all sort of ways if you read a lot/enough RPG's.

    While this is going to matter more for the subject of Forswearing with Heroes, I did want to bring it up here with Satiety because it's where we start running into the issue of Heroes being up to stuff when the PC's aren't on screen that is meaningful and has impact. Unlike the former mechanic (as it exists now), I don't feel compelled to provide an answer for what to do about Heroes Satiating themselves (or, for that matter, Beasts and Insatiables) even though it's not an insignificant action for them and for the player characters considering the impact it has on the setting, the character's conenctions, and the Hero's strength in direct confrontations, particularly since Heroes are the main disrupter of any given setting in any given game. That's because I feel like it still needs to have some looseness for the ST to implement it as best fits their story, and I don't feel like it will feel as much of a cheat as some other mechanics might when implemented-there's plausible versimilitude in the way Satiety works that allow it to be wibbly. Despite that, though, I do feel like this is one of those things a ST does want to be aware of needing a bit more thought that just wholly embracing the non-existence and non-causal relationship to actions when off screen that you can do with other mechanics. Satiation, in general, is a fairly consequential action, and that shouldn't be lost just because it can also be easier for a Storyteller to just decide their Satiety is at a certain level and they got it by doing some such to someone who you'll never meet. Which leads to a decent segway into talking about Heroic Satiety and Hunger.

    You've Ruined a Perfectly Good Monkey-The Ideas Behind Heroic Hunger and Satiety

    Okay, briefly reiterating from previous conversations-while the main goal of reimagining the setting of Heroes was to make them easier to grok and cement as antagonists, the main goal of mechanics in any reimagining Heroes had to handle the tough tightrope of both making Heroes more meaningful threats without neccesarily making them overtly powerful, placing emphasis on their threat being one of inevitability in line with the game's theme/mood of inevitability and keeping a sense of unfairness in how Heroes sidestep the usual strength-centered arguments*. The decision to treat Heroes as one of the Children of the Dark Mother opened up avenues to use traits common to Beasts and Insatiable as a way to build and explore not of those in a lot of ways without messing with too much with what Heroes already had, which we were very keen to keep in play. Lair worked to help center Beast on the whole as a game about Building Your Lair and did a lot to bring the feeling that the world revolves around Heroes the more and more they were successful, and set some stages for exploring the world of Primordial through their eyes.

    Satiety and Hunger had different challenges, but was also adept at addressing a couple of other issues, first of which was addressing what it was Heroes do. As they exist now, Heroes may individually have things they do and get up to, but collectively tend to come off as all they do is hunt down Beasts and be total pricks to them before killing them, more shallow than they had any right to be. In relation to this, where Beasts worked perfectly well in other games in a variety of roles, doing just as well as antagonists with a Seers pylon or a participant in the feasts of the Eaters of the Dead as they did as allies to Lost Freeholds and Forsaken packs, Heroes' singular focus on Beasts denied them a greater capacity as a crossover entity, and thusly have more dynamic roles in the broader narrative of Chronicles games on the whole which would come back to impact them in Primordial. All of this led to a wildly flailing conception of Heroes in terms of their moral character, from being easy to understand as just a beast-centric Slasher no more worthy of sympathetic thought than Ted Bundy to being wholly in the right as exterminators of the Begotten.

    Satiety and Hunger handily answered those issues. By providing a motivation that could be handled by conflicting with Beasts but could just as easily could be handled confronting humans or other supernaturals, it gives Heroes a ready made reason to have other things to do than bother Beasts, and thus can see them acting in a variety of capacities in pursuit of their lodestar in Hunger. By keeping the advancement of Lair (alongside the attainment of Gifts) locked to the murder of Beasts and a conception of centering the game on the notion of Building Your Lair, Heroes could have this deviation and versatility without losing sight of their grand goal and target. And by making the main vector of difference between the two Families rather than Hunger, Heroes could be kept to a similar actionable standard to Beasts, which not only helps to keep them more grounded and alike, but by having that feature also keep them on the same moral starting line as Beasts and thus have a more coherent understanding by the audience, it allows us to better highlight the shared humanity that drives everyone that is present in Hunger while allowing monstrosity in it's excess and denial to also be all the more blatant, and thus highlighting the role narrative as applied to action matters so much-because why yes, it is okay for a Hero to ruthlessly slaughter people in the name of victory for the common folk and such while it is demonstrably implorable when it's done against such things, end sarcasm tag.

    Mechanically speaking, though, Satiety strained a lot of other concepts to their limit-in particular to a point where I don't think that, as the Forsworn go, I can efficently argue for the self-unawarness of their own Heroic nature, which means yay, I probably have to sit down and figure out what Heroic society looks like without breaking too far away from their presentation so far. The main reason for that is I wanted to root what Satiety did in the idea of "Heroic Effort" alongside Satiety's willpower-like role as a resource for dramatic reversal and increased tension. I also wanted to keep in mind the main way Satiety works for Beasts and Insatiables and what that looks like in a scenario where Lair and Gifts fill in for Nightmares and Atavisms respectively. I also used it to address what I thought was the sorest sticking point of Heroic impotence, hopefully without throwing the problem in the other way.

    Okay, enough theory, let's get into the weeds.

    A Strict Diet of Human Misery, Monstrous Hearts, and Diet Coke-The Basics of Satiety

    This one's going to be short. Functionally, Satiety and Hunger works exactly the same as everyone else, which means yes, Heroes can and do Sate themselves on the shock of their fellow humans, because have I made my point already? Well, mostly-we do allow for it to also be applied to monsters and supernatural entities**, basically anything that isn't on the Kinship Does Not Apply list, because being able to Sate on supernaturals was big part of the benefit of doing this, and allowing Satiation on Beasts is just sensible. But, like, yes, again, Heroes can and do Sate themselves on humanity. You're the hero, you're the conqueror, it's your right and privilege.

    Otherwise, time does deplete it, you can spend Satiety to empower things (we'll talk about it in a bit), Heroes always have a Satiety Condition (we'll talk about in just a bit later), and if a Hero can find their way to their Heart, they can heal like a Beast in there. Notably, though, Heroes can never just wait and hope to Be Satiated after their sleep, because their Horror in near dead and pinned in their Heart, and they can't use it for Primordial Pathways (Not that Satiety has anything to do with establishing a Pathway, I just realize I never said they can't do Primordial Pathways in the last one and figured I should address it here). Going back to that first one, if a Hero wants to do something about that gnawing feeling in their belly, they'll have to go forth and Sate Their Hunger themselves.

    Heroes can activate one Satiety effect per turn reflexively, but do not have a limit on how many Satiety they can spend per turn. You'll see why this is important.

    Okay, I think that's all the general stuff. Let's get into the meat.

    Glory Lies Beyond the Horizon-Spending Satiation

    Heroes don't always strictly know that when they're pushing themselves to the limit, they're spending Satiety, at least at first. For the most part, it initially just feels like they're trying very hard, and then things just happen to work out, like the burst of adrenaline that allows mothers to lift cars off their kids or the world seemingly being kind to someone trying to do something right. Of course, it doesn't take too long, as Heroes begin to recognize correlation and causation, to know that this feeling of power from and over others that they get as they go about their journey can be swallowed down more, somehow, and turns into palpable strength and effect on the world. Such efforts require that they exert power and control over others and the world later on, in a way that feeds into their sense of security and empowered agency, but the world has no shortage of people who at least need some of their ideas about the world conquered and proven counter to the way things are. Of course, time, tide, and exposure to Beasts can do a lot to change the way a Hero understands that...if a Hero doesn't also understand that the devils can quote scripture, too.

    Heroes can spend Satiety in the following ways:
    • Spend 1 Satiety to trigger a Hero's Journey/Wayfinding roll
    • Spend 1 Satiety to impose the Lair Trait of their Heart on the scene. No one in the scene, including the Hero and Beasts who share the Lair Trait, may benefit from immunity to this Trait.
    • Spend 1 Satiety to apply a +3, 8-Again bonus to a roll. Cannot be used in conjunction with a Willpower expenditure.
    • Spend 1 Satiety to use their Gifts as though they were using it against a Beast.
    • Spend 2 Satiety to trigger a Boons of the Goddess/Boons roll, and broadcast the events of the scene as a dream to sleeping individuals within a Hero's Lair range. This ability can be used to justify introducing new Followers in future scenes, or granting a +3 bonus to Social rolls with characters at the Storyteller's discretion to end of bringing peple over to the Hero's side.
    • Spend 3 Satiety to temporarily manifest a new Gift for the scene.
    The Night and the Day Are One, I Can't Wake Up from the Nightmare of My Life-Heroic Satiety Conditions

    The general rules for Satiety Conditions remain, on the whole, the same-one for Satiety 10, one for Satiety 0, and one for 1-3, 4-6, and 7-9 respectively, and resolution remaining the same for each(or their corollary in Beast Satiety Conditions). Since Heroes have different features, though, the exact benefits of those change-but because their relationship to their Horror is also different, the nature of feast and famine also have markedly different effects on them. So, let's go over it.

    Emaciation***- Everyone feels frail and thin when they are well and truly ravenous-but for Heroes at Satiety 0, their hearts skip beats, the flesh bruises and bleeds with ease, and the mind begins to review life with desperate vividness. The Hero's slow death of starvation is compounded by a failing metaphysic, and the Dark Dream threatens to devour them whole. Emaciation behaves the same as Ravenous, except that all damage not caused by the Condition increases by 1, as the Hero's Heart begins to collapse in on itself.

    Starving- On edge and on the prowl at Satiety 1, 2, and 3, the Hero's ego is as sharp as their blade, but their sense of the world is distracted by the need for....validation? security? action? However a Hero labels it, the Hero Hungers, and their instincts drive them to Satiation. The Hero takes -2 to Waymaking/Boon rolls as their connection to the Dreaming weakens, but enemies- and allies-to-be stand out all the more strkly to them, granting a +2 to Heroic Tracking, Stalking, and Forswearing rolls. A Hero desperate for the world to make sense can spend 1 Satiety to temporarily manifest a new Waymaking Trait for the chapter, though it's implementation is subject to the same penalty as their usual Traits.

    Sated-The Hero is comfortably fed at Satiety 4, 5, and 6- vitalized without desperation, rested without unease. The first action a Hero takes after gaining this Condition gets a +1 bonus. The Hero is otherwise unburdened, but not advantaged.

    Gorged- At Satiety 7,8, and 9, a Hero has done-has accomplished much, and can now be at ease with themselves and others. The world makes sense, their place in it secure, and the moment allows for rest...so why, oh why, does this doubt, this anxiety creep in? Under the veneer of success a Hero wears, nightmares sneak their way into the Hero's imagination, an unease that keeps them from resting idly. The Hero gains a +2 to Waymaking/Boon rolls, but suffer the Normal Effect of a Nightmare of the Storyteller's choice(barring those that adjust other Nightmares) for a scene when they fail a roll, and suffers the Shaken Condition whenever they wake up with the Gorged Condition.

    Insomnia***- How-why-how did it all do so wrong? At Satiety 10, a Hero's world comes crashing down as the nightmare they thought they had conquered struggles against it's Anathema. The daylit world of the real and the night terror of the ideal become one, and the Hero has nowhere to run or hide from the truth about themselves anymore. The Hero can no longer fall to sleep, cannot recover Willpower through their Legend, cannot spend Satiety, and continually suffers the Normal Effect of a Nightmare(barring those that adjust other Nightmares) that most closely resembles their deepest fear. The Lair Trait of their Heart is imposed across the whole area of their Lair (to which the Hero has no immunity), their Waymaking Traits are traded out for themes that will torment the Hero (consider using the names of Nightmares as new traits), and Waymaking is triggered every scene and at any interaction with a Deep Dreamer****, Herald, Hero, Beast, and/or Insatiable*****. Finally, sleeping humans who have resonance with the Hero's Horror become sleepwalkers benefitting from Birthrights and Atavism appropriate to the Horror, and chase down the Hero to torment them, though never killing them. Beasts wanting to open a Primordial Pathway to a Hero's Heart can do so without penalties in the Hero's physical presence, and the Heart does not suffer from the Dreamquake Condition while Insomnia*** is in effect.

    To Thirst but Never Slake, To Hunger but Never Sate, To Conquer but Never Keep-Closing Thoughts

    So, yeah, that's Heroic Satiety and Hunger. I wanted to keep their expenditures simple but potent without being too much, and I wanted to make a Hero's Conditions feel like meaningful differences of scene change, while subtly hinting towards the liveliness (or deathliness) of the Horror beneath....Up to the last one, which I admit I wanted to be bombastic, and gratuitously cruel enough to ideally tug at even the most jaded Beast's heart (and for any left over, there is the risk to the sleepwalking psuedo-Beasts and the fact that the Toxic Lair Traits may be satisfying to see a Hero suffer under, but unfortunately everyone else also lives here too). I think they hit the right spot, but we'll see what y'all think

    *which makes it funny how the psychology and it's outcomes tends to lead to prizing strength-centered worldviews.
    **Mind you, I would also have Beasts be able to feed on other supernaturals if I had my way, though probably subject to something akin to the 2 Ranks Higher equals Agg Damage rule found with Ephemeral Entities rules to keep Beasts mostly interested in humanity on this subject.
    ***Originally these were named Ravenous and Waking Nightmare, but I wanted to try these names at the last minute. I'm skeptical about them, though, because I'm mostly sure I don't want to use real medical diagnoses as supernatural conditions. Still figured I might wanna walk them by y'all first.
    ****Yes, not an actual template, probably won't appear in the STV, but it's still a thing I want to flesh out and codify.
    *****Last minute thought, maybe also anyone who's suffered a breaking roll in the scene.
