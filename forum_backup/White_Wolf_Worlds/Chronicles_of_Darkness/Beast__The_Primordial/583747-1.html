                                                I'm not really sold either way on this yet, but I'm finding a lot of the comments about BDSM... kind of condescending missing the point of BDSM.

                                                BDSM is consensual, and as safe as the people involved make it, but it isn't fake. It isn't acting or pretending. If one could distill BDSM to a single "point," it would be to create genuine emotions/sensations through controlled activities. In "real" BDSM, if the sub wants to feel powerless, the goal of the dominant is to make them feel powerless. Safewords are just a safety measure because that's not an inherently simple or safe (either physically or emotionally) activity. They might not actually be powerless, but they want to feel that way.

                                                To me, comparing BDSM to acting is like saying that getting punched in boxing isn't "really" getting punched because it's a controlled sport, not a back-alley brawl that is going to end in someone going to the hospital.

                                                Why I'm still waffling a bit, is I think for something like the Hunger for Power, there's a question of what's more important: the reality of the situation, or the reality of the emotions of people involved.

                                                Does it matter that the other person genuinely felt powerless even if they weren't, or if you make the target powerless whether they really emotionally feel a complete lack of control?

                                                A lot of the arguments for seem to be assuming that the feeling of the Tyrant's subject is most important, while a lot of the arguments against seem to be more rooted in the total reality of the situation.
