Similarly they lack any of the tricks that Mages and Changelings have that also amount to 'don't eat me dude, I can make you sorry for it.' Beasts are exactly like Remora. And while Remora have a relatively symbiotic relationship with sharks, they're still little fish and sharks do occasionally eat them.

            So yeah, Beasts are a bit under powered. They could use an ability sorta like a Demon's spoofing ability that let's them either remain under the radar or makes them look like too much trouble to eat. Alternately they could use some kind of metaphysical bargain ability that formalizes the Kinship relationship and places the burden of protecting them from other sharks on the shark they share kinship with. Said shark would need an additional incentive.

                True, though I don't believe Alphas are a inherent thing anymore, but that doesn't need mean that they're at the bottom of the rung*, and even then, I think most Packs would be smart enough to not treat the bottom rung that bad.

                *Especially since one with the right Atavisms and Satiety to spend might have well been packing silver, and one at high Satiety could serve as a useful negotiator.

                I apologize if you find this insulting. That is not my intention. We fundamentally disagree on Vampires and likely on CofD games in general.
                Points 1 and 2 are just silly. Vampires are monsters. They might be protagonists but in no world are they heroes. Morality isn't a thing in CofD games and asking them not to use Blood Bonds is effectively asking them to walk around with one arm tied around their back. Never forget, Vampires are blood-sucking predators that hunt humans for food.

                Despite appearances to the contrary, Humanity 4~6 is the best level to maintain.

                Regarding point 3, I'm not sure I follow. The entire Lashing Out section provides rules for Vampires to assert dominance over one another without resorting to physical violence.

                Point 4, is also silly. Ghouls go through life Blood Bonded. Many Childer are also likely to. Blood addiction is a core theme to the game afterall.

                Regarding packs and treatment, think of the Darwinian nature of the game, take a look at actual wolves in nature, look again at the inspirational material for W:tF, and especially, watch Sons of Anarchy. How the players play Werewolves is one thing but generally, Werewolf culture is a rough and tumble one of layered fealties. And never forget, a Werewolf is a massive killing machine that lives for the hunt. Hunts are violent things. So again, you might be the protagonist but no hero. These are games of personal horror after all and there's a good reason why the Kuruth rules are so fearsome. Killing packmates on accident is a thing. And humans are hardly off the menu.

                Given that Beasts can hook their Lairs to one another as a means of combat, it's quite likely that Beasts enjoy a rough and tumble system of almost Darwinian combat to select who's the biggest monster in the Hive. This might change night after night, except that the strength of Beasts waxes and wanes with their Hunger and a new Beast is not that much less powerful than an ancient one. To me this is a good indicator that Beasts gang up on one another -- no lone Beast could be the top dog in the Hive for long as the Lair combat rules makes it easy for groups of Beasts to gang up on individual Beasts. You might object to this on some kind of morality grounds but I think their name says it all--Beast.

                My conclusion is that in a massive cross-over ecosystem the vast majority of Beasts participate in their own internal culture which is one of internecine "gang" warfare as the various "gangs" smash their group Lairs into one another. A small handful of "outsider" Beasts hang with other supernaturals. Those who hang with Vampires, Werewolves, Mages, or Changelings must accept that they have a precarious status in those social groups and might easily find themselves used as pawns in the machinations of the "movers and shakers" and so their status is little better than pets because they must rely on their Kin to protect them from their Kin's Kin. Those hanging out with Demons, Hunters, and Sin-eaters (or Revenants as I call them) might actually enjoy some amount of power over their "Kin" as they bring valuable abilities to the table. Those hanging out with loner splats like Prometheans (or Monsters as I call them), Mummies, or Deviants are the ones most likely to enjoy the companionship of actual equals. YMMV but the rulebook provides the tools that it provides, all else is left for us to hack or ignore. And in no universe are Beasts over-powered (which is the topic here after all).

                                        Originally posted by Joker View Post
                                        I don't have time to answer the whole post, sorry if I seem to be cherry picking. But i strongly disagree with this, You Must Obey easily being among the worst Dominate-alike out of any splat.
                                        It requires heavy babysitting of those you dominate, only lasts a scene, is limited on the number of orders you can give and orders can be ignored with a breaking point roll..
                                        That's okay. I understand what you're saying; I guess I should have qualified "best of" more directly. For me it's simply the ability to no-fuss-no-muss get off a single action and then be able to issue multiple stacked complex commands. Since you get so MANY if you're above the Satiety threshold, I'd also disagree with the babysitting in that anything I need to get done I probably have room to describe getting done. I'm also not certain that complex orders are capped at your Satiety or if that's just the QUEUE of complex orders, but either way, I think the occasions where you need more commands in a single scene but can't stay near the victim (even with, say, your stealth kit) are gonna be few and far between.

                                        They CAN take Breaking Points to ignore your commands, but that's probably something they'll reserve for actions that are normally Breaking Points, which will stop Dominate 1 and CAN stop Dominate 3, and unlike Dominate they don't do so automatically once they start to bleed for the cause. This is true even if you're using the low Satiety version and that actually is significant because it opens up some uses Dominate CAN'T do, like effective forced conscription - if you can get them into a firefight at all, and they survive, they'll continue following your commands normally. With Dominate, if they get shot you lose control, so even if the command worked they're now as like to bolt or surrender as to keep going.

                                        Also and somewhat horrifyingly, nothing stops a Beast who isn't really pressed for time from just slamming a victim with Breaking Points over and over until they lose their previous inhibition towards whatever you asked them to do. Any attempt to ignore or countermand your orders triggers a Breaking Point - but doesn't end the power.

                                        Scene-length duration IS a downside, but I personally just don't rate that highly enough to take too much away from the thing you're being enabled to do. A Ventrue gets more duration, but they have about the same raw efficacy, and the Beast still has room for more stuff because that's just one Nightmare. But this is all my perspective, based on the games I play in and the kind of use-cases that come up: any NPC who doesn't a name and a place in the setting already I probably don't NEED for more than a scene, for instance, has been my experience. Security guards, cops, people in hospitals, people whose house I need to be inside, people I need to interrogate, et cetera. Generally once-and-done folk. Making a major NPC my long-term puppet is where Majesty shines, but not quite at Majesty 3 IMHO.

                                        EDIT: Also, the Satiety Expenditure version can be used to refill Willpower or provide an Exceptional Success buff that you can make power-relevant by knowing what command you need to give. That's actually something I'm uncomfortable with in terms of power. Actually I'm a lot uncomfortable with it, there is zero sanity check on that usage aside from Rule Zero'ing the absolute fuck out of it. That's literally a potential 1 Willpower/action drip, right? They choose the benefit before rolling so if you know you're starting a fight, have another let you tag them, issue command (careful with your language of course) and now they're in overdrive beyond I think what is reasonable for a single Nightmare. Why?! Now I'm upset.

                                        On the offchance that anyone's watching, by the way, it would be nice to have official clarification: I am pretty certain that we're all right that Breaking Points are a trade against at least the specific command you've been given, but it doesn't actually SAY they don't still follow the command - it references attempts only, although I expect that's the intention otherwise why mention it at all. Something something natural language, I know.

                                                A reasonable assessment of the drawbacks on You Must Obey, but you forgot about Conditions.
