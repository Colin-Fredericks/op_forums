    Alternate themes and motivations

    09-15-2016, 12:44 AM
    I was wondering, what are some themes/motivations/justifications you've come up with and used, other than the "teaching lessons" theme? Because I think it can be done well, but I understand that a lot of people don't like it, and there's a lot more potential than just being J. Walter Slenderman. Has anyone played with the themes from Leviathan? Monstrous inheritance, stagnation, being hunted, wondering if the world would be better without you? A lot of people's reaction to the game is "people don't need lessons from monsters", and I think a lot of Beasts would believe that too. How do they cope with it? What new paths and callings do they find?

    I'm hoping this gets some coverage in the Player's Guide.

      "Teaching lessons" is just a justification for Beasts to feel better about their Feeding. It's just easier to do bad things to people if you feel justified in that they deserve having those bad things happen to them. That they deserve to "learn a lesson." I can see some Beasts doing that to feel better about themselves, but I don't think all Beasts would particularly care or see themselves as having a responsibility. There has to be other philosophies out there.

      So I would say to think about what kind of justifications Beasts might conceive of as to why they should feel better of themselves for Feeding.

                                      Most of the ways Beasts feed are essentially normal activities. The first version of the text - less so the one we eventually got, but it can still work that way - struck me as oddly suited, given their outre nature, to having protagonists live relatively normal lives and engage with the world much the way mortals do. So that would be one of my alternatives. They still consider their human life to be their real one, and continue to live it as much as they can. It doesn't stop them digging into the supernatural around them, but mostly for the purposes of protecting their lives, loved ones, and neighbourhoods (and feeding opportunities). The theme would be an exploration of the currents of power, abuse, and evil that we treat as socially normal and kind of don't notice.

                                      Another would be mediators between supernatural factions - take the family thing to its full extent. Their job is to gather the various supernatural factions into one community for the good of all.

                                      Another I had, I called the Raveners, who took their Hunger as "categorical imperative" to the full. Feeding their Horrors is right, and they do it without shame or guilt - not necessarily without restraint or conscience, as it needn't the only thing they take as right (being a monster means you get to choose how you act, not that you have to act monstrously at all times). Since we've had a thread about mortal cultists of the Dark Mother, I would probably add an element of encouraging humans to follow a similar course.
