                    I think it's a valid interpretation, actually.

                    Ultimately, the concept of Lessons is the Beast trying to justify their feeding. It's the stance that these people deserve to have the Beast happen to them (I really struggled for a neutral word there, but... There isn't really, so victimized, abused, harassed, threatened, hurt, etc etc, pick a word for you). But the object of the "Lesson" doesn't even need to be legitimate. It could be the Beast deciding to punish someone because they didn't wear green on St. Paddy's day or that they walked and chewed bubblegum at the same time.

                    I think that aspect of Lessons needs to be examined by the books. It highlights that, no Beasts really are awful and monsters. I think it's important, because even vampires at their best are predatory, even werewolves at their best are savage, even mages at their best are obsessive.

                    This isn't to say Beasts can't have redeeming qualities, but that even at their best, Beasts are still monsters.

                    It is vital that when holding up Lessons as justification, the books need to examine how that justification is also bullshit.

                    So Lessons is victim-blaming has to be valid.
