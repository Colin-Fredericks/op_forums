    The Arrival, Vacancy and Entrenchment- overlooked Inheritances (Homebrew)

    08-14-2022, 09:30 AM
    The Merger forces the spiritual half of the Beast, the Horror, to combine with its physical half, erasing the human part of the Beast.
    The Retreat forsakes the Begotten in favour of an unfettered Horror.
    The Erasure forsakes the Horror in favour of their human part, while the Inversion essentially seeks to eliminate all Horrors.
    The Divergence seperates the two to a certain degree into two seperate beings, whereas the Incarnate then talks about how a Beast can take control of their life and become something more by fusing their physical and spiritual halves.

    All of the above have one thing in common: they don't really talk about the Lair.
    The Lair is a Beast's home, their own personal corner of the Primordial Dream, a place where they are both at their most powerful and their most vulnerable, a places that passes as their soul. In the very first description of the Lair in the core, it was stressed how this primordial landscape is just as vital as the Horror.

    So, my brain thought that there must be some sort of Inheritance that focuses on this much neglected third half of the Begotten existence.

    How could this come to pass, and why would a Begotten chose this path over another? What follows are my bumbling thoughts on the matter.

    The Arrival
    The Arrival appears to be a normal Begotten at first glance: they look the same, they still feel like kin and act like they did. They still Hunger, and Heroes are still a problem with their Anathema.
    However, the second they enter their home terrain, things change: Anathema do not apply while they are at the physical location of a chamber, nor can they be placed on them here. They can freely call upon their Lair Traits while there, and even inflict Nightmares in physical chambers as if present, albeit through focusing. At the physical locations of their chambers, Low Satiety Atavisms and High Satiety Nightmares always apply.
    In addition, even when they are not present, an Arrival may overlap the Primordial Dream Chamber with it's physical location.
    Finally, each instance of a scary, horrifying event inside their Chambers counts as a Family Dinner, as long as the Arrival observes them.

    Other advantages of an Arrival:
    The Arrival can perceive what happens in their Chambers and Heart by focusing on it. Nightmares can by cast this way, too.
    Arrivals have up to four times the amount of Chambers they usually would, as well as an additional three Lair Traits they gain immediately with this Inheritance. Most strikingly, they have no limit to the lair traits per scene, and gain +[Lair/2] rounded up on rolls to inflict them. This Bonus also applies to using the Skeleton Key and opening a Pathway to the Heart.
    Burrows are unlimited and maybe freely rearranged as an Instant Action.

    Chambers actively fight back against being collapsed by intruders, increasing the necessary amount of successes to 5 * Lair for a Chamber and 7 * Lair for the Heart. Each Roll takes three times the Beast's Lair Rating in minutes, and the Chambers rolls the Beast's Lair Rating as a dicepool to cause Bashing Damage every roll (defense or athletics may or may not apply, depending on the chamber and active lair traits).


    The process of Arriving:
    The soon-to-be Arrival cares deeply for their Heart and Lair, and spends a lot of time personally tending to it, and the Lair returns the favour. Rather than increasing their own power directly, they infuse a large part of power into the Lair. The methods for this might vary, but at least one case suceeded by Feeding to the point of reaching Slumber, and then sacrificing most of their health inside the Lair before forcing their Horror to do the same, bleeding out Satiety to "water" the Lair in their own sweat and blood repeatedly. Another way would be to attract Heroes, lure them into their Lair and kill one in each Chamber through heavy use of the environment. Or the Begotten lures Prey into the Lair to Feed, but spends WP to deny the Horror the food, instead letting the terror of their victims seep into their Lair.
    If they do enough of this in short enough a time span, their Lair Blooms, and they may attempt to finally Arrive Home with an Extended Occult + Lair roll requiring 20 successes, each roll requiring 5 days. During this time, they explore the link between their Horror and themselves
    Dramatic Failure:     In addition to the effects of a failure, the character suffers –2 dice to his next roll.
    Failure: Neglecting the Horror and denying them their food causes it to take off in search for food. The Beast loses a Satiety to lure it back and satisfy its demands or has to abandon the Arrival, causing significant and potentially irreperable damage to his Lair.
    Success: Successes are accumulated towards unlocking the secrets in crafting his Anathema.
    Exceptional Success: The character either reduces total successes by his Occult, reduces the time per roll to 2 days, or gains the Inspired Condition




    The Vacancy
    Where the Retreat and Merger focus on the Horror at the cost of the human, the Vacancy sacrifices everything in favour of the Heart. Maybe traveling to the Mother's Land left a deep impression, maybe they love being in the Primordial Dream, maybe they simply hated their Horror: Begotten reach out for this Inheritance usually due to feeling more connection to their Lair than to their Horror, maybe due to feeling they have more of a hand in shaping it. Similar to how people might have come to hate their spouse, but still feel nostalgic about the home they built together, a Begotten who is conflicted about their existance might find solace in their Heart, and even when they decide to end their life, want their home to be taken care of.
    Unlike the Arrival, the Vacancy leaves an almost empty house, which needs to lure in unsuspecting victims it attempts to torment or kill to earn enough fear to pay their 'primordial upkeep'.

    (Mechanics and stuff is to follow)

    The Void is probably one of the most unusual Inheritances, as the resulting genius loci/ haunted place is more a fixture of the landscape or a mystery than a character.

    The Entrenchment
    If the Arrival is the Begotten taking their Lair as their rightful home and taking control over it as an expression of feeling only able to truly express oneself inside one's own home, and the Vacancy is leaving behind a thing once loved out of sentimentality, the Entrenchment is a desire to show everyone this one thing you have, even if it kills you.
    Essentially, the Begotten brings the Lair, and by extension the Primordial Dream, into the physical world.

    [will follow in seperate post]



    All of the above is very much still a work in progress.

    Constructive criticism very welcome!



    Edit: What I am mostly unhappy with right now is the "appears normal most of the time" thing with Arrivals. I also feel like the extent to which they have to go for this Inheritance (essentially someone living through their house, making decorating and designing their living place their hobby/reason to live) needs to be reflected in a change at the core of their being, but wasn't entirely sure how to apply this. Losing Atavisms and Nightmares when outside their "house"? Would make increasing it harder though, which runs somewhat against the theme. Chaining their Horror to the Lair, essentially taking out one of the Ravenous drawbacks, at the price of increased periodical Satiety loss? Replacing Life/Legend?

      I think your biggest problem is that this isn't really enough meat for three meals. The Lair itself is "neglected" only in the sense of its physicality. There's a reason why Lair is a Beast's power stat. Interacting with the spiritual aspects of a Lair is actually pretty important to a bunch of the Inheritances; just not directly. A Begotten can't understand their Horror without understanding their Lair.

      Arrival feels too much like just a bunch of awesome bonuses with no actual sacrifice or fundamental alteration to the Begotten's nature to call an Inheritance. What Beast wouldn't want to get all this, and what stops them from pursuing other ways of being? This simply lacks a level of finality to the story of an attempt to become a thing of legend (successfully so or not).

      Vacancy and Entrenchment are more resonant with the themes of the game, but really could just be one Inheritance with two examples of how to get there. In both cases the conclusion of the Begotten's story is to be subsumed into the legend of their Lair instead of themselves, and this results in a place rather than a ravening monster lurking in the shadows of the world and seeking prey in its own strange and unique ways. Inheritances should be broad enough to encompass multiple paths to get to them. If one deals with the power of the Beast resulting in the human side and Horror side becoming diminished in favor of the Lair taking on a legend of its own, there's no need to try to bend into knots making it into more than one Inheritance.

                      Well, being a little more constructive than glib, it probably pays to get more into that.

                      The first set of directional mechanics for the suicides called Inheritances that should be paid attention to is that the two sets follow along the lines of Beast's two main anchors, Legend and Life, with the corebook seeing the Beast's human half in Life die to exalt their Legend, and visa versa with the Life Inheritances in the BPG. There's the obvious mentality behind that about the thematics and ideas behind Beast, but one thing that should be noted is that those are both rooted in something mechanical about Beasts as characters, and that the precedence for Inheritances involves understanding the elements of Beasts as things that have equals-and-opposites, where in with an Inheritance murderkills one aspect of being to exalt another.

                      Now with that on the table, we should briefly mention that I don't think we could apply that to either Lair or Satiety, particularly with each other. Lair is the universal that empowers and motivates all of the others-arguably even the Life Inheritances from the BPG. Satiety is also pretty core to all of the Inheritances-you can see the short term hunger versus long term gains across the board.

                      With the experiment here (and every other one that preceded this one, I guess "genius loci" is just too tempting a conception), I feel like the primary way to understand is to look at Lair through it's "physical" components, that the dichotomy is Horror vs Chambers, with the goal here being to construct Chamber Inheritances that subsume the Horror. It pays to think about what that means, to destroy the ego in favor of becoming a place of power(arguably, we'll come back around to that).

                      One thing that immediately becomes of note is that you could reasonably make the argument that both the Legend and Life Inheritances we have are all Horror Inheritances, so on initial observation you could make the argument that there's room for Legend and Life Chamber Inheritances as well-except I think we can also agree that there's something incredibly at odds of a Life Inheritance also somehow abandoning the ego to be a place of power. Life Chamber Inheritances sounds and feels incredibly weird, is what I'm saying (though Kudos to anyone who figures that out). Still, in building these Chamber Inheritances, it can be helpful to think of them as Legend Chamber Inheritances, even if the Legend part is strongly implied to be silent.

                      Finally, the directional mechanics of Legend Inheritances tends towards the extremes of the real, the ideal, and their synthesis(or the waking, the dreaming, and their synthesis, if you'd prefer)-that is, all that a Beast is collapses into either a physical form, an Astral form, or is brought together towards a pinnacle.* In similar fashion, it probably pays to conceive of Chamber Inheritances as all the Beast being poured into the physical, the Astral, or in synthesis.

                      Two small notes include a) resist the urge to conceive of these Inheritances as anything that might be constructed with the Ephemeral Entities or the Horror Creation rules till the last minute-even if the idea is that there is still a sentience you can hang on those rule sets for these Inheritances, you're probably gonna land closer to intention if you construct it away from those at first, and b) it probably pays to think of how they warp and infect the people who deal with them rather than thinking of how they inflict and impose themselves on them (as uselessly vague as that is). Also, while they tend to be more powerful than what you'd probably want for this, looking at Chris Allen's Pangaeans is probably useful correlation, particularly the Really Highly Powerful ones, since those characters are more like setting pieces than individual antagonists.

                      Final contribution, If I were going to try my hand at this idea, I'd probably land on something like "The Haunting", where in a Beast collapses everything into a singular Chamber's physical location, "The Nightmare", where in a Beast more tightly winds their Astral Chambers together and severs the Burrows to reality as much as possible, creating a shared nightmare people sleep their way into**, and "The Labyrinth", which takes the way chambers work on both sides up to 11 sans their minotaur, whatever that means.

                      EDIT: As a note on my own conceptions, I can see arguments for why a Beast would claim "The Haunting" and "The Nightmare", but what I understand of my own "Labyrinth" concept? "I didn't design them to do that. Who would, really?"

                      *Life Inheritances don't do this, of course, but they're openly intended to contrast the Legend Inheritances, so some of the rules are changed.
                      **This is where "place of power" is arguable, because the Astral is a place-not-place.
