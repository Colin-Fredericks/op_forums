                Originally posted by Magnum Opus View Post

                ...then just play it as such? Since it's not stated as one way or another, and players are generally not considered to be affected by Social Influence rules, it could go one way or another. Your Mastigos has a mind shield that detects Thicker Than Water? Fine. Your Mastigos feels a faint mind influencing (not manipulation) coming his way and decides to be on his guards and suddently feel the power dissipate (because he broke the first impression). That's all.
                Well if one works under the assumption that thicker than water is active during social maneuvering, then for the hypothetical mastigos to detect anything then the following things couldn't apply-

                1) the ST would have to determine that there are no circumstances to warrant an at least average impression and that the beast made no effort to make a good first impression, which anyone can do without so much as a roll.

                2) The beast didn't make a roll to get a better than good impression.

                3) The beast didn't rely on roleplaying instead of social maneuvering.

                4) The beast just skips to forcing doors to get automatic results

                Since thicker than water, when it comes to mostly human types like mages, has literally less results than telling the ST that your character "is wearing his good shirt," I suspect that none of those conditions applying is somewhat unlikely.

                After all, under the rules, without any of those conditions, it could take a beast a month to convince a mage to babysit the beast's pet goldfish, if the mage was willing at all.

                            Originally posted by CatDoom View Post
                            For instance, you get the impression, from reading the book, that Beasts are very much defined by their own particular hunger/obsession, and therefore it doesn't make sense to you that they can be sated by taking part in other supernaturals sating their own hunger. Others may have a different perspective; for instance, that all beasts essentially serve the same Hunger, but have adapted to it in different ways. This allows for the possibility that Beasts can share certain feeding methods in common, such as being present for another supernatural being's feeding. Expressing that perspective and asking that you consider it isn't defensiveness, it's discussion.
                            Not sure what my impression from the draft text has to do with whether or not folks are being defensive and I agree some points are discussion but there's also alot of snark in this thread too.

                            You have an interesting read of the text BTW but I'm not following where Beast's actually share feeding methods. They're more like remora, along for the ride and nibbling up the leftovers. The question of why other supernaturals would tolerate their presence stands. Right now it's just a matter of troupe preference and the built-in deus ex machina of "crossover" supported by some system hacks. That's fine and all but I'd like more than that. I don't see where my desire for more hurts the folks who are already satisfied, unless people are worried that it might mean revisions on par with the whole Soul+Homecoming / Horror+Devouring situation, in which case the discussion points against changes are, indeed, defenses of the status quo.

                            Originally posted by CatDoom View Post
                            Incidentally, one perspective that I'm having a hard time understanding is your view of Werewolf as a super crossover-happy game. Sure, there are plot hooks and comments on how some werewolf powers might interact with other supernaturals, but they're just little bits and pieces scattered through the book, and I don't really think there's anything at all that Werewolves "need" from other supernaturals. Plus, it's really no different from Vampire in this respect; Blood and Smoke has all the same sidebars and plot hooks for how kindred may interact with werewolves or whatnot.
                            I'd encourage you to reread all of the sections pertinent to how Werewolf packs are built because I'm not talking about power compatibility, I'm looking at the ease with which I can include other supernaturals as members of the pack (examples using Vamps actually appear in the text). That's a 180 from the position of W:tF 1st ed where pack was one of the biggest barriers to crossover games (because one couldn't select anything but Werewolves as pack members). That type of option gives me as the storyteller and the players as a troupe a wealth of options for the kinds of crossover stories we want to tell. Right now the Family section lacks anything remotely comparable. I get the Beast's mythos view of other supernaturals but there's nothing there that actually makes narrative sense for the other supernaturals to have anything less than a hostile attitude towards them.

                            Recall that vamps and werewolves especially have their own internal beasts that are constantly striving for dominance. I just don't see where either are going to suffer a Beast's existence which makes crossover a hard sell. Similarly Demons are going to worry about something that can chew on their Cover, etc., etc. Now YMMV but I'd like to see the Family aspect of the game (and thereby its crossover bits) beefed up considerably.

                                I fail to see how T.T.W. is that much different than the Pusher merit. You're just that good at relating to people and can make good first impressions.
                                And I'm awake. Innate doesn't mean it isn't supernatural. A werewolfs shapeshifting is innate. A mages attainments are innate. Changeling pledges are innate. All of these things, while innate to the creature that has them, aren't any less supernatural than Gifts and Disciplines and the like. In fact, since you can't not start with at least a few dots in some of these even those are technically innate to some degree. So again, innate doesn't mean it isn't a supernatural ability.

                                And as far as how it differs from something like Pusher, well that's easy. It's the source, the origin of the trait is what makes it different. It's the difference between being strong and using whatever form of magic your splat has access to to make yourself strong. In the end, strength dots are strength dots, regardless of where you get them, but if you have the perceptive ability to do so you can still tell the difference. It's the difference between being beautiful and shapeshifting yourself into a more pleasing form. You gain the effects of the Striking Looks merit either way, but anyone who can tell has new information that they can work off of to whatever ends they please.

                                And that's the key here. While the effect isn't much different from a mundane effect, the source is just as important. And in this case, the source is an innate supernatural power stemming from your ties to the Dark Mother and/or your own monstrous lineage. And to those who possess the means, these things are, and should be, noticeable.

                                    Originally posted by YeOfLittleFaith View Post
                                    Of course this won't be the case with every supernatural. It most certainly won't be the case even just among every single Beast. Mummies that care for their individual followers wouldn't tolerate other things taking from their cultists either. Supernaturals come into conflict with different splats, or even among those of the same kind, for the same variety of reasons they may come into conflict with Beasts. When it comes down to the nitty gritty, it depends a lot on individual expectations and feelings. But hence choosing your family.
                                    Vampires and Werewolves don't just argue internally, they compete, they try to establish dominance, they murder one another. How a Beast doesn't end up killed or someone's bitch I don't know but, they definitely can't keep their hands out of other peoples' cookie jars because of their Hunger. It's going to be a social problem when outsiders are chewing on your food, your family, your Cover, your minions, etc. It requires a lot of buy-in from players; ironically we generally hate-on books and movies that rely on the heavy handed tactic of "because it's supposed to".

                                    The draft (Beast is no where near being a book yet -- has to go to printers, etc. first) does a good job representing certain themes (not paradigms -- a paradigm is more "how do we X", think along epistomological lines, e.g., quantitative science is a paradigm, calculus a method, etc.) but if it's going to go with Family as its crossover aid then it could definitely afford to represent additional themes that are important to the concept of Family, especially those that lend themselves to forcing players to face dilemmas and seed conflicts. In the end you make my point for me -- "Demon is more polished mechanics wise than Mummy or Beast right now" -- exactly! I'm arguing that they can polish Beast more before publishing it. In no way does this damage the existing things that you like about it.
