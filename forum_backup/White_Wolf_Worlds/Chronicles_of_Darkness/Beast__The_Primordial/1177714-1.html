    Originally posted by espritdecalmar View Post
    Very cool stuff. Do you think that our modern relationship with monsters would have an affect on Beasts or the Primordial Dream? In the past few decades, monster media has tended toward portraying their subjects in a more sympathetic light, and we can't forget the rise of the supernatural romance genre. Perhaps there are people who end up romanticizing Beasts, or people who want to become one, copycat terrorists imitating you because there's something about you that's really and dark and dangerous but also alluring.

    I've posted about this a while back in the Character Idea Thread, but I'm also interested in the point when a modern monster makes it into the Dream; some of the original, iconic creatures from Dungeons & Dragons, for instance, have definitely inspired nightmares.
    I think that's a factor to keep in mind for sure. Overall, I believe the Primordial Dream is one of the deeper parts of the Astral, so the influence of modern cultural currents manifest in rather abstract way when it comes to it. It probably takes time for something to delve that deep into the collective subconscious, but traces of it might be there. After all, with the Astral being a mirror of our mind, if a relationship with a cultural icon changes the Astral changes with it. Outside of that, it absolutely colors the interactions Beast have with people. Not a widespread sense, perhaps, but easily more than in the past.

    Modern monster becoming "a thing" when it comes to Horrors is definitely interesting. Obviously it won't be a direct translation, but I agree that's a possibility to keep in mind. I think the best bet would to work with the archetypes and fears those monsters embody and process through them as a combination of ancient and modern.

    I spent some times thinking about it in relation to Slasher movies, with the idea that "masked killer" Horrors are more common today after slashers became horror icons. It's a kind of chicken and egg situation, with modern fears fueling new nightmares and those nightmares giving birth to the very same kind of monsters that embody what people where afraid of in the first place.

                And Part 3 is done and posted. With that, the essay is finished. As a bonus, here's a Merit I wrote for my Dark Era that's relevant to the topic. Thanks for the kind words and the feedback, everyone. I'm glad this one sparked a bunch of discussions.

                Originally posted by Dark Era: The Forest That Weeps View Post
                New Merit: Recurrent Soul (oooo)

                Prerequisite: Beast

                Effects:Some monsters seem to survive through the ages as their legends resurfaces over and over. The character's Horror appears to be one of these creatures, a notorious nightmare that managed to not drown in the depths of the Primordial Dream. When the character merges with such Horror, it is not only remade into a new entity but gains access to the memories of those who accepted that Horror within their soul in the past. Many wonder if this is to be considered a form of reincarnation, a legacy of some sort or a dangerous corruption, but what it means on the practical side is that the character gains access to the memories of his human predecessors and is able to use their experience and abilities. The characters taps into the memories buried within the Primordial Dream through his Horror and his body and mind change accordingly. A Begotten has to be careful doing so, since the ordeal awakens memories that might refuse to leave.

                Once per scene, the character might spend 1 Willpower and roll Intelligence + Composure. Add his Lair rating to the successes rolled and choose a skill. The Beast gains a number of bonus dice equal to the result to all rolls that involve that skill for the remainder of the scene, as the memories of his Begotten ancestors guide him. These dice do not negate the penalties for an untrained Skill, but can be used to counter them.

                Whether the initial roll is successful or not, the Beast must roll Resolve + Composure, with a cumulative -1 penalty equal to the times she used this Merit during the session (excluding the first one). On a failure, she gains a negative mental Condition, representing the conflicting personalities that are struggling within her mind and soul. Conditions like Agoraphobic, Confused, Delusional, Distracted, Frightened and Paranoid are appropriate examples.

                            Ah, here's the essay I was the most excited for.

                            It's as you said: the most interesting thing about Beast to me is about Beasts and Heroes being trapped in the narrative cycle that has generated humanity's stories from the beginning, and in exploring the two sides either accepting it as who they are now or trying to get out of it and telling new stories. Am a little bit frustrated that the corebook never expressed this idea clearly enough (or at least clearly enough so Beast wouldn't have gotten the polarizing reception that it did). Putting a lot of faith in the upcoming PG and STG to dive more into this. Otherwise, good stuff.

                            Originally posted by Cinder View Post

                            Gonna borrow a couple of old Changeling terms to drive the point home.
                            Yeah funny thing about that:

                            Beast: the Primordial seemed to be somewhat of a successor to Changeling: the Dreaming. In a way that Changeling: the Lost isn't.
                            • Both the Begotten and Kithain have supernatural souls (one being monstrous and the other fae) inhabiting a mortal body.
                            • Said souls being based on creatures from myth and legends that have dominated the imaginations of humanity since the beginning of time.
                            • The way they became mortal is either of the supernatural soul simply being born in a mortal body and awakening at a later time in life (Homecoming/Changeling Way) or that the Horror finds a suitable mortal and "replaces" the original soul with their own (Devouring/Arcadian Way).
                            • Both's main other-realm is the universal collection of humanity's dreams and/or nightmares.
                            • Their fuel source being a specific emotion from humanity (fear/wonder).
                            • Both consider the other supernatural splats to be family, the other supernaturals simply "forgot" their true familial heritage.


                            It's just that instead of being the creatures of dreams and wonder, the Begotten and their Horrors are entities of nightmare and fears. Which in CtD terms, would make them the Thallain.

                            Bit fitting, considering BtP and Changeling20 had the same line developer.

                            Originally posted by Cinder View Post
                            Part 3: Heritage

                            A lot of the questions are related to Horrors. We know that Horrors are a portion of a Beast’s soul. Fair enough, but what about a Horror that had already merged with a Begotten and resurfaces from the depths of the Primordial Dream centuries later?
                            This does actually seem to be a thing in the official materials.

                            One thing that caught my attention in VtR's "Thousand Years of Nights", that nobody seemed to be talking about (at least around here), was this bit in the Beast crossover write-up:
                            Thousand Years of Nights, p. 133

                            Beasts themselves have human lifespans. However, the Horror the Beast binds with lives on long after their host’s death. It may take years, but the Horror eventually returns wearing the face of a new Begotten. And Horrors don’t forget. An elder who refuses to learn her lesson, or who has slipped back into old habits since last they met, may find herself experiencing a recurring nightmare.
                            This was endlessly fascinating to me since the corebook never did make it clear on where the Horrors that create new Begotten come from. Or even suggest that Begotten that have achieved Inheritance could potentially create new Begotten later on down the line.

                            Opens up a helluva lot of plot hooks.
