          So, as a baseline: what exactly is a dream in Chronicles? Well according to my understanding, it's a lot like what it is in real life. A hallucination where your brain processes all the stuff that happens to you through symbol and metaphor. Here's the thing, in Chronicles, that's connected to your soul, and through your soul, all souls. So, the usual metaphor is the branches of a Great Tree. dreams are leaves on a branch, spouting, budding, blooming, wilting, and fading away. The Branch itself is a person, usually a soul. That soul eventually connects to other souls to form a Trunk, which then trails down into the Soil and Earth, connecting a human soul to all other human souls, and eventually the souls of everything else. This is all called the Dreaming by Beasts and the Astral by Mages.

          The Hedge is where that metaphor breaks down because dreams are made semi-physical on the Dreaming Roads as Bastions. The Hedge is the Astral Barrier according to Mages, the in-between of the Waking World and the Dreaming, a mixture that is both and neither. The Hedge is a lot of other things besides, and for our purposes, I don't think it really matters, outside of Bastions being places on the Dreaming Roads being where dreams physically take place.

                                                          Not really! Bastions form when you start dreaming, Nightmares trigger when you fall asleep. Even at the most generous reading of both of those, the Nightmare triggers right when the Bastion starts forming, and unless a target has an exceptional reason to stay asleep, the personal reality of a Nightmare is going wake them up, and get them dealing with it as a real issue. The fight-or-flight instinct is a bitch to fight.

                                                          Like, The most likely place of misunderstanding is that because both dreams and Nightmares play out in a victim's head, and we're used to thinking of the two as the same thing, there's an assumption that a Nightmare is a dream. It's not. To quote Nightmares from the core:

                                                          Even creatures that don’t dream or feel fear can tremble before the power of the Dark Mother.
                                                          Bastions are a space for dreams. Nightmares can affect things that don't dream. Two plus two. "Plays out like a dream" is not "it is a dream." A Nightmare is not a three act play, it is "your reality is bugs are eating your face."

                                                          AT best, and I being very, very generous on this, if you could freeze or massively expand the period of time in which the Bastion would start forming and the Nightmare would start triggering*, you might be able to go and do shenanigans with that, but that's a niche of a niche, and thus the issue of a story and not business as usual. Hell, that's not the only way to force a person to for sure stay asleep while a nightmare is going off, but that's not going to facilitate the preventative aspect of this discussion.

                                                          You can preplan for a Nightmare, and you can pretty reasonably create circumstances that force a Bastion to persist while a Nightmare plays out-but in the idea of the original post that led us here, there's no going into a Bastion to stop a Nightmare from happening, because pretty much none of this works that way. Bastions don't exist by the time the bad things start happening.

                                                          *And I don't want to be a pedant on this, but if we wanna get clinical on this, you ABSOLUTELY fall asleep long before you start dreaming proper.​
