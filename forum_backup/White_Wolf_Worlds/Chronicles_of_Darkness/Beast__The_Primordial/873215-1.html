                                    The Unchained and the Begotten are loosely similar in that they both have major chunks of their metaphysics devoted to sympathetic connections tied to relationships — it's part of the basis for Cover and pactwork, as well as pretty much everything to do with Kinship and the Primordial Pathways. They differ, however, on two fairly key points:

                                    1. The void that gives the Begotten their Hunger and allows them to reflect their monstrous kin is an intrinsic part of their nature as cells in the greater superorganism of the Primordial Dream. Demons, however, approach the hollows they deal with in much the same way as their creator, shuffling metaphysical debt around and recalling impersonal codes to the secret laws of the universe. The emptiness that the Unchained pursue is something external to them that they seek to possess and inhabit; the Children's is an internal process that they attempt to modulate.

                                    2. The bonds conferred by the heritage of the Dark Mother are universally supernatural and otherworldly. The God-Machine as a general entity is almost aggressively mundane, and its rogue processes pick up a trick of semiotics that only works on supernatural beings through their capacity to affect a pretense of mortality — a trick that notably doesn't work on Beasts at all. What's more, while the trajectory of Begotten fatalism tends to mean characters become conceptually related to monsters they relate to and stay close to monsters they don't find repellant, demons become more distinctive as their developing niche in the universe and/or the loss of coherent Cover causes their individual strangeness to warp their interface with reality.

                                                            Originally posted by Vent0 View Post
                                                            But why is that, when Beast claims everything else as Kin? Other than authorial fiat.
                                                            "The animal parts of the collective human consciousness" and "the emergent awareness of the symbolism of human systems" exist on opposite ends of the point at which humans start having symbolism and unique systems. Beasts are notably the first splat since 1e changelings to not have some linguistic quirk as part of their template, whereas demons get the most broadly-applicable and immediately-usable such quality.

                                                            You can have the Begotten in a world without wilderness without bringing back the wilderness, but the God-Machine both heavily uses and has been developing the current state of humanity as creatures of artifice.

                                                            The "Unnatural vs Natural" argument breaks down when you look at 1. if it supported that either side is more "natural" than the other, and 2. why is the G-M the exception when Prometheans are obviously created, Arisen were created, and Deviants are created?
                                                            Prometheans are created by being infused with an entropic force that occasionally makes material agents without direct human intervention and the Arisen are the result of world-altering necromantic ritual. The Machine is a dispassionate Unmoved Mover that acts to preserve the state of the universe in those instances where it's not attempting to set up an occult matrix.

                                                            Also, nobody said "unnatural." The un-things of the Abyss can claim Kinship with the Children of the Dark Mother, but as pseudo-symbolic entities from a mad place outside of time wearing forms that loosely correspond to their true nature they can't rightly be called "artificial."

                                                            I'm honestly curious how Beasts can claim Kinship to everything under the sun and moon except, strangely, anything G-M related. Where is the unifying element that is absent there?
                                                            In all probability it's the whole "entropy" thing — lack of Kinship doesn't stop Family Resemblance from potentially getting through Cover, and angels don't have that vulnerability.

                                                            There's a few straightforward explanations that most readily come to mind for why the Machine isn't Kin:

                                                            1. Its arrival in the cosmology is later than whatever spawned the rest of the metaphysical frameworks and/from the Dark Mother.

                                                            2. It precedes and/or created the Dark Mother in a fashion that excised or never held a sympathetic connection to It.

                                                            3. It's the product of a massive-scale supernatural quality like the anti-Kinship the Leviathans of Vancouver exploit.

                                                            4. It is not supernatural in origin at all.
