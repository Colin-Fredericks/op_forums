                                        I greatly enjoyed the blog post and look forward to more on the topic.

                                        One thing that made me a little sad was the number of people who are already bitching about "hero's being the bad guys" and playing "inhuman monsters" off of one blog post that was designed and a very general teaser.

                                        I get the feeling that each week Matt's going to have to reiterate the same old lines like:

                                        "Hero's are antagonists, but that does not make their relationship with Beast's black/white or simple. Like any antagonist in an RPG the complexity of their relationship with the protagonists varies depending on the ST's play style and the type of story being told"

                                        and

                                        "Were not looking to make Hero's playable really, as their are other games in the NWoD that basically let you do that e.g. Mortal, Hunter and Mage."

                                        Maybe its just me, as I've been waiting for a splat that highlights the more "monster" or "inhuman" elements of the supernatural condition in a non-negative fashion for a while in the NWoD. Second edition has so far gone a long way towards letting you play those sort of characters without being punished for it mechanically e.g. the new humanity/banes system, the new harmony system, Really its one of the reasons I like Demon so much, as it has a morality system and take on being "human" that is utterly subjective and is a view on humanity from outside of humanity (really the one "supernatural viewpoint" we were lacking when the game came out).

                                        For me the WoD has never been about playing a Hero in any way. Its been about playing people. Be they people who are now dead and drinking blood for a living. Proto-people who are really working on becoming more. People who have seen the way reality really works and really don't want to go back to their old job.

                                        For me its about:

                                        Its about playing a Vampire that's having to compromise his morals to survive, having to lie, cheat and steal (at least) to get anywhere in the society of predators he finds himself in. Its about becoming selfish and justifying your actions to keep yourself alive.

                                        Its about playing a Promethean that really trying to "get it", trying to figure out what it is about humans that really makes them special, that makes him want to be one so bad. Its about trying and failing and trying again and sometimes really screwing up and getting people hurt and sometimes figuring it out and having to move on. Its about feeling bad about what you might have done out of former ignorance or feeling vindicated by your actions regardless of the consequences.

                                        Its about playing a Demon that is so very grateful humanity exists, because they give him somewhere to hide and resources to use against his old boss. Its doing anything you need to to stay free and stay ahead of those hunting you. If that means destroying lives, souls and car bombing a bit of infrastructure that also happens to be a hospital then that what you do, because it need to be done.

                                        This was why the thing that made me most happy that's been posted on this forum was when Matt basically said (to paraphrase) "Beasts like being Beasts, as humanity always felt uncomfortable to them and they do not want to be human again". I'd like a game that was about being a "noble/good monster" and enjoy their existence and their "job". I'd like a splat with a little levity and pride in what they are. Rather then yet another game where the protagonists long for their lost humanity such as Vampire, Promethean (though more as a logging for humanity full stop) Changeling and in some cases Mummy.

                                        Don't get me wrong, nothing wrong with those games. I'm a big fan of Vampire and Promethean for example, because they are engaging with the trope I described above in an interesting way. I'd just like to see the new game not be yet another take on the same trope and move onto focusing on a new facet of supernatural existence.

                                        Luckily for me, from the info we have so far, seems I'm getting my wish granted.

                                        "There is no God in the Badlands." - Sunny

                                                            You know, I don't like werewolves. They don't have a motivation. They don't have a personality. They don't talk and when they do talk, it just looks stupid. They just kill people. Vampires kill people too, but they're also actual characters. You can swap a werewolf with a normal wolf any day of the week and it wouldn't really change anything. I thought I was just going to skip Werewolf 2.0, but then I read David Hill's interview about Dark Eras where he talked about the Stonewall riots and how not having a place to belong relates to Werewolf and I got thinking.

                                                            You can't tell that kind of story with Vampire. You can't tell that kind of story with Mage. You sure as hell can't tell that kind of story with Demon. I mean, you can have a story where a bunch of Mages use a nightclub as their sanctum and throw a riot when the cops come to shut the place down, but it's not going to be quite the same as the police repeatedly harassing patrons of a gay bar until the patrons decide they've had enough bullshit and start fighting back. There aren't a lot of games where you can do that, and there are even fewer games where you can do that as part of the game's central theme. So I decided Werewolf 2.0 was worth buying.

                                                            There's a post on RPG.net where Matt implicitly compares Heroes to MRAs. The thread's too long and I don't know if there's a way to search through it conveniently, so I can't quote it, but it got me thinking. Is there a game out right now that subverts hegemonic cultural narratives of privilege? Not really. Exalted does it with imperialism, but I wouldn't really call it the focus of the game.

                                                            We live in a world where narcissistic sociopaths demand the privilege to harass and victimize women because that's how it's been for hundreds of years and learning how to become a better person just takes too much effort. They surround themselves with parasites who go out of their way to swallow their bullshit because they enjoy being ignorant garbage. They're a cancer to our entertainment media and to our hobby. Fuck you very much, GamerGate.

                                                            I don't know if it's a coincidence or if he was thinking about this when he pitched the game, but Matt thought up the tabletop game we need right now. We need a game that challenges longstanding cultural narratives. We need a game where the heroes are called out as assholes. The mere fact that people don't want to understand something as simple as "heroes are asshole, the game isn't about them" is further proof that Beast needs to happen.

                                                            So fuck the heroes. The Beasts' contribution to current tabletop gaming culture is far more interesting, and far more useful.
