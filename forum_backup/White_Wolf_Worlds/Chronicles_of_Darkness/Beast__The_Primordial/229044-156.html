                    Originally posted by Second Chances View Post

                    You see, stuff like this is exactly why I don't get how people are saying they don't know what to do with Beast stories. Beasts versus Slashers would awesome, especially if it was used to subvert the hero-beast dynamic! I know its different strokes for different folks, and I'm so crossover obsessed that I'm building a timeline detailing every detail of the world of darkness... but Beast seems so rich with potential storylines. I can't wait to actually run it.
                    I think the issue is that Beast isn't self contained and doesn't really have a system of drama between Beasts much akin to Mage's Orders or Changeling's Freeholds. The other issue for a few people is a lack of morality system to add the pain of being a Beast. Even werewolf, with it's telling of "You are not human" has it's harmony yardstick. Sure, you're a monster but some part of you must want human contact or perhaps some shot at being remotely human. As it stands, I see most of WoD's supernaturals would not even consider allying with Beasts but rather putting them down like rabid dogs if they have no concept of why they shouldn't be monsters.

                    There is also the lack of a new ground being broken (Vampire has it's cities/cages, Werewolf has the Shadow, Mages have the Astral, Prometheans have themselves, Changelings have the Hedge, Giests have the underworld, Mummies have History of the world, and demons have the God-Machine). It makes complete sense to break the Astral open (yet again) as Mage is going to be more focused on the Supernal than the Astral but idk. It hones with a laser focus on one antagonist but that's it.

                    Lastly, Beast throws it's self into an area which it seems to be the game line that attempts to bind all the game lines together (insert Lord of the Rings joke here) but offers somewhat little on what it offers in that direction. In truth, Hunter is ironically the best option to do so as it has organizations focused on dealing with all the game lines in a somewhat understandable fashion. Cherion is a supernatural chopshop that is built for profit, The Union is the everyman who simply wants supernaturals to get off their lawn, etc, etc.

                    Beasts, in contrast, have no underlining grander organization or ethos in dealing with other supernaturals which is odd considering they are the game line that has the most sense to do so other than Hunter.

                    In short, Beast has it's (ironically) self inflicted flaws but I'm not going to crucify it like some people have but rather like to see what it grows into.

                        Becuase a Beast has always had the pscyho-spiritual infrastructure to cope with their Hungers-they can handle the strain of the Astral influence. Heroes, getting somewhat infected by the Hunger in a feeding that already hurt them to some degree, just don't have the necessary support to not collapse under the weight of that Hunger infection.                         Regardless of whether the Hunger ever goes away or not, a Hero can over time grow their personal Astral Space enough to become more complete and sturdy,and this is likely why some Heroes end up just quitting.

                                                Originally posted by Rater202 View Post
                                                Two people have urges that are negative and harmful. One of them is aware of the nature of the urges, can try to control them, to limit the damage he causes, and ultimately try and direct his damage towards positive ends. The other is either gleefully unaware of the harm his urges cause or just doen'st care because he's so important.

                                                A Beast could be either of those people. a Hero is always the second person. That's what I'm getting from it.
                                                Yes. A nuanced sympathetic person who's been victimised by a Beast's predation and decides to fight back against those Beasts that commit such crimes, and protect their other victims, is the backstory for a hunter - a Hero is something else.

                                                Originally posted by Shock View Post
                                                Beasts, in contrast, have no underlining grander organization or ethos in dealing with other supernaturals which is odd considering they are the game line that has the most sense to do so other than Hunter.
                                                It seems to me that Beasts are rather like mortals in how they engage with the world at large. Most other lines more or less suggest that a supernatural's former human life will largely disappear - though they might keep it up, or maintain another one, it's more a cover story than a real life. Whereas for a Beast, they don't have an "imposed" new life to replace it - new abilities, and a new pressing need, but no new world to go along with it and replace their old one - their former lives feel more real to them than most supernaturals, even mechanically, via their Life. So my answer to what a Beast does it it's the same as what a (PC) mortal does. Live their life, try to get by, dig into the dark corners that surround them, hope to make things a little more comfortable for them and theirs - just that they have a big addition to the second of those things, and lots more talent for the third.
