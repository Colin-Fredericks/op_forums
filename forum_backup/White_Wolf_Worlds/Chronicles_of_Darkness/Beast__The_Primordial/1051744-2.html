                                                        I've come to the point of viewing Reconstruction in the continuum of American projects in occupation and nation-building. Early attempts at transformative change stymied by attendant instability, local resistance, insurgent terrorism, and a lack of political will. Ultimately followed by a reverse course, support for a regressive but more stable regime with a new coat of paint, and an exit that allows for a declaration of victory without many real achieved objectives. Then the entire enterprise is mostly relegated to the back room of popular memory.

                                                            Took me a while for the opening intro to "click", but once I read through the historical background it made more sense. I'm very impressed by how effectively you stuck with your guns at calling the Reconstruction "Post-Apocolyptic" and then showing just how accurate that descriptor was. I would not have thought it possible, but clearly I was wrong.

                                                            The continuity of the Mage section seems a bit weird. The connection to the Nameless War is apt, but the text seems to suggest that the Nameless War and Civil War end at similar times, when the Nameless War continues for another 35 years and doesn't finish until the dawn of the 20th Century. Likewise, the references to the Free Council also seem out of place to me, since they don't exist until properly until the literal last day of the 19th century. Now, if the intent is for that crossover to refer to the South in the early 20th century, that may be on me for reading comprehension, I'm up reading much later than I should be.
