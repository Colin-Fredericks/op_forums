    Here are some Comments I took from the Kickstarter because they deserve to be shared;

    Originally posted by Paige
    I backed the project finally, just to voice my intense dislike for Devouring vs Homecoming. There is a story in the original draft, of a Makara woman who falls in love with her childhood friend; the bond she shares with that woman seems to be the only thread tying her to her humanity. But, after her childhood naivety wears off, she realizes that her true love will always live in a world apart from her, because you are born a beast. And as a Rampaging Soul typically goes after those closest to you, there's some deeply implied danger.
    If anybody can become a beast, the story changes drastically. Now, her goal would be what, to find a way to slip the red pill to her wife and intentionally replace her lifelong partner's soul with that of a monster? That just screams Vampire.
    My take was that the Soul was always an adversary, if you read enough into the text -- realizing that when I read the draft really caught me off guard. It did not care what happened to You. The woman who dies, alone, scared, and pathetic because her Soul was gorged and sleeping when the hunter followed her into her Lair -- her Soul objectively doesn't care at all as it goes off Unfettered. Same with the Sphinx who lets herself die. The soul feels no bond or obligation to You. In the fluff, Ben seems to retain some sense of himself, but in mechanics, Merger completely obliterates you and the Soul steals your body. An animal that you share a bond with seeks to comfort or protect you. Pets threaten those who threaten their owner. Your Soul doesn't even care about getting revenge on your murderer.
    I actually wanted to see revising more towards the soul not being so hostile to your interests. Even in a bestial, animalistic way, it should care for you in some fashion. Instead, it felt more like you were under demonic possession. Now with the revision, you literally have your soul destroyed and a ghost parasite possessing you.
    I've spent months hanging on every scrap of info regarding the game, and I have great interest in the project. I mean no disrespect, and of course you can't please everybody, but this feels so anti-beast.
    Someone else mentioned that with the changes, most of the fiction in the book no longer makes sense.

                    Homecoming V. Devouring

                    I guess I'll play this game too. I thought Homecoming was very evocative and appropriate. It added absolutely nothing to the game, but not everything needs to do that. Sometimes cool imagery is reward enough. I think the revision needs some final polishing, so I can't compare the writing without being unfair. I will say that Devouring does actually add to the game. I would prefer the names actually be reversed. I think the new way would better be called Homecoming. The name would eliminate that distasteful "job offer" feeling. It would be "welcome to the family." It also downplays a Beast "making" another Beast. I don't hate it, but I don't love it either. I think Devouring would fit the old method as an appropriate title that fits better there. Obviously, that would mean both methods are valid. I actually haven't seen anything to suggest otherwise. Devouring is, obviously, the standard method now. It improves the game even if it does "kill a darling" as Matt said.

                    Sometimes semantics matter. There are a few words changed here or there that I think were changed unnecessarily that would weaken the possibility of the old Homecoming being a possibility. It is still reasonable, but a more solid footing. I think Beasts being born human instead of like a human and anybody could become a Beast does dilute the title character. I would love for that bit to backtrack, but if it was the original intent then I'll live with it. Let's assume Beasts retained that "special at birth" feel.

                    I would say a Beast is born and raised like a normal human. At some point, the nightmares start and they get the alienated feelings, all the usual stuff. This is the point Devouring and Homecoming would differ. If the proto-Beast happens upon a Beast then they can choose Devouring. If they don't it will be more difficult and take longer but eventually they will have the choice of Homecoming. Both are in fact choices. They always were. What is not a choice is the Beast having the opportunity to make the choice. A Beast would keep getting the nightmares until they choose Homecoming or Devouring.

                    Before a Devouring a Beast must be as much a Beast as just before a Homecoming. It is even more explicit in the new text. A proto-Beast can actually recognize a true Beast and see their powers activate as a normal Beast could. They can't be mortal because mortals are incapable of recognizing Beasts and seeing Horrors/Souls. Devouring would be the default because it is the easy path. Homecoming would still be an option. It isn't like anyone that has seen the first version doesn't know how to run with Homecoming. Of course, it would be nice if Homecoming at least got a mention. It could be elaborated upon in a later book if there isn't room in the core.

                    Now assume the new changes stand. Absolutely nothing about the dual paths changes. If a person is Beast enough to be Devoured then they could manage a Homecoming too. This is what happens when you try to look more for similarities than differences, aka keeping an open mind.

                    This isn't something I think is worth pitching a fit over. It is a section of the game that many people will skip over. It would only matter if it actually affected the story. Homecoming, Devouring are important to the character. They mean much less to the player. Nobody can suck Homecomings from your mind and I think I have justified them as a possibility. It then is left to the group to decide what they want. Unless the book specifically states that Beasts do not spontaneously occur, you can have your Devouring and Homecoming too. Players are special so even if most Beasts were Devoured, you can Come Home.

                                        About the tone

                                        Since the writers recognize the old tone was not what they intended, I feel more comfortable describing the original tone. From the Introduction, character examples, and basically the first few hundred pages, the tone was Beasts are "irredeemable assholes" as one person put it. Some people liked that, some hated it. The purpose of a Beast's existence seemed to be solely to feed its hunger and whatever it might like to do in its free time, not spent feeding. As a whole, the tone slapped you in the face while asking "why would you ever act morally?" It made a person feel like they must be stupid, stupid, stupid if they didn't play a Beast as a sadistic monster.

                                        The World of Darkness has a paradigm. Part of it would be while the World is dark, a player may be as good or evil as they wish. Generally, good is a struggle. That is a function of the World. Sadistic monster has always been an option. Beast did not seem to fit the paradigm. Sure you could play as good or as evil as you wanted. Of course, you were required to do evil acts to feed your hunger or let them be done in your name, as it were. A character is less about good and evil and more about mitigating required evil. Practically everything in the book makes you feel like you are supposed to play the sadistic monster. That was never the intent.

                                        Beasts have a piece of sadistic monster inside and they need to feed it. That was intended. You can play as good or evil as you want, provided you feed the monster. That is required to be a game line in WoD. The difference is the game has at least a few pages devoted to countering the notion that you are supposed to be a sadistic monster, unrepentant. The teacher bit is new. Unlike feeding, it is a soft goal. There is no penalty if you blow it off.

                                        The tone is different. There is no denying that. Did Matt go too far away from the original? Perhaps. We only have a few pages this time around. If he is setting a trend for changes then I think he is over-correcting. If this is just the counter balance to all the rest that remains the same then maybe not. There are many examples left to show what sadistic monsters Beasts can be. They do not need to be changed. Sadistic monster is a part of Beast. It was not meant to be the all-consuming part though.

                                        Seriously, the example characters don't illustrate what sadistic monsters Beasts can be? I'm using "sadistic monsters" as that is how Beasts are unabashedly described in this game. However, you might like to described it. Are the examples not some nasty pieces of work? I think so. I don't know if they are good teachers. I know that old lady taught that punk not to steal candy from kids. I know Ben taught that bully not to mess with people. How much of their actions was about teaching and how much was about feeding? I think only the player knows. The cautionary tale, don't get on a Beast's radar. They will fuck you up. Are they imposing their morality upon others? Maybe, but they have to decide on some method to choose victims-- I mean prey. Are Beasts hypocrites? Maybe, but so are many of the Kin.

                                        Can't we give the guy a break? I don't mean to say we shouldn't express are opinions, but we can be realistic. We know why the revision would be less catchy, months vs. days. I don't get the outrage. From a handful of pages suddenly it is apparent that the whole rest of the book is invalidated? Ridiculous. I don't know what some people expected. Atavisms and Nightmares don't do it for you? Nope, they changed the Introduction section so they fucked up the whole book. One of your favorite parts is changed and I can understand somebody getting angry about it. I don't see how that destroys your image of the entire book. You must not have liked it that much then. I'm still not going to call you "bro."
