    Waking Nightmares (or) On the nature and process of becoming Begotten.

    06-11-2016, 09:33 PM
    I listened to a podcast very recently that critically examined Beast: the Primordial, and while they made some very good points that I had taken for granted before the biggest thing I took away from it was a great deal of confusion surrounding what a Beast is and the nature of their transformation.

    When a Beast is devoured do they cease to be human immediately? Or, does one remain virtually the same with the exception of the Horror now residing within both your subconscious mind and soul? Is there a timeframe for which Beast must struggle trough accepting what they are and what they've become? Or does the Devouring bring an immediate sense of purpose and acceptance with one's condition? What about a Beast's Touchstones? I know I read at some point in the book that Beasts have them to remind them of their human halves, but no real rules exist and no examples are given.

    Is any of this ever explained in the book? Or was this purposely left up to ST and player creativity?

                          Well see, here's the thing. (And the main reason why I posted this discussion thread.)

                          All Chronicle of Darkness gamelines revolve around exploring one's humanity to some varying degree.

                          Vampire is arguably about defining one's humanity in the face of eternal damnation.

                          Werewolf about balancing one's human half with the wolf spirit within.

                          Mage about seeking apotheosis beyond humanity without losing their own humanity.

                          Promethean about defining and discovering what it means to be human.

                          Changeling about losing human innocence and searching for meaning despite such trauma.

                          Geist about never taking one's human life for granted when one's been given a second chance.

                          Mummy about uncovering lost humanity in the face of inevitable dissolution.

                          And Demon, freak'n God Machine Angel Demon the Descent is about what happens when an automaton gains humanity and the burned of such a gift/curse.

                          If Beasts is truly about abandoning humanity, or at the very least discovering one's monstrosity then why on earth would another supernatural splat ever want to associate with these inhuman creatures except under specific circumstances. (Circle of the Crone, the Pure, Seers, Centimany, Loyalists, Abmortals, Shank-Sen [butchered that name - . -'] respectively) How can a player connect with a truly inhuman being? Is that truly the point? Is there no room for such stories in Beast the Primordial?

                                          Not exactly. The Devouring subsumes the person and the end result is something not entirely the original, but not wholly alien. A few people have described the Horror as very much an Id type of being. That seems to fit for my reading of it.

                                          Your soul is subsumed by the Horror and integrated into it, causing the Horror to exist in the material world as your unrestrained impulse and desires. It causes the human mind to exist in the Primordial Dream as a higher/guiding conscious to the monster that is the Horror. To quote many a fused monster in Persona "I am thou. Thou art I." I would honestly compare it more to a mage's awakening. It isn't that it rewrites how you handle things, but it gives you a new view to look at the world which may cause you to view things differently. I mean the world is going to look slightly different from a normal human that gets a thrill out of proving themselves the best at a sport, vs a person that has a voice in the back of their mind demanding that competition should not result in you just winning it should end with you grinding your opponent into the dirt to show just how much better you are.

                                                          Beasts don't have breaking points, and they don't have Integrity. This means that nothing they ever do will rattle them the same way breaking points do a human being.

                                                          I run (and play) that to mean that, yes, their human 'conscience' is instantly destroyed. That said, these beings still had human upbringings, and they still have rationality. They know that killing someone is 'wrong' in our society, even if it doesn't feel 'wrong' to them. The degree to which any given Beast is going to cling to 'humanity' is going to be variable, based on their cultural attachment, social ties, how pragmatic it is to cling to, and how hungry they are.

                                                          They aren't objectively evil. Many are probably really uncomfortable with acts that used to be breaking points for them as a human - they're going to have cognitive dissonance.

                                                          But they aren't breaking points now, and the Hunger is what drives them, in the end.
