    To put what Arc has been saying in other words (as I interpreted him), Heroes are similar to Legend Slashers. You can kill the man, but if you don't end the story in your own terms, he'll either keep coming back somehow, or someone else will take up his mantle and resume the chase, likely with a vengeance. Their staying power is due to the fact that it's a very old and widespread Legend, mono-myth or whatever you wanna call it. They have the tools they need to win. They strike when you let your guard down. They don't need a 20+ combat dice pool, they have the advantage of numbers, propaganda, spying networks, inflammatory rhetoric and an underdogs-can-cheat mindset, however you define cheating. Beasts are in a tough spot, but they have a way out that doesn't show up a lot in mythologies: they can acquire a new role in the story, or add one to their monster role so that they can stay in the story.

                Something else worth remembering is that the winners write the history books . . . err, myths. One recurring feature in many myths and legends is that a successful Beast often has a lair full of trophies, weapons, and (sometimes) dead bodies - mementos of all the wannabe Heroes they've brought down. No one remembers the nameless failures - only the Hero who got lucky and actually brought down the Beast.

                So, picking up on an earlier idea, that presents another potential Storyteller technique. Periodically unleash a "throwaway" Hero on your Beast PCs. Then, when they get cocky, throw the "real deal" at them. Bonus points if this Hero finds a way to use the "leftovers" from the throwaways to get at the Beast - that'll teach them to keep trophies!




                Edit: Another possibility, if you want to bring back material from the first draft, is the idea that Beasts provoke the creation of Heroes . . . Then index the Hero's power to that of the Beast that created it.

                That way, the Beast never knows if the Hero in front of her is really "their" Hero . . .or if they've become a minor antagonist in some greater Beast's Legend. People forget that Theseus actually brought down two monsters - he was gunning for the Minotaur, but slew the murderous Procrustes, too. And not out of any real plan - Procrustes was just in the wrong place, at the wrong time

                    Originally posted by PenDragon View Post
                    I think there's something potent to work with here. Reframing Heroes not as recurring nuisances, but as a warning against unrestricted hedonism. This way Heroes challenge a Beast's beastliness in a more abstract way. They say (well, not they themselves, but the phenomenon of heroes in general says), "If you live like a monster, you die like a monster." Heroes flip the themes on their head, teaching Beasts a lesson in humanity; to accept limits and to restrict their monstrousness. In turn the Beast becomes a little more human...or dies a monster.
                    You could perhaps tie this directly into a Beast's Life/Legend traits.

                    Using my favourite example. Darth Vader's Life would be Father and his Legend is something like (looks at PDF) Vicious. All those force chokings, powering the Dark side through anger and hate.

                    When Vader is trying to corrupt Luke he focuses heavily on his Father trait. "Join me, and together we can rule the galaxy as father and son.", "So, you have a twin sister. Your feelings have now betrayed her, too. Obi-Wan was wise to hide her from me. Now his failure is complete. If you will not turn to the Dark Side... then perhaps she will". It almost works too. As soon as Vader finishes that second quote Luke does give into his anger and comes one final decision away from turning to Dark Side.

                    In contrast when the Emperor makes his attempt to corrupt Luke he plays directly to the Legend of Vicious. " You want this, don't you? The hate is swelling in you now. Take your Jedi weapon. Use it. I am unarmed. Strike me down with it. Give in to your anger. With each passing moment you make yourself more my servant.", "Good! Use your aggressive feelings, boy. Let the hate flow through you!". You can't get more direct than that, he might as well have said "Be Vicious!".


                    A mechanic that follows Vader & Palpatine's strategy closely would be one where a Beast is encouraged to use Life against the Hero and hide Legend from the Hero until the final temptation where they try and make the Hero's follow the Beast's Legend instead of the Hero's Legend. Notice that when Darth kills Obi Wan in front of Luke he wasn't Vicious, but quick and clean with a single lightsaber swing. Using Life in the Hero's presence gives bonuses to open a Door, using Legend where the Hero might hear of it could so far as to give the Hero an extra Door - but you have to use Legend to corrupt the Hero, and you really hope they don't have many Doors left when that moment comes.

                    I think this works very well for explaining why Heroes terrify Beasts. A Beast is scared that the Hero's Legend will subsume their own; and so long as the Hero is around they actually have to downplay their Legend if they want to win.

                    I think though, if you went for a system like this you must not bring back the idea of Beasts creating Heroes. If the Heroes' presence encourages a Beast to downplay Legend, then IMO the reward for a Beast who defeats the Hero should be being able to use their Legend with impunity (or at least, no more trouble than any other supernatural indulging their nature) - and I would also make Legend grant Conditions equal in potency to the ones Life grants. Except that Life's Conditions only work on Heroes, Legend's Conditions work on everything except Heroes.

                    [late edit] Maybe Legend conditions make others buy into your Legend. A legend like Scary could aid Intimidation. A Legend like Unseen could aid Stealth.


                    Oh. One last thing.

                    If the Beast successfully corrupts the Hero but dies in the attempt. The Hero not only gains the Beast's Legend as per all successful corruptions. The Beast also gain the Hero's body, hence why Palpatine is so casual about telling Luke to kill him.

                                                        Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                                        For example, one thing I haven't seen brought up yet is the nature of impostion of Heroes on Beasts, and even a degree of theft. Heroes get a set of powers inherently on their own, but Anathema, their biggest power, is about effecting the Beasts rather than improving themselves, and their main method of improvement in a supernatural sense is killing Beasts and taking something from the act(their Boons).
                                                        If the Beasts are being Beasts and the Heroes are being heroes, it's about facing the darkness and forging through with both scars and a veteran's strength. It's not improvement, it's sacrifice: every Boon takes you farther away from the hope of a normal human life, burns out your mundaneity, forges you into paladin's armor as the woman beneath fades and falls. You are stealing the monster's power, but these weapons are not comfortable or natural; they up the ante, and just like the Beasts who strive for Inheritance you know that means going all-in. The difference is an inversion: the Beast achieves something not unlike godhood, and the world suffers for their hideous whim. The Hero becomes faceless beneath the mask of her own legend, a sacrifice on the altar of the world's need. We have a thousand stories about the hero-weapon who struggles with what they must be, but those are only stories; in the end, they always find a way to turn back. The Hero knows better. To turn away from the dark is death, not just for you, but for all those you shepherd. It was always too late - the hard part of accepting and internalizing each Boon isn't that you're becoming more like what you hunt, it's learning not to see the process as your tragedy. Learning to see only the armor in the mirror, and forgetting - as the world does - that there ever was a woman.

                                                        If the Beasts are following the bevy of gentler interpretations I think people are moving towards and the Heroes are being Heroes, it's the ultimate victimization, the moment when because your target is acceptable you let all your human restrictions fall away and become something even monsters shudder to face. It's the lovechild of Dexter, Hannibal, and the Punisher, the Magnificent Bastard smiling through rotten teeth, blood on her breath. The world doesn't matter, it's just a stage; what matters is the performance, the beauty of it, nevermind that it's a lie and the props are stolen and your opposite isn't acting when they weep at the sight of your gleaming knives. A Hero in this mode is building the theatre Boon by Boon, because the truth is they were never meant for the limelight - they're not actors, they're just infatuated with stardom. She thinks if she can cut away enough fame and Legend and stitch it into her skin it'll be her story, Tom Cruise's eyes, Scarjo's throat, bones dug up from Presley's secret grave. Nevermind that the audience is literally captive and the whole thing's on fire. Nevermind that they're so far from purpose and meaning the script is pretty much absurdist. Nevermind anything and everything but all eyes on them.

                                                            I don't think Heroes are lacking in ways to hurt and kill Beasts. The problem, as I see it, is that Heroes don't have as many defensive abilities. They don't heal nearly quickly enough for it to be combat relevant, and their only source of supernatural armor is Champion's Endurance. That's enough to keep them competitive early on, but Beasts have a lot of direct damage abilities that scale with Lair. If a mid Lair Beast can get into range most of the time the Hero is going to die very quickly, and that sort of undercuts their threat factor. Trying to build an advanced Hero for a midgame antagonist is frustrating because all those other abilities go to waste if they aren't alive to use them.
