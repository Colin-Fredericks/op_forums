            My opinions on Beast are...divided, the splat is mechanically fine, but everything surrounding it, the fluff, antagonists, the whole crossover focus, everything falls flat for me. I can shit talk Beast all day, and have, but this thread is about selling Beast, so let me pitch what I personally like about the splat:

            Fundamentally Beast is about playing a total fuckwad, an expression of the human Id that justifies pure self-indulgence with the notion that they are "educating" people, if they bother to justify it at all. Now, one might read that last sentence as me decrying Beast, but that'd be wrong. Beast isn't attractive if you are looking to be the good guy, Harry Dresden, 80s TV show protagonist sort, but if you want an ambiguous character who makes others questions their core self-assumptions Beast is your game.

            Now one might say that such characters are untenable as PCs, but let me pitch you the two Beast PCs I've played with (both as a Storyteller and a Player) and hopefully they can speak for themselves:
            Beast #1: Kwame Bonsu

            Kwame was introduced to my Mage/Werewolf game set in Kyoto, a spidery fellow based on the Anansi myth he was (like his inspiration) a gregarious trickster. Also a self interested sociopath. In a game of spirit politics, schemes, inherited duties and grave perils Kwame was a breath of fresh air: a lying theif who just wanted to steal as much as he possibly could and gave not one shit about the repercussions to others. He enjoyed causing commotion for no reason and bullied characters for no other reason than it entertained him. Now that might sound like bad PC material, and I'd be first to admit he was absolutely an antagonist, but he did a good job of putting other character's interests in perspective and pointing out that, at their core, all the high minded talk of duties and schemes was motivated by very simple instincts.

            Kwame was a splendid PC...everyone greatly enjoyed when he, in the classic Anansi way, overstepped himself and got his ass beat by the two "weakest" PCs whom he'd been dicking over most of the game. Good chatharsis.
            Beast #2: Logam Moore

            Logan was a Wendigo, a shepherd of the natural world who taught people that the wild was a scary, primal place where they didn't belong. I believe his pitch line was "The wild is a cold and vicious place, stay in your cities, you don't belong here". The game being set in Antarctica meant Logan had a lot of opportunity to 'educate'. Logan sat at the edge, he rarely spoke and indeed was rarely seen by anyone. He haunted other PCs, murdered NPCs and threatened everyone with a cold, vicious terror of the outdoors. But, in the end Logan was also heroic, being the only PC who could confront and defeat the Abyss tainted Mastigos (another PC...me) who was busily damning the entire expedition for his own, impossible salvation.

            Did that make Logan good? No. He was a mass murderer. Yet his actions saved hundreds, and in the end gave the 'villain' his comeuppance.
            Both the above characters were great, they challenged the players and the narrative, and taught lessons the old fashion way: brutally. That's where Beast excels, as visions of human nature without self-delusion. Don't try to shoehorn in why the Beast is a good guy, he's not. Even if he happens to do 'good' (if you can ever call terrorizing others good) he's not doing it for good reasons, he's doing it because it sates his primal thirst. That's part of the splat's charm, in a world where other monsters obsess over their humanity Beasts are monsters without regret, begging the question of what's so wrong with that?

            TL;DR: Beasts are great antagonists, and good PCs for games where interparty conflict and antagonistic players are welcome. They shed light on the monstrousness of other splats and pose serious questions about the concepts of good and evil. Don't try to make them into good guys, that's not their point.

                                    Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
                                    I think my main issue with Beast is that it seems to be treading a lot of the same water that Vampire does; "I'm a hungry monster who teaches mortals with fear" has been the schtick of the Lancea Sanctum for over a decade now, and vampires are a lot easier to grok than incarnate fears of humanity who may have always been you or maybe ate your soul?
                                    Not really. I mean, that's like saying Vampire, Werewolf, Mage and Beast are the same because they're all highly territorial.

                                    Vampire is an extremely physical game. No matter how much you drink, how many drugs you do, how many people you fuck, you're nothing more than a walking corpse. You cling to your touchstones in order to maintain some semblance of humanity, but where does that leave you when your touchstones grow old and die, and what does it say about you if your only link to your old life is your own gravestone? You hunt people down to drink their blood because that's the only thing that can satisfy the hunger, but no matter how much warm blood you drink, it's just a reminder that you're a stone-cold corpse.

                                    In comparison, Beast is entirely psychological. You're born from primordial nightmares, a reminder that fear, much like pain, is necessary to humanity's survival. You dwell in the Astral realm and breach the barrier that separates it from the physical world. You hunger, but not for a false semblance of life. You feed on fear, anxieties, insecurities, and on the epiphanies people have when they're pushed to their breaking point. You're the Ghost of Christmas Future pointing at Ebenezer Scrooge's gravestone; it doesn't matter that he's going to die, it matters that he's confronting his death. Maybe you really do care and you're doing him a favour and giving him a chance to turn his life around. Maybe you don't, and you just want to ruin him to satisfy the Horror. That part is up to you.

                                                        I think at its heart Beast is about subverting the trope. The old tale of hero-kills-monster has been done over and over again (and indeed, we have Hunter for it in CoD) but what if it wasn't clear cut? What if the monster at least tried to live its life straight? Does it really deserve to die for what it is?

                                                        Beasts have no natural inclination toward being good - and they can easily become very, very bad. But while the hunger drives them, they are always in control of their actions, and how they shape the world around them is, to a large extant, their decision. The tagline of the game is "A storytelling game of endless hunger". In many ways the game is about addiction, or about madness - that nasty part of you you can never excise, but which you have to deal with one way or another.

                                                        That, plus you can be a m************ dragon.
