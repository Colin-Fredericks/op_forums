                You still need a Lair trait of at least 8, then you need to solidify your Legend. Either by subverting the Hero, which involves controlling a battle with the Hero and having it entirely on the Beast's terms without getting any Lethal damage or Anathema, at which point they reform their Horror and their humanity is eliminated and they no longer feel anything for their human or supernatural family. Or she can take control of the Hive, which involves showing superiority over other Beasts and 'the most powerful' supernatural in the area, Beast or not, that has contact with humanity. Lastly, she can just create a Legend through spreading a legend of her acts, similar to how a Legend Slasher becomes a Legend, except she must keep Heroes or other Beasts from subverting the Legend.

                        If you (general you, to clarify) thought the Incarnate Inheritance would ever make the Beast less of a monster, I can safely say you missed the point. From the material available from the kickstarter, it was never about that. It was about not being someone else's monster. It was about breaking the mold of the monomyth and establishing your own legend, by proving to the world that the story of the Hero is meaningless before your power. To become your own monster, on no one else's terms. A Begotten who utterly defeats a Hero is not the monster the latter thought they were. They are the monster who has shown they are more important than the Hero. That's why Incarnates can shut them down and deny their power.

                        I think the addition of alternate ways to achieve it and the possibility to destabilize the Myth was a definite improvement though.

Unlike Changeling, where something similar happens for the creation of the True Fae, the Inheritance is generally accepted as a good thing. There are very few things in the CofD that I would classify as "evil." The Maeljin/Infernal. The Abyss. The Judges of Death and Apep. And that old Inheritance was the fourth. The old Inheritance, to me, wasn't become an advanced monster. It was becoming a force of evil. I don't even classify the strix or Gentry as definitively evil, and pre-revised Beasts made that cut.

                                    So, its not an issue of being "more or less of a monster." But to undergo the Incarnate Inheritance is to subvert the monomyth as a monster.

                                                    That's not what I meant to say. I already acknowledged that subverting the Hero is a cruel path towards the Inheritance, and it is made more so by the very fact there are alternatives. I wasn't implying anything I didn't explicitely state there, just making an aside. I meant to elaborate (as I have now haphazardly done via edit, details in the text to follow instead) that none of the Inheritances are what I would constitute as an ordinarily happy ending. A Beast who wants to be free of the hold of the mythic cycle as themselves has only the option of overtaking it as a monster beyond monsters.

                                                    It's incredibly important to the theme of subversion that Beasts can be more than monsters. They're people, and they don't need to deal with or relate to everything as a hungering horror. The story you mentioned is a great example of that. The Beast eschews playing the role. She refuses to go with the narrative and prove the Hero right, even if this likely won't be her last foe. But she can't rise above the pursuit of Heroes and the monomyth's grip the way an Incarnate can.

                                                    That's why the Incarnate is tragic. For some, it may be everything they ever wanted but didn't know they did until achieving it. For others, it might be a painful and unthinkable decision, because to become an Incarnate is to forgo your Life for a Myth that fully defines you - as a Beast - from then on. The story is bent under your knee and Heros are just footnotes, but it took making yourself a true monster embodied. And one way or another, that way was going to be dark.

                                                    EDIT: It may have been too dark or unapologetically monstrous to stand as a meaningful story element representing a notion that ought to have been at least somewhat positive in Beast The Primordial. I won't deny that. It's understandable that it crossed a line for people back in the first version.

                                                    I guess what I was trying to say through all this rambling was, despite that, I was alright with it from a writing standpoint even then because it felt like it wanted to be a very grim and questionable possibility for Beasts in line with a bleak train of thought.

                                                    P.S.: I hate English sometimes. Arc is right.
