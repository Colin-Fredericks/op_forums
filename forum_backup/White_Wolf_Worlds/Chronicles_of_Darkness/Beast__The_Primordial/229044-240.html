    Originally posted by blackhatmatt View Post


    Wait, what?

    Being a Beast is now something that is, at least partially, a choice. How you feed your Horror is entirely a choice. What being a Beast means to you and how you express is a choice (character's and player's). How has anyone lost agency?

    When you say choice I'm not sure how to take it. Basically you're saying that the player chooses to eaten by the Horror. They choose to be the victim.

    The problem is that being a victim is, in and of itself, a good indicator that agency has been lost. There's a much bigger problem though -- that the players choose to be victims. This is not the reason most people play RPGs (or games at all). Playing an RPG where, up front, I know that my character chose to be a victim doesn't sound that interesting to me. Now YMMV, but this actually sounds quite disturbing. To make some simple analogies to the (now) core tenant of the game, imagine if the Horror is trying to rape your character. To become the Beast you must choose to let it happen. Or we can go with a less utter dark analogy, the Horror is trying to murder you and to become the Beast you must choose to let it happen. The character must surrender to their fate. Surrender is the very definition of loss of agency.

    Now I'm pretty sure that you didn't realize that the new intro could be read this way. And I'm sure this issue is going to get fixed in future drafts because I have every confidence that your not going to give me a game where the core tenant is everyone's a victim. Right? As storyteller's we would never come to the table with a chronicle that opens up with -- you're all victims of rape (or insert slightly less terrible crime here). Because that isn't going to be a very fun chronicle. We might be able to deconstruct such things -- crimes and victimhood -- in literature but, games aren't a literary form, even if the storytelling part seems like it is. There are some themes that we simply can't bring to the table because it simply isn't going to be fun. Remember, whatever you do, this is still going to be a game, and you have to make it so people are going to want to play it.

    I do still think that there's a simple fix. Just flip who does the Devouring and you're pretty much good to go.

    If you want to score extra points and work the Family / Dark Mother concept back in, you could make some additional alterations a la,
    The Dark Mother's seeds drift through the endless seas of the Astral Realms coming to rest in the dreams of some children. These seeds are the source of many of our childhood terrors. The bogeyman beneath the bed, the monster in the closet. Most of the time these seeds whither and die, destroyed by mind's natural defenses. A very few of these seed bearers are fed upon by other Beasts, germinating the seed within them. In these very few, a Horror hatches that hounds them throughout their dreams until at last, they turn and face it. A Beast is born when a hounded soul stands against the Horror that has stalked their dreams for so long and not only defeats it but descends to its level, consuming it in a great Devouring in the exact manner that Horror continuously threatened to do unto them.

    ...or something along those lines anyway, preserving some of the things from the Homecoming and Family.

    Unfortunately this thread is really too long so I probably won't return to this conversation (because it's going to be too hard to find again) so PM me if you want to discuss the whole agency / victimhood issue more. I'll warn you though, I'm a PhD student in the midst of preparing my reading list for my quals so I might be slow in responding (I'll be reading through dozens upon dozens of articles on semantics, phil. o. lang., info. org., info. theory, linked data, and similar topics this summer so my response rate will be downright atrocious at times).

                                            Originally posted by Rater202 View Post
                                            I'd like to bring up something I saw on an other forum: Beasts being made instead of born, and choosing to become a Beast, kinda screws with the family angle.

                                            You're not the older cousin. You're the new guy who isn't actually related to any of them and is just aping their stuff.
                                            That only works with an orthodox, dictionary definition of family, which this game clearly would have more interest in subverting than supporting.

                                            Consider Werewolf. In 1st ed., it was a given that a Werewolf character had at least other Wolf-blooded, if not full Werewolves, as blood relatives, and consequently, undergoing the First Change was an increase in status within the family. An unfortunate side effect of that familial connection was that Tribes, in the narrative, had a dynastic, monolithic hold over regions, which forced Chronicles to either involve the Pack being drawn from a single Tribe, or being the narratively rare Mixed Tribe Pack. In 2nd ed., this is not an issue, due primarily to the narrative shifting away from Tribes being cohesive, world-spanning organizations, but also due to there being more vectors to becoming Wolf-blooded than mere blood relation.

                                            Beast, as presented in the new draft of the introduction, will benefit similarly from decoupling acquiring the template's powers from blood relations in that Broods will have more agency in choosing their own power structures rather than adhering to an imposed, paternalistic framework, be it based on blood ties or Mythological hierarchy. This fluid power dynamic also aids in regulating the behavior of broodmates in a similar fashion to how the low-level antagonism that pervades Vampire courts aids in preserving the Masquerade. Additional, this non-orthodox family model avoids having members being beholden to toxic power dynamics, a very real problem with conventional family models.

                                            Crunch isn't a hobby; it's a calling.
