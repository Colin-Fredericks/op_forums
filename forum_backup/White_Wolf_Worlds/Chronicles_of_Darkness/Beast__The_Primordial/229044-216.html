                Originally posted by lovecraft lite View Post
                Yeah, an entire species created just to enforce some primal moral is kind of junky. It would have worked much better as like, a school of thought among the Children.
                No, that's not it. I have hang-ups on that, too, but I feel by and large more positively towards it. And truth be told, I'm okay with the word Horror too, although I reserve a right use it ina different context in the following explanation. No, it has to do with-

                Okay, before I procede, I need to acknowledge I'm about to run the risk of sounding like an asshole. I apologize if I do in advance.

                In society, we have gatekeepers for a lot of different things. We have gatekeepers for literature, we have them for the culinary arts and the workers unions and the teachers and such, people who define the terms and the access of it. THis applies to families too, within the looseness of the words. A drill sergeant decides who and who isn't allowed into the military family, a Mormon Mission President decides who belongs to what zone 'family' at what time, a group of friends usually has someone who is the final voice of whether someone is in the group or not, a child can marry someone into the family, and the parents in an actual family decide whether to adopt someone in or not(and is often sought after for their blessing in regards to the aforementioned marriage). It's not always an active thing, quite often it's just casually accepted, if even noticed, but there are gatekeepers in families, people who decide whether someone belongs in or not.

                Beasts are adopted in, or perhaps married into by will of the Horror, the Primordial Family in this write up. By itself, that's okay, but it loses potency when it comes to the Crossover/Family aspect of the game. Why? (This is where I risk being an asshole, hear me out) Because the authority and sense of wholeness of a character who brings someone into the family is weakened when that person is adopted/married in.

                Now, that's a shitty, shitty thing to believe in in real world circumstances, that someone brought in that way will never have a truly weighty and substantive opinion as someone born into the family. It's bullshit...But there is still a sense of it, and that sense becomes more important for the Crossover thing than it ever would in real life. See, let's say I marry, creating a person here, Known Sciences, and have been accepted into the Science Family at large*. By all rights, I have a certain authority to allow someone into the Arts Family, particularly under my purview...but do I have the authority to bring someone into the Science's family simply because they conceded to let me in? Technically, the answer is Yes, but you can feel the iffyness around that subject. I am not actually a Science, I am a sort of honorary Science, granted access to the circle, but not the keys to let other people in.

                Now, like I said, that's bollocks in a real world society regardless of how squirmy it makes you feel. That said, it has weight in this kind of narrative, and it comes to one simple reason: Werewolves. Confused, I know, I'll explain. Werewolves bring other things into their family too, their pack, things they recognize as not being Uratha but as being Pack. Humans, Wolf-blooded, Spirits, GHosts, mages, vampires, everything of that sort. We have a family bring-inner of that sort. Beast makes a different claim-you are not part of the family because you have been invited in, you are part of the family because you were born into it, which as has been stated by some of the critics is a bold claim on a cosmological front. It has vast implications. Of course, it need not be true, but it need have some weight to feel possible. In this sense, having someone whose soul was replaced by a Horror is less effective at telling the supernatural that than someone whose soul was always an Astral Manifestation.

                I like the idea of a Beast being brought into the family, but I want their soul to be their own**. I like the idea of a new class of being who incubates that soul of a Beast until it's about awakened, leading them to a choice to accept their soul or consolidate it into a simpler soul(or maybe even a Heroic Soul), and who can mentor the Beast and act as an official gatekeeper and can really set the bar of "The Dark Mother aspires for the teaching of humanity through fear", but that doesn't have to happen-the Soul is connected to the Soul of the World and the Soul of Humanity, and being Devoured can impart that sort of stuff intrinsically. If you add in an incubator of sorts, that also allows for Beasts to have that expectation of teaching and still have a soul that isn't necessarily inclined to it, as it is young in the moment.

                I dunno, this is what I think. I feel like I can take the crossover family claim more seriously if the Beast has always had their soul be their Soul, rather than being consumed into the family.

                EDIT: Let me be clear, I can take everything else, but this is the one thing that makes me go "ERGH". I'm still planning on buying this game regardless, because all the things I love are still in there.

                *Man, what were they drinking when they made that decision?

                **This isn't to say that reincarnation/"I am the new incarnation of Fenrir***" can't happen, but those souls need to be awakened still.

                ***Oh hush, you hardcore Werewolf fans, there can be multiple Fenrirs.

                                I just read the revised intro. I'm feeling kind of... uneasy about it. Mostly, I don't like the channges very much; they feel a bit like they fell short of what they aimed for, or perhaps they merely fall shor of my expectations. I was very, very enthusiastic about the first draft, so perhaps, it's just a knee-jerk reation to seein something I liked change.
                                Still I'll try to explain what bothers me with the revision:

                                1) The Devouring:
                                -I dislike the fact that Beasts can just ask people to become one of them. I don't feel like iit mesh with the Legendary Monster theme. Dragons, Giants and Tentacled Abominations don't invite people to join the club.
                                -It now really feels like the Beasts are possessed, or claimed, or whatever, as if the Horror is an alien invader replacing the person the Beast was before, and worse! they do it with the pre-Beast permission. That just feels like a suicide, and for no reason.
                                -How does it work, really? Why does it need a Beast to have the future Begotten agree to it? What kind of monster wait for permssion before eating?

                                2) The Horror:
                                -They sound too much like body-snatchers.
                                - Are they ususally just floating around waiting for future Beasts?
                                -It would be cool if they were the progeny and agents of the Dark Mother, or something, but no origin is given.

                                3)The teaching:
                                -Why, in the name of Stephen King? Of all the things legendary monsters could have been tasked to do, why this? It forces Beasts to become abusers, catharsis or not, which was one of the things people disliked most about Beast even when they didn't actually have to scare or hurt people.
                                -What exactly are those lessons Beast are supposed to teach?
                                -It feels like it's trying to make Beasts into misunderstood antiheroes. For a moment I was scared they would glittter or something (Ok, not really, but come on).


                                What I like:

                                1) Heroes:
                                -They gain in depht, complexity and nuance. Not bad for such a short description!

                                Mostly, though, I hope I'm reading most of those changes wrong. I'll perhaps feel better about them later.

                                When will you rage?
