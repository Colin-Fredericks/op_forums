                I don't know if this is an official thing; but I'm thinking of playing it this way regardless:

                • the Hunter phenomenon is when a mortal gets exposed to the supernatural and doesn't shy away from it the way the Curse would normally compel her to do*, but instead fights back against it. While these guys are not Sleepwalkers, they have a little something extra that lets them be proactive in the face of the Curse.
                • a Hero is not merely the result of an Integrity 4 human being exposed to the Primordial Dream; it's the result of an Integrity 4 human who would have become a hunter if the supernatural encounter had been anything else encountering a Beast. That is, the Hero is someone who was already predisposed to strike back; but the combination of his mental instability (represented by his low Integrity) and whatever it is that causes Beasts to inspire Heroes — three separate factors that must all be met — is what turns him into a Hero. This puts more of the responsibility for being a Hero on the Hero's shoulder, because he is the one who decided to be proactive when confronted by the Beast.
                • I'm now toying with an idea for a Hunter “Conspiracy”† with an Endowment that mimics what Heroes can do, but without necessarily being limited to Beasts: for instance, these guys can place Anathema on anyone that Beasts would consider Kin. Being hunters, not Heroes, they're also not delusional about who and what they are — though on the downside, if they ever drop to Integrity 4 they automatically become Heroes, whether or not they've ever encountered a Beast. Actually, instead of creating a new Conspiracy, I might just upgrade Ashwood Abbey to Conspiracy status. Still debating it.

                The idea behind this third point is to provide an alternative to Heroes who are a response to the Beasts and who are still sane (and all the more dangerous for it), but who don't get to blame the Beast for what he has become. They still tend to be first-order jerks; but that's more of a cultural thing than a supernaturally imposed thing.


                * It has been confirmed by Dave that the Curse is a thing throughout the nWoD, not just in relation to Mage, and is largely responsible for why the supernatural remains in the shadows: most people who encounter it pointedly ignore it.
                † In quotes because I don't see them as being any more organized than Les Mysteres, or whatever the name of the Conspiracy from Spirit Slayers is.

                My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
