                If a Hero is Stalking you, they know you by name, physical description, or personal encounter. If they have been drawn to an area by your activity and you are not yet aware of their presence they are able to gather information about you by asking around, listening to rumors, leveraging followers, and so on.

                        I think it's fundamentally misunderstanding the point of Heroic Stalking if you have to use it to figure out what neighborhood a character lives in.

                        Again, a Hero does not progress to Stalking until they have a name, physical description, or personal observation to go on — Heroic Tracking is everything from the first ping to gathering information from locals and rumor, and Stalking only comes into play when they've gotten close enough to start confirming their target.

A Hero who is Stalking a Beast has a lead and is now doing the smart thing by feeling out their target instead of immediately leaping to open conflict with a powerful foe.

                                Originally posted by Neos01 View Post
                                But then... why doesn t peaceful or lazy Beasts feeds only by letting their horrors inflicting nightmares?
                                Because, for one thing, "lazy" falls on the other side of the Satiety scale from "Horror is feeding on its own" and "peaceful" is not going to easily describe a character who saw the true shape of their soul and decided "nightmare monster inflicted upon the world" was a thing they could accept being and expanded their Lair to the point where their victims aren't being drawn purely from within a mile or two of where they sleep.
                                If they are careful going to ravenous is very unlikely,
                                What "careful" is there to be in doing nothing to feed your Horror yourself?

                                If you only feed through Inflicting Nightmares, you're going to fail rolls eventually and drop to Ravenous — or worse, eat the wrong person's soul and fall to Slumbering — and in the meantime you're incentivized to use your more obvious powers and feed your Hunger in petty ways.

                                and even when they gain an eceptional success the only clue that the hero could gain is the visual appearance of the horror,
                                This is a hell of an assumption to make. A nightmare is more than just the monster that shows up at the end.

                                described by a victim who doesn't know the beast,
                                This is also a hell of an assumption to make. It feeds on a random available person within range, jumping to the first person within range if they're not available, and the examples suggest "random" still favors people relevant to the story by their acquaintance with the Beast. Either you're hitting someone you know or you're creating a series of incidents that narrow the field to where you sleep at night.

                                and nobody else could give a clue about a beast who doesn t feed openly.
                                Another hell of an assumption. Beasts have an Anchor built around being scary in a particular way, interact with strange people and real estate, and benefit from being Known to a moderate degree, including deliberately raising their Lair rating. It may take a little longer, but the process of investigation will eventually develop a lead, because this is a storytelling game where this plot is introduced at the Storyteller's discretion and players are presumably playing Beasts because they wanted to play Beasts.

                                Also, the book says that Beasts cannot run this kind of lifestyle for fear of Hero retributions, but with those mechanics and the Beast living even in a small city for a hero it would be very hard to gain the details of the beast necessary to progress to stalking.
                                Again, Beasts can't run the kind of lifestyle where they set their Horror on human enemies because there tends to be a connection to follow for any Hero who comes knocking.


                                The basic social contract of "we agree to play the damn game" is in effect — if you're playing Beast and you're running a character who ignores the entire set of mechanics around Satiety as a roleplaying guide and otherwise runs their character as a human with a Lair that they expand but barely use and an unruly pet heart-monster that they don't want to feed, it should be obvious that there will be problems that force the Storyteller to rely on characters' offscreen awareness of your unconscious behaviors to lead a Hero to the alarm you're setting off in the Primordial Dream by starving yourself.

                                This is not a simulation. Attracting a Hero brings a threat to the area that makes feeding yourself more dangerous because they're in the area and looking for you. You can skip town or spend a couple weeks crashing on your broodmate's couch or take your chances gambling on repeat feedings or tap into the hive to disperse the signal with Willpower, but it's become a thing you have to deal with until the Hero's gone. That's the point of the mechanic: The Hero is in town and that makes things marginally more difficult for you.
