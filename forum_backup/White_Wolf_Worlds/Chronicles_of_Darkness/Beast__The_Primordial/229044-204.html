                                                    Originally posted by torete View Post

                                                    Umm, except, the whole point of VtR is acknowledging that, and trying to preserve a modicum of humanity. A werewolf's point is precisely not to be a mindless monster, and the mage thing? Their integritystat is called 'wisdom,' and playing sorcerer-kings is both a wisdom sin and people like GotV will sic you.

                                                    All the games, the point is more 'not being a monster,' while a pretty big premise of Beast is, you know, being one and being proud of it, to the point of making Heroes, jerkass with a point, to be acceptable targets.
                                                    Not quite. It is more " be in control of your monsterousness" in most cases, or "avoid being too extreme" for most lines. The game lines split between those that hate what they are and those that embrace it.

                                                    Mages, Geists, Demons, and Werewolves all embrace their supernatural role, and look in askance at those who try to deny what they are (Banishers, Integrators, etc). Their focus is on using their power and abilities with insight, and avoiding going off the deep end. For example, per Dave, the new Wisdom system can have a Seer who is high Wisdom while manipulating the world for selfish ends, while the selfless Arrow named Harry Dresden is teetering at the low end: their ability to control and efficiently use their abilities define them, not their morality. I put Demons here because they don't even think of themselves as humans; they only act as such because the GM will come down on them like a ton of bricks if they are too blatant.

                                                    Then you have the "But I don't want to be a monster" groups, who try to stave it off (usually): vampires and prometheans. And even there there is a split, where one is trying to hold on to what thry lost and the other finding what they can become. These games are the closest (besides Hunter) on being focused on "morality" and not being the monster, as the Vampires realize they are damned and fear losing themselves to their Beast, while Prometheans want to be a human and thus try to emulate them.

                                                    Changelings and hunters are the neutral parties. Hunters are human (mostly), so they come at this differently. For them, it is determining how far they are willing to go and how much they are willing to become what they hunt. Morality is going to come into play here. Not 1e capital M morality, but more the general elements of human ideas about what is right and wrong, and what the Hunter can personally tolerate. Changelings run the gamut from self hatred and fully embracing what they now are, and there is no consistent cultural association one way. I mean, when you sometimes aren't even sure if you escaped the Fae, their focus is more on recovering from their trauma and finding what empowers them (like actual abuse victims).

                                                    And mummies... mummies are wierd and complex. They are monsters, no question. They, as they regain Memories, become less monsterous and against what they are, but not because they view themselves as monsters per se, but because they realize the nature of the cycle and the fact that they are trapped. Some are okay with this, accepting their role under the Judges, while others seek freedom through Aptothesis. And yet others turn towards forgetting everything and going back to being automatons.

                                                    My point is that Beast falls into the same category as the first. In fact, their mechanic is similar to Harmony or Synergy, in that it is more of a representation of you balance between two extremes that each have merit. They aren't exceptions, but fall in with a half of the gamelines, where the question isn't about not being a monster (it is too late to avoid that) but what type of monster you want to be. Do you want to be:
                                                    • Smaug: the proud and selfish force of destruction, who has no doubts about his superiority and right to be what he is.
                                                    • Slenderman: a quiet intelligent force that stalks and kills it's pray without a trace, but doesn't seem to revel in what it is. It just does what it does.
                                                    • the Kingpin (Netflix Daredevil): a powerful force who sees himself as beneficial and his evil as necessary ro allow a better world, bur who deep down is a disturbed individual
                                                    • Batman: a force that knows they can never be truly healed, turning theor darkness into a means for good whose discipline prevents them from going to far
                                                    • Or someone else?
                                                    Cause while Beasts are monsters and accept this, they still have the power to define what type of monster they will be

                                                        Well, yeah, at their core monsters are all manifestations of this fear of the "others". For as long as humans existed we've had this "us vs them" mentality. It worked in small tight-knit tribes, where anyone you didn't know, anyone "different" was likely from a rival tribe and therefore probably competition for scarce resources at the very least, and therefore "the enemy". As our society grew this fear remained, with nothing to attach it to, so we started to invent our own.

                                                        While the other monsters are "them", I see beasts as the fear itself. Fear of the unknown. It festers beneath the surface, grows fangs and claws. The collective unconsciousness thought "here there be dragons" and lo and behold, there they sprang up. They're not really vilification incarnate, not originally, but that's how the fear that birthed them manifests most prominently in modern times.
