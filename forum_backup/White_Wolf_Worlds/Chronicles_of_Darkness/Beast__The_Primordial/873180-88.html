    Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
    How does Shadow Stalker work? What can you do besides inflict Nightmares on your host? Could you talk to the Host?
    You're specifically silent, so not likely — the main advantage is that, in contrast to Shadowed Soul, you're mobile without abandoning the ruse. It's a stalking tool, which means it's best used to gather information while going undetected.

    When imposing Mirages, Murmurs, an the like, does the Beast get to chose what form those take?
    They should probably fit the idiom of the Beast's Horror, Lair, or Legend, but yeah, that tracks; the main mechanical effect of those Traits is their penalizing of subsets of Perception roll and otherwise distracting the victims.

    How effective are the non-physical Lair Traits (Bad Angles, Isolated, Mirages, Murmurs) at defending the Lair?
    That depends entirely on how you're using the Lair. Like most predators, the Lair's best defense is to not be found before it can do its work.

    Would allowing the Horror to use Nightmares on it's own be a decent merit?
    To what end?

    Similarly, would a merit allowing to use of Nightmares on someone who is in a area resonant with the Beast's Lair, without looking them in the eye or touching them be fine?
    That seems more in line with a secondary Nightmare like The Walls Have Eyes or You Can't Wake Up or You Are Infected than a Merit.

    I saw while browsing that someone made a Atavism about being a telepathic relay, or being a hive mind in other words. The concept quite charmed me, but it was noted that the concept might work better as a Nightmare. What's your opinion on the matter?
    The Atavism in question is almost entirely mental effects that alter other people; the alternate take is a little closer to the scope of Atavisms as a mostly sensory effect, but…

    Let's be plain: the Astral's primary demonstrations of shared consciousness aren't especially hivemind-y, and the more hivemind-y section of the Astral is the one that resonates better with Beasts who favor Nightmares over Atavism — the gestalt psyches of the Mother's Land can't stop their component ideas from acting according to their own natures, and Dreamborn from the Bright Dream either possess a single person or exert their influence across a bunch of people in less direct fashion.

    Atavisms are tools the Horror uses to fulfill its Hunger, and most of those rely on a separation between Self and Other for the shock; losing oneself is a more cerebral sort of fear.

                    Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
                    What is important for resonance to the Isolated Lair Trait? Is it the physical act of being alone, or is the feeling of Isolation sufficient? (ie can I use Nightmares to create resonance?)
                    The latter, as a quality of the location — you can't use Nightmares to impose the resonance, because Nightmares are an effect on a person instead of a place.

                    What does Immunity to Isolation entail? Never feeling Lonely again?
                    You can hang out in places that feel Isolating without it making you feel Isolated, and supernatural attempts to apply the effect get a +2 to resist (or the opportunity to Clash, in unresisted cases). By a similar token, immunity to Bad Angles would mean that those forms of architecture don't get into your head like they do everybody else.

                    Similarly, what is important for resonance to Murmurs? Is the whispers of a crowd enough, or can the hallucination of whispers work?
                    Lair Traits are environmental qualities. Anything that isn't a quality of the environment (temporary or otherwise) accessible to the senses of more than one materially-aware person isn't going to provide resonance.

                    Does imposing Bottomless in the Waking World drop people and stuff into the Lair?
                    Nope. Bottomless makes it so that if you lose your footing upon entering Chamber with it, you need to take two points of lethal damage to stop your fall, and then any Burrows you could take out of the Chamber from there go to a random Chamber. Imposing it outside of the Lair simply doesn't have any mechanical effect, because there's no Chamber people are entering to trigger the roll to see if they lose their footing.

                    Does Immunity to Bottomless grant Immunity to Fall Damage, or does it just allow a Beast to never fall off a Ledge if they don't want to?
                    Falling damage is different from the damage you take to stop a fall in a Bottomless location — if you were actually falling any length reasonable to describe as "forever" you'd take a lot more than two points of lethal damage. If you have the Trait, you can enter other Bottomless Chambers without having to roll to avoid falling.

                    It's worth remembering that some Lair Traits, especially Major Lair Traits or the more unusual ones from the Player's Guide, exist more as internal hazards and modifiers than as sources of Environmental Immunity or things to impose on the material world generally — your Hoard isn't going to draw your Horror one Chamber closer to anything if you're just applying the Trait without opening a Primordial Pathway for minutes at a time.

                                                        So, anyway to not go in a too long tangent, what other themes could I explore for the Inguma when coming up with goodies for them?
                                                        The Inguma are fear of the stranger, paranoia, monsters who specifically look like — or can temporarily pass for — human. Their published Atavisms allow them to be inscrutable and make themselves look like familiar faces or create things that are indistinguishable from other people apart from their loyalty to the Beast. Their published Birthrights revolve around blending into a crowd and gaslighting victims with unretrievable messages and sudden appearances and disappearances to fixate on. Their perspective of the Dark Mother is an alien thing apart from the world, coordinating her Children through a pane of glass.

                                                        Their foundational Nightmare originates with the first cities. Where the Namtaru shock through the unclean and the naturally unsettling — scaly things, sickly things, slimy things, things with too many legs — the Inguma reveal themselves through incongruous features. Where the Lurkers are built on fear of the unseen, the Outsiders come from the sense that one of these things is not like the others. They're the terror of a spot-the-difference picture and the opening that creates for their Hunger.

                                                        Go for unpleasant surprises. Be a suspicious individual. Make Atavisms that let the Beast infiltrate, impersonate, misdirect, and otherwise walk among us. Hide damning evidence, reveal nasty complications, keep them on their toes without tipping your hand entirely.
