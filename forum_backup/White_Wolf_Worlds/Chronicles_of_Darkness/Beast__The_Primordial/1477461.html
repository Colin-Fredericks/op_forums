    The Dream of Dreams-What Do You Want from the Dreaming/the Astral?

    03-26-2022, 03:07 PM
    ″‘So I wasn’t dreaming, after all,’ she said to herself, ‘unless – unless we’re all part of the same dream. Only I do hope it’s my dream, and not the Red King’s! I don’t like belonging to another person’s dream,’ she went on in a rather complaining tone: ‘I’ve a great mind to go and wake him, and see what happens!‘”
    -Lewis Carroll, Through the Looking Glass

    I've been meaning to start this conversation for a little bit now, but I didn't want to start a conversation without contributing to it as a start. But life is busy, and I don't want to delay kicking the hornet's nest and possibly never do it because of other obligations, so I will try and provide my takes later on, but for now:

    One of the biggest things that was lacking in the Beast's core was a actual inclusion and thorough exploration of the Astral, which I'm going to collectively call the Dreaming for the purposes of this Beast conversation, and what it meant for Beast as a game on it's own terms. The Player's Guide covered the basics of it in a pretty satisfactory way, but that does leave an open door into what more could be done or refigured.

    So, with that in mind, what do you want to see done with the Dreaming in regards to Beast going forward (both in normal and weird ways), how do you want it to factor into the games Beasts can play/tell, and why do you want those things?

                  I feel like Beast is sort of where Lost was in 1e regarding this: if you read all the books you have a good grasps of the metaphysical layout of things, a few story hooks, but not necessarily the interactivity and goals needed to really drive players to want to make exploration of this part of the game an active part of the story. There's enough meat on the bones that if your group is predisposed to wanted to explore the Dreaming, you know what's there and the basics, but I've never seen a Beast player that wasn't coming in interested in the Dreaming get hooked by that material as a reason to go dream hopping at some point (I will admit, that even applies to me a bit).

                  So I think some stronger integrations into the core gameplay loops might be in order. The two big "rewards" for diving into the Dreaming are things you can do without going there. You can claim Chambers (assuming you have slots for more, and ones there don't inherently offer things you can't get locally), and you can perform minor acts of influence on human populations (at lot of the examples are things Beasts inclined towards social engineering on that level can probably get done with their Atavisms and/or judicious use of Nightmares as it is).

                  I feel like the PG is actually a bit backwards here. Instead of being an alternate path to goals, the Dreaming should be a way to plant seeds; esp. with the idea of refocusing the game as a whole towards building up your character's myth. Instead of finding Chambers or being able to poison a town against a beloved local figure temporarily, it fees like Beasts that master the Dreaming should be setting up things: creating and nurturing urban legends that can then be used to empower Chamber creations, starting rumors about someone that isolates them and weakens them to the Nightmares you're going to inflict on them, being able to engage in a loop of finding the impact of your actions on a community in the Dreaming and then fine-tuning it over and over to write your legend just the way you want it.

                              Well, this is a topic near to my heart, since I believe that the Begotten’s easy access to the Dreaming would be just as impactful as the Shadow, or the Underworld is on the Forsaken and Bound, if not more so. The Dreaming covers everything from specific dreams, to the dreams of humanity at large, and the very dreams of the world, and the Begotten have amazing access to all of it. Beasts can watch as simple words rip apart and rearrange the Dreaming, which is the kind of thing that makes one sit up and pay attention.

                              In no particular order or relevance;

                              A look at the exploration, and usage of personal nightmares. Beasts already can inflict bad dreams with a touch, what happens when one is actually inside someone’s dreams? Why would ever the Begotten chose to do so, since personal dreams are probably one of the most stifling places to be for a Beast. (Being extremely uncomfortable in a dream just conjecture, but dreams usually are extremely narrative, and someone particularly sensitive to narratives probably won’t appreciate being forcefully made into a symbol different from what they are.) If nothing else, it seems like a good place to feed and to study new Nightmares. (Maybe it uses a system like Oneiromancy from Changeling, with a Beast take on it of course) (I would like the Nightmare Shamans to truck with personal nightmares as much as Humanity’s collective nightmares. Plus, dreams are just interesting)

                              Some clear description on how feeding from the Dreaming works and messes with the waking world might be nice. And visa versa.

                              A realm representing Heroism that is constantly attempting to wage war on the Primordial Dream. I believe this idea has come up before, but it’s a good one. (Kinda something that feels like that should already be there, to be honest.)

                              I like the idea of the attention of a Hero also attracting the attention of minor raiding parties of Heroic Dreamborn to harry the Horror and the Lair, and maybe act as a early warning system for heroic tracking. All without the Hero knowing a thing, of course. (Heroes don’t seem that strong, but I think it’s kind thematic that they cause way more trouble than they understand. Plus it’s a way of even benevolent Heroes causing the Begotten some trouble.)

                              Maybe a mention of a Collector or team of Collectors who steal stuff from the Oneiros…like maybe stealing someone’s distorted desires. (Someone might have gained a new crush on a game while waiting for Elden Ring. Maybe.)

                              and I love what everyone has described so far.

                                      Talking again about the Astral continuum: There's a few different things from the Astral pre-dating Beasts that the Begotten share conceptual ground with, such as…

                                      • Nightmare creatures from the Oneiros, who inhabit nightmare scenes with no scripted temporal endpoint and instead torment anyone who enters their territory until they escape.
                                      • Chimerical beasts from the Temenos (described in Secrets of the Ruined Temple) — forgotten gods and terrifying monsters incarnating across the bridge of a human soul.
                                      • Naturae from the Anima Mundi, who are the rulers of the lands whose ecosystems they represent as well as those lands themselves — and their component creatures can turn against them.

                                      Comparatively rare as the Begotten are, it might be good to reexamine some of these in the context of Kinship and/or Hunger.

                                                  Building on what Heavy Arms said, Beasts have a particularly direct relationship with the concept of narrative as something with real metaphysical weight, and I want to see more exploration of their unusual status as stories in the active process of telling themselves. Beasts may no longer be human, but the residual humanity that they do have put them in the unusual position of at least theoretically being able to consider what their story looks like from an outside perspective, what they want their story to be, and how they might bridge the gap.

                                                  If Beast is a game about the story you tell about yourself against the story the world wants to tell about you then the various layers of the Dreaming are a battlefield where canny Beasts should be able to tilt the narrative. I want to see wordcount devoted to how a Beast might... recontextualize themselves. How to not simply grow your legend, but shape it. How you choose to feed, how the broader community understands the legend of a monster in its midst, that should shape how the horror grows and evolves.

                                                  ...Hm, having typed the above I've finally made a connection that's been eluding me. The introduction to atavisms discusses how Horrors learn new ones, and cites contact with other Beasts as the most common way; The dragon learns to swim from the kraken, and so on. We're told Horrors change as they learn new atavisms, that like dreams they are malleable, but we aren't given other examples of how this happens and there isn't much discussion of this potential for dramatic alteration outside the context of learning new atavisms. There's a mention elsewhere that Hunger can change in extreme circumstances but... It feels like there are pieces pointing toward the idea that a sufficiently motivated Beast could, over time, eventually grow their Horror into something substantially different from what it started as and mucking about in the Dreaming seems like the likeliest path to doing that.

                                                          Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                                          Some examination of how Dreamborn interact between realms and layers of the Dreams — whether that's culture clash literalizing itself in the Temenos or Oneiroi being subverted by ideas from the wider world or the beastly archetypes of the Mother's Land reflecting themselves into the collective human unconscious, an examination of the Astral in the Begotten's more continuum-based view would help with its presentation as an otherworld.

                                                          Long time ago I mentioned something like this, and am happy to see you want it too.

                                                          In a way completely different from the Shadow or Awakened realms, I feel the Astral should be like a monstrous kingdom of ideas. This isn't the brutish and wild hunts of the Shadow, but a sprawling territory of fiefs and lairs filled with beings seeking to prove the superiority of the ideological concepts they represent. They effect the physical world in a subtle way unlike the shadow. The Astral effects the physical in such a way where it's mindsets that are effected. Over time, people find themselves not trusting democracy due to the actions of a dreamborn representing the fear of mob rule the populace developed from riots earlier that year, or a serial killer finds that, due to how he is being perceived and the developing mythology around him, he feels a certain power coursing within him (which is actually the very dreamborn representing him seeking to claim him)

                                                          And the reverse is true. The Astral finds many new citizens coming from the thoughts and dreams of the physical. Phobias, aspirations, pop culture figures and all kinds find life here, and seek to prove themselves fit for rule to stay in the minds and hearts of the populace (whether that populace be the mind of the very person who conceived them, or the masses).

                                                          Very important that they aren't the ideals the supernal represent, or the predatory and alien spirits that seek only to get essence. These are living ideas that represent not absolute truths or animistic concepts that only understand the next meal, but biases and wants and dreams and fears born DIRECTLY from Humanity. They wish to rule conquer the dream realms they inhabit so that they may be the primary force driving individual's (or masses of individuals). And for a Beast, this means they have a Kingdom of their own through their Lairs, so that they may prove themselves as worthy to exist as they self actualize themselves in the mind and soul of humanity.

                                                          Until, of course, a Hero comes along to prove that, in truth, they are a false being who deserves to be forgotten
