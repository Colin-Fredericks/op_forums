                            They really don't seem like psychopaths or even textbook abusers, though narcissistic certainly fits. The very act of being a Hero seems like they're taking responsibility for their lives, in the face of the threat of the Beast.

                            One can't even be a Hero without suffering several breaking points, meaning they're usually witnesses or victims of horrible trauma. The Collector's Hero seems like a Beast with a Hunger for Punishment. The Predator's Hero is someone who refuses to suffer any more abuse, the Tyrant's refuses to 'know their place.' The Nemeses' and Ravager's Heroes seem more selfishly malign.

                            Someone said earlier in this thread that the book is mostly from the point of view of the Beasts, and so it is biased. I'm not sure if that's true, but it seems to be, with the constant talking down of Heroes. But considering the soul of Heroes have been overwhelmed by the Beast, it being no more their fault than the Beast becoming a Beast, and the only way to stop it from happening is killing Beasts, as well as the fact that they can be 'cured' and go on to become a hunter or maybe just live a normal life by getting more than 4 Integrity, they're not exactly unsympathetic.

                                                    Originally posted by Leliel View Post

                                                    Honestly, I suspect the writers were in kind of an internal war. On the one hand, you have Matt's utter hatred of egotists and bigots of all stripes (I assume it's him writing the Storytelling chapter given his posts on RPGnet), but on the other, you have whoever recognized that a Hero got like that by being a fundamentally broken person who often was a direct victim by a callous or reckless Beast.

                                                    So yeah, I suspect there may have been a bit of dissonance over the writing.
                                                    I actually wrote the Storytelling chapter.

                                                    Honestly? The way I see it, Heroes aren't inherently, black or white bad. Sometimes they're totally in the right. In my experience with playing Beast so far, the problem with Heroes isn't that they're wrong and bad and whatever, it's that they're doing it for the wrong reasons. They're going after Beasts "because they're monsters", not because of their individual behaviours. They're doing it for what ultimately amounts to ideology, which is right the same way a broken clock is. Does everyone that does bad things deserve to be slain? No. Heroes are not creatures of compromise and nuance, and that's their utter failing.

                                                    This is my opinion, mind you. I wrote Storytelling. I didn't write the actual text on Heroes. But that's the ethos I took with me when I was writing how to make their actions work within the narrative.
