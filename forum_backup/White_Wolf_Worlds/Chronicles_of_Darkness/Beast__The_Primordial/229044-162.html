            Concerning the Begotten's relation to the Unchained, I notice that the extent to which Beasts are driven to be around people — leveraging a Family Dinner requires the Beast be physically present, there's an overt range limit on who a hungry Soul afflicts with its nightmares, and generally speaking Beasts are incentivized to be imposing in both senses of the word — directly contrasts with demonic detachment.

            By the by, making sure I've got the Primordial Pathways rules straight:

            You can spend 1 Willpower and roll Wits + Resolve as though imposing Lair Traits, in which case the roll is subject to the same penalties for spiritual, narrative, or descriptive similarity, as well as the set of modifiers based on where in the cosmology you're attempting to reach your Soul from, unless you you're going to the seed of one of your Chambers or an exact facsimile thereof. Satiety Conditions further modify this roll.

            (You can grant someone else this ability to someone else via Be My Guest by spending a point of Satiety for each visitor so authorized.)

            This also works for attempting to create a Primordial Pathway out of your Lair, though going out blind imposes a hefty penalty to the roll on top of the usual difficulties with going anywhere other than the Temenos or another Lair.

            You can also leverage an existing supernatural gateway to lead to your Lair by spending 1 Willpower and rolling the Skeleton Key pool of Resolve + Occult + Lair vs the associated Supernatural Tolerance of a given gate.

            That about right?

                To clarify my position on Heroes: I'm not saying that Heroes should be, well, the heroes; I get that the game is intended to subvert the traditional hero/beast narrative. But I think that a lot of the condemnation of the Heroes involves overcompensating and needlessly characterizing them as cardboard cut-out villains for fear that if you don't completely and utterly discredit them, they'll steal the show. To me, that's every bit as flat as the traditional monster slayer narrative that this game is intended to subvert.

                To clarify my position on Beasts: the answer to “it's not their fault they were born that way” is “tell that to their victims”. Sir or madam: I'm sorry you were attacked or tortured by a Beast; but you have to understand that she had to hurt someone in order to continue living; if it wasn't you, it would have been someone else.

                In a way, it's a bit like the plight of a Promethean; and I'd say that it's a tossup as to whose existence sucks more of the two, save only for one thing: Prometheans have a way out.

                                                    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                                    To clarify my position on Beasts: the answer to “it's not their fault they were born that way” is “tell that to their victims”. Sir or madam: I'm sorry you were attacked or tortured by a Beast; but you have to understand that she had to hurt someone in order to continue living; if it wasn't you, it would have been someone else.
                                                    After years of vampires draining people of their blood, werewolves forcibly terraforming both the physical and spiritual world, and changelings engineering extremely emotional scenarios for Glamour, how am I supposed to find this a drastic change?

                                                    But putting that aside, this comes back to my constant refrain about this game: this is about the cycle of violence. It literally is the tale as old as time, the earliest narrative filled with tension and drama. Everyone has needs, and when these needs are in opposition to one another,conflict emerges. Every story Beast's live draws from the early days of survival, when surviving another day often meant someone having to suffer in kind. Even if it isn't as drastic this day, don't we have whole wars over needs? Is it too far fetched to have a game that acts as a metaphor for how the simple fact that, as creatures of need, we will inevitably have conflict over them?

                                                    Because yes, I sympathize for the victims. I don't endorse the Beast who just feeds. I expect Beasts to wrestle with a correct way to eat. But I'm also not going to be the one who tells a Child that he can't eat. I sympathize with the Beasts as well. I can do both. I can also think that both sides are assholes, Heroes for reducing things to violence and Beasts for....well, objectively I don't have a good reason too(which may be the problem), but I can still hate them for having to do what they have to do even as I sympathize with them. It's a shit situation that feeds itself.

                                                    But/And Speaking as a story? That's one of the best recipes for a story. There's a reason I rather like this game, at least as much as I like Hunter, Werewolf, and Geist.
