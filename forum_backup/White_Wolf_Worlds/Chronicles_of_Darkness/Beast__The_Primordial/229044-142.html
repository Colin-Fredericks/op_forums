        Originally posted by YeOfLittleFaith View Post
        That there's no easy or definite answer to the Hero-Beast dilemma is genuinely one of the most interesting things in the game, far as I'm concerned.

        Heroes are undoubtedly victims. When confronted with the nightmare that is the Beast, their broken ego latches on to the notion that the Begotten is an absolute monster, and that they alone know the truth. They compose themselves through the idea that it's their responsibility to end the Beast. Why else would they know? Why else would their heart scream so that the Begotten are enemies of mankind? This is more than just responsibility - it's their role. They know because they are meant to destroy the monster. And that means they're the hero, with everything that entitles them to.

        It's not a willful reaction. It's one born of desperation, an unconscious measure on the part of would-be-Heroes to not collapse into themselves. Their already shaken life sublimes into an enemy of Beasts. They are driven by these ideas, husks moved by their assumed righteousness against the one who made them this way. Against this imperative taken to support their existence after a traumatic experience, nothing else matters.

        But that doesn't make them right by default. Every living being in the world takes from elsewhere in order to survive. Humans take not only from the world, but from each other, often not even for survival, but just because it pleases them; to satisfy their desires. Arguably, Beast take more meaningfully and deeply than many humans do, but they at least can say they do it to survive. Of course, this doesn't always make them right by principle either.

        Heroes are irredeemably tragic figures despite their individual misgivings and their universal willingness to walk over everything to destroy the Beast, because they have no real escape from the cycle they are pulled into. But the bane of a Hero, what might antagonize even the noblest of Beasts against them, is that they don't even realize they're all puppets, tangled in strings. A Hero is as much of a slave as the Beast, if not more, to the perpetual narrative of the monster and the savior. But they do not, cannot recognize their position in this system; they make themselves believe it is about them, that they own this story. In the eyes of Beasts, they are ignorant - maybe offensively so, on a deep existential level that any person who truly lives should be able to understand. They accept the cycle and push it forward of their own vollition, in their weakness nothing more than prisoners seeking to drag everyone else down with them, unable to even struggle to be more.

        You can't really fault Beasts for feeling that way. Of course, you can't really fault Heroes for not having a conscious choice in the matter either. But the bottom line is, being a Beast doesn't make one inherently wrong, and being a Hero doesn't make one inherently justified; ultimately, it's understandable that they are enemies. It's hardly a fair situation for anyone involved. And it is understandable the Children could think they have the worst side of the deal and hate their counterparts for their perspective on the Hero Cycle. There is some bliss to be found in ignorance, but self-awareness only makes the inevitable more painful.

        And yet, they would rather know and curse their destiny than mindlessly go along with it.
        I love everything about this, it's exactly what I was thinking. Also it's the very reason why beast is not a game for me. Oh I have put money in the kickstarter because I find the idea fascinating and I will use the material in my other games but I will not play it. It's a tragic story about two beings who cannot fight against their nature and role in life, they are doomed to reenact it over and over again till the eventual tragic ending. The hero is a deluded psychopath and the beast just rolls its eyes because on an instinctual level it knows, it thinks "Ok we both know this dance, let's just get this over with". Such lack of freedom and enslavement to fate is completely anathema to me, it's unfair and I can't stand it. However I want to make it clear that this is just my personal preference, I don't find the idea wrong per se. It's just a different perspective, a different way to tell stories. Just not the kind of stories I like.

                I love Yolf and I cannot lie.

                But yeah, for me, the Beast-Hero conflict is actually a very broad one that covers a black and white moral struggle all the way down to a murky dingy grey. Are Heroes assholes who deserve to be put down before they do more harm than you do? Then play it that way. Is there a deep examination of fault that reveals all the things that explore humanity's failings and imperfection in the face of conflict? Play it that way.

                It helps that Family Dinner and the crossover aspect gives Beasts a dial on the direction. A Beast in crossover settings has better odds of controlling their hunger than one who isn't.

                It's a game that meditates a lot about the cycle of violence, about the needs and wants that fuel them, and asks questions about if there's a time and reason to these things. We all want things, all want good positions in life and justice in the world.We all come across people who make us angry, people we think deserve to be taken down a peg, whether that person be a roommate who pisses us off or a political figure who seems intent on ruining the way things ought to be. At what point should those be pursued and when should we let up? And at what point do we trangress others enough to warrant the same question? This game asks a lot of interesting questions about the lot of it.

                It helps that it's also non-normative with it's conclusions. Using Incarnates as the example, the way out is to break the cycle by completely dominating it. A lot of people take this view to problems in life, and you have to ask, because you play as the Beast, are they wrong? It's not an easy game, but that's what I like about it.

                When I got with the playtest materials, I really asked how anyone could get around the Hungers, because they seem to demand a certain evil. When I realized that was sort of the point, that was when I came to love this game.
