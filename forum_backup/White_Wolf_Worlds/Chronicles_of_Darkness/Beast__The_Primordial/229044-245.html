                            Beasts are outsiders. This is intentional. They are not like humans. To behave as the humans around them do would be wrong, would feel wrong, for them. There's a purposeful thematic resonance here.

                            But the fear of the Other is one of our oldest, strongest fears as a species. It makes sense that our nightmares would look like trans people, or members of ethnic or racial or religious groups we see as outsiders. We fear these people, as a culture! They are the out group! Our Heroes, on the other hand, are usually righteous defenders of our tribe, whatever tribe we identify with.

                            But you shouldn't identify beasts as trans kids, or women, or illegal immigrants, or whatever human out group you see them as resembling. Their only similarity to these "alien" groups is that they are "not like us."

                            If you are a trans person, the Other that your tribe fears is a neonazi shithead, or a conservative Christian doctor who wants to "cure" you, or harsh parental figures who deny your existence or put you out on the street.

                            If you are a black kid, you fear teachers who talk down at you, or police, or scared white folk with guns.

                            If you're a Jew, the Beast is gonna stalk your dreams in an SS uniform. If you're a parent, there's a nightmare out there that preys on kids. Sure, if you're from a certain demographic the Beast is gonna be a transgender atheist communist dragon with dreadlocks and a fondness for hooking your kids on drugs, but this is missing the point.

                            You- yes you, real world human people- shouldn't take the Beast as too much of an analogy for oppressed groups. They're the Other, and there's some overlap there, but restricting living avatars of human nightmares to the Other with which you know and identify is a mistake. Serial killers are a Others. Cannibals are a Others. Abusers are Others. Violent zealots are Others. Cult leaders and drug dealers and thieves and rapists and kidnappers are Others.

                            Beasts have as much in common with the Others you don't like as the ones you do. Maybe more! They are monsters, after all!

                            The most important thing to me about Beasts is that they should be scary. Stating up my first Beast character according to the first rules we got, I had trouble sleeping that night! That's a good thing! (and she was a bloody unicorn, it's not like I was trying for a horror show here!)

                            The idea that some thing inhuman and monstrous could be walking around thinking it's a child, like some horrible human cuckoo? That's powerful. Don't look at that story and decide it's saying that gay people are monsters walking around in human skins! I mean, the themes of hate are there, but that's just because all our fears and hatreds play out the same way. Vampires are gay people. Werewolves are people with mental illnesses. The God Machine is the big scary guv-mint. All monsters are allegories one way or another, but it's important not to let the allegory take over the monster story unless you want an allegorical story.

                            I don't want the things we're afraid of (and maybe shouldn't be) to get in the way of my playing as Fear Itself.

                            So I'm making God-Kicking Boot, an Exalted webcomic, now. Updates on Sundays. Full-color, mediocre but slowly improving art. It's a thing.

                                    In the interests of keeping the thread above a certain threshold of discussion enjoyability…

                                    Anyone else hoping the fluff for how the Unchained interact with the Begotten gets a little less mutually hands-off?

                                    After all, we're talking about a species whose sole dead zone of metaphysical connection is the very thing demons are trying to hide from, with a forbidding mobile pocket realm inhabited by a terrifying guardian and capable of reaching some of the most hostile places in the universe with comparative ease and the ability to liaise with some of their more powerful denizens on nominally friendly terms. For a lot of constructivist Tempters, that seems like it'd be Hell in all but name.

                                    Meanwhile, demons may not have much to offer in the way of worldly resources to the Begotten, but this is a group whose toolkit can easily produce gateways to other worlds, and whose innate capabilities include a couple of the main holes in the Beastly powerset vis-a-vis translation and outfitting; Beasts are the first supernatural gameline since Changeling to lack an innate linguistic workaround and gadgets help a fair bit to offset the potential complications of being Gorged or Slumbering.

                                                        I finally read the revised introduction. I would have read it sooner, but keeping up with this thread is work enough.

                                                        It just seems kind of scattered, but here are a couple of thoughts.

                                                        -The new reason for being seems to remove or lesson the kinship to other supernats. You seem less like a cousin or long lost sibling and more like a Teaching Assistant/Work Inspector who comes to tell you you're not doing it right.
                                                        -If the Beast in question chose to become what they, then why would they care about "Society tells them that monsters are villains" exactly? They knew what they were getting into when they agreed to get nibbled and even had "Gratitude" when faced with the monster who had been stalking it.
                                                        -The Begotten's conflictedness about their monstrous natures/hunger and their role to Teach with Fear seems off, because again they chose this.
                                                        -Are the Children Claimed, Fear Sin-Eaters, or not even the original person to begin with? After reading about the devouring I got a Buffyverse vampire vibe, "You have to remember that when you see him, you're not looking at your friend. You're looking at the thing that killed him." Are they one? Are they two? A specific statement on this matter is needed. All of the other supernats have it.

                                                        -Overall I am not keen on the Beast no longer being born beasts. It goes against the original stated premise of the game. A suggestion that no one asked for: Make the Homecoming the default and keep the Devouring for those who are adopted or marry into the Family, allowing for non standard beasts. It allows for more options and story ideas and even Beast on Beast conflict "You're not my real Brother" or "Hello, Canadian"* Heck, maybe that's how the Primordial Dream allows for sudden or rapid change?
                                                        -I think the Teaching by Fear reason works as one philosophy (Not covenant/tribe/order mind you) within the Beast community(?) feels limiting. Like there's only one way to be a "Good" or less hostile Beast. Another philosophy or would have added variety and even having no philosophy would add to the possibilities.

                                                        I did like what was done to the Heroes though. Becoming what they are from being Integrity 4 and gazing too long into the Primordial Dream is interesting and not the roving of Beasts is interesting. Also, it makes the Beasts dislike of them more justified and the at times the Hero more tragic and we can avoid the victimization discussion from earlier. So, I like the Heroes and the Devouring and Teaching as options.
