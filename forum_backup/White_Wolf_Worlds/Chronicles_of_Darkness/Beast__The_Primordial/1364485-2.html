                    Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                    Honestly, Beast suffers too much from it's external horror, and not enough from it's internal horror, because it's internal horror is also really, deeply, it's main draw.
                    I'm not going to pretend like I've thought about this even half as much as everyone else posting in this thread, but this point really resonated with me. When I think about the kind of game I naively wanted from Beast, when it was first announced, my conception was something like you've been marked by a Beast of legend (a Dragon, an Eldritch Horror, etc), and your transformation has begun. I envisioned a game where you can either participate willingly like a cultist, you can struggle against it like a hero, you can try to ignore it like you're in denial, or you can try to claim it like your birthright. The more you feed your beast's hunger, the more you become like it. You don't commit atrocities because you have to in order to survive, but because you're persistently nudged to crave power, and doing so gives you that power, and you *like* power. Instead of the mood being "you're already a monster and you have to hurt people to survive while building up your personal urban myth," I would have preferred "you're going through monstrous self-discovery and you need to decide what that means."

                    And the frustrating part, like everyone is saying here, is that 95% of that is already there in Beast. It's just... poorly stitched together, and really misses the mark. Some stuff is absolutely grand (combined resource/morality is fun, the various atavisms and nightmares are great, crossover potential is huge), but some decisions just leave me baffled. In particular, I think it's odd that feeding your Hunger abates the Horror. It makes it feel like "oh, I have to satisfy my Hunger in order to put my Horror at ease," when satisfying your Hunger should be the thing that *drives* your Horror. Even mechanically, the way the game works now, satisfying your Hunger weakens your Atavisms and makes you *less* like the Horror you're feeding. That just feels so antithematic and anticlimactic.

                    I didn't want a game world where I could just be a monster. I wanted a game world that would show me why being a monster is appealing. I think that's at the core of what makes Vampire, Werewolf, and even Mage so fun. And it's just kind of missing from the game about actual monsters.

                        Ok, I've thought about this even more and I guess I could crystallize my complaints above into three points:

                        1. Giving into your Horror should strengthen it, not abate it
                        2. The combined resource/Integrity stat does not have a strong connection to your monstrous activity, and so it feels just like a resource management minigame without the character having any Integrity stat at all
                        3. Your character rarely has to confront her actions in any mechanically meaningful way

                        I'm not a good enough player/ST to figure out what the answers to those problems are. My naive attempt at a bad possible solution follows: You could design a system that has actual Breaking Points, and you derive power from them somehow. Breaking Points give you Resource, whether or not you lose Integrity. Lower Integrity gives you more power for your Atavisms, but obviously the lower your Integrity, the fewer opportunities for Breaking Points you're going to have. Maybe Resources go into your dice pool for Nightmares, or you can spend them other ways. This system (1) makes you more monstrous when you act more monstrous, (2) incentivizes you to make interesting choices about how far into your monstrosity you dive, and (3) ties your character's personal horror (at the things she does) to her actual Horror.

                        I don't think that's a great idea, but it's just one idea that tries to minimally rework things to fix my chief complaints.

                        My ideas are bad and I should feel bad.

                            You're missing out on the Satiety Conditions. The Satiety structure is intrinsically tied to the Satiety Conditions and a Beast is always under the effect of one Satiety Condition or another. This is important, because it ties specifically into each of your points. Satiety itself is a resource that strengthens the Beast, enabling the Beast to wield more power. On top of that, whether being Starving or Gorged affects a Beast's power, allowing easier use of Atavisms or Nightmares, accordingly.

                            For 2 and 3, Satiety Conditions specifically encourage monstrous behavior that is problematic for the Beast. Gorged makes you more easier to be influenced, while also making the digesting Beast more slothful and prone to failing actions where the Beast should instead be proactive and engaged. Meanwhile, Starving makes the Beast act more irrationally, taking riskier behaviors and engaging in more dangerous activities when pursuing Satiety.

                            Satiety isn't a resource management game, it is a mechanic that directly influences the character's behavior with every Condition.

                            I think getting away from dealing with Breaking Points was rather helpful to Beast... Not everything needs to mechanically represented, a Beast can still feel horrible about the things they've done, or have a strong code of ethics they believe in and strive to follow. Rather, the lack of Breaking Points for Beasts makes a very interesting statement about Beasts in of themselves. To me, it makes me think of Beasts being able to witness some really frightening or terrible things... But their mind isn't going to break from that alone. They could treat with some mind-shredding extra-dimensional terror, and stand on somewhat firm ground.

He Feeds by bullying and lording it over employees (making them work through lunch, take unpaid overtime, bring the shopping carts in during a pouring rain despite the lack of rain gear). Bob is an asshole and by being an asshole he's pretty much able to remain at Sated or Gorged most of the time. He's got a small Lair (maybe three areas) and doesn't do anything really big enough to attract Heroes' attention. He's your basic asshole boss and as such doesn't really attract a bunch of attention because asshole bosses are more common than dirt in the NWoD. And he's actually a slight improvement over the previous human manager who tried to push the teenage girls into sex acts.

                                                            So, game stuff aside, Bob is sitting pretty and there's really not much reason for him to push his Legend, grow his Lair or otherwise rock his particular Beastly boat in a normal Beast set-up.

                                                            With Typhon, there is. Lilith Begot her Beastly children with the intent that they would drain Typhon's power and pull him off his throne as Kind-of-the-Mountain/Lord-of-the-Abyss. He knows that. Insatiable are His creations, sent forth to stoke fears and also go after Beasts. Heroes are Oracles that Typhon has twisted through the Primordial Dream. They actively hunt for Beasts using the Dark Gifts of Typhon (though they are unwitting pawns of his).

                                                            Instead of being random, almost incidental dangers to Beasts, Heroes and the Insatiable are (again, mostly unwitting) agents of their ultimate enemy.

                                                            So instead of enjoying his relatively safe niche as well-fed Horror/Asshole Boss, Bob needs to start building his Lair and Legend because he's being hunted. And if he's not ready, those hunters will sniff him out and snuff him out.

                                                            Of course, even as a monster, you can still decide what kind of monster you'll be. Godzilla and Mothra are monsters, but the Japanese were super-glad they were around when King Ghidorah showed up and started breaking the furniture around Tokyo.

                                                            Honestly, I'd kind of like Beasts to have a version of the Integrity trait to reflect that Beasts are human and Horror and that a balance should be maintained between them. Call it Bonding unless that's used elsewhere. The higher Bonding is the better control the Beast has over his Hunger and Atavisms. A Low Bonding leaves the Beast - especially his Horror, open to the blandishments of Typhon. Quite a few of the Insatiable used to be Beasts before they fell to Typhon.
