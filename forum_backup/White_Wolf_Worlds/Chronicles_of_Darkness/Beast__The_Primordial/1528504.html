    Agressive Play

    01-13-2024, 08:00 PM
    This situation kind of happened, so opinions on whether it went too far beyond what was acceptable in game.

    "Peter, my character, went to an informant, a woman if that matters, looking for information about the whereabouts of a girl, a relative of another character, so he offers her money, then he tries to convince her to pass on information in exchange for a sum of money. Then came a presence + persuasion test, dramatic failure, the informant takes Peter's money and pulls out the gun pointing at his face, then he steals the gun and with a maneuver throws the informant against the ground, then he steps on her stomach and the question comes back, intimidation test, only one success so she screams for help, Peter is attacked by another beast and the informant tries to escape. In response Peter trips the informant and steps on her leg with "titanic blow" and he guarantees that she won't run away, then threatens to shoot her with the gun if the beast doesn't give up, the beast attacks head on in desperation, his weapon is a pocket knife, the two have a fight, a Strength + Brawl test, Peter wins and throws him on the ground and shoots him at close range so without testing the boy dies instantly, it is specified that he was a teenager, Peter feels guilty, we didn't do a test in this case, so he asks the informant again and she simply answers, again without testing, Peter uses the nightmare "you deserve this" so that she doesn't come back later, long story, in this case just a roleplay effect so without testing, Peter goes to his car, a moment for him to brood over his guilt and threaten to break the steering wheel and go to the next scene." ​ An addendum, Peter didn't eat from this fight.

      Determining what is acceptable and to what degree is the domain of the individual play group. It's what tools like Lines & Veils and the X Card are for.

      Without further context for the situation at the table, this sounds like a relatively normal degree of intensity for a meeting-with-an-informant-gone-awry scene, with the caveat that killing a supernatural being without a roll sets a bad precedent considering the games generally indicate monsters are rare and powerful enough not to be treated like throwaway characters in that fashion.

          What Satchel said.

          Though something I would specifically point to, is if you're using Down & Dirty fight rules to resolve a fight with one roll, combat is still supposed to involve a declaration of intent It's actually more important when things like D&D or Beaten Down are in-play. If Peter's intent wasn't to kill the other Beast, his winning a single roll off shouldn't have resulted in auto-death; the rules would default to his successes on the Str + Brawl roll plus the gun's damage rating if he's not going for death... so it still absolutely could be fatal (shooting people isn't a great idea if you don't want them dead), but it would depend on the roll and the target's own defenses.

                                                  I mean possibly group dependent issues came up since I don't see any particular notes. Like I've seen a few groups that have issues with killing people who've been negated as threats, so that's a possibility.

                                                  As far as killing a teenager in game I don't see it as a huge thing since if that was a major deal breaker for the group. If this particular pair are significant NPCS to the group and thus the players would take actions against them harder. Or, if the group has child murder as a line and teens are considered part of that, then the question becomes why the ST put one in there or why they didn't specify it as something your character would notice during the struggle in case that was something that might change your mind to go from bullet in brain to bullet in knee and let them try to retreat to the Lair and recover if they want or something about how this could be interpreted as a bad idea even if it might be reasonable.

                                                  But from how you've described it, the informant escalated to death match when you botched the check. Which would be an ST decision not your decision. Peter tried to intimidate them by taking the gun and threatening them on the ground to talk or get shot. Which I mean I've seen that in a fair amount of shows, movies, and books, so that sounds fair. Bodyguard gets called in and, if I'm understanding correctly, grapples Peter at least enough to give Peter and Arm Wrack tilt. ST choice no issues Informant runs, sane move. Peter trips them and I'm gonna guess breaks the informant's leg broke at minimum based on what I recall for that atavism. Potentially more aggressive than needed, but I don't see it as a big issue unless you were describing it in all the gory details. Still potentially warranted under the circumstances imo. It then sounds like you tried to have Peter make a check to get the Bodyguard to stand down. Either the ST's rolls decided or the ST decided said bodyguard would not attempt accept that offer or request Peter drop the gun or anything else. In either of those I don't see that being something that can be held against you, though I do feel this about the first point the ST should have mentioned the Bodyguard being a teen if that's important. The bodyguard charges with a knife and you make it clear that Peter will be responding lethally if able. Reasonable considering this started with the informant pulling a gun, the bodyguard is a supernatural and has attacked with a knife while making no attempts to deescalate beyond their existence as a threat. ST does not bring up age if that's important to clarify if you would be okay with Peter ending a teenager while other options are still theoretically on the table. The dice fall the bodyguard dies. At this point you're made aware of the age thing, the informant is questioned again who folds and just tells the information they had already been paid for prior to the bloody affair. Peter uses "You Deserve This" on the informant to drive home that all of this happened because the informant got greedy. Peter then goes to sulk in his car. The Nightmare may have been excessive depending on how the informant was clearly feeling at this point. But even then Peter wasn't exactly in a great headspace either and I can imagine it pretty easily as Peter wanting to make sure the other person feels bad for what happened and to hopefully avoid being hunted for the crime of defending himself.

                                                  If I've got it all there then I don't see any particular major lines of excess there. One or two maybes with the right groups. Like yes you and by extension your character might have been able to make other choices to avoid it going that way, but nothing seems particularly excessive as choices to me. Hell you calling this aggressive play and saying you were deemed too violent a player is a bit confusing to me since your description of events implies that the only time the NPCs attempted anything approaching de-escalation was the bodyguard giving Peter the Arm Wracked tilt while the informant tried to run. Which is hardly de-escalation unless they were going, "Alright I think we all need to chill" or something to that effect. And the NPC is the one that escalated to implied lethal force out the gate by putting the gun in Peter's face after the dramatic failure.

                                                  As Teatime says this isn't something where you should go back to them trying to get added in to the group despite this, or confront the ST about it. But, at least from how you've told it with the information I've got this doesn't sound particularly overly aggressive to me.
