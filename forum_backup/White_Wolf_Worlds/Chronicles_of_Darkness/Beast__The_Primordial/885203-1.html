                                            05-19-2016, 06:47 AM
                                            Originally posted by amechra View Post
                                            It was more of a response to the idea that you couldn't play monsters in a really bright and happy game.
                                            I said primarily, not absolutely. Monsters, as the CofD uses them, live in shades of grey. These other games don't use those shades, which is where the problem comes in. In Golden Sky Stories, you're not playing a monster. You might be playing a supernatural being, but not horror movie monsters as CofD uses them.

                                            Bright happy games where the goal is making friends does fit into the CofD paradigm of addressing both the positive and the negative of such situations. "Monster" in the case of GSS just means "something biologically inhuman." With the CofD, monsters can, and often are, entirely human (mage, hunter, deviant, a number of psychics), but still invoke fear and disgust. And, the really amusing part to me, is that these are games about keeping these human friends you make despite everything working against you.

                                            Originally posted by malonkey1 View Post
                                            The story of supermen who are forever isolated from mankind by their abilities, and desperately struggling to maintain their identities against the infinite responsibility they have taken on? That sound like it could be in the CofD. Admittedly, it would skew more Heroes than Avengers, but it could stille be a plausible addition. Just wait for Deviant and I'm sure we'll have something that fits.
                                            The flaw here is that the game I'm refering to has a deliberate Good versus Evil vibe to it, not shades of grey. That sets it firmly outside of the normal CofD horror wheelhouse involving monsters. You can play it in the CofD, but the main PCs really aren't monsters. They're heroes. The entire plot of the game in question could be taken right out of the new Capt. America: Civil War movie. You're taking up the famous spiderman quote about responcibility. You literally channel the power of Goodness and Light. Sure, it can fit into the CofD if you wish it, but not as the monsters.

                                            Which brings me to the reason I brought all this up. Beast is a game about playing as horror movie monsters. Its a game about playing Overlords and Dungeon Keepers. You are playing the one who needs to take the Evil Overlord List seriously. Even if you want to argue that its possible to play supernatural beings that are bright-happy, that is not the intended goal of Beast. The point is that you are playing the antagonist part of Man Vs Man/Nature/Self conflict in stories. You're playing the things that humans need to struggle against. You want to play a walking incarnation of lust? Sure, fine. But that's only going to work if its something to struggle against or have a cost associated with it. And people will fear that loss, which ties it back into the whole Primoridal Dream fears bit.

                                            That's the whole point I'm trying to make. Play whatever game you want, but monsters, as Beasts put it forwards, have supernatural hungers that prey on humans and tend to discourage nice, happy feedings. You get power from hurting people, a natural sadist. This doesn't make you naturally evil, but it does mean that you are not someone that fits into a bright/happy game. It means that Beasts are the incarnation of fears because they are built to get power acting like a monster, and people fear monsters.
