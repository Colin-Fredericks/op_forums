        Originally posted by Papa Bear View Post

        Hardly subversive in the World of Darkness. Their start was based on playing Dracula as the "hero" instead of Van Helsing.
        I think that the subversive part in the "antagonist/ protagonist angle" is less about if they are "heroes" or "villain", and more about their role in the story- think about the Chronicle books. The "antagonists" in them is about the ones who start a story. A vampire can be monstrous, but the one who brings chaos with him and creates a story is the Owl.

        That also seem to be the thing in Beast- you are a dragon, or a wendigo or whatever, but the thing is that you are not drawn into some mad plot created by some dangerous thing in the dark. Instead, you are the dangerous thing in the dark. You are the one who devour babies, or kidnap maidens or guard some hidden treasure. Your presence creates Heroes who come and try to stop you, use you or steal from you. Instead of being caught in someone's plan, you are the one who makes the plan, and others are trapped in your story. YOU start the story, you are the dragon under the mountain, the giant in his fortress, the monster which swims under the dark waters. And every time you vanquish the damn hero who tried to get rid of you, you build an whole legend around you- until an hero would finally slay you, and then, and only then, they story would have its "happy ending".

        At least, that's how I see things :P
