                                    Alright, to add some constructive content, I'd like to suggest an alternate approach to the ideas of what it means for a Beast lacking Integrity and their morality someone suggested many pages ago.

                                    Basically, I'd say that the reason Beasts don't have Breaking Points is that the Horror is made of sterner stuff than the numinal organ that beings of the Chronicles call a soul. Normal humans, who have these instead of Horrors, find that the soul become strained when the person is forced to do or witness things that damage their self-image either by striking a personal chord (the five questions) or by things that strain the psyche (killing or witnessing the supernatural), wounding their Integrity (the health bar of the soul, in a way), and thus causing psychological damage. The Horror is immune to this sort of strain, meaning that a Beast is not guaranteed to suffer psychological damage because of things they do.

                                    Wrapping around to this thread's point, it means that right and wrong are both more and less of a pressing issue for Beasts. If you go around murdering people, it won't cause your Integrityesque to go down. And yet that doesn't mean you can't tell right from wrong, it just means that you have to chose what you'll do, knowing that the only thing you'll likely lose is a clear conscious.

                                    Really, I think Beast seems like a good game for exploring morality in part because there's no "sanity/morality meter" to point to. You have to decide how to live with what you are, and you don't have any mechanics to point to for an easy answer (note that easy answers are not always the right one, especially in 2e). What will you chose?

                                                    Regarding the whole "teaching lessons" part, I am reminded of this comment by Extra Credits on God Of War. The gameplay in that series is mostly combat and puzzles. Is solving puzzles a weird thing for a divinity of bloody strife to do so often? Sure, but it's better than to make the gameplay only consist of over the top hack and slash.

                                                    Beast benefits from "teaching lessons" like God Of War benefits from puzzles, loosely speaking. The big difference is that the puzzles are not tied to the themes. Beast would be less interesting if there was no internal conflict leading to rationalizations, on the part of Beasts and Heroes. Can you imagine how dull it would be if both sides were totally OK with what they did, all the time?

                                                    Besides, it's not all about Feeding, which bears emphatic repeating. There's the Jack Of All Trades aspect, which allows you to create a very unique monster when you use it right. Each Atavism is available to all Beasts, and Nightmares do a lot more than simply cause fear. Lairs are very customizable, and it is possible to change your personal approach to your Hunger, or even change the Hunger itself (that requires some in-character justification and roleplaying it out, though). You can homebrew a Merit that gives a second Hunger and it won't break the game. Your notions of who your true Kin are can be challenged and adapt.

                                                    Another important fact is that the Devouring does not remove all your humanity. You no longer use Integrity, but you do have a Life along with your Legend. Nothing forbids you from changing those traits either.

                                                    Your Beast does not have to be a static monster, like a page torn from a folklore book and given life by a halfhearted ritual. They should have all the splendor and terror of man and monster alike.

                                                        I really fell in love with Beast: the Primordial in its draft phase. I enjoyed that the game had no shame in portraying a monster and I felt that the game's meaning was informed just as much by what wasnt said (lack of Integrity, for example) as what was said. Then in the final draft, Teaching Lessons was introduced to provide "purpose", but this clashed hard (in my view at least) in a game that was fundamentally about searching for purpose (ie: building your Legend).

                                                        But this is a Sell Me thread, so let me explain why I'm okay with the Teaching Lessons, in principle if not how its been implemented in the text.

                                                        Teaching Lessons has nothing to back it up. There are no mechanics that I can see which tie into it and therefore as far as the mechanics of the game is concerned, Teaching Lessons does not matter. This is to highlight that Teaching Lessons has absolutely zero impact on the monster that is your true self. It doesn't care, and therefore on a level? You don't care.

                                                        Teaching Lessons is, strictly, an ideology established by Beasts in order to placate their conscience and self image. It is a toothless philosophy, and this is a good thing for storytelling purposes. This is because in a world without inherent meaning beyond the Hunger and the Feast, meaning is built by those who strive to find purpose outside of that monstrous cycle. And this is telling stories at the heart of it.

                                                        A critical error of the book that was published is that only one such philosophy of purpose was established and because there's only one philosophy presented, it's assumed that its the only philosophy that exists. But there can be more philosophies to discover. Ideologies compete with each other, and this is a critical area where Beasts can argue and fight over with each other, presenting greater conflict and drama for your stories.

                                                        A strength of Beast is that it speaks loudly in the absence of what isn't said and there is much more to explore in the darkness. So you get to have fun with that and be a real monster in the process. ^^
