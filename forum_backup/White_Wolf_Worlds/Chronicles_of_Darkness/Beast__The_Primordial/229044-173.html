                                I went back, and I am trying to figure out exactly why the "vampire (now changeling) vs. Beast: who is worst?" has any relevance. Sure, you can give examples from both of those who don't harm humans and those whose very existence is a reason to kill them due to the innate harmful.

                                But I don't really see why this would sway those humans who do hunt them why they should be killed. Nor why it would mean that the supernaturals being hunted wouldn't feel persecuted, especially if they aren't one of the supernarural who live up to stereotypes. And even those who are monsters don't think they are in many cases, or think it is natural, so they are as likely to feel persecuted as those who aren't monsters.

                                The difference between a hunter and hero is not the value their target nor the method of their creation. Plenty of hunters take up the vigil due to being victims of the Supernatural, such as loosing a lover to a vampire, a child to the fae, or a friend to obe of the other supernaturals.

                                The difference is the how and the why. The Hunters might not know what they are getting into, but it is an active choice. They aren't compelled by anything beyond normal human emotion and reason. Heroes are those who have a lack of identity that allows their role as a Beast Slayer due to a mythic compulsion. And as such, hunters pursue their targets and use human emotion and reason to pursue the monsters, while heroes are driven by their role in a story.

                                Plus, hunters can be as bad as Heroes. Heroes simply start out that bad. People seem to like to cast the hunters as good guys, but they are just as prone to being monsters as the good guys. The more extreme hunters are going be no different than a Hero, which makes sense: both are low integrity humans. And both can recover their humanity by regaining their integrity. The key is that the Heroes only come about at that low integrity because they have been drawn into the beasts mythos.

                                So if I am missing the point of the "Vampire harm vs. Beast Harm" argument, I am sorry. But it just seems odd to argue who deserves to be killed more based on the harm, thus justifying the Hunters vs. Heroes. Hunters will kill good supernaturals ( there is an entire compact dedicated to killing the forsaken and helping the pure and spirits wanting to posses people!) as much as the bad. Some supernaturals are "good" while others should be killed on sight. But arguing one splat inherently bad based of examples, and thus its splats human monster slayers as being more or less justified, is an effort in futility at best.

                                (Note, I am not saying it needs to end. I just don't really follow where this debate is going. Please fill me in if it seems I completely am missing the point)
