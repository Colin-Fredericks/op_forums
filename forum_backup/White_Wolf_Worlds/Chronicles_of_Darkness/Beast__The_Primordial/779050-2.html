    Originally posted by HaplessWithDice View Post
    Wait...By it's own text doesn't it say "this counts as a is a Ranged Attack with a thrown weapon" thus consuming your attack action? That would negate your ability to also fire a flame thrower on that turn wouldn't it? Because you already used your 1 attack that turn? Because it counted as a "Ranged Attack".
    No. There's nothing that limits how many ranged or other attacks you can make - except your available actions. An "attack" is just a class of action that's normally instant (and thus once per turn, for the sole reason that you only have one instant action). Dragonfire is just a reflexive action that is also a ranged attack; you can take any instant action desired in addition to Dragonfire, including firing a gun, swinging a sword, disarming a bomb, whatever's salient. Nothing you can do reflexively negates your ability to act on your turn unless it explicitly says something like "you can't take an action on your next turn," for example, Celerity in Vampire: the Requiem 2nd Edition. Like Dragonfire, it allows you to act reflexively, but it also specifically notes that this eats your turn.

    Being able to breath fire reflexively is pretty insane, but so are most Beast powers.

                                                        Originally posted by Ephsy View Post
                                                        Well, this discussion seems to be going in circles. Last time I saw it, the deal was: Activating the capacity to shoot fire = reflexive. Actually attacking with it = Instant.

                                                        I mean, Siren's Treacherous Song is just a few pages down, where they specifically call it out as an instant action twice - both in the action line, and again when describing the attack, as an instant action.

                                                        The confusion stems from a continued misreading of the phrase "counts as a ranged attack" which people are appending an extra meaning of "and is also, in addition to counting as a ranged attack, an instant action." COUNTING AS a ranged attack just means that for the purpose of rules that interact with ranged attacks, it is one. Have a power that makes you immune to ranged attacks? Calculating the multiple attacker penalty and want to know if Dragonfire is the same as swinging a knife? Environmental factors levy a penalty to ranged attacks? Et cetera.

                                                        Ranged attack is a status normally applied to an instant action. In this case, it's applied to a reflexive action. "Ranged attack" is not a quality that dictates action type.

                                                        It's definitely not a case of "you use the power reflexively for free, and then make an attack as an instant action." For one thing, that's incoherent, why would using the power need any action at all if it's a "free" action that does nothing? There's a line for that, it's called Action: None, which WoD has been using for passive effects since last edition. For another thing, "counts as a ranged attack" points at the activation of the power, which is reflexive. You'd need to have an instant action in the text to point at. Spending a free action that just says "you can take another type of action now" is definitely not a reasonable read, and given that the fire immunity has no duration, activating it reflexively still doesn't make sense. How long are you immune to fire? The instant you activate it, helping you exactly never? One turn? Until you say you're not? What happens if you go to sleep? Et cetera. That's a passive effect. The reflexive activation is the ranged attack.

                                                        The only reason I think people are arguing so strongly for "counts as" to translate differently is because there's a general feeling that this definitely SHOULD be an instant action, and I agree, it should be changed, reflexive attacks are BS. Especially when they're ranged, deal agg, are good for traceless arson (this comes up every game I have ever played), and also happen to make you immune to fire in the bargain.

                                                        But that's a rules change, not a typo issue, and gets into the greater conversation of "do Beasts push the upper boundary of the system beyond tolerance."

                                                        Ideally a dev'll pop in and be like "yeah, it's reflexive, but maybe that wasn't a great idea, so we're gonna make it instant" as a result of this conversation, which is my sole motivation for continuing it.

                                                        Also the issue of "how many times can you make the same reflexive action in a turn" really needs an explicit callout of "exactly once" in every core rulebook. That's beyond Dragonfire and comes up plenty with other reflexive powers, and while the common sense ruling is obviously once there needs to be no ambiguity there. To be 100% clear I would never argue for any RAI allowing infinite attacks because I don't think anyone has ever intended that.
