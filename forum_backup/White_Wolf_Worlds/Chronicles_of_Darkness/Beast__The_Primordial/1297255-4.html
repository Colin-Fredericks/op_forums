            That out of the way…

            If you absolutely must try to turn the recurring antagonist role of Beast on its head and aren't fussed about "local nobody becomes glorious monster-slayer" being the furthest thing from a fresh take, you could play into the Hero-Beast adversarial relationship by cutting out the middleman halfway through; if you're set on painting Heroes as near-universally justified then there's no need to root the Begotten in a single consistent human character.

            Lean on the fact that the Horror is a psychic entity from the collective unconscious — each one of the Astral entities called the Insatiable forms in response to a Hero's confrontation with the very idea of fear (or the power vacuum of such a creature being slain by its nemesis). The Lair remains consistent, but the Horror takes root among mortal wickedness and supernatural monstrosity, spreading fear and destruction until forced to retreat by the loss of its footholds, cornered into physical reality by the loss of its stronghold, or killed in the heart of its dream-fortress.

            Advancement then proceeds normally by tracking down the core of a given predatory idea and ending its hold on a population, at which point the character gets some boon that makes it easier for them to fight monsters but pushes a wedge between them and normal humans — and on a long enough timescale, maybe a Hero who lets the glory of their legend cloud their judgment lets another Hero's nemesis into their heart.

            There's a gameplay loop to be had there, and the existing mechanics for Heroes are sufficiently simple that you can scale growth to taste.

                            I have to admit, while I reacted to Camilla's post much like Satchel... I realized something:

                            The only thing that Beast, and playing as Beasts, is missing from what Camilla wants out of a gaming session.... is a reason to use katanas. Beast favors Athletics and Brawl for combat focused characters to keep up with their theme as bestial creatures that use their bodies to fight instead of weapons. Otherwise everything, even the "doing it because I get a kick out of pissing certain people off for playing what they think is badwrongfun," you can get out of just playing Beast.

                            Reasons for lots of immersive RP and social interactions? Check. The crossover and brood mechanics give a strong game play loop that puts mechanical goodies as rewards for engaging in this. If you want those fun Nightmares and Kinship Merits... you need to go have your PC have all that social RP to form the bonds that like to the Condition that leads to access to those.

                            Atavism focused combat Beasts are insanely intense combatants that with strong style and lots of action. Sure you'll get more Attack on Titan (as a Titan Shifter), Parasyte, or Tokyo Ghoul in visual/action themes than you'd get trench coats and katanas... but like I said the katanas are the lacking part... there's plenty of cool clothes in those anime.

                            There is nothing in Beast that compels the Begotten to be "woe is me" about anything. Their response to being part of a cycle of violence that's supposed to end with them dying is presented not as "alas what a poor fate we have," but, "fuck that noise, I'm not dying because some narrative says so."

                            The game has explicit endings to aim for in the Inheritances, and you can actively pursue the "good" ones while avoiding the "downer" ones.

                            You're not going to find the Beast fans around here particularly upset at the idea of an action heavy Beast game because... it's pretty damn common for Beasts to get violent.

                            Beast isn't actually "brash contrarianism" or even all that punk. Yes, Beasts reject the monomyth... but because it says they're supposed to die just for existing. "I want the right to live because I am what I am, and that shouldn't be a death sentence," isn't really all that contrarian or punk to assert. Plus Beasts get their power from being the ones with power, not fighting the ones with power as punk encourages as an aesthetic.

                            Beasts can be about redemption and salvation as irresponsible Beasts, or ones stuck at Satiety 0, are brought back to their senses to live a more balanced life.

                            Beasts can be about adventure and danger as they explore the world around them, their kin, and their enemies.

                            There are clear and present "evil" things to fight in the Insatiable, plus any of the equally irredeemable bad guys from the other game lines Beasts might get drawn into fighting; Beasts are awesome allies to have for most supernaturals when it comes to fighting things like the Gentry.

                            So... the katana aesthetic (which isn't disallowed, just not effective)... is really all that's missing.

                            Yet Camilla hates the game.

Yes, Beasts reject the monomyth... but because it says they're supposed to die just for existing. "I want the right to live because I am what I am, and that shouldn't be a death sentence," isn't really all that contrarian or punk to assert. Plus Beasts get their power from being the ones with power, not fighting the ones with power as punk encourages as an aesthetic.

                                                    Beasts can be about redemption and salvation as irresponsible Beasts, or ones stuck at Satiety 0, are brought back to their senses to live a more balanced life.

                                                    Beasts can be about adventure and danger as they explore the world around them, their kin, and their enemies.

                                                    There are clear and present "evil" things to fight in the Insatiable, plus any of the equally irredeemable bad guys from the other game lines Beasts might get drawn into fighting; Beasts are awesome allies to have for most supernaturals when it comes to fighting things like the Gentry.

                                                    So... the katana aesthetic (which isn't disallowed, just not effective)... is really all that's missing.

                                                    Part of what set me off of Beast when it was announced was the Heroes angle. The fact that the main villains are literally called "Heroes" raised a red flag for me and gave me some really bad vibes.

Combine that with the fact that several example Heroes in the earliest Beast books were obvious straw men with unfortunate political implications and it left a bad taste in my mouth, so I preemptively boycotted it.

Avoid the straw-manning and just call Heroes something like Fanatics or Zealots instead or focus on other baddies in the setting.
