    Originally posted by Primordial newcomer View Post
    Long time ago I mentioned something like this, and am happy to see you want it too.
    I would like to be clear: it's evident that the vast majority of Temenotic activity is self-contained and representational — the Bright Dream does not have to be concerned about geography the way the Shadow does, and most Astral beings are completely unconcerned with the material world if they are aware it exists.

    My interest is in having a little more detail given to how the described exceptions work; Astral Realms provides examples of means of transport between realms, including characters and vessels that specifically do so regularly; Naturae who lose control of their ecosystems are described as sometimes falling from the Dreamtime to the Swath or even to the Temenos as city-psyches; animals are stated to leave powerful psychic impressions the give rise to cultural imagery as well as the Animal Masters participating in the Dreamtime's great game of adaptation; the realms of the Temenos are thematically skewed but are flexible enough to incorporate visitors into their recurring stories, in contrast to the mnemonic scenes of the Oneiros; strange things like the Kindly Stranger reach into individual dreams to cast actors in uncanny pantomimes with consequences in the waking world; and of course everybody's daimon tries to steer them toward self-improvement.

    In general Mage 1e's perspective on the Astral, while distinct from the Begotten's view in ways that puts "no neat little boxes" front and center for the latter, is still the most detailed look we have at the Inner Realms and it probably shouldn't be rendered completely useless as a reference document when the biggest differences the Beast Player's Guide introduces are that the Begotten enter the Astral from the middle of the span, cut past most of the shallows of the Anima Mundi, and treat the entire Bright Dream like the Temenos once they make their way out of the Primordial Dream — Beasts are explicitly less common than mages, and while having more Astral stuff to work with would be good, it probably shouldn't be a major chunk of their reactive storytelling compared to dealing with, y'know, people.

    Werewolves can deHarmonize into not being able to leave the Shadow, changelings can get caught up in the Thorns and/or become Goblin Queens, everyone wandering the Underworld is a long drop away from a permanent stint as a Ferryman, but the Begotten are sufficiently compartmentalized that the common Inheritances are easily accessible if they just want to forsake humanity and spend all their time as a living legend, since Chambers make the Primordial Dream easily accessible even before Soul Communion keeps the most comfortable part of that other world ready for you.

    The Dreaming teems with inner demons of all sorts, and they're mostly kept to their native hells as they should be. I just want a little more to work with as far as the roads between them and the travelers thereupon.

                        Created and destroyed is also probably a bit too harsh of a way to think about it. The BPG refers to this as the Mists: a state of amorphous existence where detail and vibrancy fade into a haze of indistinct dream shapes. Something that's moved into the Mists can be rescued from them, but there's not assurance that the reconstituted sureness of its new form is the same as what it was before it went it. While there is certainly room for a Ship of Theseus style debate on a Dreamborn fading and coming back different, there is frequently a continuity of existence involved rather than complete destruction and (re)invention.
