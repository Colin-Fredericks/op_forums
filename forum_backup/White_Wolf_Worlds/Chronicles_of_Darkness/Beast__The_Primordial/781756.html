    A Few Questions about Nightmares

    12-07-2015, 02:03 PM
    So I've been reading over the Nightmares section of the book, and I thought I'd raise a few questions and concerns here. I'm a bit worried about this stuff, actually. Some of the Nightmares seem a bit hilariously overpowered, but I admit that I could be wrong, and that I could be misreading this. It is my sincere hope that the final product will not suffer from these issues, and admittedly I have no idea if they are already fixed in the final product (I hope so!).

    So correct me if I am wrong, but as far as I'm able to determine, the limitations on Nightmares are as follows:
    - only one Nightmare can be active on the target at a time.
    - lacking special abilities, you can only target one person with a Nightmare at a time
    - the target must be able to see your eyes to be hit by a Nightmare

    1. Is there any limit to the number of times per scene you can use a Nightmare?
    Granted, only one Nightmare can be active on one target at a time, but is there anything to stop me from hitting everyone in the scene with a nightmare individually?
    'Behold, my True Form!' is a single action with an instant effect. Is it considered to still be affecting the target for the rest of the scene for the purposes of Nightmare-targeting rules? If not, is there anything stopping me from building a character with maxed out Presence, sitting at High Satiety, and just using it exclusively in combat to roll something in the area of 14 dice minus my opponent's Stamina for lethal damage every single action? It's going to be -2 or -3 most of the time. Let's assume we end up with 12 dice after Stamina. That's 4L.
    Or combining it with Fear is Contagious at the cost of two more dice (which translates to about 1 less damage, so 3L) to hit two targets with it every turn?

    2. Do people eventually build up any kind of resistance to a specific nightmare if they have been hit with it multiple times over the course of a game?

    3. Certain abilities and combinations of abilities seem mind-bogglingly powerful. Am I missing something here? Is this working as intended?
    By way of example, I offer up All Your Teeth Are Falling Out, and then used in combination with Limb From Limb. I am here limiting myself to the normal effects of these abilities. No Satiety expenditure. No High Satiety/Low Satiety effects. No exceptional successes. Just normal effects.

    All Your Teeth Are Falling Out gives a flat -2 penalty to all Physical traits (and removes any trait that depends on physical beauty) which also applies to derived Traits. As its standard effect. This is an ability that seems like it would be perfectly reasonable if it existed in Exalted, but in nWoD with its smaller distribution of starting attributes, I believe it to be hilariously overpowered. Because of the smaller distribution of starting attributes, even if the target has chosen Physical as their primary, I am still taking at least one of their physical attributes down to 0. Even just suffering a -2 to Strength, Dex, and Stamina is bad enough, but the fact that it applies to derived traits as well is really the thing that takes it over the top. It doesn't just give my opponent a -2 to affect me, it also gives me a +2 to affect my opponent (because it lowers their Defense by 2). It makes them less resilient against damage, AND it gives them a speed hit.

    Now combine that with the effects of the Limb from Limb Atavism, which, as its base effect, lets you automatically inflict Arm and Leg wracks every time you do damage to a target that exceeds the target's stamina. Most people are going to be hard pressed to have a Stamina higher than 4. Average for a player character is probably going to be 2 or 3. Okay. Now it's minus 2, taking it to either 0 (if it can be lowered to 0. Not sure about that) or 1 . Okay. Now, if I do any damage at all, I automatically do an arm or leg wrack. Limb From Limb just got way, WAY better, and because AYTAFO also penalizes Defense, it is also going to be easier to land those blows to inflict the effects of Limb from Limb.

    4. Does the target know that the Beast has inflicted a Nightmare on him or her? Would there be any way for them to know this or reasonably figure this out?

                          So this thread made me go back and reread the Nightmares section again and notice some things (which resulted in the formation of the hacks below).

                          1) Nightmares are hallucinations. Not sure why they would ever be resisted by Stamina.
                          ** Hack #1: Nightmares are always contested by Resolve + Composure + Supernatural Tolerance (unless they use a resisted roll, in which case subtract the victim's Resolve + Supernatural Tolerance).
                          ---- Some may complain that this hits them with the nerf bat. Valid complaint (but see hack #2).

                          2) Most supernaturals add their Supernatural trait to their power rolls. This seems doubly appropriate for Beasts. Beasts with bigger Lairs are scarier.
                          ** Hack #2: Beasts add their dots in Lair to their Nightmare rolls.

                          3) The normal effect of All Your Teeth are Falling Out doesn't jive to well with the How to Make Kinship Nightmares several pages later. The suggestion in the latter section is that the normal effect be no more than a -2 penalty to dice pools. Subtracting 2 from all three physical traits does seem a little harsh. It's also a bit unclear to me what happens to poor people who have stats drop to zero. Do they die? Are they paralyzed (i.e., do they die?)? More to the point though, the way the fluff is written suggests that Nightmares are supposed to do stuff to the victims while they sleep (i.e. while they are in their Dream Form), but humans don't have physical traits in their dream forms so this Nightmare has zero effect while they sleep... I don't know about anyone else but that seems like a hell of problem for the utility of this power.
                          ** Hack #3: The normal effect of All Your Teeth are Falling Out is a -2 penalty to all physical dice pools. All of this Nightmare's effects also apply to "physical" tasks that take place in the primordial dream. For example, if a hapless mortal affected by this Nightmare while in Dream Form tries to hit the Beast while they are both in the Hive, that mortal suffers the -2 penalty to their attack roll, even though the pool is nominally based on his mental traits. What matters is the perception of physicality.

                          4) Behold My True Form seems to rely on biological processes to inflict damage (the victims die from fear). Since undead and entities that were never alive to start with don't have such biological processes it seems like it should be way less effective against them.
                          ** Hack #4: Undead beings (such as Vampires and Mummies) or beings that were never alive to start with (such as Prometheans) downgrade the damage from this Nightmare to bashing.

                          5) The duration rules seem a little funky. For instance, if you don't buff the duration of You Must Obey with something like You Can't Wake Up, it's difficult to see how you can benefit from the effects that come about from High Satiety.
                          ** Hack #5: Nightmare durations depend on if the victim is awake or asleep. When used against someone who is awake the duration is the longer of one scene or one hour (this lets powers like You Must Obey unfold over multiple scenes if necessary). When used against someone who is asleep, the duration is the shorter of one chapter or a number of hours equal to 10 - victim's Stamina + Supernatural Tolerance (with the base 10 - Stamina hours roughly equating to the victim's sleep cycle).
                          ---- Yes, this makes Nightmares really mean against people who are stuck in Dream Form -- just like it should be for a power called "Nightmare".

                          6) The way the You are Infected Nightmare reads is that it is hyper-effective against Vampires rather than inspired by them (since normal mortals usually don't drink one another's blood).
                          ** Hack #6: Rather than affecting anyone who has tasted her blood, this Nightmare affects anyone from whom she has drawn blood (be that by biting them, scratching them, punching them, or cutting them with a rusty razor blade). At High Satiety, anyone coming into contact with the initial victim is also affected by the "another Nightmare".
