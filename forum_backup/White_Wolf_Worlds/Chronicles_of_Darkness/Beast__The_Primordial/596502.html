    Disatisfaction with Nightmares [Beast]

    07-20-2015, 01:36 AM
    I have to say, I had a strong negative reaction to Nightmares - my brain slid off them while I was trying to read 'em.

    They felt so external to the whole thing - too... intentional.

    Yeah, yeah, cool nightmare powers, perfectly thematic, bla bla... I really don't think that Beasts should have active powers beyond Satiety expenditures and their Lair-related powers.

    If I had my druthers, Nightmares would be to your Lair as Atavisms are to your body - they wouldn't have any effect unless you call down a Lair trait, but then... well, it's the difference between the Lair being there and you using the Lair as a weapon.

    For example, Bugs Everywhere would populate your Lair with squirming things. When your Lair is drawn into the real world, they come with it - swarming over your target, softening them up for the final crunch and delicious slurp of their fear.

    As for why it works better when you have high Satiety... your Soul doesn't understand what it's like to be afraid. You do - you remember what it was like to have phobias, or to get freaked out by a horror movie. So when you're thinking more like a human, you can use that empathy to be more... precise. Like a scalpel compared to your Soul's battleaxe.

    But yes.

    I have decided, after some thought, that I don't really feel happy on these forums. I might decide to come back to post. Who knows - but right now, I'm gone.

    So good bye, good luck, and have a nice day.

      In that case... just play using Atavisms and using Lair, keeping your Beast around Low Satiety for maximum effect?

      Something to keep in mind - despite being the Beast's fuel stat, Satiety is actually still an Integrity equivalent. Like any of the other supernaturals, a high Integrity equivalent still means your more human like, while a low I.E. means your more monstrous. This holds true, regardless if we're talking about Humanity, Harmony, Cover, or Satiety. Low Satiety is all about the Hungers and the Beast made manifest. Meanwhile, High Satiety is more about putting the Beast to sleep and wandering in dreams, and effectively turning from a physical character into a psychic, and eventually all but a normal human at Satiety 10. Low Satiety is about feeding Hungers, High Satiety is more about being buddies with the supernatural instead.

      Its a shift in tone, yes. But all games do that, in a real way. High Integrity characters play much differently than Low Integrity equivalents. I think that, given your post, we're talking about a dissonance between the two play styles here. Its like the difference between playing a high Harmony werewolf instead of a low Harmony one - the two act vastly different.

      If your personal play style lends itself to one of the two styles, you can just play that style, while others could play the other style, or try to balance them (eek! Medium Satiety is bad!).

Not very monstrous.

                          Actually, with how easy it is to feed your Hunger at low levels? Its easy to use a point or two of Satiety, then go feed back up to 3. If you go higher, its easy enough to spend Satiety to get back down out of the danger zone (see below). So, no. I find that its actually easy to use the most dangerous powers fairly frequently, then indulge my Hunger immediately in some way.
                          and these two sliders balance each other at "neutral monster". So you always end up being a 50% grey in mechanical terms.
                          Except for the slight issue that you don't want to be at Satiety 4-6. Not only do you not get Atavism or Nightmare bonuses, leaving you weaker, but you're vulnerable to a Hero. The game pushes you to the extremes - either trying to maintain a Low Satiety, where you focus on Atavisms and rapidly feeding your Hunger (which, if you slip too high, you're in danger from a Hero, who likely is looking for you while you're feeding your Hunger), or try to maintain a High Satiety and focus on Nightmares and Kinship (this also prevents you from going crazy with Nightmares, since if you go crazy with them, you'll fall into the Average Zone as well as potentially drawing a Hero's attention).




                          Humanity defines your banes, the power that these banes hold upon you and the social penalties you have with mortals.
                          Exactly - you're more susecptable to mystical influences and act less human at low levels. I would also like to point out that, due to the social penalties, you are more likely to fall back on Disciplines for feeding. Majesty to get rid of the penalties, Dominate or Obfuscate to bypass the need altogether, Auspex to get around the inability to read emotions well, etc.

                          Harmony defines how often you'll go on a death-rage and shifting to one extreme or the other is bad (mechanically speaking)
                          Harmony, mechanically speaking, reflects how close you are to human flesh versus wolf spirit. It affects more than just death rages, it affects what world you're drawn into, and which form you act most like. You break towards being human by eating salad and pizza, don't forget. Acting human means we get a higher Harmony score (maybe too high).

                          Satiety defines...which powers you'll want to use.
                          And how you feed your Satiety over time. And how you spend your hours of the day - seeking out new Kinship for more Nightmares, or looking for new chances to feed your Hunger or grow your Lair. One is much more humane than the other. Harmony wants you to balance at the middle. Satiety detests balance and encourages extremes.


                          Wisdom defines how far your nimbus infects people.
                          Ahh... not quite.
                          Originally posted by Mage blog
                          "High-Wisdom mages cast magic subtly and carefully, minimizing the effects of witnessing the supernatural on Sleeper’s Integrity. They act with care for consequences, rather than abusing their gnosis to impose their own selfish ends. Low-Wisdom mages are direct and at times brutal with their spells, using too much power or creating indiscriminate spell effects because it’s easier and faster.
                          • While Gnosis determines how overtly supernatural a mage’s nimbus is, Wisdom determines how far it spreads – the lower the further.
                          • At permanent Wisdom 0 (not through “injury”, but hubris), a character becomes one of “the Mad”, a mage with a broken soul who has lost conscious control of her magic and “leaks” Supernal energy. Being Mad is incurable except perhaps through archmastery of Death.
                          • Mage characters attempting to contain a Paradox within their own pattern contest the Paradox roll by rolling Wisdom.
                          • High and Low Wisdom provides dice modifiers to interacting with Goetia and Supernal Entities, but not Spirits and Ghosts."
                          Hmm.... So, lets review. High Wisdom mages are subtle, careful, show less obvious of a Nimbus, bonus to containing paradoxes. They look and act much more like regular people. Low Wisdom mages throw obvious magic around, their Nimbus is more obvious to observers, and paradoxes tend to happen on the outside where observers can see them. Clearly, one's more obviously a mage, a mental monster, than the other.



                          And lets not forget that Demon certainly still applies. High Cover, you appear more human, you have less glitches, you have more connections with people around you, a real part of society. You'll likely focus on Embeds, subtle powers, because you don't want to risk it.

                          Low Cover means that we've got parts of your true nature showing through, you live in impossible spaces, and you have a bookshelf with only one book, repeated fifteen times, including the torn cover. Most likely worn for missions, which means we're pulling out the big guns (Exploits, demon form) which are much more obviously monster powers.

                          Satiety defines...which powers you'll want to use.
                          A focus on Atavisms mean you physically appear more than human. You literally fly, you loom larger than life, you are unearthly beautiful. You breath fire and cause indoor wind storms with a wave of your hands.

                          Nightmares are freaking people out with a look or touch. Run your fingers down someone's back, and they freak out since they feel like spiders are crawling along their skin. A glare that shoots daggers makes someone scared enough that they'll listen to your commands while you're in sight. Not exactly the appearance of a monster here.

                          One of these doesn't seem to fit the bill as far as "Being a monster" goes. I understand that Beast is a game about being a creature of nightmare, but I don't think the game is any different in saying Satiety is an Integrity equivalent or simply stating that Beasts don't have any Integrity equivalent.
                          Its all in how they act. Remember, I'm talking about acting human, not acting humanely. There's a difference. These aren't Morality equivalents anymore. Its about a spiritual balance of some kind.

                          Acting like a monster, no matter the game, has nothing to do with morality. You can be an exceedingly moral vampire at low Humanity. You just can't pass for human worth crap.

                                                          You know, I'll have to agree with MCN here. They explained themselves extremely well. Your desired Satiety isn't just a decision made with dice pools in mind, or at least not for a more observant player. The Satiety you want most is reflected in little things of your routine and the sum of your activities/life experiences. It could be jokingly referred to as your Diet.
