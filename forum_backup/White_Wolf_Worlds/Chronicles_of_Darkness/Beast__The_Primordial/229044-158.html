            If it helps, consider such statements to come not from the authors of the text but from the Begotten for whom justifications of the kind form the structure of their cultural mythos. In the same way that werewolves tend to assume other monsters are Claimed, or how changelings wonder if everything supernatural is the work of Arcadia, Beasts contextualize their experiences as always returning to the Dark Mother. The strong crossover focus of the book just pushes that particular chauvinism front and center. So, whenever the text claims something like "Father Wolf was one of the first Eshmaki," it might be good to append "Many Beasts think that--" to the front of such sentences. On a second read-through, on reflection, I think that practice might be helpful for basically everything in the first few chapters. The Storytelling chapter implies that the Children aren't nearly as self-aware as they like to style themselves.
