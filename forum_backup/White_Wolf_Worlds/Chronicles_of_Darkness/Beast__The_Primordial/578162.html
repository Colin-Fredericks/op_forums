    [ Beast ] Supernatural Hate

    07-04-2015, 12:53 AM
    I'm sure this topic may have already been discussed somewhere in the 260+ pages of the Beast thread, but I wanted to have a tighter discussion about how Beasts would interact and be viewed by the other splats. Despite the Beast book suggesting that they would generally get along, things do not seem to be as rosy as they portray in the book.

    Vampires - The relation here seems mostly neutral, leaning towards the antagonistic the more that Vampires know about the nature of a Beast. That is to say, your average Vampire has no reason to know much of anything about a Beast and may consider them a useful supernatural asset since they are not really competing for any resources by default. But the more Vampires know about a Beast the more they are liable to be unhappy with the additional level of scrutiny that may come from investigating Heroes.

    Werewolves - The relation here seems to be mostly negative. Beasts are going to create some serious negative resonance if they spend enough time in the same place as a werewolf, and the Werewolves are probably not going to be pleased cleaning up the mess that the Beast is not only making, but has very little awareness that they are making. An individual pack might agree to police that resonance issue in exchange for favors, but not many packs are going to want to do it for free.

    Mages - The relation here seems mostly neutral, leaning towards the side of pity. A mage would probably be fascinated by a Beast, and they have no reason to actively compete against each other for any resources, so there is nothing actively positive or negative about the relationship. However, Mages might feel that Beasts are a useful tool to be cultivated or destroyed because of their ability to use their Skeleton Key to explore new places (once the Mage has detected them), break through into enemy realms, or possibly fear that their own realms could be broken into. There are too many different kinds of mages to anticipate how they might react as a whole, except to say that they are liable to view the Beast as a useful or dangerous weapon and not a person.

    Changelings - The relation here seems to be dramatically negative. Things may change once Changeling 2E is released so this may no longer be true, but based on the 1E Changelings a Beast is going to remind them too much of the abusers that they have escaped. While some may accept this and fall into the cycle of abuse they are so familiar with (and maybe even crave), the vast majority of changelings are probably going to ostracize and stay the hell away from the Beast. Especially once they realize that the Beast can possibly follow them through realms, violating their already delicate sense of personal space and safety.

    Hunters - The relation here seems to be mostly negative. Hunters are going to see Beasts as the monsters they are, and in general are not going to want to cooperate or coexist unless that Beast is offering something pretty solid to the Hunters in question. More importantly, Hunters seem like they could easily become involved with Heroes and join forces to track down a beast. Even if they are hesitant about the some of the bullshit a Hero is saying, they might still use that Hero to finish their own hunt for a Beast. More disturbing, is it possible for Heroes to outright join Hunter societies and form entire cabals of Heroes with Hunter assets?

    What are your thoughts? What about some of the other supernatural splats?

      Vampires- Heroes aren't any particular threat to the masquerade, heroes don't investigate they track with their built in beast sense, and the book flat out mentions that a hero isn't going to know what a vampire is unless they start doing something obvious like baring fangs.

      You've also apparently forgotten that elder vampires need to start looking for alternate food sources when humans stop counting, not only do beasts count as a valid alternate food source, but so do heroes. So an elder vampire can get food that is nice enough to deliver itself as well as an ally that can make their powers stronger, win win.

      Werewolf- Werewolves only care about resonance when it causes spirits to get frisky, spirits that can feed by just hanging out with a beast is a spirit that doesn't need to urge or possess someone to get their daily bread. Spirits are also just as valid targets for prey or punishment as anyone else, so there are beasts who can feed by directly by helping werewolves hunt, even without family dinner.

      Changeling- I won't argue with the fact that Beasts claim kinship with the keepers is going to upset more than a few changelings, there are also going to be plenty of changelings who like the thought of feeding their keepers, loyalists, privateers, hostile hobgoblins, psycho fetches, etc, to a friendly dragon. For less trusting changelings, beasts arent immune to oaths.

      Hunter- Hunters, by and large, aren't fond of serial killers, which is what those heroes who slay beasts are, a typical hunter is probably going to assume that a hero is a particularly delusional slasher, and act accordingly. As for hunter relationships with beasts, that really all depends. Many hunters aren't of the "it's not human, kill it til it dies" mentality. Some hunters hunt criminals, so do some beasts, so beasts and hunters can form their own hunter parties. The AO include diplomats to supernaturals, who better to approach then the "sticks their noses in everyone's business" beasts? Also, since kinship applies to lucifuge or otodo, a beast can be a source of help or information to members of those conspiracies.

              That being said, if you want conflict between beasts and other supernaturals, it's not hard to imagine.

              Kinship is a two edged sword when it comes to crossovers.

              In a vampire game, the beast you meet might already have kinship with Belial's Brood, VII, druagr. or the strix.

              In a werewolf game, the beast could claim kinship with the pure, balehounds, spirits, hosts, or claimed.

              In mage, the mad, reapers, scelesti, and other left hand types.

              Promethean- Centimani and pandorans.

              Changelings- Privateers, loyalists, the soulless, hostile hobgoblins, true fae.

              Hunter- Well duh, they claim kinship with pretty much everything hunters tend to hunt, barring regular criminals, I'd throw in cultists with dread powers in the mostly human kin category.

              Geists- Sanctified, abmortals, wretched, kerberoi, or the vacant.

              Mummy- the deceived, and the lifeless in greater and lesser categories.

              Demon- cryptids

              So yeah whether a beast in a crossover game is the players best friend or archnemesis will completely depend on the beast in question.

                                                          In the Social Maneuvering rules, Impressions govern exactly one thing: time between rolls to open doors.
                                                          Despite its name, impression has absolutely zero effect on your actual chances of influencing someone. It doesn't make the actual diceroll for opening doors more likely to succeed or affect the number of doors. The only thing it does is make you succeed or fail faster.

                                                          The good impression effect doesn't even insure that you get to roll at all, as Impression only comes into play during phase 3 of Social Maneuvering (Impression). If your ST decides during Step 1 (Goals) that your Goal is unreasonable given the facts of the situation, then the encounter fails before you ever get to the point where you could apply that higher frequency of door opening rolls. So no Beasts waltzing up to Werewolves a day after killing half their pack with the Goal of making them your friend: the ST will tell you "unreasonable Goal" and you get ripped apart before Good Impression has any chance to affect the situation.
