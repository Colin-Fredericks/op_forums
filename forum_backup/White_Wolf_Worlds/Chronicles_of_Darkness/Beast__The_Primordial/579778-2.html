    Originally posted by Magnum Opus View Post
    . . .

    Skeleton Key
    A power which allow Beasts to almost instantly open gateways to any other world as far as they are aware that such gateways exist. So say a pack of werewolves want to bring the Beast and a few wolf-blooded and maybe a Vampire with them to hunt in the Hisil, the Ritemaster telling them that it will take some time since he has to open the gateway to all of them and that they'll have to take part in a rite that will last an hour, maybe more. And then the Beast just goes "Dude don't, I got this!" and opens the gate. Thanks, Beast, for ruining the scene and grabbing the spotlight again. . .
    As Beast stands now (barring further rewrites), I don't think you could use Skeleton Key in that scenario - at least not if you wanted to catch anything. If you're hunting (whether to actually bring back game, or just to see the local wildlife in their natural habitat), you have to be stealthy. The slightest noise will scare the game. Even something as simple as a twig cracking will send deer running, for example.

    And Skeleton Key is not a subtle power. Its tantamount to a SWAT team breaking in the door, as the Beast rips the gate open, "tearing the door off its hinges". A process that is immediately obvious to anything on the other side. Any animal, spirit, or (wise) human for miles around would run or hide, long before the hunters were able to cross over. So, assuming you want to catch anything, you'd still need the Ritemaster.

    Personally, I see Skeleton Key being used more as a pursuit power. Where an enemy tries to ditch the Beast for another plane of existence, and the Beast responds by going all Jack Nicholson (in The Shining). Axing in the door (err. . . gate), and shouting, "Here's Johnny!". Or breaking into an enemy's otherworldly stronghold - we've all had that nightmare where, no matter how hard you try, you can't get all the doors shut before the monster gets in, right?

                    Originally posted by Magnum Opus View Post
                    Mother's Kiss: I might have gone hyperbolic with this one, but let's just say that buffs usually don't go as far as "Here's a buff to your VERY EXISTENCE". They usually stay in a nice little box, such as Combat (D&D Bards and Cahalith) or Stealth, or Social, or whatever. It's not a buff that states that Obfuscate 1 is now perfect invisibility and the next turn to get a +2L to your attack just because a Beast blew a kiss.

                    I think the main of my concern is not with each individual powers, but from the fact that ALL Beasts have them built in, unlike Vampires or Mages or whatever you're throwing at me. I never argued that You Are Meat is too strong or that a Makara should not be a giant fish, I argued that innate abilities of a whole splat basically swat away a lot of powers that other people paid for in XP or built-in with their characters.

                    I played a Witch in Pathfinder, basically a buff character. I played an Aes Promethean. I played buff-mages in a lot of LARP, but I've never had such a wrong impression of a buff skill as Mother's Kiss. Mind, maybe it's because it seems so trivial for a Beast, whereas a Bard needs to sing for turns on end and a Mage needs to cast and bring forth arcane knowledge and Prometheans need to surge the Pyros through their very being, transmuting chaos in order. A Beast "blows a kiss" and they have a better result.
                    Actually its a dice pool for one scene that depletes, by however many dice are spent, when used because it can only fuel supernatural powers. Its also somewhat variable in how much of a pool it gives, so while it potentially can give a large dice pool of lets say 10 ( assuming the hypothetical Beast has integrity and satiety of 5 each, the supernatural resistance stat of the recipient was 3, and the recipient has family ties for an additional 3. Which requires all of those dice to come out as successes for it to happen) more likely it will yield a dice pool of three or four most of the time. This can certainly be useful, but its hardly a snap of the fingers and then the enemy falls. Also it requires the Beast to do some bleeding while speaking the target's name, while not a lot it is a bit more than just blowing a kiss or snapping your fingers. Really, to me it reads less as "You don't stand a chance without me, so I'll give you power to win," and more, "You have the best chance of any of us to pull this off, here take some of my power just in case."

                    Originally posted by Magnum Opus View Post
                    Take any movie or cut-scenes from a video game. If the character is faced with a terrible opponent and in the shadows you see another character simply snap his fingers and then the main character defeats his opponent in a few strikes before we see the shadowy figure grin, who has the spotlight? The audience is led to believe that this success had nothing to do with the main character. That is the way I feel about this power. It doesn't turn the protagonist's sword ablaze. It doesn't imbue him with courage. It doesn't make him see the weak spot in his opponent's defense. It simply...Fate victory because reasons and family and stuff.
                    Or the Shonen action thing where the hero stands up and keeps fighting past their limit because they saw their friends/ loved ones and refused to fall.


                    Originally posted by Magnum Opus View Post
                    You're right. If we forget that their mythos basically states that Werewolves and Prometheans are brothers, or that the Judges are in fact the sons and daughters of Tiamat. Same goes for Father Wolf, The Principle, The Exarchs, the whole underworld, the entirety of the Shadow, all the Gentries and Slashers (but not Hunters, because they're just humans, though Cursed humans are descendants).
                    First in a literal sense Beasts view Prometheans as the adopted kids in the family, so the mythos doesn't literally state the Garou are brothers with the Prometheans. On to the next part the Beasts believe that the supernaturals eventually crop back from primordial fear, which some Beasts believe may actually be the Dark Mother and others think she might have just been the first being to come from it, and things have descended and refined from there. Meaning if you decided to take the Dark Mother thing as an absolute that no other splat could refuse or contradict you would be basically going off the idea that if you go back far enough in the evolutionary tree and all mammals came from the same organism. (Which does very little in allowing anyone I know to claim their cat/dog as kin.)
                    Originally posted by Magnum Opus View Post
                    Going from there the players either accept that the Dark Mother is real and it overrides their own Mythos, or they flip the bird to the Beast and no one talks about it anymore (which is not better in my oppinion).
                    Or they can assume its what the Beasts think and may be accurate as far as Beasts think but doesn't work out as well since the Changeling is pretty sure her powers and form are a side-effect of getting dragged off to another world and becoming the plaything of insane fey for who knows how long.
                    Originally posted by Magnum Opus View Post
                    Family Resemblance: It goes beyond Auspex. Auspex tells you if he is or not a Super. FR tells you what monster he is and what he's good at (with an exceptional) and is totally passive. Auspex is once per scene, a single question, binary answer, active, resistable...and is not innate.
                    The innate thing is a bit more to deal with, but any supernatural attempt to camouflage
                    means it goes to a contest of wills. This means that the only easily identifiable supernaturals are the ones who are putting no more effort into hiding themselves than making it so mundane humans don't know what they are. Plus the exceptional only tells you what their best supernatural power is in the vaguest sense i.e. a vampire with high celerity will give you the impression of "vampire decently hungry and amazingly fast." Which of course also assumes you know the sign means vampire, so if its your first encounter or you've never seen anything to suggest that the smell of blood and grave dirt means vampire, it just means you smell people that reek of grave dirt and blood, but you don't know what it means beyond them not being baseline human.
