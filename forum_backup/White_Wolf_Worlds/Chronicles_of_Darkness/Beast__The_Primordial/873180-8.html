                                    Originally posted by K9ine View Post

                                    They don't. As I understand it beast as sociopathic. They may follow a code or have a conscience, but there is no significant emotional force keeping them in check.
                                    Beasts aren't sociopaths: in fact, if they have a conscience they can't be. Integrity systems aren't necessarily something that you can measure in-game (barring a few mind powers): it's an abstraction of how your character has behaved, rather than a measure of how evil/human/spirit/whatever they are. Demons also have a weird Integrity equivalent: the Cover rating is just how in-character they're behaving for a given Cover.

                                    I was discussing what makes a monster in a Mage thread, and something I said there was that Beasts are the only splat that have to be monsters: everybody else has options, albeit painful ones, that let them avoid being monsters. If a Beast stops being a monster, they unconsciously ruin the lives of people around them until their soul starves and dies. So a Beast has to come to terms with that rather than trying to cling to their old lives, and I think that's the reason they don't have an Integrity equivalent.

                                                Originally posted by K9ine View Post

                                                To add my own two cents, I personally think that Beasts, being literally half Horror and half human, can choose - not necessarily consciously - whether they think with their chemical, human meat-brain or with the metaphysical and amoral dream-mind of their Horror. In other words, the more a Beast associates himself with his Horror and the more he acts like it, the more he thinks using its mind, which is utterly sociopathic and has no real capacity to process morality or empathise with humans in a meaningful way. The more a beast associates with his human self, the more he thinks with that mind and, consequently, the more human he tends to act. Thus, as a Beast acts more like a monster, his morality becomes more and more intellectual, less something that he feels and more something that he thinks, as K9ine said.

                                                Of course, if the human is a sociopath in the first place, finding the difference between the two conjoined psyches becomes more difficult.

                                                BTW, I myself am slightly sociopathic and sometimes find getting my head around what others seem to find morally clear is quite difficult. I still consider myself to be a relatively good person though, so... yeah.
