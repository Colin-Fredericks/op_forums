                          I have these rules that I have used in my Beast games, what do y'all think?

                          Primordial Pathway Chamber Reflection

                          Opening a Primordial Pathway in the place the Chamber is reflected in no longer requires a roll, and can enter the Lair without a willpower cost (for either the Beast or the Cultists). It remains for a scene, but can be closed early by the opener.

                          Merging and Horrors

                          You don’t need to merge with your Horror when you enter your Lair or open a Primordial Pathway, unless there is immediate danger or an intruder.

                          When merged with your horror, you can use any aspect of an Atavism - normal, low Satiety, or Satiety expenditure, though the latter still requires spending Satiety.

                              ArcaneArts I read your threads for the Forswearing that corresponds to each Family. Good job! Any plans for the ones which correspond to the Inguma and Talasii?

                              Also, what do you think Hunger-equivalents for Heroes would be like? Could they be similar to my old idea of Yearnings from my not-so-good attempt at modelling Princesses as pseudo-Beasts? I like the idea that a Forswearing is a rejection of fear, while a Yearning guides a Hero on what must replace fear.

                              The Hunger for Prey becomes the Yearning for Friendship, and Predators become Companions. They nurture lasting bonds and restore broken ones by revealing the humanity that exists inside of and in tandem with monstrosity.

                              The Hunger for Power becomes the Yearning for Freedom, and Tyrants become Liberators. They stand up for those who cannot defend themselves and give opportunities for others to fulfill their true potential.

                              The Hunger for Hoard becomes the Yearning for Prosperity, and Collectors become Benefactors. They cater to the physical and emotional needs of others, and help them become independent enough to thrive on their own merits.

                              The Hunger for Ruin becomes the Yearning for Safety, and Ravagers become Sentinels. They renew other people's belief in order, law or equivalent ideal by providing support networks for those who have suffered terrible losses.

                              The Hunger for Punishment becomes the Yearning for Forgiveness, and Nemeses become Diplomats. They convince others to overcome grievances and understand wrongdoings by finding methods of compromise that acknowledge each side of a conflict.

                              The Hunger for Secrets becomes the Yearning for Trust, and Whisperers become Confidants. They protect secrets that would cause more bad than good if they ever got out, and help others deal with the consequences if they do decide to reveal the secrets.

                              The Hunger for Transgression becomes the Yearning for Wisdom, and Enablers become Advisers. They let others know when there are better options and guide them toward sensible answers.

                                              Heroes use the Bright Dream to deny the Primordial Dream, at first in mircocosm and then spreading outwards from there-but you can only lie to yourself so much.

                                              The Bright Dream and Primordial Dreams aren't in opposition to each other-the Bright Dream sprang from the Primordial Dream dreaming things up in pursuit of Satiety. Human advancement is a long line of humans finding cleverer and cleverer ways to not only eat, but to Sate their own Hunger. At the most extreme, the Bright Dream has done this so well that it feels disconnected from the Primordial, which may be one of the reasons why the Dark Mother raised Beasts(/Insatiable, depending on how dark you wanna go)-but at the end of the day, they do have a harmony between them. So much of Primordial is about that fact.

                                              So this is the point. Heroes can deny the reason why they're Hungry, but they still gotta Eat. The Insatiable think reasons' the problem in the first place, and make excess of Hunger to destroy reason. Beasts understand the relationship between their Nightmares and their Hungers, and seek to make more of themselves by making them transcendent. The overlap is there because the overlap is where the conversation is being had

                                              Hunger is the universal because everything bites, everything needs to feed. We all need some of the security that the Hungers represent, the safety and stress relief that comes with power, hoard, predation, punishment, ruin, privacy and knowledge, and transgression. Society is often at it's best when it acknowledges those needs, and it's worst when it overindulges. It is the thing that Primordial as a game cannot ignore, that it has to address.

                                              If you asked me to reduce the game down to one of it's elements to make it's point, it's Hunger. Nothing else comes even close to being the tip of the spear.

                                                      Originally posted by GibberingEloquence View Post
                                                      Fair enough. I'll make my Playable Heroes have Hungers, then. But I'll add Yearnings as an optional mechanic.
                                                      It's worth noting what the Positive Opposite of Hunger design will do for a game of Beast.

                                                      Namely, it guarentees Heroes will always be in the moral right.

                                                      Heroes, even without what I want to do with them for the Storyteller's Guide/2E, are supposed to be antagonists with dubious moral position against Beasts-maybe not universally actually bad, or least, any worse than Beasts, maybe sometimes even well justified, but it's always enough of a question to make you wonder whether the "institutional" bias of Beasts is warranted. Usually, it is, because there is a willingness to commit similar monstrosity while placing themselves above Beasts, but there's enough room for Beasts to be really bad and for some Heroes to be trying to do good that Beasts have to wrestle against that bias and be willing to contend with Heroes as individuals, having the conflict that Heroes seemingly never have. They are both assholes who start at the dark gray line between the usual gray morality of Chronicles protagonists and the black of their antagonists, and have the latitude to move up and down from there.

                                                      So, if Heroes just absolutely hunger for wholly positive pursuit, that ruins all of that. No Beast, no matter how good they may want to be, is going to compete with an entity whose metaphysical health is dependent on being, at a minimum, more moral than someone who doesn't have that account. There may be something to explore in the idea of a person who feels compelled and existentially dependent on doing constructive acts and how much one can count that as moral choice, and of course it's relationship to harm done in the name of morality, but that's fairly narrow.

                                                      Part of the reason why, in my own work on this, I gave them both Hunger, is that it anchors this element of similar kind of assholery where one is not actually better than the other. By making them oppose Fear ala contrasting the Families creates a reason they can be an attractive form of transgression over the other-who doesn't want to root for someone who gathers power and hoard and secrets, who pursues punishment or facilitates transgression, who wrecks predation and ruin upon other in the name of Victory, Enlightenment, Humility and Hearth, Health and Wholeness, Safety, Unity, and Liberation-but we can keep it clear that it's all the same sort of shit by making the ends and the means the same.

                                                      Another advantage towards the approach is that it allows us to talk about Hunger as constructive-Yes, a Beast causes shock and loss with feeding, but after that, with some limits due ot tangibility, a Hungry Child can then possibly give the taken thing back, or to the worthy, or to people who need it. They have room to completely destroy it if it's too much of a thing of strife, or to keep it and work to transform it into something that is better for the community. They can, of course, also do none of those things.

                                                      And finally, it does deny talking about the systemic problems that contribute to why Heroes get more press than Beasts, the way that the Monomyth shares space with Mummy's Law of Suffering and Mage's Lie and the presence of the God-Machine and the threat of the True Fae, and more asides.

                                                      Also, like, you want a game about the horror of those dedicated to moral ends, Mummy: The Curse (aka, The Better Angel Game) is next door.

                                                      food for thought.

                                                          Originally posted by GibberingEloquence View Post
                                                          Why, though? Aren't Heroes supposed to be metaphysically closer to the Bright Dream, at least in theory?
                                                          Gonna chime in on this from a couple turns back in the conversation:

                                                          The Bright Dream is not a morally better place than the Dark Dream. Everything in the Astral is id, to one degree or another; just because Temenotic Goetia aren't the personal fixations of an individual mind or the Dreamtime's participants in the great game of adaptation doesn't mean that they have any especial business pretending they aren't the literal representatives of culture war — it's the collective unconscious, nobody is holding them to a moral standard.

                                                          Yes, a recurring motif in the Chronicles is that the magical power associated with sociable humanity tends to be the ability to Say No To The Supernatural, but that doesn't make the driving impetus behind that denial the reverse of the crocodile-brain Hungers tap into. The superego as a character is not morally superior to the id as a character just because the one opposes the other — the fact that I keep coming back to "thank god she's on our side" as a recurring tint to Beasts as morally commendable actors should tell you something when the defining features of Heroes that Beasts tend to be concerned with in the modern era is "opposes Beasts," not "does good where Beasts do bad."

                                                          The Bright Dream is Bright because it's got other people in it, but the conceptual forces behind the human psyche have the same fundamentals as the ones behind the soul of the world. Entropy takes her due — it's inner demons all the way down.
