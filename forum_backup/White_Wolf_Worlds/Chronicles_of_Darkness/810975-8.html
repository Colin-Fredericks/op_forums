I mean, you can make a wizard and fighter team that works amazingly, regardless of the fact that both are on opposite ends of the tier list, or have a cleric who overshadows the groups witch despite the fact they are both tier 1. Tiers in CofD would serve to show how much power and versatility the different splats have. A vampire is useful in less scenarios than a mage, since a vampire will be much more specialized than a mage of the same experience would be. They both would have different strengths as individuals, have different resources, different allies, different etc, but the mage is still able to do much more than the vampire in general. Both can be completely valid characters next to each other of course, especially if they focus on different fields.

                                I'm in agreement that tiers are not really that useful for CofD. In nWoD (1e? Do we need to specify that anymore for nWoD?), I ran crossovers. We had a mage. He didn't really outshine anyone else. It was fine.

                                That said, debating the merit of tiers seems kind of off-topic for the thread OP started. I kind of wish people wouldn't come into someone's thread just to shout "BADWRONGFUN."

                                So in the spirit of participating rather than just shutting people down, here is my suggestion for Tier metrics:

                                1) Considering an individual that can do everything that the splat can do, how many situations will they have a solution for and to what degree?
                                2) Considering an individual straight out of chargen, how many situations will they have a solution for and to what degree?

                                Don't need an objective absolute measure. It's about in relation to the others.

                                The important thing in tiers is generally the following:
                                1) Versatility
                                2) Can they do anything better than anyone else/most others
                                3) Still mostly versatility
