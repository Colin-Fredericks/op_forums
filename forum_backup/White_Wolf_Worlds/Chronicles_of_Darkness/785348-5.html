                Originally posted by Aspel View Post
                Heavy Arms Putting it in a hyperlinked website would likely be considered transformative, actually.
                Not really. The transformative factor of fair-use law deals with the transformation of original works into new original derivative works. It doesn't refer to literally transforming the work from one medium to another. Taking a book you don't own the IP for, and making a movie out of it, is not fair-use transformation. The US Supreme Court has weighed in on this, saying that transformative fair use requires the addition of new content. Hyperlinks aren't content.

                Transformative use is for things like parodies, news reports, reviews, speeches, academic journals, and so on. Subnet did not transform the copyrighted material it used in a fair-use sense.

                I also disagree that "leather clad edgy vampire assassin" was ever unique to WoD.
                Ok. This isn't going anywhere because you don't actually want to discuss the nature of the suit; just point out your dead set opinion.

                So I'll go back to the more important point: Why does what a company that doesn't exist any more, who's former employees are not currently employed by the new company that owns the IP in question, matter? The old WWP lawsuit shows nothing about the new WWP's policies.

                Entire sections of copied merits/flaws/powers have also existed since before Paradox was a company.
                Yes, and as Paradox finds out about them, it's likely more C&D orders will be sent out.

                However, there's an important caveat here for older and smaller fans sites/blogs. The Dark Pack fan license provided fan sites with open use of copyrighted material within certain restrictions (which Subnet violated). So a large number of older fan sites are most likely within the Dark Pack allowances and would be grandfathered into protected status from C&D letters as long as they don't add more content (esp. new content the Dark Pack license wouldn't have covered).

                As well, both the old WWP and CCP went after websites that went over the line. I can say for an absolute fact (because I was involved) that the old WWP did issue C&D letters and took further actions against websites that were similar to Subnet; and got them shut down.

                As for why I feel that Dracula might go after homebrew--and, also, please don't call Subnet "Piracy". Whether or not you even agree with piracy, the term still has a meaning, and that site did not fit that meaning--is because he flat out stated during his intro talk that some people have been a little "too inspired by" World of Darkness.
                So, two very disparate points there:

                1) Martin (note: why do you feel it's cool to constantly call a man Dracula because he posed for the Tzimisce signature character pic, but feel like it's also cool to try to police other people's posting styles or term usage?) was pretty clearly talking about not letting the WoD get pushed aside like D&D did by Pathfinder. He wants the WoD to be the go to modern horror gaming franchise, and the management of the property under CCP has left if very open to losing that status (if it hasn't already).

                2) I'm calling Subnet piracy because that's exactly what it was. Copyright infringement has be called piracy for at least 130 years. That's the language, that's part of the meaning of the word. Subnet did not have the rights to post what they did, nor did they post it in a fashion that falls under any fair use metric that would allow them to post it regardless of their lack of the possession of those rights.

                Also, of note, you yourself have already pointed out that in many cases, Onyx Path does give away free stuff, in the form of the kickstarter drafts and open development.
                Yes, but that doesn't actually remove the copyright protections of those products. When Onyx Path gives stuff away for free, it is not an open license to reproduce those products on your own. Those documents still belong to Paradox legally, and Paradox still maintains the right to decide on reproduction and distribution of those products.

                In many game lines you cannot. WtA, MtAc, HtR, Orpheus, and almost all the CofD games don't allow such things with standard character creation. So even if that was a fair argument for a few games, it wasn't for all of them, and Subnet put up this stuff for all of them.

                        Speaking of Dark Pack.

                        Recently, we were contacted by one of our partners in regards to the community website subnet due to the fact that it has been re-posting large amounts of our published back catalog content that is still easily accessible and readily available for purchase. While we are supporters of community fan pages that help facilitate the enjoyment of our games and brands, White Wolf has always maintained a standard set amount of reasonable guidelines for said fan-sites to follow (The Dark Spiral/Dark Pack) that don’t infringe on our rights or the rights of our partners. White Wolf and its partners have been pioneers to bringing RPG products to the digital medium and making them available in a number of formats. We will continue to do this and you should expect to see more announcements on this in the near future. We want you, the community to know that your voice in desiring to have convenient access to our content in multiple forms has been heard.
                        ...
                        In an effort to minimize these types of situations in the future and help provide clarity on this issue, we want the community to know that we are actively in the process of revising the “Dark Pack” fan site guidelines for the community to use and those will be available for the public very soon.
                        And more specifically in relation to Aspel's concerns.

                        We are NOT engaged in a massive internet crackdown or witch hunt. However, when something is serious enough to having been brought to our attention that compromises both our own business and the financial well being of our partners, at that point we need to act, and we did.
