    I need help liking this game....

    05-15-2015, 01:56 PM
    I'm trying. Really. One of my big issues with the 1st edition was that the tribes weren't actually tribes. Not in the truest sense. And that would be ok, if I could figure out what they actually were. Are they like denominations of the same religion? Mystery cults? I have no frame of reference. Stew Wilson said the closest thing would be branches of Free Masonry. Well, I'm not a free mason. So I don't really get that either. The whole process of how a Werewolf goes about joining a tribe seems a bit... I hate to say "unrealistic" when referencing a game about Werewolves... but, yeah. So, I undergo my first change and (if I'm lucky) I'm discovered by another pack of Werewolves before I cause too much carnage. Ok, cool. I'm with you so far. Then they tell me that I'm expected to join a tribe and to pick one. Like, soon. When, in all likelihood, I MIGHT know one or two members of said tribe with only a modicum of information about them. How do I even know what it means to be a Blood Talon/Storm Lord/whatever? Maybe most (smart) young Werewolves spend month or years making that decision, and actually travel around looking to meet other members of their potential tribe before making a decision. But that doesn't appear to be implied by the setting. I get that, if one particular tribe dominates a certain area, new Werewolves are more likely to join that tribe than another. That makes sense to me. But if I'm discovered by a multi-tribal pack to begin with, I'm supposed to just pick one? I feel like the 2nd Edition tries to remedy this, somewhat, by tying the tribes to a "chosen prey." But this just seems a bit contrived to me. And it creates the problem of "I just found out that I'm a werewolf, how they hell do I know what my chosen prey is?" And finally, I feel like the tribes just aren't archetypal enough. I feel like I could take almost any character concept and come up with a logical reason to make him a member of any tribe. So help me out here, guys. What am I missing? I love the thematic focus of the game (the wolf must hunt). But beyond that I'm just sort of meh.

      Character creation generally assumes you've been active long enough to choose a tribe on your own, whether you've been taken in by a protectorate or a nomad of that tribe found you and saw potential in you.

      Obviously not all werewolves are members of tribes, and not all werewolves start as members of tribes. Like you said, most young werewolves spend time making that decision. If you spend a week between First Change and joining a tribe, your backstory should account for that. Since the tribes aren't exactly at war, even in territory where one tribe is more plentiful there are likely to be members of other tribes.

      The tribes specify a focus far beyond simply that of their chosen prey, each tribe is about a different thing, whether it's embracing civilization or honoring a territory or delving into the shadow.

              Yeah, the character creation rules assume that a starting character has been a werewolf long enough to learn the ropes, explore the different tribes and what they stand for, what they have to offer, figured out which one is the best fit for them, joined a pack or formed one of their own, bonded with its totem, learned First Tongue, done a few Renown-worthy deeds, gained a few Gift Facets, and learned a few Rites, all in their background. That probably takes at least a few months, maybe even a year or more. If you want to play a character fresh off the First Change, you should make a human character, then give them an Auspice, a dot of its associated Renown, the first dot of the appropriate Moon Gift, and start at Harmony 9. Add the other parts of the Werewolf Template as they're earned in-game.

              As for what the tribes are, think of them like extended support networks for like-minded werewolves. Personally, I think the closest analogy is Fraternities or Sororities. And, of course, not every werewolf joins a Tribe. If you can't see any reason your character would want to associate themselves with one of the Tribes, that character might make an excellent Ghost Wolf.

                  Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                  As for what the tribes are, think of them like extended support networks for like-minded werewolves. ...
                  And, of course, not every werewolf joins a Tribe. If you can't see any reason your character would want to associate themselves with one of the Tribes, that character might make an excellent Ghost Wolf.
                  While the Tribes aren't supposed to be archetypical per se, each does have its own ethos and overarching culture. Iron Masters are far more interested in interacting with and integrating with human culture than any of the others. Hunters in Darkness are far more interested in keeping their territory clean than any of the others. Bone Shadows are far more interested in the Hisil and its denizens than the others. Storm Lords are staunch individualists who value fortitude above all else and Blood Talons are battle-crazed warriors.
                  Now, there's a lot more to any of them than just that, as shown in their write ups, but the point is that they all have unifying qualities and interests even outside the "sacred prey". If you have an affinity for dealing with spirits and you want to grow in that, being a Bone Shadow is a good way to get in contact with people who have been doing that for years.
                  Sure, a fresh Werewolf won't know any of that, but older ones will. Unless your wolf is all on its own, it will quickly be in contact with people who know what Tribe helps which kinds of people thrive. And they should also understand forcing someone into a Tribe who's ethos they don't intrinsically share will do nobody any good. As such, most Werewolves get pointed in the right direction reasonably quickly.
                  As to why they're called "Tribes" - that's a fair question. Most "tribes" in the rest of the world are things you're born into. It is the nation of your birth, albiet typically a small nation. It's hard to say where you were born from as a Werewolf, though, as its unlikely that your parents were Werewolves. In the end, your Pack is your family. Tribes are, in a way, just really really big packs. Tribes are more about a joint culture than Pack are, so the analogy ends up holding up pretty well: families are to Packs as tribes/nations are to Tribes.

                  That all said in the Ghost Wolves writeup in the book, they view the various Tribes as creepy wolf-cults. It's a totally reasonable stance, and if its yours, maybe you would make a good Ghost Wolf.

                  More than anything, don't get hung up on that one word. The game has a lot to offer and the nomenclature isn't really all that important.

                                      On the matter of "why would a multi-tribe Pack exist", part of it is location as nofather said. Another part is pragmatism. Even though the Hunters (and Iron Masters) care most deeply about their territory, its important to everyone. And everyone realizes that while thier sacred prey is the most dangerous, it's not the only threat around. Much like the doctor/engineer/artist suggestion, it's good to have friends around that know how to deal with a variety of problems. At any given time, you can take the lead at what you're good at, but your physically-immediate in-group won't be faced with out of context problems as often if it includes people with multiple viewpoints.

                                      That said, the idea of single-Tribe packs is a highly supported one, especially in certain areas. I remember reading in one of the fictions where one Werewolf was reminiscing and said "I came from a place where being a Uratha meant being a Blood Talon." If you've got a relatively small territory bounded by the territory of other Packs, then maybe you can afford to have blind spots and let other (allied) Packs clean up the messes you can't handle. In exchange, you'll be masters of whatever your Tribe focuses on and can deal with that for everyone around.

                                      The main reason there seems to be a focus on multi-Tribe packs is the Don't Say No commandment. If your group of players feel pulled to different Tribes, then let them play different Tribes, even if it doesn't make a ton of sense from some perspectives. They tried really hard to get it to make sense.
                                      Or, since you're the Storyteller, you could say "hey guys, we're gonna play a single-Tribe Pack" and see if they can agree on one. That could be an interesting story, too, if some people are chafing at their Tribal expectations and, as a group, they lack the tools to do certain jobs.

                                              When considering the tribes, it is important to take into account the basic situation of a newly changed werewolf and of the setting at a larger scale.


                                              You've changed and you've probably killed someone. You already know what it's like to be hunted - strange shadowy beings stalking you, trying to kill you. Spirits and rats infesting your home and trying to get close to the people you care about. You've probably already fought for your life once or twice - and won, obviously, but it doesn't take much smarts to figure out that the next time could well be different.


                                              Your skin doesn't act the way it used to, and neither do your emotions, senses and appetites, even. You can't help looking at other people and, sometimes, thinking "food". You feel claustrophobic in your human skin, and when previously you felt like hitting somebody who pissed you off, now it's a challenge not to tear his throat out.

                                              This is where the tribes come in. This is what the tribes have to offer:


                                              As a werewolf, you'll have to fight for your life; probably on a regular basis. The Blood Talons show you how. They know how to protect yourself and those close to you; Fenris-Ur will teach you how to be an effective warrior. They also give you ways how to deal with the burning rage affecting all werewolves. The Blood Talons are the ones you approach when you don't want to wake up in the blood of your husband one day, and when you want to survive the next time the Predator Kings show up from nowhere and start biting huge chunks of flesh out of your flesh.


                                              This scary, hostile world of predator and prey you are now a part of? The Bone Shadows best understand how it works and how to make sense of it. They explain spirits. They tell you which places in the Shadow to avoid and where you'll be safe. Death Wolf provides you with the means of fighting your enemies with ways other than using your claws. And they also show you how to make sense of the death and pain and grief that always seems to follow the Forsaken around. They provide meaning in a world that doesn't appear to have any, at the first glance.


                                              There's a wolf in your heart, your bones and your flesh, and it'll never go away. The Hunters in Darkness know that wolf better than any other tribe. They know how to make sense of the rage, the new senses, instincts, demands of the wolf-heart. They also teach you how to hunt effectively, and how to survive when you're prey. Things like where to best hide, how to best track your prey, how to kill prey in as little time as possible are natural to the children of the Black Wolf.


                                              Werewolves are not human. The human skin doesn't change their basic nature; but despite the wolf-heart and spirit-soul there's still a human mind. After the traumatic experience of the First Change, which more often than not, ends with lots of blood, the Iron Masters are the ones who help a young werewolf to reintegrate into the human culture and world and to reconcile wolf-heart with human-mind. They're the ones who know how a wolf hides and hunts among sheep. Red Wolf knows the spirits that hunt in the cities and close to humanity, and his children will teach you how to hunt with cunning, how to adapt to situations that cannot otherwise be dealt with.


                                              The Storm Lords don't pick you up when you're weak. They won't comfort you in your time of need. Winter Wolf does not care about your weakness and failings. The Storm Lords cannot soothe any pain you feel from the First Change or from being what you are. The Winter Wolf will instead be here in your moments of strength and endurance. His children show you how to be your best, strongest self - how to stare this cold, dark, hostile world that wants you dead at every turn, in the face, and how to make it back down with an alpha's growl. They know how to fight. They know the spirit world, the worlds of wolf and men. All of these things are part of their responsibility.


                                              ... still three tribes left to cover!


                                              You've been beaten and bruised by this world. There's nothing that feels right. Friends? Jobs? Relationships? Family? Nothing has ever worked out for you. The world of men has let you down and failed you more often than you can count.

                                              No more. Something's got to give.

                                              Your old life is gone. You might have killed those you love during your first change. You're a monster and a hunter now. There's no way back.
                                              It's not your fault. It's THEM. This world isn't how it should be.
                                              There is hope. You've got the oldest, nastiest, toughest of all Wolf Gods on your side, giving you strength, rage, purpose and a mission - rebuild this world in your - in his - image. Make them all pay what they did to you. It's what you should do. It's what's natural to do.


                                              The First Change isn't the end of your old life, it's you reaching your destiny and becoming a true member of your family. It's when you realize your potential and the universe gives you what you're owed.
                                              The Ivory Claws teach you how people are not born equal. There are those whose blood and ancestry matters more. You're one of the lucky ones. You're special - the purest of the Pure. You're better than other people. It's quite simply genetics.
                                              This world belongs to its true rulers. The Chosen Ones, those selected by Silver Wolf to run this world, must take command.
                                              Go hunt those who would refuse to obey.


                                              Spirits stalk and try to kill you. The Hosts are at your heels, only waiting to take you down once you're weak. Werewolves hunt you as the competition you are.
                                              It's good, so very good, to have friends in this world. People who look out for you. Spirits who let you live as long as you abase yourself before them instead of attempting, vainly, to keep them in check. The Fire-Touched have more to offer than just protection from the spirits, strength in numbers and sympathetic ears for all your troubles and worries. They also have faith, insight and the certainty that their cause is the right one. And they're always, without exception, honest to you - Rabid Wolf sees lying as one of the worst of sins.
                                              Only a lunatic would refuse to take their offer and join them.

                                                  Note that you have been a werewolf long enough to:

                                                  1) Lower your Harmony from 9 (where it starts after First Change) to 7.
                                                  2) Commit deeds worthy of Renown three times.
                                                  3) Get two or three Gifts out of spirits (probably by partaking in a Siskur-Dah).

                                                          If your problem is actually the question of why your character would join this tribe, as opposed to the player who has read the book and judged the advantages, then flip the coin. Each tribe follows one of the first born and he picks you. Storm Wolf appears to you in the rain, Death Wolf haunts your dreams, one way or another they call you to where you belong. A Ghost Wolf either doesn't receive a calling or chooses to reject the one he does.
                                                          As for thinking of tribes as a religion, the Forsaken are the religion the tribes are just parts of a greater whole. To use the Catholic analogy the tribes are like Jesuit priests vs Benedictine monks, all part of the same church yet fulfilling different roles within it. That's why multi tribe packs work, they all serve the same cause, they just have different parts to play.
