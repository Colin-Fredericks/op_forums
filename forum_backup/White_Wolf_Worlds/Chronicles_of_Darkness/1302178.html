    Changeling in other languages

    04-03-2019, 09:06 AM
    Let's get homebrew translations going.

    I think I'm not the only one who has run Changeling in a language other than English (in my case Swedish) and I thought that maybe I'm not the only one who has translated the specific words of the game, the lexicon, or at least some of it, to that language. I searched for a place where maybe that was already covered, but I found none, so I made a topic for it here. Feel free to use these, and submit your own versions. I'll update this original post with any contributions in any language.

              Since most fairy tales find their origin in Germanic folklore (or at least got popularized in Germany by the brothers Grimm), I'll throw in the translations to German, in case anyone is interested:

              Arcadia - Arcadia

              Changeling - Wechselbalg (which literally means 'switched baby')

              Court - Gericht

              Fae, True Fae, Fairies - Fee, Wahre Feen, Elfen

              Fetch - Geholt ('Geholt' is the literal German word for 'fetch', but this is not an accurate translation. The accurate translation for 'fetch' would be 'Wechselbalg', the same word used for changeling, because what we call a fetch in CtL is the changeling in Germanic folklore.)

              Freehold - Freier Halt

              Gateway - Tor (gate), Durchgang (gateway) or Zugang (entrance)

              Goblin, Hobgoblin - Kobold (yes, I know on most modern western fantasies Kobold are different creatures, but originally "goblin" was the English translation of the German word "Kobold".)

              Hedge - Hecke, Einfassung ('die Hecke' is an accurate translation, but I personally feel that 'die Einfassung' is a more elegant translation, it captures what 'the Hedge' is better.)

              Hedgespinning - Heckenspinnen

              Hollow - Hohl

              Keeper - Betreuer

              Mask - Maske

              Mien - Miene

              Trod - Getreten


              Disclaimer: I'm not German myself, I'm Dutch, but I have studied German and it's the 3rd best language I speak, read and write. If any actual German person reads this and has any issue with my translations, feel free to correct them.

                                  Apologies for the necromancy, but I've been working on a setting in Wales, and wished to contribute my thoughts and translations of Changeling terms into Welsh:
                                  • Crimbil - Changeling
                                  • Y Colledig - The Lost
                                  • Ellyll - Fae
                                  • Tylwyth Teg - True Fae
                                  • Bendith y Mamau - Another term for the True Fae, sometimes meant in reference specifically to Keepers (In addition, Welsh Begotten sometimes refer to themselves as Bendith y Fam.)
                                  • Annwn - Arcadia
                                  • Newid - Fetch
                                  • Contract - Contract
                                    • Contract Annwn - Arcadian Contract
                                    • Contract Cwrt - Court Contract
                                    • Contract Bwgan - Goblin Contract
                                  • Teyrndlws (pl. Teyrndlysau) - Regalia
                                  • Hudoliaeth - Glamour
                                  • Hawlrydd - Freehold
                                  • Cwrt - Court
                                    • Cwrt y Gwanwyn - Spring Court
                                    • Cwrt yr Haf - Summer Court
                                    • Cwrt yr Hydref - Autumn Court
                                    • Cwrt y Gaeaf - Winter Court
                                  • Gwedd - Mien
                                  • Rhith - Mask
                                  • Y Berth - The Hedge
                                  • Y Draen - The Thorns
                                  • Twll - Hollow
                                  • Rhagfur - Bastion
                                  • Pwca - Hobgoblin
                                  • Bwci-bo - Hedge Ghost
                                  • Nyddu'r Berth - Hedgespinning
                                  • Celfyddyd Freuddwyd - Dreamweaving
                                  • Cŵn Annwn - Huntsmen (also referred to as Helwyr or Gwyrddmyn) (Also the name of a Lodge of Ghost Wolves who've bound themselves to a True Fae)
                                  • Dyled Bwgan - Goblin Debt
                                  • Brenhines Fwgan - Goblin Queen
                                  • Enw - Title
                                  • Addewid - Pledge
                                    • Tynged - Sealing
                                    • Llw - Oath
                                    • Bargen - Bargain
                                    • Enwir - Oathbreaker
                                  • Agwedd - Seeming (Traditionally, Welsh changelings use their Seemings as a title, eg, a Beast named Ceri might refer to herself as Ceri Wyllt.)
                                    • Gwyllt - Beast
                                    • Godywyll - Darkling
                                    • Elfennol - Elemental
                                    • Goreuwedd - Fairest
                                    • Cawr - Ogre
                                    • Coblyn - Wizened
                                  • Cydnabod (colloquially shortened to Cyd) - Kith
                                  • Ffawd - Wyrd
                                  • Eglurdod - Clarity
                                  • Adnabod - Kenning
                                  • Tocyn - Token
                                  • Ffrwyth Bwgan - Goblin Fruit
                                  • Criglyn - Oddment
                                  • Harlach - Bedlam
                                  • Bregedd - Frailty
                                    • Diofryd - Taboo
                                    • Distryw - Bane
                                  • Arawn - A powerful and gregarious True Fae. Some Autumn Court scholars theorize the Title might be shared among multiple Gentry.
                                  • Gwyn ap Nudd - One of Arawn's other Titles
                                  • Pwyll - A legendary changeling said to have secured an oath of friendship between Arawn and the kingdom of Dyfed
                                  Some notes on my choices:
                                  • Tynged literally means "destiny," but mythically is used in a similar manner to the Irish geas.
                                  • Bregedd means "weakness" but also "frivolity," which I thought was an interesting double-entendre.
                                  • Diofryd is an oath of renunciation.
                                  • I had quite a devil of a time with "Bane." I settled on Distryw as it can mean "destruction," "ruin," or "curse." I tried (and failed) to find an equivalent to the Irish urbhaidh.
                                  • Welsh has quite a few words referring to goblin, ghost, and the like, so for those terms I went with whatever "felt" most appropriate or evocative.
                                  • Coblyn refers to small, ugly fairies who live in mines, similar to Knockers. I chose them as a translation for the Wizened because they lead polite humans to wealth.
                                  • Gwyllt literally means "wild," but is also an epithet applied to the warrior Cyledr in the tale Culhwc ac Olwen after Gwyn ap Nudd makes him eat his father's heart, which drives him to madness. In general, I tried to find precedence in the Mabinogion for my translations.
                                  • Cawr means "giant" or "devil."
                                  • "Darkling" was difficult, but I finally settled on Godywyll, which means "darksome," "not clearly seen," or "gloomy." I considered Cysgod ("Shadow") as well.
                                  • Goreuwedd means "best face." Incidentally, I think the Flowering Kith might be translated as Blodeuwedd, so a Fairest Flowering would be Blodeuwedd Oreuwedd.
                                  • "Kenning" is derived from the Icelandic kenna, meaning "to know" or "to recognize." Adnabod is the Welsh equivalent.
                                  • There are a few different words that could be translated as "Hedge." I went with Perth for its meanings of "copse," "thicket," and "jungle." Other potential translations include Cae ("field"), Bid ("bush"), or Coed ("forest").
                                  • The Huntsmen probably don't refer to themselves as Cŵn Annwn.
                                  • Ellyll is one of those words that can refer to a wide variety of supernatural beings.
                                  • A changeling can also be called plentyn newid ("swapped child"). I got crimbil from John Rhŷs's book of Welsh and Manx folklore. Fetch comes from Irish folklore, although its etymology is unclear. It might be connected to the Old Norse fylgja, a spirit that accompanies a person throughout their life. Newid means "exchange" and I imagine Welsh Lost use it euphemistically. Translating terms for Changeling is tricky since not only is most of the lexicon connected to specific cultural myths, but also there are quite a few conceptual swappings that make attempting to map things to different languages a bit of a guessing game.
                                  • Speaking of which, Wyrd is an Anglo-Saxon term for fate. The Welsh equivalent is technically Tynged, except it lacks the mythic personification as well as evocative homophony. Coel could potentially work for meaning both "omen" and "reputation." Or maybe Hudwaith for meaning "craftiness" and "trickery." Ultimately, I went for Ffawd for its meaning of "good fortune" as well as the potentially ironic "happiness."

                                          Changelings - Knytt (basically, magical being)
                                          Contracts - Kontrakt (lit. translation)
                                          Goblins - Vättar, Tomtar (probably fell into disuse after Santa Claus "Tomten" rose in poplularity), both are literal translations but the latter has more positive/helpful connotations
                                          True Fae - Troll (troll in Nordic folklore vary almost as much as trolls in pop culture), Oknytt (like Knytt but with more malevolent connotations)
                                          Keeper - Rådare (lit. ruler of a domain; older as well as dialectal form of rå, beings that were kinda the spirits of different domains or places in nature and could very well seduce, steal and/or eat people for various reasons depending on the various versions that exist in Nordic folklore)

                                          The duality of Knytt/Oknytt would imply some kinship but also antagonism between the two groups. Having those concepts built into the language may influence local Changeling politics and opinions.
