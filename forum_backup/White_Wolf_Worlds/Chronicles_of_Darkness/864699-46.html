                                                To put it another way, a typical Condition exists to be resolved and end. If a Condition is Chekhov's gun, then resolution is the gun going off. That's why it's there. It's a temporary turn in the drama.

                                                A Persistent Condition does not exist to be resolved and end. It can be resolved and end, and each Persistent Condition is usually listed with a hint of how that would be accomplished. But it's harder or stranger to do, because the Persistent Condition exists to stick around and get in the way. It's a recurring dramatic element or an Achilles heel, a major dramatic complication we know a character by. A Persistent Condition, in its role as Chekhov's gun, has a large clip; it can fire many times before it runs dry. Those moments of strife when the Persistent Condition comes into the spotlight, but isn't resolved, are the moments when the player is awarded a beat. Most Persistent Conditions give examples of situations that would qualify for this beat, separate from how to resolve the Condition.
