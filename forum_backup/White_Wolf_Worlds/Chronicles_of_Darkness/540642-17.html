    Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
    The fear of fear or stress or having to worry about the world. At least, that's how I see it. As I understand, (and I'm not a psychology major or good at understanding people in general), people sometimes hear or see things that conflicts with how the understand the world. Maybe a movie star did something terrible, and some of the fans will be unable or unwilling to believe it. They will make excuses, or verbally attack whoever brought them the news. Most often though, they will deny that whatever happened actually happened. That reaction, though...I think it's based on fear. Fear that their worldview is wrong, or they were wrong about someone, or that they trusted someone who failed them. And that's a very human thing. The Nightmare works by cultivating that reaction, by forcing the mind to fear and reject fear and discomfort.

    I'd argue that is a very frightening state of mind. Not from the perspective of the target, obviously, while they are under the influence anyways. But I think most people would find being forced to be denial about the darker nature of the world until it's in the perfect position to hurt them to be quite frightening. I personally find that quite frightening, as well as finding that I could be heavily in denial about something. Pretending that everything is ok, just because I don't like the truth, is scary, especially because it's not really a conscious decision, in my experience anyways. I mean, it's possible I'm in denial about something right now, and I'd never know.

    But all the justifications about how I think it fits don't matter if it doesn't feel right to others. Is there anything I could do to make it better, in your mind?

    I don't know if this helps, but the inspirations for this Nightmare was the joke about the monster under the bed saying there is nothing under the bed, and the child believing them and going back to sleep, and what I've heard about We Happy Few. a game in which there are people so high on a happy pill, they don't notice that the piñata they are beating with sticks and eating is actually a rat. I find both of those things creepy.
    So here's the big one-two punch of problems with the Nightmare: There is no horror in denial on its own and the common qualities found in Mage's metaphysics that this is playing off of (mostly Quiescence) don't work like this.

    A nightmare where you realize right at the end that you were eating a bowl of maggots as though it was breakfast cereal, shook hands with a skinless horror at the bus stop, and sneezed a bunch of teeth into your handkerchief is not a nightmare where everything was Actually Normal and you saw nothing out of the ordinary, nor is a nightmare where you see something so horrible you spend the rest of the day studiously rewriting events in your head to avoid thinking about it the same thing as one where you never saw the horrible thing.

    Again, look to You Are Not Alone for an established case of how Nightmares can put things into people's heads but not take them out — it's pretty close to the described example of making the victim so afraid of seeing you that they refuse to look, and it does this by putting the idea that Something Terrible Will Happen If You Look At The Thing in their head. There are ways to paint the Ignorance Is Bliss effect you're evoking with the brush of fear/dread/horror/terror, but the nature of Kinship Nightmares is such that you have to acknowledge that euphoria-induced uncaring, the delayed realizations of a tired child, and crisis/scandal fatigue are all different things.

    Do you want this Nightmare to make its victims refuse to acknowledge an event and attack anyone who tries to tell them about it? Make it trigger breaking points whenever they give that version of events credence or neglect to silence its proponents.

    Want it to do the happy-pill thing? Lean into the horror of euphorics and maybe have it inflict the Drugged Tilt and/or the Intoxicated Condition.

    Want it to make the victims the kind of people the Seers love to see? You Are A Meat Machine is right there.

    Want it to replicate the joke about the kid and the monster under the bed? I'd honestly recommend against that one for the simple reason that it's a joke about a tired kid not realizing it's odd for someone to be in their room alone talking with them, and there's Siren's Treacherous Song's base effect right there if a character wants their words to be able to be extra persuasive, but putting something similar to You Are Better Than Them's hold on breaking point rolls into play for rolls to put together facts until the end of the Nightmare may be the way to go.

    Nightmares, especially Common Nightmares, can use different symbolism to say the same thing, but they have to say a consistent thing to be able to be the Nightmares that they are — a Kinship Nightmare you derived from a Blood Talon and a Nightmare you derived from a practitioner of the Refinement of Tin should feel different from each other even if they're both Nightmares that whip a target up into a furious rage.

    Funnily enough, I actually think that this one is closest to You Can't Dig It Out or They Are All Around You. It's supposed to make the illusory sense that something is wrong with you, that something is manipulating you...which is kind of true, but there is no Mind Reading or Mind Control in the power. Just making someone think that there is. They might go to the grocery store for their tin-foil hat, or they might try to convince their hunter friends to restrain them so they can't hurt anyone.
    If the intent is something like You Are Compromised, I'd lean towards effects like intrusive thoughts and doing things without knowing why unless you have specific things in mind for what "the target believes their mind is being probed/tampered with" means mechanically.

    I believe those together cover the range of things I want the Nightmare to do. Is there anything that represents jumpscares? Like maybe setting a trigger, like when you look away from something, and looking to something creepy rushing at you look, only for it to be gone when you blink or flinch?
    That's the domain of Atavisms or fare like Behold, My True Form! unless you've got a particular mechanical manifestation of that effect in mind.

                This Is Beyond Your Understanding (Nasnas)

                No. This isn’t right. Things like this can’t happen. Your mind must be playing tricks on you. Better to just forget. Oh dear God, let you just forget.
                Dice Pool: Intelligence + Satiety vs. Resolve + Supernatural Tolerance
                Normal: The capacity for the human mind to reject things that do not conform to its worldview is exceptional. More than this, however, there are supernatural forces that can strain or even break a mortal’s mind upon witnessing them. This Nightmare stirs up those forces in the human soul, forcing the victim to rationalize or forget such experiences.
                At the end of any scene during which the victim witnessed an overtly supernatural occurrence, she suffers a breaking point. In addition to the normal results of a breaking point roll, on a failure or dramatic failure, she completely forgets the supernatural occurrence. On a normal success, she will rationalize or re-contextualize her memory of the event towards the mundane. On an exceptional success the victim will remember the event with clarity.
                High Satiety: As normal, except the breaking point is penalized by one-half the Beast’s Satiety, rounded up, and Beast may choose which Condition (if any) the victim acquires as a result of the breaking point.
                Satiety Expenditure: In addition to the normal or high satiety effects, the victim’s rejection of the supernatural is so strong it actually reinforces her soul against it (or maybe it’s just that the forces stirred up by this Nightmare are also inimical to other supernatural forces). For the duration of the Nightmare, the victim adds one-half the Beast’s Satiety, rounded up, to her Supernatural Tolerance for the purposes of resisting or contesting other supernatural effects.
                Exceptional Success: Each time the victim fails a breaking point as a result of witnessing an overtly supernatural occurrence, it counts as fulfilling the Beast’s Hunger (base Satiety potential 3).

                The longer I study science the more I am convinced that it is functionally indistinguishable from what our ancestors would refer to as sorcery. And I would know, being both scientist and sorcerer.

                            Get Out Of My Head!

                            What's that strange pressure at the back of my mind? Wait, I don't normally act like this. Something is wrong with me!

                            Dice Pool: Wits + Satiety vs. Composure + Supernatural Tolerance
                            Normal: The subject feels as if there illusory compulsion towards some set of actions the subject finds supremely repulsive. In reality, there is only the feeling of something else tampering with their mind.. The subject gains the Delusional condition.
                            High Satiety: The subject find the pressure of whatever malaise grips them to be almost unbearable, coming to the conclusion that they must fight back or be crushed. When a opportunity occurs to indulge in one of the illusionary urges, the subject reflexively spends a point of willpower in order to resist the influence. If the subject has no willpower to spend, they instead stand there, visibly straining against the hostile influence. This only occurs once per opportunity.
                            Satiety Expenditure: The Beast may spend multiple Satiety on this effect. In addition to the Normal or High Satiety effects, the subject can only put up the most marginal front of resistance to other mental influences, as the Nightmare focuses all of the subjects mental efforts on itself. The subject rolls a chance die to resist mental effects while under the influence of this Nightmare per Satiety spent.
                            Exceptional Success: The subject immediately loses one point of Willpower.

                                        It's Coming Out Of The Screen (Common Nightmare)
                                        That's not possible, it's just a movie. You must be tired and imagining things. But what if it's real? What else could be lurking behind every media you see? There's no way to be sure, and your sense of truth is unraveling as that creature places its hand on your shoulder and smiles at you.

                                        Dice Pool: None, see below.
                                        Action: Reflexive

                                        Normal: The Beast takes a –2 penalty on her roll to invoke another Nightmare, and in return she can infuse a piece of media with her Nightmare. The first person to observe that piece of media being displayed becomes the target of the infused Nightmare, which occurs at the climax of the media's display.

                                        "A piece of media" is defined as any source of information which contains audiovisual elements and an associated narrative, usually one based on the Beast's Family, Hunger and Legend. Most Beasts use digital files for ease of storage and display, but in such cases the Nightmare is contained within the hardware instead of the software (i.e. it does not inhabit every single copy of the digital file). Beasts can also infuse Nightmares within non-digital media, such as books, paintings and songs.

                                        High Satiety: The infused Nightmare can be activated upon the victim taking a specific action in the same scene that they observed the media, like looking into a mirror, eating a snack or answering a phone call. Furthermore, if the Beast wills it, the victim suffers an automatic Failure on his next roll to differentiate between reality and fiction. If he had a preexisting negative Condition that matches the narrative of the media, downgrade his result into a Dramatic Failure.

                                        Satiety Expenditure: The invocation roll for the other Nightmare suffers no penalty and earns an Exceptional Success with three successes rather than five.

                                        Exceptional Success: As per the invoked Nightmare.

                                                To Cast Out Devils Through Baalzebub

                                                You laughed using your puppet’s lungs when the so-called exorcist arrived. You stopped laughing when you saw the hunger in its eyes.

                                                Dice Pool: Presence + Satiety vs. Resolve + Supernatural Tolerance

                                                Normal: The Horror’s presence overshadows the influence of other supernatural forces. The Beast chooses a single supernatural Condition or active supernatural effect directly affecting the victim; if the Beast succeeds at a Clash of Wills against the originator of the effect, that effect is suppressed (for the victim only) for the duration of the Nightmare.
                                                This Nightmare cannot affect inherent abilities; for example, it can suppress the effects of a mage’s spell or a werewolf’s Gift on a victim, but it cannot affect the mage’s Mage Sight, or the werewolf’s ability to shapeshift.

                                                High Satiety: As normal, except the Beast has 8-again on any resulting Clash of Wills.

                                                Satiety Expenditure: As above, except if the Beats wins the Clash of Wills, the targeted effect immediately ends, instead of merely being suppressed, and re-affecting the victim with the same effect during the Nightmare’s duration prompts another Clash of Wills.

                                                Exceptional Success: If the Beast succeeds in suppressing (or removing) the effect, it counts as fulfilling the Beast’s Hunger (based Satiety potential of 3).​

                                                    Help I can't stop.

                                                    Don’t Look At It

                                                    You can feel its gaze, like a chill down your spine. Don’t acknowledge it. You know if you do, something terrible will happen.

                                                    Dice Pool: Presence + Satiety vs. Composure + Supernatural Tolerance

                                                    Normal: The visage of the Beast distorts in the victim's eyes, becomes painful to look at, difficult to make out, or simply horrifying. The victim suffers a -2 penalty to any rolls that require them to look at the Beast, even if they attempt the action while averting their gaze (depending on the action, not looking at their target might warrant even higher penalties).

                                                    High Satiety: As normal, but the penalty increases to -4. Additionally, the Beast becomes automatically aware whenever the victim is looking at it. This makes it practically impossible for the victim to ambush the Beast. Furthermore, the Beast can psychically signal to the victim it is looking at them!

                                                    Satiety Expenditure: The Beast’s presence looms in the victim’s mind, whether they’re looking at it or not. The victim gains the Frightened Condition, with the Beast being the object of their fear.

                                                    Exceptional Success: When the victim directly views the Beast for the first time, they suffer a breaking point.​

                                                    Now You Know the Truth

                                                    You finally understand. You wish you didn’t, but you can’t stop now.

                                                    Dice Pool: Manipulation + Satiety vs. Resolve + Supernatural Tolerance

                                                    Normal: The victim is convinced that he has achieved some transcendental understanding of a supernatural nature, the actual content of which being rather vague to anyone he tries to elucidate it to. The victim gains the Obsession Condition for the duration of the Nightmare, dedicated towards investigating or acquiring further understanding of anything supernatural.

                                                    High Satiety: As normal, and in addition the Beast can point the victim’s obsession in a general direction (e.g. “vampires are real”). Also, the victim becomes particularly vulnerable to manipulation. Offering evidence or to assist in his pursuit of his obsession counts as Soft Leverage in Social Maneuvering.

                                                    Satiety Expenditure: The victim’s mind becomes fixated on his obsession. The victim’s player must succeed at a Resolve + Composure roll to resist investigating anything that could be construed as supernatural (or related to his specific obsession with the High Satiety effect). However the 9-again quality he gains from the Obsession Condition upgrades to 8-again.

                                                    When combined with the High Satiety effect, the victim temporarily gains the Unseen Sense Merit, tuned for a type of Kin of the Beast’s choice. ​

                                                    The Body is a Plaything of the Mind

                                                    Pain pulses in your skull. You can’t blink, can’t scream, only watch as your own hand, shaking, reaches for the knife.

                                                    Unlike most Nightmares, The Body is a Plaything of the Mind is a single attack action, which allows follow-up actions, and does not have a scene-long effect.

                                                    Dice Pool: Presence + Satiety - Stamina

                                                    Normal: The Beast seizes the victim’s mind with psychic tendrils, battering his consciousness and establishing a kind of mental “grapple”. This follows the usual grappling rules, except as noted below.
                                                    • Instead of Strength + Brawl, contested rolls to maintain the grapple or to execute an available grapple option use Presence + Satiety for the Beast, and Stamina + Supernatural Tolerance for the victim. Any "meta" Nightmares that applied a penalty to the activation roll of this Nightmare also apply that penalty to the Beast's roll.
                                                    • The Beast only needs to be able to see the victim to maintain the grapple, and executing grapple options requires no movement on her part. Any physical consequences are the result of the victim's body moving involuntarily.
                                                    • If the victim wins the contest normally, his only grapple option is to break free. If the victim wins with an exceptional success, or the Beast loses with a dramatic failure, then the Beast’s mind becomes vulnerable, giving the victim access to the same options as the Beast for that turn.
                                                    • With the Normal effect, the Beast is limited to imprecise mental noise that overwhelms the victim’s conscious control. She only has access to the damage (via neurological over-stimulation), disarm, drop prone, and hold options. The Beast does not lose her defense when using the hold option, and she does not fall to the ground along with the victim when using the drop prone option.
                                                    High Satiety: The Beast can exert more precise control over the victim, gaining access to all of the standard grappling options if she wins the contested roll. She can only effectively use the take cover option if she (or an ally) is adjacent to the victim.
                                                    Additionally, in place of the usual grapple options, the Beast can instead cause the victim to move up to their normal Speed, manipulate an object within their reach, or perform another physical action. The victim’s movement will appear jerky and unnatural to any observers. Roll the Beast’s net successes on the grapple contest as a die pool if the success of the compelled action is in doubt, such as if another character contests the action.

                                                    Satiety Expenditure: As normal, but the Beast’s contested rolls to maintain the grapple or to execute an available grapple option are reflexive, rather than requiring her action each turn. If she wins the contested roll, she can use her action to execute an additional grapple option against the victim that turn.

                                                    Exceptional Success: The Beast can immediately execute an available grapple option on the victim.​

                                                    What Man Was Not Meant to Know

                                                    Don’t think about it. You should never have opened that book. Now you can’t get it out of your head. Don’t think about it!

                                                    Dice Pool: Intelligence + Satiety vs. Resolve + Supernatural Tolerance

                                                    Normal: The victim believes he has learned something unfit for mortal minds, and now it worms away at the back of his mind, eroding his sanity by its mere presence. The victim suffers -2 to rolls involving Resolve.

                                                    High Satiety: As normal, but in addition the victim suffers -2 to rolls involving Composure.

                                                    Satiety Expenditure: The victim suffers the Persistent Madness Condition, which must be resolved normally.

                                                    You Can’t Trust Your Eyes

                                                    In the corner of your eye, you catch a glimpse of your reflection, bloody, hands reaching out of the mirror towards you. You spin, but the gruesome sight is gone. There isn’t even a mirror.

                                                    Dice Pool: Wits + Satiety vs. Composure + Supernatural Tolerance

                                                    Normal: The victim becomes plagued by false sensory input, such as phantom voices, unexplained temperature irregularities, or faces appearing in the corners of their vision. These hallucinations afflict the victim with the Distracted Condition. While the Beast can set a general theme for the hallucinations, they have no control over specific details.

                                                    High Satiety: The Beast can project a specific hallucination of a person, creature, or object into the victim’s mind. The victim can interact with this hallucination without realizing anything is amiss, and it will respond as the victim’s subconsciousness expects, tending towards self-justification to avoid spoiling the illusion (“this gun is real, I just keep missing”). Additionally, so long as the Beast can see the victim, the Beast can directly “puppet” the hallucination, controlling its behavior, as an Instant action.

                                                    Satiety Expenditure: Everything the victim experiences takes on a psychedelic, dreamlike quality, rendering him completely unable to determine what is real and what is just in his head. The -2 penalty from the Distracted Condition applies to all rolls.
                                                    When combined with the High Satiety effect, the victim will believe that the specific hallucination projected by the Beast is the only thing he knows for sure is real.

                                                    Exceptional Success: The victim gains the Insane Tilt. ​

                                                    You’ve Been Compromised

                                                    Well, that’s the last time you ever volunteer for a stage hypnotist’s act. You can still smell the blood.

                                                    Dice Pool: None, see below.

                                                    Action: Reflexive

                                                    Normal: The Beast takes a –2 penalty on her roll to invoke another Nightmare. In return, she can delay the activation of the secondary Nightmare until the occurrence of a specific trigger she designates. The trigger's effectiveness relies on the victim's perception. For example, if the Beast sets the trigger to "when I snap my fingers," it will only work if she does so within the victim's sight or hearing. However, the victim does not need to be informed of the trigger for it to function.

                                                    High Satiety: As normal, but the Beast can also specify a terminal condition that will prematurely end the effects of the secondary Nightmare (e.g., "when you apologize"). Again, this condition’s effectiveness depends on the victim's perception.

                                                    Satiety Expenditure: As normal, but the trigger lingers in the victim's mind even after the secondary Nightmare ends. Each time the victim encounters the trigger after the first (up to once per scene), they must roll again to contest the activation of the secondary Nightmare. If they fail, they suffer its effects once more. This cycle continues indefinitely until the victim succeeds on their resistance roll.
                                                    When combined with the High Satiety effect, the Beast can specify if the terminal condition ends only a current instance of the secondary Nightmare, or if it will also prevent any future recurrences. Alternatively, the Beast can set separate terminal conditions for both, if desired.
                                                    If the secondary Nightmare includes a Satiety expenditure effect, the Beast must reflexively provide the required Satiety cost whenever it activates. Otherwise that activation will not include the Satiety expenditure effect.

                                                    Exceptional Success: None; an exceptional success triggers the exceptional success result of the secondary Nightmare.​

                                                    And for good measure a Nightmare-specific merit:

                                                    Recurring Nightmare (• to •••)

                                                    Effect: Select a Nightmare when you acquire this Merit. When rolling or determining the effects of that Nightmare, your effective Satiety is equal to the higher of your current Satiety, or 4 plus your rating in this merit. At three dots, this means you always have the option to use the High Satiety effect of that Nightmare.
                                                    Additionally, at two dots or higher, you can invoke the Satiety expenditure effect of that Nightmare by spending a point of Willpower instead of Satiety, o                                                    nce per Chapter.
                                                    Special: You can acquire this merit multiple times, each time applying its benefits to a different Nightmare.​
