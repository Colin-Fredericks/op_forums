    Humanity Homebrews

    08-28-2016, 08:43 AM
    After growing up with V:tM and never getting a chance to ST a game, I've just started my first Chronicle using V:tR 2e. I love almost all the change in Requiem and 2e, from the Clans & Covenants to Masks & Dirges. But I don't think Humanity is going to work for this coterie and I'd like to explore other options.

    Here's my issue: while I like how Humanity has moved from being a morality scale to a 'how in touch with your human life are you' scale, I don't think it is tied into other mechanics enough. Humanity should be central to every session, yet I'm struggling to make it relevant. It seems like it is up to the ST to remind/force players to take Humanity rolls and I don't think my players (who are all new to RPGs) would enjoy that. They love Aspirations, but Touchstones are a little too inflexible for their backstories.

    So: what alternatives to Humanity & Touchstones are out there? I've heard of Atrocity dice, but my copy of Danse Macabre is still in the post. Do other STs have house rules to make Humanity more central to their games?

    I have also been working on my own Humanity Homebrew rules and I'd love some feedback on them. You can find them here: http://bit.ly/vtrnewhumanity

    My main ideas going into this were:
    • Make holding onto your Humanity about playing to your vampire's Mask
    • Make gaining Experience and Willpower about playing to your vampire's Dirge
    • Strongly link Aspirations to your Dirge and Mask
    • Simplify Breaking Points so that they are about *monstrosity* and not morality. Using disciplines and rolling for Frenzy are breaking points but murder isn't (usually). Killing can be a human act. Moving shadows isn't.
    • Humanity rolls are taken at the end of each night. As you sleep, you dwell on what you have done and wake up more (or sometimes less) a monster than the night before.
    • Humanity rolls are Humanity dots - BP. This make it more difficult to maintain humanity the more powerful you become, but also more likely to roll successes for a starting player.
    Issues I'm having:
    - Should characters be able to redeem themselves to undo breaking points? E.g. if a character broke & lost Humanity by Dominating a mortal, could they regain that humanity by freeing another mortal from their domination? Maybe this is too complex.
    - Are the new Breaking Points to simplified? I have removed lots of stuff about sunlight because I feel that's covered under Frenzy. And killing in the name of your Dirge should *probably* be a Breaking Point but I'm not sure where to put it...
    - The penalties to having a low Humanity seem weak. I want to make it clear to players that a lower Humanity means it's more likely you'll lose your Humanity next time, but the mechanics don't fit that. Losing Humanity should be a 'oh crap' moment for players.

    All ideas & suggestions welcome!

          Regarding your suggested house rules: One issue I'm seeing is that with the dice pool being Humanity - Blood Potency, it's going to become very easy to spiral into draugurdum, especially with Humanity rolls being made every day when they sleep. The more Humanity you lose, the easier it is to lose more - which is fine, that's how it works now. But with Blood Potency as a penalty, your dice pool becomes a chance die much earlier. BP 6+ Elders wouldn't last more than a few days before going dragur, maybe weeks if they're lucky, but either way this would lead to a society of pretty much all neonates and a few ancillae, with elders all being mindless monsters. Every vampire basically has a 350 year clock, which gets shorter every time they lose humanity or gain Blood Potency by means other than age. Once it runs out, they're in the draugur death spiral.

          Regarding the issues you're having:
          - Players can buy Humanity dots for experiences by default, and as with everything in Chronicles, Humanity purchases should be justified in the narrative. But if you wanted, you could always design Humanity-specific Beats that could be earned for adhering to your Mask or something along those lines.
          - Keep in mind, the list of Breaking Points in the book is only supposed to be examples. You're meant to add to, subtract from, or modify them as you see fit. Personally, I would keep the various killing Breaking Points and make doing it in defense of your Dirge incur an additional penalty to the roll (probably -1 die).
          - Humanity ties into a *lot* of stuff as written. Banes, including the Clan Banes, all get worse as you lose Humanity. Sunlight damage gets worse as you lose Humanity. Staying awake during the day becomes harder. The chances of creating a Revenant get worse. Torpor Duration increases. Penalties for interacting with mortals increase. Breaking Point rolls get harder, making you more likely to get a Persistent Condition from them. Losing Humanity is pretty rough as-is, maybe if I had a better idea what more you wanted it to do?

                      Ah, I gotcha. The way I read it, Humanity represents how human a character's worldview is. Breaking Points happen when the character is forced to face the fact that they are definitely not human. Humanity loss happens when they rationalize the act that forced them to face their own monstrosity, incorporating it into their worldview as something that's normal for them. So, going with your killing example, it's normal for humans to feel guilt or remorse when they kill other humans. Or, if nothing else, they might at least feel justified in their actions, but they at least feel something, barring people with severe empathy disorders. When a vampire kills a human, though, they don't feel anything. That cognitive dissonance of knowing "I just killed someone. I should feel bad about this. Why don't I feel anything?" forced them to face the fact that they've become a monster, so they roll a Breaking Point. If they succeed, they understand they've done something that should make them feel guilty, and they can at least feel guilty about not feeling guilty, even if the act itself didn't elicit any emotions. If they fail, they just accept that not feeling anything about it is ok - after all, they never felt guilty about eating hamburgers as a human, why should this be any different? Their worldview twists to preserve their comfort, and in doing so they become a little bit more distanced from a human way of thinking, a little bit more monstrous.

                      This is also why acts stop being Breaking Points as you lose humanity - in your mind, those things are perfectly normal, so they don't challenge your worldview anymore.

                      I would recommend going through the list of example Breaking Points under this lense - Humanity as "how human your worldview is", and Breaking Points as "things that shake your worldview", success indicating that you maintain a human perspective on yourself and failure indicating that your way of thinking changes in response to whatever challenged it and becomes a little bit less human. Decide which ones you want to keep and which ones you want to throw out. Think about what other acts you might want to add to the list, and formulate a master list you can use in your game. I would also recommend getting your players involved in this process, discussing what acts they think should or shouldn't count as Breaking Points and why.

                                      I would recommend going through the list of example Breaking Points under this lense - Humanity as "how human your worldview is", and Breaking Points as "things that shake your worldview", success indicating that you maintain a human perspective on yourself and failure indicating that your way of thinking changes in response to whatever challenged it and becomes a little bit less human. Decide which ones you want to keep and which ones you want to throw out. Think about what other acts you might want to add to the list, and formulate a master list you can use in your game. I would also recommend getting your players involved in this process, discussing what acts they think should or shouldn't count as Breaking Points and why.

Your vampiric weaknesses get harsher, you find it harder to empathize with humans, and if you truly, deeply internalize the idea that it's normal for you to do these things because you're a vampire and that's what vampires do, you might even develop an inability to enter houses without an invitation.
