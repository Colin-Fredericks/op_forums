            Originally posted by tasti man LH View Post
            One I'd like to see is 1950's-60's Japan for Beast.

            This was the time of the Kaiju Golden Age for Japan. Which of course the first kaiju movie Godzilla (1954) was a metaphor for unchecked nuclear devastation. And in the early years, was back when kaiju movies were made with the intent of being scary. Which in of itself could be used to look at Japan as they were dealing with all the fallout from the end of WWII (the American occupation, shift in government, and an even further Westernization of their culture). And the 60's also oversaw the creation of tokusatsu heroes like Ultraman, with an actual Hero rising to fight giant monsters (which could lead into the 70's and how that was the Golden Age of heroes for Japan with guys like Kamen Rider and Super Sentai).
            Oddly, I actually had a pitch for the same period for Changeling. Literally using the Gentry as a kaiju story analogue. With the devastation of the war, so many people went missing. The Gentry would have run rampant. So, this would be a period where a lot of those captives were returning.

            This is partly because the period gets a solid mention in Changeling 2e.

            Then again, I'm a huge advocate of dual-game settings.

                                                            How about Samarkand during the reign of Timur? I was thinking Promethean, since Timur gathered the best artisans and craftsman from across the empire to rebuild the city and create great works of art. It seems like an environment where new Prometheans would be born. Also, with Mongol, Turkish, and Persian cultures all mixing to make something new, it seems like a neat metaphor for a Promethean's quest to build their own identities. Timur spent the entirety of his reign building and rebuilding the city. The constant change could have an effect on the balance of Azoth and Flux, such that Disquiet and Wastelands might be milder for the moment, making it easier for Prometheans to stay here and interact with people. Of course, things unbalanced eventually must shift the other way. Lurking in the future is the death of Timur and the violent struggle for succession which will follow. For the moment, though, Samarkand is a peaceful respite on their Pilgrimage.
