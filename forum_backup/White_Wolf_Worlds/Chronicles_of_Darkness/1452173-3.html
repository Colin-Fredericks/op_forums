            I've been struggling with something I feel that it is appropriation, but I'm still learning to put into words.

            Since always, the way people outside South America think about Brazil just as Rio de Janeiro and jungle nags at me. I imagine things aren't better in a lot of other South American countries that don't get even that as recognition for their existence, but I can't talk for them.

            But the way White Wolf treats the Amazon forest worsened this in several ways. The Amazon isn't just present in the game, it is a big part of it. It is mentioned and talked about, and there is a war happening there.

            An European war. With eventual help of jungle dwellers.

            No mention of the people and cultures that live here. Barely a mention to cities, and then it is Rio de Janeiro, at most São Paulo. Sometimes mention to old Pre-Colombian ruins instead of their living descendants or the many people that have nothing to do with them.

            This feels wrong in many levels. Half a billion people live in the continent, Brazil alone is bigger than the contiguous US, we have 17 cities with more than a million inhabitants against 14 in the US, and just as many different landscapes if not more. And yet, here we are. The mighty Fera of the North came to save our forest and we're a footnote in the story...

                        At least it gets treated in a more complex way than here in Europe... I can tell you that there is outright disdain in France to everything down the Rio Grande (and when I mean disdain, I don't mean just stereotype that there is only narcos there, but really more the idea that "those peoples" have absolutely nothing of interest to teach us somehow, which i find disgusting), and it often shows in our creations... Even if younger creators/studios are starting to get away from it, thankfully.

                        Regarding cultural appropriation, I think the main difference between it and culture heritage between cultures is often that it's a majority group taking something which used to be (and is often still) depicted as something dirty or "primitive" etc. (like with dreadlocks for instance or African braids worn by Europeans or European-descended peoples). Note that the reverse isn't true (except in the mind of jypocrites/delusional peoples I guess) : Afro-descendants or Africans straightening their hairs is not them stealing or appropriating from the Europeans, but clearly an attempt to conform to the beauty standards of said Europeans. So the underlying reason is fundamentaly different and that's what is separating cultural appropriation from normal mixes and matches of cultures and cultural elements.

                        And sometimes, even supposedly owned cultural elements can be said to be badly displayed (and in some case, I consider that said ownership is somewhat in bad faith). For instance, I really don't like the usual depiction of the Greek Gods (or any Pantheon from ancient Europe from before Abrahamic faith washed over it) in pop-culture because I'm a Neo-Pagan. But I understand that I can't do anything about it because the wide family of Neo-Paganism is a very small one and it's also corrupted by Far-Right extremists anyway who give it even worse image than anything the pop-culture can do; and also because, so far, I don't risk any discrimination or anything liek that, even when coming out of the broom closet.

                                The WoD's treatment of South America is also so heavily skewed by WtA, it's hard to see some of the better stuff from Mage and Wraith that acknowledge there's a lot more to the continent than Rio and the rain forest.

                                We're also in the CofD forum, which generally does... less worse.... than the WoD when it comes to South America. CofD 2e's switch to shorter write-ups of lots of cities to show whatever character type (since this is done for broad like Vampires, and more specific like Covenants) varying by local cultural impact means they can't just do Rio over and over. Though South America and Africa are still frustratingly unrepresented in comparison. The lack of a hidden global supernatural war over the environment also means there's nothing like the Amazon War to steal the spotlight from more locally focused stories.

                                While I don't think anyone living in South America needs to specifically step up, I do think there's a struggle to find people that know the local areas well and want to be RPG writers. Brazil is the only South American country I'm aware of with a very sizeable WoD/CofD fan base that leads to some people thinking about submitting to write on the books. Which ends up with a Catch-22 between trying to represent a more diverse setting, and not having a few people in the US or Europe writing about Uruguay based on what they find on Google.

                                                            To me the main point is to let the natives speak for themselves as much as possible.

                                                            Why K-pop and manga aren't considered a case of cultural appropriation? Because they didn't got popular through white interpretations with original artists remaining "obscure". Instead most famous K-pop bands (you like them or not) the world around are, well, Korean. And most famous manga and anime are Japanese. There is a lot of other problems, like "orientalization", and certainly there are instances where we have a strong chain of cultural appropriation (anyone here ever heard about or played a wuxia, samurai, or "oriental" setting written by Asians?), but those two specific cases are ones where we actually traded with the natives themselves.

                                                            Cultural appropriation is all around us in elements from other cultures that we actually only know through the view of our "equals", and in that we actually just re-skin our own cultures and world views. When we learn then primarily through natives, then we have a space for true cultural exchange.
