    What would the other splats Archmaster-equivalents look like in your mind?

    12-22-2025, 10:55 PM
    Just curious on people's thoughts on what the other splats, if they had ever gotten an Imperial Mysteries type book, would look like with their equivalents to archmages.

    For mummies my thoughts would be ascending to Anpu's side and working to become a Judge or agent of Sutekh and having mummy minions of their own. For werewolf more bodhisattva types who embody the hunt fully and might hunt or be the host for a firstborn. Sin-eaters would imo be trying to fight in the underworld. I'm not sure for hunter or the other splats. Thoughts?

      Methuselahs and the True Fae are almost explicitly Vampire and Changeling's equivalent of archmages in terms of actors at the global scale, with Incarnate Beasts having the potential to edge into that space depending on that particulars of their Inheritance and demons likewise having the theoretical capacity to reach some sort of transcendent state depending on their vision of Hell and how effectively they pursue it.

      Prometheans don't really work in such a way that this question makes sense for them, though rumors about the Progenitors of Lineages fill some of the appropriate narrative space. Sin-Eaters likewise are ultimately here for a good time rather than a long time, thus the closest thing to "becoming an archmaster" is becoming a Chthonic God via Cabeiros — and considering that puts you in the shaky position of being something people aren't sure even exist in the strict sense, there's minimal difference between that and leaving a legacy of a successful Catabasis that leads to the Underworld working differently.

      Deviants are such a broad category of being and the metaphysics of their powers beyond the core principle of "your soul is broken" are so varied that there isn't really space for anything beyond the level of doing really well at being a deviant (maxing out Acclimation, easing your Scars in terms of both Magnitude and Scar Resistance, etc), and that same definition means most definitions of transcendence literally involve you not being a deviant anymore when the type of people most inclined to pursue that goal are creating deviants as ultimately failed test subjects for whatever procedure they're waiting to perfect before they use it on themselves.

      Hunters don't really have anything that works as an archmaster equivalent for obvious reasons, and while the nature of most conspiracies means the closest equivalent is whatever supernatural elements might be pulling the strings or compromising the principles of the Vigil for their agents, the nature of the gameline means similar problems to Deviant crop up.

      Werewolves don't strictly fit the "global-scale magical-political actor" model of archmaster-likes, though the occasional "bodhisattva predator" sorts alluded to within the line fit the headspace of "legendary being" Promethean accords to the Progenitors.

      The Arisen either don't have archmaster equivalents or have the various subtypes of Apotheosis described in 2e, depending on your definition, because that's what happens when the gameline's genesis points include a sticky note that says "Fuck it, you start out as the Prince."

      Is there a more particular metric we're using to define "archmaster equivalent"? Because the broad strokes of the archetypes that come to mind are fairly splat-agnostic.

                                      Originally posted by CalvinSiler View Post
                                      Hmm. Interesting. So how have you used heroes in your Beast games?
                                      The standard ways for Heroes to be used in Beast are either as a potential risk explaining why what passes for Begotten society doesn't linger at the Starving level of Satiety or as an actual threat in terms of shifting the predominant narrative surrounding them to "the Beast needs to be killed (by a Hero)." The former is accomplished by the fact that being at Low Satiety attracts Heroic attention and getting back above that level makes a Beast vulnerable to Anathema that then need maxed-out or zeroed-out Satiety to clear; the latter adds Heroes' ability to recruit followers to further arm themselves and steer the confrontation within the mortal sphere as a prelude to or substitute for direct confrontation in or around the Lair.

                                      They can be smart or foolish in how they go about it, but the type of Hero that serves as an antagonist in a Beast chronicle is one that is driven to kill the Begotten and derive Primordial power from their murder. Generally speaking, the "transcendence" options for Heroes are getting out of the game or the alluded-to storied existence that extends their natural lifespan in service to that drive after passing a certain threshold of success. Heroes reaching the famed status of Heracles and the like is, at best, in the same section of "legendary actors" as I mentioned for werewolves, Prometheans, and deviants — their Gifts function solely in relation to making things difficult for Beasts and their kin, and while their influence in a story may get to be very extensive indeed it's not substantially different from what a magically persuasive monster or even a normal human with a lot of luck can accomplish.

                                          I've been (slooooowly) working on an idea for werewolves. Something that turns them into the equivalents of Rank 6+ spirits, embodying some primordial aspect of predation. Gods of the Hunt. A lot of the ideas are still loose, but I realize, there are a lot of things that need hunting even at the god-tiers: Fae, actual gods and spirits, Abyssal entities, and weirder one-off things like the ones at the end of the Predators book but souped up. If their territory becomes the Tapestry entire, or large swathes of it, then we figure they're probably out hunting some pretty crazy things.

                                          If a Rahu embodies the aspect of the killing wolf beneath the Full Moon's glare, then the "archwerewolf" equivalent stalks the hunting grounds where moons devour celestial impacts, where stars eat each other, the hazy lands beyond perception where ravenous things redefine "ancient" and warp understanding itself in their hunger. Here the Wolf hunts not for flesh and blood but to tear a new meaning, crimson and hot, into the worlds below.

                                          All very early ideas, though.

                                              For werewolves, I'd look to the likes of Father Wolf and the First Pack, but in the context of (if I recall correctly) the To the Strongest Dark Era: the one that existed before the modern Gauntlet came into existence and when there was a liminal zone between the physical and spiritual worlds inhabited by beings that were a blend of both. I forget what they were called; "Pangaeans of the Border Marches", I think?

                                              The idea would be that you would need to somehow regain that "middle ground" state as a prerequisite to becoming a Predator of Legends. Your part-spirit nature should then give you a Spirit Rank that's not just a matter of which spirits defer to you, but an actual measure of how powerful your spirit-like qualities are. This Rank can then be increased in some manner until it exceeds 5, at which point you're in the realm of being a godlike Pangaean who potentially operates on the same stage as the First Pack.

                                              That said, I'm not as deeply versed in WtF lore as I could be; so its possible that what I just said is nonsense. In which case, please disregard.
