                                Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post

                                This I specifically would not want to ever be the case in anything canonical. Solomon was a Mage, to be sure. But for it Prophets called him blasphemer, whose corrupt dealings with powers man was not meant to command (including foreign gods) led to the fall of the kingdom in the eyes of those who would compile the Tanakh in the Captivity period.
                                It's all perspective, man. You look at it with your handy game books and say "Why would one man be labeled blasphemer for being a Mage if the Prophets were Mages too?"

                                I say "They labeled him a blasphemer not for practicing the Art, but for practicing it outside their approved traditions. There was a line in the sand that divided the Art into 'religion' and 'witchcraft' and Solomon crossed it."

                                See, the covenant with the Old God whom we'll call YHVH established certain parameters.YHVH, when He ruled in the Supernal, had dominion over the region now occupied by Aether. This gave those touched by His hand a predilection towards Awakening to the Obrimos path and an absolute bar on Moros, hence all the trappings of Death (inferior Arcanum) being ritually unclean in Hebrew culture. Contact with corpses and menstrual blood (the result of a dead ovum) were anathema.

                                So what did Solomon do? He trafficked with Necromancers for one thing. That pissed off the Priesthood and the Prophets to no end. He also swapped secrets with foreign Mages, which was taboo. The nation of Israel was founded in service to YHVH and He gave them all the magical lore they needed to do their job, including some stuff that nobody else knew. By sharing these mysteries with foreigners and bringing in the rites and practices of other traditions, he corrupted the very foundations of the Israelite Art.

                                Then there's the fact that the Priesthood and the Prophets held to a tradition in which the practice of the Art, if not its existence, was kept behind a series of ever-more closed doors. The Tabernacle's courtyard was restricted to pureblooded Jews. Only Levite priests were allowed inside the Tabernacle proper. The highest practice (recitation of ha-Shem, the true Name of God) was performed only once a year, in the Holy of Holies, by the High Priest alone.

                                Then Solomon hired a bunch of goyim to build the Temple and had the brass, as a non-Levite, to meddle in Priestly affairs, never mind bringing in altars to foreign gods.
