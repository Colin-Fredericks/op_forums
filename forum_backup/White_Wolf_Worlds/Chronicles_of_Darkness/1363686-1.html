    So, if we are going to do a 5-4-3 according to DE-DE2-DEC, I would also update my list-

    Dark Eras
    1) Sundered World (for reasons detailed above)

    2) After the Fall (" " " ")

    3) Fallen Blossoms (" " " ")

    4) Doubting Souls (" " " ")

    5) To the Strongest- seriously, for everything Arc has said. The amount of setting development it brought to Mage was awesome, and the whole concept of how cultures clashing together influencing the magical world was amazing to read.

    ​Honorary Mentions: Requiem for the Regina (because seriously, the whole Era is just a masterpiece, but the competition is soooo damn difficult)

    Dark Eras 2

    1) Hunger in the Black Land (for reasons detailed above)

    2) The Seven Wonders- it was really fascinating to read how Glamour and Pyros meld and mix together, as well as the concept of playable fetches and the whole weirdness which revolved around the Wonders. I must admit that when I heard about it, I was sure it is going to be the weakest to the Eras, but I was proven wrong, and I remove my heat before the writers. It was really fascinating to read, and the thought of perhaps the Wonders recreating themselves in the modern time makes for a great Chronicles concept.

    3) The Devil and the Deep Blue Sea- again, I just reaaaaally like weird Eras, and this one is full of strange mysteries and weird alliances. Bird also seems to have a cameo in here, which is amazing all by its own. Also, pirates! I doesn't need much to get me hooked on that :P

    4) Reign of Terror- I don't think that any Era has caught me THAT off guard as this one. The Tenth Choir is nothing like what I expected it to be, and feels like a way for those who wanted to play the old version of the Brood to have it while introducing its new revamp. The new agendas are also so amazing, that some of them just scream "they should have been here from the starts!", that this is all awesome by its own right. And the Bloodless, of course, are awesome. Everything is awesome.

    Honorary Mentions: 1001 Nightmares (a very cool Era, and some amazing revamp to the materials originally presented in Ancient Mysteries while in a much cleaner and elegant way. The Refrain and the interaction with Beasts is also great, and the new blood alchemists are so appropriate and very interesting. I am more ambivalent towards the new Clan, as it feels to me more like a bloodline concept, and it feels weird that it has access to two unique Disciplines. Everything else is really good, however)

    Dark Eras Companion

    1) Fall of Isireon- I loved practically everything about this Era. The immortals, the sekhem sorcerers, the prophecies which run wild through the Era only to eventually fail. The obsession towards the occult and magic of this Era, and the fact that mummies stumble confused into it, trying to understand what the hell is going on. It was really amazing to read.

    2) Princes of the Conquered Lands- everyone always wanted to know what will happen if an Arisen and an Awakened will just sit together and compare notes, and now we have it! The crossover level in this chapter is just excellent, and viewing how mage terminology and society are presented in an African culture which is not Egypt was also really cool to see.

    3) The Soulless and the Dead- welcome to the apocalypse. The very nihilistic feeling of this Era, the weight of death that it brings and how everything is doomed by plague is so horrifying for both mortals and monsters alike. It also gives us the Amirani, which is so Era appropriate and an awesome concept for a pre-Frankenstein Lineage that I am actually sad that they were replaced by their frenzied cousins.

    Honorary Mentions: Lifting the Veil (I LOVE the whole mortal occultism and spiritualism which is spread across that Era. Mortals who are not only dealing with things that they don't understand but actually searching for them knowing it will blow up in their faces, and the whole tension between true occultists and charlatans, magic and science gives an awesome feeling for this Era. In a way, it is the very antithesis for Hunter, and I love every little bit about it)

    And just for lolz...

    Unchosen Ones (or those two Eras you would have really wanted to get, yet have never seen the light of day)

    1) Book of Judges- like, wow, I can't spell it out how MUCH I WANTED THAT ERA. The biblical awesomeness that it carries with it, the wild and violent divine nature of the Judges and the constant wars has so much potential for Hunter (and Mummy I must admit, especially in 2e), that the only reason that I do not want it to get published is that I am terrified that it won't live for my expectations. Maybe I should just write it for the STV and be done with it, but writing an whole Dark Era is a tremendous task, and I have other plans to go around that task.. ahem ahem, never mind that.

    2) Epic of Gilgamesh- ok, let's admit it: the fact that in the Dark Eras series we are YET to get something about Mesopotamia and Sumer is so weird. It should have been, like, one of the firsts to get suggested. With Werewolf and its concept for the Dog Gods of Bau (not to mention the whole Sumerian- First Tongue connection), the Scelesti of Kish, that Vampire SAS about the sleeping vampire of Sin and the Utukku, Mummy with the Ki En Gir connection and Hunter's Nibiru and their version for the Epic, not to mention the Tammuz and the Daeva, Ereshkigal and the Laws of the Underworld and countless of other plot hooks for all gamelines, this Era should have been one of the greatest choices for the series, IMO.

            Let’s kick off the lists with Dark Eras: Original Flavor.

            #5 goes to “Into the Cold.” You’re going to see nearly every single Demon era on this list, and that’s because they slap. All of them. Even this one, which arguably is just “Demon but REALLY Demon, no, even more Demon than that,” but the Infrastructure? The timeline of Berlin 1960-1980? Gorgeous. I don’t know if I could ever play it, but it’s a delight to read.

            #4 is “Requiem for Regina.” British Vampires and Changelings are two great tastes that taste great together, as Arthur’s era later built upon. This is some of the best Changeling material for my esoteric tastes in the whole book, and the whole makes me want to sink into the ambience of Shakespeare’s London. Delightful.

            #3 is “Beneath the Skin,” because I could read about the Mexica for hours, especially when their empire is riddled with some of the most delightfully weird Infastructure in the whole line. The scorpions! The scorpions!

            #2 is “The Sundered World.” Put the baseball bats down and hear me out! It’s beautiful, absolutely deserving of its own full book, and a paradigm shift that elevated the entire book to something exceptional, letting the reader know right off the bat that this book is going to be special. If I was going off quality alone, it'd surge ahead to number one, no question.

            But #1 is the one I’m going to remember for blowing my expectations clear away, leaving me awestruck at how deeply I fell in love, and that’s “After the Fall.” Everything works. There isn’t a missed note in the whole thing, and it made me want to go out and read more about the period immediately. It’s going to stay my personal favorite, as much as I love the Sundered World (and rightly so).

            Honorable mentions go to “The Wolf and the Raven,” hampered from eclipsing Berlin by its lackluster Geist writeup, and “A Handful of Dust,” which did a fine job of drawing me in and left me hankering for Promethean.

            ***

            That done, let’s turn to the Companion.

            #3 is “The Year Without a Summer,” and the only reason that it’s here is because I studied British Romanticism in college. It’s a love-letter, and I’m the exact audience to receive it. It’s not strictly better than, say, Princes (see the honorable mentions below), but it’s dearer to me.

            #2 is “Forsaken by Rome,” a companion piece to a book I hadn’t yet read when I first read it. It’s so stirring, and makes one want to go forth and fight in the shadow of strange and dark woods.

            #1 is “The Master’s Tools” because I am weak and Demons are fantastic in settings of revolution and upheaval (see below) and the Battleship Potemkin hook is phenomenal.

            Honorable mentions go to the technically excellent and honestly should be in third place but I love the Shelleys too much “Princes of the Conquered Land,” and the really trippy if you’re not up to date with Mummy “The Fall of Isireion.”

            ***

            All right, down to Dark Eras 2, which I burned the midnight oil to finish so that I could pin this list down.

            #4 is “Hunger in the Black Land.” The reoccurring motif of Death Wolf and her Shadow throughout the book works well, and this era’s a gorgeous opener to the book. Dark deeds indeed in the shadow of a dead and mummified god.

            #3 is Mysterious Frontiers, a real dark horse on this list. How I scoffed when I heard we were getting cowboy Changeling, certain that it would be unable to thaw my heart, wounded so by their snub for the 1001 Nights. How wrong I was! It’s sharp as a whip, conscious of the ways the Western papered over the truth of the west, and it made me put it down halfway through so I could immediately go read Ursula Vernon’s “The Tomato Thief” again. That's praise, mind you.

            ("The Tomato Thief" isn't necessarily a straight Changeling story, but it's fine writing and a deep well of inspiration. That said, if you want straight horror of the Hedge and Fetches, Vernon's "The Twisted Ones" is perfect-- but that's a tangent in and of its own.)

            #2 is "Light of the Sun," which... I'll just let Blind Guardian handle this one for me.

            Which leaves #1, which -- just like "After the Fall" -- blew me away when I expected little from it. It grabbed me by the throat and commanded my full attention from start to finish, showing me a world that demanded attention, full of opportunity and darkness and desperate hope. "The Reign of Terror" is phenomenal and well worth the long wait. My favorite part is the slow build of dread as the piece leisurely explains the actions of the various Mummies in Paris, each one returning, in turn, to the question of what exactly lurks in the catacombs below...

            Honorable mentions go to "Arthur's Britannia," which is mostly just "remember how good British Vampires and Changelings are together?" and "The Seven Wonders," which is tantalizing with its hints and pieces to put together. I want more information about Nineveh the True Fae, dammit.

                                        Alright kids, let's do this.

                                        I mentioned in another thread that Dark Eras 2,as a more condensed project than the first Dark Eras, perhaps better demonstrates the strength of Dark Eras on the whole than Dark Eras 1 and Companion did, because the planning was much more on point. While the First Dark Eras definitely hasstand outs and plunkers, those mask what was really great about the product, in that there's really a large number of highly informative and definitely playable eras. The reason I think Dark Eras 2 demonstrates that better is because I kept shuffling around what were my top four favorite eras as I was reading it, and even now, honestly a lot of the eras I have as middle of the pack could be swapped out for the ones at the top, and even the top two of my least favorite don't really feel like they should be labeled as such. But we're not about that, so let's talk the Top 4.

                                        Warning in advance, there's a certain madness to this list.

                                        4)Hunger in the Black Lands-Honestly you couldn't have asked for a stronger start to DE2 than Hunger. Sobekneferu's reign was definitely a tumultuous time with a lot of room for courtly politics, and the supernatural layer applied to this time creates a solid clockwork ecosystem with a lot of clear and actionable parts that leads right up to an awesome near-apocalypse set piece, that the game openly tells you could be an apocalypse-the rise of Sheut Wolf* in Crocodile is a hell of a turning point to consider, and neatly sets up the recurring trend of reminding players through DE2 that that's a viable way to play. The balancing act between the Uratha, Created, and Begotten is impressive considering that, while there's thematic balances between any two of them, the three together has a jankiness to it that is only exacerbated a seemingly unintuitive era to balance on. It's not as seamless as other eras, but it's definitely strong, and each splat gets to play with something fun-a minor reflection of the Bau state with the uratha, The Osirans occupying a space in the Egyptian religious system, and Beasts getting to explore a proto-Heroic system(albeit I'm not as crazy about Oracles as everyone else-maybe if this were a older era) and their place in the world without that direct pressure and antihesis. There's a whole lot to do, with a wide variety of gameplay being supported, and it's a solid look at an era of Egyptian history that might otherwise be easy to overlooked. Just, really solid era. And this is one of the fan favorites! Man, whatever took third from this has gotta be-

                                        It's Mysterious Frontiers.

                                        ....


                                        Yep.



                                        3)Mysterious Frontiers-I don't think it's possible to have a guilty pleasure as far as Dark Eras, but this one...This one I feel a little dirty with. I mean, don't get me wrong, it's a really good era, lot of solid coverage of the goings on of the times and the sort of stories at play, solid tie-in with Mages and Changelings, the myth of the gunslinger is both dressed down but also still viable for gameplay, the mythic Wild West in general really got dressed down while getting into the mess as both a learning and gaming experience-all make a decent fourth place contender, but third? above Hunger in the Black Lands? I can hear some people asking how.

                                        The answer is "It cheated."

                                        I have points and places in history I'm a fan of, but This is Not That Case. I was at no point obsessed with Tombstone, I never really invested in the Gold Rush tales, and even the Life and Times of Scrooge McDuck is more a result of a prior deep love of Disney than anything about the western spirit. My dad watched a lot of Westerns, but I didn't neccesarily watch them with him. What this era has, though, is my dearest enemy, nostalgia. I grew up in El Paso Texas, and the entireity of the American Southwest was my place of exploration and playground growing up. I never had to get into the mythos and reality of the Wild West, it was ubiquitious. This setting was the first 20 years of my life, and even though I am an urbanite who wants to go to other country, everything to love about a Wild West Era is baked into me. In the Pirates vs Ninjas argument, I voted cowboys. The lone gunslinger is bullshit, but it's also one of my favorite archetypes. My favorite jokes about the people of the land is Gene Wilder adding "You know. Morons," to the descriptions of settlers, but I also am just hardwired to dig into the fight to set up and keep a town going.

                                        And you set Changeling as one of the ones here? Jesus Christ, talk about doubling down on the Cheating. It also brings out a lot of the best of mage as a game of Investigating Mystery and Occult Exploitation, and I'm all for that subject.

                                        And on that subject, it cheated in one other Big Way. See, the harmony of crossover is going to be a Thing I talk about in regards to DE2 a lot, and the better you sell me on the synchronization of the splats, the more you'll sell me on that. The Devil and the Blue Sea, Really Good Era, was going to edge out Mysterious Frontiers for the majority of the reading because the Mages and Sin-Eaters were being better sold as a communal group than they were here. Changelings and Mages had good representation, and there's even really solid crossover mechanics, but they didn't really seem like they were playing together.

                                        And then there was the Court of the Leafless Tree.

                                        Not only did that solve that problem(not perfectly, but it definitely solved it), it also addressed one of my big ass complaints about crossover outside of the Contagion Chronicle, which is more organizations built on the syncritization of the splats. If you remember some of the hcatter I had on the Forest of Teeth, a freehold-protectorate made up of Court-Lodges, than you know this exactly my kind of jam, and DE 2 would have been a great place ot see that sort of thing happen and where you intermingle the various metaphysical aspects, or at least keep people directly in the same halls. You only get that once in DE2, and I love it.

                                        All of that comes together in a game I have to admit to myself I will readily play in more than Hunger in the Black Lands, so yeah-this probably doesn't deserve to be as high as it is from any objective standpoint, but it's my list, and it speaks to me personally. It cheated, and I love it for that.

                                        Okay, moving on, hopefully not being so long-winded.

                                        2) The Reign of Terror-Surprising no one. A lot of people like this one, and for good reason-The Terror is a tense and scary time that can be great for opportunistic players as well as gleefully sadistic GM's. It's one of the starkest and most highly influential moments in history, and it's lessons are myriad. In particular, in today's turbulent times, it's easy to feel the need for the guillotine, and a lot of people reflect on The Terror as...not the course of action, but an absolute sentiment, don't rule out it's return. There's a glee and terror that's put to great effect in lie of this sentiment, and the three splats play together beautifully with the themes and the catches to their action in the environment, the rebellious spirit being wildly empowering.

                                        But it also casts a light on how the Revolution failed, with the hydra having too many heads, and the question to solve getting too many answers. It's a sobering message as much as it is a cathartic experience, a reminder that any revolution that can't get it's act together is more or less doomed to fall right back into the place it started from, a whole lot of death and chaos for nothing really changed. I think there's a lot of fun to be had in that, and a whole lot more to learn. Those of us who are aimed towards progress would do well to be humbled by this era, and I think it does a good job of communicating that.

                                        On the other hand, the Tenth Choir did nothing wrong, eat the rich.

                                        1) The Light of the Sun-Okay, I lied, this is the era tailor made for me. Growing up, I always had a fascination with the "opposition" of faith, magic, and the sciences, and even as adult I find the push and pull of these concepts really cool. I'm a sucker for a near-apocalyptic feel. I'm a sucker for clockwork cosmology and grit and grime street action. I love crossover. I love really actionable games with a lot of room for differing playstyles that still also have clear places to go.

                                        That's Light of the Sun in spades. Those three forces are perfectly opposed to one another both in mood and atmosphere while having very real elements at play, all centered on characters who embody a neat harmonious and synchronized middle ground that reveal the arbitrariness of it all. Mages, Demons, and Deviants are given a seamless reason to be in a group to gether and who to go after, all neatly styled to a main form of gameplay while also leaving good room for deviation, as there are Physical, Social, and Mental challenges aplenty, and even neatly mapped out in the era. Furthering the Crossover appeal is that Promethean has an Era set during this time and they also pretty seamlessly fit in with the themes of this game, and Beast isn't a bad drop either, and hey, if you have that much, might as well use the Sworn from Contagion, and hey, so long as you're doing that, why not ride a Contagion in with the Black Plague as well-you have the power to diversify this setting a whole damn lot without losing sight of it's major themes or straying outside of it's feel. The black plague is used even btter in this one as a backdrop of descending apocalypse, that binds the heady, clockwork cosmological revelations and conflicts down through the halls of the Vatican and libraries of the Holy Roman Empire to the dirt and pus-filled streets and back again, with an easy to understand way how this particular set of characters can scale that ladder. This game is one of two that actually make me want to play a Seer, how the hell did that happen?! (The answer is "SEER POPE FIGHT!") And the entire time, I'm never at a loss for what to do, because the game has it's dangling elements in terms of what's happening with the Seers and the God Machine and the Devoted and Renegades under them that I can always pick a star and shoot for it. This era made me giddy and I still love it hell and back.

                                        So, yeah, those are my top four favorite eras. Special shout outs go to The Thousand and One Nightmares and The Great War (for being exactly as satisfying as I expected it to be), The Devil and the Deep Blue Sea (for making me go "Fuck, it's as good as I expected it to be"), and Arthur's Brittania and The Scandinavian Witch Trials (for making me go "Holy fucking hell, these are way better than I thought they'd be, they're really cool).
