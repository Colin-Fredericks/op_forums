Using that connection, I've made some research and decided to base its concept loosely around the Shona people and their culture. When writing the Mumvuri, I've thought about a sophisticated culture based around harsh divisions of totems and tribes, connection to the spirit world and some sort of &quot;high shamanism&quot;, including lineages of all kinds of shapeshifters which are contained to certain tribes and a &quot;Parliament of Beasts&quot; where the leaders of all local Breeds meet together in the shadows of the world. In that culture of spirit animals and werebeasts, the Mumvuri are an anomaly- they belong to a totem which should not exist, yet that totem is enough to give them proper presentation. It is a wild land, a sophisticated land, a place of dark mysteries and shiny treasures for those who dare to pass the guardian beasts and undead witches of Punt.

                So, one of the easiest "ins" for the Uratha in Kemet is the Upper Egyptian god known as Wepwawet, the wolf-headed god of war and death, sometimes considered a child of Anpu, other times considered their equal. Wepwawet had two cities which had massive cult centres dedicated to them, Zawty (later called Lycopolis and know Asyut) and Abdju. You might recognize the latter as Abydos, the Arisen necropolis they ruled openly as god-kings in the First Turn.

                Our current musings are leaning towards Bone Shadows being the most common Tribe in Kemet at the time, based in Zawty, with a Ghost Wolf Lodge in Abdju that may, or may not, be dedicated to Anpu himself. Regardless, the Abdju Uratha are likely as swept up in the affairs of the Arisen as anyone else, considering Wolf-Blooded aren't immune to the throes of Sybaris. Picture a Su-Menet Guildmaster with a pack of Uratha at their back. That's something horrific to behold, and certainly gives an excellent explanation as to how the Arisen were able to handle Apep with a Lodge of Ghost Wolves on their side of the great battle at the end of Unas' reign.

                Furthermore, the Changing Breeds aren't absent in Kemet, and in fact likely outnumber the Uratha. The Bastet form a privileged few in the arms of the cult at Per-Bast, the Baal-Hadad are doing well under the auspices of the Apis cult, and the Distant Ones reside in the city of Nekhen, quite possibly support the cult of Nekhbet in their sister-city across the Nile.

                                        I think he's suggesting that the Lodge of Garm will have details on the Lodge that worships said Egyptian god. Maybe. Not entirely sure.

                                        *****

                                        Alright, so, the Litany of the Fivefold Soul. Fascinating stuff, it's basically an abbreviated version of the Iremite "Book of Going Forth By Day" (IE. The Book of the Dead), but it seems that unlike the later Kemetic versions, the Iremites had no fallbacks for if you screwed up. You screwed up, and your soul fell to pieces and was eaten by Ammut, the Slaughterers, and other horrible things that live in Duat. In the Book of Going Forth By Day, there are spells written that act as failsafes in the case of the omission, failure, and/or inability to continue certain funerary conditions. No relatives able to feed the Ka with offerings? No problem, there's a spell for that. The heart is destroyed? No problem- scarab amulet for that. Spells to remember your Name, spells to preserve the Ba, spells to uphold the Shadow- failsafes like you wouldn't believe. The Kemetic people were very serious about the afterlife.

                                        The Iremites, even more so. No failsafes in case you forget things, but the would-be Deathless went down to Duat with Utterances inscribed on their Sekhem to aid their passage and ensure their survival. Not quite the same as a pile of failsafes, nor as reassuring, but the Iremites weren't exactly a touchy-feely caring culture. Most importantly, it seems as though there are some distinct differences between the later Kemetic interpretations of the Soul Anatomy and the Iremite interpretation (at least according to the Litany).

                                        Ab: In the Iremite tradition, this is probably what we would traditionally call "the soul". It is the repository of Memory, of emotion, of passion, of will, and the record of all the things that you have done with your life. To 'stray from Deathlessness' was to try and go against the flow of Nebthet and attempt to return to the land of the living, which would shatter the five-fold soul and send the Heart down to Duat alone, where none of the other Pillars could support or protect it, and it would be a sitting duck for Ammut's maw. The Heart is more than just your ability to feel, it is your ability to control those feelings, for passing the Second Gate of Duat requires you to resist the passions that assault your Heart- lost loves, old enemies, etc. It is worth noting that in the Kemetic tradition, the Heart is weighed against Ma'at, and if it is heavier (IE. full of sin) then it is given to the Devourer. Given Dreams of Avarice, to have a heavy Heart is to have one weighed down with suffering and punishment. You will have been broken and bound by the Law of Suffering, and thus have become a fitting sacrifice for Ammut.

                                        Ba: In the Kemetic tradition, the personality, the mental/spiritual self that leaves and returns to the body and takes the form of a human-headed bird. In the Iremite tradition, it is the Spirit, and appears to take on aspects of the Ka from the Kemetic tradition, being a sort of "spirit double" of the Ab. The Litany says that should the five-fold soul shatter, the Ba will return and possess your corpse, which will then be burnt as an abomination, which could mean that it's the source of ghosts in Iremite belief. (References to rising, being a Lifeless abomination, and burning, plus the bird aesthetic, makes it hard to not think Strix, admittedly.) Whatever the case, the Ba is driven, it is impulsive, and it is definitely impressive. The Ba refuses to quit when other things do, and it will endure stubbornly beyond death, even when everything in the living world is screaming at it that it shouldn't exist. It's my personal opinion that this is definitely the part of the five-fold soul that ghosts come from.

                                        Ka: The Essence, the vital spark that empowers the Ba in Kemetic belief, and apparently so in Iremite belief as well. It can be sustained with offerings, spells, and acts of remembrance. The Ka is empowering, the thing that gives energy to life, but is different than Sekhem, which is the luminous, radiant lifeforce upon which the entire soul is built. The parts of the soul might be of different qualities, but Sekhem is the thing they're all made of. It appears as though Essence is literally Essence in the CofD sense, as the Litany describes the shattered soul's Ka mingling in "oases and sacred places" and that "witches and unnatural things" will make use of it. Loci, anyone? What interests me about this passage, is that there is a distinct difference between sorcerers, which appear to be acceptable from an Iremite point of view, and witches, which are unacceptable, serve their enemies, and make unholy use of part of the soul. It also explicitly calls out other Essence-using creatures as 'unnatural', which is interesting seeing as Spirits are literally animistic reflections of the world, and Pangaeans have been called "pillars" of the world, with more on that to be elaborated in The Sundered World in the Dark Era Companion. I know that the Vinca Mages make use of the stone hearts of Pangaean gods as menhirs, but now I'm wondering if some made their way into pillars, idols, and altars in Irem.

                                        Ren: The Name, which in Kemetic belief existed as the repository of your identity. It is no different in the CofD, as true names are a thing that the Deathless, amongst others, make a big deal out of. The Heart might contain the literal memories, sensations, and human experiences of life, but it appears to be the Name that translates that into identity. The Name protected everything else, partially by ensuring that the deceased would be remembered and the appropriate rituals to protect the other Pillars would be performed, but also acting as a definition for the rest. The Name translates Ab, Ba, Ka, and Sheut from ineffable intangibles into a person- something knowable, something memorable, something comprehensible. The Name is the anchor to which everything else is affixed. The Litany describes a Decree as a declaration of identity, but the footnotes complicate it. Decree means more than that simple word, it also means "I become" or "I choose to be" or, perhaps most importantly, "I am". It is worth noting that the hymns of the Deceived Temakh are long litanies of descriptors, of facets of their identity, and that end, always, in "I am". I have come to the conclusion that those poems are, in some way, their true names rendered in such a way as mortals can attempt to understand. The fate of the Name of a shattered soul is to be forgotten, and being a forgotten ghost means losing Essence, and losing Anchors, until you are consigned to the Great Below.

                                        Sheut: The Shadow, a dark reflection of a person that contains something of who they are, it appears as though the Shadow is the repository of secrets, of truths, and of death itself. I honestly have very little to say about the Sheut as a literal piece of the soul anatomy, and more about it as a metaphor (which could be the same thing? Idk). Most Affinities and Utterances based around Sheut are based around, either the dead, Morality/Integrity, or secrets, revelations, and mysteries. The Sheut is more than a Shadow, it is the Darkness, and this revelation really made it click for me. The Shadow is the inherent nature of the CofD as expounded into a specific person. It is death, it is secrets, and it is mysteries. But more importantly than all that, the Sheut is the inherent monstrosity of a person, that great alluring darkness deep within, and it is mastery of the Sheut that allows you to overcome that and grow beyond it. Avoiding mastery/knowledge of the Shadow is to put a pretty mask over a monster, and pretending to be more than an animal while still struggling with your baser nature. Also worth noting is that the Litany describes the Sheut as "howling" in Twilight so that mortals will curse it. Another potential source of ghosts, or possibly something worse?

                                        The Sahu, being your spirit-flesh, appears to be mingled self-image and inherent truth/identity. The Akhu is someone who survives with their soul intact and gains power in the afterlife. By that definition, all Deathless are Akhu and need only rebuild their Pillars, but they are Akhu in service to the Judges. A Deathless in Apotheosis is a true Akhu, a free person with magical power, the Pillars of their soul united, able to ascend to A'aru. A Sin-Eater would be someone who specializes in aiding ghosts, either the Ba or Sheut of the dead, in putting themselves back together so that they may go to the afterlife whole and pass the Weighing of the Heart. Vampires are, honestly, a lot worse off than I earlier thought. They've been stripped of Ba and Ka, and must devour it from others. Their Sheut threatens to overtake them, and their Ren is slowly crumbling. As for the Ab? I honestly don't know. You could probably say that a Vampire is someone who has "eaten their heart" (IE. To escape justice), in which case they are Lifeless monstrosities desperately trying to shore up their Ren to make up for a lack of everything else and resist an unfettered and untempered Sheut, stealing Ka from the living. It could be that post-mortem Embrace leaves certain Mekhet Hollow because it rips their soul from the path to Duat, leaving their Ba behind, while a normal Embrace binds the Ba to the body. The Strix, though, are definitely unions of Ba and Sheut or, heck, even just Sheut. Or Ba. Depends on your perspective. I'm leaning towards Ba & Sheut or just Sheut myself.

                                                            For your reading pleasure: The Sekhmeti, a homebrew Bloodline

                                                            The ancient Egyptians tell a story of the mythical past, where men conspired against the will of the gods; Ra, god of the sun, sent forth one of his Eyes in the shape of Sekhmet, a furious lioness, in order to slay the wicked. Yet Sekhmet's bloodlust was not sated by the slaughter of the men she had been sent to kill, and set about the murder of the entire human race. The only way to end her rampage was a trick by the gods in which Sekhmet was given copious amounts of alcohol (dyed red, so as to pass as blood) that eventually incapacitated her; drunk and calmed, she transformed into the domestic cat Bast, and was appeased by yearly rituals so that she might never again threaten to consume humanity.

                                                            This story paints a far kinder picture of events than what truly transpired. Sekhmet did not peacefully fall, nor was she quickly tamed and forgiven; the gods lashed her with the fire of the sun and drove her into the unyielding desert, banishing the red-stained horror to suffer eternally in the lonesome night. Hiding beneath the sands during the day and sneaking forth under the stars to kill again, the lioness cultivated a brood of lesser servants to act as extensions of her will. As a fallen god, she imbued them with immense power; as a patron of warriors, she trained them in the ways of battle. Full after a grand slaughter that consumed an entire Egyptian city, Sekhmet retreated into the red lands of the desert and trusted her creations to handle themselves, never to be seen again.

                                                            The Sekhmeti name themselves after their supposed patron goddess, but the long ages since Egypt's pagan heyday have diluted their identity to simply being Lions. They are few in number, and most are immeasurably ancient, dating back to well before the rise of monotheism; they rarely Embrace from the modern humans who have forgotten the true faith from so long ago. The Sekhmeti preach a creed with few tenets - love for their distant feline mother, utter hatred for the sun and the masculine god who condemned them, and disdain for civilization stemming from being cast out of it so long ago - and largely steer clear of wider vampire society.

                                                            Some conspire to find and reawaken the fury of their lost progenitor, while others are content to merely survive the march of history and quietly plot the end of the sunlit world. A handful of the Sekhmeti rise to prominence in the Circle of the Crone, and others would likely enjoy the more revolutionary and anarchic factions of the Carthian Movement, but the vast majority of the bloodline are apolitical recluses.

                                                            Nickname: Lions

                                                            Parent Clan: Gangrel

                                                            Bloodline Disciplines: Animalism, Protean, Resilience, Vigor

                                                            Bloodline Bane: The Sekhmeti count their Humanity as two lower than its actual rating for the sake of determining damage from sunlight. In addition, the eyes of the Sekhmeti flash like those of one possessed by the Strix, causing many in the wider Kindred community to scorn them.
