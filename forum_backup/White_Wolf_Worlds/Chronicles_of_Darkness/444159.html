                  All of the above. Plus, Disquiet can be resisted, and not just through sheer willpower (the roll to resist Disquiet is based on Resolve + Composure; so someone with a lot in those two traits can go for quite a while before gaining any Disquiet — and as Christian A pointed out, one failed roll doesn’t put you in “torches and pitchforks” territory); various supernaturals who are aware of Disquiet have developed means of combatting it.

                  Not all Redeemed are immune to Disquiet; that’s generally the crowning achievement of the most successful New Dawns. But there are Redeemed who are immune. Alchemical Pacts can take the edge off Disquiet; or they can intensify it, depending on the composition of the Pact: to minimize the problems, diversify your Pact’s membership.

                  And while Disquietism doesn’t prevent Disquiet, it can redirect it so that the Promethean doesn’t have to bear the brunt of it.

                  My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                      Indeed; Prometheans walk a lonely road, and it isn’t easy. But that’s half the fun, right? Do you view Disquiet as an obstacle or as a challenge?

                                      Remember, Disquiet builds up slowly; and it’s possible to establish trust with the locals, deal with their problems, and leave town before Disquiet poisons that trust. As Krobeles pointed out, one failed roll isn’t going to result in the person coming at you with a pitchfork or running away screaming in terror: take a look at what the stages of Disquiet represent, and remember that it will take four failed rolls to reach the final stage.

                                                      I meant that the Disquiet roll only happens once per scene, and high willpower VS low azoth will stave off the Disquiet for a long time. Also, it's written somewhere in the books that children are not as influenced by the Disquiet as adults. I'd give the kids a neat +2 or +3 to resist the Azoth, if only to give the players the feeling that the kids will never betray them. Disquiet must never be a deterant to a game, it must always be a tool for the storyteller. As far as first edition goes, don't use Disquiet RAW, it will be a pain for you. Ask yourself basic questions.

                                                      "Is this character an important or reccuring character?"
                                                      "Is being affected by Disquiet a story-breaking moment right now?"
                                                      "Is being affected by Disquiet a crucial moment right now?"

                                                      These questions will help you deal with Disquiet better than the scene-rolls ever will.
