    How do you Portray Intelligent Characters ?

    08-31-2016, 12:05 AM
    I wanted to get some tips on portraying characters who are much smarter then the player or gm. I've had a few players who want to play a character who has a Genius IQ but don't know how to portray it because they themselves are not a genius. Any tips that might help ?

    Example:

    I have a player who wants to a 40 year old genius inventor. I know we could just say he does x and roll to see if he can do it but I wanted to see if their is away I can help the player portray the character better.

                  Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                  Remember golden rule of Storytelling engine games: 'I do not know, but my dots know it!'

                  Genius character can solve her problems where her Intelligance could help - she just declare and if roll is good, ST goes: 'Your brilliant mind have cracked this..."
                  That's kinda boring. I'd much rather allow high intelligence characters to take control of the storytelling for a few instants when she's describing her super-intelligence.

                  Think of something as follow. The PCs are planning a heist in a public place - they want to get access to a secret room which they think contains an important artifact.

                  INT PC: "We have 3 minutes to pull this off. You get in at 11:27, it takes you 23 seconds to cross the corridor up to the door, you'll have to practice this a lot because then you'll only get 4 seconds to install the machine on it. Start it at the exact moment Mike falls to the ground and screams in pain, distracting the guards and covering the noise. Then [...] and bam we're in!"

                  ST: "Well your plan sounds good, but did it assess the situation correctly? Roll Int+Subterfuge-3"

                  Int PC: "Success!"

                  ST: "The plan goes smoothly and the door opens. On the other side of the door you hear a growling - maybe this wasn't the best idea after all"


                  First, it has a feeling of a heist movie, so it's cool. Second, it gives the high-intelligence player a real control over the situation just by virtue of his super-intelligence. Third, the plot moves forward faster - this ain't D&D, we don't need to map and describe every tile the players walk on.

                  Is it a perfect solution? No. But it's a start at least

                      I've ran with something like this: If the character has a pool of Intelligence + Wits of 6 or higher, once per chapter they can roll that pool with a -5 penalty. Each success equals a +1 worth of equipment the character remembered to bring along. If needed, we'd run a short flashback to how the character got said equipment.

                                      I think the Anticipation and Declaration rules from Mirrors do a good job of representing high-intelligence characters if used well. Anticipation allows a character to have foreseen a potential problem and prepared for it, even if the player didn't. (Sherlock walks into an ambush, and bullets start flying. Sherlock's player rolls well on an Anticipation roll, and determines that Sherlock anticipated the ambush and is wearing a bulletproof vest, despite nothing being said about the character even having such a vest previously.) Declaration allows a character to observe or remember something relevant to the current situation that would give him an advantage. (In another scene, Sherlock is trying to get information out of one of the gunmen who tried to ambush him earlier. Sherlock's player makes a Declaration roll, and determines that Sherlock notices that the gunman's eyes are dilated, and he seems pale and shaky. The man is apparently an addict, which Sherlock can use as leverage in the interrogation.)

                                      The Building Equipment section of the CoD rulebook also mentions that characters can device a plan for something like a heist or raid, which provides an equipment bonus to related actions (at least until the plan falls apart). Since devising a plan would almost certainly involve mental attributes, intelligent characters should be taking advantage of this whenever possible.

                                          I'd suggest looking at how highly intelligent characters are portrayed on television and in movies as one option. They tend to do it one of two ways. Either the super-intelligent person is able to regurgitate random factoids regardless of the discussion, or they've anticipated whatever anyone is doing in advance, and have magically nullified it as a result. That's not exactly what genius intelligence is, but these can be consequences of a person being intelligent. The various permutations of Sherlock Holmes, including the recent modern television and movie adaptations, can model a sort of hyper-awareness of sensory input. This might be an idea for what you're looking for in someone with more than 5 dots. Of course, extreme intelligence tends to be portrayed as a game-changer, wherein the individual so blessed can do nothing stupid. This is false, of course. In reality, a smart person may have consumed large amounts of information and be capable of recalling it, but that doesn't equate to knowing everything, and a genius may be able to anticipate an opponent's actions, but such efforts are always subject to the data at hand.
