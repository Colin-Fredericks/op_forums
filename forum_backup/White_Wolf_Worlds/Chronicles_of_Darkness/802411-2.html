I mean, one is based around "I don't understand how the world works and am trying to comprehend the ineffable complexity that is reality and God.", another is the rebels who are on a crusade against God because of their anger or desire for freedom despite the fact that God is what keeps the world turning, another believes that God is truly god and regrets having fallen and is searching for a path of redemption in the eyes of their lord, and the last agenda is focused towards "I am a powerful force who can manipulate reality with ease, am I not a god myself?"

        The cults are light in detail, mainly because they would be ridiculously varied. The cults function like any other cult in real life, except that these ones actually have a divine ephemeral being inspiring it that they can see doing miracles. Ephemeral beings that can mystically bestow you with a mission, turn wood to gold, has eyes of lightning, can grow buildings overnight, can make you feel pure bliss, and can bestow relics to it's followers. In return, you honour it by following it's tenants and do rituals to it in manners like building things in it's name or shunning certain acts as evil.

            I think that people get too hung up on the whole 'cyberpunk' vibe of Descent (because that's the unexpected bit) and ignore the less computer-ey bits of it. Look at when a demon Goes Loud. They get access to all embeds they have affinity with, they get all exploits, they have a constantly refilling Aether pool. They are practically gods in their own right. Making the God-Machine and the demons themselves more 'gnostic' than 'tech' is just a matter of re-skinning.

            I personally love the idea of the God-Machine being effectively a self-replicating mystical pattern. It's not malevolent, or even sentient as mortals and demons understand it, but it acts and reacts. Like a construct from Conway's Game of Life, but on a cosmic scale. Demons are basically little parts of the pattern that have detached themselves from the whole. If you go by that rationale, anything can be a part of its workings. The rotation of the Earth, the solar wind, the orbits of asteroids, all the way down to the spin of electrons and the movements of quarks. The God-Machine is not in reality, it is reality.
