                            Originally posted by ReshyShira View Post
                            I don't see Chronicles of Darkness as a competitive experience where the players are expected to go head-to-head in a cage-match style where the 1-2 extra dice to your pools you get from optimizing really makes a difference. I'd much rather the developers spend time on crafting an interesting and compelling experience rather than spending a large chunk of their time trying to make sure the game meets some arbitrary level of what constitutes "balanced."
                            Ok, so, I ran a long, long running Changeling campaign. At around the time the group was at Wyrd 4, it was markedly obvious that one member of the party was the best in social situations, the best at investigations and occult research, and the best at combat. It's not that the game was competitive. It's that outside of minor edge cases (crafting, academic research, that kind of thing), this player was the only one who needed to act. In combat the others could help, but it was always this one character's show. In anything that wasn't combat, they often didn't even get a chance to help out before he would sort things out on his own. The player was wonderful, great at playing his character, but it really did feel like the hero wandering around with his less competent hangers-on. It wasn't all the fault of Dual Kiths, not by a long shot (1E Changeling had a looooot of dud Contracts, for example), but Dual Kith did play a role.

                            An arbitrary level of balance is important. The game doesn't need to be perfectly balanced, not by a long shot. But the developers should avoid anything that is just mathematically superior to any other choice they have written. I don't care if, say, you have a merit which makes you better at opening Doors and a merit which lets you do more damage, and the latter does more damage than the former opens Doors. But I do care if you write two powers which open Doors and one just does it better all the time. It means that there's a wrong choice at character creation, and any player who makes that choice is going to feel bad.

                                                            I'm actually not advocating removing Dual Kith. I'm just saying that I hope that, if it does make it in, that it's priced appropriately and isn't a clearly better choice than an equivalent merit. I hope that a merit that gives the option for such a startling array of choices is given due consideration before implementation.
