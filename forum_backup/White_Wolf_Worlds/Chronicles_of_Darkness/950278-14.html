    In an attempt to be serious:

    It seems to me that Genius currently looks at the mad scientist as it's own thing, rather than just the Victorian version of the "wizard in the tower", which is further a Dark Ages stereotype of the eremite (hermit)--the solitary person who cannot live in the current society for personal reasons, but tries to communicate with others through...well, art, inventions, 'magic' of a sort, etc. They lead lonely lives of quiet desperation, trying to get people to understand their vision. But they cannot communicate this vision in terms that the people around them understand, so they live in tension with society, neither being truly apart nor forging a connection.

    The question becomes, I think, how do we widen this core experience to get the full diversity of characters possible and get it to fit within the mechanics of the Chronicles of Darkness.

    Or...am I missing something else that can broaden and deepen this core image even further?

        I don't understand why you object to the continued assertion to "what geniuses do isn't real science". It's a personal horror game. The idea of being a scientist and suddenly being unable to do actual science is genuinely horrifying, and it's one of the most brilliant bits of personal horror I've seen in any ChroD line.
        I believe the main problem wasn't that "Mad Science isn't Science", but that the book doesn't say anything about what Mad Science is- it is not science, it is not magic, it is not some mad blessing of Chtulu or rainbow magic given by unicorn. It spends word count on saying what it's not instead on what it is. (I relay on Arc's review for this, considering I haven't read the book myself)

        And Lemuria? They're among my absolute favorite antagonists in any fiction, because they're so obviously wrong yet still so seductive- if you were a Genius, wouldn't you want to say "fuck reality, I'm right!"?
        again, haven't read the book, but it is a combination of having a full organization of mad individuals who simply can't work together which strains the suspension of disbelief together with the fact that it seems that Lemuria's oppression was, in general, a good thing and the the genii were the ones to ruin it (not to mention that the "humanity should be controlled" theme leaves a bad taste in the mouth)

        And the "science fetishism" thing...yeah. For one thing, that comic is smug and kind of douchey. Imagine if you shared a fun science fact with someone and they shot you down like that. Would you have any desire to interact with that person? "Hey, did you know that chameleons don't actually change color for camouflage?" "Ugh, you dumb science poser. I bet you don't even own a lab." You yourself point out how often it harps on how what Geniuses do isn't science- so of course it's going to be a fetishized, false form of science. Because that's what mad scientists do, especially in horror fiction! Horror fiction basically treats "mad science" as magic, and Genius takes that to a beautifully absurd conclusion by crafting a world built on science-as-magic.
        again, the problem seems to be that the game focus only on aesthetics without any content- it use "science fetishism" to cover a big hole which has nothing with it. Genii do nothing to create nothing and violate rules which aren't exist using a big nothing, with the only reason you could even say they are mad scientists is because they wear white robes and googles and scream "SCIENCE!" every now and then, making them into a caricature of themselves.

        Again, never read the book, only the review- and even that has probably caused me a Breaking Point or two.

                            This is not some shocking relivation that Arc has exposed after us fans overlooked it for years. It's stated outright in chapter one under The Broken Theory Law using the exact words "Geniuses are not scientists".

                            The mad scientist archetype is the victorian version of the wizard in his tower. Frankenstein was actually an alchemist not a scientist. The iconic wizard merlin was based on a Welsh bard. The more you examine the origins of the mad scientist archetype the clearer it is that mad scientists are a much older idea than even science itself.

                            That's why geniuses don't do science. Not because the game lacks content, but because it uses the actual content of the mad scientist archetype, which isn't science.

The prophet who denies the existance of the old gods and teaches of the new is as much of a Genius as Frankenstein. The horror aspects of Genius draw more specifically from victorian to modern mad scientists.

                            But at it's heart Genius is not about science. It's about Transgression.


                            And you know, if that puts you off then that's fine. When I open a book about Mages I'm looking for Kaballah which has different mecahnics to witchcraft, which has different mechanics to hoodoo. Awakening isn't the mage game for me and that's fine.

                            If you want science in your mad scientist game then Genius might not be the game for you. And that's also fine.


                            But if you take Genius for what it is: A game about the people who challange old ideas, create new ones, then watch those ideas transform in ways they could never have convieved of (through a very thick layer of metaphor), it's not perfect but it does what it sets out to quite well.

                                        Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
                                        snipt
                                        considering that I personally haven't read Genius, and don't have a lot of interest at doing so (which is why I've mostly stayed out of this thread other than reading the review- which is just because readings reviews is an hobby of mine) I can't fairly discuss 90% percent of what you said- but there are two things with which I don't agree with you (which is kinda rare- I think that the last time I didn't agreed with you was somewhere in the old forums, with most chances that it was something about Witch Finders :P )

                                        The first point is that not giving an answer to how a Genius does things is ok- it's not. Now, I don't say that you have to have a definite answer. You may keep it as a mystery, but it needs to be defined somehow. If it isn't magic, and isn't science, and isn't some rainbow unicorn poop god- than what is it? If there are theories about the subject than I think its ok- things can be a mystery, because mystery creates stories. But if the Genii (I think this is the plural form. I saw people in the forums use it) don't even care about how what they do work- than all it gives you is a gaping hole in the middle of the game. You don't know what you do nor how you do it, and you don't really care. It makes it feel empty, and that kills stories. The Genii are, after all, supernatural creatures, and Mania is a supernatural energy and Inspiration is a supernatural Tolerance trait- it is ok for things to not make sense, but all of that juice comes from somewhere, and someone should try to investigate. It is better to have as many theories about the subject as there are Genii than having none.

                                        The point is, that is Arc is right, Genius suffer from a strong drive to not define itself, and there is a limit to how much interest one can have at something which is defined by being "not it". Even the Principle is defined- it is the Principle, a power of change served by enigmatic beings called qashmalim. Genius, on the other hand, seem to be about people who do weird things and don't know why or how.

                                        Now, the main problem I have with "Genius don't do science" is this- they do science, perhaps even more than anyone else in the world.

                                        Science is not some sort of force, it is not some weird technique or way of action. Science is a way of thought- science, at its very base, is rising questions, which lead to experiments, which lead to new questions. Science, at its very core, works to disprove an existing theory by conducting an experiment. Science is not about death rays and mars colonies- it is about finding new information, making a theory and test it. In order for a Genius to not "do science", he needs to not question anything, not seek after new subject to investigate, not make new theories about the world and not try to test them. Now, yes, it looks like the Genii has the ability to violate the normal rules of reality- but it doesn't mean they don't do science. It means that by being Inspired, they can disprove the laws which most of humanity- most of the universe- has to follow. They challenge the world itself, making the impossible into possible through the Mania which flows through their minds. The act of rising questions and seeking for answers is science- the constant doubt in your own theories is science. You don't "do science"- you "think science", and as a scientist it kinda hurts me every time I read the words "do science" as if it is like "doing magic" or "do miracles". If Genius do not do science, then what do they do? Building ray guns out of pure inspiration for no reason? That makes them sound kinda boring, as they don't actually do anything- just being used. That is the problem with science fetishism- when you look deep down, it is simply not that interesting.
