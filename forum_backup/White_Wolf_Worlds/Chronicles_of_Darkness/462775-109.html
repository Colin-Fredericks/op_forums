                        Animals of Size 1 are generally best represented by swarms, flocks and other groupings of the animals. Swarms are measured by their radius in yards. A swarm inflicts one point of bashing damage to anyone within its radius.
                        A swarm can inflict more damage by condensing. Every time the swarm condenses to cover half of its full area, it inflicts one additional point of damage per turn.
                        Therefore, a swarm of eight yards in radius inflicts two bashing damage per turn if it constricts down to a fouryard radius, three bashing if it halves that again to a two-yard radius, and four bashing damage per turn it if condensed itself down to a one-yard radius. Though condensing doesn’t usually happen all that often in nature (save in the case of creatures such as killer bees), it is an easy enough thing for a being with supernatural powers to command them to do so.
                        Armor is effective against a swarm only if it covers one’s full body, but even then it provides only half its rating. In addition, targets are distracted by the swarm, suffering –2 dice on all rolls while they are within the radius, even if they’re not specifically attacked.
                        Causing the Tilt: The Swarm Exploit can cause this Tilt, as can similar powers from non-demonic sources. The Tilt can occur naturally if someone disturbs a nest of bees.
                        Ending the Tilt: The swarm cannot be attacked with fists, clubs, swords or guns. Only area-affect attacks such as a torch affect it. Each point of damage inflicted by a flame or other applicable attack halves the swarm’s size. Once the swarm is reduced below a one-yard radius, either all insects are dead or the few remaining disperse.

                                    The massive scything claws and crushings fists of Gauru could count as "area-affect" if you wanted them to. But imo a big part of what makes the Hosts scary is that you can't really solve a beshilu problem by picking a fight with a swarm of rats. Recall the between-chapter fiction of the pack dealing with rat hosts, in which the werewolves are forced to retreat because picking a fair fight with a billion rats isn't a feasible solution even in the short term. I know it's Werewolf, but sometimes it's okay to face the players with an encounter they have to escape from or prepare for. Also werewolves regenerate at least one bashing per turn, so it's more of a stalemate than an inevitably loss.

                                    'course if you want to let your players just club 'em to death, do that. I'd personally still include the -2 penalty - no matter how they're represented mechanically, being covered in angry rats seems like it would be pretty distracting.

                                                            Huge text dump, apologies ahead of time.

                                                            Originally posted by Khanwulf View Post
                                                            Forgive me, but I'd like to ask a set of follow-ups. Wolf-blooded are so often it seems just *assumed* to be around, already connected to packs, that I perceive a distinct gap between "normal human herdling", "unaligned (frequently unaware) wolf-blood" and "aligned and useful wolf-blood".
                                                            Pretty accurate way to look at it.

                                                            If the pack has a way to identify wandering wolf-blooded, how could we expect them to react if:
                                                            1. There is some possibility the wolf-blooded are unaligned or do not know they are wolf-blooded; in other words, they are not knowingly under direction by a pack.
                                                            First remember that detecting wolf-blooded is possible but requires some closeness. From there, feeling them out might be different from one group to another. Suggesting something about wolves might get the wolf-blooded to react a certain way so you know they're 'in the know,' without necessarily overplaying your hand by, say, turning into a giant wolf and potentially scaring them. This actually happened to me in my game and it went about as bad as it could have.

                                                            There's a ton of detective abilities werewolves have access to. Something like 'Know Thy Prey' might clue a werewolf in to whether they're part of a pack as it shows a lot of social Merits.

                                                            A lot might depend on the atmosphere. If a pack is being besieged by the Pure, this wolf-blooded might be some sort of spy. If the wolf-blooded is a particularly nasty looking fellow, with the scent of gun oil and silver, you might watch out. But otherwise some casual questions might help you figure out what they're there for, and if they're up for grabs. 'Do you live nearby?' or 'I work down the street, how bout you?' can be asked in so many different ways, and if a wolf-blooded is just visiting it might not be worth attempting to recruit them. If they live in your territory it's worth keeping tabs on them, at the least. You can get to know them better and if they seem not entirely afraid of the werewolf life, like they aren't freaked out about their Tell and Boon, then bringing them in is likely to go smoother. It helps that there's some sort of inherent incentive for wolf-blooded, they feel better when part of a pack and with werewolves.

                                                            'Being pulled into a pack is as natural a thing as most Wolf-Blooded will ever feel in their lifetimes. This is why so many Wolf-Blooded are fiercely loyal to the pack. Not to any one member, but to the pack itself. It is a home and family and lifeline and sanity that some of them have never fully experienced before.'

                                                            2. The pack is within their territory versus outside of it (either invited to a neighbor's territory or in unclaimed territory, hunting or no).
                                                            Inside your territory, if the wolf-blood is a permanent resident or works there, you're definitely going to want to do something about it. Just finding out if they're part of a larger pack and, if so, what that pack's relationship with yours is would be important. If your pack is outside their territory, or nomadic, you would probably be more reluctant to strike up any long term relationships, as any wolf-blooded potentially belongs to another pack, and it's quite likely they'll be angry if some stranger is sniffing around. This obviously leads to good plothooks and it's not always entirely up to the werewolf. Even aside from things like the Exciting Tell, there's plenty of ways for a wolf-blooded to just get wrapped up in your life.

                                                            Generally I would assume that a pack hunting within enemy territory encountering an unknown wolf-blooded would result in Action!--at least from the wolf-blood's perspective....
                                                            Likely. The wolf-blood might lead you right to your target, especially if it's a werewolf, human, or another wolf-blooded. If it's something unrelated you might just skip over the wolf-blooded. But they can act as hidey holes for spirits, heralds and servants to idigam, and hosts for hosts. They might even be able to tell you something useful about the neighborhood they know and no one else does. Interacting with them could be dangerous for other reasons, though. If the pack whose territory you're in is vicious you might want to keep your head down for the hunt.

                                                            Also, how might reactions vary between Forsaken and Pure? I assume this is where the rubber meets the roadkill for "The High Respect the Low;" the Pure don't do that bit.
                                                            Forsaken are more likely to negotiate and talk things through. Pure are more likely to just take. They're not stupid, though, so they might stalk a bit to see if they could get more, like if they go home and there's a whole house full of wolf-blooded. Or if the wolf-blooded is protected somehow, which wouldn't necessarily stop them but may give them pause to organize more. Ivory Claws are probably the most vampire-like, moving things around so that the wolf-blooded gets fired and has to work in their territory. Slaughtering a family and somehow the wolf-blooded finds out that there's a house for sale really cheap, they just have to move. Then once the wolf-blooded is all settled in the werewolf introduces himself. The Pure aren't the only ones who can do that, though, some Forsaken are going to be brutal and ghost wolves like the Temple of Apollo are probably really good at recruiting, being a cult and all.

                                                            Something else I wanted to point out. There's a rite that hasn't been talked about much. Hunting Ground. The book doesn't say this outright but the way we rule it is that, unless you've used this rite, a Gift or Rite that works 'on your territory' doesn't have nearly as much range. In addition, and this is RAW, it definitively outlines your territory, so you wouldn't wander into someplace and not realize you were setting foot on another werewolf's territory unless you were being really unobservant.
