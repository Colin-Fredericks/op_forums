      I am not able to watch that video where I am, but based on what you said, I can say that I try to use the appropriate calendar depending on the setting of my story. For example, I would use a sidereal calendar if it was based in ancient egypt.

      There are other things to consider, such as the precession of the equinoxes, the fact that solar and lunar cycles are not commensurate, and other factors such as the gross innaccuracies of the Greek and Julian calendars in real history ( How they made such an amateur mistake like doing a leap year every three years instead of four, especially considering it came from the Egyptians who were accurate, I don't know. But just wanted to give a concrete example.) I like to keep all the mistakes made by the culture and generally reflect with how advanced they are with astronomy and mathematics.

      How can I know if what I claim I know to be true is rejecting the idea that there is something I might not know? How can I know if what I claim I don't know to be true is rejecting the idea that there is something I do know?
      -Zhuangzi

          The video suggests a global calendar based on human cultural development, that effectively just adds 10,000 years to the Gregorian date (so it would currently be 12016 of the human era). It dates from the earliest known stone temple ruins in Anatolia.

          (note: we only assume it's a "temple" because that's archaeologist for "we have no idea what this was actually for". Religion is the go to answer for anything they can't figure out a utilitarian explanation of. Keep that in mind whenever there's a discussion of "ceremonial" tools, or writing that can't be deciphered but clearly isn't an inventory list so they assume it served a spiritual purpose)
