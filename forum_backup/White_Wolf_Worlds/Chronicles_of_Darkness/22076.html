    Time Travel and You

    12-02-2013, 10:55 PM
    So there are a few things that I would like clarification on Time Travel in this setting. Specifically the Splinter timelines of Seattle (as it's pretty much the only part of the book that goes deep into the possibility).

    I figure other people have their own questions and hopefully I can give my oppinion as you all have helped me.

    so anyway:
    1. On Stigmatics: The book indicates that the timeline will make them not exist after 4 rotations of a repeated timeframe. Does this mean that they stop existing period or do they exist in the same way that travellers to this timeline from the main timeline do? ie they are completly out side of it.
    2. On Cryptids: how do cryptids work when they are created in a splinter?
    3. So the book indicates 2 ways that an Unchained can affect the main timeline through changes made in a splinter: By doing something physical and expending a dot of Willpower, and Pacts. Am I getting that right? or are there more? What if you never confront a, let's say a house wife, but you manipulate events (during that single year that that splinter encompass) to slowly erode at her sanity or the way she thinks on a given subject. After noticing a major change in her behavior, can the demon spend the dot to make that psychological harm affect the main timeline?
    4. When a Demon changes something in a splinter timeline, how does that stick if the demon decides to exit the splinter and enough time goes by that the shard starts the time over again? If I were to make an assumption, it would be that the events that were changed will come to pass through differrent events that don't neccessarily need the demon be present to inflict them. So if the demon destroys a building by blowing it up and spends the dot of will power and leaves to the present, some other, perhaps likeminded human (or otherwise) within the shard would set us up the bomb instead of the demon (this ofcoarse affects much more than just abuilding being blown up.
    5. Supernatural creatures within the shards. The book indicated that there are supernatural creatures in the shards same way as there are humans. So that would mean changelings and fae and mages. Do any of them have any sort of protection against the nature that they are in?
    6. On places: I wasn't quite sure. can a demon leave seattle in one of the shards? or does the map warp around somehow or do interesting flicks of space?
    7. On realms: so the book hinted that the shard had a shadow, access to the hedge, and it's own lower depths (if they were a part of that timeline). Does that mean that there is a separate Abyss and a separate Supernal? Perhaps just encompassing just the symbols that are represented in the shard?

                          Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
                          If I may add a further question; what is the length of the 1889 shard? The original "Sister Cities" seed in the GMC implied it was a completely alternate timeline, but the Demon book has me confused. When does the 1889 splinter run from?
                          The 1889 splinter is a weird one. It's ongoing and doesn't reset and doesn't affect the dominant timeline in any way. The culture and technology don't advance at all, although it suggests that some aspects of the setting have 'evolved' from their original year (fashion norms, for instance). Also, the calendars all say 1930. I'm not official, but I personally take that to mean that calendar-wise, the year repeats but events don't. So even though December 31, 1930 was on a Wednesday and January 1, 1930 was also on a Wednesday, nobody notices that there are two Wednesdays in a row. (and perhaps time ran forward as normal -- real world standard normal -- until 1930, at which point certain things froze, but that's entirely personal speculation)


                          Originally posted by xiongrey View Post
                          @MythicFox but the wording is still unclear. do they lose their physical form and will never be seen again? or do they not exist according to that timeline like demons are when they visit. I'm getting confused with the wording. It says they cease to exist but they don't define what existing means in this context. According to that timeline shard, demon's don't exist but they still get to remain conscious and physical.

                          EDIT: another question, if a demon needs to spend an extended amount of time in a splinter, does their cover erode when the cover isn't where it usually is supposed to be? (at work, at school, home for dinner ETC)
                          Personally, I'd take the book's lines of "From the moment a stigmatic is created, a splinter reality starts writing him out of existence. A splinter stigmatic has only four reboots before he disappears entirely, with each successive reboot after the first removing him a degree further from all of his connections." and "The fifth reboot erases him entirely." to mean exactly that. The stigmatic is no longer a part of that splinter. The splinter's history has been rewritten such that he has not only ceased to be but ceased to ever have been. He is gone forever. I would consider it a 'fixed change' that persists through the reboot, as per the text just above the sidebar about stigmatics and splinters.

                          And as for your second question, I think it's one of those "Depends on the Cover and depends on the Storyteller" sorts of things. But given that a demon has to worry about Cover erosion for some of that stuff even without completely moving to an alternate reality, I'm pretty sure the answer is 'Yes.'

                          Although I, in a game I was running, might hold off on compromise rolls for that sort of erosion until the demon returns. But that's solely because I like the dramatic impact and narrative possibilities of a demon losing track of time in a splinter and coming 'home' to find out they've made a Big Mistake.


                          Also, now that I have the PDF open, lemme take a moment to try and answer the rest of your original questions as best I can (but note that I'm not anyone official in this regard, just offering opinions based on what I'm looking at in the text):

                          2. Given that stigmatics and cryptids are very much alike, I'd probably suggest treating them the same.

                          3. Pacts and "making a change in the physical realm and spending a dot of Willpower to make it stick" seem to be the only ways. Indirect changes, like emotionall fallout from a change, is probably one of those 'time travel is wonky and a lot of stuff should be up to the Storyteller' sorts of things. (like whether or not stigmatics can escape)

                          4. Aside from the fact that I wouldn't consider blowing up a building to be a 'small-scale change,' your interpretation seems pretty straightforward.

                          5. There's nothing to suggest that other supernatural creatures are treated any differently than normal humans in a splinter. They likely have no clue that they're in an alternate timeline and aren't any more immune to the quirks of the splinter than anyone else (aside from stigmatics and demons).

                          7. Unless the Storyteller is playing with the cosmology, I'd argue that it's one 'set' of Supernal Realms and Abyss across all splinters just like with the Shadow, the Hedge, etc. (Especially since Imperial Mysteries says pretty firmly that the Hedge does connect to a portion of Supernal Arcadia)
