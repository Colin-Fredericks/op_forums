                        @Satchel: Grendel's mother was probably an Oroboros, and so the dragon that Beowulf did battle with was probably a Nidhoggr (considering the overt damage it inflicted in its assault), based on Shock's interpretation. This might be an interesting topic to cite in literature inspirations, or maybe as flavor mythology somewhere in the manual (or both).

                        I wasn't expecting the B-Splat discussion to go this smoothly, and short of clarifications on the Chimerae it looks like we're about done with that item. But, before moving on to the next problem ticket I want to take some time to discuss some fringe mechanisms in the Oroboroi condition.

                        Typhoon: It was mentioned that Typhoon is inappropriate because when dragons are depicted as weather spirits they have control of this capacity. I was justifying the 'wild weather' as a strictly Oroboroi shortcoming, and now that we have official terminology I thought I would clarify.
                        Nidhoggr can and do control the weather to their whim. In short, they may trigger a Typhoon effect (GMC hazardous environment) any time they please, at a rating likely based on their rank (But not equal to their rank, because even low-end Nidhoggr should be able to make reasonably dangerous weather phenomenon to cover their tracks). Conversely, the usurper Oroboroi control the weather subconsciously, almost making it a secondary backlash mechanic like Heartbeat. There are of course advantages for weather exploding when a usurper dragon gets into a rage, but its unpredictability is dangerous.
                        I was trying to make Typhoon the Dragon equivalent of Frenzy: an Oroboros doesn't lose control of herself but rather the weather around her. Is there a better way to present this system to make that intention more clear? Perhaps the phenomenon should be severed from the Furnace mechanic. Any help with this would be greatly appreciated.

                        Jade Allergy: Frogs also mentioned that Jade is a pure substance and dragons should not be maimed by this substance. Again, this is a matter that is assisted by the clarification of terminology: Nidhoggr are not bothered by Jade in the slightest, but the usurpers are. I think if we explain that the act of stealing the Hearts in the first place contaminated the Oroboroi with the blood of treachery the Jade pill would be easier to swallow. Thoughts?

                        Vomiting Up Organs versus Devouring: Devouring the Hearts of other Oroboroi has consistently been one justification for increasing a character's Furnace. It's the easy button for growing power. The problem is, devouring doesn't actually SEEM like an easy button, because the normal way to grow Furnace was never clarified. I tried to fix this in the CIJ half-book by making standard Furnace growth a body and psychological horror; an Oroboros must rip out the very things that make her human in order to grow her divinity, or keep them at the cost of her species as she greedily snuffs out other dragons for power. In practice, though, Frogs is correct that such a system encourages roll-playing instead of role-playing. So we need an alternative. How do we present the idea that a dragon turning to Heart devouring is a means of avoiding hardship? I think the 'vomit up your mortal soul' thing is good, but we need a different way to present it in the manual.
                        Maybe emotions aren't the way to go. Maybe it needs to be the same kind of loss someone making a bargain with the Forgotten would suffer?

                                I'm not the system writer a lot of other folks are, so I've been keeping quiet, but I would like to chime in here. Personally, I'm of the mind that a discount in experience cost, skipping training time altogether, and the role-play value of forever diminishing your species, should be enough to show it's an "easy button," and I was not a fan of the organ-vomiting thing. It's the equivalent of using steroids instead of putting in more hours in the gym, or buying a degree from a diploma-mill instead of earning it at a respectable school. Giving in to your rage and tapping the dark side of the Force rather than practicing the discipline of a Jedi. You get your power quickly and easily, while another person gets similar or better results by putting in time and sweat.

                                If you really think another cost beyond that to raise Furnace is needed, I could see two possible ways... either you're refining your divinity and thus growing more remote from ordinary humans (taking a penalty on social rolls as you have trouble connecting and others think you're just stuck up), or having to give up an Asset to fuel the fire. Consuming part of your Hoard, retreating from a Province and letting it lie fallow as you focus inward, the emotional wrench of letting a Maiden go...

                                            Welcome back Demo!

                                            Okay, so consensus seems to be that Furnace growth should be tied to 'liquidating' Assets. I think this is excellent because it re-emphasizes the selfishness of a Devourer; they're eating the Hearts of other dragons because they can't bear to do away with their own Assets.

                                            I have a few follow-up questions before I start working this into the document:

                                            1.) Should Asset loss to grow Furnace be a dot-by-dot matter or a binary All-or-Nothing sacrifice? Would a dragon be able to retreat slightly from her Province to pay the needs of her growing Furnace or would she need to intentionally dissolve the community?

                                            2.) Should Asset loss have a specific number of dots required to raise Furnace? If so, should that requirement be the new Furnace rating attained? That is, to earn Furnace 3 without devouring a Heart an Oroboros must liquidate three dots of Assets?

                                            3.) If Assets are lost through other means (death of a maiden by a third party, theft of Hoard), should the Oroboros still be able to grow Furnace through that loss? I'm leaning towards 'no' because the Oroboros must harvest the raw benefit of the Asset through its destruction (steal the talent of the maiden, collapse the Province, consume the Hoard, shred the Ideology).

                                            4.) Should shredding Ideologies place them on the sin list as discussed? If so, it might make playing the game more interesting as Furnace grows, because the Oroboros effectively accumulates bans as she naturally grows her Furnace.
                                            Alternatively, should the dissolution of ANY Asset add a sin to the Mandate? For example, if an Oroboros steals the talent of her Maiden she can no longer interact with that maiden, such is a violation of her personal mandate. Mind, such would only work if Asset depletion is all-or-nothing.


                                            Even just typing it out, it seems like All or Nothing sacrifice is the way to go, along with requiring a certain number of Asset dots lost to build the Furnace. This creates an interesting inversion for Dragon as compared to other lines: building Furnace the standard way results in more Energy per turn, but a LOWER maximum Energy (That is, until the dragon recovers from the loss with the pursuit of a new Asset... possibly one stolen from another Oroboros).


                                            Any further thoughts on this, or shall we move to the Treasury (IE the big, BIG thematic enchilada)?

                                                The first part ties into part 2 of what you asked. The second part is a rather simple affair to answer: Dragons shouldn't be dissolving their community but rather have it be self sufficient long enough to get what she needs to get done so I favor "slightly retreat" option. After all, if things fall apart right after a Dragon stops being completely involved, it's a sign of weakness, not strength.

                                                But this would be more up to the players to do the cost/benefit analysis. Retreat now and risk looking weak in exchange for greater power, or hold on for a later time where things have calmed down enough.

                                                        08-17-2014, 09:20 PM
                                                        Originally posted by PaladinDemo View Post
                                                        So, what I missed?
                                                        A lot. We started in on developing the new Philosophies (Pandalfino wrote two great ones, FallenEco is working on a third, and we have two to go), I released a "Christmas in July" half-book with the GMC compatible Ablutions and Edicts (There's a link to it in the first post of the thread), I sort of blew my top, we settled down, and now we're straightening out the thematic elements of the game (Antagonists, B-Splats, Fringe Mechanics, Realms Invisible, and Supplied Mythology).

                                                        Quick overview of things that are different but not in the CIJ half-book: the dragons of old are now called Nidhoggr to reduce confusion between them and the Oroboroi, and they present a seriously terrifying threat (they want the Hearts of their relatives back, and they attack under cover of natural disasters). Also, the Deep Ones are now called the Forgotten and their bargaining is much more existential (They're trying to capture the quantifiable definition of life to make themselves real). Pilot Lights are now the combination of having both the Dragonborn Merit and the Bond of Brood Merit, Dragonborn can also be created when an Oroboros blesses an expectant mother (they're called Spartoi in such an instance), Chimerae are animals subjected to the bond of Brood (Gravitas is working on that), and... Oh, the part in the CIJ about sacrificing organs to raise Furnace has been vetoed, we're going with Asset dissolution instead.


                                                        I want to wait just a little longer before we move into the Treasury, just to make sure everybody has time to process what has been discussed so far and put in their thoughts. If you want to jump in on the thematic discussions, I recommend starting on Page 7 and reading through... this post.
