            Alright, let's get into Chapter Four.


            ...But first, a Devotion!

            Originally posted by Sin Again: Daeva
            Speak of the Devil
            (Celerity •••••, Majesty ••••)

            And he shall appear. Kindred with this Devotion learn to realize this legend, seeming to always be near those thinking of them.

            This Devotion costs 4 Experiences to learn.

            Cost: 1 Willpower

            Requirement: The victim must have been affected by the vampire’s Awe within a number of nights equal to Blood Potency.

            Dice Pool: Presence + Stealth + Celerity

            Action: Instant

            Roll Results

            Success: The vampire appears somewhere within the vicinity of the victim, just out of sight. If the victim speaks the vampire’s name aloud within the time period noted above, she’ll be aware of it, and can use this Devotion without spending a Willpower.

            Exceptional Success: The vampire is mystically aware of the surroundings she’s about to appear in, and can pick up the flow of a conversation in progress, or insert herself into the scene in an otherwise dramatic fashion.

            Failure: The Blood lies still in the veins. The Devotion has no effect.

            Dramatic Failure: The vampire teleports… somewhere.
            Normally, I don't preview a lot of stuff from the systems chapter, but this one is one of my favourite Devotions we've ever done.

            Anyway, Chapter Four. Let's start with the Alu.

            Those of you familiar with Requiem's First Edition probably remember the En, a bloodline of power-hungry Daeva who could ignore many of the downsides of diablerie due to bonding with a mysterious demon that bonds with them when they join the bloodline. The En are rather reviled among the fanbase for being able to get past a lot of the limitations of being vampires for not that much of a downside. I don't hate the En, but I do think they're kind of a period when they should be a comma. It's the difference between

            "Join us and you can diablerize to your heart's content."

            and

            "Join us and you can diablerize to your heart's content, but..."

            You need that but, or else they're just kind of boring. Being able to do cool stuff should always come with a hefty price, at least in Vampire. That's drama!

            So, I wanted to include the En, but I felt that if we wanted to be true to their concept, they wouldn't work as a bloodline. However, they would work wonderfully as a Horror. So, we decided that the focus should be on that aforementioned demon. The Alu was a much more compelling catalyst for drama, and it offered a chance to make the En's diablerie proficiency into something deeply horrific.

            That said, we did include a short option on using them as a bloodline, and even a few plot hooks about what happened to that version of the En (if they existed at all).

            As for development stuff, the Alu didn't change that much through the course of production. Their Condition was initially a bit simpler, but I added a few bits and bobs from their original incarnation once final drafts were in. I also included the little sidebar from our friend "M. Lucky."

            So, without further ado, here's the Alu/En. Tomorrow, let's look at the Nemites, new(ish) covenant.

            Alu: The worm turns

            "Ambition outlives the ambitious."

            Newbies at Elysium are always an event.

            Usually, it’s somebody’s hot shit new childe, or another pretty young neonate who thinks she’s Queen of the Damned. But last night, it was this ancilla no one’s heard from in decades, apparently seeing an opportunity to start fresh. Except, the moment he sets foot in the room, Martine — d’Entrement’s dirty little snitch — just starts pointing and screaming at the dude like his head’s spinning in its socket.

            No one would tell us what all the fuss was all about. We just had to sit there and wait for the goon squad to drag the poor bastard out; didn’t go quietly either. Don’t have the foggiest what became of him, but I doubt we’ll be seeing him again any time soon.

            They tell me Martine never did stop screaming. Not till they staked her for the night.

            Time is an uncontested hunter. Even if a vampire never meets Final Death, the burden of her inhuman nature will eventually claim her. Tragedies beget atrocities, and atrocities beget degeneration, and degeneration begets bestiality. The Requiem is unceasing malice for the sake of survival, and the once-human mind will invariably break. The Kindred call these wretches draugr, and their vile, frenzied existences almost always meet a quick end.

            Almost. Once in a while, with no Humanity left to tame the Beast, something else slithers in. For as long as vampires have haunted humanity, the Alu have moved in the shadows. These bizarre creatures use draugr as meat puppets, or bargain with more humane vampires for a safe haven in return for an immense and terrible power.

            Background

            Alu are crimson, gelatinous worms that float through Twilight. Things of spirit yet devoid of it, they have no eyes, ears, or any other sensory organs. Before it finds a host, an Alu’s existence is little more than profound loneliness, punctuated by brief flashes of immense craving. If they were born with hopes or dreams, those things decay in the face of a ceaseless, deafening void.

            A Worm’s only drive is to find a body, and to this end, the creature can sense vampires on an instinctual level. If it’s lucky enough to come across a draugr, it can devour the beast’s last bits of soul before taking over as the only thinking entity inside its mindless shell. There is no resistance. Once complete, this process creates a composite being: the En.

            But joining with a draugr is only the first step. The new En is a quick learner, eager to devour the world around it and experience all the pleasures of sight, sound, and — most of all — taste. Once it finishes digesting the last vestiges of its host’s soul, a new drive coalesces. While they must sustain themselves as all vampires do, the only true meal for an En is the Amaranth, and it will bring Kindred and draugr alike to heel to sate that hunger. Vitae may fuel their stolen bodies, but they require the spark of a soul to make eternity worthwhile.

            Finding a draugr isn’t the only way to become an En, though. It’s the easiest, but vampires with no Humanity rarely survive in the All Night Society. Instead, if an Alu comes across a “normal” vampire, it can try to take a willing vessel. By striking Faustian pacts, it can assure it never suffers the torment of its primordial existence again. In exchange, all the vampire has to do is commit a little diablerie now and again. And again… and again…

            En Isiratuu

            No on still living has ever witnessed an Alu’s creation, and no one knows how long they live (if they have a lifespan at all). However, they do share some history.

            Many centuries before the fall of Rome, a city sat in the cradle of civilization. In that city, the Kindred were brash, lording over the night without the restraint of a Masquerade. The kine were their playthings, less than slaves, though there was no brotherhood among the dead. They warred on each other with such spectacle that it would make modern quarrels seem like playfighting. The Dark Strangers claim it was in this city that they first came to be, created with dark magics to aid the Kindred in their endless wars. Or perhaps they were simply opportunistic, like bacteria feeding around a festering wound.

            In this damned city, a monster called En Isiratuu rose as king. En was a Daeva, perhaps one of the first, and where his soul once sat, a powerful Alu guided him. He committed countless atrocities upon his people, both enemies and friends alike, for all before him was his birthright and dominion. Many tried to assuage the empty heart of their king, but all were met with failure. En could not be motivated by or pity or shame, for his cruelty existed for its own sake.

            Although En’s kingdom fell, tonight, he’s the ideal all Udug Hul aspire to. Some Kindred familiar with the myth wonder if such mastery over kine was possible at all, even in ancient times, and En’s similarities to the Epic of Gilgamesh are… suspicious. But the Alu simply say that facts seem like lies to unbelievers. Joined Worms refer to themselves by En’s name both in tribute and as a reminder of the heights they might achieve.

            Body Snatching

            The presence of an En leads to disappearances of Kindred and kine alike, as many victims as the creature thinks it can get away with. The more the En eats, the more brazen it becomes, as restraint becomes unnecessary in the face of unrestrained power.

            Alu incursions often start when the All Night Society is at its most fractured: anywhere Kindred fight amongst themselves with enough desperation to engage in diablerie, or where draugr are left alone to fester. As crises expand beyond control, a vampire will do anything for survival, and an offer to preserve even a fraction of her power in these dire times is more than enough for Alu to get a foot in the door.

            With newfound strength, the Alu works to fulfill every desire and dream its host has. However, as the Worm digests each meal their bargain provides, the vampire’s psyche fades away until nothing remains but a new En.

            Systems

            Without hosts, Alu are completely vulnerable. Anyone who can physically interact with creatures in Twilight can kill an Udug Hul with a single strike, crushing it like the Worm it is. They have no traits of their own, move slowly, and aren’t cognizant of the world around them beyond the presence of vampires. The one thing an Alu can sense with clarity is the taint of diablerie, which lights up like hellfire in the creature’s non-vision.

            Whenever an Alu enters a draugr or hollows out a Kindred host’s soul, the amalgamation becomes an En. Unless otherwise noted, En operate as Humanity 0 vampires, albeit ones with human intellect and an inherent diablerie addiction; they do not have clans and do not suffer acquired banes.
            • The creature uses its host’s traits. However, it suffers a –3 modifier on untrained Social Skills instead of –1, and failures on mundane Empathy and Socialize rolls are always dramatic. An Alu often resembles its first host’s personality, sometimes even convincing itself that it’s still that vampire in some way.
            • With a successful diablerie, the En can choose to steal any bloodline gift the victim knows rather than a normal Discipline or Skill, if possible. It must meet any basic requirements or prerequisites to use the effect, but it can learn the power for future use. This does not allow the En to learn other powers of that bloodline, including higher dots of a unique Discipline.
            • If the host body dies, the Worm is ejected into Twilight.
            • If an En Embraces, it must spend a dot of Blood Potency. Its childer are always Daeva, regardless of its body’s original clan.
            • If it can see a vampire, an En can always sense if it has ever committed diablerie.

                                The Nemites. Let's talk about them!

                                For those of you who haven't read The Danse Macabre, the Nemites are a minor pagan covenant who worship at the altar of unconditional vampire love. They believe the love (and Vitae) of humans is sinful, a distraction from the purity of Kindred love, and they've adopted an odd syncretic cult around the worship of Diana and her herald, the Rex Nemorensis, whom they...well, they don't call it diablerie. But that's what it is.

                                I've always liked the Nemites, and when I was looking at covenants we might convert for this book, they sprang out at me. They have a cool, creepy concept that's niche enough to be it's own thing, and they have a Daeva vibe that still leaves room for other clans.

                                Originally, this covenant was going to figure into the Blowouts chapter, because I'd envisioned them having special Merits that let them be better at social gatherings: their big advantage in First Edition was people power, and that made a kind of sense to me. However, as I went on through planning, a kind of sense was all it really made. The problem with creating Merits for special subsystems is that if a player buys one, you either have to use that subsystem, or they've wasted their Experiences on something that will never come up. I don't envision the Blowout system being necessary for every chronicle (it might not even be necessary if the coterie DOES throw a party, if the stakes aren't high), so as much as that would've been aninteresting interpretation, I decided they should be a little more accessible.

                                Our version of the Nemites doesn't differ too much from their original incarnation, but I think it offers a lot more modes of play, which is always my first concern. The author (Dark Days Radio host and the forum's own Dr Ether) had the brilliant idea of adding a multi-level marketing veneer to their cult, and that gave them a focus I think the original version lacked (it also distinguished them more from the Circle of the Crone). Now the Nemites are less out and proud, but far more sinister, hiding within the other covenants and siphoning off their human resources to feed their voracious Vitae addiction. They create pyramids of vessels, keeping those at the top fed and punishing those who would sully their downline with human blood. The other big change was giving them access to Cruac, though that isn't necessarily their focus: Cruac is another way they hide themselves among the other factions (though considering "the curse of the lone ritualist," one wonders just how tied to the Acolytes they truly are...)

                                This covenant didn't change much in development. The only major addition was another Merit to reflect their First Edition Merit discount, and a little sidebar expanding on the Rex Nemorensis, which is a concept that takes a bit of work to get your head around.

                                Anyway, that's it for me, so come on over and have a drink with the Nemites (just don't sign anything). Tomorrow, would you like to hear about a horror or a ghoul family? If no one votes, I'll decide for myself, but just know that you have the power! Also, if you haven't done so, please consider leaving us a review or a star rating on the Vault. I can't emphasize enough how useful that is to boosting sales. People often overlook community content, and a review can be a push in the right direction!

                                The Nemites: The Tree Surgeons

                                "Don't just maximize your downline: Bleed it dry."

                                You want to join the Nemites because

                                You’re looking for community. You want someone to have your back. You need to feel valued by those ahead of you and those behind you. You want to escape a stagnant Requiem within the other factions of the All Night Society. You need to rise above the rest.

                                The big picture

                                Love as vampires experience it is a strange beast. Their dead hearts no longer flutter when they see a person they connect with; their bodies don’t stir to have sweaty, glorious, awkward sex in one-night stands; they don’t get a shot of endorphins when they see an old friend for the first time in years. They can’t even mourn properly, unchanged and immortal as multitudes pass on into true death.

                                The Nemites know this isn’t real love. It’s animal-like, born out of the primal needs of the ape brain. Human “love” is a biochemical response to sex and the need to protect offspring. Grief is also just an organic response, the lizard brain reacting to loneliness. But the love of Kindred is stronger — purer — and divorced of any built-in exchange of services, or the need to protect each other.

                                Perhaps, they say, true love originated with the dead, wrestled from the grip of the gods. If so, the Blood isn’t just a nonchemical process: It’s divine.

                                The Nemites are a cult and aspiring covenant that subscribes to the ideals of unconditional love among vampires. Through the emotional manipulations of Majesty and the binding power of Vitae, the love they build isn’t a temporary bond born out of primitive need, but instead a union of a holy essence all Kindred hold within themselves, a “multi-level” network of roots that nourishes their community and offers a path to spiritual ascension.

                                Their rituals, both socially and supernaturally, form an extended network, cleaving members to each other and allowing blood to be siphoned from the bottom of their hierarchy. At the base of the tree is the Rex Nemorensis, the eternal king who subsists on this spiderweb of tributaries — one that falls apart if members break their promises. Thus, the only way to maintain this link is to trap more saplings in the system. The love of the covenant must be everything to its members, while outside connections must be cut. Permanently.

                                Where we came from

                                [REDACTED]

                                Our practices

                                A community is only as strong as its roots, and ours are fed with blood. Blood is an offer of freedom and an escape from stagnant power structures that would enslave Kindred for the rest of their Requiems. With us, you’ll find a community that quells the Beast and soothes the loneliness of death — one with an exciting path to promotion.

                                All kings must die, but ours is reborn in the grave, just as Kindred take their second breath with the Embrace. The title of Rex Nemorensis is claimed in a ritual combat called the Passing of the Golden Bough. Taken from our sacred grove, a branch is fashioned into a stake for a successor to drive through the reigning King’s heart. Drinking deep of his Vitae and drawing his soul into theirs, the victor offers the slain monarch’s blood to the community. In an orgy of gore, of entrails and bone and divine madness, the new King binds us together in an ever more perfect love. It’s exhilarating. Enlightening. Something we all crave, leading us all to seek the Bough. As a final act, the new Rex Nemorensis rips the spine from the former, planting it in the sacred grove as a symbolic green shoot.

                                Like Hippolytus, we server ties to mortal desires that would bind us to a profane world, and which would dilute our downlines. Our love is founded on true, sanguine devotion — what other vampires crudely call the Vinculum. But ours is no involuntary bondage as that awful word suggests: We are a family formed by the purest agape.

                                Each Nemite is expected to tend to their downline. Some run self-help groups, others mentor investors through mindfulness training. The goal is same: Be a good influencer, keep up the engagement of prospects, and crush mortal distractions. No, no, it’s not so oppressive as it sounds! This is community building. This is working together for the benefit of the Upstream, and sometimes that means acting for the good of all rather than the good of you.

                                You too can benefit from our growing and dynamic community. You too can be a sponsor! But just how do we spread our message to the masses? “To grow out roots, we must recruit!” We silently exist within the other covenants, mimicking a dynastic house, or a Dragon chantry, or even a Carthian cell. Our veneration of the reborn king nestles within the Lancea et Sanctum’s teachings just as well as it does within the Circle of the Crone’s. Hidden in plain sight, we identify those who’ve been trampled by the humanist sects and offer them a path out. Acolytes are the easiest marks, as our rituals and blood sorcery closely follow those taught by the Dark Mother.

                                Conflict comes between our family and the Circle when, like a weed, we grow too wild and deep within their garden. They see us as usurpers, enforcing a lockstep faith, but we don’t seek to drown out their choir. We just want to create harmony. It’s a shame some wish to sing their own song so selfishly. Such singular voices must be stifled, and if doing so adds to the Upstream? Well, at least they found a way to make a contribution.

                                Care must be taken to maintain our downline, but feeding upon mortals is profane, opening our divine souls to sin. Drinking human blood pleasures the Beast and drives us to dark temptations. Yet, as keepers of Hecate’s faith, we stand at the crossroads of life and death, and for this we must balance the scales. Just as we transmute blood into Vitae, death into life, we cleanse the world of sin as we suckle on the blood of man. And so, our faith is a paradox: We strive for purity while tainting ourselves with mortal sin. However, the act of gifting blood to others — watering the roots of our society — balances the scales as our love transforms the polluted blood of mortals. Therefore, each night we gather, our downline presenting kine as offerings to the family, and we collectively feed. Share the sin, save the sinners.

                                However, there are limits to that principle. When the roots break down, when the downline fails, there must be reparations, and those who violate our family’s faith must balance the scales. Just as the Rex Nemorensis must water the roots with their passing, so too must the penitent make sacrifice: One of their mortal blood relatives must be offered up in a symbolic act of severing their connection to the profane world.

                                We count all clans within our number, and each one has guided our tree at different times through history — even (allegedly) the Julii in our earliest nights. However, of all the Kindred, those of Daeva blood tend to stand at the forefront of our community. The nature of the Serpents’ curse means our teachings have great benefit to them. Coupled with their command of mortal hearts, they quickly master the downline and spread our roots far and wide.

                                Speaking of, how about we get you signed up?

                                New Merits (Excerpt)

                                Community Chest (•)

                                Prerequisite: Nemite Status •

                                Effect: A community is defined by its members. Maybe that’s a truism, but the Tree Surgeons martial their personal connections almost to the exclusion of all other assets. As such, they always have backup. Your character gains access to dots in Allies, Contacts, Mentor, and Retainer equal to Covenant Status, distributed as you see fit. This benefit applies to Status in all covenants. For example, if she has three dots with the Nemites and one with the Carthians, she gains four dots of these Social Merits.

                                Drawback: These resources belong to the Grove, and drawing on them for selfish purposes is bad for the Upstream. Furthermore, if your character is accessing another covenant’s social resources, she’d better be willing to share them with her real family when times get tough.

                                                        Welcome back! Let's look at a ghoul family.

                                                        The Children of Nirriti are from all the way back in the original Ghouls sourcebook. Those of you who've followed these threads of mine probably know that I have a complicated relationship with Ghouls. There's some really good stuff in there, but it's also offensive and puerile in a way that really tests one's patience. The 1E Children definitely fall on the puerile side of Ghouls. They're also pretty boring. They did bad things to make their very-specific-backstory master feel good about herself, and generally had a lot of sex and performed a lot of masochism.

                                                        This is one of the few instances in our updates where we've just done away with the backstory altogether. Normally, I try to preserve at least a seed of it, but my brief for the author was to use the name and some of the trappings, but to otherwise jettison the rest. If you squint, you could probably fit the old backstory in, but we didn't feel anyone would miss it all that much. And, as always, if you like the old version, you can always use that in conjunction without our updated systems.

                                                        The 2E Children of Nirriti take the original's vague sin-eater concept and puts it to the forefront: these are ghouls who can feed on a vampire's blood and lessen her curse, in exchange for giving her the ability to perform higher levels of Cruac (normally ghouls can only do level one rites). In terms of development, the only really big addition was a sidebar about the Nemites, who fit very well together with our version of the Nirriti Cult.

                                                        Anyway, I'll let the write-up speak for itself. Tomorrow, I'm going to take a break, because I need to catch up on some housework, but on Monday, we'll have a look at another ghoul family: the Brigmans.

                                                        Children of Nirriti: The ones who pray for you

                                                        "O Goddess! Invest thy sin in me!"

                                                        Where we came from

                                                        In the beginning there was chaos. Then, the goddess Nirriti granted humanity the sanctity of death. With a final stop, she gave the confusion of living a destination and a purpose, and in return for worship, so say her Children, the goddess provides them the power to keep disorder at bay.

                                                        The Children of Nirriti sprang up in India in the waning nights of the colonial age. Perhaps they were once true worshippers of the Vedic goddess, but if so, that hasn’t been true as long as anyone living or dead can remember. The self-proclaimed Chosen are one of the most fervent ghoul cults in the All Night Society, and the Serpents own them body and soul. The Daeva wouldn’t allow it otherwise. By taking Vitae from their patrons, these ghouls “eat” Kindred sins for a short time, lessening the Curse.

                                                        Where they acquired this trait is unknown. The party line, of course, is that the goddess Nirriti blessed them to work her will, infusing their blood with the ability to leech chaos from the world. For their part, the Daeva believe this deity was one of their own, some Serpent who was clever enough to appropriate a goddess of death as her disguise. This doesn’t answer the question of how she imbued her servants with a way to mitigate her bane, but perhaps her assumed identity became more than a mask.

                                                        If Nirriti was just another Serpent with a god complex, she could’ve done worse than styling herself after a Vedic god, one with so many names to hide behind. Any vampire of any gender can wear the mantle of “Nirriti” and gain a dedicated coven, just as long as they’re willing to suffer (or indulge) in the cult’s theology. To the Chosen, Kindred are gods who walk among the living. With their own Mistress of Death watching over them, smiting their enemies and granting them boons, the Sin-Eaters accept every twist of fate and feeding as divinely ordained. They believe they bleed the taint of a fallen world by accepting its burden from their goddess. Perhaps their rituals only stem the tide, but eating a little chaos is better than allowing the void on the edges of reality to claw its way in.

                                                        Who we are tonight

                                                        The Chosen are no longer recluses in saffron robes and isolated temples. Perhaps you’ve even met one and not known it: Sin-Eaters work their rituals around unassuming jobs and in quiet suburbs, or concentrate their operations in “chaotic” regions where wars, disease, and decay flourish. There’s no better way to keep the madness at bay than to wade through it.

                                                        Some Nirriti cults still hold to the old Vedic litanies, but others have transmuted their practices into the Wiccan, Pagan, Santerían, or even secular Satanist traditions. While Sin-Eaters can be found all across the All Night Society, they’re most often tied to Serpents in the Circle of the Crone and (more and more of late) the Nemites, as most of the family’s teachings center around the practice of blood magic.

                                                        However, even outside the auspices of the Mother’s Army or the Tree Surgeons, the family practices Crúac as a sacrament for their deity and as a practical way to punish sin. As the Chosen send more of their scions into the world, they prove themselves devoted additions to all the other Kindred covenants. A few are even associated with the Lancea et Sanctum; it’s no great leap from pagan sacrifices of body and blood to the pomp and ceremony of a Black Mass. Sanctified Chosen and their regnants are careful not to flaunt the family’s Crúac use, but many justify it as the cost of fighting sin. Sometimes you beat the Devil by using his own tools against him.

                                                        The forms of sin include betrayals of their blood kin or profane acts against their particular version of the faith, or at least their regnant’s interpretation if it. All relationships and pregnancies are strictly controlled by the High Priests and Priestesses of each branch of the family, and all is done in service to the Goddess of Death, for without her they cannot hold the void at bay forever.

                                                        This is the role of the Children of Nirriti — but what do they do when the goddess defies their traditions? The Chosen don’t worship Kindred idly; they expect their deities to uphold their duties, smiting the family’s rivals and providing them the Vitae required for their rites. The vampire who abuses her Chosen, who wallows in sin and allows chaos to reign, will find her ghouls’ magic turned against her. When a false divinity abandons her followers, her flock may rise up and perform a final ritual, consuming their goddess whole and raising another, better one in her place.

                                                        Benefit: Once per week, if a vampire feeds Vitae to a Sin-Eater in a special ritual, her Humanity is considered one dot higher for the purposes of all acquired banes, to a limit of ten. If she’s a Daeva, it’s two dots. This lasts until she suffers her next breaking point or a week passes, whichever comes first. In addition, while bearing a vampire’s sinful Vitae, a Chosen ghoul’s own blood is sufficient for sacrifices in Crúac rites higher than the first level. However, unless she can store enough Vitae to cover the rite’s entire cost, she must inflict herself with lethal damage for each Vitae in excess of her reserves, or share costs with other sin-bearing Children.
