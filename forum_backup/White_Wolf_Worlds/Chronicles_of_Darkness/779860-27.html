                    Okay then.

                    Can I also say we might want to rewrite the Fury Attribute rules, though? Maybe something like "a number of dots to be distributed among the Fury's Attributes, which all start at one, equal to/equal to two times the dots in Spite its Quagmire has"? Or maybe "The Fury gets extra points in all Attributes equal to the Spite dots of the Quagmire, divided by three (minimum 1)"? Otherwise, all but the most toxic Quagmires have Furies that are absurdly weak, and I don't think that's the intention.

                    Example Lesser Fury: The American Tikbalang

                    "Of course this is the right path. The question is whether you're following it-not my fault if you trip."

                    It's easy to forget, but Quagmires are clever. They come into this world inherently understanding exactly what hurt the planet so much they condensed out of its pain, and what was behind that pain. True, they don't really spend that much time wondering if their lashing will actually solve that pain (they are, when it comes down to it, wounded beasts-not known for nuance), but when they unleash their Flood, they genuinely wish to direct it at the source of their pain (or something tangentially related, if they are frustrated and are sick of existing as Quagmires). They at least have a good sense as to the motives as to how they formed, to better counter them in the first place. Generally, the kind of actions that scar the earth badly enough for a Quagmire to form over the damage was motivated by greed, a callous disregard for the world, or more likely both-but sometimes, it was made because the causes could see no other way. African poachers who need ivory money to feed their destitute villages, miners in toxic coal plants that have no other skills, and the desperate clawing of failing businesses that have lost all other recourse don't often have the malice that clinches the opening of a Floodgate, but sometimes, the world isn't that neat-especially if the causes' desperation was the aftereffect of human cruelty to them. The Furies produced by such a well-intentioned act of destruction inherently know this, leading to a being that, while it doesn't respect humans, understands they are not all evil threats to their territory. This makes them both less and more dangerous. Less, because they actually bother wondering if the humans nearby deserve their wrath and have a sense of primal morality that can be appeased. More, because their sympathy with humans also gives them a similar pack instinct-the calmer Furies recognize the value of having not just minions, but intelligent advisors and lieutenants, in pursuing their revenge. Perhaps it is actually the evolutionary design of the planet that calm Furies come into existence when the causes for their pain have, themselves, someone to blame-all Furies can truly offer is karma, but karma is a temptation few can match.

                    The Hawaiian Fury that calls itself the Tikbalang is an example of this. Its Quagmire formed in Halekala National Part in response to the introduction of a sprig of kudzu, that most invasive of vines, to the area thanks to a team of reckless drug runners not checking their cargo as well as they should have. Thankfully for the park, the Quagmire was able to control the plant's spread long enough that the park officials noticed and quickly burned out the kudzu before it became a real ecological danger. In the normal course of events, the Quagmire should have dissipated, were it not for the fact that the dealers weren't keeping to the profession willingly-rather, they had the misfortune of their female family members being targeted for trafficking by the Filipino branch of the Yakuza, the Japanese version of the mafia. However, one of the higher-ranking members, a charming sociopath by the name of Daiki, had been running behind significantly when it came to getting an excess of narcotics sold safely, so he decided to expand the market a little bit, start shipping to American buyers. Being a rather cheap sort, he decided to blackmail the girls' relatives into helping him-in effect, toxifying their innocence. This, combined with the fact that he had no real intention of fulfilling his end of the bargain (too useful), and the Yakuza's own business of helping with illegal dumping, was what caused the Quagmire to pool, and the unwilling smugglers' Filipino beliefs gave the Tikbalang-named after a guardian of sacred sites that works by tricking would-be intruders-its form. Seeing as how further catastrophe to the islands was narrowly averted by the Tikbalang's own quick thinking when a park ranger was passing by the defiled site, its new agenda is to punish the true defilers of both its ground and the sacred act of mating (Tikbalangs are heavily associated with weddings) with extreme prejudice-and to ensure a new invasive species doesn't take root.

                    Much like the horse-headed bogeymen that are its namesake, it is also very cunning in pursuing this; it's approached the smugglers in complete honesty about its motives and goals. Apparently, Daichi doesn't know what a forming mystery cult looks like-or vigilantes, for that matter.

                    Description: In its true form, the American Tikbalang looks mostly like its namesake-a long-limbed, naked man with the feet and head of an animal. It's not a true example of the myth though, and it shows-rather than a horse's head and hooves, it has the talons and face of a Hawaiian hawk, showing its more aggressive nature. More pertinently to Sirens, it is just as much a creature born of the woods as it is one that lives in them-what appears to be swarthy islander skin is, in fact, the flexible bark of tropical trees, and in place of a mythical tikbalang's three horns it has great green ferns, swaying in the wind.

                    It is a testament to the park service that the American Tikbalang lacks an element many other Furies take for granted when sculpting a Vessel-Pollution, namely. Maybe if it could find another example of an invasive species it would count, but frankly, the Tikbalang doesn't want to test the theory; it not only likes its mind, it feels dirty to go against the reason it was born in the first place. Instead, it mostly uses Air, Water, and Plant elements when making a new form to interact with its creators and cultists, favoring either versatility or endurance depending on its needs. It uses Flesh, occasionally, when the cultists bring it a hapless criminal to vent their fury on in its bloodiest ceremony, but that is currently a rare occasion-mostly because of lack of opportunity, not because the Tikbalang can't see a use for the talents of violent thugs. In all Vessels, it appears to be an old, long-bearded man of Filipino descent, body scarred from years of living off the land-but always having the three horn-leaves, shrunken and hidden, somewhere on its body, growing out of its sculpted skin.

                    Storytelling Hints: Some Sirens may wonder why, exactly, they need to get involved with the American Tikbalang and its unwilling criminals turned mystical vigilantes. It obviously likes, or at least tolerates, humans enough to work with them openly-it isn't even particularly invasive when regaining Pneuma, letting its cult kiss it when they don't need to speak for a while or tagging along on their missions of justice. Since the focus of its ire probably deserves to be made into fertilizer, what's the big problem? The problem is that, as with most Furies, the Tikbalang thinks like an animal, and animals see things in terms of species, and species don't have much variation-a man of Japanese descent that has committed any crime, no matter how minor, is obviously a Yakuza and one of Daiki's allies, worthy only of being sacrificed to the park. Its cultists are more capable of nuance and convincing it otherwise, but given how wise the Tikbalang comes off as (and it is genuinely perceptive, even without its Omen Trance Numen), they don't usually question it unless they see evidence otherwise. Beyond any sense of obligation to innocent jaywalkers, the Tikbalang isn't as good as hiding its crimes as it is as masterminding them-the Hawaiian PD is slowly starting to think they may have a racially motivated serial killer on their hands, and it won't be long until they figure out it has something to do with the schedule of a particular merchant ship. Any Siren that wishes to avoid police scrutiny into their domains is advised to at least talk down the Tikbalang.

                    Even nonviolent contact, however, is bound to be eerie and hazardous-the Tikbalang was partially formed from its makers' own moral conflicts, and it inflicts this doubt in others as a facet of its nature. Its Influence affects all forms of being lost, includingin a metaphysical, spiritual sense, allowing it to inflict crippling feelings of self-doubt and inadequacy on strangers, which combined with its other Numina lead to a pretty psychologically devastating assault. Worse, it does so even-especially-if it suspects the intruder on its territory is a potential friend, as it wants to see if the subject is capable of the same self-assessment and guilt as its creator-cult, and thus similar enough to count as a packmate. Those that fail, running away or collapsing into a gibbering pile of shattered self-image, it judges to be to different from its pack to be of any use-it doesn't kill these, and usually leads them out, but it will take some convincing before it ever lets anyone near the grove where it and the cult perform their rites to heal the Quagmire, and only if they submit to another test.

                    Still, the object of both the Fury and its accidental creators' ire is a genuinely evil man, one who leads some of the worst people in a very bad bunch. The American Tikbalang is a living reminder that people who are hazardous to the Earth are often dangers to their fellow man in their avarice as well-humans are as much part of the environment as the trees and the air, and by scarring one element, you affect the others, however indirectly. Thus, the Tikbalang can be something of a wild card in any story it figures in, being both an immoderate response to a real wrong and a dangerous outsider to the criminal underworld, one motivated not by money or even power, but the incorruptible desire to tear down everything a family has built and piss on the ruins with its former victims.

                    Virtue: Sympathetic

                    Vice: Merciless

                    Rank: 2 (the American Tikbalang was initially 3, but destroying the kudzu Appeased the Quagmire enough to lost a dot in Resentment)

                    Attributes: Power 1, Finesse 3, Resistance 2 (the Quagmire has a Spite rating of 3)

                    Influence: Being Lost 2, Woods 1

                    Manifestation: Twilight Form, Craft Vessel, Image

                    Max Pneuma: 15

                    Numina: Dement, Hallucination, Omen Trance, Pathfinder

Sadly making for Siren and STing both take from inspiration, but I promise that I will get back into making things for Siren. For now, I am working on the Sense Conclusion. What I have so far is:

                                                    Sense in general: Reading the Introduction target to see if they have the Opus within them, or scanning the Introduction area for the Opus targets

                                                    Sense 1: Reading the target to see if they contain the opus (think like reading chemical composition, corpse decay/forsenic science or the like)
                                                    Sense 2: Sensing the Opus like using a Radar within Sensory radius (Think of the Sense Life spell from Mage 1e, but make it so you can specify with the Opus)
                                                    Sense 3: Read specific parts within the target that the opus specifies. This can be up to being able to read another being's character sheet for example.
                                                    Sense 4: More intimate reading of objects or people. Being able to read into memories, past experiences or usages (somewhat like Trace, but more unlimited)
                                                    Sense 5: Being able to throw your senses elsewhere. Essentially able to do near astral projection perhaps. Dunno if this is a good idea or not for the power.

I've gone through and revised most of the Verses Document, mainly making wording better and specifying why Refraction (what protects Sirens from being seen for what they are when using Verses or Alterations in mundane form) occurs.                                                             Holy crap, I'm on a roll. I have some excellent news. I've finally finished off the Vaporian Alterations and I've began and finished the Discordian Alterations as well! I've been stumped for awhile on Vaporia, given that they're based on seas with strong currents and/or foggy vistas, but I couldn't find examples of those. Instead, I elected to make most of the Vaporian Alterations to be based off of the weirdest traits found in marine animals. I had the same problem with Discordia, but then I decided to make their Alterations based all around violence or viciousness. I hope this is enjoyed.

                                                            I've also managed to finish up the "Creating a Siren" section. It's abit of a minor achievement, but it's something. I've also made out Experience Costs table at the end, if anyone would like to suggest adjusting costs, just let me know.

                                                            I was also kind of hoping to ask for some feedback on an idea I had. Up until now, I felt that Siren was well off in terms of gameline and everything fitting together. But now I feel like the Mood should be changed. Right now, I don't feel the mood of "Uncertainty" fits it well enough. Yeah, life is bound to be turbulent, the same goes for everyone. But the point is, I don't feel like it would be that way for Sirens all the time. Hence why I feel a change is in order. And the change I have in mind is making the Mood about "Survival" or something like that. In short, I feel that Sirens would be better played as both intermediaries between Civilization and Nature and as the ultimate Survivalists.

                                                            The reason I feel the theme fits so much now is because a Siren's Drowning and subsequent Surfacing are born from the attempt to survive in spite of problems, the Song was born out of an attempt to pull back and survive. There's Echidna and her obsession with making the greatest survivalists who can live in the Celestial Ocean, the Celestial Ocean itself and Survivors being like a post apocalyptic wasteland, and finally hiding from Flensers and general human populace whenever Refraction occurs.

                                                            What do you all think?
