    Tiers Reconstruction Project

    02-10-2023, 12:01 PM
    One of the more significant innovations that Hunter brought to the CofD as a whole, imo, is the concept of Tiers- while it has evolved beyond its initial presentation in 1e Core, the division into Local/Regional/Global has became a good tool for Chronicle building, with further gamelines adding Tier 4, Cosmic, into the arsenal. Now, while the paradigm works well storywise, when it gets tied to mechanics it gets a bit... clunky. There are compacts people believe they should be conspiracies, conspiracies people think that should be compacts. Some people dislike global games yet want Endowments, some dislike Endowments but want to play globally. Some want to use Hunter's system for orgs and the Code and Tactics and so on, yet don't want to feel obliged to hunter down some or all monsters, while others claim that hunters are only those who actively hunt monsters, and that working with them/for them/seeking coexistence dilutes the themes of the game. Some people like a certain Endowment but dislike the group which use it, and some want to play a certain group but the Endowments of another one.

    Now, Hunter is famous for being very sandboxy- it is not that hard to hack down those aspects, and the game itself does so all the time. Compacts with Endowments? Check. Bygones allowing to have powers beyond your Tier and organisation? Check. Chapters presenting a option of a variant Conspiracy localised in space and time? Check. Presenting Hunter groups which have different manners to uphold the Vigil? Check. Giving concepts such as Cabal and Cults (and 1st Edition Cancer Cells) that show other sides of the Vigil, for better and worse? Check, check, check. I am pretty sure that given time and wordcount, 2e would have really went deep with those concepts, as they have been featured to a degree in the Core together with 2e, but as the current future of CofD in general is not very bright, it doesn't seem likely. Still, given that both editions have already given some nudges in those directions, and by taking up parts from other gamelines, it should be easy to justify any modification that fits what you want to do. Still, sometimes, people need the "feeling" of a system- as I learned in Debate Club, while things could look obvious, sometimes you actually need to build up some framing just to allow people feel that they have a structure. Sometimes, it is just enough to slap some names on abstract concepts and you suddenly things just become much easier to build around- and this is what we are going to do here. Giving paradigm to abstract concepts, reconstructing the Tiers in Hunter into something a bit more adjustable- or at least, try to and fail spectacularly.

    So- how is it going to work?

    Organisational Aspects
    In this system, each Organisation is being composed of different Aspects- which feature the properties of the group in terms of what they can do and how they operate. In this system, the range of influence that a organisation may have is merely one Aspect, which is detached from other Aspects and as such allows further customisation of the group. Each Aspect also has some influence on the "Hunter Template" which is granted to its members, in terms of things such as Endowments, the Code and Status Benefits. Generally, each organisation has one Aspect of each category, with certain exceptions which are to be detailed in their own sections. The Aspects are Tier, Radiance, Ladder, Banner and Beacon, which together describe what the organisation is and their role in the shadow world.

    Tier
    Tier represents what it always represented- how spread is the organisation in question. Organisations are mostly confined to their Tier's sphere of influence, yet individual cells may act outside of it. The Tier, however, represents support, connections, resources and the ability to both coordinate and plan within the scale it represents- as such, members found outside of the Tier's influence are in risk of losing access to Status benefits and lack the ability to call for backup when needed. Mechanically, the Tier represents the amount of benefits it could grant to its members- each organisation can provide 3+Tier number of Merit dot equivalent of benefits, spread across their Status: [Organisation] as they see fit, with one benefit at Status 1, another at 3 and a third in 5.

    The Tier Aspects are-

    Convocation- Tier 1, local presence, 4 Merit Dots

    Compact- Tier 2, regional presence, 5 Merit Dots

    Conspiracy- Tier 3, global presence, 6 Merit Dots

    Crown- Tier 4, cosmic presence, 7 Merit Dots

    Radiance
    If Tier is the range of control a organisation has, Radiance is how well it is able to utilise them. The Radiance of the organisation decided how well defined it is in terms of a singular hierarchy, and how freedom does it members has compared to the protection it may offer. Under Radiance rules, the hunter may invoke a Status within another Organisation which does not hold antagonsitic relationship towards theirs as if they were members in the organisation with a rating equal to half of their own Status: [Organisation] rating + Radiance rating. If the hunter themselves act antagonistically during the process, this Status may be revoked. Additionally, the same rating will be reduced from any attempts to cover your tracks from your own organisation.

    The Radiance Aspects are-

    Convention- Diffused within their Tier. -1 Rating.

    Council- Influential within their Tier. +0 Rating.

    College- Ubiquitous within their Tier. +1 Rating.

    Ladder
    Just like how hunters could be found across different levels of their own organisations, the organisations themselves are tied together with each other, and are often connected to one another through a network of favours, tributes and contacts. The Ladder Aspects represent how independent the organisation itself from other groups, and whether they have access to the supernatural benefits known as Endowments. Organisations found low in the Ladder are well scrutinised from such access by their parent organisations, while those who are close to the core have larger benefits. If their rating in the Ladder is higher than their Tier, the group can not access Endowments whatsoever- but it also means that they are independent from shady allies. Otherwise, use the associated rating as a modifier in the Endowments equation, which should be be balanced with drawbacks as usual.

    The Ladder Aspects are as follows

    Cell- lowest unite of a hunter organisation. 2 to the Endowment Equation

    Chapter- regional variation of a larger organisation. 1 to the Endowments Equation

    Chamber- independent organisation. +0 to the Endowments Equation

    Confederacy- alliance of independent organisations. -1 for the Endowments Equation.

    NOTE #1- the modifier as presented in this Aspect relates specifically for whether or not the organisation is "independent"- for example, a Cell which is a local variation of the Aegis Kai Doru, which hold their own traditions and ideology about Atlantis being a Ur Civilisation created by aliens and that have accidently stumbled into the larger conspiracy and were forced to become its "allies", will suffer from penalty as they are not fully submitted to the conspiracy. However, a cell composed of hunters initiated to the Guardians directly and which are part of the Aegis Kai Doru's own hierarchy will be considered as part of a Chamber, and as such will not suffer from Endowments penalty.

    NOTE #2- Confederacies, in that respect, refer to Factions as presented in the Contagion Chronicles, with the simple change that they are being composed solely from Organisations and treated as official alliances in that regard.

    Banner
    Banner represents the reason that the organisation exists- whether they uphold the Vigil in the name of government, religion or even merely profit. Organisations, after all, do not exist in vacuum, and most are tied, in one way or another, to a more mundane aspect of everyday society, something which allows them easier access to contacts and resources. The Banner of the organisation gives the hunters a certain theme for their Code, which describes the guidelines according to which they could change it.

    The Banner Aspects are as follows-

    Cabinet- the organisation is a governmental agency. Code- Patriotic. Nickname- agents

    Clan- the organisation is made from a bloodline or dynasty of members. Code- Ancestry. Nickname- scions

    Conclave- the organisation is a construct of a phenomena acting outside of the organisation's control. Code- Occult. Nickname- imbued

    Corporation- the organisation is a part of a certain business and hunts for profit. Code- Capitalistic. Nickname- contractors

    Creed- the organisation is a part of a religious tradition of structure. Code- Religious. Name- exorcists

    Consortium- the organisation is a scientific or academic group. Code- Scientific. Nickname- archivists

    Community- the organisation is simply made from people of a certain socio economic status, ethnicity, social movement or some other collective. Code- Ideological. Nickname- vigilantes

    Beacon
    Beacon represents the brand of Vigil that the organisation upholds. While most members seek to hunt down monsters and protect the world from the darkness, it is far from the only motivation for people to enter the darkness, and not everyone are strong enough to withstand the demanding pressure of the Vigil. As such, the Beacon actually describes what is the goal of the group. Unlike other aspects, organisations can have up to 2 Beacons, a Primary one and a Secondary one. For hunters, at least one of those Beacons is the Company Aspect. Each member of the organisation, however, has to choose one of the two Aspect, which defines their own Code.

    The Beacon Aspects Are-

    Company- those who fight the darkness in the benefit of humanity, known as hunters. According to their Code, Monsters are those who embrace the supernatural (rules as written)

    Cabaret- hired killers and mercenaries, known as assassins. According to their Code, those marked by their contract are deemed as Monsters.

    Congregation- followers of a powerful vision, known as seers. According to their Code, those who disrupt their future are deemed as Monsters.

    Cabal- dedicated for murder, known as slashers. According to their Code, those marked by their Undertaking are deemed as monsters.

    Consociation- viewing monstrosity as an ideal existence, known as espers. According to their Code, those who are loyal to their Organisation deem its rogue members as Monsters, while those who go rogue deem their own Organisation as Monsters.

    Coven- believing that monsters are equals, known as brokers. According to their Code, those who hunt monsters are deemed as Monsters.

    Craft- seeking to uncover occult mysteries, known as witches. According to their Code, those who destroy supernatural knowledge, artefacts or locations are deemed as Monsters.

    Cult- followers of a supernatural entity or inspired philosophy, known as cultists. According to their Code, those marked as sinners by their idol or faith system are deemed as Monsters.

    NOTE #3- Covens in that regard refer to monstrous organisations from other gamelines, such as Covenants, which are adapted to allow mortals to access their benefits. Consociations are, in essence, Deviant Conspiracies. Crafts fold the occult organisations of "human splats", such as Krewes and Orders, into them.

    Well, that's the first draft at least! Let me know what do you think! (I am a bit disappointed I didn't manage to shoehorn Tactic benefits, and I wondered a bit about adding Aspects dedicated for hunting specific monsters, but this the state as is)
