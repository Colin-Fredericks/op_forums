                                So, I just bought CtL 2e and I'm making my way through it. I have. . . questions. I'm coming back to WoD, or CoD now, after about 4 years of D&D where every ability/effect/consequence is explained with almost airtight clarity (I suppose I can't expect any less from the pseudo-lawyers who make MtG, lol). To be clear, I LOVE WoD. I love the setting and loved the sandbox/toolbox approach of NWoD in 2004, but after reading clear, defined rules in D&D for the last few years, I'm feeling frustrated at the ambiguity and, to be honest, ephemeral feel of a lot of CtL 2e's rules.

                                I came across this question from a week or so ago:

                                Originally posted by Jakondite View Post
                                So the game introduces a few mechanics in 2e related to an elementals 'elemental' affinity. In 1e, your elemental was based on kith - but in 2e, since Kith is not bound, do you just... pick an element that is tied to the elemental that doesn't contrast with the kith you've chosen?

                                What are the limits on the choices of elements? For instance, if your a Storm Elemental, is your element 'Wind, Water, and Lightning' or is it specific to storm clouds to work your seeming benefit? Can you manipulate all versions of your element in question (for instance, a water elemental manipulating ice and vice versa.)

                                Following up on that question, there are three contracts whose elemental seeming bonus allows an individual to incorporate 'their elemental affinity'. If your not an elemental, do you just pick an element when you learn that seeming bonus?
                                This is exactly the kind of stuff I'm talking about. Is it left ambiguous intentionally? If it's up to the ST to make the final arbitration, I feel like that's a lot of undue burden added to the STs responsibilities.

                                Some more things I'm struggling with:

                                - The Seeming ability for Elementals is described as "As long as your character touches or is surrounded by his element, he may use it to take mundane actions at a distance of up to three yards (meters) away; these actions use his usual traits. This includes unarmed attacks, but not attacks with weapons." What exactly constitutes as a mundane action? Lock picking? Building a table? This ability is all well and good if I'm made of wood or water, but how am I touching lightning? I guess I could be carrying a zippo and use it to touch fire? As the quote above states: what defines my elemental affinity?

                                - The Darkling Seeming ability goes like this "If you spend a point of Willpower, your character may touch something insubstantial and become part of it for three consecutive turns, transubstantiating into smoke, shadow, a sunbeam — whatever’s handy." What can I do for those nine seconds? Can I attack? Can I speak? Can I move?

                                - I'd like to see a clear example of touchstones and clarity damage being used. I think I understand the basics of touchstones, but I'm having trouble visualizing the "write your touchstone beside *composure + 1* box on the clarity track" when the clarity track is like the healthbar, but the touchstone track is beneath it, not beside it. -_- Also, for clarity damage, it keeps referencing "down to clarity 1, 2 or 3 from the right", but the track on the character sheet has a blank on the first box, then it starts 1, 2, 3 from the LEFTmost box. I think if I had to really do it, I guess I could make it work, but it just sucks that they couln't have an actual example in the book.

                                Anyways, that's what I've got for now, I just feel that with so many abilities left up to interpretation, it could cause lots of rule talk/arguments at the table, taking away from the core mood and gameplay. I love CoD, and want to make CtL 2e work, Maybe I'm trying to define things that are meant to be ambiguous for imagination or creativity's sake?

There's no explicit mention of the Navigation mechanics in Pathfinder's description, just the implication of known direction and distance.                                         My table talked it over and we thought that being immune to the Lost condition when navigating to the target declared by the Pathfinder contract was fair, but I wanted to see what everyone else thought, and if we were missing something in the text.

                                                Originally posted by MartManTZT View Post
                                                - The Seeming ability for Elementals is described as "As long as your character touches or is surrounded by his element, he may use it to take mundane actions at a distance of up to three yards (meters) away; these actions use his usual traits. This includes unarmed attacks, but not attacks with weapons." What exactly constitutes as a mundane action?
                                                An action that is not the activation of a supernatural power, generally speaking.

                                                - The Darkling Seeming ability goes like this "If you spend a point of Willpower, your character may touch something insubstantial and become part of it for three consecutive turns, transubstantiating into smoke, shadow, a sunbeam — whatever’s handy." What can I do for those nine seconds? Can I attack? Can I speak? Can I move?
                                                You can definitely move.

                                                - I'd like to see a clear example of touchstones and clarity damage being used. I think I understand the basics of touchstones, but I'm having trouble visualizing the "write your touchstone beside *composure + 1* box on the clarity track" when the clarity track is like the healthbar, but the touchstone track is beneath it, not beside it. -_- Also, for clarity damage, it keeps referencing "down to clarity 1, 2 or 3 from the right", but the track on the character sheet has a blank on the first box, then it starts 1, 2, 3 from the LEFTmost box.
                                                Both of these problems are stemming from something you seem to have overlooked: those numbers underneath the Clarity track appear to be for marking where Touchstones are attached; normal characters are not going to have more than six Touchstones, but it's well within the realm of possibility for a character to have Composure 5 and five dots in the Touchstone Merit

                                                Presumably you record the Touchstones in the appropriately numbered lines for their attached ratings' boxes, and then the first box in excess of your Wits + Composure + Icons total gets marked in such a way to indicate that the preceding boxes constitute your Max Clarity (which matches the number underneath the leftmost unusable box, numerically).
