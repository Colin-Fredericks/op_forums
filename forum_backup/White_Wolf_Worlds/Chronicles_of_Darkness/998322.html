    Chronicles of Darkness not as horror

    11-04-2016, 02:11 PM
    I do not play Chronicles of Darkness because they are horror games. Horror scares me, and I don't like that. I play them because you can play a vampire or a mage or a hunter, and that makes me feel empowered. I play CoD for the angst (I love angst), for the romance, for the intrigue and scheming, but NOT for the horror. Am I alone in this?

    My name is Colleen. My favorite song is "Wildwood Flower." My ambition is to write the complete history of the White Wolf/Onyx Path universe.

      It's not really the centerpoint of my games but it is definitely there. I think that you lose something if you focus overabundantly on a single aspect. You need angst, romance, intrigue, scheming but also horror, tragedy and even things like hope and (for me at least) comedy.

      All in all as a storyteller you have to make your game entertaining, use whatever means at your disposition to make that happen.

      But i think there is a perception of conflict when the game is presented as a horror game. Horror is there most assuredly but i dont think its meant to be the only flavor of the Chronicles of Darkness. All the same becoming a vampire is not meant to be considered a purely positive outcome either.

      Hope i have communicated my thoughts clearly. I tend to ramble and have a difficulty translating thoughts to words.

          CofD is a game system with many splats, each of which are adaptable enough to wear many masks. Horror is just one flavour of many.

          Me, personally, I like horror, but it is very much a sometimes food. If a game is an unrelenting march of horror, people break under it. That can be fun, now and then, as catharsis, but do it all the time and it gets dull.

          I've had one Mage game include stories of horror and betrayal - with generational sins, questions of temptation to use awful means to achieve terrible ends, and cultish altars slicked with gore and tears - but this game has also had stories of love, heroism, and moments of downright childish wonder (the most recent being a father sitting with his daughter and teaching her with breathless awe the names of the real, living, breathing dinosaurs wandering by in front of them).

          Make your stories what's fun for you and your friends. If you look hard enough, you'll find people just as in love with your style of stories as you.

                          Hm. Well, I tend to come at them as horror games first and foremost, but horror is an especially hard genre to keep balanced in long form narrative, so it's typically not the only genre element in the game.How much of a role it plays comes down to the style of my players and the type of setting we're working with, but I think horror is too baked into the mechanics to excise it completely without hurting the game.

                          So my Hunter the game has a tone of action-comedy modeled off 80s pop culture pastiches, but that makes it easy to blindside my characters with those rare and raw moments where they realize the horror of what they're up against. My take on Requiem is largely political, but it grinds the characters down into a routine of increasingly morally dubious actions until they take a step back and see what their characters have become. And my Changeling... Actually Changeling is just horrifying all around.
