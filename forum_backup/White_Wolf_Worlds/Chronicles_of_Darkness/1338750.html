    Incoporating 'Tourism' As A Theme In VTR

    10-01-2019, 02:54 AM
    Oh, well, hello there, dear reader.

    Tis I! A nerd... Looking for help with his plot.

    Sigh.

    Look, the gist is this; when it comes to designing a Chronicle, I tend to let my 'senses' dictate where, when, and what the plot of my game is going to be, even if I don't fully grasp the details. And so, yeah, I know that I really want to use 'tourism,' as a theme with the VTR game I have in the works. I'm not a complete noob; I can see how the comparisons can be easily made. But in terms of how it can relate to the plot, well, here are a few of my notes:

    -Setting = Amman, Jordan, Spring 2002. Centered around a fictional 'The Midnight Sand Cafe' (named in part after something of a source of inspiration, The Sand Cafe by Neil MacFarquhar)
    -Player Guidelines = All of the PCs, newly turned, will all be tourists (doesn't matter from where)
    -The Hook = The Prince of Elysium (whose background is somewhat up in the air) has declared that tourists cannot be fed upon; ostensibly, because he wants tourism to flourish in Amman itself. And yet, when the responsible sires are brought forth to testify, none of them can remember performing the embrace. This will lead to the discovery that, across all of Amman, tourists are being brutally murdered/disappearing by someone, or something. Eventually, it will be up to the PCs to solve the mystery, while trying to survive being a kindred.
    -The Hitch = The Prince will forbid them, under any circumstance, of attempting to contact their family, not until they have at least accustomed to being kindred.
    -Sources of Inspiration = The Grand Budapest Hotel; The Talented Mr. Ripley; various 'vacation con man' stories

    What I need help with:

    Two things, essentially.

    1.) The villain. I know this should be, like, the number one thing, but I just don't know what to do. Originally, I was really looking into using Carl Hagar, the dhampir, from NH:WD. But as the pieces come more into place, it almost seems like the story doesn't need him, or else I just idk. Or, maybe the threat is from within; maybe the Prince's reasons for the tourist restriction isn't as clear cut. What do you think?

    2.) Yeah, just, a bit more enlightenment and back and forth about how to use tourism as a theme for horror.

    What dost thou thinketh??

      Hey there! This is a really cool concept for a Chronicle. I hope this helps:

      The first thing that comes to mind for the villain is the Prince himself, using the Embrace of these tourists as a false flag against unwanted elements in Amman. With tourists being forbidden prey, someone needs to be punished. He could, in effect, be guiding their investigation to support a narrative that paints his enemies or rivals as the responsible parties attempting to sabotage his praxis or embracing to stage a coup. Whether or not the players pick up on this is up to them.

      As for tourism as a theme, I think you have a great setup. As fledglings stuck in a country far from home, they have pretty much nothing in the way of Kindred accepted currency to gather resources and assistance. Except for themselves. Through the process of performing favors for the local Kindred, they can get a first (or second) hand account of the secret history of Amman as a natural extension of their duties. With that comes its own horror, not to mention anything they accidentally happen upon.

          So, if the Prince is the villain, that leads to two more points.

          First, if the neonate's sires don't remember embracing the PCs, then they must have been mind wiped, or something to that effect. He would thus remember 'the good ol' days,' before eventually relocating to Amman during the 70s, being Embraced sometime then.               On the first point: Mind control is certainly an option. The other being that their sires are simply lying. The coterie are so young and inexperienced that all their elders would have to do is waggle their fingers and go, "Ooo! It's scary Kindred Brain Magic!" and they wouldn't know the difference. That leaves open a few more avenues of investigation for the players. Perhaps an overworked Ghoul didn't shred the right documents, the players catch a few sentences from one of their sires in conference with the Prince complaining about promises made to them, Kindred aligned against the Prince put an earworm in their heads suggesting that all is not as it seems.

              As for the second: The patsy (or patsies, as the case may be), would likely be a combination of personal and pragmatic factors. One I came up with are remnants of the Iblic Creed Sanctified who rose to power during the Soviet invasion but have since fallen to the current, more secular Prince. Pushed to the fringes, given voice but no power, they may be agitators and inhibitors of the Prince's vision of a return to the "good ol' days." Perhaps the only reason he hasn't stamped them out of existence is because they have some sort of leverage on him? Did he inform to their inquisitors during the height of their regime, betraying his brethren to ensure his own survival?

              Perhaps it's not the Iblic Creed Sanctified who are the real problem, simply an obstruction. Perhaps there is another Kindred, from the Prince's yesteryear coterie, nursing a vendetta and growing movement against him. Perhaps his reasons are valid. The players could choose who to back.

                          Tourism offers a lot to play with.


                          The Colonial Route
                          First off, a lot of places that make income through tourism present a false facade*. As a tourist, you get a cleaned up version of the city, that sweeps any poverty or social ills under a rug -- rent prices go up, a lot of people get evicted, public spaces start disappearing, etc. Tourism, as an industry, then employs those residents, has them do the low-level labor, and pays poorly. So right there you've got a system that profits by displacing and impovrishing locals. This isn't necessarily always the case, but it is much of the time. It's a good analog to vampirism -- my material wellbeing is dependant on factors that likely harm your material well being.**


                          The Hidden City Route

                          Then, there is the fact that tourism takes a city, cleans it up and makes it juuuuuuuuussssttt exotic enough to be novel, but not so different that it's unfamiliar and threatening to tourists. Think of Disney World. There are the streets and paths that the visitor sees, and then there is a system of tunnels and hallways that the staff use to prop up the facade. And that facade *is* the product that you are being sold. You do not want to see what it costs to make this place pretty.


                          The Outsider route

                          Tourists are not residents. As a tourist, you get to see very comfortable, pretty, surface-level version of society. You don't get to be home. You're alone in a crowd. Since they can't leave the city, the surface-level for-show city will start getting boring. But your PCs are outsiders. They can sample what the city has to offer, and they can pretend that they fit in. But they don't. A pretty good metaphor for being a vampire. Your relation to your masqurade is similar to the tourist-city relationship.


                          Some bottled conflict

                          There's the fact that tourists are great to feed on. They're unattached, get lost, wander the city like idiots, drink too much, wind up dead in a river. Nobody notices if one goes missing. No community to come together and start up a neighborhood watch... unless the victim was important. Then you have an international incident on your hands -- which is a pretty good inciting incident for some conflict.


                          I think all three are compatible, but the Colonial Route might be the best for that gnawing horror that something is. not. right.


                          *I misspelled the word as 'fasad' I and looked it up. Turns out 'fasad is an Arabic word for rotteness which seems relevant.
                          ** I think the corebook mentions something about vampires gentrifying your neighborhood.
