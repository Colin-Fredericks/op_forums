            A fair question. To me it's a matter of principal. That character clearly believes it's ok to expose mortals, so you should modify the code and alienate other Hunters. Anything less would be exploiting a loophole in the rules to avoid the logical consequences of his beliefs.
            "Most hunters are more worried about the practical impact of exposing people to the supernatural in direct pursuit of the Vigil rather than its impact on their own level of comfort with what they do" isn't exploiting a loophole any more than a Merciless hunter who's made his peace with murder is. Experience is going to make the average hunter recognize that other hunters generally knew what they were getting into when they resolved to keep the Vigil long-term, that they're often better-equipped to deal with monsters than average mortals, and that while it stings your heart a little to admit it, sometimes the monsters win in a straight fight and you have to prioritize survival over common cause. Those that are still genuinely deeply bothered by it can easily pick a tenor and target for their obsession or shut down until someone spots their inner conflict, but Vigilant hunters just try to avoid running into those decisions too often out of practical necessity.

            There's more than one way to blatantly expose people to the truth and it seems entirely suitable for membership in the Compact focused around recording and circulating media capturing the supernatural to have to still make you pick your priority if you're going to leverage it to maintain High Integrity instead of hanging out at the level where your instinctive paranoia kicks in at signs of supernatural danger.

            "I think the Government is in on it and so is Big Pharma, so there's no need to step in and help the institutionally-backed conspiracy goons when a werewolf's got their back to a wall" does not strike me as a reason to shift your Code compared to "I have cameras everywhere but stopping people from getting hurt isn't as important as gathering evidence," nor does a blasé attitude toward indirectly traumatizing mortals seem like something likely to arise out of institutional devotion — media capture of occult phenomena has a couple inherent buffers against being an outright and immediate breaking point trigger next to, say, bringing Roommate Steve along on your next recording session.

                    Alright, for starter, I would like to thank you all for actively taking part in that exercise. The result is most interesting, and we already managed 6 groups out of 12. I am impressed.

                    However, we have two alternative opinions of the Ascending Ones, so a tie-breaking might be needed. Here's my own opinion on the points where King Raven and Sunless Nick disagree:

                    * Causing a person to suffer a breaking point from exposure to the supernatural: I'd say no. As far as I know, nothing in their code or modus operanti contradicts this.
                    * Refusing aid to a fellow hunter in need: Maybe yes.
                    *Learning that a monster harmed a person when you could have done something to stop it but didn’t: No. True, the diplomacy can lead to this, but the point of the negociation still is to ensure as little people as possible get harmed.
                    * Putting the well-being or autonomy of a monster over that of a person: ... Not sure. Maybe yes, if they consider some humans deserve it. But I admit it's stretching it.
                    * Gaining or bestowing power from an obviously monstrous source: No. Whatever the Elixirs are, they aren't obviously monstrous in source, at least in my opinion. Some Elixirs might be, such as that one that turns you into a vampire for a short period of time in Nightstalker, but I don't think some Elixirs triggering Breaking points is enough justification to use your freebie.
                    * Betraying a fellow hunter: maybe yes.

                    Hope that helps^^

                    Now for Satchel's question:

                    Salient question for the exercise: How valuable are the High Integrity breaking points to this discussion considering their tacit irrelevance to the vast majority of active hunters doing active hunter things?

                    Like, seriously, I wouldn't play a NetZero Army of Truth hardliner and blow any number of freebies on opting out of High Integrity breaking points that will go away the moment I go Vigilant when the cost is alienating even non-VALKYRIE hunters and picking up a Low Integrity breaking point in the process. I'd have better luck trading out betrayal for loyalty to the message or the compact for that kind of cost.
                    Fair enough. I still think they should count to some extent though, as some players might want to keep a high Integrity. How about we give half-value to every point from 10 to 7?

                            That putting up a video of supernatural activity is not going to be enough by itself to warrant a breaking point in a casual viewer, that trading out a breaking point specifically has to happen in the direct pursuit of the Vigil and not as a thing you do after a hunt, and that the Network Zero portion of the trade-out section is probably not talking about the group's de facto core practice when it says they tacitly encourage blatant exposure.

                            Also that the Army of Truth and other militant/extreme branches of hunter organizations are unlikely to maintain High Integrity during an extended stretch of hunting, and that the most militant faction of the rip-open-the-veil compact is probably going to have more egregious violations of the Code to inure themselves to like getting the hidden monster to reveal its killing form in front of a live audience and failing to prevent all casualties rather than the unexpected-failure-of-planning catch that mere exposure is there to tag onto.

                            Unchallenged high Integrity isn't generally for mortal characters who are deeply committed to hardline supernatural-proximate lifestyles, particularly when the threshold for middling Integrity is one point below the starting character average. You're better off playing into the scale's default support structure if you're worried about losing a single point of Integrity from narratively-infrequent breaking points with no inherent penalties attached to them.

                            It seems fairly clear that membership in a higher-tier group is supposed to help keep you from falling below Middling range rather than to hold you above it — these are groups that have committed to the cause, after all, and that implies a certain base level of Vigilance.
