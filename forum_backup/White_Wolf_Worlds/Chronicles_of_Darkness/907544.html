    WHW's Setting Hacks and Houserules; 2nd Edition

    06-05-2016, 05:47 AM
    Basically, a place for me to drop my homerules and setting changes, so people can tell me implications I've overlooked or tell me why it's a bad idea.

    DISSONANCE CHANGE: RATIONALIZATION IS MAGIC
    Dissonance doesn't obscure supernatural. It obscures Supernal Magic, and Supernal Magic only. Rationalization will follow the path of least resistance, depending on each victim individually - this means that for a devout Catholic, Magic might look like Miracles; for UFO geek, like Alien Tech; and so on.
    Why? Because it makes covering your track as a Mage harder, allows for fucked up cults where Mage pretends to be a god figure while casting in front of "his" Sleepers, and makes Hunters really dangerous. It also allows for Sleeper pawns that won't forget their orders or role, as long as they are properly indoctrinated; a Sleeper Servant will really believe that you are his Lord and Master, and will aid you in your Heavenly War of Gods (read, Consilium business), he will just see everything through different lenses.
    Yes, Mages are dicks and their servants are really low on Integrity. It's intended!

    GNOSIS DISTRIBUTION: GNOSTIC REVELATION IS HARD
    Most common Gnosis rating in the game is 1 and 2. For most of characters, Gnosis is permanently locked at 3. Gnosis of 4 is extremely rare, and you probably could gather all Gnosis 5 Mages in the world in one room. Gnosis of 6 and more is Archmage territory. No character may purchase Gnosis above 3 without unraveling unique mystery (read: you need a plot milestone and GM approval).
    Why? Because we like smaller scale and lower power levels. It also creates interesting consequence for Legacy Teaching, forcing Mages to rely on Soul Stone and Daimonicon methods much more than on simply ("simply") grabbing a tutor.

    CRAFT YOUR OWN LEGACY!
    As long as you do enough research, creating your own Legacy, or creating novel Attainments for existing one is equal XP wise to following orthodox path. You may freely modify Primary and Optional Arcanum of your Legacy, as long as it makes sense.
    Why? We aren't very attached to idea of Mages gathering around Legacies, and prefer image of solitary Mages doing their own personal research much more. Individualism, ho! Pre-existing Legacies are still a strong pick, due to their social benefits. Charging less XP for unorthodox Attainments means more variety between Legacy Members, allowing them to be dramatically different in their approach, even if they share the ideology. This also means that it isn't that uncommon for Cabals with strong Cabal Symbolism to develop "Cabal Legacies".

    HORIZONTAL GROWTH: ATTAINMENTS
    Because Mages will be stuck at low Gnosis, they need an alternative way to continue pursuing magical research. This is done by allowing developing extra Utility Attainments for their Arcana. Difference between a Gnosis 1 Apprentice with Death 3 and Gnosis 3 "Master" with Death 3 is going to be the fact that "Master" developed fuckaton of Attainments to personalize his magical style.
    This also helps me with the problem of "All mages with Arcanum X can cast all spells, how do I give them limitations on what they can do?".
    Why? Because we love horizontal growth.

    CONDITIONS ARE MAGIC
    All Spells are Conditions and Tilts. Period. Psychic Domination? "Psychic Domination" Condition. Frogification? "Frogification" Condition. And so on. This means they can sometimes be ended early, and you *can* Pattern Bolster them away.
    Why? Because it makes stuff like Psychic Domination less insane against Mages.

    PATTERN BOLSTERING BOLSTERED
    Pattern bolstering removes any Condition or any Tilt. However, Environmental Tilts and Persistent Conditions will reapply next turn. Pattern Bolstering takes effect immediately, even if you can't spend all the needed Mana, but you are *forced* to use following turns to pay for it.
    Why? Because solving all your problems with Pattern Bolstering isn't as fun as it sounds, but solving some obnoxious one is good. Giving you Pattern Bolstering immediately helps with stupid annoying instant kill tactics and rocket tag problems that are rather relevant at low Gnosis.

    SOUL STONES FOR EVERYONE
    Cool shit happens when Soulstones are stolen. In order for them to be stolen, people need to actually *use them* and *carry them*. Each character starts with one Soulstone for free. This is often an item that soaked all the superduper energy from their Awakening. Additionally, using Soulstone always considers you to be "a Mage of equal Gnosis".
    Why? Because we want each character to have a signature item of power, and we want people to use them.

    ENVIRONMENTAL YANTRA? WHAT'S THAT?
    Environmental Yantras don't exist, because literally no one used them. Instead, symbolically powerful Environment can buff your persona Yantra by 1. This cannot be combined with other ways to buff your Persona.
    Why? Because with 2 Yantra Slots, people basically did this: Persona for 3, High Speech or Soulstone for 2, ignore all +1 Yantras forever.

    EXPERIMENTAL: LEGACY YANTRAS? WELL...
    Legacy Yantras that are are appropriate for your Persona can be used as your Persona Yantra, with +1 bonus on top. This may not be combined with other ways to buff Persona. You can Legacy Yantra normally, too.
    Why? Same reason Environmental Yantras needed to be recalibrated. Also, because most characters will have only 2 or 3 Yantra slots available, all of the small +1 Yantras simply *won't be used*, as everyone and their dog is going to grab Persona, High Speech and some +2 Yantra, ignoring +1 Tools forever.

    EXPERIMENTAL: PATH OR ORDER TOOLS? WELL...
    Using a Tool can buff your Persona bonus by 1. Can't be combined with other ways to buff Persona. You can still use tools as standalone thing if you have leftover slots.
    Why? As above.

    TALKING ABOUT PERSONAS...
    1. Renamed from "Shadow Name" to "Persona"
    2. All Mages start with 1 dot in it for free.

    APOSTATE AND NAMELESS EQUALITY
    Characters without Order training start without Rotes, 1 Status Merit, Order Tools and so on, and get XP refund for it that can be only spent on Merits. Characters in Orders can do this to show their incomplete training, and they can also customize 1 order rote skill.

    More will might come later, will get edited in + posted as a standalone post. Feel free to comment.

                                  My problem with current Pattern Bolstering is that it's too slow in turn-by-turn situations unless you achieved Gnosis 3, and it's pretty easy to inflict scene-deciding tilt or condition. I don't like Rocket Tag (whoever wins initiative gets to decide outcome of the scene by virtue of taking first action) and Counterspelling is very impotent, so Bolstering upfront and putting you into Mana Debt is a good compromise. And of course, Mana Debt ("get healed now, spend next X turns paying for it") is great, because it makes Mage hemmorage Mana like crazy. Starting Mage will get dry after purging 3 tilts, which is...very quickly.
                                  Getting into Mana Debt also means you can't pay Mana to reduce Paradox, so inflicting Tilts on your Mage opponent still has tactical value - you might not take them out in the single turn, but you are doing damage and putting them in a bad spot.
                                  I ignored the Physical Tilt/Mental Condition rule because it creates weird situations where Tilts turn into Conditions out of combat and vice versa.

                                  Persona being powerful is a good and bad thing for us, because we want to encourage Persona more than we want to encourage other Tools, mostly because Persona is self-limiting. It creates patterns, and patterns create vulnerabilities, and allow playing around them. Magic in Mage is very broad; by making your most powerful Yantra one that forces you to follow certain restrictions, you are willingly limiting what kind of Magic you can work with efficiently. Fire focused Mage won't be as effective at Wind Magic, for example, and thus is probably going to stick to Fire based soluitions, which in turn makes it possible to play around that and expect him to use fire spells.

                                  Well, half of the starting Cabal is Nameless and they decided to do that before we did that homerule,so yeah :P. In general, we don't perceive Pentacle as designated Protagonists, and they often end up being straight out antagonists.

                                  Here are some interesting setting consequences arising from the Gnosis Cap:

                                  1.Magic at level 4 becomes incredibly rare and precious. What kind of magic it is?
                                  - HEALING MAGIC. Lethal and Aggravated Damage is hard to heal without easy access to 4th dot of Magic! Mages thus have to rely on Medical Care, Accelerated Healing Times, and Pattern Bolstering to deal with lethal wounds, unless they can find a Mage willing to use their healing spells on them.

                                  - Teleportation. Simply Teleporting in and out is harder. This makes Orders less global and makes travel plots actually viable.

                                  - Removing Curses and Vile Destinies that are not results of Supernal Magic (because those you can, you know, just Dispel) becomes super hard, because you both have to find an Adept of Fate, AND perform spell-dictated quest.

                                  - Oaths are much more binding. While it's still easy to dispel them, actually modyfing them is really, really hard now.

                                  - Imbued Items become much more valuable, simply because there is a lot less Mages capable of creating them. Same thing about Fetishes.

                                  - And so on...

                                  2. Because Adept Magic is much harder to get your hands on, it increases value of alternate means of getting these effects done. Artifacts that grant their users access to Adept level Magic are incredibly potent and desirable; some Consilia are going to rely on Artifacts to provide some of their services or estabilish infrastructure, and these thingss getting destroyed or stolen might...cause...problems

                                  3. This should get it's own entry later, but basically, Quintessences. Sometimes, Mages really really really want to cast that 4/5 dot spell once. And...it's possible. Kinda. Sort of. By solving a special Mystery, Mages may try to arrange special circumstances that will allow one shot casting of a spell beyond their understanding. Obviously, each such solution is highly unique and basically a plot point that might take days, months or years to put in motion; they always require great deal of sacrifice and work. They don't have a general mechanic, because each case is unique.

                                      After having some fun with the idea of multiple Attainmens, I really like how it's working. Relatively newbie Mages feel like humans with magical powers, but the more experienced ones, ones who invested in the Attainments, start to really drift away from their human self. I think it really helps hammer the idea that Mages can become disqualified from being humans by their own choice; Unleashing Spells didn't really work here (too easy to Dispel, too easy to make these changes inherent parts of the character); Attainments that are, for lack of better comparison, kind of like Ordo Dracul's Coils and Scales push the angle of "look what you can become" really well without being too pushy or overpresent. I also like how they tend to make you grow horizontally (allowing them to do more stuff and or in different ways), instead of vertically (making all your stuff more powerful), so even an "Elder" Mage with a whole gauntlet of Attainments doesn't feel oppressively overpowered in a way that Adept and Master Arcana wielding Mages do.

                                      EDIT
                                      Also, because a lot of them require Mana upkeep (even if it's something like 1 mana per day), it ramps up and makes more inhuman mages more and more reliant on stable sources of Mana, which in turn makes Mana even more valuable; it actually gives them ways and reasons to constantly be at Mana deficit, never stabilizing at a "comfortable living standard", because you can be so much more.
