    What's up with the Ulgan?

    08-12-2016, 01:33 AM
    Maybe this is coming from the fact that I didn't read Promethean 1e, but I am very confused about the Ulgans' relationship with ephemeral entities. It's clear that they are deeply connected to Twilight, but it seems extremely inconsistent whether this connection is meant to be with ephemeral entities in general, or spirits specifically, or spirits and ghosts but not other EEs, or what.

    A great deal of the text concerning Ulgan incorrectly treats Twilight like a place, which I've made note of in the errata thread, but moreover, it seems like in some places they're specifically connected with spirits, as in the animistic entities native to the shadow, while other times it seems more like it's using "spirit" as a catch-all term for any sort of EE. To confuse matters further, their humour is called ectoplasm, which in Mage and Geist is something specifically connected to ghosts, but the Ulgan generally seem to be more associated with spirits than with ghosts. For example, the Extempore writeup recommends a focus on ghosts over spirits as a way to differentiate Extempore with an excess of ectoplasmic humor from Ulgan, saying; "Extempore tied to ectoplasm might be more closely died to the ghosts of the dead than the spirits of the natural world, with an accompanying difference in character." But then the section on Ulgan Bestowments is completely focused on ghosts with no mention of spirits, saying, "Whereas Osirans tease at the line between the living and the dead, Ulgan revel in the macabre. They’re death warmed over and maintain strong ties to the Underworld. Ghosts, the hungry dead, shades: these are all kin to the Ulgan."

    Can anyone help shed some light on this for me? What exactly is the Ulgans' relationship with the ephemeral supposed to be? Are they Spirit Masters? Ghost Whisperers? Ephemeral Entity Generalists? Does their affinity for Twilight entities extend to Angels, or Goetia? What the heck is their deal and why do they seem so inconsistent?

      The Ulgan in 1e were specifically aimed at dealing with spirits, and were generally treated as sacrifices-turned-shamans who found themselves harried by spirits at every turn. I didn't really care for them because using spirits to learn about humanity seemed a little bit silly, and also because I was bitter that they broke from the four traditional humours. One of the later books suggested the idea of 'Orphean' variant Ulgan who deal with ghosts rather than spirits, That still didn't really scratch the itch for me because the Osirans were already our 'deal with the dead' Lineage.

      So the Ulgan are a little weird for me, and it seems like 2e has only muddled things a little more. I don't really have a good answer for you, other than that I might love to see a constellation of ephemeral-focused Lineages; one each for spirits, ghosts, and anything else out there.

                  Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                  Maybe this is coming from the fact that I didn't read Promethean 1e, but I am very confused about the Ulgans' relationship with ephemeral entities.                   Something to keep in mind. Remember, this game came out when the only ephemeral entities were ghosts and spirits, plus the brand new qashmal. Nothing like the strix, no angels, and while goetic demons existed, they were considered to be a form of spirit. There were astral projections, but that's rare enough to be negligable. And the qashmal, well, they're pretty rare and show up and leave as plot points, not really long term NPCs. So, 1e formed around using the only two entites that were available at the time, and that carried forwards.

                  Also, I'd like to point out that, at the time of Promethean, there was kind of a trend from White Wolf books to use the Hisil for anything related to spirits in general, not just animistic spirits. Vampire summoners? Hisil, go! Ephemeral Prometheans? Use the Shadow! Mage summoners all used the Shadow, even when they wanted an abrahamic themed angel as well! A lot of books at this time had that kind of feeling to them. The nWoD was still heavily tied to the cWoD cosmological mindset and hadn't quite broken away yet to make its own thing. In fact, Promethean was probably the very first step, and continued with Changeling, which moved towards the "everyone with their own playground" model instead.

                  So, a good chunk of the Ulgan are based around the trend of fifth element = spirit = Hisil that was coming off the current mindset of the game. And it kind of just got ingrained. And the focus on spirits over ghosts was naturally the result of the Hisil being far more developed than anything related to ghosts. Despite being in the WoD core, ghosts were kind of ignored for a long time in 1e and had little material.

                  A great deal of the text concerning Ulgan incorrectly treats Twilight like a place, which I've made note of in the errata thread, but moreover, it seems like in some places they're specifically connected with spirits, as in the animistic entities native to the shadow, while other times it seems more like it's using "spirit" as a catch-all term for any sort of EE.
                  To be fair, Mage does the same thing. Especially when you create portals into it. Treating Twilight as a pseudo-realm is actually pretty common.

                  And, yeah, using "spirit" as a general term for ephemeral entities is kind of common too. Its hard sometimes, because the overwhelming majority of writers we have for the game aren't technical writers, and to be frank, the CofD games deliberately don't nail down definitions and key words in a lot of cases.

                  To confuse matters further, their humour is called ectoplasm, which in Mage and Geist is something specifically connected to ghosts, but the Ulgan generally seem to be more associated with spirits than with ghosts.
                  Geist wasn't even a proposal yet, and Mage 1e's use of ectoplasm actually supports calling the Ulgan element ectoplasm. At this point, ectoplasm was treated as "unliving" ephemera. You used it to shape ephemeral tools and houses and the like. Literally the building blocks of spiritual objects. The Elemental Tamer of the Cave Legacy also used ectoplasm as their element.

                  the Extempore writeup
                  Extempore are basically just a universal variation of the core five Lineages under the umbrella of being the "super customizable Lineage." I wouldn't put too much faith in this as something to compare against.

                  Can anyone help shed some light on this for me? What exactly is the Ulgans' relationship with the ephemeral supposed to be? Are they Spirit Masters? Ghost Whisperers? Ephemeral Entity Generalists? Does their affinity for Twilight entities extend to Angels, or Goetia? What the heck is their deal and why do they seem so inconsistent?
                  They're meant to be "constructs made of Spirit matter." They're not spirit masters or mediums, they're machines made from ephemera and shoved into flesh. Each 1e Lineage is powered by an element - fire (electricity, but hey), wind, water, earth and hea... spirit. Ulgan are just constructs powered by spirits. They learn about humanity from watching and observing how the spirits and ghosts form around humans.

                  There's no spirit master, no ghost whispering. In fact, there's no real drive to deal with these entities other than a source of answers and companionship - ghosts and spirits aren't affected by Disquiet, and the former still remembers what its like to be human while the latter often are the products of human life. This makes them a good source of information about any questions about humanity you might have almost right from the source.

                  -------------------------------------------------------

                  That said, for many players the Ulgan remained very strange and an odd fit. They felt forced in to fit the 5x5 model, especially when the game didn't even provide any spirit-related or ghost-related abilities until after the core (I forget when Silver came out). Ulgans who followed a Refinement other than Copper or Silver really didn't have any drive or desire to deal with spirits in a lot of cases - spirits generally don't help out with the study of pyros or body building. In a very real way, Ulgans feel inconsistant because they're pseudo-shaman outcasts that don't quite fit into a game that doesn't deal with other supernaturals well. They don't quite match up because spirit specialists don't quite fit into a game that focuses primarily on human nature. Its hard to include the Hisil in a game that overwhelmingly doesn't care about the Shadow.
