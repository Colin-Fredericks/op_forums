    So, this might be a good time to mention I’m not basing them on any particular folklore. My own whimsy and imagination are all I have, for better or worse. Of course, oneiromancy doesn’t require a particular skill, so I can’t play on that as kith/seeming shaper, to reinforce or subvert. So I must use my mind to fill out the gaps.
    Empathy has historically been a useful Skill for oneiromancy and remains only slightly less so in 2e, where it's still used to spot tampering in a Bastion through Kenning, gauge its Fortification rating, and add to deeper subliminal cues for bonus dice on Manipulation rolls with Shift Conditions.

    I think of them as a gentle, caring, weaver of dreams. That’s where the arms come in, for some reason, I associate multiple arms and dreams with weaving. Perhaps it’s some connection with spiders webs and dream catchers. Or maybe a Tv Tropes article. Anyways the theme is being a positive, constructive force.

    From the book the term premiers in:

    Originally posted by Dancers in the Dusk, p24-5, "Incubi"
    Changelings use the term “Incubi” to mean any being that has intruded into a dreamscape without the permission granted by a pledge. An Incubus can be anything capable of entering the dreamscape, though the term is not usually applied to beings that mean to aid the dreamer. Some fae who solved the problem of their fetch peacefully even call those fae who inflict nightmares on their doubles Incubi, claiming they are no different from anything else deliberately torturing a dreamer in their sleep.

    Origins
    The origins of Incubi are as ephemeral as the dreams they inhabit, and fae that fight them are often forced to admit they have no idea as to the precise nature of their opponent. Some are recognizable as True Fae, entering dreams by Dream Poison for their own inscrutable purposes. Some are fetches, striking back at the fae they replaced through their linked dreams. Others, oneiropomps are forced to reluctantly conclude, are changelings pursuing vendettas.

    The “known” Incubi, the ones that can be described as one familiar group or another, are only a very slim majority. Stranger things are found in dreams; beings that do not appear to fit with the changelings’ sketchy knowledge of the world. Self-aware dreams that pass from dreamer to dreamer, archetypes that resemble famous individuals warping the dreams of their fans, strangely powerful versions of the dreamer that shape the dreamscape through oneiromancy and attack intruding fae, creatures that may be hobgoblins, blurring the edges of the dream and the Hedge, bizarre effects that might indicate the birthing of a new member of the Gentry, beings that may be ghosts, manifesting in the sleep of their former loved ones rather than in the physical world, vampires, mages and werewolves — and odder things — using their own powers to enter the sleeping mind and still more intruders beyond recognizable form.

    Some Incubi seem determined to prevent others (such as the fae) from altering the dream they’re in. Others shape the dreams they are part of themselves and attack those that attempt to interfere. Many Incubi blend so well into the dreamscape, camouflaging themselves as native dreams (if indeed they aren’t), that they go unnoticed until they strike. Some engage in direct dream combat while others shape the dreamscape to be hostile to travelers. Some might even be the dreamscape in some way.
    The Underhill draft of 2e had incubi as a mostly-consistent phenomenon that the final version has done up as eidolons, but in the line's published history "incubus" is a catch-all for "thing in someone's dreams that's not supposed to be there," of which Dancers' list includes a great many things that are goetia that have misplaced themselves or are being reflected up through the Astral shallows into dreams.

    Non-fae intruders in dreams that aren't an existing stattable monster should probably have frailties appropriate to the bans and banes of equally powerful goetia, modeled as eidolons but distinct from them just as Hedge ghosts are.

            Originally posted by espritdecalmar View Post
            In the past, I tried to write up a multi-limbed kith, but ran into the same problems that I think every crunchy RPG with combat elements does when it comes to that sort of thing.
            The problem comes from wrong (but common) assumptions about how multi-limbs should play out. And from our tendency to simulate physics instead of abstracting situations.

            IRL few if any animals regularly engage on multiple focused tasks despite number of limbs. Even wielding two weapons in combat is far harder than it seems and most actual techniques will reduce the complexity of the task instead of trying to achieve two distinct sets of actions.

            At the end of the day, multiple limbs have just as much effect as the brain of the entity is equipped to use them. That means they can give a bonus to whatever task you want, nothing more. In fact, you can reasonably have multi-limbs as a disadvantage (Persistent Condition in our case), as the Changeling isn't actually adapted to use them and they keep getting in the way all the time instead of coordinating. A neutral stance of having more hands just to hold things and doing nothing is already pretty good in comparison.

            So instead of assuming that multiple hands have to give you multiple actions of some kind, stop to think what effect you want for this specific character, and this effect is what will define the best path to rule them. Different characters with the same number of arms beyond two may have them through distinct rules as they get distinct effects from them.
