                                                        Originally posted by Dave Brookshaw View Post
                                                        That is not my intention: Hunter's shapeshifters and witches are NOT Forsaken Uratha or Awakening mages. They're a simplified version of them warped to make them better antagonists in a Hunter game.

                                                        If you use Awakening rather than Witchfinders to build your bad guys for a Vigil game, your characters will get roflstomped. That's not a failure of Awakening as a system - it's doing what it's meant to. The Hunters native to mage are Mages (fun fact - Banishers are the nWoD's version of cWoD's imbued hunters) because one of Mage's themes is how weak and blind Sleepers are compared to the Awakened.

                                                        Witchfinders, Spirit Slayers, Mortal Remains, and Night Stalkers are there to get away from this kind of problem.
                                                        This seems to be a fundamental tension between the game designers and many in the fanbase: in general, the game designers tend to prefer that each game be viewed as a separate universe in and of itself, as that gives the designers the maximum freedom to design each game precisely how they want it. Conversely, many fans would prefer that the gamelines be designed in the context of a shared universe so that they can have maximum freedom to mix and match elements from different gamelines as they see fit.

                                                        Stuff like “if you want to run mages and hunters together, pick either Mage or Hunter and disregard the other game” is an example of a developer not wanting to compromise any of his vision for the sake of making games crossover-friendly, and is precisely the stance that players who want the games to be crossover-friendly find the most annoying. “Pick one gameline and interpret everything else through its lens” was a key element of what gave the original World of Darkness its reputation for being hostile to crossovers; one of the big innovations of the new World of Darkness was that crossovers were much easier to manage, in part because you didn’t have to say “this is using game X as the baseline”. You could easily put Kindred and Uratha side by side and have them interact with each other without having to decide “is this a Vampire game with Lupines in it, or is it a Werewolf game with Leeches in it?”

                                                        In the post-GMC era, the pendulum seems to be swinging back toward the original World of Darkness’ way of (not) handling crossovers, with this business of the Lie and the Vigil being (thus far) the most blatant example: in the quote above, Dave is flatly saying “you should never do Mage/Hunter crossovers”. If we accept this rationale, Mage and Hunter effectively exist in separate universes, because mages and hunters will never meet: mages will contend with Banishers instead, and hunters will contend with “gutter magicians” instead. This is precisely the sort of thing that ended up leading to the Fractured Cosmos section of Ascension (the Time of Judgment book for Mage: the Ascension) — and it’s precisely what I don’t want to see happen to the new World of Darkness.

                                                        The “lights in the darkness” premise of Hunter, as I understand it, is “don’t underestimate ordinary people; they’re capable of more than anyone gives them credit for.” That’s why Hunter has a “Moral Support” Tactic that can help a cell of hunters cope with Lunacy; and it’s why I hope that the hypothetical Chronicle Book for Hunter will suggest ways for hunters to take the edge off of the Lie. I’ve already suggested a comparable Tactic: an “After Action Report” where the cell of hunters compares notes and attempts to reconstruct what actually happened, giving them a way to get around the memory-wiping effects of the Lie. More generally, other gamelines have a tendency to describe how mortals are victims who are ultimately powerless against the monsters in the darkness; Mage’s stance that Sleepers are weak and blind is just one such example of this. Conversely, Hunter says “mortals aren’t that helpless, after all”. In a true crossover between Hunter and any other gameline, the hunters’ arsenal of tricks ought to be as effective against the “genuine article” as they are against the “cheap knockoffs” presented in Hunter’s supplements.

                                                        Put another way: games such as Mage are free to make sweeping statements about how helpless ordinary people are; but in the context of a shared universe, these should not be taken as absolute statements: there should always be cracks in the walls that cordon off mortals from everyone else; and Hunter should be all about chipping away at those cracks. This isn’t a case of “choose one gameline to be dominant”; this is a case of “no one gameline gives you the whole picture” — not even Mage.
