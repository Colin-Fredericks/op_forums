                                        Originally posted by tasti man LH View Post
                                        Is there clear and decisive proof Dread Powers can't recreate "classic" powers of vampires, shifters, and witches?

                                        Can you? Sure. Is it easy? No. Does the book provide any guidance on how to do so? No.

                                        The really odd thing is that it wasn't this way in 1e. Hunter 1e had the following to say about Vampires:

                                        Originally posted by Hunter: The Vigil
                                        Powers of the Blood
                                        All vampires have the Drain Dread Power, with dots in that determined roughly by their age (for every 50 years dead, assume they gain an additional dot in Drain). Most drink blood, but some seem capable of consuming other elements (memory, dreams, flesh) to gain Willpower, instead. They use Drain, therefore, to gain Willpower to fuel their Dread Powers. Note that each Willpower taken in this way (via the bite) incurs one lethal damage. (See “Bite,” p. 157, World of Darkness Rulebook.) Vampires can spend Willpower to heal, meaning they effectively repair their own bodies by consuming the bodies (blood) of others: one Willpower heals two bashing or one lethal; aggravated cannot be healed. Oh, and bullets only cause bashing damage to vampires.
                                        Common Dread Powers include (but are not limited to) Agonize, Ecstasy, Fury, Hypnotism, Impress, Unholy Attribute (Dexterity, Stamina, Strength).
                                        This is not a lot of text, but provides most of the guidance you need. Furthermore, if you were to try to apply this to 2e you would quickly find that several of the suggested Dread Powers - including the most important one Drain - are not to be found in Second Edition. There is a Numen called Drain but it is not a valid replacement. The Numen is only capable of stealing essence or Willpower, and doesn't require a grapple. The key point made above that Vampires steal Willpower while also dealing damage would have to be houseruled, as would the rule that they take Willpower equal to their dots in the Dread Power (since the Numen Drain doesn't have ranks), and the fact that they need to initiate a grapple to do so.

                                        Similarly, the Dread Power Ecstasy from 1e has not been duplicated in 2e. In 1e this is used to model the euphoric effect of the Kiss, but if you wanted to do this in 2e (or use the power in some other way) you would again have to houserule.

                                                            Originally posted by Leliel View Post
                                                            We seem to have different ideas of "isn't telling." "This power makes this monster a demon, ephemeral or physical" seems pretty clear.

                                                            And of course, there's also a vampire in there - Sea Trow. So it already has an example of "non-Kindred monsters" as an example to follow. And Amarok as something that could be werewolves. So you'll forgive me for being a might skeptical of your criticisms.
                                                            I'll be frank, I was already aware of the Infernal Dread Power and the Sea Trow. There are multiple problems with each of these.

                                                            First, having the explanation (if it can even be called that) of demons exist in a Dread Power is just silly. If I am looking for information on how demons work the Dread Powers section isn't where I would start looking. This, if nothing else, is incredibly poor layout and should be called out as such.

                                                            Beyond that though, Hunter 1e had a separate section in chapter 5 after Dread Powers where it explained in detail how demons work. Much of this information didn't make the transition to 2e. This is fine for the most part - maybe demons don't need to have a Fiendish Flaw and so on in 2e. But 1e gave advice on what demons do. What their motivations are. Why they corrupt mortals. It explained how Hunters can spot demons, how demonic pacts work and so on. All these things are what make the demons demons. In 2e, you've just got generic Horrors with a True Name. Everything else you have to invent for yourself. Worse there isn't even a Dread Power that lets Horrors regain Willpower when mortals indulge their Vices, meaning by the book you can't build the kind of demons that existed in 1e. I don't know about you, but to me driving mortals to sin is much more core to the idea of a demon than immunity to fire damage, and this should be reflected in the mechanics.

                                                            As to the Sea Trow - I think this monster rather proves my point. In order to make this monster function somewhat like a Vampire, they had to break the rules established in the Dread Powers section earlier, and even then it doesn't quite work. This monster has a bite attack that can only be used in a grapple, deals 2 lethal and inflicts the drugged tilt. The drugged tilt here seems to be intended to replace the Ecstasy Dread Power from 1e, or the euphoric effects of the Kiss described in VtR. It's a poor substitute since it doesn't make the target placid, just bad at fighting back, but I digress. The attack itself is built using Dread Attack 2 (Bite) - which specifies that the attack deal 2 lethal and inflict a condition or tilt. However the Dread Power also states "If the creature’s natural weaponry includes a bite, it doesn’t need to establish a grapple in order to bite" so the rule about the Trow needing to grapple and then bite (like a Vampire) isn't possible using the Dread Powers they give you, and has to be handwaved in.

                                                            Worse though, the Trow doesn't have any mechanics for drinking blood. It has the Drain Numen, but unlike the Drain Dread Power in 1e, the Drain Numen just directly steals Willpower from the target (and can be done from across the room). You could houserule that the Trow automatically uses the Drain Numen for free whenever it hits with a bite attack, but they don't suggest that in the book and frankly at that point you might as well just ditch the Dread Powers given to you by Hunter 2e and make your own.

                                                            This is all leaving out the fact that Hunter 2e doesn't have any few short paragraphs saying "here is some guidance on making a Vampire (or Werewolf, or Mage, etc) using the rules presented in this chapter". Frankly I would happily exchange the page detailing the Sea Trow for an update of page 316 from Hunter 1e (which gives rules and advice for Vampires).
