                                              Originally posted by PenDragon View Post
                                              By no means a counter-point, but a small word of warning: Disquiet can cause to pretty wonky problems in Crossover groups. Indeed my general experience with Disquiet is that players don't always respond super well to "You must now irrationally dislike that guy".

                                              If you're running with Demons and Prommies side-by-side you may consider coming up with a reason why Demons aren't affected, at least not as severely, by Disquiet. (Their alien mechanical minds operating differently seems a good one).

                                              Well, yes. But you don't have to say "You must now irrationally dislike that guy". I am a part of a crossover game where I play a Prommy and the other group members are two Immortals and an Awakened Mage. I am basically an Unfleshed/Nuclear thingie, and my Disquiet manifests as a mix between a very queer, uncanny feeling that something isn't right. That my Promethean isn't being genuine (Like the uncanny valley feeling you get from a near life-like robot) and at later stages, a sense of dread that this person-thing has to be killed, before he kills you. I roleplay the early stages, with roleplay that seems sorta bad, where it seems almost artificial and false. The later stages, I handle via narrative quips here and there. For eksample, they started having dreams, wherein the Mages magic was null against the Promethean, as it brutalised the Mage and burned him in atomic fire. The Immortal couldn't be killed, so she had a dream wherein the machine-man raped her repeadedly and broke every bone in her body over the course of many hours. These dreams were followed up by a scene in which my Promethean lifted a small tank in front of the mage, making him realise that the Promethean was very capable of the things he had seen in his dream, and a scene wherein we had to change cloths and my Promethean stared for an awkwardly long time at the Immortals naked body, making her think that perhaps that dream had a hint of aweful prophecy in it.

                                              So yeah. Disquiet in crossovers are a tough nut to really crack effectively, and its a hard balancing act between giving it enough narrative focus to make it cool for the Promethean, and sufficiently little so as to not detract from the plot of your chronicle. I can entirely understand if you choose to curb it in your game.
                                              Although i will say, that if you do choose to let Demons be affected by it, it will most likely make your Prometheans experience much more enjoyable. One of the things i truely enjoy about my aformentioned Prommy, is how an unnatural aversion like Disquiet can conflict with the wishes for companionship of a genuinely good and kind individual. That is of course assuming that your Promethean player is up for the challenge of roleplaying it, effectively.

                                                  That is the preferred way to handle it, yes. Provide a reason for hate and let the players make the decisions. (By the book it's a compulsion, and by the end they'll have no say.)

                                                  Even so, different strokes for different folks. Not everyone is going to want a scenario in which one of the players will invariably be assaulted by the others. This is one of those "The More You Know" things that it can't hurt for Storytellers to keep in mind.
