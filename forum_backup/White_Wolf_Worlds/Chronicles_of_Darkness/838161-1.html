                        To be honest, I've had it a bit of head-canon that the Arcadia of both Mage and Changeling fame is the same place but from alternate realities; one which fell into ruin (possibly) and one that never existed. The hard part is finding out which of the two is which. :-p Mages do have the whole time school of magic to mess with after all. What's to say the Hedge wasn't an alternate timeline that latched onto our own universe and integrated itself in? Or, worse, that the Arcadia of Mage went back in time and became the Arcadia of Changeling?

                        (I know there's an actual explanation somewhere that elaborates on the nature of this, and the interactions therein, though I've yet to read/find it; as I said, head-canon above)

                        It therefore makes sense to me that the Hedge is a sort of counterpart to the God-Machine, if not a literal one. Whereas one is mechanical and orderly, but has some chaotic goal that makes no sense, the other is natural, beguiling...and yet has some kind of logic to its end goal once you dig into it (Gentry get Changelings, Changelings become Gentry). They are both spanning entire worlds, worlds unto themselves and with an innate logic that is alien to the human mind but, once you are a part of it (or left it) it makes all too much sense.

                                    Well, in my head cannon... well, it all starts off with a line from Compacts and Conspiracies. The company is supposedly ran by people from another dimension who see the world traveling into the same Apocalypse that their dimension feel into. At the time that book came out, I immediately dismissed that. It didn't match anything I wanted from the game. Now, however, with the God Machine being out, and my fun with putting it front and center in some new games I'm working on?

                                    Here's my story. In the "true" timeline, all supernaturals could operate openly. There was no Masquerade, Veil, Quintessence, Mask worth talking about. Magic was an open secret, and, to be honest, the world was worse for it. Humans lived as pawns for these other, greater forces. Technology grew, but only to suit the needs of the supernaturals in power. So, one day, the humans that resisted this order, and came up with a plan. They would use what occult abilities they managed to steal and subvert from the other Powers, forge a machine that could protect them, and then thrust it backwards in time to the distant past, where it could change history, and save the human race from monsters among them.

                                    And thus was born the God Machine. Its purpose? Survive. Cast the darkness back into the shadows where they belong. Evolve and guide humanity to a new destiny. And so it did. Supernaturals adapted by hiding from humanity. And then experiments. And adapting, replacing, growing. Whatever purpose the alien-minded Machine had was so mangled, that now it saw no problem controlling time and human biology to constantly create new forms. It creates psychics and andriods. Eventually, it will help all people achieve Heaven as part of itself, everyone become angels. All become part of the Machine.

                                    Enter the Gentry. Two forces, both seeking to influence people's destiny. On one hand, because It has a Grand Design to achieve. On the other, because it makes an amusing story, a pleasing fantasy. By nature, the two are opposed. The God Machine is the Hero's Journey, slaying the dragons. The Gentry are the dragons of fairy tales. Every person the Fae take robs a human of its destiny. They warp and transform people in a way that works against the God Machine's Mission. So, it does what it always does. Build Infrastructure. It studies and builds and tries to enforce its reality over the Hedge. And, to an extent, it works. It makes a great story, so the Wyrd actually encourages it. Its when the God Machine wants things to be stable and predictable that it all goes sideways.

                                    But the God Machine isn't stupid. It knows. It understands. So... it arranges stories to applease the Gentry. It makes sacrifices, weaving grandoise plots and wars to entertain the Gentry, all the while subtle Infrastructure is building in the background, unawares. Sometimes, these parts are ruined by hobs and other fae. Sometimes, the Gentry spot something the G-M didn't want spotted, and wages war.

                                    It happens time and again. But what does the God Machine care? It has eternity to bring the Wyrd to heel. While the Wyrd itself enjoys the narrative - its not often the True Fae get to be the heroes of the stories, fighitng off the evil invader.

                                                        In my experience, many people place God-machine and Wyrd as opposites, embodiment of order vs embodiment of chaos. But this, I would say, isn't how Wyrd works.

                                                        True, it seeks stories. People are characters, and it likes twists while roles are at least acknowledged. But it is so much more.

                                                        Some would say it is fate itself. That fickle mistress whose plan is incomprehensible, and has many turns, but at end, everything is controlled, commanded, just like God Machine. But still it doesn't completely cover Wyrd.

                                                        You see, though it is commonly thought as thing of Arcadia, it exists everywhere. It is force that is at base of everything, force that connects and holds together all that is, was, will be and could or couldn't be. It likes stories, but it isn't it's only power. Changelings make contracts with it to gain powers over beasts, over abilities and elements, by contracting that aspect of Wyrd. We suppose, but we don't know, if it is sentient. Force from Star Wars is most similar to it. Order, chaos, balance, they are all just aspects of Wyrd.

                                                        And God- machine isn't omniscient or omnipotent. It has knowledge we lack, but that knowledge is just things we lack, still subject to Laws of Nature. God-Machine uses laws we ahven't discovered yet, changelings go outright against this rules by bargain made with power that is writer, paper and ink of these laws. True fae are congregration of this power, and it's different aspects. It needs Infrastructure, occult matrix, angels to operate it. It needs to feed Angels Essence. And in end, it is made of metal.

                                                        God machine knows that Wyrd is strange power. It has theories, and research on it, beliveing it to be related to Ephemera. But it is wary of it, for it knows that Wyrd is old power, that it is all and so much more, and very unpredictable. Few incursions in hedge have fallen, unable to adapt to alien realm, their mission harder for each step they make towards Arcadia. As such, god-machine doesn't bother much with True Fae unless they actively threaten operation, and why should it? They are not of this World, and they don't matter. As such, most Changelings don't know of it.

                                                        As for True Fae.... it is complicated. Some of them sometimes take interest into God Machine, helping or hindering it's plans in accordance to their plans, boredom and nature. They mostly leave it alone-it is so boring but at least it doesn't bother them. Maybe they know it's goal, maybe they can guess. It is uninteresting to them so they leave it alone and hope it does same.

                                                            Story Ideas:

                                                            1. The local Freehold has been thrown into chaos by the sudden appearance of a clockwork being claiming to be an Angel. Ignoring all questions and brushing aside attacks as if they were of no consequence, the Angel informed the Freehold that somewhere in the city a brother Angel has fallen from the grace of god. God demands reparations from the fae- a tithe must be claimed. A changeling will die by the Angel's hands each night until 10% of the fae population is dead or the newly formed Demon has been destroyed or returned to God. The changelings must choose between attempting to hide from a relentless hunter, track down a monster even more elusive than they are, or discover where in the city the Infrastructure housing this peculiar occult matrix feeding on fairy blood is found and eliminate it (with or without the Demon's help). The first two options mean capitulating with the God-Machine and hoping none of its servants ever Fall again and the nightmare resumes itself. The latter involves trusting a known liar and risking divine retribution on a far larger scale.

                                                            2. Those Changelings that know of the God-Machine are as uncertain of Its nature as anyone else. Some believe it is the modern form of Cold Iron, the personification that cursed the True Fae long ago to wither under its touch. Others say this is nonsense, that Cold Iron is most effective when it is not been forged or processed by the factories of industrious humanity, and the god-machine if anything represents all too much of this industrial era. To them, the God-Machine is more likely a sign of infection. If Cold Iron broke a covenant with the fae because humanity learned the art of shaping it with hammer and fire (this idea itself a controversial subject among the lost), then the God-Machine must surely be a natural consequence of that breach. Forging iron was an important innovation of man (and the God-Machine loves such innovations- if for no other reason so that it can piggy back its Infrastructure off of human buildings), and the advent of steel and electricity arguably came about due to that initial step. Thus, these fae claim, the God-Machine is like a cancer growing in the metaphysical rift left over from Cold Iron's curse. No wonder that certain bridge burners worship the thing so devoutly- they believe that strengthening the machine ensures the Gentry's vulnerability. Perhaps one day, when the God Machine is everywhere, the links to Arcadia can be severed permanently.

                                                            3. The God-Machine doesn't appear entirely aware of it yet, but the Fae represent a potentially devastating threat to many of its most important occult matrixes. In Seattle there are alternate timelines accessible only through broken splinters the GM can watch over or guard. Other locations are dimensionally disconnected from our world and folded into spaces that are bigger on the inside. Still other realms move to different time periods,or form temporal bubbles wherein events repeat themselves over and over again (usually violent and horrific tragedies). Due to the fickle nature of the Wyrd and its apparent connection to Time it is apparently possible for a changeling to access many or all of these locations via the Hedge. While it has yet to be proven that a Changeling could wander into the Hedge on open a gate INTO a closed off piece of Infrastructure or Occult Matrix, it is know (albeit, by only a handful) that a changeling can LEAVE such a location by entering the Hedge. This is true with the Seattle setting (as per Demon: The Descent) and it may be true with other pieces of God-Machine tech at the ST's discretion. As usual, once a door into the Hedge is formed it remains there and is accessible to the fae from either end of the gateway.
