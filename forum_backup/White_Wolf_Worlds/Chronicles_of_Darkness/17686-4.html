    In the very first episode of Vividred Operation magical girls fight side by side with an entire naval task force; and after that they're officially inducted into the military. In Mai-OTOME magical girls replaced conventional military, and considerable mundane resources are spent on running the magical girl training school. In Mahou no Tenshi Creamy Mami, Mami gets has a mundane producer backing her. Then there's Nahona where a quick google search suggests that the TSAB is the openly acknowledged government of several worlds. Even Sailor Moon, well she ends up as the queen of Earth. How much more mundane backing can you get?

    So, being backed by a mundane organisation is not the standard format for magical girls, but it's hardly unkown. It's Camaraderies that lack precedent. When magical girl organisations scale up, they don't form secret societies. They integrate into or rule over mundane society. Dream just assumes they do so without being overt about the magic, since this is the WoD.

        Vividred Operation has a magic power plant supplying electricity to most of Japan as a central plot point; Mai-Otome is set in the distant future on a lost colony world; after the first two seasons the Nanoha franchise leaves Earth entirely for worlds ruled by the TSAB; Sailor Moon doesn't become queen of Earth during the story proper, but sometime in the future; and Creamy Mami is a singer on a concert tour, not a political figure. Mami's the only one of your examples that has serious support from a mundane organization and is living on modern Earth, and the scope of her story is strictly personal.

        In fact, with respect to modern Earth the TSAB actually is a secret society, like the Camaraderie idea, during the first two Nanoha seasons. No one affiliated with the Bureau ever thinks of enlisting the help of Earth's governments to chase down the Jewel Seeds or the Wolkenritter, or of looking for mundane recruits on Earth. They assume jurisdiction over Nanoha and Hayate, based entirely on their knowledge of magic (as the TSAB doesn't rule Earth in any sense.) And while several TSAB members reside on Earth for years, they carefully conceal the fact that they came from other planets from anyone who doesn't already know. In short, they act almost exactly as the usual WoD/CofD supernatural conspiracies do, as long as the franchise stays on Earth.

        The standard situation in the Dream version is, I repeat, much more of a Western pulp/superhero trope. It's Doc Savage and his close advisors, the Shadow and his network of informants, Batman and his "family" of vigilantes, Buffy Summers and her high school friends. You don't get that trope with magical girls. (One reason for that, I think, is that to be plausible it almost requires the protagonist to have a fair chunk of experience before the story starts; that is, to be functionally an adult. By definition magical girls are not adults. Thus if a magical girl is supposed to use a network of mundane allies to deal with her troubles, much of her story has to be spent on her recruiting those allies, decreasing the time available for magical fireworks.)

                                I think what you're asking is why nobody wrote a Princess focused Hunter: the Vigil suppliment? The answer is because no one got around to writing them.

                                My concept was a public relations firm that won a high profile election against a Noble candidate and got famous as the go-to firm for Noble releated issues.
