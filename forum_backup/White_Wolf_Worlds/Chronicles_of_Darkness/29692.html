    Apotheosis and Guild Status (probably some spoilers)

    12-13-2013, 11:45 PM
    Okay, so taking as a given that a lot of the text on apotheosis, even in the storyteller section, is extremely vague (if "off the supernatural radar" isn't a phrase that is wildly up for interpretation, I don't know what is)...

    It seems to me, from reading Guildhalls, that Guild Status is set up so that it doesn't just....disappear, if you stop showing up to meetings or if everyone forgets your name. It has to be actively stripped from you in order to be lost. So does that mean that you keep it after apotheosis? I mean, obviously you won't be able to use it most of the time: if you come out of the woodwork and start using your Guild Status 5 to boss around the Journeymen, they're going to cry foul pretty quickly. And likewise, you probably can't gain more. But the mystical aspect of the status (and the guild affinities that come with it) should stay with you assuming no one remembers you long enough to track you down, hold you still, and perform an Obloquy, right?

    Just looking for a sanity check here, really.

      It's a good question that really doesn't have an answer.
      I mean you're a free mummy now, no longer bound to the judges will or the call of the ancient relics. You've got a brand new unlife ahead of you without needing to return to sleep. But that also brings a lot of trouble. It would be difficult to give up the guild, an organization and congregation you've been a part of for literally thousands of years. It would be even harder to give up on your meret, the mummies you call allies. And remember, Apotheosis is a myth. The stuff of legends. Who's going to believe you? Some may even get mad at you for reminding them of something they cannot possess.

      So yeah, I bet it would be hard for a mummy to give up their guild. It's basically their entire way of life since before time began. But on the other hand it would be dangerous or foolish to reveal yourself as an apotheosis mummy. Though someone's going to notice that you've got less power and you don't need to return to your rest, and your form doesn't change. An apotheosis brings freedom but it also bars you out of a world you've been a part of for all you're unlife. What's a mummy to do?

      In terms of the game, I assume you keep it until you rescind your status for someone else, or the other mummies just forget about you.

          Since the thread title is marked with a spoiler warning I don't think you need to put spoilers in your post, RisTigger, though I could be wrong.

          Interesting question, OP. The Guild structure the Arisen exist in was sort of 're-constructed' by them, when they arose in the first Sothic turn (judging by the Sothic cycle page in the core book, and other mentions of Guilds being that they're, well, re-constructed from what they remembered of their mortal roles in the Empire), It's become such a powerful identity to them that Guildhalls of the Deathless goes out and says this Y-splat is more integral to the Arisen than other Y-splats are to other splats, and that the Guild magic which infuses a Mummy is second only to the Rite of Return itself.

          So it's hard to imagine that something so ingrained into the Arisen would be shed upon Apotheosis, which doesn't even completely strip them of the Rite. Apotheosis isn't like the New Dawn in Promethean. It's not about necessarily becoming human (although you do, in a sense, because it's about reclaiming Memory) but rather unbinding yourself from the chains of Duat. Since it doesn't strip away the Rite, and therefore your affinities, utterances, and Pillars, I can't imagine it stripping away belonging to a Guild.

          On the other hand, no Guild wants anything to do with an Arisen who has achieved Apotheosis. Perhaps I might rule it like the Guild effectively removes your Status, but you get to keep your Guild affinities? The Guild doesn't recognize you, even though the Rite itself does...or something.

          This might be what's so unsettling about the Deceived, actually! The teasers we know of them thus far, between hints in Guildhalls and things CAS has posted here lately, seem to imply they know something about Apotheosis. I don't think they've achieved it, but they have some sort of connection with it and they are a Guild after all

                                              To me, the Judges are very hands-off and distant in terms of Morally Ambiguous Otherwordly Overlords, compared to other splats. The God-machine works in mysterious ways but its motives and methods are confounding and seemingly nonsensical, and its angelic servants are mindless automatons (well, until they Fall); the Judges have a clearer goal - collect vessels imbued with Sekhem - and its agents are surprisingly individualistic (the Rite didn't intend for the Arisen to have Memory). The Exarchs and their Seer agents are a bit more interactive with the world, with understandable goals. The only way the Judges really interact with our world is their whipping of the Arisen, but even then they themselves are never seen. The only way the Arisen know if they've displeased the Judges are when they make an unscheduled Descent roll.                                               So what that all means, to me, is that I don't think the Judges really cared about the Deceived, because they're distant and largely uncaring about Arisen concerns. The Mummies of the Lost Guild are technically Deathless, unlike the Shuankhsen, so they were presumably all made by the Rite of Return and bound to the Judges.                                               The only thing besides vessels the Judges care about, I think, are the Shuankhsen. They issued a standing order to return any and all Bane relics to Duat immediately because of their danger. The Devourer seems to be the only enemy that the Judges care to combat. Whereas the Deceived are out for revenge against the Arisen, the Shuankhsen hate them because they serve the Judges. The Arisen think they're near gods, but its Shuankhsen who have Morality instead of Memory. If anyone knows the capital-T Truth then its the Shuankhsen, in a way.
