    Is Chronicles of Darkness Ending?

    08-28-2023, 05:10 PM
    I've been checking the Current Projects and Monday Meetings and while DtR: The Devoted Companion, MtA: Tome of the Pentacle and HtV: Tending the Flame hit DTRPG in the last year or so, it very much feels like Chronicles of Darkness is wrapping up production compared to Onyx Path's other gamelines - or at least, has been significantly reduced in priority compared to Exalted 3E, Scion, Trinity Continuum etc. The only active projects seem to be The Hedge and The Clades Companion, aside from ready-made character sheets, jumpstarts, and VTT tokens, which feels like a slower pace compared to earlier years. We also know that some previously-confirmed projects that were in the works, such as MtA: Fallen Worlds, seem to have vanished from production entirely without any official word on their current status.

    NWoD as an entire whole is nearly 20 years old and CoD very recently reached 10 years old, so it is understandable if after two decades Onyx Path has decided to reprioritize and while I prefer not to speculate if possible, I can imagine that the situation with Paradox and WoD 5E probably does put a dampener on the future of CoD projects. Even outside of possible intercorporate context, it feels like the writing is on the wall with OP redirecting attention to Storypath / Storypath Ultra and away from Storyteller, so perhaps it is easier and less complicated to develop for, who knows? Not to paint with broad strokes, but there's at least some community sentiment that feels CoD 'dying' or wrapping up production seems to be a likely case.

    Not much more to this thread than expressing my feelings, I'm not touching new ground or saying anything that hasn't been unsaid by any means, and I don't mean to fearmonger or stoke any fires. Would just like to know people's thoughts, if they agree or feel to the contrary, maybe throw out ideas for what they would've wanted or liked to see before the seeming redirection of priorities hit the gameline. I personally would enjoy if Fallen Worlds came back into production, or one or two more Werewolf books featuring a more detailed look at more (and new) Lodges and the Hisil with associated spirit weirdness, maybe even more Contagion Chronicle stuff isn't just outbreaks.

                                  Originally posted by ShadowcatX View Post

                                  They're competing against their own products. It's better for them financially to support 1 vampire game and get 90% of the profit at 50% of the costs and sadly they decided that will be WoD.

                                  But on topic, it isn't necessarily ending. None More Dark Publishing has carried on the VtR flag and I've heard whispers that there may be a Mage or Werewolf equivalent coming at some point.
                                  1) This would actually be ignoring how both Masquerade and Requiem both flourished while both were products gettting support before Paradox came in. Roleplaying games don't exactly hedge each other out like other media can, since the majority of participation with them is in the reading rather than the playing, and doing World and Chronicles at the same time did a lot to hype both up to anyone who might otherwise be attracted to one, particularly since it was no longer a situation where it was one or the other and thus there's not as much resentment.

                                  As has been cited over in the threads for World several times, Paradox's reasoning may have made branding sense when they got it, and it may make sense when you consider the way they've bene hewing 5E towards Chronicles' design sensibilities, but particularly with how their handling of it has been failing to meet their own expectations, the refusal to greenlight Chronicles products alongside World is bleeding them money unneccesarily. Arguably they're making the same mistake you just made, except they have industry professionals who have been with World and Chronicles all the way to now, and in particular saw during the Darkness Renaissance of the 2010's how fostering both franchises elevated sales and performance for both, so why they would be making that mistake can only come down to some form of arrogance.

                                  2) While I am also a fan of None More Dark Publishing's work with Requiem and look forward to how they'll expand to other gamelines, a line needs to be maintained in understanding the work they do through the Storyteller's Vault versus owning the game license and having official products made-namely when it comes to its creator's monetization and time affecting things like marketing and production rate. While they can market the same way anyone else can, it comes down to the writer's pimping out their work on their own time and often on their own dime, as opposed to even Onyx Path's small base of contractually-dedicated employees with a contract to do nothing but market the shit out of games-NMD basically only has word of mouth as an exceptional promotional tool- and NMD's basis for paying their writer's all comes down to how the STV allows for payouts in the first place, as opposed to a company holding the rights having the power to increase the cash-per-word as fits their interests and designs. NMD gets by with having a small enough number of writers that they can get paid decently with each project, and even then a lot of that is subject to passion for the game and writing than the comfortable bumps of payment.

                                  None More Dark's supplements are better understood as a bandaid to the larger problem of Chronicles not getting supplements (let alone any new gamelines or ambitious projects) than a proper fix, and the fanbase needs to appreciate there is a upper limit with that that will eventually get hit. I can pretty much guarantee you every writer with NMD would rather just have Paradox approve new books than continue how they're going.

                                              Originally posted by ShadowcatX View Post
                                              It doesn't ignore anything. Wether the primary participation is reading or not you're still double dipping into a pool of limited resources. The economy isn't doing the best right now and people are hard pressed for disposable income.

                                              Disagreeing with you isn't a mistake. It is possible we are both correct, but I'd be happy to wager on who is more so.
                                              If you want to gamble against a professional in the industry with perspective and training, you're welcome to lose your money.

                                              Even within the parameters of Paradox trying to turn World of Darkness into a multimedia source of profit, including video games, shows, and more, a look at their yearly earnings and picking up what we know about their contracts with Onyx Path, Renegade, and other publishing houses demonstrates a capacity to support it financially as well as CCP did during the Renaissance, even accounting for the change in economy.

                                              Now whether Paradox cares is an entirely different matter. The purchase was very much the passion project of some people who also didn't really have any idea what they were doing. Martin Ericsson and the folks around him who started the whole debacle were entirely focused on World as the product, all gains from Chronicles rendered more as a mechanical toolkit for working World. This is demonstrable in both the implementation of Chronicles mechanics in the actual product and an unapologetic animosity for Chronicles in the marketing*. By the time that initial team fucked things up so hard that Paradox stepped in and closed the door on their own White Wolf Team, that fallen tree was rather a large subject.

                                              It seems likely that Paradox picked up the biases and ideas of their failed White Wolf Division, keeping the course but with competence this time (in theory)-easier to run with a plan than turn the ship on a whole brand's plan. This goes double since Paradox has taken a stance that increasingly seems like they're trying to make their money back and then get the hell away from the product. Justin Achilli having been a major hire and figure for them during Reckoning and Apocalypse doubles down on this idea-you want to build an improved World that cannibalizes bits and bobs from Chronicles, you hire the guy who took to early Chronicles as a way to do World of Darkness Lite.

                                              Now this is where the actual "What are you doing?" of it all actually comes in. If the idea is that only the World brand is strong enough to help them get that, it's an understandable basic way to think about it, but it's also something of a losing game, increasingly so as games and books continue to bomb** and the World brand under them is making less and less productive returns. Chronicles probably won't make them gangbusters money, but it's a proven and demonstrable source of income, and any discussion with Onyx Path at the time of the purchase would easily reveal that there's plenty of TTRPG money to be made from Chronicle's Second Edition (Geist and Deviant getting proper full length supplements for their respective bits are the first major things to come to mind, but the fact that even the old hands of Requiem, Forsaken, and Awakening having freelancers ready with pitches is a good indicator the wheat was quite ready to harvest), and the promotional capabilities of Paradox could magnify that-and going over the big deal with multimedia, as data comes in from doing that, it probably would open the doors for experimenting with Chronicles in video games as a way to make that money back, all the while allowing them to sit back and really work out a plan for World instead of desperately trying to tread water.

                                              Now, if the idea, after all the assumption aside, is that they still don't trust the Chronicles Brand to do anything for them, that leads to the other "What are you doing?", which is "why aren't you just cancelling Chronicles outright?" And honestly, if it were a matter of financial concerns, I'd've expected them to just do that from the start-it's otherwise obvious they don't trust Chronicles as a big ticket item and otherwise see it as a brand conflict with World, and announcing it's cancellation basically keeps them from having to handle the customer base for as long as they have. it costs them more to not say it than to say it (and it's not like it's a decision they couldn't take back later on).

                                              Anyways, all of that is an aside-the greater point is that Paradox's most likely reason for it mostly just trying to make a plan work, and that initial plan was made by people whose relationship with Chronicles was awkward-clearly wanted to use ideas and mechanics for it's products, enough so that cancelling it early on would seem exceptionally ghoulish, but hated the brand for the perception of what it did to the the assumed heyday of World of Darkness*** and were quite ready to reverse the roles. They might also realize Chronicles forms a decent fallback if they need to massively shift gears, but for the most part, the people at Paradox running the show are not people with experience with this particular bit of ambition and kitsch, and they've mostly demonstrated that by not paying attention to the lessons of experts on the subject.

                                              *Ericsson and crew have personally demonstrated a love and admiration for Chronicles, but apparently still fell like several promotional posts for World explicitly shitting on Chronicles were a good move for winning an audience, so deal with that how you will.
                                              **A bit hyperbolic, I'll admit-their products are selling well enough in terms of raw numbers, but it's not doing well enough to make up on the initial investments, which is where the real problem is.
