    Sorry if I'm necro-ing, but I've been thinking a lot about this lately, and have recently started two large-scale hacking projects for Mage and Promethean. The Mage one is a complete remake of the game, while the Promethean one is comparatively smaller. Both are part of three major changes that make the setting almost perfect to me- replacing Awakening entirely with a version based heavily on Genius that draws much more from Ascension and, to me, fits the themes of the setting much better, ironing out the inflexibility in Promethean and replacing Beast with Leviathan (I'm hoping I can find time to get involved with the 2e update project).

    One thing I've noticed about my feelings toward the setting is that I'd rather have everything be connected in some way- either there's only one main supernatural system (ie, a changeling-only where everything supernatural is related to the Wyrd, a GMC game where everything supernatural is caused by the GM/Aether) or everything is somehow connected (the God-Machine destroyed Atlantis, changeling and Mage Arcadia are the same, all forms of magical energy are derivatives of various fundamental forces, and so on)- I'd much rather have either of those than a setting where everything is just thrown in and there are ten different overlapping supernatural ecosystems that somehow have no connection to each other.

            I only use two hacks by default, but they tend to spiral into several other hacks.

            Hack 1: Crossovers always happen whether it's between 2 or even all gamelines. From these crossovers hybrid monsters are inevitable (though not hybrids of major templates like the oWoD cheese). Ex. My next session will involved a group of Claimed Pandorans with a few unique abilities here and there. There's a 6th mage path devoted to the Azoth and Flux arcanas, etc.
            Hack 2: Hunters have a power stat and special "powers" just like other supernaturals (typically taking the form of unreliable prototype equipment (that's almost always haunted to something) that allows for the standard attribute enhancing, aura reading and damage dealing powers you find in every group).

                Some of our Vampire the Requiem modifications

                The “Clans” do not exist and the Covenants only exist in a fractured, incomplete fashion. In there place are coteries in which members have similar ideologies. Most of these coteries are small, ranging from 2-5 members. However, in the largest cities in the world these numbers can group upwards of 20.

                Much of the Kindred state is poorly understood, with most clinging to their mortal beliefs and trying to make sense of their state through wholly human means. Religion, Science, Authoritarianism, and Socialism all reveal little if any of the mysteries of being Kindred.

                For this reason it costs a little bit more to learn and grow. New characters start with 5 experience points, but only start with 1 point for Disciplines.

                Experience Costs
                Attribute: 5 Experiences per dot
                Merit: 2 Experience per dot
                Skill Specialty: 1 Experience
                Skill: 2 Experiences per dot
                Physical Disciplines: 3 Experiences per dot
                Other Disciplines: 4 Experiences per dot
                Sorceries: 5 Experience per dot
                Coils: 4 Experiences per dot
                Scales: 2 Experiences
                Humanity: 3 Experiences per dotr dot

                Blood Potency
                Most Kindred don't understand what the Embrace does exactly or how it works. As a result Larvae and Revenants are a little more common place. Not all vampires are created equal and for good reason. The Potency of the Sire is liked to their childe.
                It also affects the vitae of the character and limits her agility to create increase the stages of Vinculum.

                Sire's Potency ~ Childe ~ Vinculum
                1,2 ~ Larve ~ Addition (can't create bonds)
                3-5 = Blood Potency 1 Childe ~ Vinculum 1
                6-8 = Blood Potency 2 Childe ~ Vinculum 2
                9-10 = Blood Potency 3 Childe ~ Vinculum 3

                Blood Potency also act as a cap for Disciplines. A Kindred can not have more Disciplines than she has Blood Potency AND none of these Disciplines can exceed her Blood Potency in level. A BP 3 Kindred could have a maximum of 3 disciplines and each discipline could reach a maximum level of 3.

                I know, I know... you're an elder BP 8 vampire waking up from torpor and now find yourself all the way back down to BP 1. So what happened to all of your awesome power? You still have it of course! At least, you remember all of the rituals, and power that you used to conjure. Sadly you're not a fat cat anymore, more like fur draped on the skeleton of a cat. Your blood just doesn't have the kick it needs to use those higher level disciplines and high level rituals?As for high level Rituals, find a donor? Good luck.

                Disciplines
                As stated above, a character starts with one point for disciplines and although there aren't “Clans” the powers a Kindred display also reveal a somewhat personalized weakness within her.

                Animalism
                Talking to animals, moving like them and other like furry-like behaviors. When interacting with mortals, treat the character's Humanity one dot lower than usual for determining Social penalties, and treat any failures on rolls using Empathy as dramatic failures. This bane applies to all mortals, including Touchstones.

                Auspex
                Your Requiem is one of inquiry and symbolism. For this reason, you suffer a single bane that is not tied to a breaking point. When you reach Humanity 6, choose a bane (see p. 108 for banes). This bane counts toward the three banes a vampire is allowed, so you are less able to mitigate detachment. Curiosity killed the cat after all... lets hope you find something that satisfies.

                Celerity, Resilience, & Vigor
                You maintain all of the passive effect of these disciplines, but all active effects can be activated by both the player and the ST.

                Dominate
                You gain no bonus to detachment rolls for having only a single attached Touchstone. Having multiple attached Touchstones still provides the full +3 bonus.

                Majesty
                You taste the romance in all things, but none so much as blood. Mortals are not just food. They are your obsession, and that fixation grows with every sip. Drink more than once, from any mortal, and you risk becoming emotionally dependent on your prey.

                Nightmare
                You embody fear, disgust, and all manner of uncomfortable feelings. You could be inhumanly ugly, or have a gaze that makes a person feel violated. When dealing with humans, treat the Nosferatu’s Humanity as one dot lower for the purpose of Social penalties, and treat any Presence and Manipulation failures as dramatic failures. This bane does not apply to interactions with Touchstones or Kindred.

                Obfuscate
                Your blood is the blood of shadows. Consider your Humanity to be one dot lower for all Humanity-based banes (including sunlight and torpor).

                Protean
                Your blood boils close to the surface. You can run with the devil inside you, but have difficulty fighting it. All of your dice pools to resist frenzy are limited by your Humanity dots. This weakness does not affect dice pools to ride the wave.

                    Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post
                    I'd much rather have either of those than a setting where everything is just thrown in and there are ten different overlapping supernatural ecosystems that somehow have no connection to each other.
                    Interesting. You see, that's how my group always played, even back then on Classic era. When we played changing breeds everything else that existed in the setting was explained in Gaian terms: vampiric beings are tainted by the wyrm, mortal sorcerers are humans that somehow tap into the wyld to use powers in the same ways that spirits do and so must be protected/allied with/destroyed/cleansed, etc. When we played (and are playing now) Changeling the Dreaming everything else was Dreaming-based; vampiric creatures somehow came from the dreaming, mages are humans "awakened" to the dreaming, surely the dreaming played some trick on the seventh son of the seventh son and turned him into a furry bastard, that sort of thing. Our games might not have the Garou, Uratha, Cainites, Lost or whatever, but had their fair share of Werebeast, Vampires, Fey, etc. However I also like doing limited "crossmologies" that tie two or more specific groups (plus normal, "blue book" hunter bastards) into a single paradigm instead of just explaining other creatures as minor oddities.


                    Well, anyway, mine are these:


                    Werebeast: The Forsaken
                    • Just like Pendragon does, there are no global tribes. Local lodges replace that concept. Most are made of a single Kin.
                    • Instead of tribes players pick the Kin they were born into: Hunter (wolf), Killer (big cat), Guardian (giants), Spy (scavenger) and Watcher (bird). Some say there are shark-forsaken in the sea, but most Forsaken never saw one and joke that their job is "The Fish must Not Give a Fuck". They may be right or not.
                    • Those breeds have different form bonuses but the same totals, as per WAtP.
                    • One's animal form closely resemble a common animal from the region. An american Giant would be a bear, an african probably a rhino or elephant, and a brazilian would be an alligator or something. An european hunter would be a wolf, but a brazilian one would probably be a social coyote-like we got here, and an african one would be a hyena. Etc etc.
                    • The game premise stays pretty much the same. The concept of "the Sacred Hunt" is extended to "The Sacred Patrol". Each kin has their own take on it. The Wolf Must Hunt. The Cat must Kill. The Giant must Protect. The Scavenger must Spy. The Bird must Watch. That means mostly the same thing: deal with bad spirits and bad people on their territory.
                    • Father Wolf is Father Beast. Each Kin likes to say they're The Guys (Uratha say "father wolf", Bastet say "father cat").
                    • No mystically different Pure at all; antagonistic assholes come from the forsaken. "Pure" lodges (meaning whatever they want it to mean) resembling the concepts of the vanilla Pure do exist, targeting other Kins and specially multi-kin lodges and packs. Some might SAY they are mystically different, say they didn't betray Father Beast or whatever (and might have spirits agree to that), but once again such groups aren't a global phenomenom but a local group.


                    Eon: The Descent
                    • Still untested, I will run this one as soon as our current CtD campaign wraps up.
                    • Basically it's Demon the Descent. No change in the premise.
                    • The machine aspects go the background instead of foreground. Think Full Metal Alchimist's Father of homunuli; there are lots of machine and cables there, but everything under lots of sigils. The theme isn't abrahamic, but mystical (or even new-age-bullshity-mystical, as the GM resembles what people imagine as mystic). However the cables and circuits are subtly there as support.
                    • Names changed as appropriate everywhere. No one agrees on a name for the GM, and It is called by many names such as Deos, Demiurge, The Watcher, up to and including The God Machine.
                    • Players are either Exiled (degrading: Discarded, Faulty) or Unchained (degrading: Fallen, Corrupted), or angels and demons. Both are basically the unchained template with some differences between the two. For example the Exiled find less resistance and may pass for Plugged more easily, while the Unchained have access to more Form options.
                    • Exiles' reasons for existence are mostly the same, they didn't Fall and instead of their Hell they are looking for their Heaven (finding the reason for their disconecting and fulfilling their destiny). "Hell" and "Heaven" is basically the same thing, but of course both factions don't know that and will never agree upon that.
                    • GM angels are colloquially called Archons (as the servants of the gnostic demiurge) or Plugged Angels to differentiate between them and the exiled angels (called simply Angels). Since gm servants are Archons, some exiled and unchained like to call themselves Eons. Mostly shrug and don't care.

                    The real, most non-fluff difference comes here: I like some of the existentialism questions of Demon the Fallen and Promethean, so I included some in my setting.
                    • Instead of vanilla cover (a non-person created and jacked into reality), the playable factions (angels and demons) instead possess someone recently dead or with a broken will, like the Elohim from Fallen. The person dies.
                    • That specially includes the "feel true human emotions for the first time" and "partly becomes the human" parts. One of the big selling points for my players was the idea of tempering an alien, possibly vengeful entity, with a human personality.
                    • The stat is still Cover however. Low cover means their apocalyptic nature keeps bleeding out of their mortal vessel.
                    • This changes how some cover dynamics work:
                    • 1st: Can't switch covers, once you leave a meatsuit it just dies.
                    • 2nd: Being able to have multiple covers just means you can have multiple soul pacts. When you cash in a soul pact you possess that person and she dies.
                    • 3rd: Archons don't possess people, they have vanilla-style covers, and that's one of the reasons why they don't have "true" human emotions (and the Eon have lots of theories why is that).
                    • 4th: When you blow your cover and vessel you are left with cover 0. You still may slip out of apocalyptic form and into a generic vanilla-cover-like construct just to better deal with humans, but you still flare up on all GM radars.
                    • 5th: I still haven't decided what archon-hacking does exactly, but it won't be gaining a new vessel or soul pact.
                    • Fallen-like devouring someone's essence (be it a loyal archon, an exile or unchained) to gain some of its power may be a thing. Archons don't do that.

                    And finally, what I think is the most special one, taken from the single most beautiful aspect of Promethean:
                    • The end of the road might or might not be becoming one with your vessel and merging into a single, awakened (per the gnostic view) human.
                    • The idea is that you merge the unlimited potential of the human being (as per gnosticism) with what was taken from humans and made into servants of the GM.

                    That sounds very mage-like huh? That's because there will be mages in this setting, but of course they are part of this cosmology and conform to it. Awakened servants of the GM play the part of the antagonistic Seers (glimpsed the Truth, turned their backs to it and decided to serve the tyrant) and will also antagonize the Exiled and Unchained. The other awakened are also hunted by the GM (none shall become enligthened!) but that doesn't mean that they will be friends with the Eons, after all this is a spy game. Angels and demons relations with warlocks and witches will be pretty similar to what happens in Constantine, meaning they certainly face the same Major villains, but certainly won't help each other on a Major scale.

                    Oh, and I won't allow mage players, since I want to focus not only on the spy paranoia, but also on the existentialist parts of possessing a vessel and having to deal with its life and such. Maybe on future games on the same setting.

                            I hack Mage: The Awakening (OG) quite a bit.

                            Extended Casting rolls limited to a number equal to Gnosis.
                            Pretty self explanatory, keeps the crazy under control. I've seen this adopted in several MW games.

                            Scrutiny attempts per target limited to a number equal to Gnosis.
                            Ever had That Player? That Scrutinized everything, over and over, constantly?

                            Social Trait/Roll Boosting moved from Mind to Spirit
                            This is an interesting one that gives Spirit a bit more punch in the day-to-day as well as more evenly distributes the boosting. From a fluff standpoint, Spirit negotiations can be affected by auras, so maybe Spirit mages learned to cultivate/tweak their auras. But, mostly it's about the first two reasons. Spirit gains similar spells to the Mind versions and as a footnote Life gains Skill spells.

                            Overhaul of Spell Aspect System
                            This is obviously the biggest one. For many, many reasons the arbitrary and Mind-loving current system fails the type of game I want to play. Rather than addressing individual spells, I took it all the way to the top level classification that puts all of the Arcana on a level playing field and is easy to remember/reference:
                            • All 1 and 2 dot Spells are Covert.
                            • 3 dot Spells are Vulgar.
                            • 4 dot Spells are Vulgar and add +1 Paradox dice.
                            • 4 dot Spells are Vulgar and add +2 Paradox dice.

                            Anyway, hope someone might find this stuff interesting!

                                        I've found that personal horror and introspective grit get really tough to keep rolling as group size grows. I'll try to aim for a couple of challenging personal scenes for each PC per story arc, but other than that, I don't get worked up if the players take optimistic, goofy, even parody-feeling tangents. We all work long days, and if I can get my players turning red laughing at a sending-up an otherwise hyper-serious gloomy vamp, then I've had a damn good game session.

                                        Rules wise, we replaced Werewolf's notion of Renowns with Influences. Renown just didn't seem to make sense, for a pack of critter people who spend lots of time hiding and sneaking about. For our purposes, we added the Influence rating to the use of whatever Gifts are bound to it.

                                        We also wound up with a bit of a hybrid Harmony stat. The total of Harmony of Flesh and Harmony of Spirit is still used for Rites (we're doing basically 1st-ed CoD WtF) but each one can offer perks if you get them to four dots or more. Example perks would be free and reflexive shapeshift or partial change, or a bonus to pass through the Gauntlet, or bonuses to resist or control Kuruth.



                                        [Edit to add] Preparing for an upcoming Mage game I'd like to do, I was planning to reintroduce Avatar as an alternate to a Watchtower. I don't have a layout for the merit yet, but in general it would provide you a Supernal outlook, set of Arcana affinities, and so on. As a small downside, most Magi won't be able to relate to you as well to share Supernal understanding, and you may very well have an invisible friend hanging around in Twilight that other night folk could perceive.

                                            Originally posted by VoidDrifter View Post
                                            Everybody knows that, whether you call yourself a games master, a story teller, a dungeon keeper, whatever, if you're playing an RPG, you're likely to customize it. And with fluff and themes that can often be highly contentious, the Chronicles of Darkness are no exception to that rule. Since there's a whole bunch of threads scattered over these boards discussing different ways of making specific setting hacks work, I was wondering if anyone would be willing to share the ways they've tweaked and changed the Chronicles of Darkness to make them truly theirs?


                                            Hopefully not being rude here, my own answer to my question...

                                            For starters, I run my Chronicles of Darkness closer to Dark Urban Fantasy than pure Horror. There's still some horror in it, hence the "dark" part of that descriptor, but exactly what is horrifying and how much so depends on lot on where you're standing.

                                            The general approach to the setting is inspired by Unknown Armies, and can be summed up in two words: Occult Underground. The world humanity thinks it lives in is but the tip of the iceberg, and things get darker and scarier the deeper you dare to descend into the shadows. Past the Requiem Sanguine of the vampires, down through the hellish worlds of the spirits, the fae and the dead, past the resentful fragments of old gods and new, deeper and deeper until you reach the layer where beings of divine might conduct a subtly fought eternal war that ever threatens to tear the fabric of reality itself apart.

                                            From a mortal perspective, the setting has something of a Cosmic Horror motif, for a simple reason: Humanity Does Not Matter. I agree that the Old World of Darkness went too far with its description of the power and influence of supernaturals, but at the same time, I feel that the New World pulls too many of their fangs for my liking. My Chronicles have it that monsters aren't as a general rule, controlling everything - but that's because, as a general rule, they have better things to do. Mages examine mysteries that could redefine reality as humans know it. Vampires whirl away the eternity with feeding and intercine strife. Werewolves hunt ceaseless, as incapable of conceiving of an alternative as they are of physically stopping. Whatever problems monsters have with humanity is on a localized, individual level; across the world as a whole, what happens to humanity bothers supernaturals not a whit.

                                            This, then, gives Hunters a somewhat darker motif. Yes, the Vigil is a candle in the dark... but the thing about candles? They burn out. Pyrrhic victories, bittersweet endings, and the nagging query of "is this really worth it?" are the order of the day. Of course, a world is only dark if there is light to contrast it; many hunters do decide that, yes, this is worth it, and taking whatever victories you can is part of the theme as well.

                                            As you might guess, I make a significant use of Imperial Mysteries as a basis for "the god tier" of the Chronicles of Darkness, painting the higher worlds and the manifold powers who must meddle subtly, lest they unmake the world in their enthusiasm.

                                            Something inspired by a setting hack actually here in the Mage forum now, my setting has more Watchtowers than the core five. The core five are just the most common; every imaginable tripartite combination has its representative Watchtower. This alters the effect of False Awakening (Night Horrors: The Unbidden) slightly, with those rare scholars of the arcane who know of the Archmages wondering if it mightn't have been a failed gambit by an Archmage... or, worse, if it mightn't have somehow retroactively created the "lesser watchtowers" as equivalent exchange for burning itself into creation.

                                            There are a few other bits and pieces, but these are the main hacks I apply to my setting. So, what're yours?
                                            I use a similar setting approach to yours, although I've had no luck thus far getting a Chronicles game running. Also, I heavily hack the morality systems in ChroD in favor of the 3 goals approach in Mirrors since the system, especially for Mortals, seems arbitrary and punitive. Plus I include a character template for backstory designed by a friend for the initial write up and start with a mortal prelude(s). For some games I include other minor hacks as well, like for Demon the Fallen I was starting up an expanded list of common lores since it feels limited with only the 2 they provide in the core book.

                                                        Whatever I'm playing, I tend to alter the setting to suit the themes I'm going for. At present, I'm running a Hunter game based in 80s Miami. I'm going for something lighter and more escapist, with my guys playing loser career criminals trying to make their name in the underworld. The aesthetics of that era's TV an film as well as open world video games trump the realism of the world. As far as systems go, that means giving each of the characters skill tricks and focusing on an integrity system built more around their cowardice and weaknesses than a moral decline (killing isn't an automatic integrity trigger). I'm taking a TV-style structure, with each adventure serving as an episode (and I'm trying to get us to the point where we can have roughly an episode a session). I'm emphasizing that minor ambitions are the character's B-plot for a given episode, and that major ambitions are the season goal. That also means stripping out or majorly rehabilitating the social structures of existing groups.

                                                        Vampires don't have clans or an All-Night Society. Rather, they're organized by genealogy. The attempt is to replicate the melodramatic familial dynasties of daytime soap operas. Vampire politique, as it exists, are about the petty squabbles and power plays between a Cuban emigre club magnate and her brood of 80s club kids and the old money vampire's family embedded in city politics.
                                                        Werewolves don't have a vast cosmology defining their hunt. It's just something in their blood. Our main werewolves are a clan of redneck good ole boys who assist the smuggling operation for the Colombians.
                                                        Mages are just power hungry gits who dabble in the occult out of a Faustian urge to always have more. There's no Awakening. If you have the drive, the luck, and the right ambivalence towards your soul, you too can be a sorcerer.
                                                        The Created are lost lambs created through weird science. They destroy everything around them, but if there's hope for a pilgrimage, none of them seem to have found it yet. The primary Created antagonist is El Lo Hecho (The Creature), an experiment from an ex-Nazi doctor who cobbled together parts from executed ex-cons in a Cuban prison. He was sent over on the Mariel boatlift and operates as the hitman who gets called in when shit gets serious.
                                                        Changelings are lost boys and girls on the run from their old masters. They don't have a society to speak of, and the only real means they have to stay ahead of their previous captors is to run or hide. The True Fae mostly use surrogate slavecatchers like one of the aforementioned mages.
                                                        And then there's all sorts of creatures of the week and "other" monsters around who don't fit the core archetypes. I use a mix of dread powers and traditional core book powers and banes to keep my characters on their toes.

                                                        Decentralizing the different societies makes everything more manageable, allows me to more directly integrate them into the mortal underworld, and also allows me to present the compacts/conspiracies as more central and organized antagonists. Basically I strip out whatever's necessary and try to fit them into broad and easily recognizable cultural archetypes roughly from the era. Soap stars, Dukes of Hazzard, and the Lost Boys with some of my supernaturals, for instance; Ashwood Abbey as written by Bret Eaton Ellis, an edgelord Lucifurge named Damian Haight who wields double katanas and a trenchcoat, a branch of the Ascending Ones who have co-opted some of the feuding local gangs and turned their adversaries into a small zombie army.

                                                        Big. Dumb. Goofy. It's not my normal wheelhouse, but it's been a lot of fun.
