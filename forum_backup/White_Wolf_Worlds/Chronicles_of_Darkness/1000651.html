    Mix and Match! [Just For Fun]

    11-09-2016, 03:52 AM
    so, lately David has posted an interesting experiment in which he combined features of both Changeling and Vampire into a single, new "crossbreed" template called the Admonitoria, and in this spirit Caladriu made the Ravani. So, let who's in favor of brainstorming similar, literal corssover splats? For example, I assume that a vampire-mage crossover would be something similar to the Tremere before their meeting with the Father (you know, being a breath drinking monster or something like that). A vampire-werewolf may be something similar to CWoD's Abomination, but perhaps in a way that won't make me cringe. And, of course, a Beat-Sin Eater combo may be truly interesting to explore. If you feel really creative, you may even suggest some fan splat-canon splat mix up- like a Twice Born mummy being rebirthed into a Leviathan's body, or a Genius-Mage combination of pure madness. Even if we only count the canon gamelines, it already give us 45 different combos (I'm not counting Hunter, although the Lucifuge and perhaps few other conspiracies may be potential candidates). Actually, I wont even stop you from combining non-playable splats with playable one (like Idigam-Beast, or Hero-Sin Eater) as long as the outcome would be appropriately terrifying.

    So- what are your ideas for the such potential "crossbreeds"?

      Mortwights [name pending]: The One's Who Don't Stay Dead.

      Nicknames: Specters

      Description: Both Kindred and Sin-Eater scholars are perplexed by the strange hybrid creatures called Mortwights. They are Kindred, but are sustained through the deathly energies known as Plasm. They possess a Geist, but it's twisted into a being of endless hunger that seeks to consume the Psyche of the Mortwight. It feeds like a vampire, but also sups from the lingering dead and their mementos. And it can bring both Kindred Disciplines and Sin-Eater Manifestations to bear against those who threaten their eternal existence. All that can be said for certain is that these creatures are rare, for despite their myriad of powers all Mortwights lack the ability to Sire any others of their kind.

      Genesis: There are a few theories regarding how Mortwights came into being. The most popular theory amongst Kindred scholars is that the first of their kind were Revenants. The story is told like this: One night, a Revenant in the throws of Frenzy fell upon a young Sin-Eater, and in the feeding frenzy the Revenant somehow was able to Diablerize the Sin-Eaters Geist. This act gave the Revenant's Beast sentience, and transformed the wretch into the first Mortwight. Sin-Eaters however have a different theory in regards to these hybrid abominations: A Vampire most likely discovered an Avernian Gate and got lost in the Underworld. Over the centuries of existing in the land of the dead the vampire's physical form dissipated and it became something akin to a Vampiric Geist. Then, it simply made the Bargain with a poor mortal soul to return to the land of the living. Except rather than returning the nascent Sin-Eater to life it instead made them a living corpse that still feeds upon Plasm. There is also one final rumor though, whispered by the most fearful of Elders among the Kindred and the most experienced occultists of the Bound. They whisper that there are disturbing similarities between the Shadows of the Mortwights and the accursed Owls of Dis. Perhaps they were once just Sin-Eaters who found themselves invaded by the Strix, and then forever bound together to form a conduit of hunger for the Underworld. Whatever the case, the Specters exist. And they are incredibly dangerous to both themselves and world.

      Character Creation: Draft #01

      Clan: Mortwight; For all mechanical purposes the Mortwight is treated as being it's own Clan. However, it doesn't have any Clan Disciplines or a Clan Bane. Instead of a Clan Bane choose a Bane of your choice that doesn't conflict with your character's Threshold. That Bane will come into effect like a Clan Bane would when your character drops below Psychomachia 7 (More on that later.)

      Threshold: Chose a Threshold to represent the facet of death your Shadow, and to a lesser extent your character, represents. If your ST permits the Revenants Origin or Strix Origin the your character's Threshold becomes the same as the Sin-Eater you Diablerize/Merge with. Your character benefits from the associated Keys for the purpose of character creation, but afterwards they are considered to have no associated Keys.

      Archetype: For the purpose of Willpower recovery and story purposes a Mortwight may have any of the Sin-Eater Archetypes become Masks or Dirges.

      Mask, Dirge, and Silence: Mortwights choose a Mask and Dirge just like any other Kindred, except they must also choose a Silence. This functions like the Dirge for your character's Shadow, and will allow for the Shadow to both recover Willpower while in Shadow Play (more on that later) and can trigger breaking points for the Mortwight's Psychomachia. A Mortwight while in control doesn't recover Willpower for indulging their Silence, but they can gain the Dark Synergy Condition (more on that soon.) The same is true for the Shadow when it indulges in it's Mortwight's Dirge. Other than that, the Mask and Dirge function as normal.

      Vampiric Strengths and Weaknesses: These function normally with the exception of a few of them due to a Mortwight being powered by Plasm rather than Vitae. Other than that, Vampiric Powers like the Blush of Life and Blood Buffing run off of Plasm in a point by point basis. In instances where Humanity is referred just substitute it for Psychomachia, and where Blood Potency is referenced just substitute it for Tempest.
      > Blood Bonds: A Mortwight's blood is more akin to Plasm rather than Vitae, and as such they are unable to create Blood Bonds and Ghouls with it among the living (more on that soon.) They are still susceptible to being Blood Bound though, and more horrifying still is that it effects both Mortwight and Shadow regardless of who accepted the Blood Bond.
      > Frenzy: The biggest difference is that unlike regular Kindred, a Mortwight's Beast is sentient and has clear but alien objectives and plots of it's own. Rather than entering a regular Frenzy a Mortwight rolls to resist a Shadow Play. The rules of Shadow Play will be explained later, but functions similarly to the Beast Play sidebar in the VtR 2nd Edition Core Rulebook.
      > The Kiss: This functions almost identically to a regular Kindred's Kiss, with the exception that a Mortwight gain's Plasm rather than Vitae, and a Mortwight cannot cause the Swooning Condition from the Kiss. In addition, a Mortwight may target Ghosts and other beings of Plasm so long as they're Manifested or Possessing a physical host.
      > Torpor: When a Mortwight enters the Daysleep or Torpor, their spirit enters the Underworld. This time spent in the Underworld is only at a limited capacity since they will lack access to their Disciplines while in this spirit form, and is very dangerous since they risk violating the Old Laws during such visits. The only instance when this is not the case is when a Mortwight is Staked. Like an ordinary Kindred they will remain cognitive and bound to their physical form until the Stake is removed.
      > Final Death: There's a reason Mortwights are so feared by both Kindred and Sin-Eaters. They're nearly as hard to permanently kill as an Abmortal. If a Mortwight's hit boxes are completely filled with aggravated damage then it's physical form is destroyed but it's spirit and Shadow flee to the Underworld. After one full night and day, the Mortwight's body will reform around it's spirit in the Underworld at full health at the cost of 1 point of Tempest (more on that soon). If the Mortwight doesn't have one point to surrender then it still reforms at full health, except that is takes one year and a night to reform. During that time it's disembodied in the Underworld, it's spirit can be potentially bound just like any other Ghost. If that happens then the body doesn't reform and the Mortwight loses 1 point of Tempest for every day it's bound until it hit's Tempest 1. If it remains bound for a year and a night after that then it's Shadow dissipates and the Mortwight simply becomes a regular Ghost. If a Mortwight is ever destroyed while in it's disembodied form then it loses a point of Tempest and will reform somewhere else in the Underworld. If this would bring it to 0 Tempest then it's destroyed for good. However, if the Mortwight is brought to Final Death while in it's physical or spiritual form through fire, sunlight, or one of it's Banes then it's permanently destroyed.

      Sin-Eater Strengths & Weaknesses: Most of the Mortwight's Sin-Eater abilities function as normal with the exception of a few due to the the Curse that infects their body, soul, and Shadow. In instances where Synergy is referred just substitute it for Psychomachia, and where Psyche is referenced just substitute it for Tempest.
      > While a Mortwight will not fall to Torpor when their hit boxes are filled with lethal damage, they still must roll for Shadow Play as per the normal rules of Kindred Frenzy.
      > Mortwight's benefit from Kindred immunities when relating to poisons, narcotics, and diseases while not using the Blush of Health. When using it treat them as regular Sin-Eaters until the blush wears off.
      > A Mortwight cannot negate damage like a Sin-Eater can. They do however benefit from all the damage reductions of a Kindred, and may spend Plasm to heal like a Kindred spending Vitae.
      > A Mortwight cannot restore health by destroying a Memento or by murdering a mortal. They may gain Plasm by destroying a Memento in such a faction, and regain Plasm equal to twice the Memento's rating. They cannot gain Plasm by murdering a mortal at all, except of course through feeding off one like a normal Kindred of course.
      > Mortwight's benefit from the rebirth mechanics mentioned in the last section instead of those natural to Sin-Eaters.
      > A Mortwight may feed upon a Ghost's Corpus after it's been defeated like a regular Sin-Eater, but it yields less Plasm (Max is equal to the Ghost's Rank.) Feeding in this fashion is identical to feeding as a Kindred.
      > A Mortwight may attempt to Diabletize either Kindred or Geists. Doing so is identical mechanically to how a Kindred commits Ameranth, with the exception that Geist resist using Psyche or Rank depending on if they're Bound or Free. Sin-Eaters who are Disblerized die from the blood loss, although their Geist is either set free or consumed depending on whether the Mortwight succeeds or not. Mortwight's gain a free dot from a Geist's Manifestations from Diablerizing one, comparable to Disciplines gained from Diablerizing a Kindred. Attempting Amaranth is always a Psychomachia breaking point and a desire of all Shadows, and succeeding in Amaranth gives the Mortwight the Tainted Condition for a number of weeks equal to the Kindred's Blood Potency or Geist's Psyche or Rank.

      Disciplines, Blood Sorcery, and Coils of the Dragon: With the exception of having no Clan Disciplines, Mortwights may use most vampiric powers with the exception of certain Coils of the Dragon. At character creation a Mortwight gains 2 dots in non-Clan Disciplines, and if you ST allows the Revenant Origins then you may transfer over your Revenant's Disciplines to your new Mortwight character and gain a free dot to spend upon becoming a Mortwight.
      > The Coils of the Wyrm and Coils of the Voivodai don't properly function for a Mortwight. This is because the Coils of the Wyrm weren't designed to incorporate the Shadow's malicious intelligence, and because the Mortwights cannot create a Blood Bond with their own blood. That doesn't mean that Mortwight Dragons don't try to create new Coils to accommodate their unique existence, just that each night is a struggle against an equally brilliant nemesis from deep within themselves.

      Keys, Manifestations, and Ceremonies: A Mortwight begins play with a number of Keys and Manifestations comparable to a regular Sin-Eater. The difference is that Keys, Manifestations, and Ceremonies relating to fire and sunlight are off limits to all Mortwights, and that after character creation they possess no associated Manifestations. Otherwise these powers function normally. If your ST is willing to use the Strix Origin, then you may carry over any Keys, Manifestations, or Ceremonies your character knew as a Sin-Eater after your character becomes a Mortwight.

      Tempest: This power state functions nearly identically to that of a Sin-Eater's Psyche, with two noticeable exceptions. 1) A Mortwight suffers feeding restrictions equal to that of a Kindred as their Tempest rises, but only in regards to feed off the living and Kindred. 2) Thematically, rather than Psyche representing how a Sin-Eater and Geist grow and work together, a Mortwight's Tempest represents how powerful the conflict between the Mortwight and their Shadow has become. High Tempest Specters are walking storms that send ripples across Twilight and into the Underworld, as both sides spend centuries plotting and planning against each other for end that will take centuries to accomplish.

      Psychomachia: This represents a hybrid of both a Kindred's Humanity and a Sin-Eater's Synergy. Mortwights gain a free Touchstone at character creation tied to Psychomachia 6, and may purchase the Touchstone Merit from VtR 2nd Edition. However, these Touchstones need not be solely humans from the Mortwight's past as Psychomachia is more about which side of the Mortwight is truly Them rather than how much one still relates to the human condition. This is expressed in how like a Humanity Touchstone they can be used to resist a Shadow Play and can become detached from you depending on how low your Psychomachia goes. But unlike a Humanity Touchstone, these can be tied to inhuman concepts like the Underworld or even other supernatural creatures. So long as it reinforces the Mortwight's identity over the Shadow, then it can become a Touchstone. For should a Mortwight's Psychomachia ever reach 0, the Shadow permanently takes over control of the Mortwight.

      Shadow Play: - to be continued soon.


      That's all I've got for now. Boy has this been a long one.

          After seeing the Admonitoria, I've noticed that it works so smoothly because vampires and changelings are already so similiar in real-life theme and in-game mechanics. Both are predators that draw main sustenance from parts of humans. Both stabilize their psyches by making an effort to reconnect themselves to humanity. Both put up a disguise that hides their true nature from mortals. Both affirm their identities by acting on archetypical personalities. Both are addled by weaknesses drawn largely from superstition and old wives' tales.

          That said, my half-formed ideas are...
          • Mage + Vampire : Magic is Sacrifice. Not necessarily yours, though. Created when a nascent mage is Embraced during a Mystery Play, or when a vampire in torpor undergoes an Astral Journey into the Supernal. Integrity equivalent is Ethics - the remnants of internalized moral values of society. Gnosis increases with age, like Blood Potency. Gains Mana from drinking blood. Can diablerize mage souls. Uses the ten Disciplines instead of ten Arcana, but as broad purviews of abilities instead of a linear progression of five powers.
          • Werewolf + Bound : Spiritual liminality. Created when a nuzusul that dies during his First Change is approached by a Geist, or when the body of a mortal making the Bargain undergoes First Change as a subconscious defense mechanism. Integrity equivalent is Concordance. High Concordance represents the human psyche overpowering both Uratha instinct and the Geist's compulsions. Low Concordance represents becoming more of an wolf-monster piloted by a lesser death-god. Reaching to both the Shadow and the Underworld. Ceremonial rites that affect both spirits and ghosts.
          • Changeling + Demon : You no longer have a place to call home, so make it yourself. Created when a changeling attempts to barter away his Durance to a demon and succeeds, or when an angel Falls in Faerie. Integrity equivalent is Facade - the veil of humanity hiding you from both your pursuers and your own traumas. Rising Wyrd gives you more Frailties (replacing Glitches), but more Facades for you to hide behind. Building your Cipher gives you more Personal Approaches.

                                                  I know these are trite, but someone has to do them:

                                                  Vrykolakas, Dead Skin Wolves, The Cold Ones.

                                                  The Embrace has no right to work on one of the Uratha, creatures of flesh and spirit with their souls already called for, but sometimes the Curse is strong or insidious enough and a corrupted corpse raises as one of the strangest types of Revenants: a Vrykolakas, the Undead Werewolves, the Blood Hunters.

                                                  Rules:

                                                  Shapesifting: Vrykolakas can asume all five forms and benefit (and suffer) from all their special rules save the listed under "Healing and Physical Intesity", below. Substitute Blood Potency for Primal Urge to determine máx time in Gauru.

                                                  Blood and Bone: as an Uratha, Vrykolakas are defined by the Hunt.

                                                  Harmony and Touchstones: Vrykolakas must balance his undead existence with his werewolf nature.

                                                  Renown: Vrykolakas have the same sources and types of Renown as other werewolves, but it is granted by the Beast carving his flesh from the inside. Renown brands show up glowing red like those of the Pure. They can Flash Renown to reap its benefits.

                                                  Gifts and Rites: lacking in spirit, Vrykolakas only have access to Wolf Gifts, no Moon or Shadow Gifts or Rites of any kind. They cannot Reach.

                                                  Vitae: any cost in Essence is paid in Vitae 1:1

                                                  Blood Potency: Vrykolakas start at 1 and can increase and reduce it like Kindred. They can always feed from Kindred or other Werewolves.

                                                  Disciplines: Vrykolakas can only learn the basic Disciplines and do it as out of clan cost as they lack any Clan. They may learn the special tricks of Covenants only at the Storyteller's discretion.

                                                  Healing and Physical Intensity: Vrykolakas heal and suffer damage like Kindred. Gauru form doesn't grant autohealing, but lets you ignore BP limits. No Vitae used to heal in that form counts towards your Vitae/turn.

                                                  Frenzy: In addition to the usual sources of Frenzy, Vrykolakas list all basic Kuruth Triggers and one list of their choice as additional Frenzy triggers. In addition to the usual physical bonus of Frenzy, they also substract their BP of any attempt to control their mind or emotions while frenzied. They remain Frenzied for the same period of time as Basu-Im as per their Harmony rating.

                                                  Banes: Vrykolakas suffer from both the increased Silver Bane as one of the Pure, plus the common Banes of Kindred. They can take additional Banes like Kindred to inhure themselves

                                                  Kindred Senses: as usual, they stack with Wolf Senses when appropiate.

                                                  Embrace, Blood Sympathy, Ghouls and Vinculum and Vitae Adiction: All attempted embraces by a Vrykolakas result in a regular Revenant, which applies the increased Blood Sympathy to his Sire. Vrykolakas cannot create ghouls or establish Vinculums but his Vitae is as addictive as that of any other Kindred.

                                                  Predatory Aura: As kindred.
