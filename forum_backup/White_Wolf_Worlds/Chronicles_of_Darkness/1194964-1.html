        From the RPGNet thread:
        Each player creates a certain kind of vampire that appeals to them (helping them find a concept that appeals to them, and the game widgets that go along with that concept, is the lion's share of the elevator pitch for running a game, I find). The players are encouraged to consider their survival strategy--now that you've been a vampire for a little while and you've got a grasp of what that means, how do you make it from night to night? That varies a lot from character to character, based on what Backgrounds they purchased, how they have decided those are going to work together, and what kind of character concept they're going for. All of this joins together to give each character a fairly tight set of overall long-term goals.

        Example: Player 1 is very into the horror aspect of the game. He wants to be a classic Gothic vampire, monstrous and deformed, stalking the shadows and preying on the helpless. He chooses a Nosferatu vampire. He buys two dots of the Domain Background, and defines that as territory in the city sewers. He buys two dots in Herd and two dots in Contacts, and decides that this represents a network of homeless people that he has a good deal of influence over. He blows four dots in Retainers and defines those as ghouled animals who do his bidding and act as his eyes and ears. So this Nosferatu has a defined territory, a relatively safe base of operations from which he can strike out. And he has a number of servants to act as his sources of information. If something is happening in the part of town above his sewer territory, he is probably the one vampire in town most likely to know about it. A very typical Nosferatu, with a fairly sound survival strategy.

        Player 2 wants to wield wealth and power--none of this skulking about in the sewers for her. She chooses Ventrue for her Clan, and buys two dots of Influence, and four dots each of Resources and Contacts. She decides that she is a scion of an old-money family that has been a factor in local politics for generations, ans she inherited many of her father's interests when he died (which is probably why she was chosen for the Embrace). She lives in a large and very old mansion, and she's one of the local kingmakers--you don't get to be Mayor unless you have her blessing. She's often throwing charity events in her lavish home (though she's started doing this through trusted mortal proxies lately), spreading around lots of money and giving out lots of minor favors to maintain her influence over the local power structure. She owns the local newspaper outright through a shell corporation, and although she is a silent partner and the paper itself is largely a dying legacy medium, she can make an inconvenient story just disappear every once in awhile when she really needs to. She has a minor but comfortable place in the local Camarilla hierarchy--basically, she's a pretty average Ventrue Neonate.

        (If you're wondering how all of that gets established, it's Session Zero give-and-take between the GM and the players, often in the form of the GM looking at the dots on the character sheets and asking what the player has in mind for them. "You have two dots in Domain, what does that represent?" "I thought it could be, like, a distinct territory in the city sewers." "How do you maintain control over it?" "I'm a scary Nosferatu; also, my Contacts and Retainers." "So your guys and your beasts don't do much for you except maintain your territory, then?" "And gather information for me." "Okay, that works for me. Now then--what does this Influence represent?" "She's from an old money family who has been a factor in local politics for, like, ever." "Okay, that's fine. How does she fit into the local Vampire hierarchy?" "I thought maybe she could own a newspaper?" "That's not as big a deal as it used to be, you know." "Yeah, but in an old-fashioned small city like this, I figured it might still be worth something." "It's kind of an important thing to let a Neonate have control over--the other Ventrue are only going to let you keep it if you are very open-minded about cooperating with them." "Yeah, I figured. I really see her as the type who sucks up to her superiors, and bullies her inferiors. I figure she ought to be kissing up to the local Ventrue Elders every chance she gets." "Okay, that sounds good, I can work with that.")
        I'm bringing that over in case people don't have time to read it, they can at least glance at it.

        This seems to be a really good example of a game with player agency, of being able to come up with and play the character they were wanting. Pretending these players are real, this is really great. On the one hand, I have players thinking about their characters, what they know, and what they want. On the other hand, I don't have them coming together as a group. That puts more work on me to constantly have connections forced onto the characters.

        I think that's why I like how the Dresden Files RPG does character creation because baked into it are connections to the other characters as well as defining known locations for those characters.

        Thanks again for the replies! I really appreciate it!

The idea is to show the descent into that, to start out still seeing your old girlfriend once and a while, and slowly watching the terrible things that being a vampire makes you do change your mindset, until you suddenly realized that you just snapped a man's neck and mindwiped your girlfriend multiple times, and you know what you don't even care.

                                        The horror isn't in just BEING a monster. The horror is in BECOMING a monster, in being able to see the difference between then and now, in knowing that it was always inevitable, in seeing how naive you once were in thinking you could still hold on to your humanity.

                                            I'd like to point out another obstacle for the adoption of personal horror in an RPG table environment is that it requires a heavier commitment from players into the proverbial suspension of disbelief in order to achieve a greater sense of immersion in the game.

                                            Not every player is prepared for that (some people can't stand scary movies and all their sub-genres). It's also something that comes more easily with with practice to ST and players alike. STs need to learn the kind of thing that may afflict characters on a deep emotional level, balancing on the razor's edge for avoiding actually presenting them with unacceptable situations. Thus if the player is in video-game mindset, this is unlikely to come to pass.

                                            Players need a little bit of directing by that point. It's why I love conditions. I can hand out a card to a player that reads they're freakin' spooked: they'll learn to act the role, practice some method acting, get immersed more easily.
