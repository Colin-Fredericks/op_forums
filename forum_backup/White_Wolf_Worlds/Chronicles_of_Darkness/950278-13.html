                Alright, there's a lot of stuff in the Chapter 1 post, much of which I agreed with, but the part that's most relevant to me is the last section, so I'll mostly respond to that.

                Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                TL;DR-What Can We Do to Make Genius Good?
                "Genius is cheap self-congratulatory inspiration porn, fight me"
                Not gonna lie, this is probably the very best line in the entire thing.

                Make Inspiration the Divine Fire.
                I honestly had this as personal headcanon for a long time, and while I probably don'tthink it should be made quite so explicit as to outright state it, I do think Genius could benefit from playing up Inspiration as an actual force in the world.

                Commit to Science.
                I actually want to go in the opposite direction. It's gonna be hard to get away from the science fetishism is we keep things clinging too closely to science, and there's a lot of room for Inspired artists and creative types. There's a lot of different types of "Genius" other than nerds with sliderules. People whose fevered sculptures come to life, songs that enthrall those who hear, politicians with nonsensical plans of office that keep the nation afloat anyway, a martial artist who has perfected a technique lethal to victim and user.
                You said a lot about intelligence fetishism too, and maybe making the Genius umbrella cover more than just intellectual disciplines could help with that.

                Really, though, that's just my opinion on it, and if science remains the focus, it could still certainly work.

                Break the World.
                The Magnum Opus as an important was one of the first ideas I threw out there, and I'm glad you agree. Breaking the World is probably something that Genius so desperately wanted to do, given the pulp action angle, and it'd be nice for that to actually be possible, even if on a small scale.

                Fight the Chaos.
                This is definitely a neat angle to take for the "why" of Genii, if not the "what." Ultimately, the what is a way less important detail in the long run.

                Don't Sweat Mage.
                While I've been looking a little at Mage mechanically, since they have similarly expansive and freeform powers, I really agree. Mages are philosophers who take truths and fit the world to them. Genii should take the world and try to get some approximation of truth.

                Kill the Statistics.
                I agree completely. Yes, yes, yes! Hell, some might argue that Inspiration would be more likely to be found in the marginalized groups, seeing as they're usually the ones who get screwed over enough to be that mix of desperate and driven, but that might have some unfortunate implications of it own.

                Kill the Money.
                I think it'd be a good idea to replace the Resources-based stuff with actual Roleplaying-based requirements ("your fancy freeze ray needs a giant diamond to work, Doctor Fries."), and I'll probably use the crafting mechanics as a start point.

                Bind Them to Humanity.
                I think one thing we'll be doing, for starters, is making the Catalysts more about people, and tying them into that. Grimms aren't angry at the world, they're angry at the Board of Directors, the Ethics Committee, and those pussies with their high-powered lawyers! Staunens don't travel the cosmos for funsies, they love seeing their normal buddies react to the crazy aliens and you geometrically impossible time machine.

                Redefine Lemuria.
                I actually kind of ignored Lemuria entirely when I played, because I could never figure how the hell they were supposed to rule anything. Maybe instead of having a unified philosophy of any kind, Lemuria is purely a construct of convenience, an archaic system of feudal hierarchy that offers the big guys quality lackeys and offers the little guys protection.

                Restructure the Foundations.
                Foundations felt weird to me. Y-splats have usually been about outlook, but the Foundations are more about discipline and area of study. Yeah, a lot of Y-splats do that, but even relatively focused ones like, say, the Ordo Dracul have a lot of room conceptually. You could have a vampiric alchemist and an undead psychiatrist and a supernatural geneticist and they could all be under the Ordo banner. Meanwhile, the Foundations feel at least a little narrow. I might just be because of the way they're written, though.

                Get Creative With the Sciences.
                I mentioned this earlier with "Commit to Science," but opening up Inspiration to things other than science helps. Inspiration definitely stands on the fringes of human endeavor, so kooky fringe science, radical political theory and experimental art should definitely be the main deal. Some time and space will definitely be devoted to that.

                Get More Global.
                I agree, Genius is real eurocentric a lot of the time. That said, as an American white cis male, I might not be the best one to push this to where it needs to be.

                Scrub the History.
                Ol' buddy ol' pal, if that's the case, you are gonna hate the time travel section. But rest assured, that's binned.

                Ditch the Insanity.
                You're right. And I may not have been sufficiently mindful of that in working on 2E. But I'm definitely thinking decoupling "crazy" from "Genius" is a necessary step.

                AND ABOVE ALL ELSE!

                Ignore This Asshole On Whatever Point You Want If You Disagree With Him.
                Honestly, that would be a real Unmada thing for me to do. You gave a lot of criticisms, and it's clear you don't like the work, but it's also clear that you're giving it a fair shot, and all the deuces you've dropped on it are pretty well-deserved.

                PS: Ayoko Von Schreber is so fake a name I wouldn't be surprised if it actually went right back around to being real.
                I won't lie, I googled it. "Von Schreber" is a name, "Ayoko" is a name, but "Ayoko Von Schreber" is not that I could find.

                Genius: the Transgression 2E is a thing that's being worked on.

                                            I know we've already decided to move on how to fix Genius, but I wanted to quick revisit something.

                                            Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                            So, I was watching a review of Rosencrantz and Guildenstern Are Dead, link here, and it reinforced a notion I had sort of worked with while reading Genius, that it is not nihilistic but absurdist. At it's heart, the conflict seemingly revolves around trying to define a reality that is summarily can't be defined, and that the force the drives them to do so can't take no for an answer. The question, then, is how does the Genius act to enter this state, and what do they do once they have arrived?

                                            And here I admit I'm surprised: The absurdist streak in Genius is worn proudly on it's sleeve. The Venture Brothers and MST3K quotes were the biggest tip-off. (But then, to be fair, you probably haven't read that far.)

                                            To me, what Genius is trying to do is mine horror from the absurdity. Some people won't respond to it, and that's fine, but for me horror and absurdity can be (and often are) opposite sides of the same coin. They only differ in approach. That takes a certain mindset to appreciate, but I'd argue that that mindset is what draws the more rabid fans to it. Let me give you an example of what I'm talking about: The Lemurians.

                                            Now, to understand where I'm coming from, I have to introduce the concept of "gamer of a certain age." There was a time before geek hobbies were so widely accepted, a time before the Internet blew everyone's minds with geek overload. Those were magical, horrible times. Growing up, you carefully floated geek references to things, because if the people around you recognized it as geek-oriented, you'd get shamed for it. Sort of like living in a John Hughes/Steven Spielberg movie. So you developed a sort of "hanky-code for geeks", and those who got it were part of your hidden tribe. Instant friends!

                                            But then you went to college, and/or moved out of your parent's house. Time to get a new social network. So you used your hobbies to break the ice. For roleplaying, this introduced you to God's special one-off humans on a disturbingly regular basis. (Google Al Bruno III binder of shame for examples.) It became a social network of sorts, just one where dysfunctionality (sort of) greased the wheels. This was before we all internalized "no gaming > bad gaming", because not all of us were convinced that we'd find others who would like us without having to endure at least some dysfunction in the relationship.

                                            So while you express disbelief that Lemuria, as written, could possibly work, gamers-of-a-certain-age remember living through it in their gaming groups past and maybe present. Thus, they swallow it uncritically. Consider a Genius's point of view: When I tell people about my enthusiasms, they think I'm spouting gibberish. When I introduce them to my work, somehow things always go haywire in the most embarrassing way. When I tell people of things I've seen, things I've learned, they shy away from me. Maybe the cops get called. The only people I can interact with are people who do what I do, see what I see. They don't understand me, and I don't understand them, but they know what I'm going through, and sometimes that's enough. Meanwhile, real life keeps piling bills and obligations outside my door, and I don't know what to do. I don't want to function in the real world, and maybe I can't. And I'm deeply afraid that this is forever, the rest of my life.

                                            Do you now see how people can be enthused by Genius? Do you see how people can believe that some group like Lemuria can function? Do you understand why the absurdity is so front-and-center? Do you now know what I mean when I say that horror and absurdity are the opposite sides of the same coin? Do you now see the hell I endu...Never mind. Hopefully, I've made my point.

                                            Regardless, we all agree that Genius needs some work, some de-absurdification and cynicism-draining, and a sincere approach to the experience of actual geniuses trying to cope in a world that can't or won't recognize them while alive, and might not recognize them after they're dead. And there's numerous problematic things besides. But with the proper lens, it'll help to understand the basic work as-is, and help translate it into the game we want to play. Just some thoughts.

                                                        For the record I am too young to be "of a certain age" and I never interpreted Geniuses as suffering because mortals misunderstood them.

                                                        Quite the opposite, I felt that in Genius: the Transgression mortals understand the Inspired perfectly. When they fear a Genius or laugh at their theories, they're absolutely correct.

                                                        Originally posted by The Genius Website
                                                        "They all laughed at my theories! They called me mad! But now I'll have my revenge!"

                                                        If only it were so simple. They laughed because you WERE mad. They laughed because your inventions crumbled when unveiled and your theories turned to gibberish. You wept when you saw your equations riddled with childish errors.
                                                        And Geniuses don't fail through no fault of their own. They fail because raw inspiration doesn't come with good project management skills, and often because their ideas are fundamentally bad in the first place. There's an entire group (Seattle's Atomists) who want to create a utopian fascist police state governed by all-seeing technology. That plan's garenteed to fail because "utopian fascist police state" is an inherently self-contradictory concept.

                                                        Incidently, if they were player charachters instead of antagonists and I was the storyteller I wouldn't have them fail to build it. Frankenstein's monster turned against him, not remained lifeless on the slab. I would (assuming the players do well) have their master computer take full control by the end of act 2; act three would be all about dealing with the inevitable consequences of living in a fascist police state. Alternatively their master computer decides that the only way to make a utopia is to ditch the fascism.


                                                        This all requires a fair amount of player by-in. You have to choose an unrealistic goal, or create a charachter who's likely to screw up on a reasonable goal. But there's nothing wrong with requiring by-in.

                                                            Arc, I'm trying, but this is like...I just can't "get" what you're saying.

                                                            I don't understand why you object to the continued assertion to "what geniuses do isn't real science". It's a personal horror game. The idea of being a scientist and suddenly being unable to do actual science is genuinely horrifying, and it's one of the most brilliant bits of personal horror I've seen in any ChroD line.

                                                            And Lemuria? They're among my absolute favorite antagonists in any fiction, because they're so obviously wrong yet still so seductive- if you were a Genius, wouldn't you want to say "fuck reality, I'm right!"?

                                                            And the "science fetishism" thing...yeah. For one thing, that comic is smug and kind of douchey. Imagine if you shared a fun science fact with someone and they shot you down like that. Would you have any desire to interact with that person? "Hey, did you know that chameleons don't actually change color for camouflage?" "Ugh, you dumb science poser. I bet you don't even own a lab." You yourself point out how often it harps on how what Geniuses do isn't science- so of course it's going to be a fetishized, false form of science. Because that's what mad scientists do, especially in horror fiction! Horror fiction basically treats "mad science" as magic, and Genius takes that to a beautifully absurd conclusion by crafting a world built on science-as-magic.
