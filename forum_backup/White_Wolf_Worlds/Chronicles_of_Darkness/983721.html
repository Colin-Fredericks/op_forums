    Mixing up Antagonists

    10-07-2016, 11:26 PM
    Aside from "evil" versions of themselves, each of the Supernaturals has a specific opponent they face: Vampires have the Strix, Werewolves have the Hosts/Ridden/Idigam, Mages have Abyssal Entities, Sin-Eaters have...who/whatever they have to deal with. But suppose they occasionally run into each others' enemies? Who would you be most interested in seeing go up against each?
    Are there any you think each would be particularly effective or ineffective against?

    Kindred vs. Heroes? Mages vs. Idigams? Changelings vs. Pandorans? Beasts vs. Gentry? Go nuts!

          It's hard to imagine anyone really dealing with an idigam.

          I've put some Pandorans in other games but you have to fidget the rules around since they go dormant unless there's a Promethean nearby, after some time.

          Claimed work pretty easily across the board. Ridden and urged too.

          The hosts mesh well with a few. Hunter, vampire, and mage at least. They're capable of being a city-destroying threat, but still be encountered individually and in swarms without them ripping peoples souls from their bodies like Pez from a dispenser. As an added benefit they are very insidious foe, and can take over touchstones and other mortal contacts. Nothing keeps them from hollowing out supernaturals, either. They certainly could be a threat in Promethean, Changeling and Demon but I think it would sort of diminish the themes of the games. I could see Beasts working along with them against werewolves.

                                      I just finished a Mage chronicle where I used several Strix as antagonists. The Mages were trying to prevent the escape of one of the Bound, with all of the tragedy that would entail, while the Strix, who are drawn to imminent disaster, where doing there best to make sure the show did go on. What worked best was that the Strix are neither Spirits or Ghosts, meaning they didn't fall neatly into the cookie cutter holes the Mages tried to stuff them in. It was nice not being able to wave their wands and make all of their problems disappear.
                                      ​I did throw Pandorans at some Mages in my first chronicle but that was just because the Mages came between them and their Promethean prey. All the Mages really needed to do was step out of the way but they didn't know that.
                                      ​Mixing the antagonists like this can be difficult, but the fact that they weren't tailor made for the abilities of the splat makes them more challenging, more of an unknown.

                                                          Sublimati are Pandorans that have achieved intelligence and sapience by consuming Vitriol. Huntsmen are from Changeling second edition, they bring back Changelings. Stirges are strix, shadow-owls that delight in tormenting and teaching vampires. Unfleshed are Prometheans made of entirely artificial structures, such as a robot.

                                                          I don't believe the Gentry did attempt to take other supernaturals in first edition. They were aware of them but presumably they wouldn't be as malleable as a raw human.

                                                          In werewolf, the beshilu and azlu aren't fans of one another.

                                                          Bale Hounds and Scelesti wouldn't like each other. The Abyss and Inferno are apparently at odds. The Abyss wants to destroy what is for what they are, the Inferno just wants to corrupt it. You can't corrupt if it isn't there.

                                                          I imagine the Centimani would work well with the Pure, providing the situation was right. Also the Circle of the Crone and, of course, Beasts.
