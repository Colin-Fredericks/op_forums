        Originally posted by LostLight View Post
        One of the reasons why I feel that saying "the Contagion is a result of corrupted infrastructures" was a very strange and limiting choice. There are so many example for contagious infections through the CofD, and trying to connect them all to the God Machine kinda ruins some of their flavor. For example, having Malkavia a Contagion Strain would mean that that if we wish to stop the Contagion we need to think how the God Machine interacts with the vampires, which kind of infrastructure was corrupted in order to bring forth that plague and combine all kinds of God Machine related phenomena instead of focusing around vampires and their lore. The same for the Geryo, on that note. Instead of focusing on the horrors of supernatural powers getting out of control and the terrors of the Contagion's corruption directly, I need to shove the God Machine into everything- and while I really like the big screw head in general, it makes me feel kinda "meh".
        Gonna point out, albet much-belated, that the book does not imply a causal relation from "God-Machine" to "Contagion" any more strongly than any other theory about where the thing that upsets the apple cart comes from.

        This is the vector for a chronicle with a unified focal element for crossover between games whose otherworldly elements have widely varying degrees of connection to and involvement with the world; Mage's cosmology gives the broadest granular view, but the Exarchs inhabit a part of the setting that can only interact with the Phenomenal World through agents, and Mummy's similar zoomed-out lens on cosmogony doesn't undo the fact that the Judges are beyond approach. Meanwhile, Demon's pretty clear that the God-Machine is physical, fallible, and global; if anything was going to serve as the incubator for a many-faceted disease of reality, it'd be the sum collection of Infrastructure, which takes many forms and accomplishes many things but is always weird and always subject to tampering from outside.

        It's not that the Contagion comes from the God-Machine, it's that the Contagion spreads through everything, and in a crossover game like the Contagion Chronicle is build to be, the God-Machine is a particularly well-distributed host superorganism for the Contagion to grow and fester within. When you have a supernatural sickness that takes many forms and spreads through supernatural means, the big buggy magic engine that makes reality work the way it does doesn't get an exception clause built into it for the same reason that demons interact weirdly with Begotten Kinship.

        The games need to interact with each other in a compatible fashion, and "the God-Machine exists but the Contagion hasn't ever managed to insinuate itself into this notoriously-flawed system and thrive, especially not near all the parts that fuck around with time and space" makes demons occupy a weird position in a setting where they are by-default assumed to exist alongside all the other gamelines' characters.

            When every Contagion example is centered around an infect Infrastructure, when the views of the Factions revolves around the God Machine being sick, when the guidelines for designing a Contagion speak around revolving around an infected Infrastructure as the source of the Contagion, when Antarctica, the place of pure Contagion, is all about a corrupted machine from before the God Machine, when the developer of Promethean's Nigh Horrors (or one of the writers) says they don't want to make references to the Contagion in their book to avoid referencing the God Machine, when the developers and writers of the book tell you that the God Machine being sick is how the Contagion spreads and there is literally no counterpoint for that explanation through the whole book and everyone just agree that yes, the God Machine exists and it being sick is how everyone gets infected, then you need to have a very creative explanation to say that the God Machine being sick is not the source of the Contagion. The only thing which somehow stands as a counterpoint is the Geryo strain, and even then the writers said that the intention is that the Geryo are tied to the Contagion, and Shunned by the Moon even says that if the Geryo strain is a part of the Contagion, the God Machine was a part of it.

            So yeah, the God Machine is everywhere and sends its tentacles all around the world. I know it, and I even like it- like I said, I do like the big screw head. And yes, having it as the source of the Contagion could help to tie all the splats together- but with Demon being out, the God Machine became a central piece of a certain gameline. Not that I have complain. However, once you do it, and you make it that the God Machine being sick is the Contagion (in practice, at the very least, as again- all Contagion instances require that you start with a corrupted Infrastructure or some other broken part of the Machine), it becomes way too big then it should be. There is no instance of, for example, a Neithian Architecture structure getting corrupted without the touch of the Machine, or that Hisil gets infected and corrupted without a shining gear out there, or anything like that. Personally, I think that the source of the Contagion should have being a mystery- supernatural powers getting out of control, and people get sick by the strange powers of the Contagion. Instead of saying "the God Machine carries the Contagion and spreads it through the world", the book should have said - in my opinion- "not even the God Machine can resist the corruption of the plague". Instead of styling infected Infrastructures as the source, they should have been styled as the victims. IMO, the Contagion should have been its own thing, which can infect any supernatural being, can show up everywhere, and that can also infect the God Machine. It should have been another mystery, another horror, something that stands on its own so that I could use it without needing to even say the word "Infrastructure" in the game unless I want to. But that's not really the case.

            In short, when it was made clear that "the God Machine being sick" was the intention of the writers and developers, it also means that if you want to use the Contagion in your game in the way that the writers have intended, you need to put the God Machine in the center of the problem. I think that the book would have been better if it would have taken a different path, such as stating outright that the Contagion is an independent thing. In that way, for example, Father Wolf wouldn't need to "dabble with the powers of the God Machine" in order for the Geryo to be a Contagion strain, it could just be the effect of Father Wolf himself being infected, perhaps the result of his weakening, or the sickness of the Plague King, or something like that without having to need to say the words "God Machine" in a Werewolf book, just so that you could say "Contagion".

            Again, all personal opinions and such. Tl, dr- I would have preferred to have a less strong connection between the Machine and the Contagion, and that I could, by canon and supported by canon, to have one without the other.

                The Contagion, whatever its actual origins, is a thing that warps reality around it and is capable of infecting pieces of the God-Machine, a massive interconnected and inscrutable entity capable of meddling with the flow of time particularly to ensure its own continued existence.

                The Geryo, whatever the truth of their origin, are ancient primal entities whose power is imprecise or ill-contained enough that the collateral damage they inflicted upon reality led Father Wolf to lock them deep within the Border Marches to sleep for an age.

                The Begotten, whom the Geryo strain can infect as easily as werewolves, draw their powers from a primal source filtered through human mythology, and that source is the same part of the world-soul that werewolves are immune to the deleterious effects of by virtue of their connection to the wider world.

                The God-Machine, inclined to secrecy though it is, taps into volatile powers that can have a massive impact on reality if handled improperly and splinters off bits of itself constantly as it does industrial magic, opens and seals gates to other worlds, and alters the inhabitants of the universe in ways that are typically impossible without the long run-up of preparation that cheats physics just so, and it does this at least in part by prognostication and time travel in a setting where ideas can be literally infectious and the physical world can be affected by the stuff of pure thought.

                If you are playing in a Contagion Chronicle game, you are dealing with a setting where the God-Machine exists and is active at the same time as a thing that can affect it as readily as it can affect anything else; the book is clearly written under the assumption that the timeframe players are engaging with is the modern day rather than a historical period like those found in Dark Eras, and even if this was not the case the nature of both the Contagion and its largest host make it plain that assuming the historical origin of the Contagion is tied to the God-Machine just because the God-Machine is definitely sick with it and its purest manifestation appears to have come into the setting through devices the God-Machine tried to eat is a shaky assumption at best.

                If you are playing in a game where the Geryo and the Contagion as it is presented in its Chronicle book both exist, you are playing in a game where the volatile underpinnings of reality are visibly subject to breakdown in a way that is capable of spreading from host to host and even leaping to completely separate vectors, and also a diverse group of world-shaking primordial entities have been freed from their imprisonment within the foremost wall between worlds in the setting (the one that keeps the world of teeming spiritual power from overtaking the physical world as a replacement for the border zone created by the god of boundaries and destroyed/sealed off by that same being's death).

                The Night Horrors book for Promethean — a game that has, historically, had a lot of tangling up between its core metaphysical Principle and the God-Machine and which includes in its Second Edition corebook a sidebar positing potential relations between them that nevertheless identifies them as separate entities as surely as the Demon Storyteller's Guide's section on Prometheans does — not making references to the Contagion Chronicle (which is a book dealing with the Contagion in a global context as a thing that has already spread through the God-Machine) does not have any bearing on where the Contagion comes from just because the developers didn't want to get too deep into this thing that exists as its own thing with a big interconnected crossover context attached to major outbreak events in a book whose contents exist to provide antagonists, story hooks, and ludonarrative expansions to the game Promethean: the Created in particular.

                The Contagion exists in a context where it can break the normal rules of reality, disappear from a region for decades at a time, and be completely unheard of until its purpose-built wardens and stewards reach out to the locals in the midst of an outbreak to share what they know. Most of the gamelines make that difficult to plausibly attach to a unified phenomenon that can wind up halfway across the world instead of a series of isolated incidents across the globe. Demon's primary antagonist does not have this problem, because it is a mysterious interconnected edifice that does weird bullshit all the time but generally has an agenda counter to the destruction of reality itself, and the God-Machine is already correctly assumed to be in a constant state of cold war with its rogue operatives and sometimes itself.

                The examples in the book mention the God-Machine because the book is for use with all the gamelines and Demon is one of the lines where the scope of the bigger picture lends itself to events in Antarctica being related to events in San Francisco, but a Contagion outbreak in a place with no established Sworn or False power bases is going to be largely indistinguishable from any other weird one-off incident until the characters pull the thread or let it fester. If you want to use the Contagion without using the larger context it's assumed to inhabit, that's a problem you've created for yourself, but it's entirely possible for you to bring in an instance of the Contagion through vectors other than its most prominent and most active one if you're just using it as seasoning in a gameline that defaults to local or regional scope.

Wanted to make something out of it, and wanted to shape your community.

                                            I'll stop talking about the delivery systems themselves, but by this comparison, it would be fucking weird to not address the God-Machine as a carrier. The G-M is deliberately a system that involves itself in the lives of every fucking thing, whether they notice it or not. It has more reach in the mortal, material world(that thing that every splat participates in) than the Curses in the Blood, Spiritual Resonance, Supernal Resonance, Azothic Connection, the fucking Wyrd, any Compact or Conspiracy system outside of maybe YouFuckingTube, The Twilight Network, Lifeweb, or Primordial OR Bright Dream by themselves. Combinations of these might approximate that reach as well, but limit the degree of influence as well-the G-M is pretty universal in it's own terms of own materials, from the physical to the emotional, psychological, and spiritual, where as everything else finds some kind of barrier.

                                            You can make an argument that the G-M was relied on a hair too much, but you can't deny the reason why the G-M, as a infiltrator and contaminant-in-it's-own-right, is a hyper-relevant means of contamination in the world. Maybe one out of thirteen could get away with it, but with fucking affecting the commonal base reality more so than anything else so pervasively, the G-M just makes sense.

                                            And mind you, that's it as a carrier, because at no point does being the carrier make you the god damn origin, please think on this for a second I implore you.

                                            Because seriously, none of that delivery system analogy pins the blame of origin on the delivery system. The Contagion has the freedom to be from anywhere-but it's unrealistic that it's delivery system doesn't have something to do with the God-Machine unless the Contagion in question is specialized.

                                            I have five Contagions I have boiling in the background of my two more pertinent projects, and without even glancing at them, I could tell you all of them, each uniquely crafted, could be communicated by the G-M and, likewise, have nothing to do with the G-M asides from the convience of it as a carrier. It would, therefore, be weird not to account for that.
