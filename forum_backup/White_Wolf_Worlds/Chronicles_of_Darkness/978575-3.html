                                            Too interesting a proposition, so little time. I'll let myself post without reading 4 pages first.

                                            VAMPIRES: For Requiem Kindred, the distinction is simple. They are quite used to numerous origins to their kind, and even to the eventual "first vampire" showing up. Their society is based more on the fact that by sheer coincidence their curses work in very similar ways. So, no big deal. Partially.

                                            There is one thing Requiem Ones would find surprising, and maybe of concern. The way Cainites have an incredibly stable Blood Potency, to the point of it being partially hereditary. It is so stable, in fact, that those vampires call it Generation, and have a hard way to deal with their own rare deviations on the rule (like Diablerie). They would need some time to get used to it, but not a big deal, since they are already used to always begin unlife in the "shallowest" blood, and knowing that one day they will grow in power, Diablerie or not.

                                            Masquerade Cainites would have a harder time. First, for most the "newcomers" would defy all they know about the Curse, doesn't matter what legend about Kindred origin they believe. For most, though, it wouldn't make a great difference by itself. Blood Potence, though, would be a far greater concern for individual Cainies. It means that Generation isn't nearly as relevant to the Requiem Ones, that they don't have Weak Blooded in the same way, and that their elders can be even more fearsome, because they can achieve lower "generations"just with time. That's frightening from a Masquerade perspective.

                                            Since we're not discussing rules, I don't see the point on comparing other characteristics of the two. Distinctions on senses, and specially on Disciplines, can be simply waived for now as distinctions on rules, not setting.

                                            WEREWOLVES: Here the first connudrum comes with the very notion of contact, because both would be very attentive of such "world collision". Once we get through this, there is still all sorts of problems. The Garou have the Pack as their social basis, that is true, but also the Caern and Sept, two institutions the Uratha lack altogether, and that would be the first point of strangeness. The Garou would find the Uratha unorganized, innefective and more of a strange kind of spirit than kin, like just another kind of Fera. The Uratha would find the Garou obsessive with control and with war.

                                            They would probably be able to make alliances or enter conflict in a case-by-case basis, and none would ever trust the other entirely, because of the disregard of one by the rules of the other. Uratha don't see the world directly in terms of Wyrm, and hardly cares about things outside pack territory. Garou not only don't care about interactions between worlds, except in terms of their own war, they actualy want to see the world whole again, and that means lowering the Gauntlet as much as they can.

                                            MAGES: Metaphisicaly speaking, the two groups have nothing to do with one another. Awakening's true magic comes from a reality more "real" than the known world, from old misteries, from a link with something outside the character that makes him aware of truths above the world we perceive. Ascension's true magic is about shaping reality to one's own belief, about the power of the collective human mind and the power of challenging this common perception, about dedicating oneself to something until you can take things in your own hands and make it true, doesn't matter how much other people disbelieve, there is an Avatar, a piece of divinity, but instead of linking the mage to something bigger, it makes the mage herself bigger.

                                            But both groups are also used to learn by themselves and live (to some degree) with people they disagree. The Nine Traditions would welcome the Pentacle and the Free Council with the same caution they have with one another, already, and the Pentacle would do the same to them. There would be some discussion about how to mingle the groups until they become aware of how different they are, and then they simply stay allied but distinct structures. Marauders, Nefandi, Scellesti and the like would probably remain distinct groups, trying to manipulate or hide as they always did.

                                            The greater shock would be between Seers and Technocracy. Both have good numbers and the "domain of the status quo". But are, at the same time, astoundingly far from each other. They would hunt one another inside the power structures of society, and probably reach for their more traditional enemies for an alliance. The bid of the Seers for power and control of magic is common among Ascension's Mages, but at least Tech and Trad as groups always cared something about the world at large. Those "Seers" are just plainly power-hungry, and maybe the Tech can get some help to deal with them. Technocracy, on the other hand, want to wipe "dangerous beliefs" from this world, while the Seers never cared too much about such abstract concepts. That conflict would be the focus of the next hard decisions of both Traditions and Pentacle/FC, to whom ally, if to someone at all.

                                            CHANGELINGS: Probably Dreaming's fae would scare the hell out of the Lost at first. Those things are True Fae by themselves. The Fae would, by their part, wonder the immunity of the Lost to Banality. In the end of the day, they would probably live apart with some careful contact, but this contact would be as passionate as one could expect, since both are, still, moved by emotions, stories and relationships swored as an Oath.

                                            DEMONS: Even more than any of the previous, the Fallen of both groups have absolutely nothing to do with one another. There is, surely, clear similarities between their stories, but that's all.
