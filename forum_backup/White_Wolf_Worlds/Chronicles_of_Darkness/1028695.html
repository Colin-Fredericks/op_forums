    A difference between Chronicles of Darkness and classic World of Darkness

    01-08-2017, 05:42 PM
    I've noticed that in CoD, things tend to be a lot more mysterious and left to the Storyteller's discretion, whereas in classic WoD, there a lot more secrets revealed and cut-and-dried information given (even if it is only given to the ST). This is starting to change with Dark Eras, but I think it's still true overall. What do you guys think?

              There's also a lot of variation between the individual game lines. Mummy is far more "cut and dried" in ST-oriented material in the CofD than it is in the WoD (though part of that is that there's two essential versions of Mummy in the WoD and was never a full game line).

                  My go-to summary tends to be that the titular Darkness in the World of Darkness is a darkness of tone, whereas the Darkness in the Chronicles of Darkness is a darkness of opacity. The World of Darkness expresses its character through the exaggerated state of the world: rampant crime and disappearances, a maltheistic cosmology, and harsh injustices to the characters dwelling within. The Chronicles of Darkness express their character through the proliferation of mystery: communities are more geographically isolated, conditions are foggier, and there's strange things lurking at the corners that even the other strange lurking things can't piece together.

                  It's not to say that either setting has a monopoly on those qualities. The World of Darkness has strange things that exist outside the context of the primary cosmologies of the gamelines, and the Chronicles of Darkness occasionally take on an oppressive, maltheistic tone. But they specialize differently.

                          Interesting, I got the Warhammer effect more from CofD, specifically from Requiem and Awakening. What's the point of accumulating power and connections? The Torpor will take it away. What's the point of fighting the Seers? The Exarchs already won. Ascension, IMO is much brighter than Awakening.

                          Yes, WoD has it's big conflicts and looming apocalypse scenarios, but it always had the possibility of "saving the world" at the end (and you don't have to run for those scenarios). Even the last fight of the Garou wasn't inevitably doomed.

                          Also, I could agree that CofD doesn't as "cut and dry". If you don't have worldwide organizations and records, let alone persons who remember things, it naturally becomes murkier. Or, like in the case of VII, for example, it intentionally gives you different "truths". That's the sandbox approach, it's good if you want to build your own world. In Masquerade, you get a book about the Tal'Mahe'ra, which, while certainly has unexplained parts and mysteries in it, it describes the faction in detail and what is it. It is good if you want ongoing story and for example novel lines (because you could build on the previous products' lore) or want to bring the worldwide fandom together (because it provides common ground).

                          Basically, with CofD you have more tools and bigger freedom (and consequently less fear from lore-lawyers) to play with them, while in WoD you have a "traditional" rpg setting, with detailed history (although the unreliable narrator part is very important), events and definitive writings of the organizations, splats, etc.

                                  I would disagree with the Warhammer effect at least in Requiem. The way I see it, there's even MORE purpose in accumulating power and connections in a Vampire game than in, say, a Mortals game / in real life. Mortals die, and don't get to reap the rewards after that point. You accumulate power to enjoy NOW, or to hand off to the descendents of your choice. Vampires can (attempt to) set up systems such that their power and connections will still remain when they wake up from torpor again, and not only enjoy power NOW, but ALSO enjoy it in the future (especially if they set up the proper systems to protect it while they're gone).
                                  As for Awakening, it depends on how active you play the Exarchs. If the Exarchs are a constant crushing force on the Pentacle, yeah, very much a Warhammer effect, they've already won and there's no way to beat them (or is there? dun dun dun...). If you play them as only occasionally interested in crushing the Pentacle, then you can very much have meaningful victories, just on a smaller scale. Think more "We've blown up the Death Star! The Rebellion is saved!", less "We've killed the Emperor and the rest of the Empire has just rolled over", and then you have a framework for removing (most) of the Warhammer Effect from Awakening. It definitely does suffer from the issue that the Bad Guys have practically irrevocably 'won' in the biggest possible picture, forcing you to focus on smaller-picture victories.

                                  edit: generally on the idea of vagueness, OP is totally correct. There 1 major exception I can think of; Mummy: the Curse relies on a very specific mythology and history, the discovery of which is part of the game experience. Otherwise, they've tried to design as many ST inspirations as they could. Many points in the mythology are meant to make you read them and go "Ooooh, I want to play a Chronicle where the players discover the truth behind that! It would be cool if VII were ABC, which would mean they would be allied with the Nameless who could be XYZ and against the Strix who are alphabetagamma" etc etc

The Exarchs may just be the representation for how the Ten Arcana try to dominate each other and the Fallen World. I can see why you would think that though, because for me the Sect war from Masquerade seems incredibly monolithic and all consuming.                                                           For point b) in order for the Exarchs, or any player in the Ascension War for that matter, to be able to influence the Fallen World or Supernal Realms at all they must have two things: they need symbolic resonance which concepts from the Fallen World, and they need Quintessence from the physical world.
