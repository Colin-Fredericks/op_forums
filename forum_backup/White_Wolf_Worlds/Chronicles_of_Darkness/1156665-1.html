        It's kinda confusing to tell whom you're directing your comment to Marcus, but I'll pick up the torch of torpor and feeding for a moment to wave it too:

        Torpor as a fix to BP feeding issues is needed only if your vampire elder has disregarded his mystical side strongly in favor of mortal manipulations. There are many ways to get around feeding restrictions at this point, and latitude to develop more ("I eat dreams", "I consume your willpower in each delicious tear", "I invade the spirit world and consume its essence!" and etc...).

        Of course, down-to-earth vamps won't be doing any of these. They're rolling in issues related to politics, domain management, and the content battle to keep jealous underlings from burning their haven. It's distracting, to be blunt, and also the core assumption of VtR for most of its denizens. That's fine--no complaint.

        Where both your mystical and mundane vamps will get into trouble, however, is Languid. That condition is nasty and will drag a high-BP down the road to torpor pretty quick. The solution of "don't lose your Touchstone" is all fine and good until an enemy figures you out--then you have a big problem and an in-character shock coming with serious mechanical side-effects.

        --Khanwulf

                    Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post

                    Interesting question - Could your enemies be counted as Touchstones? Like in Brother Clement example - he hunts all werewolves from the bloodline of murderers of his Sire and 'brother'. None the less those werewolves are reminding him about his life, don't they?
                    Short answer: yes. Longer answer: technically anything that's not a vamp could be a touchstone regardless of what that person thinks of the vamp--it's the meta-level of player versus character going on. Sure you hate your rival's guts, but they spur you on to greatness and given the opportunity to just off them you'll hesitate and rationalize your way out of doing so.

                    A character like Brother Clement might even shepherd his werewolf targets/pack/lineage through the ages, ensuring that they have opportunities to rise to the occasion of being his prey. Because, yaknow, nothing is so unsatisfying as hunting down dangerous creatures and discovering they're as threatening as a Saltine cracker. They might not even realize who or what he is until he lets them or they gain enough power to figure it out... knowledge can be deadly, after all.

                    Meanwhile he gets his touchstone connectivity fix.

                    I'm entertained by this conversation because I'd already planned on setting up a similar scenario for a PC: they get an especially hardy vamp who takes him involuntarily as a touchstone, and therefore doesn't really want to kill him (or, let him be killed), even as the PC's mortal goals conflict with those of the vamp. Eventually, of course, the toy will get broken and they'll both need to sort out a new relationship.

                                Originally posted by Griautis View Post

                                Obvious embarrassing hole in argument is obvious. In my defense I have caught myself confusing Claws of the Unholy with the DA:Vampire/WoD Multi-Discipline power (Devotion) that does something virtually identical. So yeah, CotU is to me one of those points that wander for me in the grey area between "solid Merit" and "solid Devotion"; I should stay away from it.

                                My more recent mental benchmark really boils down to "how much should the power/ability cost in XP"--especially if you just assume everything is supernatural somehow (even if not labeled as such). The differences being that Merits are 1 XP per dot (plus any potential prereqs), while Devotions have a formula for their cost that may make them more expensive.

                                Originally posted by Griautis View Post
                                On the topic itself - I'd make it a merit, which would invokve a clash of wills between one's obfuscate and Auspex to hide the true facts of his identity. Should he clash exceptionally, then eh can feed his own information to the Auspex user.

                                There's something to be said for interacting with/observing other monsters as they struggle to be "human". Just hunting them down and killing them would not, I think, be sufficient (and would quickly detach your touchstone, since it shouldn't be "werewolf hunting" (an action) but could be "werewolves of the line/pack of X"). There's something to be said for expressing vengeance and the defense of humans as a part of your relationship with the werewolves as a Touchstone, based again on the humanness of such expressions.

                                Again, if you're just going out and fighting/killing them, that's not really a meaningful interaction with your touchstone, IMO. There has to be more context behind it. All this is subjective, however, and I'd want to look deeper at a play who proposed such a touchstone to understand what his point really was--is there an effort to evade roleplaying and bone up on conflict at work?

                                            Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post

                                            So if Clement want's to 'play with a food' ( i.e. werewolves ) that still could be Touchstone? Just killing the outright would be only Aspiration?
                                            ​Correct, up to a point (Malus' point, to be exact): destroying a group, or even a person, would be an Aspiration. Recognizing the essential humanity struggling within werewolves, and identifying with their challenge of balancing the monster and the man, would enable him to take a given werewolf, or even a pack, as a touchstone. He can still have an antagonistic relationship with them, however the character of that antagonism changes from extermination to... something else; perhaps something paternalistic, or authoritative, or sporting, or even competitive.
                                            There's a gulf of difference between "I hate you and want you dead" (which is straightforward, sensible and simplistic to an inhuman degree) and "I hate you and making you suffer and struggle gives me meaning."

                                            Or, even, "I hate you and my hating you makes me human--so don't stop, don't fail... don't fall, because then on the precipice of that storm-lost time, what will I become without hate?"

                                            Alternatively, my cat has an interaction with the field mice in the backyard (roughly a dozen interactions a year, on average), but they are not meaningful interactions. He forms no relationship with each mouse beforehand; there is no period of introduction. He enjoys hunting them until they break (which doesn't take long given he's proportionately the size of an apartment building); and he gets Willpower for indulging his Vice... or Virtue, depending on your point of view. Yet, he goes out several times a day to surveil the holes and watch over them. He probably took "mouse holes in the back yard" as his Touchstone, and interacting with them like a tiger in the jungle prowling water holes reminds him of his primal cat-ness.
