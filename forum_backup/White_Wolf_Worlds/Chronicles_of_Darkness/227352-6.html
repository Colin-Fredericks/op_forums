    Originally posted by Aspel View Post

    The issue is that without some overarching reason--the Sensitivity in Princess, the Orders in Mage, the Oath in Forsaken--you don't really have any splat-wide reason for doing anything. Yes, individual Sin-eaters with a conscience--likely player characters--will care. But there's no reason that the average Sin-eater would. The setting only works if the players decided they want to be good guys who benevolently take up the mantle of ghost busters.

    The other half of this is that the fluff acts as if they care but gives them no reason to do so. Why would krewes fight over territory for the right to... solve crimes and lay ghosts to rest? Why would they bother with Abmortals that aren't serial killers? I mean, the Hungry Exorcist eats ghosts and that's supposed to be super bad, but... why? Why should Sin-eaters care about that?

    The average medium in a fictional setting is going to be motivated by a sense of purpose, but that's because we're only focusing on that one medium who makes for the best story. We don't see any others in the world who ignore their gifts or who use their ability to talk to ghosts to figure out the password and security codes of bank vaults, or just have ephemeral beings shake the tables and make spectral manifestations pretending to be dead loved ones to con people. Yes, the PCs will likely want to do good things because they're good guys. But many won't care because it doesn't personally effect them. Order and Covenant and Tribe gives someone a reason to care.

    Geists don't have that. So their entire society seems to work on "It's the right thing!" but there's still talk of infighting and all that even when there's no logical reason for it. The setting is very flat. And even then, this is the World of Darkness. Not every PC is going to want to help lay ghosts to rest because "it's the right thing to do".
    I didn't say that they nessesarily had to be good, just that they shouldn't be apathetic. As long as your story is progressing and people are having fun, you're doing something right. You could just as easily have a story about a selfish Sin-Eater Necromancer plummeting the Dead Dominions for power (and exploiting ghosts to the same effect) as you could a Sin-Eater playing ghost buster.
    Personally, I wouldn't want a clear cut villian like the Abyss in Mage or the Strix in Vampire. Those seem too "Evil for Eldritch unknowable reasons" kinds of evil for Geist. I think the fact that the antagonists in Geist are antagonists wither because you wronged them yourself (Kerberoi, angry Ghosts/Geists) or because you have different political views to them (Sacrosanct, Abmortals).
    That said, I agree that antagnost aspect of Geist is slightly lacking, especially since the Kerberoi cant hound you into the living world, so as soon as you're back up, you're rather safe.

                        I shut up about this but I think it needs to be restated: as TKR just pointed out, the Geist is you're best tool for fighting apathetic douche bag character concepts. They can act as an antagonist to push your characters to do what you (as a storyteller) want them to care about.

                        Hey, look, a ghostly serial killer! I don't care. Gonna go get drunk! - says the player.

                        Ah, but your Geist is pretty pissed off by the whole ordeal. Kind of hard to enjoy your party with an angry Geist that you can't tune out in your ear... - you reply, grinning.

                        Edit: and to the people who reply and complain that they don't want Geists to be the antagonists of the story: not all antagonists are bad guys, or the bad guy. They're just the characters who make trouble for the protagonist. The landlord who wants to be paid by your broke hero is an antagonist, even though your story is based around your hero fighting to get paid by his cheapskate boss and the landlord is perfectly in the right. Basically, anyone who forces the protagonist to do something is an antagonist, if not a major one.
