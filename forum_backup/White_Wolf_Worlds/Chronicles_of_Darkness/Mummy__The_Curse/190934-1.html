    Originally posted by Whithers View Post
    And it could well be that by framing the mechanics so clearly in one specific culture, including the others simply ceases to be possible.
    I don't disagree, but I don't see that as a negative thing. A Mummy can be immersed into another culture and come to think of itself as part of that culture, but it will never be anything but wrong. That doesn't necessarily stop you from playing an "Aztec" Mummy, so long as you accept that your character will eventually find out that they aren't Aztec at all. However, some games benefit from having a narrow focus and I believe Mummy: The Curse is one of these games.

    Some of the new Ghost rules make it very easy to create undead from other cultures though. They won't be quite as powerful as Arisen, but with the right Numen you can have an animated corpse with the ability to reinforce its stats, regenerate damage, and an Age trait that works like a power trait for the purpose of resisting magic, plus a few other magical abilities of your choice. With the Lucid Dead Numen, such creatures are even playable characters.

                        Bear in mind that the proto-egyptian Nameless Empire also predates the culture we think of as "ancient egypt" (which is to say, Kemet) by a millenium or two.

                        Originally posted by Azahul
                        A Mummy can be immersed into another culture and come to think of itself as part of that culture, but it will never be anything but wrong. That doesn't necessarily stop you from playing an "Aztec" Mummy, so long as you accept that your character will eventually find out that they aren't Aztec at all.
                        I think that depends really heavily on what you mean by "being" of that culture. If I've lived in a place for longer than most of the people there have been alive, if I know the culture of that place inside out and am accepted by that culture as a member (or even a respected elder or divine being), and I identify with that culture, does my memory of being part of some other culture first really negate that?

                        I think there's plenty of room for a mummy to remember her origins and still consider herself part of, for example, an indigenous south american culture. Or for that matter to consider themselves a USian. Or whatever. Origins don't dictate identity, they just inform it.

                        (And now I am imagining a super patriotic USian mummy being all "Son, it doesn't matter that I came from Egypt, your ancestors came from Ireland and the Sudan and Germany. I was there when Lee surrendered and I've been voting since the 15th amendment, I dare you to tell me I'm not an American")

                        Edit: Actually, the longer I think about it the more I think that the mummy who mistakenly believes he has always been Aztec would be an absurd rarity. Memory of basic identity stuff like "I am a servant of the nameless empire" and "I am a member of X Guild" is some of the last stuff to go when memory lowers. So a mummy who believed something counter to those facts would almost need to be straight up delusional, rather than just being forgetful.

                        On the flipside, high-memory mummies are simultaneously more likely to clearly remember being from egypt, and also (by virtue of thinking more like humans) more likely to want to identify with a nearby, living culture. Knowing you're a citizen of the nameless empire doesn't help you much when it's millenia dead and you crave all the things (like community, belonging, ritual) that come with actually being immersed and identified with a living culture. While low memory mummies are probably content to just ignore that and do their jobs, the high memory ones are probably going to integrate.
