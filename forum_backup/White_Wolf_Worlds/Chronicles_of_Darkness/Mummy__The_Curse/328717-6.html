    (Speaking as another reader who has just caught up) I think it is that: the Deceived at least feel coordinated and successful, while much of the Arisen's adventures... frankly reek of failure.     Deceived: Villain Protagonists (maybe Anti-villains)
    Arisen: Failure Heroes (maybe Byronic Heroes, at best)

    My impressions of the characters and their arcs. The players are excellent, in that each character's development is following a natural progression.

                                    Session 25

                                    The Deceived were watching the skies closely as the Radiance neared the world. Akenakhi waited with them. When the attack came however, it came from the Earth, not the heavens. Jakarta itself became the engine of the Radiance's arrival, and they were forced to watch as the light of corruption flooded into the world from the city centre.

                                    The Prophet appeared before them, looking breathless and scared. "The Radiance's servants have opened a way to Earth," he said. "But we have a chance. It is a light like any other, and our enemies have ensured that it will come close enough for you to grasp."

                                    Akenakhi opened a new entrance to the Vault of Stars, and the Deceived stepped through. Rakhen-Roh and Akenakhi, able to perceive beings in Twilight, immediately noticed that the girl made of stars was absent from the Vault. They had little time to look for her, however. Akenakhi unleashed Palace Knows Its Pharaoh, and moments later they were on the surface of a cold, frozen planet. They could sense the roar of an Epic Unleashing not far from where they were standing, on the other side of a forest of stone spires. These spires were clearly constructions built by some long-dead race, concentric rings that rose to a peak at their centre.

                                    And then the Radiance appeared in the skies above them, a flood of light and colour ever-shifting and ever-changing. Imhotep knew that if he could reach the central spire, stand upon it, then he could take the Radiance and bind it to his name as a Seba.

                                    The children of the Radiance began to descend from their parent's body, celestial parasites from the gulf between worlds defending their host from its attackers. Most were drawn to the blazing Sekhem on the other side of the spires, and the Deceived were eager to keep it that way. They dispatched their attackers quickly and quietly, rending them apart with blasts of sand and fire. Rakhen-Roh rushed into spire first, eyes on the shadows, fingers splayed and hands ready to unleash Wrathful Desert Power. Imhotep was more circumspect, trying to avoid attention and sticking to cover as he made a more circular approach. Behind them, they sensed a surge of Sekhem as Akenakhi unleashed the second tier of Revelations of Smoke and Flame and ascended into the sky, immediately drawing the attention of many of their attackers.

                                    In the midst of the spires, Rakhen-Roh was attacked by a Shuankhsen. Thanks to Akenakhi, he knew it to be the one named Nenheta, her ring-toothed maw distinguishing her from the others. She struck at him and vanished into the shadows again before he could retaliate, bleeding him with repeated hit-and-run attacks. At last he called on Tutkepertanu, and the Shan'iatu thundered onto the field, calling on meteors to smash those who dared strike at his holy flesh.

                                    Imhotep had dodged the fighting. He reached the central spire and began to ascend. A creature of crawling chaos, its body made up of the combined forms of near half a dozen Arisen, was busy fighting those worms closest to the tower, and he reached the top unopposed. For a moment he was tempted to summon his Temakh, to allow Siranuthis to match wills against the star god overhead, but his pride left him sure that he could succeed without aid. He raised his voice and called, and the battle of wills began.

                                    Akenakhi was badly beleaguered, beset by the creatures of the Radiance. He summoned the Cyclopean Monolith, watching as Chthonic Beings spilled forth to defend the structure. These manifold horrors battled with the Radiance's children in the throes of their titanic fury. For a moment he was tempted to try and break the seal on the monolith, unleash whatever creature lay inside, but he stilled that impulse. Better the enemy he knew, he decided.

                                    Tutkepertanu smashed his way through the remaining defenders to take his place by Imhotep's side. The Shan'iatu clearly seethed at the fact that the Mummy, not the Temakh, was the one performing this ritual, but it could be fatal to interrupt Imhotep now. Instead, he turned his efforts towards aiding Imhotep. Any creatures that came close would be destroyed.

                                    The Radiance clearly realised what was happening. Its children turned their attention towards Imhotep, charging through the fire and magic and fury of the surrounding defenders, trying again and again to reach him. They breached the lines, spilling down towards the spire, and Imhotep spared a moment from his task to call forth Ashemenhotep. The god-like creature destroyed the closest attackers in moments and leant its deadly bow to the death of those creatures yet to close the distance.

                                    Akenakhi won free of the creatures attacking him and rushed to join the defenders on the spire. All of a sudden, there was a woman made of stars standing atop a nearby spire where they had been nothing moments before. He recognised her, even though she had grown in stature and her head had been replaced by that of an immense serpent. It was the woman from the vault. She raised a hand and he felt himself gripped by unseen forces and currents, hauled towards her with inexorable will. He struggled, only to find himself being crushed, his bones breaking one by one.

                                    Other Arisen and Mummies were joining the battle. The Herald came to Akenakhi's rescue, tearing the ghost apart before it could ensure his death. Sekhem flared on every side as the immortal children of Irem defended their world.

                                    In the centre, Nenheta appeared on the central spire. She launched herself at Imhotep, trying to prevent the ritual from being completed. Her teeth tore into him, ripping out enormous chunks of his flesh, but Ashemenhotep seized her before she could dispatch the Deceived completely. For a moment she struggled in his grip, before she was burned from the universe by the Winged Archer's will. Seba spilled from her body, dozens of glowing orbs ripped from mortal souls.

                                    As their enemies and betrayers held back the forces of the Radiance, Imhotep felt the great being falling within his grasp. It was incomprehensibly powerful, but it could not deny its own nature. There was the sound of rushing air and the howling of mouthless demons, and then the Radiance was gone from the sky and tied to the soul of Imhotep, the Forever Scornful.

                                    Neither Deceived waited long to repay the Arisen for their help. Ashemenhotep turned on the Shuankhsen who had joined the battle, killing one even as the Herald destroyed the other. They watched as the Herald vanished, shifting across the gulf of space and back to Earth, and the one they knew to be Sesu Ra followed in its wake. Tutkepertanu turned his powers upon Hetenatra and Sem-Huf, the other Arisen who had joined the fight. Their burning bodies soon decorated the surface of the planet.

                                    For his part, Akenakhi was on the cusp of death. He flew straight for the portal back to the Vault of Stars, and sealed it shut behind him. He never intended to speak to his allies again. He burned a dot of Sekhem to draw back from the brink of death, and closed his eyes. A part of him hoped that they would remain trapped on that foreign planet. Somehow, however, he suspected that the world would not be so lucky.

                                    Satisfied that their enemies were either vanquished or fled, the Deceived turned their attention to the wealth of Seba that lay around them. Tutkepertanu bound them one by one, until his soul was filled with the burning gleam of captured stars. Imhotep, exhausted, was unable to summon up the energy to add to his own collection, but had no qualms about allowing his ally to muster such power. With that done, Tutkepertanu unleashed Palace Knows Its Pharaoh and trusted that Fate would reward their efforts with a return home.

                                    Their faith was rewarded. The two Deceived stepped out into the sunlight of Jakarta. Rakhen-Roh was utterly drained by summoning his Temakh, and immediately struck out for his tomb. Imhotep, however, was not prepared to allow his enemies a moment's peace. He used Name the Extinguished Star to find where Bast, Aqhat, Kotira, and the Maa-Kep were located, had the cult arrange a helicopter and weapons, and gathered those warriors he could and mounted an immediate attack.

                                    At first, Imhotep saw no need to put his Temakh in control. He unleashed Blessed Is The God-King and began to hurl Utterances at the newly constructed temple in the islands off Jakarta, supplemented by mundane explosives deployed by his cultists. Rebuke the Vizier kept him safe from retaliation, as Arisen began to emerge and sought to strike back, but eventually an Utterance slipped past his defences. Scarabs swarmed the helicopter and ate the pilot's eyes, and they plummeted to the ground. The Su-Menent warriors charged in to fight, Sekhet Bastet sprouting a scorpion's tail and Aqhat charging with axe in hand. Their blows found purchase even in his incorporeal flesh, and he sacrificed the Soul of the Dead Star Seba to blast them back and give himself a moment's respite. Then Imhotep allowed Siranuthis to take complete control. Fire roared from his Temakh's mouth, but the Su-Menent continued their assault unperturbed. Bast's form, shielded by the Ancestry of Forgotten Stars, resisted even his attempts to destroy her with fire. The fight quickly turned against him, and he took to the skies in the hope that he could rain destruction down from out of their reach. Bast simply followed suit, displaying a mastery of an Utterance he had not known she possessed. The aerial battle was quick, tearing his flesh apart, and he turned himself to sand to escape. His final words lingered in the air.

                                    "We will never stop."

                                        Epilogue

                                        The above post was the Deceived's perspective of what happened in this post.

                                        Imhotep's failure to destroy the Su-Menent when they were at their weakness meant that the Arisen would retain their foothold in Jakarta, however tenuous. The new temple would become the staging ground and fortress of their enemies, watched and monitored at all times by servants of the Deceived. In the city itself, however, their might was uncontested. Their cult, spreading and branching from Lone Star Studios, crept its way into positions of power. The Arisen were denied access to mortal influence and strength, kept to the outskirts and limited in what they could accomplish. The city had become a stronghold of the Tomb Legion and a campaign for the Shattered Crook, for only the Priests still challenged their might.

                                        Well aware that his enemies still lived, Imhotep made sure to fortify the holdings they had taken. The Arisen's memories would inevitably work against them, he felt, and as time passed they would lose perspective and forget what they were up against. Once their guard had lowered, he hoped to have a chance to drive them from the city entirely.

                                        For Rakhen-Roh, it was the new temple that burned in his mind. Its presence on the outskirts of the city was more than a nuisance or a threat, it was provocation. Subtle as ever, the hidden master began to lay the foundations of schemes that, he hoped, would one day see the blight of the Su-Menent purged forever from this corner of the world.

                                        As for Akenakhi, he chose to flee the city, to return to Paris and leave behind the monsters who now reigned in Jakarta. He never spoke another word to the Deceived, and for their part, they hardly seemed to notice his departure. He hoped that Europe would give him the stability and safety to pursue his studies into Apotheosis in more detail. Abandoning his Purpose in such a way, however, all but ensured that his descent would be a quick one. He would not achieve the freedom that he sought in this cycle.

                                        While their new empire faced threats from without and within, warring with Arisen on one flank and the plague of Shuankhsen appearing throughout the city's slums, the Temakh were well pleased with the activities of their hosts. A great blow had been struck against the puppets of their enemies. It was, they hoped, the beginning of something more.
