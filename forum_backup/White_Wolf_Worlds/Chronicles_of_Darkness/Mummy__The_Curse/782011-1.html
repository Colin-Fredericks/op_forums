        Well, to increase the suspense until the book is made public, it isn't "just" a fiction collection. It introduces a new plot point that can be dropped into any Mummy chronicle--the Blue Nile, a mythical Relic claimed by many Guilds to be their own. Supposedly, all Arisen would recognize it, and all felt its creation, even if they can't remember what it is and what it does. Also, the fiction seems to have some interesting ideas in it. It clarifies what a sekhem Sorcerer is capable of, and their place in Arisen society. It nicely explores mesoamerican Mummies. It addresses how many different lifestyles and personalities even low level mummies can have. But all the stories tie all of this around the basic, fundamental urge for mummies to seek Relics. Though, so far, no two of these stories has been about the "same" Blue Nile, they are in a way all about that Kepher-induced feeling of need, and about how a Relic's draw (especially a legendary relic) can shape stories in different ways.

        I do have a question that either a developer or a writer could answer for me. I noticed that in Father of Ab, the Nomarchs of LA are mortals. On the one hand, this feels like it could be a mistake on the writer's part. On the other hand, it seems like it'd make a lot of sense for various Deathless, with their brief descents, relying on long-lived humans to give their guild continuity. I couldve sworn all Nomarchs were Arisen, but seeing humans-as-nomarch brought a lot of interesting ideas to the table. (PS, sorry if this hasnt separated into two paragraphs. My mobile and this website have an odd relationship with regards to paragraphs and autocorrect)
