        Thoughts so far.

        I like the book's chapter organization. I know this might seem small but it really matters. This book is organized in a structurally sound, organized, logical manner that has me nodding along where everything is. So I like that. A lot.

        I like the way first two chapters are written, both information wise and style wise. I like the Guilds, both the major and minor and the potential for others, I like the Degrees, they are very nifty. The Judges are there but not there at the same time and I like that. The way that society for the Arisen works is cool too. The mention about other immortlas, including vampires, and why they can and can't fit in is perfect. I also like the nuanced element between The Timeless and The Endless, its brilliant. This is of course in addition to the whole element of time is all weird and non-linear, which I LOVE.

        So so far, with what has been posted, I like it.

        Now I just need to wait (patiently I guess, lol) for the mechanics chapter and then the ST adventuring chapter that is 7, which I really want to see and hope is really really long. I want awesome fun campaign advice.

        So yeah, so far so good. (I backed at the book level as I know myself if I even remotely like most of it then I will want a book to add to my shelf.) *(Heck, I don't even have to like or need all of it, I bought Hunter Witchfinders for the one chapter on Gutter Magic, lol, even though I will never play a Hunter game)

                                                What fascinates me is the declarations of what each of the Guilds have achieved... Like with the Mesen-Nebu, the alchemists, it's described how they create potions, medicines, magical alloys and such. And this was before the Rite of Return when they were mortals. Now it could be argued that all of these things qualified as Relics and therefore things Arisen of the Mesen-Nebu can't do anymore... But I dislike that, because for one, there are Affinities and Utterances that can kinda do similar things. But also because I don't want everything the Guilds did in the past to be Relics.

                                                Personally, I really want Arisen to be able to teach their Cultists magical things. To have effects that Cultists can duplicate. And I'm not really looking for... powerful effects here.

                                                What I'm kind of thinking to do for myself is... Basically look at the Supernatural Merits that mortals have accessible to them and... You know that saying of "Fast, Cheap and Good, pick two"? Well, pick none, and Occult on it's own will sorta get you near the ballpark. And maybe under some personal guidance of their Arisen, Cultists learning occult ways could unlock those Supernatural Merits to be better than their general practice allows.
