                                            Burrow Cultists
                                            Here, a man, walking through a crowd of humans to whom he no longer feels ties of kinship. He's not entirely isolated, though; a rat squirms and twitches under his coat, resting close to his heart. This man is different to the humans all around him; he sees places of sublime invisibility, speaks tongues with rats, sees what they see, and feels their hammering breaths in their throats more keenly than his own slow, rasping gulps of air. He feeds that little rat blood from his breast. Some might look on this man and think him a witch, the rat his familiar. The truth is the opposite: the man is the familiar here.

                                            Something about the essential matter of the human body and soul renders it perfect for the Hosts to exploit. The most brutal and direct way is, of course, direct infestation. Most humans who interact with Hosts come to a horrific end, cored out as husks, eaten, or just murdered - but there are always a few who think they can make a deal with the Hosts, and the Shards themselves can make use of catspaws and servants. Most of these collaborators remain purely human, kept in line through fear, threats, or bribery. Some rare few Hosts have learned the Essence-arts of forging closer ties, though - the binding of human to Shard, creating a familiar and servant empowered through the Shard's occult resonance.

                                            Burrow cultists usually come from among those caught up in a Beshilu's mad schemes. They may literally be cultists, worshipping the rat Hosts as divine beings, or they may be occultists hungry for power and willing to shoulder the horrendous risks of dealing with the Hosts to achieve it. A few are press-ganged into the role, forced to serve as a familiar lest horrible acts be wrought against their family or the like. Most come to a sticky end, sooner or later; that's part of the point of creating them, after all. A burrow cultist will almost always play second fiddle to the Hosts; they are slaves, not masters, even if they derive power from their bondage.

                                            A burrow cultist is created via subjecting the human to a partial infestation; it is likely the first Beshilu rat-witches who discovered the process did so due to botched infestation attempts. The Shard must enter the human's body and begin infestation, enough that its spiritual fragment begins to bond with the human's energy, then withdraw again before the human's own identity and life is snuffed out. For obvious reasons, this generally rules out the more grotesque forms of infestation - a hole gnawed through the human's torso is likely to be fatal, after all - and most rat-witches binding a human as a familiar therefore do so by entering the mouth and throat, although there are a few who use surgical tools to take a different approach or to ensure their familiars are appropriately marked as their property. Where a Beshilu has an actual cult working to its will, this act is performed as a ceremony with all the feverish zeal that the rat Hosts are so keen on.
                                            Creating a burrow cultist comes with its limitations; a cultist is specifically linked to the particular Host Shard that created them, and the Shard must render itself down to its base vermin form to be able to taint the cultist in this way. Unless a suitable victim is already procured and on-hand, the Shard will need to find a new host to infest after the rite is performed; some choose to remain in their base form for a while, especially if they need to keep a low profile, using their human familiar to shuttle them around and protect them. Few Hosts feel comfortable remaining this vulnerable for long, though.

                                            Once created, a burrow cultist has a variety of uses. They serve as excellent catspaws and minions, spiritually bonded to the Host to allow for close oversight, and possessing their own tainted powers that put them ahead of a mere baseline human. They can take advantage of the burrows that the Beshilu gnaw across the Gauntlet, and of course serve as a fallback of last resort if the Shard's current vessel is destroyed. In short, they are valuable but sacrificial pawns. A few Beshilu grow attached to their burrow cultists, cultivating and maintaining a faithful familiar for years or even decades. Some have been known to shed tears when a treasured familiar dies from old age, or to hesitate from or even entirely refuse to infest a familiar even if it would save the Shard. These are rarities amongst the mad ratkind, of course; most exploit their cultists ruthlessly. Most rat-witches who know the secret of creating a familiar only maintain a single such servant, or at most a mere handful; they have a hard time reining in their paranoia that too many such close slaves might plot or scheme against them, or might be used to get at them.

                                            For a burrow cultist, life is a bizarre and twisted experience. Reborn in a moment of pain and the squirming of ratflesh within their throat or guts, they are touched by a fragment of the Shard's power and its insane fear; most burrow cultists suffer minor twitches, occasional spasms, patches of hair falling out, or other outward signs of the spiritual damage they have suffered. Jumpiness and mild paranoia are common. Some are entirely gripped by fear, terrified of their new masters, but a fair number of burrow cultists are where they are entirely voluntarily, and either revel in their power or serve the Hosts with fervent zeal. Other humans seem more distant after the change, a little more alien, a little harder to read. Rats, on the other hand, feel like kin or children; any residual disgust at such vermin is washed away by the taint, and a burrow cultist will happily handle and surround themselves with rats without any qualms. That's not to say that all burrow cultists devolve into crazy rat ladies - in most cases, their ability to move around in and interact with regular humanity is of great use to their Host master after the latter has moved to the later, more monstrous stages of hybridisation - but they feel a definite remoteness from the species they once considered their own.

                                            One particularly stark difference is that a burrow cultist can sense the Gauntlet, and perceive the ragged tears and holes chewed in it by Beshilu. It's in these burrows that a cultist feels most at peace and satisfied, as the Shard's taint resonates with the Gauntlet. Clever cultists make use of their ability to hide in such burrows, or to cross over into the Shadow; the hisil remains thoroughly inhospitable to even a tainted human, so most cultists either learn to move stealthily through the shadowscape or to enter it only for brief trips. For a power-hungry occultist, becoming a burrow cultist offers a direct and effective means of accessing the spirit world to treat with its denizens, albeit at the cost of servitude and likely death at the claws of a verminous horror. There are always a few willing to make that kind of deal.

                                            Burrow Cultist
                                            Int 3 Wits 4 Res 2 Str 2 Dex 3 Sta 4 Pre 2 Man 3 Com 2
                                            Academics 2 Investigation 2 Occult 3 Athletics 2 Brawl 2 Larceny 3 Stealth 4 Survival 1 Weaponry 1 Animal Ken 4 Empathy 2 Persuasion 1 Streetwise 3 Subterfuge 4
                                            Health 9 Willpower 4 Size 5 Defence 5 Initiative 7 Speed 11
                                            Merits: Fast Reflexes 2, Fleet of Foot 1, Resources 2, Safe Place 2

                                            Feed Beshilu: A burrow cultist can feed their blood to a Beshilu, nourishing the Shard with Essence. By inflicting 1L on themself, the cultist can provide the Beshilu with up to three points of Essence each day.
                                            Burrow: A burrow cultist gains the Gauntlet Cloak Dread Power, fuelling it with Willpower instead of Essence. The cultist can also perceive and enter the gnawed burrows that the Hosts created in the Gauntlet, letting them cross over into the Shadow. Most burrow cultists are not able to sense or cross over at loci, although they do acquire this ability if they reach Occult 5. As well as crossing over, a cultist can hide within a Gauntlet burrow that has been gnawed out by the rat Hosts, and gains the Home Ground Dread Power while doing so.
                                            Rat Tongue: A burrow cultist can speak with and be understood by rats and other closely related species of vermin, and they feel a strong inclination to obey the cultist, even if given suicidal instructions.
                                            Rat Ride: A burrow cultist can project their senses into those of a rat they touch, riding its mind with their own for as long as they wish. They can let the rat take its own course, or spend 1 Willpower point to take control of its actions for one scene. While riding the rat, the cultist cannot perceive their own senses, and appear to be in a trance or asleep. Should the animal be killed while the cultist is riding them, the cultist suffers 1L and loses a point of Willpower, as well as treating the experience as a breaking point. Returning to the cultist's own senses is a reflexive action. Should the cultist be killed while riding the rat, a fragment of their soul becomes subsumed into the animal, with unpredictable results.
                                            Familiar Link: By spending 1 Essence, the Host that the cultist is bonded to can perceive through the cultist's senses for up to a scene. The Host can communicate mind-to-mind with the cultist while in this state. By spending 1 Willpower, the cultist can perceive the specific location and state of the linked Host for up to a scene.
                                            Gate of Flesh: By spending 3 Essence as a reflexive action while within 1 mile of the cultist, the linked Host may move from their current location to the burrow cultist's current location, killing the cultist in the process. If the Host is currently embodied in a vessel or as a hybrid, they use the cultist's flesh as a gateway, tearing their way through in a bloody emergence. If the Host does this at the instant they use their Discorporate Dread Power or while they are in their basic form, they instead simply infest the cultist from within, immediately taking the cultist's body as their own. Doing so empties the remainder of their Essence pool and inflicts 5 points of aggravated damage on their new form. A Host cannot make use of Gate of Flesh more than once per month.

                                            - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer
