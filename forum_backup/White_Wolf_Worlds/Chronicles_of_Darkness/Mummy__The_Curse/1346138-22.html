                And I’ve added to the writer group feedback that we could consolidate the ‘permanent-death’ conditions for mummies so people have an easy reference. But be aware that there’s not a lot of ways you can permanently eliminate a mummy*. It’s not the kind of game where that kind of death is the problem. The horror comes from death not being the end.

                            Originally posted by Azahul View Post
                            I'd beg to differ, but we're getting into Assassin's Creed-esque "the right meteor at the right time could shape the course of human civilisation" hypotheticals and they're probably a tad specific for this conversation.
                            That only removes artforms from the world's artistic paradigms. It's an incredibly blunt hammer, when what you need is the soft and insistent shaping of mummies and cults providing consistent artistic, imperial, and cultural pressure over centuries and millennia.

                            Suffice to say, the concept of the Judges wanting their servants to rediscover Memory and their sense of self feels like a strange direction to go in for Mummy thematically, at least for me, and let's leave it at that.
                            This is the crux of the misunderstanding, I think: no, the Judges do not want their servants to rediscover Memory and a sense of self. The Shan'iatu, in creating and encoding the Rite of Return, recognized that a certain amount of Memory could be situationally preferable to the raw might of Sekhem, especially when it comes to that all-important human interaction that is necessary for shaping artistic paradigms in the mold of Irem. They didn't anticipate Apotheosis; they knew that any recovered Memory would be eroded by the waves of Sekhem after the conclusion of the Descent. But the rising and falling pattern of Memory is a feature of the Arisen, not a bug.

                            The Arisen are thinking slaves kept in rising and falling states of ignorance for eternity, not perfect automatons, and that's by design.

                                                        I personally really liked the message that Apotheosis had in 1e- that in the end, it is better to be a free person and not a bound god. Freedoms means you give up on your power, but you regain your soul in exchange- and when the whole world is doomed to feed the ever hungry Devourer and suffer eternally under the Law, what more could a person ask other than freedom?

                                                        So for me, Apotheosis is not about becoming like the Judges- it is about putting yourself above them. That was the goal of the Shan'iatu- that's why they envied humanity, and why they sacrificed Irem. They tried to have the cake and eat it- being gods who stole the prize promised to mankind. That's also why they failed. You know what they say about the virtues and flaws and angels and humans.

                                                        I don't know, people may say that Mummy is a fatalistic game where everything is doomed to be eaten by the Devourer, but I don't think so. I find it really hopeful. They say that A'aru is barren, with only two souls shinning in its starry wastes. I see that against all chances an in spite of the wrath of the Judges, the hunger of Ammut and the envy of the Shan'iatu, humanity managed to put two souls up in the heavens, where they are eternal. A proof that humanity exist. And how wonderful is that?
