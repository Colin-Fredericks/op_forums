                        Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                        I STILL sis not got answer if Judges are more like Great Old Ones - or Idigama...
                        Neither.

                        The Judges are a step (or a step and a half) below omnipresent cosmic forces like creation, destruction, and manifest existence, second only to the defiance and unpredictability of all things. They are foundational to ordered reality as experienced in Mummy: the Curse, and it is this closeness to root divinity that likely makes their described appearances and physical manifestations so terrifying.

                        Derived from this, they are a set of forty-two THOU SHALT NOTs that Irem's legacy of civilization is built upon, serving as channels for the Devourer to receive her tithe of souls, because they do not care for the world they judge except insofar as they are compelled to by their place in the cosmos. They are cruel and their place in the cosmos specifically includes "humans are important, because they're the only things we're allowed to feed to Ammut so she'll let the universe at large continue to exist," which I am given to understand is kind of the opposite of how that power dynamic usually manifests for the Great Old Ones.

                        They are likewise not terribly close to the shifting proto-symbolic entities that are the idigam, who can certainly have a passive impact on the world around them as spiritual beings, but who lack the occult grounding one expects from something reality itself is built on; the idigam's creative approach to reality stems mostly from attempts to define new concepts, which makes them extremely late to the game as far as primordial beings go.

                        The Judges are sharp edges on the rock of coherent reality, and the world theoretically dances to their tune because the song they're playing is stimulus-response that they refined into specific forbiddances at some point thousands of years ago. They're not disinterested in our world, and if their emergence from chaos has any truth to it it's so far in the relative past as to be irrelevant to anyone not banking on an objective and eternal order to the cosmos.
