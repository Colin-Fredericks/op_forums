    How do you play a mummy game ?

    09-23-2014, 09:19 AM

    So… let’s say, I own all NWOD gameline and played or GMed all of them except for Mummy. I admit I didn’t read all the book but sufficiently to get a grasp of the game spirit (which for exemple suffice to pull me in with promethean or demon), but not quite yet for Mummy…

    The game in itself bears a lot of cool concepts, I love the Irem stuff, the memory focus etc etc… But ultimaly I don’t see HOW I could set a game with it, HOW could I push my players to go with a game were you begin as an all-powerful and mighty amnesic monster (which is cool) but who rapidly fell to a weak depressive guy who mourn over is dusty relics and wait to asleep for another millennia…

    Technically, for an over-thousands of years chronicle the game can be just good like vampire is (put apart the memory problem), but focusing an entire campaign in the few month after the beginning of the Sothic cycle and be stuck with the few historic occurrence of the cycle seem too restrictive for me.

    Nevertheless, I think I miss something in the book which make me overlook the awesome potential of this game. Could you, for exemple, state for me how a typical mummy game/campaign could be done with those restrictions?

      I just started running a solo game for my fiancee wherein her cult is lokoing for relics in 18th century Brazil (yes, they're looking for El Dorado). After that point the Chronicle will involve her being woken up several times a century to deal with her expanding business, Brazil's independence in 1822, contesting with some of the other Mummies in the area (like Pardal O Vermeilho, from the Eve of Judgement adventure in the back of the book), and her personal favorite, dealing with the recurring tomb-raiding family which will include expies of our favorite pulp action heroes: Rick O'Connell from The Mummy movie franchise, Indiana Jones (and Nazis!), Lara Croft, and Nathan Drake.

      There will also be some flashbacks dealing with why the cult moved over to Brazil in the first place, probably some stuff in the Golden Age of Piracy, and a brief interlude with an Arisen I made a while back who controlled a fleet of viking raiding parties as his cult.

      There is a great Actual Play on RPG.net, and Old Gods of Jakarta here on this very forum.

      I hope this helped.

          The rules for raising a Mummy from Duat actually allow for Mummies to be relatively active, if they want to be. I set my game in 2012 not only because it was the start of the Sothic Turn, but also because it allowed for a few years of space between then and now. If a character does end up completing their Descent, I can fast-forward a few months to a year and have a cultist raise them back into the story, without getting uncomfortably far into the future. That's a good thing, because I don't really run story-driven campaigns, instead preferring large, sprawling sandboxes.

          The Memory system is excellent though, probably one of the great mechanics in any World of Darkness game. Flashbacks allow for brief diversions away from the sandbox into more linear story-driven events, events with an extremely personal edge to them for the player characters. They make for a good change of pace, and I have a few potential ones that I hope will be more than a single session in length, giving the players a chance to step back and play out themselves in another time with different power levels. It also has the effect of putting the emphasis squarely on who the characters are, and how they react to who they were. Memory really helped my players become invested and interested in their own characters right from the outset, far more quickly than it has ever happened in any other RPG I've run or played in.

          I have been posting recaps of my sessions on this site, so you can read those if you want an idea of how I run things. From what I've seen, virtually every Mummy chronicle begins with the characters rising to face down a particular threat. They begin with power and without any context, so they have little idea of who they are facing or why they need to kill them. Almost inevitably, there should be more going on than what initially appears. They lose power more or less in proportion with the knowledge they gain. I hope in my games to be constantly feeding more and more information about the situation as a player's Sekhem drops, so that their understanding of events grows. At its best, a player should start out with the power to effect any change they want, but rarely any idea on how to go about it (barring violent ones). After a while, they end up with the knowledge of how to influence the system and get what they want out of it, but they've run out the power to do it quickly and easily. Not enough to make it impossible, ideally, but enough to make it quite hard to accomplish.

          I could ramble about this game for days, but I'm going to cut it off there. The core of the game is in the balancing of Sekhem and Memory, power and context in my mind. Who you are and why you do what you do is key to the game, but you learn these things in inverse proportion to the power you have to act on it.

                  I would also try to avoid the idea that as Sekhem drops, Mummies become weak and depressed. I mean, they obviously get weaker, but even Tier 1 Utterances are pretty strong and a Mummy has a variety of innate bonuses from Affinities to Pillar-boosted Attributes that make them plenty strong at low levels. High Sekhem makes you phenomenally powerful, it's true, but it also is the mechanic by which your purpose is enforced and your will kept shackled. At high Sekhem, you drop really quickly. It makes the pace of play frantic and frequently violent, as players try to milk the most out of their limited power. By the time it's dropped to middling to low levels though, they have the breathing room to take things a little more slowly, explore their characters, learn about the world, and flesh out their own wants and ambitions. They can certainly choose to bemoan their lost power, but a Mummy will spend a lot more time at low Sekhem than at high (and they all know that they'll return to high in due time anyway). When things slow down, and the players are no longer rolling descent checks every few scenes, that's when the players have time to think about who they are and where they want to be going.

                  The beautiful thing is that the above mechanics are enforcing a kind of behaviour that is perfectly in keeping for the Arisen, according to how they are presented in the books. Mummy: The Curse has some of the best mechanics for encouraging players to inhabit their characters that I've seen.

                          Mummies last a lot longer during a Sothic Turn, because they don't have a Purpose and thus descend at a slightly slower rate, and don't fall from Sekhem 1 to 0 until they want to or something makes them. It's still pretty easy for a Mummy on a normal Descent (one driven by a Purpose) to last at least 6 months at a time though, much longer if they start eating relics and/or hunting for those Descent resets and have some kind of long-term Purpose (one of my players has "Protect your people", which is unlikely to definitively end unless his people get wiped out and he fails his Purpose).

                          Memory 7 is when a Mummy gains a recollection of every past Descent, but even then there are still gaps you can explore. Besides, it's not as if you can ever convey every single event that has happened to that Mummy in 6,000 years, so flashbacks at high Memory still work to add more detail to the broad brushstrokes you can paint when they attain that level. Even Memory 10 has the qualifier "The Mummy remembers nearly everything about her life in Irem and prior Descents" (emphasis mine). A Mummy should know just about everything about their past at that point, but there is still room for the occasional flashback.

                          As for numbers of players, I would prefer two or three. I would definitely be hesitant about going up to four or more. Mummy is an intense game on the character side of things, and you need to be able to give each player time and room to establish and explore their own characters and how they react to revelations about their own history. On the GM's side of things, you're the one that needs to be writing flashbacks and timelines for every player in your game. More players dramatically increase the amount of work you have to do, more so in Mummy than in many games. So yeah, your initial impression is pretty much spot on.

                                      It's a little hard for me to understand the way this is all worded, so I apologize. I'll try to answer, though!

                                      Memory exploration is the part of Mummy that's suited for long-term play. Waking up to defend your tomb, hunt monsters for your cult, or search for relics are, compared to re-discovering Memory, are more suited for shorter play. Like Azahul said, your Memory is never exactly perfect; even if you remembered 'everything' there's still 6,000 years worth of memories. It's almost like you have 'supernatural Memory' and 'mundane memory.' Would a mortal even be able to remember everything after all that time, let alone having it hindered by sinister magic? You know what I mean? Because Memory, too, is largely about remembering morality and what it is to be human; it isn't solely, or even most importantly, what you remember doing per se.

                                      Then you have flashbacks to periods where they remember even more than they currently do in the campaign, and then flashbacks as to how they forgot that stuff. Then flashbacks about how that same cycle has happened dozens of times. Now your PCs sit at Memory 10 wondering if they should accept that eternal cycle, or if they should seek out the rumors of the Heretic and Apotheosis

                                                  Originally posted by reseru View Post
                                                  Had I a PC who willingly wanted to lose Memory then I would count myself a blessed GM for having such interesting things at my table ^_-
                                                  I think the aspect of Mummy that is so interesting and different from virtually every other RPG is how little control the PCs actually have in their own backstories. In that the game presumes that during the first session, the characters remember either nothing (while Memory 0) or very little - just bits of their most recent Descent, with 6000 year of history before that completely unknown to them (Memory 3). In this way the ST has a lot of control over the players' backstories, which the ST can revel to players as Memory rises, during death cycles, or (if you're in DC) with Memory mirages.

                                                  This can make for very interesting moments of players discovering things about their characters, and while I think an ST needs to be respectful to not cross too many lines I think it's also appropriate for the horror genre (of which Mummy is a part) for the ST to depict the character remembering something from their past that is a terrible failure on the character's part or truly horrific or possibly heretical against the Judges. Over a span of 6000 years I'm sure most Mummies would do things that they rationalized as appropriate in the moment (or while at low Memory) which, if they remembered, would really cause a crisis of faith or self-doubt.

                                                  So while high Memory can be useful, and is definitely the path toward Apotheosis, I think it's also a useful tool for STs to remind players of their characters' past failings. I think Apotheosis would entail taking ownership of those awful or disappointing things along with the successes; some Arisen can do that and get there while many others just break under the stress of so many memories, degenerate, and possibly repeat that process over and over.

Did you turn a new leaf, or did you simply forget how awesome the old leaf was?
