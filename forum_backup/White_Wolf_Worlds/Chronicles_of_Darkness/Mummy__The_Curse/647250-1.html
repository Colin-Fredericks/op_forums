    The book says that in the case of a rechargeable vessel it can be between four and twenty-six weeks (there's an algorithm based on the dot rating of the relic and dice rolls). It also says that if storytellers want to be a bit stricter with it they can substitute weeks for months or even years.

    So, to summarise, the limit can be anything from once a month to once every 26 years.

                    Mummy is a game about being the movers and shakers behind the secret history of the world. You are the unliving mysteries at the hearts of international conspiracies that date back before the invention of the written word, and endlessly work the inscrutable will of nameless gods even as you dig through the sands of memory to find your true self. Mummy is a game where you may well work with a foe who killed you a century ago in order to undo a plot of your own doing that's since slipped your mind and has now spiraled out of control due to generations of mistranslation by mere mortals.

                    It's a lot of fun.

                        Mummy is a game about playing a character who's as old as civilization. It's an exploration of old age - what it means, how people deal with it, etc.

                        Mummies are creatures of contradictions. They've seen the birth and death of entire cultures; and yet, they can't remember if they supported the births or brought forth the destructions of nations. When they arise, they have all the power in the world - they can destroy entire cities, if they so choose - but they have so little time to do anything, really. The sand is always running through their fingers, and the irony of a mummy's immortality is that she has to make constant priority calls.

                        The powers of the Arisen are vast and glorious and terrible, but also very specific. The Arisen are old testament gods - pillars of fire, plagues, death curses, parting the red sea, that sort of thing. There is a sadness to their vast power - theirs is a kind of magic that is ageless, but it's also static, can't change, and that the world has largely left behind; like the Arisen themselves.

                        The Arisen rise into the modern world...and find that nothing fundamental has changed, socially speaking. The downtrodden are still exactly that, and the rich still benefit from exploiting everyone else. The details might be different, but to these creatures who have seen millenia pass, the world is functionally the same as in the culture that created them.

                        The Arisen sympathize with those poor and downtrodden - their is an existence of service and slavery that can never, ever end.

                        To be Arisen is glorious. Your flesh is immortal. Your spirit seethes with magical power. You have seen the roots of civilization and whisper in their secret language. When your eyes open, they belong to a divine patron, a lord, a god. You’ve seen the gates of the underworld and know for immutable fact that death is not the end of eternal power.

                        To be Arisen is terrifying. Your body is a falsehood—a simulated corpse made out of congealed necromancy. You attended the deaths of civilizations, and you can’t remember if you held back the barbarians or put a knife in the emperor’s back. Your eyes are hollow, dry pits; your life signs are a lie you can only tell the world while your true power trickles away. You have seen the gods of the Underworld and know for a fact that death is not the end of eternal service and duty.

                                                        What are their projects? Whatever they wish. For immortals, it helps to have long term activities to keep one occupied.

                                                        The Tef-Aabhi plan out architectural (and, for the Flesh-Masons, bloodline) plans to concentrate Heka. The Mesen-Nebu continually seek to improve things, adding to their collections, amassing power for their Cults, adding more beauty to their surroundings, etc. The Maa-Kep manipulate from behind the scenes and help others with their labors, sometimes just making sure everything stays together. The Sesha-Hebsu are scribes and researchers, investigating mysteries and compiling knowledge. The Su-Menent maintain tradition and guard secrets and horrors best lost to time.

                                                        But even besides Guild motivations, any individual Mummy might want revenge on a rival, to create a monument to their Judge, seek the answer to an enigma encountered in a previous Descent, grow their cult to new heights, advance their status (in either Guild or Nome), or just help out a friend (or their descendants, should they have passed away in the mean time).

                                                        The character's Judge also presides over something, and forwarding that also offers a longer Descent.

                                                        And then, of course, there is Apotheosis...
