    Originally posted by reseru View Post
    It presents the Shuankhsen as sympathetic villains. We, the players having read the book, know why they're sympathetic, but the Silence prevents them from explaining that to your PCs in-game. Having them yearn for freedom from their cruel taskmaster, mysterious and unknowable as She is to the PCs because of the Silence, is something that many Deathless understand.

    "You know those monstrous crocodile-jawed mummies who are unquestionably evil? Turns out not all of them like being that and want a way out just like us."
    But the Silence stops the Shuankhsen from telling you that they're eating you for a totally sympathetic reason too.

    Besides, revealing that you knew the Shuankhsen as a slave who was slaughtered in life seems like an obvious Memory-related event in any Chronicle, but it's harder to show rather than tell the player about a Shuankhsen Apotheosis.

                    Though I appreciate your point, Reseru, it doesn't answer why the Deceived work just as well, if not better.                     I run Mummy with a 2e hack; this removes some of the issue (as it's easier to rationalize balancing out Integrity) but that too precludes the moral realizations of Memory.

                    I mean, the Judges punish the Arisen for daring to act human; the Shuankhsen get rewarded for tormenting and opposing the Arisen.

                    I guess I'm trying to say that they are already tragic. Nothing can add to that tragedy; they're already slaves sold to a terrible power to pay for making their whip-masters demigods. Like, that sucks. That sucks so much, and then after however long that torture was - The Rite of Return broke time, so I'm going to say "lifetimes" - Ammut spat them back out and said "k go break shit. Get revenge if you want."

                    If you want to enhance their tragedy, I'm not sure Apotheosis is the way to do it.
                    I make my Shuankhsen antagonists deeply connected to the PCs personal lives. I make them seeking "righteous" vengeance. I don't make them anything but monsters - and they are already apex monsters.

                                                            Originally posted by shkspr1048 View Post
                                                            First off, I'd say the Arisen condition is a Failure State across the board, but then, I'm a firm believer in personal autonomy, so I'm biased.
                                                            Your opinion on the merits of being Arisen isn't really the point though, and is outright wrong from a game design perspective. Being an Arisen cannot be a failure state because that's the default mode of play. More than that, there are ways to move on from being Arisen, one of which, Apotheosis, is arguably the game's "win condition". There is only one way an Arisen character can become unplayable, and that is to fall and become Shuankhsen, making it the failure state.

                                                            When it happened in my campaign I allowed the Shuankhsen character to remain playable because there were some ideas I still wanted to explore (and also because the game was rapidly nearing its end) but I didn't try to shy away from portraying that moment as a fall, a tragic end to an Arisen. That word "end" was important, there is a finality to that moment.

                                                            My original question was asking you what you feel you gain thematically by diluting the game's themes in this respect and providing a way of mitigating the "you lose" ending baked into Mummy. It's similar to giving, say, Changelings a way to recover from being recaptured by the True Fae. I'm not sure it makes the game stronger...
                                                            Originally posted by shkspr1048 View Post
                                                            Second, I fall back to my earlier statement that using Jaws of the Devourer is the quick and easy way, as opposed to the measured re-discovery of the self; or, in the Shuankhsen's case, reclamation of the self. That said, I don't think stealing Pillars from Arisen is as much an ethical stumbling block as, say, creating Fasad or killing mortals to regain Pillar points.
                                                            I dunno, I'd put the violation and cannibalisation of another being's soul on much the same level as murder or spiritual enslavement. On the Morality chart I'd certainly have it no higher than a Morality 3 sin.

                                                            Sadly, we can only agree to disagree when it comes to using experience to restore pillars. In the (sadly, far too few) conversations I've had with writers who have worked on Mummy I did actually talk about the presentation of Shuankhsen, and from what has been said I would feel very confident in saying that cannibalisation is the only method of gaining Pillars for Shuankhsen. And I would maintain that the reason it's not mentioned one way or the other in the core book comes down simply to what I was saying above. Shuankhsen are the failure state. You aren't meant to play them. Why would they need to address the expenditure of experience for characters that aren't going to be earning experience?
