                                        Inspired by Second Chances' write-up for the Purified in Arcanist's Sundered World Chronicles thread. LostLight also gets a mention due to some of the text alluding to some of his stuff.

                                        ​Purified in the Black Land

                                        As a source of one of the oldest written records on the magics which create Purified, Kemet was home to a considerable number of these Immortals. Ancient Egyptian mummification was clearly derived from a form of the ritual for purification, but was a debased and fruitless process that did nothing more than preserve the corpse. This was because almost all of the ancient Egyptian Purified were priests and magicians who reserved the accurate techniques for themselves and a few of their most favored acolytes and companions.

                                        In the Black Land, both the inner and outer paths of purifications are pursued by those seeking immortality. And the Purified are a discrete yet influential minority among its shadowy denizens. Stories and theories exist in this era on their nature and origins, and circulate among both mundane and supernatural circles. What those tales are, though, depend on who is the one doing the talking.


                                        The Chosen of Old Gods
                                        "The story is true. Once, men and women journeyed from the villages of the People into the furthest reaches of Pangaea. Like the Wise Ones, they sought out the gods for power. Yet unlike them, they did not wish to take their hearts from their defiled corpses. Those who earned their favor were bestowed great gifts, becoming their heralds and champions in the two worlds. Few of these Wanderers survived the loss of our paradise. But they walk among us, still."

                                        Not enough time has passed yet to distort the tales of the Asilal-thakh, the "Distant Wanderers". And the Uratha have managed to preserve most beyond the passing of those who would come to be called the Vinca. They remember how some of the mortal herd ventured on pilgrimages to the Border Marches, to seek out and commune with the gods who dwelled within. Most are never seen again, but those who succeeded would return to the People with a god of Pangaea as their totem.

                                        When the Sundering came, however, the Wanderers suffered just as the Uratha and the Wise did. Most were crippled as they found themselves separated from their patrons by the rising Gauntlet. Others were less fortunate, as the barrier destroyed those Pangaeans who failed to escape to either world for one reason or another. A majority of these immortals die on the spot, or otherwise go insane without a proper channel for the powers they wielded.

                                        Like other survivors of Pangaea's destruction, however, the Returned adapt. Many would shed one aspect of their nature to cope, just as the Pangaeans they served did the same. Those who severed their ties to the conceptual heart of the world became more akin to denizens of the Shadow. Their powers were diminished somewhat, but they were still as undying as the spirits were.

                                        In time, these immortals would find their place in the new world. And they would soon be joined by others, those who discovered new avenues to achieving immortality. These individuals would pioneer the first practices that would form the inner and outer paths of purification, and would thus give rise to the contemporary term of Purified.


                                        Returned from Neter-Khertet
                                        "Many of the Empire's secrets remain lost beneath the sands. But the desert always yields them to those declared worthy by the gods. However, this has not stopped others less deserving from seeking out the relics. And we may have lost what may be the crowning glory of the old kings to such thieves. Why do I tell you this? Turn your sight upon that priest, my child. Do you notice how he carries the weight of so many years despite his youth? Focus, now, and see him for what he truly is."

                                        The sorcerers of the Weret-Hekau consider it their duty to uncover, preserve, and (if necessary) destroy the works of the Predynastic ancients. But they accept with trepidation that they are not the only ones who have successfully harnessed the wonders of their kingdom's mythical past. And some of those they compete with claim to have greater rights over the Nameless Empire's relics by virtue of the fact that they had lived at the height of its glory.

                                        Few Hemka have successful dealings with them. Those men and women who live through countless flood cycles, yet never grow old even as their minds grow wiser with age. They are believed to be the custodians of Irem's greatest - and most dangerous - sorcery, which was said to make mortals ascend to become divinities unto themselves. That these people are undying by nature leads many Awakened to believe that they claimed this secret for themselves, and only share it to those they deem worthy.

                                        Among the Priests of the Fivefold Soul, it is those who made their decree to the Ab who are perhaps best equipped to deal with the Returned. Like them, they deal frequently with the demons and minor gods that populate the Neter-Khertet, and that the strongest of those entities maintain alliances with several of these Undying Ones. Such alliances, and the fact that the Undying themselves have powers of their own, have kept the Hemka from probing deeply into their affairs. And, more than once, this has even led to hostilities breaking out between members of both societies.


                                        Heretics of the Nameless Empire
                                        "Our rebellion was not the first heresy to plague the Empire. Other gods worked from the shadows to spread their influence among the slaves, and perhaps even into the halls of the Guilds. And they, too, had prophets of their own. Men and women who claimed to have been alive before the first pillars were raised. They preached that their gods once walked among the mortals, and that they may do so again if the people raised their voices in praise to them. The Shan'iatu punished those prophets as brutally as they did our own. But many of them managed to swim against the River's flow to return and continue their masters' work."

                                        Even at the height of their power, Irem was still plagued from within by practices its rulers considered taboo. The Cult of the Crocodile God and the White Rebellion were some of the most notable of these dissenters, but many other voices rang in opposition to the hymns of the Judges. The leaders of some of these heresies were witches, occult practitioners who learned to harness the Essence of the soul through blasphemous rituals.

                                        These heathens were hunted down by the Shan'iatu whenever they banded together. But it was those witches who were favored by the demons living in Neter-Khertet who were counted among their greatest adversaries. Some of these individuals are even blessed with immortality by their patrons, and could return from Duat so long as their souls were not sundered outside their bodies.

                                        In the days of the Old Kingdom, some Hemka and other lesser scholars who have searched for a connection between the Returned and the Nameless Empire come to believe that the rituals and practices used by the former may have stemmed from the latter's experiments with Sekhem. A small minoriy, however, theorize that it was the reverse. The first iterations of what will come to be called the inner and outer paths of purification may have been appropriated by the Scorpion Kings, and repurposed by them to manipulate instead the true lifeblood of the cosmos.

                                                            glamourweaver. Anything yet on a term for those closest to the Vajrastra darshana in Age of Azar? I've managed to write down some text on the Tjati, Sebau, and Sepermeru for the First Sothic Turn, and added it to the GDoc.

                                                            The Tjati act as priest–viziers to the ruling class. They believe worthy rulers are manifestations of the gods, and are determined to make sure they act that way. As a result, these Hemka readily embrace their purpose as guides and luminaries, emulating Imhotep’s example by inspiring the Pharaoh and the people to greater heights.

                                                            Sebau are harsh teachers. They instruct other sorcerers to honor Ma'at in their duties, but also carry the duty of hunting down those who have strayed from that obligation. A sorcerer must strive towards a life of balance, they say, lest he be punished by the gods in this life. And should he fail to seek proper penance, his judgement is forfeit, and his soul is condemned to the jaws of Ammut as soon as it passes into Duat.

                                                            There are some Hemka who choose to distance themselves from the people, and instead devote their time in the pursuit of ancient and forgotten lore. These sorcerers dwell near the desert, where they roam as nomadic gatherers of stories, knowledge, and mysteries. It is they who pioneer the growth of their tradition, as many of the Weret-Hekau's discoveries on the Nameless Empire were made by these intrepid seekers.
