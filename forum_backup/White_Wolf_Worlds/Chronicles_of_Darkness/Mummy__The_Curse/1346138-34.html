    I’m away from a decent keyboard, so I’ll probably write further comments this weekend when I have a chance. That said, I see two overarching issues to be addressed.

    1. While affinity and utterance mechanics received a much needed cleanup, the new mechanics, particularly in the immortals chapter, are just not fully developed and don’t reflect functional systems for some really great ideas.

    2. At least some of this is a consequence of intermittent use of copy/paste (see sorcerer merits and arisen damage for examples).

    However, many of the rules just don’t work, or don’t make sense. Non-linear time is great, but when did arisen learn 3 utterances prior to their first descent? 1E addressed this in Sothis Ascends, but that explanation is now out the window for 2E.

                    The more I think about the subject, I start to understand what doesn't clicks fully for me in the Endless section. I'm not even sure that it is the wordcount (not that I would ever have complains about more wordcount for Immortals). It is the presentation.

                    If the presentation were to start talking about the Endless as a template- that is, as a singular subset of beings- and focus around how the Endless in general work with and against mummies, and then gives a small subsection about each of the dominant Immortal types and their behavior (perhaps in a splat like way, which may be their goal as we don't have art yet), then would give us a good chunk of mechanics for "how to make any immortal you want" with some special tricks unique to each Immortal splat (like a "defining merit" or something like that), and then continue to present a sample character for each of the dominant splats, I think it would have felt better for me. I could have got that the Endless are a big thing, and that there are many methods of Immortality, of which those with the greatest ties for Mummy have been explored in depth. Now, it feels like they tries to squeeze seven supernatural beings (and dropping a two) which are barely connected to each other into a single mechanical system, which doesn't really work for them all (the Purified are basically a Major Template in 1e, for example).

                    What I am trying to say is that it feels like the writers tried their best at squeezing the whole Immortals book into less than a chapter and gave some very loose rules at the end of the section, with dedicating a few lines to a chosen Immortal types. Perhaps instead they should have went with a more cohesive presentation, where the Endless are a singular phenomena of people who manipulate Sekhem to extend their lives, like vampirism, or even the Paths of Awakening, or something similar of that concept (an X splat, so to speak). Just my two cents on the subject. Besides, the main reason that I think about it is because Immortals is my favorite CofD book (followed by Inferno and Hunter: the Vigil tied, and Witchfinders in the third place), so I assume if someone was not a big fan of 1e, they probably care less about the subject.

                        Something I was hoping would be covered in the Body Thieves section is what happens when you end up in an unfamiliar body. There are roleplaying aspects to consider, but also some mechanical issues: is there a period of adjustment while you familiarize yourself with your new form? Do you just swap out your physical attributes for your new host's physical attributes? If the host has biological mental conditions (but not Conditions), what happens with your mental attributes? That sort of thing.

                        Such rules would also be applicable when an Arisen takes over a cultist, though in that case I could also see a case for the Mummy having access to some “lateral character development”, trading in Attribute dots where her original form was weaker than her new form in exchange for more rapid development of an Attribute where her original form was stronger than her new form.

                                    Just to clarify to make sure you get the correct impression (not saying you didn't, but I assume that there are other Immortal fans who may or may not back the Kickstarter according to how Immortals are presented in 2e)

                                    The section (which is less than a chapter, that includes also all kinds of antagonists and allies to the Arisen) details that the Endless are a collective name that the Arisen use for Immortals who are neither Deathless or Lifeless, and that they are connected to Sekhem by the virtue of having immortality (Sekhem is pure lifeforce, more or less, after all). It then proceeds to present a writeup for the Blood Bathers, Body Thieves, Eternals and Reborn, without mechanics but with some mechanics-like fluff (like describing the features of the Bathing Ritual, or the Talents of Body Thieves, which do get mechanics for their talents). Each section is followed by a sample character, which is weird because we are yet to know the mechanics for the template, and as such gives the feeling that each immortal is a different splat on its own, with its own mechanics. Then it gives a few very short lines for Patchwork People, Purified and Visitors (which changes more or less nothing for the first two and making the Purified to stand out even more due to their connection to the Shadow, and basically the Visitor's description from the Dark Eras companion). It then concludes to give a description of the Endless Micro Template, with three merits to support it, where one is reprinted from Hurt Locker (Supernatural Resistance). Endless also have Sekhem rating due to their defining merit.

                                    As such, it gives a very unclear feeling about the Immortals- if the Endless are the same Template, why each is treated as a different creature earlier? How do I deal with things like the Purified in the Endless Micro Template? Are they a cohesive group of beings, each representing the powers of Sekhem and Immortality differently, or each are a special beast which has its own ways to use Sekhem, and the Endless Template is like the Horror rules- a general system for all kinds of unrelated creatures? How far Sekhem interact with their immortality? It is clear for some (like Bathers and Eternal), and less clear for others (like Mental Body Thieves or the Reborn).

                                    That's my review of it- it is not a bad or flawed execution, only that it feels a bit lacking for me as an Immortals fan.

                                            Actually, I'm curious as to exactly how much homebrewing would be needed. After Chapter 5 came out, I broke open my copy of WoD:I again to review things. And you know what? Mechanically, there really isn't much there that needs changing. We've already got Blood Bather and Body Thief updates in Dark Eras Companion, and the “Lesser Immortals” (Visitors, Patchwork People, Wardens, Reborn, Eternals, and Harvesters) really don't have much in the way of mechanics for the 1e→2e change to trip over; so I focused mainly on the Purified. And you know what? I only found three things that need updating, and two of them are debatable:

                                            First, there are several references to the Purified being able to interact with both ghosts and spirits in Twilight; but in 1e, that was standard: anyone who could interact with Twilight could interact with ghosts, spirits, qashmallim, and virtually anything else that might be lurking in Twilight. So a 2e update of the Purified would need to clarify this point: do the Purified interact with ghosts, spirits, both, or everything in Twilight? (My personal preference would be “ghosts and spirits” — the former because of their human nature, the latter because of what becoming Purified did to them; but I can see arguments for several of the options.) Incidentally, this is the same issue that needs to be addressed with the Amkhata.

                                            The second thing is Integrity. A major difference between 1e and 2e is that Morality wasn't challenged by witnessing the supernatural; Integrity is. Are the Endless (not just the Purified) immunized from that? Frankly, it's an issue that ghouls, wolf-bloods, Proximi, fae-touched, and other “lesser template humans” share: they're now immersed in the supernatural world; are they going to inevitably and rapidly be broken by it? (That said, I think that's something that ought to have been addressed in the Chronicles of Darkness rulebook, not here.)

                                            The final thing that needs updating is the experience costs: specifically for Chi, Siddhi, and Spirit Numina.

                                            Oh: and some of the Siddhi make reference to “Numina” when they should reference “Manifestations and Numina”.

                                            Really, that's about it.

                                            And with that in mind, I think what I'd really like to see would be a “v1.1” of Immortals that leaves the vast majority of the book intact and only makes those tiny changes that are needed to make it compatible with the 2e rules. Maybe rebrand it as a Mummy supplement, adding some information about how each kind of Immortal interacts with the Deathless; if not, just change its name from “World of Darkness: Immortals” to “Chronicles of Darkness: Immortals”, toss it into the Scroll of Fading Memories, et voilà!
