    The Book of Going Westward, snippets of a fan project

    06-19-2023, 07:25 PM
    Hi everyone. So for some time now I've had an idea in my head that I want to hammer out in ernest this year. Its an ambitious project and frankly I don't know if my notoriously flighty muse will ever have the willpower to see it through completely. As a means of encouraging myself to keep working at it and as a fail safe that I can at least get some ideas out into the world even if I never finished the whole goddamned thing I decided to start this thread and present a few snippets and exerpts as I have the time and energy to produce them. These are all super rough draft bits and will go through a few drafts before I'd be satisfied to submit any of them to the Storyteller Vault. Please keep that in mind, but criticism, suggestions to improve or expand, etc are all very welcome. I am also very aware I am a wordy wordy bastard and will need to trim these down before the final draft. Sorry about that.

    Anyway, the intent of this project is to explore as a fan of Mummy the Curse the proposed Book of Going Westward which would have (if we'd hit that kickstarter goal) explored all 42 Judges in greater detail. I like what the writers have done so far with the 11 or so Judges we've discussed and I wanted to give my take, expand on their ideas and cover the remaining gods of Irem in some fashion. I'm opting for a first person narrative on this because it helps me get into the Lovecraftian mindset I prefer to use for Mummy and it kind of gives the reader "permission" to take or ignore what is presented as fact or just the foolish incorrect musings of one flawed persona. Hopefully you will enjoy these snippets and it will encourage me to keep writing them. In addition to what I'm presenting here I want to talk about rites and traditions of the gods, some of their more noteable cults (one example each of the big three- Tribes, Enterprises, Conspiracies), what kinds of Mummies are drawn to them, and known examples of their emanations. The speaker is one of the First Prophets of the Su-Menent sharing his knowledge in the wake of a Judge's Emanation that recently devastated a Nome with a mind to prevent future tragedies. The lovecraftian inspiration varies from subject to subject, but I think it will be pretty obvious for this first snippet.

    (I have two other ideas, one involving a guide to new immortals and expanding on the abilities of established ones presented as a 'record' on the tomb wall of a Mesen Nebu who has worked with several would be and established immortals as a means of studying the nature of their Dedwen, and another presented as the journal of a mortal archeologist on a doomed expedition for the cursed city of Ubar that becomes a first person narrative about the Shu-ankhsen's powers, traditions, factions and lives explored with a similar depth as the Guilds received in Guildhalls of the Deathless. Again, who knows how far I'll get working on them, but I'll get as far as I get).

    Anyway, without further adieu.

    Appearance
    It is whispered that the one called the Lion’s Jaws withdraws far from the City of Black Spines and his fellow Judges when they are not called upon to preside over Fate and judgement of wayward souls. Often he resides regally upon a raised dais on the deck of a great solar baroque ferried by the oars of one million restless shades. The dead mark his passing through Duat by a deep growl like distant thunder. At times he is seen sailing through the heavens to distant corners of outer Duat or even the far reaches of Neter Kertet. At other times he seeks the solitude of shadowy Nu, the uncreated sea that laps beyond the shores of this reality. Here he contemplates the calm stillness before the advent of Gods and men. Upon his vessel he is attended by a court of a thousand musicians drawn from the ranks of the ghosts and demons alike, playing unearthly melodies equally beautiful and grotesque upon flutes of bone and sistrums of glass. Here too dance a company of blue skinned courtesans, presenting a poise and grace unseen by the eyes of the living. Soothing spices from nameless oasis in Duat and obscure lands far beyond death are burned in braziers of green flame, and each night great feasts of finely cooked beasts of forgotten times and wines of unspoken vintages are presented in Ankhi’s honor. And thus do his attendants spend their eternity, seeking forever to please their ancient lord and stay the primordial wrath that burns within his heart.
    Ankhi wears the visage of a great, hulking leonine monstrosity. Those who enter his court says his full size varies with each visitation, from a mighty cat the size an elephant to a horror that puts the great pillars of the lost city to shame. Always he towers well over the heads of his servants, gazing down on those who would dare his patience with gleaming red eyes. His voice is a low, calm whisper, often a surprise to those guests unfamiliar with his habits. His servants describe with a dawning sense of horror that what they had taken to be a pale mane of fur encircling his face and jaws is in reality a thick, macabre tangle of bleach bones. Vast tangles of rattling spinal columns emerge in mockery of strands of living hair, complimented and interspersed with femurs, rib cages, leering skulls and more to form a thick osseous jungle. Those with more bravery than sense who have ventured close enough to witness such things claim the bones seem to emerge wholesale from Anki’s flesh as living hair does, and my brothers within the Su-Mement claim the array of skeletons ranges widely from human to all manner of beast unto things that ceased to walk our world long before the Nameless Empire. Some even claim the bones themselves speak, whispering strange blasphemies and curses in tongues none of my colleagues could identify.
    The rest of Ankhi’s body appears more akin to a terrestrial lion, which a few of his visitors might find comfort in. However, his hide shows numerous signs of battle and violence, as if Ankhi or some unknown foe had savagely marred his hide with tooth and claws in ages past, leaving an apparent network of lacerations and angry scars covering his flesh. The most prominent sign of scarring lies upon his face, however, his jaws appearing to have been cut wide open so that no flesh or cheeks hinder the sight of the grinning maw he presents to visitors. Most of his guests have the unsettling sense that his teeth are far to wide, too sharp, and too numerous. When displeased his jaws are said to open far too wide, more like a serpent than a feline. The inside of the maw features row upon row of teeth like a legion of spears, like the jaws of a shark. A hideous, bloated tongue far too long and serpentine to be found in a feline mouth sometimes emerges, sometimes accompanied by a host of similar appendages riddled with long, sharpened protrusions running down their grotesque lengths.
    There are few who have survived an encounter with Ankhi in the midst of his rage. Rarer still are those willing to speak on the subject. It is said that every blasphemy cursing the gods, every bitter whisper and angry cry lamenting fate, every word petty and great spoken in anger over the course the last several thousands of years or unto the next thousand echoes across space and time and slips into the darkness of Duat to reverberate in the heart of the Lion’s Jaws. It is only by a will of absolute steel and the aid of a vast entourage of attendants that the Roar keeps that primordial rage in check. When his wrath grows beyond his ability to master (says, perhaps, when a servant or a visitor finds a way to displease him) Ankhi is said not merely to lose his composure but his very grip on rational thought at all. He loses himself to a blind, uncomprehending wrath that is terrible to behold. His jaws distended, an animalistic scream like nothing of the living world thunders from his jaws. His boney mane thrashing with a rattling cacophony that is only defeaned by the rising scream of his rage. The wise will flee or work to assuage his wrath as quickly as possible. If unabated, the screams will rise higher and higher, the jaws seeming to extend beyond any rational limit as the skin on his lips rips further open. At this time the truth of his visage becomes clear, as what was taken to be scars and lacerations begin to tear open…each revealing a sickening row of teeth and lashing tongue, each new maw upon his flesh adding to the cacophony of his rage. Any rationality in his form is abandoned, as his body becomes a walking grotesquerie of gaping maws too vast and endless to conform to rational, Euclidean measurements. His screams shall reverberate across Duat. Shades and demons and less comprehensible things will rip and tear at their flesh to deafen themselves from the hideous sound, but in their minds the merciless screech will still reverberate. There is a legend, supposedly written on the scroll of ages and glimpsed only by Re and winged Isuit, that should a day come when Ankhi roars out the full unbridled depths of his fury it will shatter the gates of Duat like glass and the lands of the living themselves will quake beneath his screams before reality cracks and falls to the merciful silence of the Devourer.
    This, in turn, may explain why Ankhi holds those who fall beneath his judgments in such contempt even compared to the mercies of his fellow gods. Those that cannot control their petty, mortal fits of rage are unlikely to receive sympathy from the Roar.

                      A tribal cult devoted to Maa-Nantuuf. Have not had as much chance to write as I would like. Might make up for lost time this weekend.

                      The Cult of the Black Ram is reckoned among the servants of Maa-Nantuuf to be the largest and most ancient Scorpion Cult to ever exist. This claim is dubious on many accounts, most obviously because making a proper census of the notoriously secretive and isolated societies of the Arisen is a near impossibility. More to the point, however, is that there ceased one truly united Cult of the Black Ram many eons ago. Since the most ancient beginnings of the cult in the Nile city of Khent-min the servants of the Black Ram have grown and fractured and expanded across history. The cultists of Maa-Nantuuf have never been shy about insinuating themselves among the larger cults of younger gods. Thus the Cult of the Black Ram has operated within the temples of the god Min (a god some Arisen scholars claim to be simply the bastardized worship of Maa-Nantuuf by the Egyptian communities that inherited the ways of Irem) and Khnum, and later the mystery cults of Pan and Cernunnos. Roman, Celtic and Germanic faiths spread the Cult across Europe and Northern Asia. The Black Ram has conspired and cavorted with numerous other societies and entities in the shadows. Despite this the society fractured and diverged innumerable times as it spread. Despite a common heritage of Rites and traditions one cult is unlikely to remain in contact with another for any extended period of time. The witch trials of the past few centuries did much to scatter and isolate the old cults, in many cases reducing their numbers to only a singular priest or priestess in a given community. Others were lucky enough to survive in small families that kept their traditions alive in secret. One such extended family is known as the Nesbits, and still operates in the town of Salem in the Americas. The Cult of the Black Ram, even when taken as the sum total of all the associated cults, should not be understood as some monolithic entity. There was never a singular immense Witch-Cult as Margaret Murray would have it, let alone some sinister conspiracy of devil worshipers often proclaimed by the Christian Church or certain foolish individuals in more recent decades. Maa-Nantuuf is one of numerous horned gods, benign and sinister, that the devout and desperate have prayed to throughout time. That said, the Cult of the Black Ram perhaps deserve the suspicion and doubt held by outsiders better than many rival faiths. It should be remembered that Maa-Nantuuf prohibits the polluting or indulgence of harmful substances. On the one hand this has made the Cult of the Black Ram a reliable source of herbal remedies and primitive healing. The Rites of Maa-Nantuuf can protect from poison and disease to those devoted to him. However, those protections can also be rescinded from those that offend or threaten his cults. Thus enemies of the Black Ram tend to fall ill or suffer infection with terrible frequency. Others are rumored to be cursed with completely unnatural taints, waking in terrible pain to find their bodies infested with insects, rodents, broken glass and more grotesque phenomena. Other cults lay certain clever traps to test outsiders, offering tempting bargains or strange concoctions with the promise of power or luck, and thus subject the foolish to the judgments of their god. In any case, the Cult of the Black Ram has made many strange bargains and alliances across the centuries, and thus may be a dangerous crossroads of entities and agendas the Arisen have little understanding of.

                          A cult of Nekhenhu

                          Enterprise
                          In the most ancient days before the raising of Irem’s pillars the Queen of Innocence was said to favor the land of Nekhen. This land and the artifacts scattered with her boundaries are symbols of great triumphs and tragedies for the Arisen. It was in the land of Nekhen that the first Pharaoh arose when Irem vanished, King Scorpion, a ruler who by all available accounts was true loyalist of the traditions of the Nameless Empire. It was only later dynasties that corrupted our ways into the lesser culture of the Egyptians. Yet it was also here that the powerful relics of Scorpion inspired a nihilistic struggle during the first Sothic Turn, a war that blighted the land and inspired the Arisen to begin the Diaspora across the world. But Nekhenhu demands her servant never to look away from the truth, no matter how terrible. Thus it is that many of the great relics from that ancient place have made their way into the Ashmolean Museum of distant Oxford. The Arisen that call this university town home refuse to allow the shame of their actions to fade. Among the collection of the Museum is a broken macehead bearing the insignia of the Scorpion King. Is it the remains of the ancient relic that launched such a bloody conflict. Not even the Arisen know the answer.
                          A large cult known as the Khri Habi (Scroll Bearers) have served the interests of the Closed Books and the Innocent for centuries. Oxford hosts one of the largest collections of texts ancient and modern in all the world. Inevitably, some of those texts proved to carry potent magic. As decreed by the Judges those text were rendered sent on to the Judges whenever the mummies discovered them. But even a mundane text can provide knowledge wondrous and terrible. In the interest of preserving such macabre secrets, it has been the tradition of the Oxford Arisen to donate their texts once they have been rendered free of magic and curses. For over a century certain texts (often those of an “obscene” nature, however the English society chose to define the term in any given era) were placed in the Phi section of the Bodleian Library, a collection of restricted and blasphemous manuscripts. The Phi collection was supposedly abolished and the works redistributed among the other stacks several years ago. But in truth the Phi collection still exists, now consisting of a little over 200 noteworthy texts, tablets, scrolls and accoutrements of the occult. In keeping with the mandate of Nekhenhu prospective cultists among the Khri Habi are invited under guidance and supervision of one of the senior directors of this portion of the library (e.g. one of the local Arisen) to peruse the shelves and learn the dark secrets of our world. Other cultists are seeded among the professors and graduates who venture far and wide to collect new truths and terrors to bring to their Arisen masters. Of course, rumors persist that a few choice texts hidden among the hoard are not as disenchanted as the scroll bearers claim, and serve as a potent arsenal in defending the library and school from rivals. The associated universities of Oxford often fund expeditions and research into matters some would claim men were not meant to know. Such is the will of Nekhenhu.

                                          Some updates. I've made some decisions about the format of the book and the identity of the writer. I decided to attempt (not necessarily succeed) to make the Book of Going Westward a sort of foil to Dreams of Avarice. I thought up a few ideas, none of which really interested me, until I landed on something that appealed. I like the idea of the Book being a game prop like Avarice or the WoD Book of Nod. Since the Heretic was a Mesen-Nebu who created a text, I wanted the Book to likewise be a relic produced by a different guild. Although the Su-Menent have never been my favorite Guild it just made the most sense for a work about the Judges to come from the priests that serve them so closely. And since that would make the Book an Uter, I figured we might as well go full on Necronomicon (although this is more an Evil Dead thing than Lovecraft) and make it a text wrapped in human skinned, inked in blood, etc. From there things took some weird turns.

                                          The Book is written by Antaeus, a ill fated Sadikh that went mad from the revelation of what he truly was. He was a servant of Senwosret (who Antaeus refers to as Sesostris), a Second Prophet an Tp-A priest of the Anatolian region, a servant of Arem Abfu the Final Judge. He is a high ranking archivist of the Shepherds of the Chamber and is obsessed with maintaining a high memory and collecting forbidden lore and keeping it hidden. He remembers the first Sothic Turn, and has long wondered what secrets were tattooed upon the enigmatic Sha from that era. In his madness Antaeus finds a method to reproduce those ancient texts due to the connection between the Sha and himself as a Sadikh. It just happens to involve betrayal of his master on an epic scale. By harvesting the hearts of his fellow Sadikh and invoking the ritual he has devised Antaeus can twist the ritual of the engraved heart into something more ancient, inscribing the words handed supposedly handed down by Sutekh into his Book.
                                          Most of the action takes place in the city of Pergamon between 200 BC and into the 4th century AD. Pergamon once had a library that rivaled the one at Alexandria, and was so famous for using animal skins instead of papyrus that the word ‘parchment’ was originally derived from Pergamon. That and the famous Temple of Asclepius patronized by the famous ancient surgeon Galen? Yeah, just seemed like too natural a fit for a Su-Menent and servant interested in knowledge.
                                          Other elements I’m putting into all this-
                                          1. The Book supposedly has seven copies created by Antaeus. Unlike Dreams of Avarice there is no built in means for the text to reproduce itself so I wanted there to be a finite number large enough to appear wherever needed with little justification required. The text we are concerned with apparently made it into the Order of Izft’s secret vault along with numerous other works about the Arisen, and from there appears to have made it into the library of the Czar Ivan the Terrible (possibly along with other works from the vault, most likely given as part of a dowry from his byzantine bride). The first edition book WoD: Immortals (which first introduced the immortals seen in Mummy’s current edition) established that a large minority of the Patchwork People once served in the KGB as high ranking officials, and now many of the facilities that maintain their immortality are run by the Russian mob. So, you have a group of very dangerous people with access to Russian occult secrets suddenly in dire straights with the collapse of the Soviet Union and needing a steady source of income to maintain their immortality. Inspired by the scenario from the Cthulhu sourcebook Bookhounds of London (except set in Eastern Europe and/or West Asia) there was a sudden boom in the occult blackmarket as a direct result of the scramble to get at the Czar’s secret library and other vaults beneath the Kremlin. The Book of Going Westward wound up in the midst of this, being traded from collector to collector until it wound up in the hands of Prokneft (the Scorpion Cult from 1st edition masked as a major oil company in Siberia but secretly interested in relic collection and gathering intel on rival scorpion cults). The problem is that with the rise of Putin the Russian state is interested in reaquiring their lost artifacts, the Mafia is interested in keeping ahold of them and the Patchwork People are interested in playing whichever side they have to against every other side to stay alive. A bit of a chaotic situation to walk into to say the least, let alone exit with such a rare artifact. The opening narrative is a field report to the corporate CEO about the artifact and the hornet’s nest they walked into.
                                          2. There are vague implications in 1st edition that there is some kind of hierarchy to the Judges. Some are stated to be above others, some are the ‘most foundational’ and so on. I’m attempting to ‘rank’ the Judges based on this idea, dividing them into the Five pillars and then dividing them further from ‘highest’ to ‘lowest’. Most my totally unscientific opinion about which judge sounds more above another. The ranks correspond to the seven gates of Duat, which make up a chapter of the Book in turn. Like Dreams of Avarice we break up the ‘chapters’ with biographical entries from Antaeus. Some Judges are also contexted or opposed with another Judge- some examples are obvious, like the First and Final Judge. Some are more subtle. As often as possible I’ll take note of these oppositions and describe what that means for their cults.
                                          3. My biggest hang up right now is how to present the text of the ‘Sha’ entries. I’m not great at writing in especially archaic languages and I’m afraid the attempt will be laughable. Writing as Antaeus has been tricky enough. I wanted to use the Egyptian Book of the Dead for inspiration but…well, at its heart the BOD is just a esoteric instruction manual to troubleshoot the afterlife. Literally most of the writing is ‘go through that door, tell the demon guard this, don’t walk there, etc’. In the end I’m probably going to cop out and have the texts be in a cypher or too archaic and spiritual or some piss poor excuse and have Antaeus just describe what they say in his own words rather than a translation. A little lame, but trying to jump from modern to fake Classical to Fake Bronze Age speech is kind of killing my brain and progress has been pretty glacial already.
                                          4. Speaking of progress, I have a draft of Antaeus’ introduction. Not happy with it at all, frankly, but its not the worst first draft I’ve ever made. Cleaning it up a bit and posting it soon. The Prokneft introduction….okay, that might actually be my worst attempt at a first draft. That’ll take longer.

                                          Anyway, to show you something here’s my current table of contents.

                                          The Book of Going Westward

                                          Table of Contents

                                              I eagerly await this project. And I think I can speak for most of us that the snippets you have shown so far are superb.
                                              In addition, I did some searching on the Judges and how often an allusion to there being a hierarchy. Here is what I found.

                                              The Judges of Duat

                                              Arem-Abfu, the Final Judge (Unstated, but likely high)

                                              Usekh-Nemtet, the First Judge (Often seen as the judge of judges, his understanding of the divine order is unparalleled.)

                                              The Judges of Shadow
                                              • Am-Khaibit, the Eater of Shadows (Unstated)
                                              • Artem-Khet, the Crocodile’s Tears (Unstated)
                                              • Her-Uru, the Face of Dread (Unstated)
                                              • Nebha, the Flame (Perhaps the least (most foundational) Judge of Shadow.)
                                              • Nekhenhu, the Innocent (Unstated)
                                              • Tcheser-Tep, the Exiled Hand (Unstated)
                                              • Tenemhu, the Retreater (Unstated)
                                              • Unem-Sef, Eater of Blood (this Judge is whispered to be feared even by his own kind.)
                                              The Judges of Spirit
                                              • An-Afkh, the Bringer of His Arm (Unstated)
                                              • Heraf-Het, Whose Face Is Behind It (has a reputation for being one of the highest of Judges.)
                                              • Neb-Abitu, Lord of Horns (Neb-Abitu is one of the highest Judges of spirit.)
                                              • Nefer-Tem, the Eternal Lotus (Nefer-Tem is perhaps the highest of Judges, second only to Usekh-Nemtet.)
                                              • Neheb-Nefert, the Beautiful One (Neheb-Nefert is among the highest arbiters of the mortal soul.)
                                              • Ser-Kheru, the Disposer of Speech (Unstated)
                                              • Shet-Kheru, Orderer of Speech (Called the Orderer of Speech, the Arisen speak of him as brother to Nefer-Tem, and thus among the highest of Judges.)
                                              • Utu-Nesert, Vigorous of Fire (Unstated)
                                              The Judges of Heart
                                              • Akhi, the Roar (Unstated)
                                              • Hepet-Khet, Embraced of the Flame (Unstated)
                                              • Neb-Heru, the Lord Above (It’s rumored that Neb-Heru even keeps watch over his fellow Judges for the same reason.)
                                              • Neheb-Ka, the One Who Unifies (Unstated)
                                              • Qerrti, the Double Nile Source (Unstated)
                                              • Ruruti, the Double Lion Judge (Unstated)
                                              • Ser-Tihu, Disposer of Wrath (Ser-Tihu is perhaps the highest arbiter of the mortal heart, second only to Usekh-Nemtet in his wisdom.)
                                              • Tutuutef, the Giver of Wickedness (Unstated)
                                              The Judges of Essence
                                              • An-Hotep, the Bringer of Sacrifice (Unstated)
                                              • Fentu, the Snout (Unstated)
                                              • Hetch-Abhu, Shining Teeth (Unstated)
                                              • Maa-Nantuuf, the Seer of What is Brought Forth (Unstated)
                                              • Neha-Hatu, the Stinking Body (Unstated)
                                              • Set-Qesu, Crusher of Bones (Unstated)
                                              • Ta-Retinhu, the Fiery Foot (Ta-Retinhu holds a special dark place in the annals of Arisen legends.)
                                              • Uamenti, the Seed Entwined (Unstated)
                                              The Judges of Name
                                              • Bastu, the Stare (Unstated)
                                              • Kenemti, the Penitent (Unstated)
                                              • Khem-Inhu, the Overthrower (Khem-Inhu is a higher Judge)
                                              • Neb-Imkhu, the Lord of Faces (Called the Lord Imkhu, the Arisen consider this Judge to be brother to the Lord of Horns.)
                                              • Sekhriu, the Balanced Scales (Unstated)
                                              • Tem-Sepu, Source of Command (Unstated)
                                              • Uatch-Rekhet, the Rectifier (Unstated)
                                              • Unem-Besek, the Eater of Entrails (Unstated)
