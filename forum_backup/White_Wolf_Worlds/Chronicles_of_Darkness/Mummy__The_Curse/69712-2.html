            Thematically, it should be easy to cross-over with Vampire and possibly even Mage and/or Geist, but yes, generally speaking it takes more work for the storyteller to cross Mummy over with games like Changeling and Werewolf, partially because of Mummy's ingrained cultural bias and metaplot.

            One of the issues I think makes cross-over difficult is that all nWoD games are distinct entities from one another, despite needing the "Core" rulebook to play any of them. Themes are a bit underdeveloped (Hunter, and Geist) or weird (Werewolf, Mage, Promethean and Mummy). On the whole, Vampire and Changeling (and possibly Demon now) do the best job of making players walk the fine line between human and monster. Werewolf could definitely have benefited from a much more humanistic viewpoint (humans are pack animals too) and Mage is simply consequence-free with respect to humanism. Ultimately these are damaging to the horror aspect of these games, as horror is a genre that is based upon the human/other dichotomy.

            Note that I don't mean the current themes of these games should be rewritten. They are popular as is, so expecting a lot of change isn't something I expect or even advocate for. I am trying to articulate what it is about these games that I think makes it hard to run them as HORROR games and to cross them over with each other.

                        Now, I might be being completely unreasonable, CAS might just be a very busy guy, he might not have the sort of time free that Rose or Matt spend on various forums chatting with fans. If so fair enough, I don't know his circumstances and it would be wrong of me to draw any conclusions without having all the facts.
                        The contact that we've been getting with game developers in recent years has us all a bit spoiled (myself included). Game writers tend to be freelancers with multiple projects going at any given point and not all of them are going to be social media gurus, public-relations oriented, or continually available for discussions they are not being paid to have. And even if they love their projects and their fans, they have to concentrate on their daily bread. Given the immediacy of the internet, however, we are expecting more and more of them, whether they are compensated for it or feel up for it or not. Not everyone who signs up to work on a book or game line is prepared to serve it forever after. Having a more organized way of getting updates and spoilers to fans without relying on the writers to promote them themselves would be ideal, if unlikely.

                        Mummy might be a difficult sell because it insists on being what it is, but that doesn't mean that its chosen focus or mechanics are mistakes or that they have to work out for the worst. Its success as a game will likely depend on how Storytellers pitch it and run it, since it does demand a lot from the ST and the ST can do a lot to set the tone (and make it sexy to players). Its financial success will depend on a number of factors, including how visibly it's run (at stores, cons, etc.), how much information is available for it online, and whether or not it gets a God-Machine update (even if it's just a short conversion guide to bring it up to speed). But its financial success shouldn't be laid at CAS's feet alone, or even primarily.

                        This isn't to say I don't have my own issues with the game; nothing is perfect. But not every game is going to be everyone's cup of tea nor should we expect each line to appeal to everybody. This is part of why the WoD has as many splats as it does, and why it promotes some games that come at things differently (like Promethean and Mummy). If one splat doesn't work for you as written, you can try another or you can take what you like from a splat and rework the rest to suit your tastes. It was refreshing to see Mummy shaking things up, even if it risked offending or turning some folks off.

                        My own attempt to support Mummy has gone well beyond that one game, but it's something, and I do try to post Mummy spoilers and such when I find them.

                                                    Few interesting additional points brought up in the last couple of posts.

                                                    1) Is the game too mono-cultured? Personally, I have no problem with the Egyptian emphasis. Do I think it is a problem for other people looking at the game? Absolutely. All the other game lines have made a point of trying to be as universal in their cultural underpinnings as possible.

                                                    Yes Vampire is inherently based off of Eastern European myth. However, the developers made every effort with the different clans to give different cultural bases for each. Bloodlines only made vampires more multicultural and in B&S even the Covenants change from country to country. This same approach was applied to Werewolf, Mage, Promethean, Changeling and Hunter. All are based in one cultures idea of a supernatural "type" at their heart (Werewolf has more in common with Native American ideas then the Western European Werewolf folklore) but branches out into many other cultures ideas of the same type of being an incorporates them all into the splat type.

                                                    Giest and Demon take a different approach by basically paying lip service to their central concept (Ghosts and Demons) while kinda being a deconstruction of that creature type. In addition, they rely on game specific fluff which has little to know precedence in real world folklore traditions. So they effectively side-step the whole issue.

                                                    Mummy is inherently Egyptian, even if its fluff roots in in "proto-Egypt" it uses all the tropes and language we associate with Ancient Egypt and even some the meta stuff relies on its culturally specific nature to say the least. Would the game have been more accessible if they had gone for a more multi-cultural take on Mummy's? Maybe, I would have liked to see the other types e.g. self-mummified, bog mummy's, ice mummy's etc. and this could have been accomplished by using a less culturally focused "Ur" civilization. However, that's neither here nor there really. The fact is that cultural specificity has heard the game to a certain extent.

                                                    2) Mummy being difficult to Crossover. This points been discussed at length already. The only thing I have to add is that I don't see crossover gamer's in the NWoD as a "minority" (to use Azahul's term) you see a crossover thread spring up at least once a week in the more active forums on this website. Plus I know from the conventions I've been too that its a common practice among many gamer groups. So the issue that Mummy is not crossover friendly is indeed an issue with the game that will have impacted it's reception.

                                                    3) Writer - Consumer contact. KismetRose brought this one up again from one of my earlier posts. I agree that social media allows for a greater level of interconnection between creators and consumers. Likewise, I believe it makes such communication much more available to constant. Is is detrimental to the writers process? Could be, could not be.

                                                    Rose and Matt have outright admitted that the time they have spent with the fans on the forums has helped them shape games and been useful in weeding out problems that might have otherwise made it through into the final version of said games. Has their correspondence taken away from time they could be writing? Yes. Has the fact that fans have pointed out issue that have meant the writers have had to go back and rewrite things, thereby taking away from their time to write new content? Yes. Have either of these things been inherently bad? No. They've only made the fanbase feel appreciated and hightened the quality of the games being produced.

                                                    I don't believe for a second that any writer in correspondence with the fans is going to take the time out to address every question, complaint and issue people post to them. I do believe they will read these things and answer what they feel is most important or interesting. Ultimately, that's fair. They have lives and jobs to be getting on with and them taking the time to answer such things is a blessing for us. Considering the common sense factor involved. I honestly don't think any amount of fan feedback will "stop a writer from writing".

                                                    I don't know how many projects CAS is juggling. I don't know his schedule or his workload at the moment. So I can't say anything for certain. What I can say from the evidence I have is that CAS was enthused about Mummy, it was a vision of a game he had in his mind since the OWoD version came out. In every interview his love for the game has come across. He was obviously passionate about it. He wanted it to be a good game with interesting things going on.

                                                    Given all that, I would expect him to care about the feedback the fans of the game are giving him via social media. As its the best lipnus test he has to see about the games reception and what people might want from it as the line progresses. At least perhaps indulging in the basically interesting activity of having a bit of banter on the forums with the fans. From what evidence I have it's not like the Mummy fan base inundated him with requests, complaints and questions. From what I have seen on this forum at least we've been slinging around the same old complaints and questions since shortly after the game came out. Sure some of these questions are "spoilers" for the metaplot getting revealed as the game line continues. Others are not and it would hurt no one for them to be answered or explained.

                                                    4) DC's editor problems. Personally, I'm glad they got a new editor. Considering that the last one had not finished editing a 100 page book in 7 WEEKS! I mean come on, even part time that's a joke. I know editors (part time ones) and they have got through 300 pages of text in 2 weeks. Hopefully the new editor won't be such a joker and actually get on with it. Also am I the only one thinking they should have put out for art while the 7 WEEKS of editing were going on? That way the book could have gone straight into layout.
