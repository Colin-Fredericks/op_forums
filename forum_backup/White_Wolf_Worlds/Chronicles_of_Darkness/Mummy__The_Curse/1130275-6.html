                                        Originally posted by Ben Linus View Post
                                        I would like to see something similar to what was done in Vampire and Werewolf. In both was brought a little-worked enemy in supplements as something important the configuration that return to the world, in the case of Mummies, could be a rebirth of the inhuman races before civilization , beings who represent the enemies of the judges of any mummy.

                                        That would even bring all sorts of mummies to the basic book, since they though they continued to hate each other, would have a common enemy.
                                        The rebirth of the pre-human species feels like it's really just retreading much the same space as the Idigam Chronicles. Alien beings from prehistory with a Lovecraftian bent suddenly return to the world after a long absence?

                                        Also not sure why it would necessarily unify all the different strands of Mummies? The Deceived have a Movement founded on prehuman influences and accursed mutations in the Rotting Temple, and I don't see Shuankhsen being phased by the manifestation of earlier horrors in the slightest.

                                        For the Arisen, I think the obvious shake-up would be to simply update the setting. The original Mummy was "set" in 2012, the first Sothic Turn after the Heretic had achieved Apotheosis. Fast forward even a handful of years and it's clear that a pretty seismic shift is happening in Arisen society. Rio touched on this change, with one of the Arisen in the city being a hidden Mummy who had successfully achieved her Apotheosis. The entire point of the Sothic Turn was to spread Dreams of Avarice in a situation where Arisen had the time and inclination to pursue alternatives to their current state of existence. We're in a situation where most Arisen who have woken for the Sothic Turn will still be awake barring unfortunate happenstance, but at the low ebb of their power. The Guilds need to react to the enormous shock of Apotheosis going from rumour to reality, and some Arisen may be so bold that they pursue it without secrecy. The invisible, undetectable presence of those Arisen who have achieved Apotheosis will be felt in places as they influence comrades who no longer remember their old existence.

                                                            Atamajakki has a good point about the number of player options that are theoretically possible in Mummy without even including the Arisen.

                                                            As a wild idea Mummy 2E might want to adopt the Tier's of play system Hunters used for a while now.

                                                            Tier 1:
                                                            Cultists (Basically normal Blue Book humans with access to some unique resources)
                                                            Witnesses (Since I don't see them getting that much more powerful in 2E just more fleshed out)

                                                            Tier 2:
                                                            Sadikh (These guys, for me are the obvious mini-splat akin to Ghouls, they just need a bit of reworking)
                                                            Ghost Cultists (One again, basically made with Blue Book rules but with some unique merits or numina)

                                                            Tier 3:
                                                            Arisen
                                                            Deceived

                                                            I'm dicey about including Sekhem Sorcerers as a player option. Mainly because most of the 1E fluff about them was focused on them being considered upstart pretenders to the Shan'Iatu's power who kinda mangled Sekhem everytime they managed to pull off any magic. I think they might be better off in an antagonist role aking to Alchemists in Promethean 2E.

                                                            Of course, while this approach would put renewed focus on Cults, who we pretty much all agree need a lot more love in the new addition, it still does not exactly solve the problem of getting a group of Arisen to work togeather aside from Guild affiliations or sharing a Cult.
