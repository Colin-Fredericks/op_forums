                        Session 21

                        A creature rose from the frothing water. It reminded the Deceived of a snake, albeit a headless one. Its long, fleshy body tapered off into a rounded stump. It swayed back and forth, and they noticed that its skin was coated in a clear film impervious to the water streaming from its body. Then the hissing sound sharpened, and its upper body peeled open. Light spilled forth. Squinting, the Deathless realised that the creature now more closely resembled a cobra's hood. The splayed flesh was studded with tiny orbs, the source of the light. They were human eyes, blind and milky white.

                        Imhotep was overtaken by a sudden compulsion. His gun rose into his hand without him noticing. He began to steadily, methodically, empty the clip into Rakhen-Roh's back. Akenakhi rushed to the water's edge and plunged his hands in. He unleashed Obedient Clay, turning the water inside solid and freezing the creature in place. With a force of will, Imhotep threw off the creature's mind control, and they retreated to a safe distance.

                        The Deceived debated whether they should retreat and regroup, but there were so many Seba falling around them that they felt they couldn't risk abandoning them. They rushed to the gondolas running along the outer edge of the Dreamland, where they had seen a cluster of Seba fall. They claimed them without opposition, noting that overhead there was a growing corona of Seba collecting in the skies above. They headed back into the theme park. Seba were descending all around them, but they didn't have the time to go after every one. Instead, they struck out for one of the largest concentrations they could see. They were almost there when fire began to fall.

                        All three of them felt the Epic Unleashing seconds before the first meteor struck. Akenakhi recognised Sekhet Bastet's work. The meteors were not targetting them, but instead struck at the buildings they had noticed as bearing the most conspicuously Egyptian and Greek architecture. Fate knew which targets it wanted destroyed, and wielded the weapon given to it by She Who Is Power with deadly precision. They could not escape all harm, however. As fires roared about them, Akenakhi, Imhotep, and Rakhen-Roh each had to fight against the flames.

                        When the explosions ceased and the sound of the fire catching hold all around them was reduced to a dull roar, the group resumed their cautious advance. They stumbled across a ritual circle of charred and blackened bodies, clearly a site subjected to a ferocious bombardment, and claimed the Seba at the centre. With that done, they turned their attention to the skies. The Seba that had been descending across the Dreamlands were now dispersing across Jakarta instead. Whatever damage in buildings and human lives had been done, it had clearly been enough to break the net trapping so many of Fate's wonders in the area.

                        The last major cluster of Seba had fallen in a nearby administration building. The humans they encountered in the theme park from that point forward were all clearly suffering the effects of Terror Sybaris, seeking nothing more than to escape the area as quickly as they could. There was no sign of anyone attempting to deal with the flames burning higher and higher around them. When they reached the administration building, Akenakhi spotted a badly burned man lying slumped against the building's wall. He asked his fellows to stop and pulled the man to a safe distance, briefly dressing his wounds. When he asked what had happened, the man raved about "the blind eyes" until, impatient, Imhotep ordered Akenakhi to follow them into the building.

                        Rakhen-Roh summoned Preacher, his flaming ghost servant, and ordered it to remove any flames that barred their way. Privately, he struggled to overcome his instinctive fear of the flame, the burns on his hands itching from the heat, but he was able to make the ascent along with the others. When they walked out onto the roof, they saw the creature they had seen in the pool earlier that night. It stood in the midst of a crowd of humans, most of them dressed as tourists or theme park staff. When the humans turned towards them, the Deathless could see that their eyes were milky white and blind.

                        Once again, the creature, the Blind Eyes, bent Imhotep to its will. It turned him against his allies as the humans rushed Rakhen-Roh, trying to bury him beneath them. Akenakhi claimed the closest Seba in a blind hope that it would give him a way to fight this creature. He found himself claiming Cyclopean Monoliths Arise (see below). Only moments later, a Chthonic Being appeared on the roof and slammed into the Blind Eyes. The two titanic monsters toppled from the roof, coiled around one another. Imhotep emerged from the spell he was under, and Rakhen-Roh blasted the humans apart with waves of sand from his hands and mouth. He ended up panting, surrounded by the broken remains of their bodies. The Deceived were furious and quickly snatched up the remaining Seba. They were done with the Dreamlands.

                        As they picked their way through the remains of the administration building, Imhotep experienced a silent exchange with his Temakh Siranuthis. He felt a pressure bearing down on him, telling him that he should have unleashed Blessed Is The God-King in the fight. With his Temakh's full power, that abomination would have been no match for him.

                        There was no sign of any Radiance cultists, or of the Blind Eyes, as the group left the Dreamlands. They sent Akenakhi to the artist's collective, where he had been instructed to build his new tomb. It was intended as a simple structure with no purpose other than to house a new entrance to the Vault of the Stars. The Deceived, meanwhile, continued on to their retreat.

                        They had retrieved two Seba which were of little or no use to them. They gifted these powers to two of their most faithful servants, Ardit, the dancer whose job it was to monitor Yarikh, and Abdul Biran, the occultist who had led them to Dariyat after his capture. Ardit received Strength of the Divine Blood, while Abdul was given the Wings of A'aru. The latter was a mixed blessing. They brought Abdul before a gathering of their cultists, human and mutant alike. Rakhen-Roh had him stripped naked, save for a white cloak, while Imhotep extolled the man's virtues before the crowd. Rakhen-Roh slit the cultist's throat at the climax of the speech, and his lifeless body toppled to the ground.

                        Seconds later, Abdul's ghost manifested above his corpse. A pair of white wings spread out and, with a single beat, sent him soaring above the heads of the congregation. The cultists cheered, for the gifts of their masters were great indeed.

                            Whoo boy, ok, so this session was a big, big deal Seba-wise. I wanted an excuse to use some of the ideas I've been toying with before the campaign ends in about 6 weeks' time, so I flooded the game with Seba to claim. Akenakhi, Imhotep, and Rakhen-Roh each had to sacrifice a dot of Sekhem over the course of this session in order to refresh their Willpower in order to actually be able to claim so many Seba. They scored the following:

                            Imhotep

                            Effervescent Pillars of the Soul (1 dot Seba)

                            Power: There are many variations of this Seba, but each contain a single Pillar dot. The Seba must be sacrificed in order to claim it. Mortals who gain a Pillar dot are treated as if they possessed the Stolen Pillar Numen. In other words, they may not gain Pillar points, but may purchase Affinities. These dots do not fade, however.
                            For Mummies, these are new Pillar dots in every respect.

                            Curse: Unlocking the secrets of universe do not come lightly. At the moment they sacrifice the Seba, the bearer gains a new Derangement, chosen by Fate. This Derangement is permanent.


                            The Chains of Broken Will (1 dot Seba)

                            Power: The bearer may summon forth spectral chains to bind their target’s will with hissed words of a diseased, necrotic tongue. This requires an instant action. The attacker rolls Manipulation + Expression + Ren, contested by the target’s Resolve + Composure + Sehkem. If the bearer wins, the target may not spend Willpower for a number of days equal to the difference in successes. They may not lose or recover Willpower by any means in this time.
                            If the bearer knows the target’s true name, the chains become real. The target suffers a -3 penalty to all actions for the rest of the scene.

                            Curse: Once per story, at a time decreed by Fate, an attempt to use Willpower goes horribly awry. The Storyteller may choose a roll on which Willpower was spent to become a dramatic failure.


                            As One with the Sands of Time (2 dot Seba)

                            Power: The bearer of this Seba becomes a part of the primordial desert, the future desolation to which the Earth is condemned. Wherever they go they leave behind them traces of dark, red sand. It can be found in the shape of their fingerprints where their hands lay for the briefest moment, or scattered across the pavement where they have walked. Anyone who touches them can feel the grit, even when the bearer appears to be clean, and will find their hand comes away with sand attached to it.
                            The bearer may spend a point of Willpower and an instant action to be consumed. Their body disintegrates. It will reconstitute at the next dawn at a location chosen by Fate.

                            Curse: To wield the doom of the world is to be enslaved to entropy. The bearer cannot heal damage naturally so long as they bear this Seba. Supernatural powers and medical treatment may still restore them.


                            Soul of the Dead Star (2 dot Seba)


                            Power: The bearer calls upon the energies of a dead celestial body, long since extinguished and crushed beneath its own weight. For a single moment the ghost of the star burns in Neter-Khetet right where the bearer is standing, pulsing out waves of heat and crushing pressure to afflict those creatures standing in Twilight. The bearer can activate this Seba as an instant action. For a single round, treat all beings in Twilight within 10 yards as if they were standing in a flame with Bonefire size and Chemical Flame intensity. All such beings must make a Dexterity + Athletics (or Finesse) roll or be immediately thrown 10 yards directly away from the bearer.
                            This Seba can be sacrificed to draw the fire and pressure into the real world, afflicting everything corporeal thing within range except the bearer.


                            Curse: This power may not be drawn upon with any frequency. The bearer must wait until an appropriate celestial convergence has occurred before its power can be activated again. Roll 10 dice, and for every success gained the bearer must wait a week until the Seba can be used again. This does not preclude the bearer from sacrificing the Seba.
                            If the Deceived choose to ignore this Curse, they may activate the Seba once per session.


                            Rakhen-Roh

                            Effervescent Pillars of the Soul (1 dot Seba)

                            Power: There are many variations of this Seba, but each contain a single Pillar dot. The Seba must be sacrificed in order to claim it. Mortals who gain a Pillar dot are treated as if they possessed the Stolen Pillar Numen. In other words, they may not gain Pillar points, but may purchase Affinities. These dots do not fade, however.
                            For Mummies, these are new Pillar dots in every respect.

                            Curse: Unlocking the secrets of universe do not come lightly. At the moment they sacrifice the Seba, the bearer gains a new Derangement, chosen by Fate. This Derangement is permanent.


                            The Aetheric Witness (1 dot cosmic Seba)
                            Power: The bearer of this Seba gains the ability to create Essence. They can spend a point of Willpower to immediately gain Essence equal to their Resolve. If they are unable to use Essence themselves, they can donate it to a being who can by touching them.


                            Curse: The bearer becomes a subject of great interest for all kinds of Twilight-dwelling entities. They can perceive him even from Neter-Khetet, and know instinctively what he can do.


                            Time's Great Burden (2 dot cosmic Seba)


                            Power: The bearer of this Seba carries the weight of time in their hands. They may target any inanimate object they can perceive as an instant action, and roll Intelligence + Composure. Every success reduces that object's equipment modifier by 1 point as it ages and degrades decades in the space of seconds, and also reduces its Durability (and maximum structure) by the same amount.


                            Curse: Objects around the bearer age at the same rate. Once per session, a mundane item they are attempting to use begins to age in the same manner, forcing the bearer to make a reflexive Intelligence + Composure roll in the same manner as the Seba's power.


                            The Message From Beyond Time (4 dot cosmic Seba)


                            Power: This Seba grants visions of what is yet to come. At any point during a turn, the bearer may reflexively activate this Seba. The turn immediately begins again, with the bearer at the top of the initiative order regardless of what their initiative may normally be (assuming the bearer is in combat). This Seba may be activated reflexively even if the bearer just died during the course of the turn. The turn-that-was is actually a vision of the future experienced by the bearer, and the insight grants them the ability to act with the ability of one who knows what is about to happen. This ability may only be activated once per scene.
                            Once per session, the bearer of this Seba may receive an urge to take a particular action, as dictated by the Storyteller. They receive no words or explanation for this urge, but Fate usually dictates that it be an action that will benefit them in a manner relating to their current situation.


                            Curse: For mortals, this Seba can become unbearable. It presses down on them with the knowledge that they will not last, and that from the perspective of the stars they will be reduced to dust in the blink of an eye. Any mortals who bear this Seba cannot regain Willpower for any reason, and suffer a -2 penalty to any checks to avoid gaining a Derangement.
                            For the Deathless, confident in their immortality and protected by their failing memories, the scope of eternity is not quite as much of a burden. Their minds do grow increasingly alien from exposure to this knowledge, however. They suffer degeneration checks for Morality sins one higher than their Memory.


                            Akenakhi

                            Effervescent Pillars of the Soul (1 dot Seba)

                            Power: There are many variations of this Seba, but each contain a single Pillar dot. The Seba must be sacrificed in order to claim it. Mortals who gain a Pillar dot are treated as if they possessed the Stolen Pillar Numen. In other words, they may not gain Pillar points, but may purchase Affinities. These dots do not fade, however.
                            For Mummies, these are new Pillar dots in every respect.

                            Curse: Unlocking the secrets of universe do not come lightly. At the moment they sacrifice the Seba, the bearer gains a new Derangement, chosen by Fate. This Derangement is permanent.


                            Through Another's Eyes (3 dot cosmic Seba)


                            Power: The bearer may spend a point of Willpower to take an instant action, targetting a mortal human they can directly perceive. The bearer rolls Intelligence + Occult, contested by the target's Resolve + Composure. If they succeed, they immediately possess the target, body, mind, and soul. This lasts for the rest of the scene. The bearer's body appears to fall into an inert coma until the possession ends. The bearer must use the target's attributes and skills for any actions they take, and cannot call upon any supernatural powers they may have while possessing the target. They may, however, activate any Seba the target may possess, and can even use this Seba again to leap to another body, and another, and another, as long as they have Willpower to spend.
                            While possessing the target, the bearer has no ability to sense their true body or tell if it is in danger, and will only notice harm done to it when they return to it.
                            Ending the possession can be done at any time by the reflexive expenditure of another Willpower, and ends automatically if the target is ever rendered unconscious. When the possession ends, the target awakens with no memory of what they did while under another's influence.


                            Curse: The bearer finds that their mind wanders beyond the confines of their body, travelling without their consent to places they may not wish it to go. Whenever the bearer sleeps or rests, Fate may choose to have them possess a target somewhere of its choosing. If one of the Deathless possess this merit, it can even send their mind through time as well as space, leading to the possession of someone important in their past or future descents. Obviously, the bearer cannot regain Willpower from their rest if this occurs, as they spend their sleep occupied with the perils of another body.


                            Cyclopean Monoliths Arise (4 dot cosmic Seba)


                            Power: The bearer calls on the dust of ancient races, willing them to return their prehuman architecture to the world for the briefest time. Impossible towers and structures burst forth from the ground, restored momentarily to the height of their alien majesty. The bearer may spend a point of Willpower to make an extended Stamina + Academics roll, with every roll representing a turn of dedicated effort. A tower rises directly beneath the bearer's feet and soars towards the heavens. It grows 10 yards in height for every success gained on the bearer's roll, and extends in a 10 yard radius around where they are standing. Any others who are standing inside this radius are lifted as well, and must succeed on a Dexterity + Athletics roll at a -3 penalty every round the tower spends growing or else fall prone. Any other structures in the way of the tower are somehow unharmed, it melds into them without harming them as though it does not occupy the same physical space.
                            When it is complete, the tower is a seamless structure that appears to be made of a single block of dark basalt. It has no windows or doors to speak of in its walls. Its top is perfectly smooth and flat, save for a raised seal directly below the bearer's feet. This seal has never been broken, and it is not known what may be found inside. Those who bear this Seba have the unnerving impression that the monolith is a prison, and rarely have any interest in freeing whatever lies within.
                            This structure invokes Terror Sybaris like the Deceived in any who can perceive it, a creeping horror overwhelming the minds of mortals who can see it. When it returns to the earth at the end of the scene, it takes with it the memory of it from the minds of any mortals, no matter how well they performed on their Sybaris check.


                            Curse: Chthonic Beings are drawn to the monolith for their own inscrutable reasons. They travel from across the world, through the void, even traversing time itself in order to be close to it. Once per story, the Storyteller may choose to have the bearer encounter such a being. It will not be hostile to the bearer, but the same cannot be said for those in their presence. And when the bearer summons the monolith, they converge. The Storyteller may choose to have one descend upon the tower for every turn the bearer spends summoning it. Only the Deceived may hide themselves from these creatures, ignoring the Curse as per normal.

                                I forgot the Seba given to the cultists. Abdul received (to his regret, I guess), the following:

                                Wings of A'aru (1 dot cosmic Seba)

                                Power: This Seba has no effect until its bearer is slain. When that occurs, it is immediately sacrificed and returned to the cosmos, unless bound by a Deceived as a Keshina.
                                When the bearer dies, their soul flies forth on the winds of heaven. If a mortal bearing this Seba dies, they immediately become a ghost with the Untethered Numen and gifted with a pair of enormous, feathered wings, allowing them to fly.
                                If a Deceived bearing this Seba is killed or completes their descent, their soul is loosed into Neter-Khetet with these same wings, giving them a great advantage in evading their Temakh.
                                Arisen and Shuankhsen bearing this Seba do not experience a normal Death Cycle when they die. Instead, their winged soul wanders Neter-Khetet in the same manner as the Deceived but without the questing fingers of the Temakh. They may observe the living world from there, scouting and exploring in a way not normally available to them, until they choose to end their Death Cycle by passing through an akhet.
                                In all cases, the bearer of this Seba triples their base speed when flying.

                                Curse: To be touched by Heaven is a heady experience, one few minds are equipped to deal with. The bearer's Vice changes to Pride at the moment of their death. If it was already Pride, they gain the Megalomania Derangement.


                                Ardit, the warrior dancer in charge of monitoring Yarikh, was given this:

                                Strength of the Divine Blood (2 dot Seba)

                                Power: The men of Ubar claimed their King was one-third Divine, and the bearer of this Seba has an inkling of what that must have been like. If a mortal claims this Seba, their body changes over the course of a month to one more befitting a child of the gods. They gain the Giant Merit and Striking Looks 4. They are often described as being possessed of a god-like aura, a golden halo that leaves those around them in awe. In addition, they gain the option to purchase the Witness merit on the spot, going into experience debt if needed.
                                An Arisen or Deceived who claims this Seba do not gain any benefits.

                                Curse: The bearer gains the Megalomania Derangement. In addition, should they ever lose this Seba, they are shattered by the loss. They lose the merit and powers they may have gained. Mortals suffer the Dwarf Flaw, their bodies wracked by pain and twisted into shrunken shadows of their former selves.

                                    Session 22

                                    Imhotep had received a kepher vision amidst the chaos at the theme park, but it had been prompted by a Dramatic Failure rather than a Success. The resulting vision had put him on a trail leading to an abandoned chemical factory, one that a quick conversation with Akenakhi confirmed had been previously used by the city's Shuankhsen as their hideout. The Deceived were determined to snatch up every weapon they could get their hands on, and resolved to scout out the area as soon as they could.

                                    First, however, Imhotep wished to recoup some of his lost power. He requested permission from Rakhen-Roh to feast on some of his spirits, and it was granted. The ghost Preacher was called, and Imhotep unleashed the Black Huriyah to feast upon the hapless being. This restored Imhotep to Sekhem 4 before Preacher was drained and spent. That done, the Deceived collected Akenakhi and moved on the chemical factory.

                                    Their cult obtained a boat, allowing them to observe the site from the river. Rakhen-Roh sent his snake out to explore the building. The snake entered through one of the many holes in the wall, the building reduced to a dilapidated state after the Shuankhsen occupation and Arisen siege. The reptile was able to navigate the ruins easily, but its master noticed many collapsed roofs and blocked tunnels that would make exploring the building difficult.

                                    Oh, and he also noticed the zombies.

                                    It did take a while. The ruins contained quite a few desiccated bodies. Eventually, however, Rakhen-Roh noticed that those bodies with splintered rib cages and missing hearts had a habit of stirring from time to time. As cautious as ever, he asked his allies to wait before entering the building. His mouth opened wide and he vomited forth two more fire spirits, similar in nature to Preacher. He commanded the ghosts to destroy the creatures inside, and then watched through the eyes of his snake as the events unfolded.

                                    From the outside, Imhotep and Akenakhi saw the occasional flickering light coming from the ruins. Rakhen-Roh could see his manifested servants sweeping through the building, wielding Pyrokinesis to start flames on the bodies of every animate corpse. The dead flesh caught fire easily. Without any living prey to stir them into action, however, the creatures seemed to pay little mind to their immolation. They lay where they were, without sound or movement, and allowed the flames to consume them.

                                    Once the building was clear, the Deceived entered. Rakhen-Roh's efforts in scouting the place allowed them to find a clear path through the building quite easily, with Akenakhi's mastery of Obedient Clay clearing those blockages that could not be by-passed. The air inside stank of rot and ash and a sharper, acrid scent.

                                    Imhotep located a chamber with the charred remains of four old corpses. Inside was the Seba, the Dead Lie Screaming, and he claimed it on the spot.

                                    They retreated to a safe distance, and the Deceived and Akenakhi conferred. Seba were not the only weapons available to them, and Imhotep wished to wield the Black Huriyah to restore his Sekhem to true majesty. They needed more ghosts for both purposes, a legion of screaming fire spirits at Rakhen-Roh's beck and call. They asked Akenakhi for advice, and he suggested three locations. The Church District, where his old meret had fought a group of humans who worshipped the ghosts as parts of a deity, the Old Dutch Quarter, where the Unbending Spine had worked with a powerful being towards ends Akenakhi was still not entirely clear about, and the area around Bast's tomb, where a graveyard played host to the Drowned Choir of flood victims.

                                    The Deceived chose the Church District, since it seemed the least likely to lead to direct confrontation with Arisen. It wasn't long before Rakhen-Roh and Akenakhi, both able to see into Twilight, were able to confirm that there were a great many lost souls in the area. Its former history as a red light district was betrayed in that netherworld, and there were spirits of dead gangsters, prostitutes, and clientele scattered amount the streets. Rakhen-Roh ordered their car to stop when he found a pleasing cluster of ghosts and consumed them on the spot, his augmented Sybaris sending every mortal in the area cowering in fear. Imhotep then demanded that he be given the name of one of the remaining ghosts, and Rakhen-Roh was able to pry that information out with some cunning use of Voice of Temptation. Almost immediately, the spirit was assaulted by the Black Huriyah. It tried to compel Imhotep to abandon the attempt and leave, but the Deceived's will was too strong.

                                    By the time they left, Rakhen-Roh nurtured three more ghosts in his stomach, and Imhotep had risen to Sekhem 6. They were a little bit frustrated at Akenakhi with how unwilling he had been to participate in events. Rakhen-Roh argued that the ghosts weren't "real" people, just records of those who once had lived, no more alive than a book. Akenakhi was not quite convinced by this argument, but for the sake of peace with his new allies he kept his mouth shut.

                                    Akenakhi returned to his tomb, but the Deceived wished to confer with Yarikh to discover what he knew of the Radiance. They summoned the Su-Menent to the Lone Star Studio to speak with him. Imhotep had him acting properly cowed by this point, but he wasn't able to provide any information of use. He had never heard of a creature like the Blind Eyes before, and he could only speculate as to what the Radiance's powers might be. Before they dismissed him, however, Rakhen-Roh chose to let slip that they had encountered another Arisen on the path to Apotheosis. Yarikh stiffened, but asked no questions. He waited until he was dismissed, and left.

                                    They then returned to the retreat. Once there, Imhotep demanded that Abdul's skull be brought to him. Umar arranged for it to be cleaned and polished, and presented it to him in person. In order to wield his new Seba, he needed to know Abdul's true name. This was a piece of lore that Akenakhi had been quite eager to learn. With Abdul's ghost present to provide all the necessary information about himself, they were able to determine his true name quite quickly and easily.

                                    And then the Deceived asked Akenakhi to commit himself to their cause. He was to use what he had just learned, and everything he knew of the nome, to discover the true names of every Arisen in Jakarta. Beginning with his meret.

                                    It was not a request Akenakhi appeared to have any problem carrying out. The true names of Sekhet Bastet, Sesu Ra, and Aquila came to him easily, and he offered them to the Deceived willingly.

                                            Session 23

                                            A note: This session occurred concurrently as this Old Gods session.

                                            The Deceived were completing their preparations when they felt the heavens shatter. Time buckled and broke under the strain, and the world splintered. Imhotep immediately marshalled his forces for battle. The Deceived cultists seemed to have been spared the effects of time's sudden departure, shielded by their master, and he gathered mutants and soldiers alike and had them armed with the weapons of the Tomb Legion. Thus prepared for battle, they embarked upon the transport helicopter the Deceived owned for just this purpose and flew east, towards the light that had appeared in the evening sky.

                                            Akenakhi was strapped into his seat beside Ardit, who had grown rapidly since being gifted with a Seba from her masters. Lurking in Twilight nearby was Abdul, white wings spreading from his back and darkness in his eyes. The helicopter stayed low, winding through the city streets to avoid detection. They sensed Sekhem flaring somewhere in the East, a powerful unleashing of some kind, but ignored it. They were certain that the Radiance was behind this strange event, and they had no intention of letting their enemy get away again.

                                            The world became increasingly fractured as they approached the shoreline, large gaping cracks appearing that led to an empty void. These cracks grew larger and larger as they neared the light until finally there was nowhere else to go. The helicopter entered the void.

                                            They were inside a large cage made of light, the helicopter hovering in the air about thirty feet above the tableau below. The Deceived could make out Arisen, and Akenakhi supplied their names. Aquila Ashex, Sesu Ra, and Sekhet Bastet, accompanied by their Sadikhs.

                                            The helicopter circled and came down to land. As it neared the ground Akenakhi spotted the two mortals frozen still between the two groups. One was the Prophet of Java, but Imhotep, impossibly, recognised the other man as well. It had been six decades since he had last the seen him, and the man had been old then. His name had been Adam, and he was a servant of the Radiance.

                                            Akenakhi took off running the moment the helicopter hit the ground, determined to save the Prophet. He unleashed the Revelations of Smoke and Flame at Tier 3 as he did so, transforming into a pillar of flame that arced across and slammed into Adam's chest. He made no move, no response as his flesh bubbled and steamed.

                                            The Deceived watched as one of the Arisen, Aquila, unleashed an Epic unleashing of her own and rushed to attack Akenakhi. One by one, the other Arisen and their Sadikhs began to unleash Utterances that clearly showed they were preparing for a fight. Imhotep did nothing, however, and Rakhen-Roh followed his lead. Instead, Imhotep roared flame into the air and, once he had their attention, demanded that the fighting stop. He wanted to find out how Adam was here, and what, exactly, was happening before he allowed events to continue.

                                            It looked for a moment like he might have his wish. Akenakhi backed away from Adam. Aquila paused rather than continue her assault. And then Sekhet Bastet strode up to the Radiance's cultists and cut off his head. Moments later the Prophet stirred, bowed to Bast, and vanished.

                                            Imhotep was outraged. His authority had been denied defied. As Bast advanced on him, demanding to know who he thought he was, he decided to show her. He unleashed Blessed Is The God-King, allowing Siranuthis to take control. Of the rest of the scene, he would remember nothing.

                                            Akenakhi watched in awe as a Shan'iatu thundered its rage and power. The Arisen, led by Sesu Ra, charged in to attack. Rakhen-Roh spewed forth flaming ghosts to aid them in the fight, and then he vanished to be replaced by Tutkepertanu. He hadn't known, hadn't suspected the true nature of his allies.

                                            And then his attention was diverted as he was set upon by Aquila and Bast, each angry at him for different reasons. He called upon the relic he carried, the Serpent Named, to draw out Bast's soul and deprive her of her Utterances.

                                            In the middle of the field, Sesu Ra and Luca were cutting a bloody swathe through the Deceived cultists. Ardit retreated into the helicopter, but was left bleeding out in a corner of the vehicle after a barrage of bullets. In Twilight, Sesu Ra's ghostly protector was busy eviscerating Rakhen-Roh's flaming ghosts, but not before they did a damned good job of turning the Tef-Aabhi Guildmaster into a flaming torch. And Aquila was lured away from Akenakhi as the Shan'iatu began to summon increasingly powerful Utterances to the fight. Akenakhi watched as her many enchantments were trivially stripped away from her. Tutkepertanu wielded her true name in conjunction with Rebuke the Vizier to leave her with nothing, and Siranuthis bound her with chains using the Chains of Broken Will Seba.

                                            Quil was the first to retreat. His enchantments were stolen from him by Tutkepertanu, and he fled. He stumbled past Bast's motionless body just as she was able to escape the enchantment separating her five-fold soul from her khat, and she grabbed Kudama Jav and vanished. Aquila summoned a Chthonic Being to cover their escape, but it did little damage before the flames at the hands of the Shan'iatu sent it fleeing. As meteors began to fall within the cage, Sesu Ra, Luca, and Aquila made for the rents leading out of the void and back to reality.

                                            Everything was still and quiet as Akenakhi left Twilight, the flames around him dying down. The Shan'iatu didn't look at him, didn't acknowledge him. Instead they walked the perimeter of the cage, alone, speaking words he didn't understand.

                                            Akenakhi took stock. Ardit was the only surviving cultist, save for the ghosts swarming in Twilight.

                                            And then suddenly he was standing on the beach, and all around him Jakarta was resuming its everyday existence as if nothing had happened.

                                            The Shan'iatu were gone. Instead, Imhotep and Rakhen-Roh were standing beside him, looking drained and exhausted. Akenakhi stared at them for a long, long time. "We're going to need to talk about this," he said.

                                            The Deceived said nothing. They walked back to their helicopter in silence.

                                                Session 24

                                                The Deceived had spent their time since the fight with the Arisen gathering forces and fortifying their cult's holdings and frantically replenishing their pillars. They were sure that their presence must have been revealed to the nome, and they were confused at first at how long it was taking the Arisen to respond. Then, slowly, they began to get word of the scope of the destruction levelled against the Arisen cults. While their own had fallen, briefly, under attack by the Radiance's forces, they had come through relatively unscathed. According to the reports sent by Imhotep's spy in Rekhenaten's cult, Lies, the strongest cult in the city had been devastated and most of the others had been wiped out entirely.

                                                With their foes off-balance, Imhotep saw the perfect opportunity to strike. He ordered Lies to tell Rekhenaten that Yama-Het was trying to contact him and organise a truce. Lies drew upon the Choir of the Dark Stars to convince Rekhenaten that his enemy was still alive, and they named one of the Deceived's suborned night clubs as a "neutral" site for the meeting.

                                                Meanwhile, Akenakhi managed to divine Rekhenaten's true name, supplying it to his allies as they prepared the club for battle. Rakhen-Roh shapeshifted into Yama-Het using photographs his cult had taken during their surveillance of her cult as a guide. They then waited inside the club, Imhotep behind a one-way mirror and monitoring the outside of the club via security cameras.

                                                When Rekhenaten arrived, he was not alone. He brought armed cultists who encircled the club, and he had Tobi by his side. Imhotep gritted his teeth at the sight of the resurrected Sadikh. Rather than enter the building himself, Tobi walked inside. He spoke to Rakhen-Roh in a language neither Deceived understood, but Rakhen-Roh was able to bluster well enough in Iremite to divert attention away from his choice of language. Finally, Rekhenaten entered the building, two cultists flanking him.

                                                Imhotep didn't waste any more time. He called down the Black Huriyah, wielding Rekhenaten's true name. The dark angels converged on the Arisen and drained the final dregs of power from him, raising Imhotep's own power in the process. Rekhenaten crumpled to the ground, Tobi dropping as well. The two cultists were overwhelmed with Sybaris, one turning to flee as the other begged for mercy. Rakhen-Roh snapped the fleeing man out of the grip of Sybaris and spoke to him, mixing his words with the Voice of Temptation, convincing the cultist to abandon his master. The man trembled in the grip of the Deceived and succumbed to their influence, killing his fellow cultist as a demonstration of loyalty.

                                                The Prophet of Java appeared in the room with them. He had come to tell them of the birth of a powerful weapon in Jakarta, the power held by an innocent soul. The Radiance was nearly upon them and this new power would be vital in the fight to come. It lay close to the grasp of one of the Devourer's beasts, however, and needed to be retrieved before night fell.

                                                Imhotep looked at the body on the floor and weighed his options. It was still the middle of the day. He had time. He told the Prophet that he would return, and ordered his cult to burn Rekhenaten's body (and Tobi's, and the dead cultist for good measure). He seized Rakhen-Roh and the new convert and made speed to Rekhenaten's estate. Lies was waiting for them outside, and led them through the mind-bending maze of strangely linked rooms that made Rekhenaten's tomb almost impervious to intruders. The Tef-Aabhi's cult had gathered there, a strange mix of the poorest and most destitute and the richest and most influential of Jakarta. When Imhotep declared who he was and what he had done, they attacked.

                                                Blades were drawn, others just tried to rush the mummy, but Imhotep only laughed. He drew himself up to his full height and his stare alone was enough to cause the cultists to pause in their tracks. When he opened his mouth to reveal a gullet brimming with flame, they began to back away.

                                                Those who were willing to forsake Rekhenaten would join the Deceived's cult. All others would die. After a few demonstrations on the most stubborn cultists, the rest were virtually clamouring for the opportunity to swear their loyalty. While the Deceived could not be sure of the true strength of devotion expressed by their newest converts, they hoped the mystic chains that bound their cultists to them would be enough to ensure minimal acts of betrayal. Imhotep elevated Lies to the highest ranks of their cult for his long devotion and the success of his efforts. At last, Rekhenaten had been defeated.

                                                The Deceived then returned to meet with the prophet. Time was running short in the extreme now. He took them to the west side of the city, to a small orphanage. The building was surrounded by a chain-link fence topped with razor wire, but other than that there were no signs to show why this building was different from any of its neighbours. The Prophet could tell them only that a Shuankhsen, the one known as Gator, lurked inside. If they were clever, they would not need to fight him. When asked how they would know what they were looking for, he told them that there would be a girl in danger. They would know when they saw her.

                                                Akenakhi was ordered to stay in the car. Rakhen-Roh and Imhotep adopted the guise of children, and scaled the fence. They negotiated the razor wire and dropped into the courtyard without being spotted. Behind them, the sun was setting.

                                                Inside, the orphanage was small and cramped. Adults would need to walk single file. Each room they passed was occupied, a single bunk bed and cupboard shared by two, sometimes three children. The children themselves were gaunt creatures with hollow eyes, most of them sporting bruises. Imhotep was able to get one of them talking after some effort, and found out that there was a "black" room upstairs where a new girl had been taken.

                                                As they headed for the stairs, they overheard two women yelling at one another from somewhere out near the back of the building. One was taking the other to task for "damaging the goods". The Deceived never saw these women, only heard them, and were able to creep up the stairs without being noticed.

                                                Imhotep then listened at several doors on the top floor. He heard sobbing coming from some, silence from many, and a beautiful, earnest lullaby drifting from the one nearest to the stairs. Rakhen-Roh, meanwhile, was trying to pick the lock of the door to the room Imhotep thought was the Black Room. He had hardly started though when they heard footsteps on the stairs.

                                                Imhotep decided to try for one of the silent rooms. He stepped over to it, swung open the handle, and froze. Two words sprang to mind.

                                                "Oh shit."

                                                There was a large desk in the room, but it was dwarfed by the man behind it. He was at least seven feet tall, with reddish-bronze discolourations running up his neck. He looked up at Imhotep and, for a moment, the Deceived could see the shifting of the Shuankhsen's khat beneath his skin. Gator.

                                                "You know the punishment for disturbing me," Gator said softly, rising to his feet.

                                                In that statement, Imhotep knew two things. The first, that the Shuankhsen hadn't yet pierced his disguise. The second, that the Shuankhsen evidently paid little enough attention to the children that he hadn't yet realised that Imhotep was not one of the orphans. His racing mind fell upon the first plausible excuse, and he reinforced his words with the Choir of Dark Stars.

                                                "There was a man. He broke in and took the girl."

                                                Gator's reaction was immediate. He lunged to his feet and charged from the room, thundering down the stairs. Whoever had been ascending must have followed him, because for a moment everything was quiet.

                                                Imhotep wasted no more time. He rushed to the door and melted the lock with Obedient Clay. The door swung open. The room inside was dark and smelled of urine and dried blood, the floor stained in places. In the middle was a young woman tied to a chair. She said something as the Deceived walked in, her voice high and scared, but it wasn't in any language they recognised. Rakhen-Roh drew a knife and cut her free while Imhotep grabbed her arm and pulled her to her feet.

                                                Outside, Akenakhi and the Prophet saw Gator rush out the front of the building. Akenakhi panicked and pulled away. A frantic glance out the sideview mirror saw Gator put his head down and charge. There was a screen of metal and the entire vehicle shuddered, once, twice, and Akenakhi felt wind rushing into the vehicle. For a terrifying moment it felt as if Gator were going to tear the car apart, but then he was free. When he looked back to grin at the prophet, he saw that the Prophet had vanished.

                                                Akenakhi ditched the car and made his way back to the nightclub. The Prophet was waiting there when he arrived. The Deceived reverted to their usual forms and showed up a few minutes later, and to his astonishment Akenakhi recognised the battered young woman with them. It was Aulia, the cursed daughter of a scientist working for Last Dynasty International. His former meret had kept her, half-guest and half-prisoner, as they worked to try and find a way to lift the curse that had left her speaking in tongues. He was so excited that he almost didn't hear the Prophet as he said that she needed to die.

                                                The power the woman held was that of an immensely powerful Seba, one that would draw on the bearer's life force. If Aulia kept it, wielding the power would kill her. If the Deceived killed her and claimed the power, it would cost them their Sekhem to wield. For Imhotep, the decision was easy. As Akenakhi argued with the Prophet, trying to find a loophole that could keep Aulia alive, Imhotep drew a knife and stepped up behind her. He had cut her throat before Akenakhi could react.

                                                The Seba that burst forth from her body was more powerful than anything either Deceived had ever seen. It blazed with glory. Imhotep reached out and took it into his soul, claiming Witness the Divine, a five dot Cosmic Seba. He sealed it to his name as a Keshina, and walked from the chamber, Rakhen-Roh by his side.

                                                Behind them, Akenakhi knelt and wept for the price that had been paid.

                                                    And the new Seba:

                                                    Witness the Divine (5 dot Cosmic Seba)

                                                    Power: Bound within this star is the name of a creature that has long since passed from this world, a being lesser civilisations may once have called a god. The bearer learns the creature’s true name, and may use it to summon this being. It coalesces from the firmament to walk the Earth once more, roaring into being and causing the edges of reality to burn in its presence.
                                                    Invoking the name is an instant action that can be done at any time, so long as the bearer is willing to pay the price. Doing so sacrifices this seba and calls Ashemenhotep to the world for a single scene. Deceived who bind this Seba as Keshina do not need to sacrifice it, but may only call upon its power once per Descent.

                                                    Curse: The power to fuel such a creature cannot come from nothing. Only life can give it form, and even then only for a short time. If a mortal uses this Seba, they lose their life in return. It begins to drain out of them, causing them to sicken and die. For a Mummy, they lose a dot of Sekhem the moment they invoke the creature.
                                                    The Deceived may shield themselves from this curse, but the price must still be paid. If a Deceived chooses to avoid paying the price themselves, they must destroy a relic or kill a mortal at the moment of the invocation, allowing another to pay the price for them.

                                                    Ashemenhotep – The Winged Archer in Fire

                                                    It is not a being. It is a presence, a distortion in the world. Mortals see a blazing shape, the impression of wings, a figure that could almost be humanoid if it did not exist in too many dimensions. It is fire without smoke, a terrible judgement upon the wicked. None can see it and refute its divinity.

                                                    Those who can see beyond the veil witness the true nature of this being. Where most see a being made of fire, beings of magic see through that outer shell. The fire is not its being, but merely a product of its presence. The power of Ashemenhotep is more than this world can bear. The universe consumes itself in its presence, leaving only fire to show its passing.

                                                    Attributes:
                                                    Power 10, Finesse 10, Resistance 10

                                                    Numen: Materialise, Untethered, Impassion Soul (Protect the Bearer, Serve Fate, End the Shuankhsen), Old Soul 10, Regeneration, Spectral Toughness

                                                    The Bow of Fire – Those who face the wrath of Ashemenhotep are struck by bolts of fire, hurled from afar. It wields a 10L weapon that does aggravated damage to beings vulnerable to fire, with a range of 100/200/300.

                                                    Power Beyond Measure – Ashemenhotep causes Terror Sybaris as if it were a Sekhem 10 Mummy. To Arisen and other supernaturally sensitive beings, it registers like the unleashing of an Epic Utterance for the duration of its existence.
