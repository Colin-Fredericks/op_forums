                                            I've been looking at the Affinities from 1st Edition and comparing them to the preview Affinities and... I think I'm noticing some things. First, I'm noticing that there doesn't seem to be any... entirely new Affinities. Same names, same general ideas involved, less evocative text. Second, I think I'm noticing that the Affinities that granted Merits have had that feature stripped out of them. For example, Enlightened Senses in 1st Edition gave you the Common Sense and Meditative Mind Merits. The Preview removes that and instead says you're immune to surprise attacks.

                                            Now... I'm just guessing here regarding the motivation for this, but I think it's under the idea of "If someone wanted those Merits, they can just pay experience for them." But something I've noticed in my games is that those Merits tend to be... undervalued, relative for experience costs, in relation to supernatural powers for players. "Why spend my precious experience points on something that seems so mundane as Common Sense, when I can pick up a power that lets me beast-mode on enemies?" That kinda thinking. And I appreciated how 1st Edition Affinities wrapped up Merits into powers for the Arisen, or powers they grant to Cultists because it's an nice way to build up power that doesn't add a lot of text. And make more use of those Merits.

                                            But, this got exchanged with a flat immunity to surprise attacks and... I'm not sure how comfortable I am with that. It seems to be doubling up a bit on what the power provides, since it adds +Ren dice to perception checks, presumably you'd be adding +Ren dice to checks to notice surprise attacks. But instead, it just completely nullifies a mechanic from the game. I'm not sure how I feel about that, and I'm wondering what I'm forgetting from powers other supernatural creatures have in regards to having immunity to surprise attacks...

                                            Edit: To be clear, I think there might be some new Affinities for the Guild Affinities, even though there are only three listed for each Guild... I would need to go through the Guildhalls of the Deathless book I think.... But I'm pretty sure at least Nest of Dolls is completely new.

                                                    Yeahhh, I'm... kinda in the same boat on a lot of this, in regards to the powers. Things feel missing, and many of the things that are still present feel... tamer than they maybe ought to be, stripped of tone in spots, weirdly more laser-focused in a couple cases than one might expect for the Arisen, and... we have precisely four Affinities and one Condition carrying over a mechanic that seemed on the chopping block in the edition shift. Granted, God King's Scepter at least feels like a good use of it if it's going to be anywhere, but... it doesn't feel worth keeping the mechanic around like this if it's only in such a limited capacity and on a small handful of powers, tops. Something less... clunky could easily slot in there in place of it. 9-Again, a die bonus... something rooouuughly statistically similar that doesn't bring back a weird (if at the time more plentiful and flavorful) mechanical quirk from Mummy 1e just because it was there.
                                                    Not saying it has to necessarily go away though. It's been a long while since I read through Mummy 1e and I'm away from the PC with all my PDFs, but were there any effects that cursed others with increased Target Numbers under specific circumstances? That would have been a neat way to carry forward the effect into 2e while expanding it if it wasn't present before (well, the Dead Dreamer Condition in the Appendix preview applies the "increase Target Number by one" effect when not following the assigned task, so that's one use). Or spread the weird fate-bending around, blessing cultists in their presence, things like that. I guess the point is, if more powers played with it and in more ways than just "three of effectively the same power on the Physical-Mental-Social axes, plus one for all uses on their own cultists", it might feel like a more natural fit, at least as far as literal fate-bending can feel natural. Add it to more Affinities outside those four, give another Utterance or two a horrible, personal curse that fucks over the victim's target numbers in different circumstances or improves others' target numbers when working against them... something along those lines. There is room for it if it's gonna continue to exist.


                                                    On the other hand, the merits list is cool, liking the options across the board. The fact that the Arisen can start their Descents with lower Sekhem depending on context is also fun in a way, though I can honestly see a number of tables dropping that for simplicity and Bigger Numbers.
