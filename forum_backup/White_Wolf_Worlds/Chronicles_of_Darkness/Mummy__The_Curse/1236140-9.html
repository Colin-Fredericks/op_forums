    Originally posted by Vent0 View Post

    To utter once more the timeless chant: Irem was not Ancient Egypt.

    But the egalitarianism is (probably) largely a conceit for less-baised modern gaming sensibilities. So players can play male or female (or other) Mummies without sweating demographics.
    Irem is indeed not Egypt. For example, if a player came to me with an idea inspired by an ancient egyptian goddess I could let him run with it. But if that player came to me with the same idea set in canon Irem, I'd have to say sorry the Iremites were super patriarchical and didn't worship any of the dozens of cool Egyptian goddesses because they gotta be darker and edgier than their descendants. Or some fucking reason like that.

    Can you tell this is one of the very few additions to the Irem backstory I thought was a big ass misstep and cheerfully ignore at every possible opportunity? It also doesn't jive with the theme well for me. These are literal asexual or at least hermphroditic cthonic cosmic horrors doing the whole lovecraftian indifference to the suffering of mankind thing who send a punch of god-sorcerers to build this city who are all on record as regarding the human race as a whole as literal animals who at best show a dim promise when properly trained. Neither of these forces strikes me as likely to decide that one important factor in who has the power in their new society should rest on if they got a penis.

    Also...totally personal bias here but...its so boring. Bronze age patriarchies are a dime a dozen in regular history. Egypt always stood out to me by not following the model of its peers. Making Irem just like everywhere else in early history felt like a reduction more than anything. It took away goddesses, limited character backgrounds, and left me uninspired.

    My opinion on the subject, obviously. You feel different, more power to you. Its not a direction I can say I got behind, and if second edition wants to maybe down play that aspect I'd be fine with that decision.
