      Off the top of my head, the general guidelines I recall were that 1 experience in the Chronicle system ≈ 5 XP in the old Storytelling System, with scaling dot costs based approximately on the cost of a three-dot purchase in the old system, and a general bias towards making supernatural powers a little more affordable.

      Taking that into mind, and cracking open my corebook, I'd venture something like...

      Start with 10 Merit dots instead of 7.
      Start with 5 free experiences.
      1 experience for a dot of a defining pillar
      2 experiences for a dot of another pillar
      3 experiences for a favored or fated affinity
      4 experiences for an unfavored affinity
      3 experiences for an utterance
      3 experiences or 1 sebayt experience for a dot of Memory
      1 experience to recover a lost dot of Willpower
      When you would normally award sebayt XP, instead award one sebayt beat. Convert 10 sebayt beats into 1 sebayt experience, which can be spent to improve Memory.

      Good luck improvising freestyle Virtue and Vice effects for the powers that cite the original seven Virtues and seven Vices.

              Thanks for these tips. They have been very helpful. I'm sharing some GMC update house rules below in case anyone finds them helpful.

              Updating Memory, Virtue, & Vice to better align with GMC's Integrity and B&S's Humanity

              Remembrance replaces Virtue. This is a defining characteristic from the Mummy’s past self. Regain 1 Willpower by pursuing knowledge that would shed light on who she was during the time of ancient Irem. Regain all Willpower once per story by taking a great risk to regain some token of her past.

              Authority replaces Vice. This is a defining characteristic of the Mummy as an agent of the Judges of Duat. Regains 1 Willpower by acting in accord to her Judge’s archetype. Regain all Willpower once per story by taking actions to further her Judge’s agenda that put her at very great risk.

              Memory: The morality stat in Mummy, begins at 3. Sins are replaced by Breaking Points. Mummies are penalized for having low Memory scores and rewarded for having high scores.
              • Enlightened (Memory 9-10): Gains the Eidetic Memory and Unseen Sense (Judges of Duat) Merits. These merits are lost should the Mummy’s Memory dip below 9.
              • Clear-headed (Memory 7-8): (as Memory 8 in the M:tC rulebook).
              • Inattentive (Memory 5-6): Rolls to interact with humans are at -1. Meditation rolls are reduced by -1 when outside of Tomb.
              • Oblivious (Memory 4): Rolls to interact with humans are at -1 and rolls to interact with technology (through certain Mental skills) are at -1. Meditation rolls are reduced by -2 when outside of Tomb.
              • Confused (Memory 3): Rolls to interact with humans are at -2 and rolls to interact with technology (through certain Mental skills) are at -1. Meditation rolls are reduced by -3 when outside of Tomb.
              • Amnesic (Memory 2): Rolls to interact with humans are at -3 and rolls to interact with technology (through certain Mental skills) are at -2. Meditation rolls are at reduced by -5 when outside of Tomb.
              • Senescent (Memory 1): Rolls to interact with humans are at -5 and rolls to interact with technology (through certain Mental skills) are at -3. Meditation rolls are reduced to a luck die when outside of Tomb.
              • Akhu (Memory 0): (as in the M:tC rulebook)


              Tweaks for Sybaris

              Sybaris Effects:
              • Terror Sybaris (as in M:tC, resisted by Composure + Resolve roll)
                • Dramatic Failure – Mortal gains the Frightened Condition
                • Failure – Mortal gains the Spooked Condition
                • Success – Mortal gains the Shaken Condition
              • Unease Sybaris (as in M:tC; degree equal to Memory - Sekhem)
                • -8 or less – Mortal gains the Enervated Condition
                • -7 to -5 – Mortal gains the Soulless Condition
                • -4 to -3 – Mortal gains the Obsession Condition
                • -2 to -1 – Mortal gains the Delusional Condition
                • 0 – Mortal gains the Spooked Condition
                • +1 – Mortal gains the Shaken Condition
                • +2 or more – Mortal is immune to the effects of Unease Sybaris


              Experiences

              Experience Costs:
              • Attributes: 3 / dot
              • Merits: 1 / dot
              • Skills: 2 / dot
              • Skill Specialties: 1 each
              • Defining Pillar: 1 / dot
              • Other Pillars: 2 / dot
              • Favored or Fated Affinities: 4 each
              • Other Affinities: 5 each
              • Utterances: 5 each
              • Memory: 3 / dot
              • Willpower: 1 / dot
              Bonus Experiences:
              • Starting Character – 5 experience points
              • Around the Block – 10 experience points
              • Rising Star – 15 experience points
              • Acclimated Arisen – 20 experience points
              • Descent Veteran – 30 experience points
              • Mover and Shaker – 40 experience points
              • Urban Legend – 55 experience points
              • Legendary Immortal – 105 experience points


              Sekhem uses, changed time scale for Descent rolls forced due to time active.

              Sekhem:
              • Attribute/Skill/Power Maximum (as in rulebook)
              • Descent Rolls (pool as in rulebook but rolling conditions as follows)
                • Time:
                  • Sekhem 10 – roll after 7 days (failure adds 3 days)
                  • Sekhem 9 – roll after 7 days (failure adds 3 days)
                  • Sekhem 8 – roll after 7 days (failure adds 3 days)
                  • Sekhem 7 – roll after 7 weeks (failure adds 3 weeks)
                  • Sekhem 6 – roll after 7 weeks (failure adds 3 weeks)
                  • Sekhem 5 – roll after 7 weeks (failure adds 3 weeks)
                  • Sekhem 4 – roll after 7 months (failure adds 3 months)
                  • Sekhem 3 – roll after 7 months (failure adds 3 months)
                  • Sekhem 2 – roll after 7 months (failure adds 3 months)
                  • Sekhem 1 – roll after 7 years (failure adds 3 years)
                • Sins against Duat: (as in M:tC rulebook, p. 155)
              • Effortless Power (as in M:tC rulebook)
              • Lifesense: Can detect Sekhem (as per kepher in the M:tC rulebook)
              • Possession Immunity (as in M:tC rulebook; works against the Strix too)
              • Repairing the Sahu: Can sacrifice one dot of Sekhem per turn to repair one level of aggravated damage.
              • Unclean Remains (as in rulebook)
              • Undeath (as in rulebook)
              • Using Third Tier Utterances (see Utterances below)


              Affinity Changes

              Affinities
              • Replace any Willpower expenditures with the Pillar of the appropriate type for Pillar Affinities, use the character’s Defining Pillar for expenditures on Guild Affinities.
              • Ignore any mechanics linked to Virtues or Vices except as noted below.
              • Affinities (Note that many Affinities operate well without any changes (except for substituting Pillars for Willpower). Affinities listed below required major revisions due to use of derangements or Vice/Virtue mechanics.)
                • Ancient Horror Unveiling: Target is affected by the Spooked Condition. If the target’s resistance roll is a dramatic failure he is affected by the Frightened Condition instead.
                • Fearsome Soul: Replace Vice with Authority and Virtue with Remembrance.
                • Nihilist Awakening: Target is affected by the Broken Condition. If the target’s resistance roll is a dramatic failure he is affected by the Fugue Condition instead.
                • Paragon Shames the Weak: Targets are affected by the Humbled Condition.
                • Radiant Lifeforce: May suffer 1 point of Aggravated damage to remove the Broken, Enervated, Fugue, Madness, Obsession, or Soulless Condition from herself. She gains the Lethargic Condition.
                • Rouse the Khaibit: Replace Vice with Authority and Virtue with Remembrance.
                • Soul Infusion: Use of this Affinity to drain Willpower causes the target to gain the Soulless Condition. If the target is already affected by the Soulless Condition then they gain the Enervated Condition instead. This Affinity cannot be used on targets suffering from the Enervated Condition.
                • Voice of Conscience: Target is affected by the Inspired Condition.
                • Voice of Temptation: Target is affected by the Leveraged Condition.
                • Wisdom from Ruin: Ignore the first ability of this Affinity altogether.
                • Words Summoned Forth: Target is affected by the Obsession Condition. If the target’s resistance roll is a dramatic failure, he is affected by the Delusional Condition instead.


              Utterance Changes

              Utterances
              • First Tier: Costs 1 Pillar of any type.
              • Second Tier: Costs as First Tier +1 Pillar of any type listed in the Utterance’s description.
              • Third Tier: Costs as Second Tier + 1 additional Pillar of another type listed must be spent, 1 Willpower must be spent, or 1 Sekhem must be sacrificed. (See notes below.)
              • Ignore any mechanics linked to Virtues or Vices except as noted below.
              • Utterances:
                • Ancestry of Forgotten Stars: Tier 3 costs each Hierophant the additional sacrifice of 1 Sekhem.
                • Awaken the Dead: Tier 3 costs additional sacrificed Sekhem (1 dot = Memory + Sekhem hours duration, 2 dots = Memory + Sekhem days duration, 3 dots = Memory + Sekhem weeks duration, 4 dots = Memory + Sekhem months duration, 5 dots = Memory + Sekhem years duration)
                • Blessed is the God-King: Tier 1 causes the Arisen to be affected by the Steadfast Condition. Tier 2 causes the target to be affected by the Inspired Condition. Tier 3 cost is the additional sacrifice of 1 Sekhem.
                • Bound as Fingers in a Fist: Tier 3 cost is 1 additional Pillar (Ka, Ren, or Ba). Ignore all mentions of changing victim’s Virtues and Vices.
                • Cthonic Dominion: Tier 3 cost is an additional 1 Willpower.
                • Command the Beasts: Tier 3 cost is an additional 1 Willpower.
                • Discipline the Obstinate: Tier 3 cost is the additional sacrifice of 1 Sekhem.
                • Doom Affliction: Tier 3 cost is 1 additional Pillar (Ab, Ka, or Ba).
                • Dreams of Dead Gods: Tier 1 causes the victims to be affected by the Confused or Distracted Condition. Tier 2 causes the target to be affected by either the Shaken or the Swooning Condition. Tier 3 cost is an additional 1 Willpower and causes the target to be affected by the Soulless, Charmed, or Tasked Condition.
                • Dust Beneath Feet: Tier 3 cost is the additional sacrifice of 1 Sekhem.
                • Gift of the Golden Ankh: Tier 3 cost is an additional 1 Willpower.
                • Harvest the Divine Flesh: Tier 1 causes the target to be affected by the Arm Wrack, Blinded, Deafened, or Leg Wrack Tilts. Tier 3 cost is the additional sacrifice of 1 Sekhem.
                • Inscription of Flesh: Tier 2 first effect is changed so that it can be used to cause or cure a Disease in the target. Targets affected by a Disease suffer the effects of the “Grave” Sick Tilt. Tier 3 first effect is changed so that it can be used to kill ordinary mortals instantly. Mages, Hunters, Sin-Eaters, mortals with Supernatural Merits, and mortals under the effects of supernatural Conditions are immune. Damage inflicted to Prometheans by the second Tier 3 effect is Lethal rather than Aggravated. The fourth Tier 3 effect can be used to add or remove the Arm Wrack, Blinded, Deafened, Insensate, or Leg Wrack Tilts to the target. Tier 3 cost is an additional 1 Willpower.
                • Jar of Clay: No changes from M:tC’s GotD.
                • Jar of Teeth: Tier 3 cost is the additional sacrifice of 1 Sekhem.
                • Kiss of Apep: Tier 3 costs 1 additional Pillar (Ren, Ka, or Sheut).
                • Mastery of Heka’s Bounty: Tier 3 third effect causes Mortal victims to be affected by the Beaten Down Tilt instead. If a mortal’s resistance roll is a dramatic failure then that mortal also gains the Broken Condition. Tier 3 costs 1 additional Pillar (Sheut, Ab, or Ren).
                • Obedient Clay: Tier 3 costs the additional sacrifice of 1 Willpower.
                • Palace Knows Its Pharaoh: Tier 3 costs an additional 1 Willpower.
                • Power of Re: Tier 3 costs 1 additional Pillar (Ab or Sheut).
                • Redacting the Word: Tier 3 costs 1 additional Pillar (Sheut, Ka, or Ren).
                • Rebuke the Vizier: Tier 3 costs an additional 1 Willpower.
                • Revelations of Smoke and Flame: Tier 3 costs the additional sacrifice of 1 Sekhem.
                • Rite of the Scarab Minion: Tier 3 costs 1 additional Pillar (Sehut, Ren or Ka).
                • Secrets Stripped from Skies: Tier 3 costs the additional sacrifice of 1 Sekhem.
                • Seeds of Life: Tier 3 cost is 1 additional Pillar (Ka, Ren, or Ab).
                • Torn Veil of Fogetting: Tier 3 costs an additional 1 Willpower.
                • Water of Life and Death: Tier 3 cost is the additional sacrifice of 1 Sekhem.
                • Word of Amanuensis: Tier 3 costs an additional 1 Willpower.
                • Words of Dead Fury: Tier 3 costs the additional sacrifice of 1 Sekhem.
                • Words of Dead Glory: Tier 3 costs an additional 1 Willpower.
                • Words of Dead Hunger: Tier 3 cost is 1 additional Pillar (Ren, Ba, or Sheut).
                • Wrathful Desert Power: Tier 3 costs the additional sacrifice of 1 Sekhem.

                                          Lulz. Depends on what one defines a box as. We can quibble over mechanics forever; ditto for how one judges good vs. bad. I posted my house rules so others could use them as inspirations for their own house rules.

                                          Ultimately the overall quality of the game experience relies on the the storyteller and players. Mechanics are merely means to an end. Recovering Pillars, like getting more Vitae can be the simple matter of a dice roll and not something that serves as a barrier to doing interesting things. Abstractions like dice rolls are meant to save time, not consume it.

                                          Doing interesting things is entirely dependent on the troupe as a whole not on some bit of text in a book or online. YMMV.

                                          From my PoV Mummy is a game of reflection and a number of its systems hang on Meditation, the average character should expect to meditate a bit each day. A clever storyteller can work a good amount of story into meditation itself. Memory, IRL, is a very reflective process, this can be good, you can use meditation time as exposition time and plant all kinds of story seeds. Remember Mummies have really disjointed memories that frequently lack context. This can be a big part of this game. Again YMMV.

                                          Note I'm not sure what the real quibble is here. Willpower is much harder to get back in this game. In my own games I prefer that players only have to spend it to do non-trivial things (and all Affinities are rather trivial effects). So...YMMV

                                              Your houserules are your houserules, I'm just expressing my criticisms of their basis.                                                   I get that complaint. IMO, it is even better to just contain the corpse-likeness to when Utterances are used and when your Sekhem is high (IIRC you look like a corpse at 8 Sekhem and above anyway), boiling it down to 2 triggers for Terror Sybaris. This aligns better with the apparent intensions of Affinities and Utterances.

                                                  The main problem with Sybaris on the whole is that it is trying to channel both Lunacy and Disquiet. Unease Sybaris/Disquiet is a very kludgey mechanic due to the social nature of WW games. Irrational NPC behavior makes the game more difficult to storytell. Rolling penalties into the Integrity and Humanity mechanics as GMC and B&S have done is a much better way to go. Eliminating dice pool penalties becomes enough incentive for increasing one's Memory (or Humanity in the case of a game like Promethean). The Breaking Point system makes increasing Memory (or sometimes just keeping what you have) enough of a challenge, making extra mechanics like Unease Sybaris or Disquiet seems potentially excessive. One thing I hope the developer re-examines the Unease Sybaris system; it might not be necessary, I'm not certain that it fulfills a necessary role in the game that can't be more easily accomplished through ordinary storytelling.
