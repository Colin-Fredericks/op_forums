                And again, Immortals are an essential component of a mummy's cult, which is why the core book needs to at least talk about them, to highlight just how important they are.                         Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                        And again, Immortals are an essential component of a mummy's cult, which is why the core book needs to at least talk about them, to highlight just how important they are.
                        I kind of can't help but feel, as it relates to the current conversation, the misconception of this statement is part of the problem-because, no, Immortals aren't important to the cult, they just help out in regards to it's high regards.

                        Immortals were introduced as a concurrent point of interest for characters who don't experience death easily and can experience multiple points of interests along the historical axis along as a means of exploring various other long held issues. First Edition, though, proved the game works quite well without them.

                        In theory this is a fine theory to approach the concept of Mummy, particularly since Mummy itself was not especially popular as was Immortals in terms of wide trends. Put two mildly popular things that have longevity together and also share similar themes and conflicts along the parallel lines of both, go forward.

                        The Problem is that Mummy has been a little lacking on what Mummy fans and Would Be Mummy fans have been looking for in terms of Mummy, and when confronted with the alternative just aren't satisfied.

                        Personally, I can't speak to the latter as I have not yet caught up on the material and still trust it's not a big issue-but as someone who wanted Mummy and didn't want any any of the immortals in my Mummy Core and must look for positives in that notion regardless, the notion that the Endless do not deliver a satisfactory update on experience is somewhat alarming. One I take with a grain of salt, because I frequently find fans blowing things out of proportion, but alarm all the same. Fortunately, the sluggishness of the former hasn't slowed me down much.

                            Personally, I can't speak to the latter as I have not yet caught up on the material and still trust it's not a big issue-but as someone who wanted Mummy and didn't want any any of the immortals in my Mummy Core and must look for positives in that notion regardless, the notion that the Endless do not deliver a satisfactory update on experience is somewhat alarming. One I take with a grain of salt, because I frequently find fans blowing things out of proportion, but alarm all the same. Fortunately, the sluggishness of the former hasn't slowed me down much.
                            ​I'm only going off of the what's expressed here, and LostLight's comments in particular are helpful for this, so thank you.

                            But I agree with the above: Immortals can and should be their own thing, though I think there is much utility in highlighting how from Risen's perspective an "Eternal" who runs on sekhem can be very handy to anchor their cult while away. From the immortal's perspective they may not have much cross-line understanding on how they actually work as a supernaturally-empowered being. They know they're unnaturally long-lived. They know they either did, participated or must continue to do something for that long life. And that's about it.

                            Anything else they'll pick up from running into other supernaturals--full splats, in particular, and mummies are good candidates to find them and brand them with their own worldview. Mummies are fellow travelers, so to speak. So are vampires. And spirits.

                            I don't mind anchoring Endless with sekhem as their power resource, give them a supernatural tolerance merit, and then run the rest of their powers as horrors. That's "clean". The rest of the types of immortals could be given a short paragraph--essentially "ideas on how this manifests" and then the section could move on to mechanics and cult roles.

                            Endless thus, from a mummy's perspective, represent linear memory to the Risen's asynchronous time-memory. That's not insignificant to the product.

                            I do hope that they receive greater treatment in another book. Not by giving them half a dozen power stats (Chi? What?), but by elaborating on their common sub-type powers and providing mechanics for them as well as "this is what it means" text. A good method would be to treat powers as either or both of merits and horrors, and make use of the Conditions and Tilts system.

                                        In terms of tying them more to Mummy, I think that their various rituals should be recast as Sekhem Sorcery Rites. If you don't want to require any of the Sorcery Merits, make them Open Rites. But especially with the Blood Bathers and Body Thieves, the rites they use feel perfectly in keeping with what we know of Sekhem Sorcery.

                                        One place to trim word count is to cut out those Endless who don't use rituals to become what they are: the Mental Body Thieves, the spontaneous Reborn, etc. It's not that they don't exist; it's just that they'd be addressed elsewhere.

                                        The Endless Micro-Template bothers me. One of the points of Micro-Templates is that all of their benefits are in the Merits: they don't give you a power stat. As well, the fact that you only get to treat it as Supernatural Resistance if you take an additional Merit to enable that feels clumsy. If you're going to give the Endless a Sekhem rating, just make them a lesser template like ghouls, wolf-bloods, et al. Better, don't give them a Sekhem rating: they don't need it, and the suggestion of tying their Rituals into Sorcery already connects them with Mummy's metaphysics well enough. Either way, I don't see a point in the Micro-Template.

                                                        I agree that the immortality-specific rituals of each Endless need to be more formalized, but I don't think they necessarily need to be Open Rites, both for distinction and as to not unintentionally skew perception of the current line-up of Rites. As said, some aren't even based on rituals, but I wouldn't say that's the strongest reasoning for removing them either. Of the Other Immortals in the core book, the ones presented in detail are all those who have the most flawed immortalities, and each foil and contrast the Arisen in different ways because of this.

                                                        The Endless micro-template is non-standard, but it's clearly more like a common sub-template to these characters' supernatural gimmick. Gift of Years has pretty distinct benefits that suit the nature of these immortals, and that's in addition to access to a plethora of Supernatural Merits and a handful of Timeless Merits from Chapter 3. Supernatural Resistance as intended might be a little redundant, but there is pretty comprehensible value in the way the other merits are handled.

                                                        As tie-ins and supporting characters in Mummy, it also makes sense for the Endless to have Sekhem, as there are multiple mechanics and antagonists that interact with the trait in particular ways, and it strengthens Endless associations to the main cast and the game as a whole if they can be targeted or endangered in the same ways as Mummies and their lot.
