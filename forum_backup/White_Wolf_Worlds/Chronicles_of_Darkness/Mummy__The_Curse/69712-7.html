                        Originally posted by Jesse Heinig View Post
                        In this information age, anyone can look up the historical facts of the Boer War or the fall of the Ottoman Empire or the formation of Turkey.
                        The Boer War, huh? With Mummy's obvious ties to Egypt, I was fully expecting we'd see the rise of Egyptology, the fall of the Ottoman Empire (and the Khedivate), al-Thawra (the Arab revolt), pan-Arabism, the Mahdist campaign in Anglo-Egyptian Sudan, Gallipoli, the Young Turks, the Khalifat Movement, maybe even early Zionism. T. E. Lawrence, Howard Carter, Muhammad Ali Pasha, Atatürk, Omar Mukhtar, Freya Stark, Abdel-Kader al-Djazairi, Richard Francis Burton, Charles George Gordon ... all that. The Boer War seems rather further afield than I was expecting, though I'm certainly glad to see it included. Its a damned interesting subject, and its always good to see more coverage of Africa in the WoD. Especially since the Nameless Empire penetrated very deep into Africa, perhaps all the way down to the Cape now?

                        Makes me wonder what other surprises we'll see. The French Foreign Legion and their conflicts with Touaregs and tje Wassoulou and Toucouleur Empires in the Sahara? Italy's attempts at colonizing Libya and Somaliland? Perhaps even the British colonial venture in India? Yeah, its a bit further afield but it fits tangentially. Mummy's era is decades after the Sepoy Mutiny and company rule has already ended in India (as has the Mughal Empire; the last Mughal Emperor, Bahadur Shah II, going into exile in Burma). The British are ruling India directly at this point. Nationalism, colonialism, Theosophy, Orientalism and many other isms are all mingling in weird ways. Also, with the collapse of the Ottoman Empire, there was a movement in Muslim India (which was never ruled by the Ottomans) to revive the title of Caliph.

                        All very tantalizing. Can't wait for the book.

                        The Mummy segment of Dark Eras is also useful even to people who don't set a chronicle in that period, in that it can serve as a flashback device. Mummies might acquire lost memories of time spent in that era.
                        I like this...

                        Plus, that time period is recent enough that cultists might be grandchildren of people who lived through some of those events, which allows groups to play with narrative threads that follow the familial and cultural ties of various mummy cultists and their mortal associates.
                        But I like this even more. Cultists really are taking their place as a big part of M:tC, and I'm looking forward to seeing that role expanded as future supplements come out. Really hoping we'll see a "player's guide to cultists" or some such somewhere down the road, but maybe that's just me.

                        Naturally, I also included a few new Merits and powers that are pertinent to the time period.
                        Sweet.

                                                            Mummy is quite rich in the number of ways that mortals can engage with its mythos - cultists and sorcerers obviously - but also anything in the art and antiquities world (the relics and amkhata are fascinating in their own right), pulpy adventurers* (of whom mummies and their entourage are particularly suitable foes), secret societies that aren't run by mummies (for whom shadowy history, sacred geometries, ancient relics, and alchemical secrets are bread and butter). And all of these can intersect with each other as well as they can with mummies themselves. Add in the fact that playing one mummy and surrounding mortals is one of the suggested campaign frames, and a "mortal player's supplement" would be a grand addition.

                                                            * Who I really hope get a mention in Dark Eras.
