    Was mummy designed to be a one chronicle game?

    01-12-2014, 11:27 AM
    This is regarding the game design. Was mummy designed to be a one chronicle game that the players can wrap up in a few sessions and move on? Given the nature of the descent, this sees like a default mode.

      I don't think mummy was written as a one chronicle game. Playing only one descent could limit the active time of the mummies, but it is always possible to play over multiple different time periods, or play cultists in the downtime between the awakenings of the mummies.

      When playing only mummies, it depends on wether the mummies are on the clock. If they are on the clock they will succeed all descent roll after completing their objective. This would still mean at least one or two whole chronicles of activity and more if the mummy only accomplishes his objective late in his descent. Asuming four chapter stories and four story chronicles, the characters would have 35 sessions or 398 days of time.

      During a Sothic Turn there is no objective, so mummies may always make descent rolls to stay at high Sekhem longer. And when they hit Sekhem 1, they do not have to make automatic descent rolls anymore, so they could stay active indefinitely (unless they provoke their judges or are killed with aggravated). Using methods explained in Guildhalls of the Deathless, this low Sekhem rating can be overcome by boosting their effective Sekhem.

      So if you want to play a multiple year chronicle (both in character and out of character), I would either play during diffent time periods, play during a Sothic Turn or also give the players cultist/ghost characters.

                  There's a side-bar in the section on the Descent rules describing how Sothic awakenings affect the Descent.

                  To OP, a Descent is a framework to sort of plan on how long the campaign will be. If you're running Mage, for example, you still break your sessions up into a variety of stories, and you do the same for Mummy. Like someone said, during a Sothic turn you can just hang out at Sekhem 1, but even then Sekhem 1 lasts "one chronicle" so you can justify any campaign length that you and your players need to tell the story. There's just a sort of finish line at the end of the tunnel.

                  "Well, what if you don't finish the story but their Descent ends?" you might ask. That's not a problem; that can be an engaging problem! What happens when your arch-nemesis has stolen a 3,000 year old relic and is going to use it to harvest the souls of your cultists, but you just died for the final time? Well, you wake up for your next Descent and have to pick up the pieces! It's not a set-back - it's conflict, and conflict is a good story

                          Well it's definitely the easiest game to play in one chronicle. But...

                          I started to really think about this question and I kind of realized something. A mummy is actually going to be a lot more active than the book implies. We live in a very active world where you can get to the other side of the globe in a matter of hours. A mummy, a mostly sedentary creature, for the first time is able to see the world without expanding so many resources and time. We don't live in a world where all you're ever going to see is your small town of 300 people and only talk to those three people you actually like. We have the internet, we're connected to everything. Because of this it's wouldn't be all that hard to imagine a cult being much more active than in the past, making deals with people in another continent or exploring multiple cities for relics. We're also so much more connected and things move so much faster, the cult will probably need a mummy now more than ever. Before a mummy would probably be awoken every couple decades for one reason or another. But now a mummy can do so much more with all the resources they've amassed. And that's also something that can be explored in a chronicle, how does the mummy adapt to this new world. Also because of this, mummies will have more time than ever to devote to personal exploration and increasing memory.

                              Also, for as much as the judges and the nature of the descent actively discourage you from trying to stick around in the world for personal reasons, the game also encourages you to start recovering your memories. A mummy who is in the process of recovering their memory and becoming more human (and possibly pursuing apotheosis) will tend to try to bend the rules and get as much time to pursue that goal as possible. While the judges and mummy society tend to frown on mummies who do stuff like ordering their cult to resurrect them for a trivial purpose as soon as possible after the end of their last descent...that's exactly the sort of rule-break-y thing that such a mummy would do (and hope no one notices). And since mummy society is very "beg forgiveness, don't ask permission" and they don't make a habit of checking in on each other for rule-breaking (they are all busy people with shit to do), it's the sort of thing you could feasibly get away with.

                              In other words, the mummy who is only summoned up by their cult twice a century for tasks of Great Import is probably a great example of most mummies, the ones who are still good little servants. The PCs are probably not most mummies, or if they are they probably won't be for very long.

                                  Here is my take,

                                  The mummy book was pretty vague about how often a mummy can be awoken. I got the impression that once every few generations was the average. Sure, a mummy can be awoken more often, but this might have an expense in Sekhem or other supernatural power. I got the impression that a wise cult does not awaken their mummy too much. After all, there must be some reason that mummies are not permanently awake as cults continuously restart their descents. Again, the book was not too clear about this.

                                  Now...a mummy that has started his descent has a clock ticking. He/she might stay awake for a long time, but it would be at a substantially weaker sekhem 1 or 2. Another thing, if the mummy player tries to stay awake longer, the mummy will need to do illegal things. What I mean by illegal would be, not doing what your Judge wants you to do, getting more memory, or cannibalizing Sekhem. All of these things are a big no no in mummy. If you want to say awake the longest you would need to reset your descent at Sekhem 1, 2 or 3 to get the most bang for your buck, which, ironically, is when you are the weakest.

                                  I can see a parallel with Demon the Descent, be a good angel that is a slave or a free demon that is weak. A mummy that extends his life and get more memory and do his thing is probably going to draw the wrath of the judges. The judges will probably send a more loyal mummy with Sekhem 10 to crush the offending mummy and send him back to Duat for punishment.

                                          Originally posted by Aiden View Post
                                          Actually, it is very straightforward how often Mummies can be expected to wake up. Pg. 214, in the sidebar 'How Deep the Well'. It is in the ST Handbook.
                                          That doesn't really answer how often cults do wake their Mummies, though. I mean, by the default rules, a cult with a Mummy with a one-dot centering Relic could keep their patron active indefinitely, but that's clearly not the intent of the game, which describes long periods of inactivity.

                                          However, there IS a good reason for a Mummy to not want to be summoned too often - Memory. At Memory 3, which most Mummies tend to return to unless they make an effort to stay above or below it, you remember Irem (or things you heard about Irem; it's hard to keep them separate) and your prior Descent. (*) The more often you get woken, the more you lose, especially since you lose a dot of Memory at the end of every Descent. You have to get up to Memory 5 before you can even get rough knowledge of your other Descents, and keeping that relies on regularly re-purchasing Memory 6+. Most Mummies aren't going to bother, and so are going to want to make sure that their cult summons them only when opportune - when they're necessary, or when there's the opportunity to earn a lot of Descent resets. (**) This is even true of Mummies who are interested in building up their Memory, as increasing your Memory prompts a Descent roll, so it's important that they arrange their agenda and circumstances to earn a lot of resets.

                                          (*) - I exclude Descents where the Arisen completes a brief objective and returns to Duat without going through a full Descent from this and the Memory loss. I assume that many tomb violations and cult ceremonies are handled quickly and easily, with the Mummy returning to death before they're finished "booting up" or soon after, and thus not incurring either hit.

                                          (**) - In addition to those listed, I presume that "secure vessels with the intent of returning them to Duat" is within every Judge's agenda, and so always earns a reset.

                                                          True, but it's likey the Maa-Kep and Su-Menent already have their eyes on such a Mummy, and are very eager and willing to earn a reset by mulching a potential heretic. In a game session yesterday, one of my PCs decided to go on a full anti Irem rant because he didn't have much memory anymore, and took a hit to his Sekhem just by CONSIDERING the idea that the Judges were lying. Granted, he wasn't politely hinting, he was getting pretty.. "blasphemous". It was to a cultist of another Mummy, thankfully (Otherwise theyd've both suffered a Sekhem hit). But youd be amazed at how effective the ancient Iremite technology of Carrot and Stick works in the right context. The player of the Cultist (Who was a Mummy's off character) is now starting to wonder about how much the Judges care, or how much this cultist trusts them watching his mummy crush wither and deage before his very eyes for speaking against the Judges.

                                                          The way I began to think of it.. I think the default is the opposite of what we assume. We assume that MOST cults are smart, and don't wake up their Mummy often.. so that they have few memories, little real impact in the world or place in it, but are SMARTLY conserving said memory. But that isn't the case. The standard PC mummy is at Memory 3 for a reason--its the lowest you can get without truly abandoning your humanity. Judging from the sidebar and all, and how often most functional groups tend towards "everyone is a mummy" or "I'm trying to be awake most of the time so I can really do cool stuff".. and how hard and weird things have become in the last century or so, once the first few Sothic Mummies woke up, and the Egyptology boom hit.. I think most Mummies have been overused, by the Judges and their Cults. I imagine MOST of a Mummy's life is a neverending Memory 3 cycle, with occasional spikes.. mostly just remembering your last life, and thus have Playable Continuity.. but little of the past. Youre just a mummy right now, with occasional bits of flavor from the past.

                                                          At default level of Memory, most mummies wouldn't even remember the countless petty lifetimes they've lived. They still can't even remember the major ones, yet. But by endgame, memory 8 and 9? You may find yourself exploring a far MORE coherent and active existence than we think. The developers said Mummies experience life as a flash of hazy images, scattered across time, with little continuity. But if a Mummy at memory 3 just.. kept going, his older lives would disappear from his mind. It isn't that they didn't LIVE all this time.. its just that their Memory of it is gone.

                                                          But the intended route of the PCs is to learn how to master their Curse, not be enslaved by it. Get frugal. Figure out how to not piss off the judges, but also not earn a quick ticket back home to Duat.. how to explore himself, while also keeping the Judges and other Mummies happy. They learn to play the game.. and suddenly, a "starter" mummy becomes a SMARTER mummy.. ones that don't just burn through their thousands of years worth of XP in a death cycle Memory game. Ones that learn to grow, like living people do. And indeed, some begin to see a way out, off in the distance.. if theyre willing to give up the gifts that came with their Curse.
