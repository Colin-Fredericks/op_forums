                    From the updates we know that first drafts of Deceived finished up in late November last year redlining happened and then second drafts happened and then CAS was redlining those second drafts. Now, with most books it seems they go through two lots of redlining then move on to editing. Sure their are some exceptions that have three lots of redlining. However, if Deceived was allowing normal standard for most books released by Onyx path CAS just needed to finish redlining the second batch before editing could happen. Now it seems likely that different writers were getting their stuff to him to redline at different times, hence the reason the process has taken a while.

                    Now one of the writers drops out for whatever reasons, life happens and all that. However, we can assume that said writer was working on the second draft version of his section. Meaning that he's closer to what CAS finally wants in the section then when the writer started presumably. Now it seems CAS is either currently sorting out a replacement writer or has already done so (we don't know what "on top of it" really means) to start the section the same way all the other writers started their with his outlines presumably.

                    However, would it not be quicker and easier for CAS to take what the original writer had (which is likely at least partially in second draft) and finish it himself (obviously still crediting the writer for his work, no plagiarism)?                     ​I know there is a lot of assumption in the above theory/line of questioning. I would like to be more specific but we simply don't have the info in the community to make more solid statements about how the Mummy books are being made.

                                                        04-03-2014, 10:38 AM
                                                        Originally posted by JoeB View Post
                                                        Link to the podcast please? :-)

                                                        Mummy also has a special place in my heart, Im the only ST running it for DGS, been running it for over a year now and making do with the core and Guildhalls. Added content, teasers and bonus materials are great to have, just wondering why we dont get them on the Mummy blog or Facebook page.
                                                        Because that would require some engagement with the game's community.

                                                        Every since the Core came out last March (for general release) its become increasingly apparent that CAS has no interest in fostering a Mummy community or having dialogue with the games fans.

                                                        Honestly, if you look pre-Core release there were the blog posts, yes they got increasingly delayed as things went on but they were a thing. When the Kickstarter happened a lot of people joined in on it and it got a good community going while it lasted. Rich T was overseeing that for the most part though. Shortly after its release we got a couple of Kickstarter e-mails, a Dark Days Radio Podcast, a two part interview and then a single Guildhalls teaser. Around the Guildhalls release CAS came and made about 3 posts on the then "new" forums. Since then its been utterly silent.

                                                        Weirdly, I'm not actually surprised by this in retrospect. I reread CAS's old interview the other day and re-listened to the Dark Days podcast. I get the impression from it all that CAS has a very clear vision of what he wants Mummy to be. He has a VISION for the game. This makes sense, according to sources he's had this idea and wanted to implement it since at least the time he was writing Mummy the Resurrection.

                                                        If he has a solid idea of what Mummy is and can be and what it is not and can't be that's fine. I do get the impression that its making him want everything to be 110% perfect before he even tried to develop it further. Sounds awesome right? Means every product released is going to be super high quality. Yeah maybe, but it also means its utterly shaped to the developers preference and what he deems right or wrong for the setting a game line. If things have to be one persons version of perfect, then that can degrade their appeal to people who don't share that vision. Plus, we've all worked with perfectionists everything takes 3 times as long and their's no guarantee anythings is better then it was before the perfectionist nick-picked and corrected it for the Nth time.

                                                        CAS's negative attitude and dismissal of Crossover stuff in interviews is a good example of developer vision hurting the wider appeal of the game. CAS sees Mummy as a game concerned with the (and I use the term loosely) metaplot of the history of Irem, the Shan'Iatu and the quest for Apothesis. That's a fine set of things for the game to run off when combined with the PC's personal history and memory and such. However, being so consumed with the idea that a player and ST have to engage with that aspect that one does not provide support for if an ST or player wants to take the game in a different direction (say a cross over with Mage about hunting down ancient relics that may be Atlantian or may be Iremian and something else is afoot with the Seers) is restrictive and looses the broader appeal the game could have. All of this because the game is so tailored to the vision of the developer.

                                                        If the developer has such a strong standpoint on the game, then why would he go and get feedback from the community? Why engage with them other then to reveal the occassional preview so that they can get a feel for whats coming and talk about how awesome the new stuff is? The only time open-development (as for the new chronicle books or V20 line books) or lots of community intensive discussion (as for Demon the Descent) works is if the devs are interested in getting the community's input on the game and letting them shape it in some tiny way. Otherwise that stuff is just time and energy the dev could be using to write the game line.

                                                        I don't want to sound like the Mummy devs "owe" the fans anything. They don't we have no real ownership of the game, its not "ours" its a great bit of fiction that we can use as a spring pad for our imaginations to have a fun time. As such we should be thankful for the fact that the devs came up with this stuff. I am. Honestly, despite all the bitching and the disagreements I might have over the way the Mummy line is being handled and developed I am greatful that it exists. Likewise, I know how easy it is to sit here behind the anonymity of the internet and cut into hard working peoples efforts and generally be negative. I'm aware how much harder it is to be writing these books and continuing to develop this line and setting.

                                                        The only thing I want to add to that is that I'm a big believer in "death of the author". People take what they take out of a story. The author can intend to imply or want to imply or outright state certain things with their writing. That's great and I'm happy to listen to their explanations of this stuff and treat it as "cannon". At the same time, books and games are different. Your audience is going to want a wider array of possible interpretations and uses out of the writing of a game. As a game's writer surely then its benefits you to at least make a nod or effort towards options or interpretations outside of those you see as cannon. Even if they are 100 word long sidebars.

                                                        Wow, this ended up being a lot longer then I intended...stupid rants. Going to take a step back again.

                                                        "There is no God in the Badlands." - Sunny
