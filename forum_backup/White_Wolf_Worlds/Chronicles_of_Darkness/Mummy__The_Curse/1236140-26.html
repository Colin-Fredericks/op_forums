                            Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                            With the exception of raising Memory, I'm not sure that even pursuing a life of your own makes a Descent roll likely.

                            I do remember of somewhere it stating that avoiding following the Judge's Mission calls for a Descent Roll, but I can't remember where it is, and it isn't in page 201's list. I don't see blasphemy against the gods and declaring your love for a normal person in Iremic as regular things you do just to be yourself.

                            But again, I don't remember where every Descent roll is described. This same section doesn't inform that creating a Sadikh provokes a Descent Roll, for example, and it does.
                            The books don't outright say it, but 1e makes pretty direct implication that failing to pursue or investigate what it terms the "first purpose" — the reason the mummy was awakened — results in accelerated loss of Sekhem; depending on how the ST opts to run it this could be anything from directly ignoring the imperative the Call was used for provoking a Descent roll to simply being more lenient with which timer is being used while they're actively on the job, but the narrative function of it is to emphasize that the Rite of Return instills the Arisen with purpose when they arise outside the auspices of Sothis ascending.

                            As for the rest, it's worth remembering that the Judges are counted among the gods of Irem (and that it's still a Descent trigger if someone else does it and you don't correct them), that one of the reasons mummies might divert from their course is due to developing personal connections (and recall that, enhanced capacity for linguistic acquisition notwithstanding, the native language of the Arisen is Iremic, hence the further isolation they experience from their cults and themselves being the main speakers they will encounter), and that developing Memory is not an abstract exercise devoid of emotional context.

                            1e had a sidebar going over the two broad categories of reasons mummies might be diverted from their purpose, and they boil down to pursuit of self-knowledge and occult lore (potentially including Apotheosis) and satisfaction stemming from fixation or loss of faith (throwing themselves into the pursuit of e.g. power, wealth, or love). Touchstones can be based on mortals you once knew, and uncanny resemblances to loved ones you now barely remember is one of the multiple angles this game takes toward motifs of reincarnation.

                                            Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                            My take from the core book is that Ammut isn't antagonistic to the Arisen, or at least shouldn't be. Yet, the Shuankhsen are. What's the deal with the Devourer (aside Devouring things)?
                                            The Devourer is universal entropy. She can be patient, given that the laws of reality forbid her from taking Sekhem that does not give itself to her, but her stated end goal is the cessation of all meaningful existence. She is playing the very long game necessary to make that complete in the race against ever-transforming creation, and having a collection of mummies whose vengeful tendencies are turned upon the servants of the Judges with the advantage of her own devouring jaws and memories that don't crumble away between lives like the desert sands are her most direct way to tilt things toward feeding the world into her maw.

                                            I'm also under the impression that while Relics get more attention, Vestiges are actually more useful. Relics only have their listed powers to go for, and potential Sekhem. Vestiges give you extra Willpower recovery during sleep, and bonus dice to recover Pillars every chapter. Those two benefits seem to me to be better in general than anything else between those items, and the Judges don't seem to care a lot if you keep Vestiges to yourself. Did I miss something here, or is that correct?
                                            Vestiges give you extra Willpower recovery if they are resonant with you, and ditto for the bonus dice on Pillar recovery; while this does lend more benefit than is obvious to the Balanced and Overburdened Merits (and to a lesser extent the Virtuous and Vice-Ridden Merits), a narrow subset of all vestiges being notionally more useful in the long term than relics is not surprising, especially given that it's the Sekhem that the Judges want from the relics the Arisen are charged to retrieve. (Also, the resonant benefits are all a vestige in your Lifeweb can provide for the first story after you're awakened thanks to the way the Call works.)

                                            How do Traps and Curses work in a Tomb? At some point it's said that they trigger when a point of Peril is lost, is that it? And for how much time do they take effect?
                                            They're stage hazards that apply their environmental Tilts on anyone who stumbles into them just as they would natural circumstances; the individual Tilts specify how they're ended, with many of them simply lasting for the scene or being permanent qualities of the Tomb.

                                            The notes about what happens when you sacrifice a dot of Perils or Provisions instead of Geometry just interact with the subsequent note on Attacking Tombs; otherwise, the security and amenities in your Tomb are interacted with as they would in a normal scene.

                                                        08-10-2025, 09:28 PM
                                                        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                                        Are Open Rites available to anyone (if not stated otherwise), or just to non-splat humans?
                                                        They are the same phenomenon that Geist identifies as equivalent to zero-dot Ceremonies in the "The Root of Power" sidebar in that corebook. Anyone can perform them unless stated otherwise; the first line of the description is there to identify the difference between an Open Rite, a completely mundane action, and a supernatural power. The God-Machine effectively performs Open Rites on a grand scale through the medium of Infrastructure, and some Mysteries in Mage are the result of occult maneuvering or mishap to produce a supernatural outcome without directly casting spells.

                                                        Was the Second Irem in Antarctica ever explored in another book?
                                                        First Edition had no holdings for any of the guilds in Antarctica in Guildhalls of the Deathless. The Second Irem is a sample-setting-based plothook that isn't expanded upon elsewhere much the same as Destroyer Wolf stalking the Shadow of Basra or the city of Adelaide being the territory of reflection-dwelling bunyips who make the place periodically inimical to supernatural existence.
