            A few thoughts as Mummy is my favorite 1e game.

            Regarding storyteller v global info. I'll put a name to the problem--metagaming. Knowing info your character doesn't. My typical solution to this is to allow the narrators to be unreliable ones. As storytellers simply don't be afraid to twist facts around to suit the needs of your stories. Be upfront with your players and they'll enjoy the process of unravelling the mystery of which of the book's facts are facts and which are fictions. Of course this doesn't suit troupes that take a dogmatic approach. But these are sandbox games.

            Regarding the multicultural factor. Agree that they shouldn't be Arisen. Don't agree that other mummified immortals shouldn't show up someplace like a STG. In sandbox settings it's always good to provide folks with tools for apple cart upsetting. The key to mystery play after all is ensuring that the players don't know for sure what's around the corner.

            Other than those 2 pence, I'm looking forward to seeing where Mummy 2e goes.

                I've been giving some more thought to a Tokyo setting for mummy, and I figured I'd share an NPC idea I've been playing around with:

                Nefer-Mehenu, Mistress of the Deceived

                Under the grounds of Jōkan-ji temple lies a hidden chamber, small and unadorned, just large enough to house a single occupant and a few personal effects. Even if the Arisen of Tokyo were aware of its existence, none would suspect that this modest vault is home to a being of great and terrible power: Nefer-Mehenu, Guildmistress of the Akhem-Urtu. Known to her loyal cultists as Fudai Bosatsu, "the Deathless Bodhisattva," the Mistress of the Deceived is a bearer of the Philosopher's Temakh who espouses a creed that superficially resembles Esoteric Buddhism. She built her current cult among the prostitutes and entertainers of Edo's Yoshiwara district, who collected coin and secrets from those among the elite who came to slake their vices among the impoverished and reviled. Her followers gained access to the halls of power when wealthy patrons took them home as wives and concubines, and today her nameless sect stands at the center of a conspiracy with ties to many of Tokyo's most influential families.

                For her part, Nefer-Mehenu has only vague memories of a time before she came to dwell in Tokyo, but she believes that she originally came to this far-flung corner of the world seeking solitude and peace. So long as the stain of the Arisen continues to befoul her city, however, she knows that she will find no respite, for the fevered ravings of her Temakh claw ceaselessly at her mind, demanding ever grander and more terrible acts of vengeance against the slaves of its ancient betrayers.

                            I love it.

                            While not nearly so developed, I've had the idea for a Cult shared by two Deceived (one of the Dancer and one of the Keeper) who exist as a cruel parody of the Shaolin temple, espousing a creed of existence without thought that consists entirely of instinct and action. While it's a little more pulp than I prefer (evil kung fu master mummies are hard to handle seriously), they're what always comes to mind when I imagine any Asian setting for Mummy.
                            Actually, the first things that comes to my mind when I think of Mummy in east Asia are, well, Buddhist mummies, and particularly the practice of Sokushinbutsu in Japan. The practice of self-mummification may actually have originated in India or China, and it seems almost tailor-made to be a cover for mummies with (at least superficially) Buddhist cults in any of those regions. Actually, Buddhism is, in some ways, a great foundation for mummy cults.

                            Buddhism encompasses a lot of very diverse sects with widely divergent beliefs, some of them quite small and obscure, and mummy cults could potentially find places to thrive within that complex framework. In most cases, the core of a Buddhist school consists of monks, whose at least partially cloistered existence could lend itself to secret rituals and hidden treasures. The basic philosophy of Buddhism emphasizes the role of suffering in human existence, and prescribes a path of right behavior by which one can escape from it, which could be made to align with the Law of the Judges. While Buddhism isn't necessarily theistic in and of itself, many schools of Buddhism describe a sometimes bewildering array of bodhisattvas, deities, demons, and other supernatural beings that could be identified with the Judges and other figures in Iremite theology. Likewise, the "Pure Lands" and heavens (or hells) described in some schools of Buddhism could easily be equated with Duat or A'aru by the Arisen.

                                                            The Strix might not show up in every Vampire chronicle, but they point to both the internal struggle with the Beast that every one of the Kindred struggles with and provide one of the many reasons that the Danse Macabre is so full of mistrust, paranoia, and backstabbing. Not every Changeling is likely to cross paths with one of the Huntsmen, but all of them live in fear of being dragged back to Arcadia and shape their lives accordingly.

                                                            Keep in mind that the average Arisen either thinks the Lost Guild are long dead or tell themselves that their secrets have been lost to time; we as players didn't even know about them until three books into the line.

                                                            Apotheosis, meanwhile, ties into the core conflict of Memory that informs every single Mummy chronicle. Even if your meret never crosses paths with the Heretic or never finds a copy of Dreams of Avarice, they still have to contend with their nature as powerful slaves who have lost much of their identity; Apotheosis is a neat way of encompassing everything core to Mummy on a thematic level.
