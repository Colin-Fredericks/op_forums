    Crossover Brainstorm: Various Shan'iatu Origins.

    04-13-2014, 02:30 PM
    Okay so no need to worry, this thread is not trying to figure out the actual origin of the Shan'iatu. Its an attempt to come up with different methods to fill in the setting without waiting for other mummy books, and using the rest of the NWOD as a resource.


    Please feel free to discuss your own takes on these, suggested ideas or tweaks, new ideas, and preferred methods of use. If you don't like crossover stuff well you don't have to participate, this isn't expected to be a standard game.



    My first idea was to make the Shan'iatu a 3rd Tier Krew of Sineaters with the Judges as their Geist. There various sorcery is composed of their Keys and Dominions, and the high magic they use to do unique things are High Level Ceremony, with level 9 and Ten rituals used to make Gods and Immortals. They used their connections with the dead to catalog and find relics, and eventually develop Ceremonies to make their own, and teach mortals Merits so they can make them for them as well. Necromancy seemed to be the Shan'iatu's specialty but they also showed other wizardy tricks like elemental mastery, and the knowing of secrets. This would also put Sineaters as natural members of the Guilds, and perhaps ancient Dominions and Keys for things such as alchemy, and slavery.



    One idea I didn't take time to consider because of how obvious it is is to use them as Mages. Would you make them the latest iteration of a group trying to remake the Nameless city? Or would they be true Pre Atlantis? Or Hell what is wrong with making Irem Atlantis? The default Shan'iatu seem to be remanents of a more Ancient Secret so they may even be from a prehuman civilization? Would you have them contain Archmages? Would they be Exarch Related? They could even be the Exarchs themselves, I wouldn't mind a story exploring that idea. 42 mages is a large number, a whole consilium so even if they were all Masters that would be insanely impressive, and if they contained at least one Archmage per guild that would also be impressive. But perhaps the Gods of Irem are Archmages and the Shan'iatu being the Earthly mages preparing Omens and seeking Ascension. What twists would you take on this? Their magics could explain all the feats, and Imperial Magic could make for the Rite of Return and the conquering of the Duat. Maybe the Duat could even be a Supernal realm?



    Ancient Changelings would be a very interesting take, maybe the Judges where their Keepers, or the Fates the Wyrd has in store for Each Shan'iatu to become? Relics after all most closely resemble Tokens, so maybe their is some connection there. Perhaps the Nameless Gods are beings they encountered that aren't Fae, much like the Seasons and other patrons of Courts. Perhaps the Duat is actually in Arcadia? Perhaps solely accessible through Underground Hedge Paths in the Desert? Perhaps they have a connection to the Lost Pantheon, with members of the Ancient serving as the Gods of Irem? A lot of Irem could be developed just by a group of High Wyrd Beings with Social and Mental traits above 5, not to mention Contract use, pacts and Goblin MArket/Contracts providing pretty much all the magic they need.



    What if the Shan'iatu are the Maggot Host, Shartha that Father Wolf Chased into the Underworld. Perhaps they chose not become one and chose other goals for themselves, or perhaps they could not even become One, and thus had to decide another goal to fill their final drive. Perhaps they are an origin of Vampires animating the dead with Larvae, that would evolve into Revanents and Vampires.


    What if the Shan'iatu were simply Mortals with Supernatural Merits? Mortals can make Relics, and various supernatural merits can do a wide range of supernatural effects, particularly if they are long lived and have grown a vast amount of secrets. And Relics themselves could cover insanely potent feats, so perhaps the magic is just the cultivating of human projects towards supernatural ends, after all that explains most of the God Machines feats and it can do things like mass extinction and time travel. They could even have been a Hunter Conspiracy, After all we already have Relics as an Endowment, as well as Alchemy. Maybe they were something like a Conspiracy with 7 Conspiracies/Endowments working together. Hell I could see them having connections to the Aegis Kai, The Cheiron Group, and the Ascending Ones! Uter and Alchemy.


    What if they are Ghosts/Revanents from a fallen civilization? Using various Ghostly Numina and Influence, and cultivating Relics. Much like the Mortal option much can be explained with Relics, human nations turned to arcane ends, and the little cheat of Numina and Influence.


    And then there is the idea of the Priests being a conspiracy of various beings, and the Arisen and Irem could be a product of the Synthesis of multiple dark Secrets. After all everyone seems to fancy being a god, or to conquer. These are simple goals. Relics before Mummy where already the general default magic item of the NWOD. The Power of Werewolf Rites, Supernal Magic, The Wyrd, Ceremonies, and other Secrets combined could do some scary stuff if they worked towards the same goals.

    Thoughts and additions? What would be some fun angles to Run?

                              The Sesha-Hebsu entries in the Core book and in Guildhalls of the Deathless state much the same. The Sesha-Hebsu believe that magic is divided into two parts, the "Will" and the "Word". The Sesha-Hebsu were created to be keepers of the "Word", the formulaic aspect of magic, and some among them have memories that indicate the Lost Guild were keepers of the "Will". Their theory is that this gave the Lost Guild too much knowledge about the nature of magic, which was why they had to be purged. Used in concert, the Word and the Will allowed the Shan'iatu to create spells from whole cloth, or something along those lines.

                              Given the fact that the Lost Guild are Deathless creatures that somehow survived being purged before undergoing the Rite of Return, it would seem that the Sesha-Hebsu are at least partly on the right track there. The Deceived certainly learnt something that allowed them to endure an assault by the Shan'iatu, not to mention the 6,000 years since.
