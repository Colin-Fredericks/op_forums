    All things Shadey

    05-31-2024, 06:15 AM
    How do you use Shades in your game? What is their purpose?

    I can sort of see them as a Sekhem/blood linked alternative to ghosts.

    But they appear to lack the cohesive culture and perspectives of ghost (Underworld, Anchors, Essence from being remembered, all the stuff in Geist obviously).

    They seem interesting, but lacking in detail or depth - intentionally so according to the author, so we can create our own answers - so how do people use them?

    Are there any example stat blocks? Does your cult include Shades? Are they just Ghosts with more freedom (avoiding direct sunlight instead of being anchored, spilled blood instead of being remembered...) And Do they work with Geist/other games at all?

    ---

    From what I have gathered from conversations and reading through the forums, Shades seem to be more closely related to vampires and the Strix, than Geist.

    But from a metaphysics pov it feels like the main thing ghosts have it their memories and attachments (Memories as a mechanic for the Absent, and Anchors/passing on for Ghosts in general, and the Ocean of Fragments being erasure of their identity) - so linked to Ren in terms of Mummy.

    Shades by comparison seem to be more linked to Life force (blood spilling/Sekhem seeking) and/or Suffering? maybe.

    As literal Shadows they seem likely associated with Sheut, and there is some question in my head as to what that means.. like if they gained sustanence from inflicting suffering (rather than just freshly spilled blood) but their influences being related to Vices seems and interesting angle to push on.

    Like using someone's vice to encourage the spilling of blood would be a cool concept.

    Do they join cults to get ready access to people willing to spill blood? Would they join a Krewe with appropriate doctrine and ceremonies?

      I would say that (for whatever reason) Shades are envisioned to play the role in Second Edition which Ghosts played in Mummy the Curse first edition. Any reference to ghosts in Duat from the first edition, for example, seem to now by default refer instead to Shades. If I had to guess I would say that they wanted to make a greater distinction between Geist and other game lines and so needed a discreet "ghost-like but not a ghost" entity that applied more exclusively to Mummies and their kind rather than ghosts at large. Beyond that you the inspiration for Shades appears to come from works like the Iliad and the Odyssey wherein blood sacrifice plays a pivotal role in summoning and controlling spirits of the dead. Considering them the pieces of the Egyptian soul associated with the Sheut is interesting and something the writers themselves have postulated without stating definitively. For my part of tend to think of them as a "Bronze Age Ghost"...by which I mean if I am going through ancient middle eastern mythologies of the underworld and come across a cool story or ability ascribed to the restless dead I assume that to be some kind of Numia that Shades possess that more modern ghosts lack for unknown reasons. You can also think of Shades as ghosts that are doomed to descend into Duat instead of the Underworld as depicted in Geist, if that helps.

                                      Originally posted by orathaic View Post

                                      I do not know why you think Duat is worse for shades than the Underworld is for ghosts, considering that shades can maintain their Essence by hanging out in the dark and only suffer Essence bleed from direct sunlight (in short supply in the sunless lands of Duat) and the Underworld actively absorbs ghosts who aren't able to cannibalize or memorialize their way out of starvation every day.

                                      I'm also not sure how "they could have a bunch of cultists do a bunch of bloodletting for them" in any way functions as a response to "On a long enough timescale they're going to get bored and depressed and stop fighting the pull of Duat." The larder of the oblivion-goddess is the last stop on the road of life in the grand scheme of things — Ammut can afford to be patient because at the granular level she's set up to win every time. Those bloodthirsty cultists are mortal, or at least impermanent enough that they will stumble eventually, especially when the Devourer's grandchildren by proxy serve gods that punish mass murder.

                                      It's a mystery what exactly shades are, but they aren't ghosts, so raising their Rank without "giving up a huge part of themselves" by becoming a geist is a moot point even without getting into the scarcity of mummies undergoing death cycles or Geist's own demonstration that greater occult power is fairly far from essential. Shades bear Influence over shadows and Vice and need fresh blood to Manifest if they aren't Invested ephemeral cultists (who get the ability to join their Arisen master in the Trials), and the most immediately explained source of Sekhem for them to raise their Rank involves a mummy rolling a dramatic failure to resurrect from a death cycle (they're not on the list of things that can cannibalize Sekhem from relics).

                                      It could be that there's a rite to summon specific shades out of the Outer Realms like one would summon akathartoi; it could also be that shades become fiends in some fashion, or can find their way back out of Duat through some obscure cosmological crack in the walls (Maybe they're one of the many origins of the Strix). Maybe the personlike shades of Duat's first layer are doomed to run and hide until they give up forever, maybe there's a demon-city where powerful shades have banded together in brutality sufficient to awe any fiends that would try to bring them harm, maybe braving the deeper Gates or taking deals from Ammut or Anpu or the Judges lets them leave for the sunlit lands with obligations and/or occult power.

                                      All of this is speculative because Duat is sufficiently distant from the world the game is played in that its mechanics are almost entirely narrative; it suffices to be said that shades are conflict-averse enough to spend most of their time hiding (often in the Ashen Storm) and waiting to make opportunistic attacks on things with Sekhem they can steal, that they are limited by bright light (especially sunlight) and sustained by bloodshed, that they do not need Anchors to remain in Neter-Khertet, that they can only get stronger with stolen Sekhem, that they are drawn toward Duat with a pull strong enough that circumstances produce a steady stream of them at the akhet, whose guardians cut at them on their way in, and that the shades in Duat more closely resemble living people.

                                      Functionally all this means is that the shades you are likely to encounter in the normal course of a game may be more monstrous or unsettling to communicate with than humans and will probably be comparatively limited in the human memories they have a grasp of, because in the thematics of this game about personal and civilizational entropy, the personification of entropy doesn't need to offer a concrete threat to monsters with human memories who can't go outside during the day and have powers that make people their worse selves to make them eventually gravitate toward the hell-portal where demons and a bunch of theoretically friendlier (or at least more tractable) faces can be found until they get tired of that, too.

                                                  Originally posted by orathaic View Post
                                                  There is a list?
                                                  "Arisen, Amkhata, immortals, and sorcerers can cannibalize vessels, draining them of their power." First line of the "Cannibalizing Vessels" section on page 213 of the corebook, with the subsequent two paragraphs describing approximately what each of those can do with the magical contents of a vessel. Judge manifestations can drain Sekhem from vessels in a different way detailed in their own section. Shades and ghosts don't mention the ability to consume Sekhem in either section.

                                                  But that doesn't necessarily require they only gain Sekhem hmthis way...
                                                  If they could do so there would be a pretty obvious ludonarrative incentive to not have them gain Rank one-for-one with the amount of Sekhem they consume since cannibalizing a relic doesn't require a roll to do and all but the weakest relics contain more than one dot of Sekhem. You might make the argument that any given relic has Amkhata squatting around it waiting to get the Sekhem and deterring shades from doing so, but that would seem to be an extra couple of steps that would still be less reliable defense against contact with something that can hang around in a dark room indefinitely.

                                                  This is an interesting question, why then are they more monstrous? Is it a bias of which ones travel to Duat (the constant stream?) that those who happen to be more human follow their instincts and travel to Duat? Or is it that travelling to Duat causes some change in them?

                                                  Could that passage actually be talking about how physical they become rather than their mental state? Like ghosts becoming physically solid in the Underworld?
                                                  It could be in the same sense that 1e's description of the Underworld has ghosts become more lucid and 2e's mechanics for ghosts have an Underworld survivorship bias toward the Rank with mostly-complete human faculties and negligible communicative difficulties. The material on shades in general indicates most of the ones you find out in the world have to be convinced to speak and kind of treats their possession of human memories as exactly that — possession of them in the abstract — whereas in Duat they seem more like whole people, which could be something to do with ambient Sekhem or may have some relation to the same phenomenon that the Strix use to diablerize each other and gain new Skill dots from hosts.

                                                  Whatever the case, their stat profile in the part of the game where stats matter gives them Integrity and the same reversed Virtue and Vice as ghosts rather than the more wholly alien priorities of spirits, so leaning towards a more feral/less prosocial human mode of conduct as a whole seems apropos for opportunistic Sekhem-thieves who feed on bloodshed and can develop Numina that mimic Shadow Affinities.
