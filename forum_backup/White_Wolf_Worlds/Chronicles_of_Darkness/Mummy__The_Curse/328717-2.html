    Session 12

    The Deceived watched from afar, and admired the chaos as the Arisen Temple crumbled and Rile came forth to attack the survivors. They removed themselves from the area before the authorities could arrive, and waited for Lies to tell them how it panned out. They learned that Rekhenaten had provided a cover story of a demolition gone awry, and that there was a new Arisen active in the city named Mekshonat, the Seal of Babylon, Guildmaster of the Maa-Kep.

    For the moment, Rakhen-Roh and Imhotep were content to watch and wait and revel in the pleasure of their Temakh. They received word from their observatory that a manifestation of Seba was due in North Jakarta, at a Dreamland theme park. Remembering that there was at least one other party in the city actively seeking such wonders, they decided to make a move promptly to head off any possible competition. The Arisen could wait.

    Accompanied by their driver, Deriyat, they made the hours long drive from the Retreat to the Dreamland. It was an enormous, sprawling resort town. It seemed in constant competition with itself, new buildings and attractions under construction to lure customers away from the other parts of the reset, even as the oldest parts lapsed into disrepair and abandonment. When they arrived at a part of the park that seemed to be a cheap knockoff of Disneyland, full of ancient Greek and Egyptian architecture, Imhotep had a brief vision of the sun setting behind a set of gondolas followed by a building with the facade of the Egyptian Sphinx. They knew they had come to the right place. At sunset, the Seba would manifest.

    Deriyat bought them tickets. As they were entering the park, a female ticket seller leaned across and spoke to Imhotep. "You are not welcome here." Then she blinked, looked confused, and in asked if she could help him. The Deceived entered the park with their hackles up, scanning the crowd for enemies. Something knew they were here. Filled with paranoia, Imhotep almost assaulted a man he thought was looking at him, but Rakhen-Roh managed to get his fellow Deceived to back down.

    They decided to split up and search the park. There was certainly power here, the architecture of many buildings reminded them of the structures they had seen built by Tef-Aabhi masons in lives long past. Imhotep and Deriyat wandered the streets, Imhotep realising that the point of it all was to summon Seba to this place. These structures did not exalt Duat, they sought to capture the light of the stars.

    Meanwhile, Rakhen-Roh found a hidden door among a wall full of hieroglyphs. It opened to reveal a well-lit steel corridor that stretched into the distance. He stepped inside, and the door shut quietly behind him. He turned around when it clicked shut, and saw the door was gone. Instead, the tunnel stretched on in that direction as well. He walked deeper into the building, trying to work out what was going on. Every couple of yards was a strange symbol, a stylised eye not unlike the symbol of the Eye of Horus, save that it seemed to have a line drawn through the pupil. He could hear a loud buzzing sound, like that of many flies, and could smell something foul. Despite that, the first he knew of the trap that waited for him was when his foot passed through the solid floor and he toppled into the pit below.

    Spikes slammed into his body. After a moment of shock, he pulled himself upright. He checked quickly to make sure his snake was alright and hauled himself off the spikes. It was a deep pit, crude metal pillars sunk into concrete at the bottom. There was something down there with him, the desiccated remains of an animal or small human, it was hard to tell based on what was left of it. Flies were crawling all over it, and him.

    Disgusted, he pulled himself from the pit. When he reached the top, he realised the tunnel was different. He could clearly see the exit back the way he had come, and when he looked in the other direction he could see a T-intersection separated by two more pits. As for the tunnel itself, it was no longer well lit or clean. The walls were stained with some indescribable substance, and looked as if they hadn't been cleaned in years. The moment he pulled the rest of himself up and out of the pit, however, that vision was gone. The never-ending steel corridor returned.

    Rakhen-Roh decided to check whether this same event occurred when he was looking through his snake's eyes, and found that his Beast Companion was entirely unaffected. So long as he was borrowing its sight, he could clearly see the pits, the entrance, and the tunnel in the distance. He returned to the entrance, opened a door that didn't appear to his eyes, and called Imhotep.

    The two Deceived, and Deriyat, then entered the tunnel together. Following Rakhen-Roh's snake, they skirted the pits. At the end of the corridor was a small speaker, only visible in the snake's eyes, which crackled as they approached. A distorted voice spoke. "I don't know you. Name yourselves." The Deceived responded with questions of their own, but the speaker only emitted a static that could have been laughter.

    After that, they reached a stairwell that descended into a basement level. There, the illusion seemed to have ceased. There were several crates there, emitting an absolutely foul smell, as well as a rack of clothes containing uniforms for many of the hotels and theme parks in the area. Rahen-Roh prised open the crates. One was filled with various spices and incense. The others, however, were packed almost to the brim with strange, fleshy, worm-like lengths of flesh. If they had ever been living creatures, they weren't now. Decomposing and fused together by congealed slime that leaked from their bodies, they were the source of the smell that permeated the room. Each one was about the size of a small snake, but they were pink in colour and covered in skin instead of scales. They were very hard to peel apart, the slime that covered them appeared to have acted like a kind of glue.

    Towards one end of the room was the same eye symbol they had been seeing elsewhere in the building. When Rakhen-Roh approached it and touched it, a part of the wall appeared to vanish to his eyes. Beyond was a circular chamber. He stepped inside and salt water splashed around his ankles. There was a smell of spices and incense, and something else he didn't catch. Looking through his snake's eyes, he could see hieroglyphs painted on the walls that weren't visible to his own eyes. They were in a bastardised version of Iremite, a call to something from the stars. Not a Seba. Something capable of responding, a being from the void beyond.

    Rakhen-Roh told Imhotep to destroy it all. As Imhotep spoke fire into being, the chamber behind them began to fill with smoke and an acrid smell unlike anything they had known before. Rakhen-Roh returned to that chamber to find out what was happening. The smoke was coming from the crates. With every hieroglyph that Imhotep destroyed with the Tongue of Fire, more of the strange fleshy worm-like things began to shrivel and char, burning as if they were subject to the flames themselves.

    Afterwards, the two Deceived conferred. Rakhen-Roh was certain that something had been summoned. Whether the worms were failed attempts or a by-product of the ritual, he wasn't sure. Neither of them knew what to make of it all, however, so they returned to the surface with Deriyat in tow.

    After finding their way back through the tunnel, they came out to find a nervous young man waiting for them. He seemed startled that they were still alive, and told them frantically to follow him. "He" had eyes everywhere, but the boy knew a place that was safe. "He" wouldn't see them there. They could talk.

    They followed the boy, who said his name was Imat, back to a nearby resort. He had temporary housing on the bottom floor near the back. It wasn't a guest room, just a small bed and closet. He asked what they were doing, how they had survived going inside. The Deceived avoided the questions and pumped him for information instead. On edge and unbalanced as he was, it didn't take much to get him talking.

    It had all started six months ago. He hadn't been working for long when he started receiving notes telling him to do odd jobs around the park. There were always generous tips accompanying these notes, so he did them easily enough. They weren't anything too strange, just moving boxes around, painting new signs, running errands of that sort. But then the jobs started getting stranger. He noticed that some of the places he went didn't seem entirely... real. The textures he could feel and the sounds and smells didn't seem to match what his eyes were telling him in those places. At around the same time, the boxes he was moving began to smell foul. The messages he was receiving changed as well, sometimes becoming paintings on the wall, messages over the intercom that only he could understand, signs only he could see. Eventually he couldn't take it any more. He tried to stop. That was when the accidents started. Every time he refused a job, there was a workplace accident. Someone near him or someone he knew would be injured. A few people even died. He got the message quickly. He couldn't refuse the watcher.

    The Deceived dug up some more details. Originally, he made deliveries to the building they had just explored. More recently, the boxes, the ones that smelt like something had died inside, were to be taken from that building. His instructions were always to dispose of these boxes. At first the watcher had him dump the boxes in the ocean, but then a storm hit a few weeks back and a lot of strange worm-like things washed ashore. Ever since he'd been disposing of them at the tip or in the swamp, whichever the watcher thought most important.

    He also thought he wasn't the only errand boy in the park. Sometimes he had to make deliveries to the nearby SeaWorld, or the Atlantis Water Park, or the Gondola Rides. There were signs that he wasn't alone in this work, like piles of parcels of which his were only one. SeaWorld in particular seemed to have a huge area cordoned off from the public, an area that felt distinctly like one of those places where appearance didn't match reality. When asked which place seemed to garner the most traffic, however, he described for them a building not far from where they were, one with a facade painted to resemble the Sphinx.

    The Deceived told him that they would fix his "watcher" problem for him, probably that night, but if he heard anything new he was to contact them. They gave him Rakhen-Roh's mobile number and left, heading straight for the Sphinx. It turned out to be an amusement ride, a house-of-horrors-esque parade of fake mummies and traps designed for some cheap scares. They checked the supply closet Imat had said was where he left most of his items, and found three large, empty crates.

    Rakhen-Roh had a Kepher vision and realised that the Seba was about to manifest. Imhotep, however, felt his mind cast further afield. He had a momentary glimpse of an artists' commune, of an old wooden swan, paint peeling, floating on a green lake. By the time his mind returned, Rakhen-Roh had located a door disguised as a sarcophagus which led them behind the scenes. Imhotep put the vision from his mind, for the moment, but made sure to take a note of it as a sign from Fate.

    They passed the rattling, hissing machinery, hiding among the wildly swinging shadows. They tried to hide when they spotted figures approaching, but Rakhen-Roh was caught out in the open. He saw three naked men coming towards him, the eye symbol he had seen in the last building carved into the flesh of their chests. They howled when they saw him, revealing teeth filed to points, and charged.

    Imhotep and Deriyat sprung their ambush then. Roaring flame, the Forever Scornful scorched the flesh of the first attacker. Deriyat ducked past that man to drive a knife into the gut of the one behind. He doubled over, then looked Deriyat in the eye and smiled. With a sudden movement the Deceived couldn't quite follow, he grasped the cultist by his shoulder and tore the arm straight out of its socket.

    Then the attackers were on top of the Deceived in a frenzy. One clawed Rakhen-Roh's left eye straight out of its socket. Imhotep burned the first to death, but the second barrelled into him and forced him into the gears grinding away in the walls. The machinery shredded Imhotep's flesh, forcing him to Seal. Rakhen-Roh rewrote his foe's flesh, causing him to bleed from the eyes and mouth, but it wasn't enough to stop the frenzied fighter from tearing massive wounds in his body. In the end, the attackers all died in flame. Imhotep burned his foe to ash. Rakhen-Roh was almost overwhelmed, before fire roared out once again and the third enemy fell writing to the ground.

    Plucking an eye from each foe as recompense, Rakhen-Roh arranged their bodies into the shape of the same eye symbol that seemed to be the mark of their enemy. Imhotep knelt beside Deriyat, realised there was little he could do with his lacklustre medical skills to stop the bleeding, and summoned fire to cauterise the wound. Deriyat fainted from the shock, but it did stop the flow of blood.

    The Seba chose that moment to manifest, and Imhotep felt Siranuthis drive him to claim it. He reached out to it with his voice, binding it in a chorus of words that sealed it to his soul. The Warding Gaze, it was called, and he knew it would protect him from harm.

    They changed their appearance to disguise their injuries, covered Deriyat in Imhotep's coat, and left the park quickly then. Rakhen-Roh was concerned with avoiding attention, and didn't realise at first what Imhotep was staring at as they left. But to the Singer, it was obvious. It was hidden in plain sight, on logos and among decorative hieroglyphs, as graffiti and concealed in the background of posters for new attractions. Everywhere in the Dreamland, the eye was watching them.

    Needing time to recover, the Deceived returned to their retreat. Imhotep and his Temakh communed, seeking to learn the Utterance Whispers in the Earth. Rakhen-Roh knew that the first of his ghostly servants were ready to be summoned. They allowed three days to pass in quiet contemplation, healing their wounds and preparing for the next fight.

    The favour of their Temakhs was still strong, and they finished the session on Sekhem 7.

        And the latest Seba they encountered:

        The Warding Gaze (2 dot)

        Power: Born of long-dead beliefs surrounding the Eye of Horus, this Seba retains no apparent connection to the art form that birthed it. It grants its bearer protection from harm, and guards them against those who would wish them ill. Any who intend to harm the bearer feel the gaze of an old, old power upon them and, for a moment, see visions of a single, glaring eye. Thoughts of aggression pale when confronted with the gaze of the ancient. Even if they can bring themselves to strike, the power of this Seba may shield the bearer.

        Anyone wishing to attack the bearer feels uneasy. This, in itself, has no mechanical effect. Anyone determined to inflict harm upon the marked one can still do so, but muggers will pass them over for "softer" marks, the intoxicated will avoid them in a barroom brawl, and any other ill-wisher who does not have a grudge against the marked one specifically will go elsewhere.

        Those who do strike at the bearer must make a Resolve + Composure roll. On a failure, they suffer -2 to rolls to attack, harm, or intimidate the bearer.

        Curse: Just as those who would harm the bearer feel the gaze of this Seba's guardian, the bearer himself must endure being constantly subjected to that very being's judgement. Anytime the bearer commits a Morality 5 sin or lower, they roll a chance die. On a success, they take a point of a bashing damage. On a failure, they take a point of lethal damage. On a dramatic failure, they take a point of Aggravated damage.

        For some reason, this judgement does not occur when the sin is committed in pursuit of the bearer's Immortal Virtue (if they have one). It is as if the watcher accepts the virtues of the ancients as readily as the Temakh themselves.

                    Session 13

                    Imhotep and Rakhen-Roh each spent time in study, Imhotep completing his study of Whispers in the Earth and Rakhen-Roh gaining an understanding of Wrathful Desert Power. Thus armed, they decided to investigate Imhotep's strange Fate-given Kepher vision of an artist commune. They had set their cult to tracking the place down, and eventually located it in the city's south. An otherwise normal middle class suburb filled with rows of identical homes was broken in the middle by a large block that had been entirely claimed by the commune. It was surrounded by a chain link fence, but the main gates were open and a sign proudly read "Artist's Village" above the main entrance. It was hard to see the village itself from the road, as large trees encircled the property, many of them sporting tree houses in varying stages of neglect. This time, the Deceived chose to leave their new driver, the dancer Ardit, behind with the car while they went to investigate. They changed their appearance to that of locals and entered.

                    The village turned out to consist of about a dozen houses, from large and luxurious homes to barely upright shacks. Scattered around the slipshod pathways and outside the doors of the various dwellings were examples of their inhabitants' art, ranging from landscape portraits and sculptures to musical instruments and CDs. There weren't many people around, most of them apparently gardeners tending to the beds of different plants that filled the spaces between the houses. Rakhen-Roh was examining a particular set of landscape portraits, which had the advantage over many of the other works by conforming to his Temakh's preference for art that speaks the truth, when the entrance to the hut opened and a woman asked if she could help him. Her name was Kahiyang, and although she appeared to be in her early or mid thirties her hair was streaked with quite a lot of grey. The two went inside to speak, and to allow Rakhen-Roh to examine some of her in-progress pieces. Kahiyang revealed that she was one of the wardens of the little enclave, tasked with making sure groceries were bought and the electricity bills were paid. Rakhen-Roh discovered a man named Sahai, a sculptor of some repute, owned the commune.

                    As he went to leave, Rakhen-Roh heard a strange scratching coming from the walls. It sounded not dissimilar to the noise of many insect legs, scurrying around just out of sight. He was so distracted that he nearly bowled over a pretty young woman who was approaching the house. Imhotep wandered over in time to hear her introduce herself as Selvi. She was excited to see new people, and offered to show them around. She just wanted to speak to Kahiyang and would be right out.

                    The two Deceived listened in on the conversation, but once it was clear that the two women were just checking up on one another Rakhen-Roh asked Imhotep if he had noticed anything insectile around the place. Imhotep hadn't, and the two debated what the cause might be. To be sure, Kahiyang's home wasn't the most orderly, but it didn't look like the type to be literally crawling with bugs. Selvi returned before either of them could come to any conclusion.

                    Their tour of the grounds led them quickly to the centre of the village. There, right in the middle, was a large pond filled with green water, plastic and polystyrene, and a rotting wooden swan with paint peeling from it as it drifted across the surface. At the centre was an ugly, twisted building that loomed like a hunchback over the surroundings, a misshapen staircase leading around the exterior. Three bridges crossed the pond at evenly spaced points, each one carved with representations of gremlins. Selvi cheerfully explained that the whole lot of it had been designed by Sahai when he thought the musicians in the enclave could use a place to record their work.

                    They entered through a door labelled "Studio Mixing" to find that the building consisted of only a single room, with a small cabin built into one wall containing the mixing equipment. The walls were slashed back and forth with a variety of brightly coloured murals, most of them either works of abstract art or an overabundance of enthusiasm. Selvi proudly claimed responsibility for many of them. The Deceived examined the artwork without much interest, and didn't notice at first when a change came over their guide. A minute or two had passed by the time they picked up on how she had gone suddenly quiet. They turned just in time to see her walking for the door, leaving them behind without so much as a goodbye.

                    The Deceived tailed her. She left the building without looking at them and continued on towards a copse of trees at one end of the commune. There was a small swampy grove within, decorated with hundreds of small statues. Most of them were of humans depicting a range of angry and furious expressions, mixed in with a small number of clearly distressed animals. A rusting aviary, empty save for a few pots of rotting ferns, stood in the midst of all this. Selvi walked into the aviary where an older man, perhaps fifty years old with iron grey hair, was waiting. The two began to undress, apparently oblivious to the two Deceived.

                    Imhotep coughed loudly to get their attention, but neither noticed. Both were topless by the time he grew impatient and kicked over a nearby statue. Both figures turned towards him. The man demanded to know what they were doing, but the sound that came from Selvi's throat distracted them before they could respond. It was like a loud clicking, quite distinct from any human sound.

                    The man said he was Sahai, and coldly informed the pair that they had better leave. They had woken the swarm, and it would be the best thing for them if they left before they made matters worse. If "they" thought their queen was under threat... Sahai left the threat hanging.

                    Sure enough, the Deceived heard footsteps behind them. Half a dozen gardeners were converging on them, pruning sheers and other tools held threateningly. Imhotep's immediate response was to draw a gun and point it at the half-naked Selvi. If anyone moved, the queen would die.

                    The threat seemed to work. The gardeners all froze in place. Sahai suddenly appeared a lot more nervous. He answered their questions at gun point, telling them that the queen did not yet exist. The Dream-Scarabs were experimenting. They thought Selvi would be a good mother, a vessel to bear their queen to term. They had been attempted to conceive a queen for some months now.

                    The term "Dream-Scarabs" caught in Rakhen-Roh's mind. He remembered a brief flash of the sandstone temples from his mortal years, of things that were meant to lurk within the art of Tutkepertanu, ensuring that the Shan'iatu's art was treated with proper reverence. It was little more than a myth or rumour, but he began to feel a painful pull on his consciousness. His Temakh wanted him to speak, to pronounce an Utterance, the first Tier of Words of Dead Hunger. Rather than fight, Rakhen-Roh allowed the words to flow from him.

                    Neter-Khetet was revealed to his sight. He could see the scarabs now. They lurked within the statues all around them, every one of their multi-faceted eyes fixed on him. The oversized beetles were larger than his fist. There was one in every statue. He glanced back towards the village, and saw the studio building was crawling with hundreds of the creatures.

                    At that point, they heard the sound of running feet and saw Kahiyang approaching. She half-screamed, half-yelled when she saw the gun in Imhotep's hand. She started begging them not to hurt Selvi, saying she was like a daughter to her.

                    Rakhen-Roh announced then that he was the bearer of Tutkepertanu. The pronouncement seemed to shock Selvi, who up until this point had displayed no emotion at being held at gun point. Oblivious to Imhotep's finger creeping towards the trigger, she walked closer and spoke. Beneath her voice was the sound of another that communicated in buzzes, clicks, and hisses, the sounds somehow emerging from her mouth together.

                    "The Image of Primordial Woe was destroyed long ago."

                    Rakhen-Roh ordered Imhotep to lower his gun, and suggested politely that the gardeners remove Kahiyang. There was a moment pause, and then two of the men seized her and dragged her away. Rakhen-Roh then allowed his disguise to drop.

                    "Not destroyed. He lives in me."

                    Selvi seemed to relax. She explained to Rakhen-Roh that the Dream-Scarabs had gained from their hosts an awareness of themselves, a desire to see their race expand. They did not want to rely on their nest any more. They wanted a queen, to be free to procreate without dependency. She asked if Tutkepertanu would allow them to do so. Rakhen-Roh agreed, despite Imhotep's reservations. It seemed the scarabs themselves weren't entirely sure what they would be like if they had a queen, but Selvi suggested that a new generation would be free from the commandments writ upon their shells.

                    She then turned to Sahai. He carried the nest. He'd been carrying it for decades, creating scarabs to help him set up the commune. He tried to run, but Imhotep was faster. Held at gunpoint, he knelt, and Imhotep shot him in the head. Siranuthis seemed pleased by this, granting a descent reset.

                    As Sahai died, the Deceived saw a Seba emerge from his blood. Rakhen-Roh stepped up to claim it, gaining the Nest of the Dream-Scarabs. Tutkepertanu was pleased with his servant's actions, rewarding him with a descent reset as well.

                    They then struck an alliance with Selvi, or at least the scarab riding her. They would allow the scarabs to continue their quest to make a queen, but they wanted the commune to act as a base for their cult in the city. They would have some of their cultists take possession of the village, and induct many of the locals. The Dream-Scarabs were to avoid interfering with their business in return. While the creatures were not sentient enough to recall these instructions in their natural state, they should immediately recall the agreement upon gaining a body and be able to ensure a minimal amount of disruption. Selvi agreed, and had the gardeners dispose of Sahai's body. They took a picture of him first, however, so that Dalila would be able to masquerade as him for official purposes.

                    Finally, Imhotep used the Choir of the Dark Stars to convince Kahiyang that the events she had witnessed had been the arrest of Sahai for a variety of offences, and that he was actually a police officer. He assured her that Selvi would not be charged, as her involvement had not been willing. Hoping that meant the loose end had been tied up, the Deceived returned to their retreat to begin organising the details of the takeover.

                        And the new Seba that was the cause of all this session's fun:

                        Nest of the Dream-Scarabs (3 dot Seba)

                        They lurk just beneath the surface, behind paintings and pictures and photos. When you see their homes, they see you. If you're feeling weak, or rundown, or out of sorts, that is when they strike. You won't remember what happens next, nor anything that happens while under their grasp. When next you sleep, however, you will remember. You'll remember the scrabbling, insectile legs inside your skull. You'll remember the jaws clicking, chewing through your thoughts. You will remember every night, for the rest of your life.

                        Power: Using this Seba requires the expenditure of a point of Willpower whenever the bearer creates a visual piece of art. Doing so makes the art piece home to a Dream-Scarab. These are simple, near-mindless creatures once spawned to ensure that the sacred art of the Image of Primordial Woe was treated with due deference. At the moment of their birth, the bearer of this Seba may nominate one Passion for the scarab. See below for the Dream-Scarab's details.

                        Curse: The Dream-Scarabs have another agenda, one that their creator has no control over. They will "defend" their art by mindless striking at any who view it, but that is the only service they will perform. A bearer of the Painter's Temakh once speculated on what he believed their true purpose was. When in Neter-Khetet, the scarabs lack the intelligence to conceive of anything beyond keeping their home safe. Once they are inside a mortal's mind, however, they suddenly have the intellect to conceive of both themselves, and the future. It gives them the desire to see their race expand, to no longer be dependent upon this Seba for survival. They want to make a queen... but failing that, getting their hands on this very Seba would do.


                        Note:

                        Dream-Scarab

                        Attributes: Power 2, Finesse 1, Resistance 1
                        Size: 1
                        Corpus: 2
                        Numen: Demi-Ghost (see Chthonic Being), Impassioned Soul, Possess, Wall-Crawler (again, Chthonic Being)
                        Passions: Protect their home (the art piece they were created for), Create a Queen (if a Deceived chooses to ignore this Seba's curse, the Scarabs they make lack this Passion), [One Passion selected by the bearer]

                        Appearance: To those who can see into Twilight, the Dream-Scarabs appear as oversized beetles comparable to those created by Rite of the Sacred Scarab. Imhotep used the Choir of the Dark Stars to convince her that the Deceived were with the police, and discovered that the woman was named Jeset. She was Tikcha's daughter, but she didn't know where her mother might be. She suggested they try the house at the top of the nearby hill, and offered to take them there. She walked them up after collecting some camera equipment from her room, explaining on the way that she was an amateur film-maker and intended to go and meet with some friends after she showed them where to go. Rakhen-Roh was very interested in this revelation, and said, somewhat enigmatically, that he hoped everything she filmed was true.

                                                    In the end, Rakhen-Roh needed no help determining which house Jeset was leading them. It was a large home with a three-storey tower on its southern face. Its garden was filled with stone statues, all of them depicting chimera and hybrid monsters from a variety of different mythologies. Walking among the rows of orchids were two Amkhata, only visible thanks to Rakhen-Roh's Deathsight, one possessing a lion head and a snake body, the other with a snake head and a jackal's body. He warned Imhotep of what lay ahead, and set his Dream-Scarab on Jeset before she could ask what was happening.

                                                    The Amkhata had clearly noticed the Deceived, but they didn't attack. They watched from among the flowers instead, clearly waiting for something.

                                                    In the end, the Deceived blinked first. Rakhen-Roh walked into the garden, a nervous Imhotep behind him, and a possessed Jeset in tow. The Amkhata didn't attack. Instead, they circled around the Deceived and followed behind them.

                                                    They knocked on the front door. It was answered by a woman in her mid-thirties, who asked them suspiciously who they were and what they were doing. The Deceived used the same lie they had on Jeset, claiming to be from the police. When asked, the woman said her name was Dianna, but told them that Hama Nefertat was too ill to be disturbed. She suggested they contact his lawyer if they needed to speak with him.

                                                    Hama, it quickly became clear, was some kind of religious figure for Dianna. The Deceived noticed though that, as they spoke to one another in Iremite to decide upon the best course of action, that Dianna's eyes were growing wider and wider. Finally, in a breathless voice, she asked how it was that they spoke the language of magic.

                                                    Imhotep leapt on the opening. He claimed to be of the old magic, and that he had many gifts he could bestow upon the ignorant. He asked to be taken to Hama, and convinced Dianna to let them inside. They were taken through the building, where Imhotep observed there were many fine works of art, and led up to the top of the tower. There, in a hot, dry room, they had their first sight of Hama Nefertat. He was an old Indonesian man, white hair and a long white beard. He was clearly sick, and he struggled to sit up as they entered the room. His thin arms and hands were folded atop the bed sheets, letting them see just how little flesh was left on his bones. Imhotep took stock of the frail mortal, but Rakhen-Roh was distracted by another sight. In Neter-Khetet, a large creature was perched at the top of his bed. It had the body of a falcon and the head of a bull, and worms could be seen tunnelling in and out of its flesh and eyes.

                                                    Dianna introduced the Deceived, telling Hama that they knew the language of magic. Interest immediately sparked in his eyes, and he asked them about themselves in a hoarse whisper. Imhotep replied in Iremite, declaring that he was Imhotep the Forever Scornful, a child of lost Irem, and asked Hama about himself. The old man took a moment, and when he replied it was in Indonesian. He seemed to have understood most of what Imhotep had said, but told him that his grasp of the language was not complete and he wished to avoid misunderstandings.

                                                    Hama revealed that he considered himself a mystic, leading a group he called the Chimera's Children. He had travelled a lot in his early days, and in a dig in Iran he had discovered writings about the chimera. By discovering holy combinations of the sacred animals, he hoped to birth a creature capable of ridding him of the cancer eating away at his bones. The Amkhat by his bedside was an experiment with the use of a wormheart as the False Ka, and he used its excretions to keep himself alive.

                                                    Rakhen-Roh used his Deathsight to determine that Hama's Vice was Envy, and decided he could use that to his advantage. He used Voice of Temptation to persuade Hama that he deserved what others already had, that if he joined the Deceived and helped as their servant, he could have life everlasting. The Deceived explained that their most devoted servants could transcend death, returning to their bodies after the moment of expiration as a holy revenant. Whether it was the magic in Rakhen-Roh's tongue or the power that he promised, Hama was quickly convinced. He accepted.

                                                    There was an obstacle, however. The Chimera's Children were a fractious cult. They numbered about two dozen in total, but they were fragmented into three main factions. Hama had affectionately named them for the holy components of the classic chimera. A man named Endeng led a group Hama called the Snake's Head, a woman named Fitri led the Lion's Head, and Tikcha Anasama led the Bull's Head (the closest holy animal to the goat's head of the Greek Chimera). Hama would summon them all to dinner that night so that the Deceived could convince each of them. Without the support of the faction leaders, they wouldn't be able to control the Chimera's Children. They worked for Hama because he kept the sacred knowledge to himself, but he couldn't guarantee that they would accept new masters.

                                                    That night, Imhotep ensured that he was given a seat at the opposite head of the table to Hama. Rakhen-Roh stood behind his chair, arms crossed, in an eerie mirror to the way Dianna fussed over Hama before taking a stand behind him. Slowly, members of the Chimera's Children began to file into the room. Most were dressed as if for a formal occasion. Endeng, a tall man wearing thin-rimmed glasses, was the first faction leader to enter the room. He looked at the Deceived and their position in the hall and seemed to assess the situation. He gave a courteous nod to Imhotep and then proceeded to sit at Hama's left hand, quickly engaging the old man in quiet conversation. After that came Fitri, a homely woman of maybe thirty years, dressed in a simple white dress. She sat closer to the middle of the table, slipping in and taking her seat as if she were no different than any other member of the Children. Finally, Tikcha made her appearance. Imhotep recognised her from her picture. She wore a sleeveless top and suit pants, showing the muscles in her arms quite clearly. She was followed by a group of young men and women, each of them as physically fit as she appeared to be. She ignored the others when taking her seat.

                                                    Hama waited until the first course of the meal had mostly been devoured before addressing his people. He described the Deceived as powerful secrets with many gifts to offer, and told the Children that he thought it would be a good idea for them all to be baptised into Imhotep's service. There was silence in the hall, and then Endeng rose to his feet. He hoisted a glass of wine up high and called for a toast, saying that he hoped that there would be much to learn.

                                                    The reaction to that toast was more explicit. Tikcha got to her feet, sneering. "What authority do these foreigners have to command us?" She went to walk from the room, and Imhotep roared fire at the door to get her attention. Summoning his voice, he loomed over her and spoke of his authority. He spoke of his six thousand year war and the magic he commanded that made even the immortal dead tremble in fear. He told her of civilisations he had seen destroyed, of the magic he had learned at the feet of inhuman masters, and of the power he commanded that was greater than any other being in this world. That was his authority, and she would do well to bow to it like the fleeting mortal that she was. He used Blessed Is The God-King to add Fate's own blessing to his words, and Tikcha returned to her chair.

                                                    There was little opposition after that. The Chimera's Children agreed to serve the Deceived, although exactly how firm their conviction was remained up to debate.

                                                        Session 18

                                                        With nearly every supernatural threat in the city's south either destroyed or subsumed into their cult, the Deceived decided to reintroduce a number of their mutant cultists into the city's sewers. Imhotep decided to gather together a group of beggars and other human refuse, easily missed by the city's authorities, to give to the mutants in preparation for their hunt. Rakhen-Roh led the kidnapping, lulling humans from the streets with promises of food and shelter and using his Voice of Temptation to tempt the more recalcitrant and cautious individuals into the trap. Imhotep had the entrances to the mutants' tunnels sealed, and then gathered the mutants in the largest cavern. He stood at its head and began his war speech. They would not be hunted any longer. They were the strongest fighters in the city. With the mutants at the head of their army, they would topple the city's Nome.

                                                        At the height of the speech he ordered the captured humans let loose, giving them to the mutants to hunt. Rakhen-Roh had whispered to each man about a path to safety each man could reach, if he were willing to leave his fellows behind to be eaten. When the prisoners were released they scrambled and fought their way past one another rather than worked together, and the mutants descended upon them all.

                                                        Rakhen-Roh was able to endure the toll this took on his sense of self, but Imhotep was finding it harder and harder to find the line between where his consciousness ended and his Temakh's began. He dropped to Memory 1.

                                                        The two Deceived then oversaw the operation shipping the mutants back to the city's sewers, although they were many miles from their original home. During the course of this operation they received a call from Umar at their main headquarters, telling them that a man had walked into the building and asked to speak to the Deceived. Imhotep asked for a description and whether he might be Arisen. Umar described a middle-aged European man with a slim build, no one the Deceived recognised, and certainly not someone who sounded like a denizen of Ancient Irem. The Deceived told their cultists to watch the man closely, to kill him if he tried anything, and gathered a group of armed cultists from the retreat. Imhotep was able to muster four warriors on short order.

                                                        They made their way to the city in a convoy, Umar calling in to make frequent reports on the situation. The mysterious stranger hadn't said or done anything of interest since walking in, but the Deceived were on edge. They weren't used to being the ones on the defensive, in any sense of the word. When they arrived they entered the underground car park and stepped into two separate elevators. Rakhen-Roh and the cultist warriors went in one, Imhotep stepped into the other. The two elevators rose to the first floor, but Rakhen-Roh kept the doors to his from opening. Imhotep stepped out and beckoned imperiously to the stranger to follow him, and returned to his lift. The man rose from his seat in the lobby, surrounded by surreptitious cultist guards, and followed Imhotep. They rose up through the building until they reached the top floor, and Imhotep then led the way to the conference room without speaking. Rakhen-Roh's snake was being carried by Imhotep inside his shirt, allowing Rakhen-Roh to monitor the situation in case the guards were called for.

                                                        Imhotep took the seat at the head of the table and gestured for his guest to take the one at the other end. The Deceived presented a front of being relaxed and comfortable and deliberately intimidating, and it had a visible effect on the stranger. The man sat down looking more than a little nervous. Imhotep waited for him to speak, thinking he sat in silence. He only slowly realised that he was singing an ancient dirge as he sat there, his Temakh asserting a moment of control over his body. He quieted himself as smoothly as he could, and fixed his attention on the guest.

                                                        Finally, the stranger mustered his courage to speak. "I believe you have taken something that doesn't belong to you."

                                                        Imhotep laughed, the stranger flinched. The Deceived asked the visitor for his name. There was a pause, and then the man's flesh began to run like wax. His skin darkened quickly, and his form returned to one Imhotep recognised. It was Yarikh.

                                                        If Imhotep was impressed, he didn't show it. Yarikh kept speaking. He was amazed, he said, to find out that the Deceived existed. He'd heard of the myths and legends, but he hadn't thought he would ever meet someone of the Lost Guild. He hadn't really believed they still existed.

                                                        Imhotep cut off his nervous babbling by asking him why he had come. Yarikh said that he had been tracking his stolen canopic jar. It had taken a lot of effort to latch onto it with his Kepher. He had needed to put a lot of effort to pierce the fog of magic that surrounded it. But, eventually, the scarab's trail had led him here, to this place, and the little information he had gleaned from the omens had told him that it had been taken by masters of True Name magic.

                                                        Imhotep asked quietly if he had shared this discovery when any other Arisen. Yarikh said he hadn't. He had come hoping for a peaceful resolution, because he believed much could be learned at from one another.

                                                        At this, a broad grin spread across Imhotep's face. "Ah," he said. "You want us to help you achieve Apotheosis."

                                                        Yarikh was clearly disturbed by this. He wanted to know how Imhotep had known that, but Imhotep declined to tell. Finally, Yarikh sighed. "Yes," he said. "I would very much like your help. I wish to reclaim my True Name, and I can think of no one else with the knowledge to help me."

                                                        The snake slithered out from Imhotep's clothing and turned to face him. Looking into its eyes, Imhotep said, "I think you had better join us." Rakhen-Roh walked into the room a few seconds later. Imhotep was pleased by the fact that the Su-Menent was clearly off-balance.

                                                        The Deceived proposed a deal. They would help Yarikh on his path to Apotheosis, but they wanted his help in gaining their revenge. They asked what help he could provide.

                                                        He knew magic of his own, he said. Secrets known only to the Su-Menent. And he could supply them with more pressing knowledge as well, news on the activities of the city's Arisen. Many came to him for advice or knowledge about the supernatural, and he could pass this on to the Deceived if they wished.

                                                        The second part could certainly be useful. The Deceived asked him what magic he could share, and he unleashed Ancestry of Forgotten Stars Tier 2. Imhotep was unable to stop his shock from showing as Yarikh's body unfolded into a hideous demonic visage. Smiling to himself, Yarikh retook his usual form. He offered the secrets of the Su-Menent, particularly knowledge of Four Jar Magic. Yarikh himself used it extensively to prolong his own descent.

                                                        Rakhen-Roh was fascinated by this concept. His plots were more often foiled by the little time he had available to enact them than they were by direct opposition. He told Imhotep that destroying Yarikh might yet be unwise. Imhotep agreed, to Yarikh's obvious relief.

                                                        Imhotep then proceeded to browbeat Yarikh into accepting a list of terms. One of the Deceived's cultists, Ardit, would infiltrate his cult with his knowledge. She would monitor his activities to make sure he did not betray them. The canopic jar would be returned once he had proven himself to their satisfaction. And he would teach Rakhen-Roh Four Jar Magic, the power Yarikh called the Jar of Clay, immediately. Yarikh agreed, but asked only that he be allowed to begin his study of True Name magic in return. After some consideration, Imhotep offered to teach him Calling Down The Black Huriyah.

                                                        Yarikh left. He would meet with the Deceived, in disguise, at neutral locations while they studied one another's magic. After that he would contact them only through Ardit. The Deceived forbid him from coming to their headquarters uninvited again.

                                                        Once preparations to monitor the Arisen were complete, Rakhen-Roh and Imhotep turned their attention to the Golden Triangle. The business district was one of the last areas in the city's southern region where they had no influence. They reached out to local politicians, finding a slightly corrupt man who could help them force the Batavian Maritime Banking and Insurance Firm to sell their building in the city's centre. The preparations would take several months, but Rakhen-Roh, at least, was content to wait while he tested the limits of his magic.

                                                        Kotira and Aqhat were the two main obstacles within the area, although they believed a mummy named Sesu Ra also made his tomb in the area. With Kotira inactive, Sesu Ra nowhere to be seen, and Aqhat preoccupied with laying siege to the Shuankhsen, there was little supernatural opposition to their efforts. After nearly six months of effort, the Arisen cult relocated to new offices outside of the central precinct.

Imhotep dropped to Sekhem 4, although his descent was abnormally slow (note: I have never seen someone roll so many dice with so few successes, he made something like 5-6 descent checks and only "passed" one of them).
