    Open-Source Shan'iatu - Help Me Out!

    07-30-2015, 02:48 PM
    So I'm making some Shan'iatu. Help me make more!

    We know the Decieved...

    ( The Musician )
    ( The Dancer)
    ( The Poet )
    ( The Philosopher )
    ( The Painter )
    ( The Voice )
    ( The Keeper )

    ... but I'm trying to come up with stations for the OTHER guilds, like...

    THE SHELL
    (The Embalmer)... the Shan'Iatu who prepares the body for the ritual of death.
    (The Speaker)... the Shan'Iatu who speaks to the dead on behalf of the deceased.
    (The Jarbearer)... the Shan'Iatu who creates the sacred canopic jars.
    (The Funerist)... the Shan'Iatu who prepares the ceremony of death.
    (The Purifier)... the Shan'Iatu who bathes and cleans the body of the dead.
    (The Organ-Taker)... the Shan'Iatu who removes the organs from the corpse prior to the Embalmer's treatment.

    THE SCRIBES
    (The Accountant)... the Shan'Iatu who ensures the economy of Irem functions in divine symmetry.
    (The Census-Taker)... the Shan'Iatu who knows the name and family of every mortal servant of the Nameless.
    (The Minister)... the Shan'Iatu who visits far away lands and creates sacred agreements which ensure trade and slaves.
    (The Character-Maker)... the Shan'Iatu who designs and creates Hieroglyphic characters when needed.
    (The Historian)... the Shan'Iatu who must keep the history; how the Shan'Iatu came to be and the true workings of the world.
    (The Ink Master)... the Shan'Iatu who creates the sacred pigments which enable Iremite magic.
    (The Cartographer)... the Shan'Iatu who Names and Knows the world around Irem.

    THE AMULET
    (The Eye Smith)... the Shan'Iatu who is concerned with watching the borders of Irem for retribution from wronged enemies.
    (The Inquisitor)... the Shan'Iatu who deals with heresy of the heart, within the people of Irem.
    (The Warden)... the Shan'Iatu who oversees slaves not yet trained and broken; prisoners who do not yet work or fight.

    THE EFFIGY
    (The Chisel)... the Shan'Iatu who creates sacred insciptions and reliefs.
    (The Architect)... the Shan'Iatu who designed the sacred buildings of Irem.
    (The Earth-Mover)... the Shan'Iatu who can create or divert rivers, who tames the earth for the Nameless.
    (The Sculptor)... the Shan'Iatu who creates the living statuary of Irem.
    (The Gatherer)... the Shan'Iatu who carves and digs the riches of the earth out of hidden places for the empire.

    THE ALCHEMISTS
    (The Pharmacist)... the Shan'iatu who performs alchemy of the Body.
    (The Metallurgist)... the Shan'iatu who performs alchemy of the Earth.
    (The Dowser)... the Shan'Iatu who can feel the reaction of one substance to another, thus finding what is needed.


    If you guys would prefer greater detail in writeups to borrow these NPCs, let me know, but for now what I need help with is coming up with seven stations for EACH of these! I need more Amulets, more Effigies, more Alchemists...

    ... and if you have better ideas for Shell or Scribes, by all means!

                      I like your ideas for the scribes, but I think that at least one of the Sesha-Hebsu Shan'iatu should reflect the guild's role in presiding over the criminal courts of Irem. If I were to make a few changes, I think I'd keep Cartographer, the Character-Maker, the Historian, and the Minister more or less unchanged. The Accountant and the Census-Taker could probably be rolled into one station, since census-taking and tax-collecting tend to go hand-in-hand, and the Shan'iatu probably view their subjects as property anyway. As for the Ink Master, I might actually make him one of the Alchemists, since magical materials science is kind of their thing. That opens up two spots, which might be filled with something like these guys:

                      The Justiciar: The highest justice in Irem, the Justiciar is said to have a clearer and more complete knowledge of the will of the Judges than any other. It is whispered that even the other Shan'iatu seek her council when a dispute arises among their august number, and the scribes of the Sesha-Hebsu assiduously record every utterance that she deigns to share with them. In the rare event that the judges of the guild are unable to reach a consensus on a case brought before their courts, the final decision belongs to the Justiciar.

                      The Speechwriter: While it is left to the sixth guild to tend to Irem's puppet-pharaoh and to glorify him in pomp and ceremony, it is the Shan'iatu called the Speechwriter who shapes the decrees that are passed down in his name. While each of the Shan'iatu has a voice in deciding the laws and policies of the Nameless Empire, it is the Speechwriter's role to choose the words by which those decisions shall be known. Though he may lack the eloquence of the Poet or the illuminating rhetoric of the Philosopher, none can match the precision of his words, through which the will of Irem's rulers is understood by all of their subjects.


                      I also have a few ideas for the Maa-Kep:

                      The Carnifex: The Shan'iatu who meets out punishment to disobedient slaves and subjects. While lesser officials carry out most of the brutal punishments decreed by the scribes, the Carnifex makes an art of torture, inventing punishments so nightmarish that the mere rumor of them keeps most workers in line. He also presides over the public executions, and sees to it that the skills of capable convicts are preserved in amulets. Thus, the enemies of Irem become her servants, even as their souls are sped along toward their final judgement.

                      The Master: The head foreman on the Shan'iatu's most important projects. For many among Irem's massive underclass, this is the only one of the Shan'iatu whom they will ever see, for she spends much of her time overseeing work sites and receiving reports from the slave drivers of the Maa-Kep. His withering gaze is a far more effective motivator than any whip, but in truth it is not the slaves that he monitors, but the slavemasters. A servant may forget his place when he is raised above his fellows, and those who hunt the disloyal may themselves hide secret heresies. The Master watches the warchmen, and teaches humility to the proud.

                      The Silence: The Silence hears, but is unheard; sees, but is unseen; knows, but is unknown. The Silence is a rumor engraved on the minds of every man, woman, and child in Irem. None save the Shan'iatu can claim to have encountered the silence, but the master craftsmen of the Maa-Kep see his handiwork in strange and potent amulets that appear from time to time in the hands of guild novices, common workers, and even slaves. These masterpieces of the engraver's art reshape the minds and souls of their bearers in strange and often dramatic ways, and it is through them that the Silence pursues its unknowable agenda.

                          Some ideas for the Scribes-

                          Back in Irem the Lost Guild gave a Name to all of creation. That task done, they left the Closed Books with the unforgiving task of crafting the language. Irem's style of writing was as much a series of pictures as it was a language. It was the task of Scribes to piece the sacred names into the Word, crafting a language wholesale with the benefit of the Minoans innovation of an alphabet. And yet they did so, succeeding in mapping the first language and the rudiments of mathematics as well. Theirs is the power of the story, the Will given articulation and form, depth and substance and nuance.

                          1. The Witness- historian and lore keeper, the witness cast an unblinking eye over the world as it passes. He seeks always to speak duly, plainly, describing all that happens without embellishment or opinion. What will be, will be.

                          2. The Storyteller- wild of mind and quick of tongue, the Storyteller is the myth maker. The world that is has no interest or use for him. People love more that which has never been and shall never be. There is a rumor that if he were ever to speak truly on what has transpired in the world that he himself would cease to exist.

                          3. The Messenger- the lady of the long road, the collector of stories from afar. Language is of little use if it is not communicated to those in need of information. The lady speaks the words that must be heard, and collects the words said in reply. She is the translator, and the mediator. She gives meaning to those tales and messages spoken in strange tongues and foreign contexts.

                          4. The Lawgiver- language has other uses as well. Laws and judgments must be crafted and enforced, and each must be remembered so that justice can be applied. The lawgiver remembers the old codes, even if their meanings no longer apply.

                          5. The Censor- Every writer needs an editor and a critic. The Censor serves both purposes. He is despised by the others, none more than the Storyteller. Yet he gives the works of the Scribes direction and definition. Or so he claims. It is fair to say his greatest love is in the destruction of the Word. A stanza here, and sentence there, every line he erases grants him terrible power. If the others have mastered the aspects of the spoken Word, he is the master of the Unspoken. The keeper of secrets. The knower of what must not be known.

                              I've been toying around with an alternative set of Shan'iatu for the Su-Menent, in which each is defined through their study of a particular part of the "animal shell," and by extension a particular aspect of mortality. Let me know what you think!

                              The Carver: Among the Shan'iatu of the Shell, the Carver is the foremost student of the animal form. It is given to him to understand that which gives structure to flesh - to know the myriad shapes and functions of bone and to plumb the delicate minutiae of chitin. Moreover, it is she that best grasps the meaning of living and once-living shapes, from the subtle dance of knuckle-bones that underlies the musician's art to the wisdom concealed in the shadow of a sun-bleached scapula.

                              The Ecstatic: The Ecstatic stand apart from the other Shell-Priests, eschewing their silent hall in favor of savage, sun-burnt plains and shadowed dens of vice. Viewed by her peers with a mixture of awe and revulsion, she is the mistress of the heart, and the knower of mortal passions and desires. Of all the Shan'iatu, it is she that comes closest to truly understanding mortal virtue and morality, but in knowing virtue she has likewise become intimately familiar with vice, and the intoxicating pleasure of transgression. Dwelling in a near-constant state of sacred rapture, she sups on the lifeblood of her lovers and anoints herself with the offal of corpses, rejoicing in the desecration of the pure and the elevation of the obscene.

                              The Feaster: Though they share the form of man, the Shan'iatu are free of its frailties, and of their number it is only the Feaster who truly understands the nature of hunger. Among the Bearers of the Four Jars, he is the keeper of the stomach, and his is the wisdom of feast and famine. He alone comprehends the nature of the arcane process by which fruit and grain become muscle and bone, an alchemy far more subtle than any practiced by the Mesen-Nebu, and so too is he intimately familiar with the signs and stages of the fatal hunger-sickness that reduces the body to dust.

                              The Haruspex: Perhaps the most enigmatic of the Bearers of the Four Jars, the Haruspex is the keeper of the liver. Altogether more subtle in its workings than the lungs, stomach, or intestine, the liver is nevertheless essential to the cycle of living and dying. More than that, however, the liver is as a mirror to the heavens, for those who know how to read its secrets, and in the flesh of the organ the wise may read the will of fate. Through this grisly oracle, the Haruspex knows the day and hour of every birth and death in the Nameless Empire, and perhaps even glimpses the ultimate fate of the world itself.

                              The Poisoner: Sickness and filth are the domain of the Poisoner, the keeper of the intestine among the Bearers of the Four Jars. Though he does not revel in defilement as the Ecstatic does, he is fascinated by that which the body rejects, and knows the doses at which even the most nourishing of substances becomes more than the animal shell can bear. Her knowledge of medicine rivals that of even the wisest alchemists, for unlike the Sorcerer-Priests of the Mesen-Nebu, she is unafraid to explore the vicissitudes of putrefaction and degradation. As a consequence of his studies, he is the foremost master of the embalmer's art, for that which can still the living body can sometimes also stay the decay of one already dead.

                              The Skinner: The Skinner's art is one of impressions and appearances, for what are beauty and ugliness if not conditions of the flesh? The skin is far more than a mere vessel for the vital organs of the animal shell; it is the boundary which distinguishes the self from the other, and through which the other perceives the self. Just as the people of Irem know their priests by the ceremonial leopard skins they wear, so too can the Skinner step into any role by taking on the flesh of another. Thus, it is she who understands best the nature of mortal relationships, both between those living and between the living and the dead.

                              The Whisperer: The whisperer stands among the Bearers of the Four Jars as the keeper of the lungs, and is the wisest of the Shan'iatu in the subtle secrets of the breath. From the first scream of a newborn infant to the death-rattle of a shell already emptied of its fivefold soul, and from the ache of a lover's sigh to the stench of a murderer's labored breath, his studies encompass that which is felt and heard but remains nevertheless immaterial and unseen. Thus, she is also the foremost expert among the Shan'iatu on the subject of ghosts and the twilight realm of Neter-Khertet, and is served by a legion of silent ghosts, utterly enslaved to her will.

                                  Here are a few ideas for the alchemists I've been toying with. The first is inspired by nothri's idea of a "Cultivator":

                                  The Sower: The art of the Sower is subtle and slow, but without it the Nameless Empire could never have grown beyond a cluster of rude mud huts. To transmute water, earth, and sunlight into life-giving grain is the most miraculous of alchemies, and the wisdom of the Sower ensures that the scourge of famine shall never fall upon the servants of the Shan'iatu. The Sower is no mere farmer however; in his great walled gardens he cultivates specimens of most every growing thing to be found under the sun, and Irem's foremost masters of alchemy have often had cause to supplicate the Sower for some rare reagent required to create the most potent of brews.

                                  The Instructor: As Dedwen flows through the world, it takes on myriad forms, and only a few are so crude that they can be held in the hand or decanted into a flask. The Instructor fashions the Dedwen of human virtue and skill, refining the dross of the ignorance into the treasure of true mastery. Only the most promising initiates of the Mesen-Nebu draw the attention of the Instructor, and many come to regret it, for she is not a forgiving master. It is not merely skill that the Instructor demands of her pupils, but moral rectitude as well - under her tutelage, they become worthy not only of this life, but of the life to come... assuming that they survive.

                                  The Trader: The Trader knows that magic can take unexpected forms. The essence of alchemy is the transformation of that which is less valuable into that which is more, and even the uninitiated can accomplish such transmutations with a shrewd eye and a silver tongue. There are many in the Nameless Empire who look down upon the Trader's art, and it is whispered that the Shan'iatu regard her as the least of their kin. But the Trader knows that there are things which even mighty armies cannot take by force, and without trade the mighty city of Irem would be a mere village.

                                  The Weaponsmith: While the priests of Irem promise life eternal among the gods to the mewling and fearful masses, the Weaponsmith knows that these are empty words. There is nothing that lives which cannot die, and even that which cannot die may be destroyed. All it takes is the proper set of tools. The Weaponsmith is the undisputed master of a rather specialized form of alchemy: that which transmutes life into death. The nameless empire has few enemies worthy of the Weaponsmith's attention, but when he decides to devote her talents to the destruction of a foe, none can stand against the instruments of his wrath.

                                              I was reading over the relics in the corebook just now, and was struck by this line from the description of the Blasphemous Depiction:

                                              It was created in a direct assault upon the Nameless Empire from within, a betrayal from the very top. Its maker's name has been scraped from every monument, burned from every text, blasted from every memory. Even the desecrator's gender is unknown, and all his or her servants were killed to bury the story. No depiction remains.
                                              Considering what we know of the political structure of Irem, "betrayal from the very top" sounds tantalizingly like one of the Shan'iatu might have turned on it's peers some time before the betrayal of the Deceived. Since the Depiction is an effigy, the traitor Shan'iatu was most likely one associated with the Tef-Aabhi; here's my treatment:


                                              The title of the seventh Shan'iatu of the Effigy has been utterly erased from the histories of the Nameless Empire, but those who have looked upon the deep gouges that mar the walls of the Tef-Abhi guildhall or wondered why the Fathers of Idols serve only six masters share whispers of The Desecrator.

                                              In Irem, the Shan'iatu are above the reproach, for it is with their voices that the Judges make the Law known to the people of the Empire. Though disputes are known to arise among the senate of sorcerer-priests, for even the Shan'iatu cannot match the perfect wisdom and understanding of the Judges, it would be against their very nature to violate even the most minor precepts of the divine Law.

                                              And yet, just as the theologians of later epochs tell tales of betrayer-gods and fallen angels, the common folk of Irem discreetly share stories of the Shan'iatu who turned against his own kind. The tales differ wildly with regard to the nature of the Desecrator's crime - some say she conspired to murder one of her fellow sorcerer-priests, or that she consorted with enemy nations, or even that she aided and abetted the Great Uprising in the hopes of overturning the senate and becoming sole ruler of the Empire. Only a few, all of them respected members of the Tef-Abhi, know the true nature of the Desecrator's transgression, and they dare not speak of it save in the strictest confidence. Even they do not know what punishment was meted out to him, however; only the Shan'iatu themselves can say what fate has befallen the one and only traitor to their order.

                                              ---

                                              I also have another idea for one of the alchemists, inspired by the Lesser Djed Regia:

                                              The Stone-Dresser: The greatest works of Irem are wrought from stone, from the unscalable walls of the city to the sacred Djed pillars for which it is best known. Stone forms the foundations of the Nameless Empire, both literally and figuratively, but before those stones can be laid they must first be quarried and shaped. Quarrying and hauling stone is work for slaves, but identifying suitable materials and cutting them to the correct specifications are duties worthy of an artisan. It was the Shan'iatu called the Stone-Dresser who first perfected these arts, and it was at his feet that the Mesen-Nebu learned to imbue the works of the Nameless Empire with the permanence of the world itself, impervious to the ravages of time the weapons of Irem's enemies alike.
