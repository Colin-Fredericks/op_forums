    Originally posted by Nephilpal View Post
    An Arisen in Apotheosis is self-determining and a creature of limited time. He can make relics because he is fundamentally mortal and because he is a master of his corner of the world. On a long enough time frame, he will die and stay dead. His only near-immortal legacy is to bring more magic into the world.

    A Restless Star who has attained Apotheosis do not get to create relics. His existence is to serve the music of the spheres, not write his own melody. He is eternal, and his legacy is one of submission to Fate for all time. He will bear witness to relics coming and going. He will hold the stars in his hands and feel them slip from his fingers, only to gather anew the fruits of the Great Beyond when the time is right. He feels relics as a numinous pulse of the living universe, but he does not partake of their making. The magic he is connected to is infinitely greater than him. Fate provides. Fate takes away. Fate is inscrutable. Fate is.
    Man, the Deceived really do get the short end of the deal. They cannot escape slavery, ever.

        I want to make sure I understand how the kepher process for finding seba works. As I read the book:

        1) There's a general kepher roll that anyone can attempt that requires lengthy astrological divination, a clear view of the night sky (or its equivalent), a specific Occult Specialty, etc. Get cracking on those star-charts!

        2) Fate can intercede and grant anyone a passive kepher roll that doesn't require the Occult Specialty, the ritual, or the night sky. You just have a hunch.

        3) Deceived, specifically, have a much easier time finding seba than anyone else. They can choose to roll their Sekhem as the kepher dice pool instead of the normal pool (if their Sekhem is higher), don't need minimum Occult ratings or Specialties, special rituals, the night sky, etc.
