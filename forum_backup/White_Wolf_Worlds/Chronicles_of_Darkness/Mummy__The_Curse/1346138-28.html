                    Anyways, so I caught up with the material today. Thoughts so far?

                    By and large, Mummy is a lot more accessible now, and while I didn't realize how I liked the mechanical occultness of the systems and even the information drip (yeah, I suddenly realized that I actually had enjoyed the journey of discovering the truth about Mummy over multiple books, I'm as surprised as you), that accessibility can only be a good thing. There's some mechanics that answer things Mummy already did that I know long time Mummy fans know my opinion on, but meh, it works. While there's definite things to improve (Affinities and Utterances, though I wasn't as bothered by it as others, definitely need some more oomph, thank god for expanded materials), I enjoy the new version...with one exception.

                    Mummy the Curse was a highly experimental game for the Chronicles franchise and as a roleplaying game on the whole , and I would personally argue it being revolutionary, or at least capable of it (seriously wouldn't have minded seeing more games take cues from it). It obviously ended up fairly niche, but I contend it ended up so for the right reasons.

                    Now as a fan of that game who wanted nothing more than to see Curse in the Second Edition, this scratches the itch. I got a game I really like with a lot of neat additions and more openness lined up with the rest of the game in an incarnation I really like.

                    But I am disappointed Curse wasn't quite as revolutionary in it's Second Edition form, that while it adds cool things, it doesn't really challenge conventions in the same way First Edition did.

                    But that's a tall order for anyone, and I'm not going to let it stop me tipping my hat to everyone on the team for a job well done.

                    Besides, we still have three chapters to go. I've still got room to be proven wrong, and I wouldn't be surprised to find out I'm wrong in some way.

                                                    Originally posted by reseru View Post
                                                    That's totally fair! I just don't think people should be surprised when certain ideas aren't published in official material, especially when it would conflict with the themes or setting of the game.

                                                    It's like wanting Sekhem start at 1 and then increase as you buy it with experience: if that's more fun for your gaming group then you can do it because you can hack the game to be whatever you want, and I won't stop you, but I might question you and defend the default, but don't be surprised that it's not the official, published mechanic.

                                                    The default doesn't need defending. Questioning can actually be helpful in further refining ideas to make stuff more fleshed out and prepared for the table.

                                                    Also, if you check back with everything I've said regarding this tangent, I've been very clear that I'm talking about houserules, things that I would want to do with the likely material, and how I can adapt what is available to my own purposes. I rather like preserving the themes of sacrifice in exchange for power, and so I would prefer having rituals creating such horrors to be bloody affairs involving dead animals, blood and a willing sacrifice.

                                                    I generally encourage my players to enjoy having their characters do messed up stuff, emphasizing that their characters are monsters and to explore doing dark things for power. I take no control over their characters in a scene because I want the player to always have agency over what their character does, even when their character would have no agency over themselves. For example, if I have a player of a werewolf go into Death Rage, I'll present the player with two options: 1) They play their character as they want during the Death Rage and I will liberally bribe them with Beats for doing things the player wants to happen that will cause their character any grief. 2) I do a Cut-to-Black, then have a discussion with the player as to what scenarios they're comfortable their character waking up to and finding out what they've done. If the player doesn't want any of their character's friends hurt while the character is uncontrollable, then none of their character's friends get hurt. I'll engineer the scene to keep them safe.

                                                    One of the things I love about Chronicles of Darkness games is being able to have a safe environment to share a story of horror, dark things, and taking the role of the monster. This is why I emphasize buy-in, both on a player being interested in an idea, and being able to sell me on the idea. Both parties need to be willing and consenting for it to work, which is why I generally stay away from "surprise" events. I dearly love the Beat system for being able to bribe players and reward them for complicating things for their characters, especially when showcasing their characters as monsters.
