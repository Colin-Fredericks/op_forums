    Different kinds of Amkhata

    05-31-2014, 07:24 AM
    With both Guildhalls and Cursed Necropolis having a unique Amkhat in their sample chronicles, it's pretty clear that these monsters aren't limited to being perverted representations of the five-fold soul. While, personally, I feel that the majority of Amkhata, certainly all Lesser ones, should stick to being lion/falcon/bull/jackal/snake hybrids outlined in the core book, keeping them as dark spirit-monsters that reflect aspects of the Arisen they prey upon, these new Amkhata have got me thinking about other animal parts that could make for an appropriate and evocative monster.

    To me, the most obvious one that hasn't been used yet is a crocodile. Ties in to Ancient Egypt, it's an ancient apex predator, all that good stuff. I imagine a Greater Amkhat containing crocodile parts being a powerful stealth predator that likes to strike from or near water (at least if it uses a crocodile frame), and sport solid natural armour to protect it.

    New versions of the False Ka are considerably easier to justify, since none of the existing ones have any connection to the soul to begin with. Flies (providing a cloud that provides concealment against shoot/a -1 penalty to melee attacks due to the distraction) are an easy one to imagine, but what I like most about the False Ka is its potential for unique abilities. Obviously, Araq from Guildhalls is the clearest example of this. I'm currently wracking my head trying to work out what a Scarab Heart would do. The Ancient Egyptians even made heart scarabs to put in mummies, but I can't work out an effect that feels appropriate.

    Anyone else been thinking about unique Amkhata to use in their games?

          Twisted beasts as they are, the Amkhata haunt the nightmares of the Arisen. Creatures that exist in defiance of nature's forms - chimera - sometimes develop spontaneously as a consequence of the destruction of lifeforce. Sometimes they are created by alchemic rituals.

          Not all Amkhata are created out of animal parts. Western Alchemy speaks of the Rebis, an amalgam of male and female parts inhabiting the same body. There are natural ways of achieving hermaphrodite status; the creation of a Rebis is not one of them. Just as all Amkhata, the Rebis is a distortion of reality.

          All Rebis are the product of an occult ritual, in which a female and a male volunteer (victim) are joined together. The result is a person to which all rules for Greater Amkhata apply. As creatures familiar with the human experience, a Rebis is more intelligent than an Amkhat, and as thus, better able to use its abilities more effectively.

          Similar to the Amkhata, the Rebis feeds on Sekhem, giving it plenty of reason to hunt the Arisen.

                          So, another topic got me thinking about the gods of Irem and their relationship to the Decrees, and I remembered this topic. Basically, we know of six gods of Ancient Irem (as in actual gods, not Judges), one of each of the Decree animals plus Azar, who to my knowledge is just a humanoid deity. The Amkhata are blasphemous combinations of the decree animals, but if you look at them as blasphemous instead due to being perversions of the gods that opens up the prospect of Amkhat with human parts, sort of like what's described above.


                          Personally, if I were to use an Amkhat with a human part I would have it as just a regular head/frame. I quite like the idea of an Amkhat with a human head, lion body, and falcon wings to make a proper sphinx, with the primary benefit of the human head being intelligence and the ability to speak. Particularly as a recurring character, something that dogs a character through many descents. An Amkhat's ability to retreat to Twilight makes it pretty hard to kill, especially if it has a human intelligence guiding it and telling it when to retreat.
