                                        Originally posted by Merkonan View Post

                                        Maybe, but while I value the beauty of a half-truth as much as anyone, our Deus Est Machina doesn't really seem the type to do so when bald-faced lies require a lot less creativity.
                                        Better question to ask would be why would it care about the drama with Mummies? It's busy fixing timelines, preparing the Occult Matrix, and more. Perhaps a revelation was to move the Prophet to create a cult simply to dispose of later once a project had been completed and largely cares about more active players in the game. Mummies are on a time limit, Achieve a goal, and then return to their tombs. As powerful as they are, they're mostly in stasis, old men taking naps in their tombs. None of which helps or hampers the God Machine in the slightest.

                                        Sure, it can take on secondary goals. Goals that include discovery of Sekhem and how it interacts with the occult matrix, odd discovery of the Irem Relic, Repairing the festering holes in the time line that the Rite punched into the world, etc, etc.

                                        But none of those are it's primary operations and purpose. For every resource use to harass the Arisen, the more chaos the Unchained can create. Ongoing projects need regular Outputs, Materials need to be shipped, and Infrastructure needs to be maintained. None of that stuff really affects the Arisen other than perhaps the most casual way.

                                        As a result, the GM, if it does exist in a mummy story, is purely a background character who's influence just barely makes it's self known and most likely will be confused as rival mummy cults, plans and mechanics that other Arisen have in place, some courier from the Judges, or some weirdness that this world throws out every so often.

                                        In short, you don't need to involve the Big Man in this. He's got more important things to do.

                                            Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
                                            Just making sure I'm reading this right; Azar is the potentially unwilling king of Duat, there are two different groups of 42 Judges, and Irem was physically pulled (intact!) into Duat? (Wholly unrelated; will we ever see coverage of the Shuankhsen in a playable context like the Deceived got? I think they're massively sympathetic, but that could just be the Oblivion fanboy in me).
                                            The Judges of Duat are left kinda ambigious to a certain extent. Likely so that the reader and ST can make up their own mind for what fits a given Mummy game that might try and uncover any truth in regard to the matter.

                                            That being said, their is a fair bit of evidence that the Judges of Duat and the Judges that birthed the Shan'Iatu are different entities. Its mentioned for example that the Celestial Judges are residents of paradise pretty much. Basically the opposite of Duat. Likewise, they are the "Judges of Life" while it appears the Judges of Duat are the Judges of Death that the Shan'Iatu sought to create/become (and that Azar asked about before they chopped him up "Who are the Judges of Death?").

                                            Other factors are the remoteness of the Celestial Judges as exceedingly abstract patterns of Fate that seem to dwell above even the Nameless Gods like Azar and Anpu. While the teaching of Irem had the Judges of Duat as very interested and involved in mortal lifetimes (since they had to punish transgressions) and that they were much closer to mankind then the Nameless Gods.

                                            This gets more complex and interesting when you add in that CAS has mentioned several times that the Judges of Duat are not just the ascended Shan'Iatu. Instead one might view them as an attempt by the Shan'Iatu to enter Duat and rule under Azar there. However, the end result might have been the creation of a separate group of entities by the Shan'Iatu, as effectively a by-product of them sacrificing Irem (and possibly themselves) to Ammut. In a more liner way, perhaps getting new "true names" changed the Shan'Iatu more then they expected and instead of becoming the same old beings in new forms they became new beings full stop.

                                            Frankly everything that happened after the Rite of Return was enacted is really still a big ???. We don't know when or were the Judges of Duat came from after the Rite, though the timeless nature of the Rite of Return makes it look like they existed before the Rite was enacted. I suspect this is something we once again will not get a hard answer too, though Dreams of Avarice might provide more insights (given that the Heretic can remember what is going on in Duat now).

                                            Also as someone pointed out, the fiction at the start of Deceived does have an unreliable narrator who seems to get several elements wrong (such as the Shan'Iatu's "human" gender) if what the devs have to say is any indication. So while I am sure it does paint a more complete picture then the story we got in the Corebook, I'm also pretty open to the idea that their are some purposeful errors or red-herrings again.

                                            "There is no God in the Badlands." - Sunny
