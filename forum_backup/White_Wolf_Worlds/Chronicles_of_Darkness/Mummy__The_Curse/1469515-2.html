            53)Something huge and swollen with corpse-gas crawls on all fours out of the Underworld into Neter-Khertet, eyes spilling smoke and mouth more lamprey-like than human. It heads towards a nearby hidden trove of relics the PCs’ meret have stashed away, and will single-mindedly seek to gather them up and return to the Underworld, where the Arisen can’t possibly follow.

            Wat do?

            54)In the ruins of Crocodopolis in Egypt, hidden from the view of tourists and natives alike, in the local Neter-Khertet sit monolithic houses of Amkhata once thought of as gods by the ancients. Scribes and Sekhem sorcerers and psychics made these things, these stitched-together horrors, to imitate the terrible glory of the Judges and the more slothful gods of Duat.

            But Anpu shook with rage at seeing such mockery, and he waged war on these mighty Amkhata, locking them away in spirit-temples made of ephemera, prayer and futile hope.

            Antinous, Attis, Cybele and Serapis and more are names of the things Anpu locked away here. These prison-houses contain valuable relics and vessels, but breaking into them is a fool’s quest. (Strangely, in a way no mummy can explain, werewolves keep randomly popping up in the house-tomb of Serapis. Truly, the will of Anpu is strange and weird and unguessable. Such is the way of gods and monsters indistinguishable from gods.)

            55)The meret makes a horrifying discovery while hunting relics in the possession of a world-famous museum—-a set of relics from Ki-En-Gir, Irem’s most notorious enemies.

            Memory is clear: Ki-En-Gir magic, while mighty, was not the equal of Utterances, relics and Sekhem sorcery. The Ki-En-Gir city-states did not power itself with Sekhem, but some other, now lost to time mystical energies.

            And yet...the proof is indisputable. This flies in the face of everything Memory teaches the Arisen. Wat do?

                60) Your Judge has given you a commandment; seek out the Relics known as Hero's Mercy, Sleep of Eternal Rest and The Movement of Echoed Dreams, a fairly normal request. There are a few differences and complications though...in addition to not knowing anything else about these Relics, you are told to return these Relics to their Arisen creators and none of these Relics belong to your Guild...or any of the other Major Guilds. (In honour of BoLD's release, I figured throwing some unusual Relic forms and their Minor Guilds at players could be fun. Remember the Judges don't really care the inter-Guild politics, and are more upset with the Temakh of the Shan'aitu than the Arkhem-Urtu themselves. Plus I like the idea that Neoliths, Ankhs and Seba are just as important to the Judges schemes).

                    61. TE Lawrence, more famously known as Lawrence of Arabia was an archeologist and intelligence agent for the British government against the Ottoman Empire during world war 1. He was also the man who first dubbed the legend of Iram of the pillars the “Atlantis of the Sands”. He also intended to explore Arabia searching for Ubar/Iram (in the real world they are speculated to be the same city), even suggesting using an airship to do so.

                    That’s it. That’s the adventure hook. Expedition in a dirigible searching for the cursed city of Ubar in the 1920s. Definitely going to attract the interest of several cults, half want it to fail and half succeed. And of course nothing good is likely to be waiting there for them.

                            62. This idea comes from a conspiracy theory regarding the Great Fire of London in 1666 AD. Freemasonry was, of course, very popular in that city, especially among those with an interest in engineering and design. So logically it makes sense that many of the architects employed to rebuild the city after it was reduced to ashes were a part of that society. But of course the speculation is that this strange society or some more mysterious order using it as a front deployed these men to build the city to their specifications. Some have even gone so far as to claim the city of London has been aligned along a grid pattern that matches such things as the Seal of Solomon or the Kabbalistic Tree of Life. Obviously in the Chronicles of Darkness this can all be perfectly true, and a Mummy Cult or a Meret of Mummies could have pulled the strings of these architects. But I realized this idea isn't confined to London. How many large cities don't have some tragic history of large scale accidents from fires, or earth quakes, or hurricanes, etc? One has to wonder in the Chronicles how many of these disasters weren't actually the work of an Arisen with geomantic ambitions for the city. After all, several Utterances are capable of causing the ground to shatter, or to summon down meteors, or to transform the Deathless into a pillar of fire hotter than the Sun. It would only take a little careful planning, a knowledge of Iremite Geomancy, some carefully recruited architects into the ranks of the cult, and finally one night of destruction followed by a few strings pulled with the recovery efforts and any given city could be realigned to serve the occult needs of a given Mummy or Guild. Of course, a rival mummy could just as easily do something similar to spoil those efforts...

                                                Here's one:
                                                [Deceived]
                                                A Bane Seba called the Crown of Wepwawet is discovered by a lone Su-Menent while following Kepher in Uraq's Empty Quarter, but is quickly consumed by the item's foul power, making her one of the few non-Deceived to ever attuned a Seba. Unfortunately, it's a Bane Seba!

                                                She who wears the Crown of Wepwawet starts gestating a reincarnating Chthonic Being (presumably named Wepwawet). Irregardless of gender, the mummy quickly discovers they are pregnant, and monstrous appetites designed to feed the horrible fetus quickly descends upon them. Memory suffers rapidly, and strange new recollections fill the wearer:

                                                ---there is a land of worms, where soulless gods and almost-gods feast forever, under a red cinder of a dead sun, hunting each other as the last remaining food sources

                                                ---I am [untranslatable], and I will hunt in this new world of hateful light and vibrant life for it is my nature to be [untranslatable] and ever-hungry

                                                ---feed me, mother, that i might consume you from within and be born---

                                                Thr PCs Deceived meret defeated the Su-Menent, but were unable to claim the accursed Seba.

                                                Now, centuries later, the Crown of Wepwawet resurfaced, this time in the hands of a Shuankhsen! Can the PCs' meet successfully capture the hateful star of false inspiration? Or will it escape once more?

                                                The Crown of Wepwawet: Bane Seba ●●●●●
                                                Ammut has consumed stars, though she she rarely does so now. Some stars house Chthonic Beings, alien to any cosmology known.

                                                The star Ammut ate, then regurgitated a dead star as the Crown of Wepwawet, which contains the broken identity of a single Chthonic Being associated with said devoured star, a horror that must take new flesh by impregnatinvg itself into the Crown's new wearer.

                                                9 weeks later, a terrible thing of stardust and whirling gyre maws and a vaguely trilobite outline tears itself free from within the wearer, artifically ending their Descent and attaining a state of Twilight.

                                                System:
                                                [/b]The pregnancy is immediately initiated by the Deceived sacrificing a single level of Bashing damage.
                                                The pregnancy last nine weeks. At the end of every werk, the mummy must suffer yet more damage:
                                                ● Week 1-3: Each week, one level of Bashing damage.
                                                ● Week 4-6: Each week, convert one of these Bashing levels of damage to Lethal.
                                                ● Week 7-8: Gain one level of Aggravated damage per week.
                                                ● Due date: The Chthonic Beingvis birthed, bloodily erupting into Twilight as an aberrant fetal horror.
