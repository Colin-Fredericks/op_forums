        Originally posted by Azahul View Post

        This is a random tangent, but are we meant to be impressed by the meteors from Secrets Ripped From Skies doing Sekhem in Aggravated damage to structures? Won't buildings typically have well into the double digits in Structure? I can't help but feel like this number would need to be a great deal higher to actually level a building.
        More importantly, do we need to bother tracking damage to structures? Like, when does that even come up? I think most storytellers would just narrate that the roof is blown off and the walls are sputtering fire, I certainly don't want to track the health of my condo.

        Edit: All this talk of Mummy powers being too weak might run too close to splat comparison, I think what we really need to do here is check them against inspirational material. Fortunately, that's an easy pick. So without further ado How do Mummies in 2e match up against Imhotep from the 1999 Universal action film The Mummy?

        FEATS OF IMHOTEP:

        Summon murderous scarabs that burrow into people's brains
        Check. Mummies can absolutely do this via Right of the Sacred Scarab. They don't eat people quite as quickly as the ones in the movie, but we can forgive leaving out a touch of death effect.

        Endure five men with automatic weapons blowing the shit out of him at point blank range
        This one requires a touch of math. Lets assume the men in question are working with a well above average 10 dice each (since that makes the math easy). That's about 3 successes a head plus an additional ~2 from rerolled 10s. So 17 damage total, plus the innate weapon damage so that's another 10. Even if Imhotep had 1/0 armor and 10 health levels (drops it to 22) he's looking at a 2 agg rollover. That said, Seal Flesh and Sahu Restoration do cover the 'shaking off overwhelming damage' section fairly well. So our Mummies can do it, but it'll cost them.

        Turn into sand and flow through a keyhole, then into a sand twister, then later into an entire sandstorm with his face
        Wrathful Desert Power covers the last two precisely, down to the description. Weirdly turning into sand to pass through things wasn't in any of the Affinities, but regardless Mummies can do it.

        Rain blood/frogs/locusts on Cairo
        Invoking the Tempests Fury. Again, almost exactly from the movie.

        Cause a complete Solar eclipse
        No. Though this doesn't seem to do much in the film, so I can understand the omission. Still, this definitely falls under "effects that deserve the cosmic keyword".

        Drop, like, a thousand meteorites on Cairo
        Yep, Secrets Ripped From Skies Tier 3 with the kicker does exactly this. Albeit at great cost. A side note why doesn't this have the Cosmic Keyword? Certainly laying waste to Cairo is chronicle altering.

        Turn many if not all of the residents of Cairo into zombie-esque slaves
        Gift of the Golden Ankh kinda scratches this, as does Awaken the Dead's 2nd Tier, but neither really accomplishes it. There are shades of it Invoking the Tempests Fury's Madness effect, but without a way to direct said madness it's not quite right.

        (Nearly) resurrect the body of his dead lover using a living human vessel
        Awaken the Dead can do this for a little while. But this is understandably not the domain of Utterances.

        So it turns out you can be a pretty good Imhotep. You'd need most of the Pillars at 4+ and looooots of experiences, but it's totally doable. This has actually been a reassuring experiment.

        Double Edit!: Yay, more Utterances is a stretch goal now!

                                11-13-2019, 11:40 PM

                                I'm really thrilled to see this finally going forward. I had some comments provided below, and I'm looking forward to the final product. Thank you for all the work that's gone into it so far.
                                1. I’d really like to see a stretch goal that addresses the remaining Judges, perhaps in a supplemental pdf. I’d also be interested in knowing if the reduction to 7 judges in the book was primarily a page limitation decision, or if its’ intended to feed into the premise of the game.
                                2. A note about pillars in general: I was surprised that pillars retain the same basic mechanics as before. It feels like a missed opportunity to tie their progression character development, particularly in a way that can buoy either memory or power progression (with the consequences inherent in choosing either). I always liked that the 2e version of Renown in W:tF required character development and pillars seem ripe for something similar.
                                3. Cult is under the Timeless Merit section, but it seems that it could also apply to a cult let be non-immortals, given the right circumstances. There isn’t anything inherent to its benefits that require supernatural immortality.
                                4. Dead Celebrity – Elvis has entered the building.
                                5. Dead Flesh – it’s an interesting idea, and feels like a gorier version of the 1e use of bandages to reconstitute a dead Arisen. It’s not terribly clear exactly what’s involved, however. Are you literally ripping flesh off corpses and slapping it on your body, replacing missing limbs, or what?
                                6. Funerary Text – without most of the mechanics available, it’s hard to assess, though the appendix preview gives some idea of the consequences of the Trials. I look forward to seeing how this interacts with the Descent. It fits a nice theme of Arisen adapting to overcome the limitations of hardships of their existence.
                                7. Resplendent Soul/Tenacious Eternity – I like this pattern of a single merit having different benefits for different types of immortals. I’d love to see more of this explored in the stretch goal pdf.
                                8. Tomb – the use of the Tomb merit makes sense, but it seems as though it may portend the loss of the greater variety of sacred geometry in CN:Rio.
                                9. As an organizational note, mixing the general merits with the cult only merits is a bit confusing, and it might make sense to give them their own section.
                                10. Merits were one of the two strongest parts of this chapter, with a lot of customization opportunities for the Arisen and their menagerie of cultists. I really liked how this was explored, particularly with regard to the options for cults, which leads to…
                                11. Cults were the best part of this chapter. For a fundamental element of the Arisen existence, these received shamefully short shrift during 1e. Arguably, we knew more about cults among the Deceived than among the Arisen, and this section goes a long way to making these more vibrant and flexible options for play. I’d really love to see a book on designing and running cults at different times and places in history.
                                12. Cult Archetypes: one thing I’d have like to have seen is a greater variety of archetypes, or the ability to create multiple layers, such as a tribal family that operates through the ownership of major corporation that the members control, with different benefits (or cult initiation characteristics) defined by where in the cult you’re located.
                                13. It seems as though dots applied through the cult merit should be able to purchase cult merits, in lieu of increasing Reach or Grasp. This would be especially useful in the context of a shared cult.
                                14. Recovering Pillars: pillaging a vestige feels like it should be more rewarding. One point per dot (and unrenewable at that) seems excessively limiting, given that vestiges presumably play a role in the lifeweb.
                                15. Physical Attribute Boost: As a point of clarification, are you boosting all physical attributes with one pillar, or one attribute per pillar spent?
                                16. Utterances and Affinities: I’ll echo some of the other sentiments shared here regarding the underwhelming nature of these sections. I like some of the changes put in place for several of these, including the Power of Re and Sacred Scarab, but several others felt like missed opportunities. With a large number of affinities and utterances missing (Night Creature is a personal favorite), I would feel better if I had some reason to expect future supplements that will fill in the gap. 1E had some great supplements, but not a huge number of them. Perhaps this is something that could be included as a stretch goal?
                                17. Specifically to Affinities, you list prerequisites for the various soul affinities as “Pillar 1”. This seems unnecessary, given that they’re all one. Organizationally, have you considered separating them into sections of affinities associated by pillar, it would save a bit on word count without losing any information.

                                                Originally posted by Stannis View Post
                                                I'm really impressed with everything presented so far, honestly, and I don't expect powers to necessarily get a boost from edition to edition when we already have a lot of setting changes too, with time travel and Immortals and Judges to name a few. Plenty of powers in D&D remain pretty much the same from edition to edition and it doesn't hurt the game.

                                                Basically, I'm a bit surprised people are talking about reducing pledges based on the content of one chapter. Onyx Path have shown they listen to fans and the rest of what we've seen and heard about this game so far has been excellent, so maybe we should exercise some faith?
                                                I have a few other niggles that are bothering me outside this chapter, but I should note that the chapter on character mechanics is from my perspective the most important chapter in any roleplaying game. I can always invent my own lore and setting. I can always just write my own story too, if it comes to that. What makes this a game is the presence of my players, and what defines the scope of the game is what those players can do.

                                                It is a seriously big blow to me that 1e actually has more player focussed content than 2e. Sure, tons of cool new setting ideas, but I'm going to be coming at this with a group of players who have played Mummy before and at the moment if asked the question "Well what can we do now that we couldn't do before?" I... I don't know that I have an answer? Nonlinear time is the main hook, but I'm still waiting to see the precise mechanics of that because I have very serious concerns for how it interacts with player agency, and ultimately it's still kind of just setting stuff. I could run a 1e game worked around the ideas presented in 2e (Sothis Ascends even had ideas on nonlinear time so even that isn't wholly new) and give my players more options of what to do with their characters. The fact that there are numerically fewer options wouldn't even be so much of a bother if those options that remain weren't primarily similar to or even just direct transfers of 1e powers.

                                                Ultimately, at the table, there is only one GM. Everyone else is a player, and this Chapter is the most important because it will be the vehicle through which the majority of people will experience the game. And if it's weaker than 1e's equivalent, just giving fewer options and being less compelling outside of a few choice Merits, that is probably the biggest stumble a new edition could make. Mechanics interlock with the narrative of a game on a fundamental level, they are the setting and story in a game, so all the amazing ideas in Minor Guilds and time travel and expanding on the Judges really don't amount to much if they come at the cost of what players can do.

                                                I have not reduced or rescinded my pledge, for what it's worth. But I am worried. This is my favourite RPG, bar none, but I'm struggling to see how I could excite my group of Mummy veterans who have literally seen most of what this chapter has to offer before, and that's definitely damaging my own excitement. I definitely understand people being turned off by this. Kickstarter is already a leap of faith, and Onyx Path is great for the transparency they provide through these drafts. It's like a safety net of sorts... but that safety net works by telling backers that this game might not be for them, and if that's the impression they get then rescinding their pledges is absolutely the right call.
