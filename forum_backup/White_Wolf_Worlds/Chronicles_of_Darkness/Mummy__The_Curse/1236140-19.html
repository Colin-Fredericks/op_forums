Any other powers are mechanically different things, but in-universe identified as (and for most purposes, are ) powers granted by the relic.

                The first free dot of an Eternal’s Relic makes them Endless, and grants them all the perks that come with it. By increasing the Relic’s rating, you can gain additional abilities for it like any other. Buying a Supernatural Merit grants the capability to the Eternal like any other Immortal with it. So for example, an Endless with Telepathy would gain the ability to read minds whether they were an Eternal or a Blood Bather. If someone stole this Eternal’s Relic, they’d still be able to read minds, but they’d likely be very worried until they regained the Relic.

                        Regarding the Driven condition only resolves on replenishing Willpower or does it also resolve when retaining Willpower?

                        Essentially if you never resolve the condition, are you permanently retaining Willpower points on exceptional successes?
                        Resolves on replenishing, as stated, subject to the usual "a Condition can go away if it ceases to be relevant" caveat, e.g. if you go through the entire story without resolving it and come back to the Trials again with a different outcome. Given that without Inspiration and similar exceptional success threshold-lowered effects the best odds most characters will have of exceptional success is a coin toss and mummies don't need to sleep (and are narratively incentivized not to), most player characters will only regain Willpower from their Balance and Burden.

                                                In terms of archspells and Imperium Rites being able to leverage chronicle-spanning activities to change the world, specifically by creating a globe-spanning collection of cults and immortal servitors? Sure, with the obvious caveats that:

                                                1. Several godlike beings (including the Exarchs and the Judges) will have Strong Opinions about this and run interference accordingly (and that interference and the nature of Omens means the outcome wouldn't be the same even if you tried for a direct replication), and
                                                2. Unless the game in which this happens is using it as a starting point for the chronicle, any archmage (or circle thereof) would need to play through the processes necessary to reach that stage (if it's being done by player characters) or be affecting events the players can be aware of and potentially interfere with to some degree (if it's a background process relevant to the chronicle).

                                                An archmage can destroy the universe, too — the playbook Imperial Mysteries operates under means nothing is wholly off the table — but that doesn't mean it's trivial, easy, or practical to do so. The most likely reason one would is for the same reason so many of them have tried to replicate Atlantis (and why the Shan'iatu tried it in the first place): it's their ticket to Heavenly residence.

                                                Supplementary caveat that the Rite of Return was given to the architects of the Nameless Empire by the Devourer to suit Her designs and a whole lot went wrong with it by the temakh's standards, so recreating the feat as one of the few beings who isn't magically locked out of trying to perceive the whole picture of Arisen timelines is a bit more than unWise, to boot.
