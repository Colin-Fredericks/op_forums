                    The section on Shuankhsen manages to be both great (frankly, Bane Rites are much closer to what 2e Utterances should be like, even if Bane Rites and Affinities do suffer a little from the same problem of there just not being many of them) and kind of frustrating. As mentioned, forcing Shuankhsen to be relentless, non-stop murderers hurts their impact. It's also weird that their stats are capped at 5 when they have 10 Sekhem to play with. The sample Shuankhsen from 1e, with her Intelligence 6 that she couldn't use until she had hunted down and devoured the Sekhem of an Arisen or two, was a great concept and I'm not sure why that limitation is in place. I also just think the Sekhem 10 component feels... kind of off. These were the fuel for the Rite, it seems a bit strange that they came back with as much power within them as the principle beneficiaries.

                    Those are all pretty minor quibbles though. The mechanics are stranger. There's chunks of the Bane Rite section that feel like they're meant to invoke some kind of flavour, but I'm hazy on the context. For example, the Rite of the Lost Cause has a different impact on the Shuankhsen's Cult based on their Remnant, but those effects are phrased as "emotional bouts". That suggests to me that the effects are temporary, but we don't really have a time frame for when they trigger (presumably when the Rite is performed?) or how long they last for. On top of that, the "emotional" bouts experienced by the Cultists include "Flesh-eaten, rotting vessels of death", which isn't precisely an emotional state. And why does Ren seemingly turn the Shuankhsen's own Cult against them? And the Rite also grants the Shuankhsen a Bane Rite or Affinity, but only once? That feels unnecessary, every Shuankhsen has this Rite, just add another starting Rite or Affinity to their total if you want them to have an extra one.

                    Then there's the Bane Relic Rite, which reduces the "Sekhem" of a Relic to 1. Technically, Relics don't have a Sekhem rating, they have a dot rating which translates to Sekhem when consumed. What impact does reducing a Bane Relic's Sekhem to 1 actually have on the Relic? Is its power reduced to be on par with a Rank 1 Relic now? Does the Relic's rating also change to 1? (That would have the amusing side effect of making the Relic easier to find with Kepher by reducing the Scope of the investigation, and conversely makes it unfortunate if the Rite achieves an Exceptional Success on a relic a Shuankhsen is trying to use as bait.)

                    Also, why does each Bane Rite have a "Cult" section? Is this meant to provide an indication of the kinds of cultists drawn in by the Bane Rite? It feels a little superfluous, not having any mechanical benefit that I can see. More problematically, the section is just... there, appearing without introduction or context several Bane Rites into the section, which makes it a tad hard to parse initially to determine what the reader is meant to make of those paragraphs.

                    And I may have missed something, but I don't understand the relationship between Bane Rites and Affinities. When do you choose a Bane Rite in relation to a Bane Affinity, and why does the Remnant involved need to not match? Is it trying to say that a Shuankhsen with Sekhem Clot can't use Rite of the Storm?

                    There's a lot of evocative language in this section, but sometimes the actual mechanics appear to be on the hazy side. I feel like a lot of the time I'm missing core rules that weren't detailed in the document. Maybe I'm just struggling because the broken up PDF format makes it a little awkward to flip back and forward between the relevant sections, but there are other niggles like Body Snatcher (is the ability to use it right before losing your last dot of Sekhem meant to allow the Shuankhsen to immediately then start a new Descent in a new body?), Schism of Flesh having a "Curse" in addition to the "Decay" of other Affinities (and that "Curse" being the cost of Beats, which isn't super relevant for what will typically be an NPC), and Swarm Messenger being really clunky (you need to have a Fasad present to use it? You attack with it, but the attack is a contested roll instead of just treating the target's Sheut as armour? Why does it give people a Tilt that will kill just about anyone within a minute, but the Tilt lasts a week? And why is this Decay the only one that both doesn't effect the Shuankhsen, and may well be beneficial to them?).

                                                            Looking just at the manuscript, there's some confusion concerning Sorcerers. On the one hand, we're told that a Sorcerer can only learn so many Rites (Resolve, or Resolve + Occult) — though it does suggest that you can stretch this limit by deliberately failing Breaking Points involving supernatural phenomena. And it definitely said that Open Rites count toward these limits. The first bit of confusion is that #2 says that you get one extra slot per level in Sorcerous Knowledge, but that you can't have more levels of Sorcerous Knowledge than you have does in Occult. But #4 says that everyone has a number of slots equivalent to Resolve, and that Sorcerous Knowledge gives you additional slots equal to Occult. Some clarity would be nice.

                                                            And it also says that anyone can perform Open Rites. As well, Preview #2 speaks of Open Rite Mastery, a term which doesn't appear in later previews. That's two different approaches as to how to handle Open Rites.

                                                            Which one is right?

                                                            My preference would be for Open Rite Mastery, where said Mastery gives you more flexibility and reliability with the Open Rite instead of a virtual guarantee that if you deviate from the stated procedure even slightly, Bad Things Will Happen. One advantage of this is that you don't need to dedicate any of your slots to Open Rites if you don't want to, and you don't even have to have any slots to use them, which is consistent with the complete lack of “Rite Slots” in the Chronicles of Darkness Rulebook; but you still get some benefit from putting an Open Rite into a slot (i.e., taking the time to master it).
