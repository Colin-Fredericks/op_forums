                  Official word is something along the lines of "Never say never, but that's not really what this game is about."

                  If you want to be clever about it, embrace the identity crisis parts of Mummy. Where is the line drawn when it comes to one's identity? If a mummy's cult lands in Peru and stays there for generations upon generations, if the Arisen spends more time there and/or remembers more time spent there than he did in the City of Pillars when he was alive, if his original body was burned and one of his native Peruvian cultists provided him a new body and set of memories - if any of these things happen, or all of these, does he get to call himself Peruvian?

                  If playing around with that idea doesn't work for you, you could always play with a variation of the Purified from WoD: Immortals, or come up with something yourself using M:tC as a baseline. If you want, you could always go with the idea that whatever powers your mummies is a bastardized version of the Rite of Return.

                                          In those cases I see it less as the Arisen inspiring those practices, and more that the Arisen became immersed in that culture at a later date and co-opted it as part of their personality.

                                          Mummy as a rule seems to have a very strong fix on what it wants to be, from a cultural perspective. It's a game about Egyptian Mummies, and pulling from outside sources for new types kinda works against the central themes of the game. On the other hand, a Mummy who has lost enough Memory to believe they come from another culture is completely within the themes of the game, so long as you accept that said character will be wrong. I just wouldn't hold out hope that the developers will begin adding new creature types based on every culture in history that has at some point preserved its dead.

                                          Still, to add to the list of existing creatures you could use to represent Mummies from other cultures, you have Abmortals from Geist (pretty sure they could survive long enough and generally take the form of a preserved corpse) and whatever creature you decide the Blasphemous Depiction Effigy creates.

                                                  Actually, I would argue that Mummy is very much about playing Egyptian Mummies. Yes, thematically, it is completely about old age and the exploration of identity. A game is made of more than one aspect though, and I will disagree vehemently with anyone who claims aesthetics are an unimportant part of an experience. Mummy: The Curse's aesthetics are thoroughly Egyptian in nature, and that is every bit as much part of the experience as the questions imposed by Memory and the loss of power. The way in which you choose to frame a metaphor is important, just because it is a metaphor doesn't make the medium through which you tell it valueless. And from the five-fold soul, Utterances like Rite of the Sacred Scarab, ancient tombs and arcane geometry, and the myriad other pieces of flavour that evoke a feeling of Ancient Egypt it is quite, quite clear that Mummy: The Curse is a game about Egyptian Mummies.
