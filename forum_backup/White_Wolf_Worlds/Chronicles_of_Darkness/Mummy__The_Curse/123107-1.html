                        My thoughts on CN: DC in no particular order:

                        * Back in the Guildhalls adventure, Bes-Mat should have had way more screentime than he did. (Or will have, from an in-universe perspective.) He's a much cooler character in here than the bland conservative Maa-Kep security guy who just hung out in the background, being overshadowed by pretty much everyone else.

                        * Unfortunately, this book has a lot of one of my biggest gripes about Mummy: lots of awesome weirdness going on the the fluff and the plot and no rules for it. Geomancy, mirages and oases, Dedwen, ritual magic apparently unconnected to Affinities and Utterances, the list goes on.I want systems for creating my own geomantic effects! I want heka and Dedwen and the Word to be actual things with rules of their own, not just Guild-specific flavoring for the mechanics we already have! It's really frustrating to have all this cool, unique stuff in the descriptions and no way to use it more concrete than handwaving. I really hope that we'll see at least some of this in a future supplement.

                        * Hebeny is awesome. That is all.

                        * Speaking of Hebeny, I note that both he and Seb-Hetchet have the requisite Memory and Pillars to attempt Apotheosis, at least if the lowered Pillar requirements are in effect. Hints for future metaplot?

                        * Is it just me, or is Ahm-An Taht one big Doctor Who reference?

                        * The art was kind of hit-or-miss. A lot of it was great, but quite a few of the character portraits didn't at all match their descriptions. Seb-Hetchet in particular--his description makes it clear that he's the signature Tef-Aabhi from the core (the classy-looking guy with long hair, a white suit, and a watch; also shown on the cover of this book), but his portrait is some generically scruffy guy with a five o'clock shadow.

                        * I'd never considered that the Talismanic Record could serve as a poor man's Memory, but as written, it totally could. Interesting.

                        * The Deceived are awesome. Also, apparently astrologers. Is this another facet of their role as masters of Naming? Knowing the secret names of the stars, upon which Fate is written...

                        * Also, still no clear indication that the Deceived are actually evil. For all that the book demonizes them when talking about them from the perspective of the Arisen, the actual description of what Hab-Utet is trying to do is pretty ambiguous. I mean, he's described as wanting to "tear down the works of Irem", but given what we know about what Irem was like, that's not exactly an inherently malevolent goal. (I mean, let's face it: in the world of Mummy, no one's hands are clean, and killing a few endangered animals to make a super-Amkhata is actually pretty tame as apparently "evil" acts go. The ostensible "good guys" in this scenario have done much, much worse.) I'm getting the impression that it would be entirely possible to play a heroic Deceived, even if Hab-Utet turns out to be evil incarnate.

                                        Once again to reiterate a point I made in my previous point in regards to this issue.

                                        Its not that the NPC's can do stuff the PC's can't with Geomancy. DC outright says that The White Jackel only managed to achieve the effects he has (and hopes for) with the DC nome because he's got hold of some Deceived Magic. Its just that he's managed to blag that he's pulled this off with a mastery of Geomancy and the aid of the other 4 Guilds knowledge (in particular the Mesen Nebu's Alchemy and the Sesha-Hesbu's mastery of the Word).

                                        Likewise, he's had the dream of Irem Reborn since the first Sothic Turn and has been working towards it since at least the 3rd Sothic Turn for EVERY ONE OF THIS DESCENTS there after (thats 1,400 odd years of Descents from an Arisen with a very active cult). If a player came to me with that background for a character I'd be happy to throw some interesting stuff their way e.g. realizing certain facts about Arisen magic, and having to make deals/getting manipulated without knowing it. Most PC's in experiences don't have that sort of character focus on large scale projects.

                                        In a more general sense,the idea that Geomancy can achieve unique effects is not actually stated anywhere in Mummy. Its presented an an underlying concept of how the Tef-Aahbi believe heka can be concentrated an manipulated, but heka is itself merely an idea to explain certain effects one might have on the world with hard work. The geomantic affinities and utterances to have some unique effects that benefit the Mummy and those the Mummy favours. In the latter's case its usually a method of accelerating or simplifying mundane activities.

                                        In general that's just he idea of "good design" taken to a mystical level, which is pretty much what the Tef-Aahbi are all about.

                                        I used to have the same hang-up on the issue of the Su-Mennet not having separate ritualized magic because their are priests. Until I realized the point I made previously about all Arisen magic being Rote. The Shan'Iatu made sure that all the abilities of the Arisen they intended were Affinities, and Utterances are but the smallest fraction (containing only the "doing" part of magic, rather then the base principles) the least of their Magic. The Shan'Iatu made sure the Arisen were limited, shacked to the purpose their were created for in very particular ways so that the Arisen developed and worked along lines dictated by them. Allowing their servants the ability to create wondrous effects not envisioned in their own plans would go directly against this.

                                        Hence they made sure that while a Tef-Aahbi could play with the loose concepts of heka/geomancy he would never know enough of the base metaphysics of magic to actually control/create with it. At best he has the set patterns of the heka/geomancy related affinities and utterances. The Tef-Aahbi can follow his base ideas about the way heka/geomancy work to try and do something original, but at best he's going to be able to maybe prove a theoretical point, never actually create something new. This is not the Rite of Return removing that knowledge or anything, the point is the Tef-Aahbi, even when they were a mortal guild in the city of Irem, never had all the base principles to do that sort of thing. The Shan'Iatu were very, very particular about keeping the base principles of magic away from their servants.

                                        As a metaphor think of a person working on a Car construction line. One group of guys that bolt the gear-boxes together. They know how to do that and they have enough knowledge of gear-boxes that they can fit them on many different models of car. They don't know how to make gear-boxes though. They could have a go at designing one from the fact they handle gear-boxes so much, but since they don't completely understand how it functions (only that it needs to function just-so to fit with the rest of the gear system) their chances of getting it right are pretty low to say the least.

                                        The Tef-Aahbi are those gear-box guys. The gearbox is idols/geomancy. The self-designed gearbox is their playing around with the ideas of heka/geomancy. The guys making the gearbox and have actually designed ones that worked/invented the gear-box are the Shan'Iatu.
