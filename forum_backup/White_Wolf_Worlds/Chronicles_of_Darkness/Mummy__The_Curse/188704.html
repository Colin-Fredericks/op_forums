    Infinite Macabre Mummy

    07-05-2014, 11:20 AM
    Alright, been working on something for a friend who wanted help translating splats into a space-opera setting. Got inspired after looking over the necrons from Warhammer 40k, but here's what I've done so far. Need help deciding how to add a more cosmic feel to the Arisen, since the decrees are based on creatures from earth, and might be better to base on something more universal. Here's what I've done so far:

    Optional Premise
    When Atlantis fell beneath an ocean of stars, a new empire rose in its stead. A now nameless empire, it created the Stygian Gates with arcane technology, for there was no Underworld prior to the fall, and the old ways required new means of commanding reality. From the sands of time, they rise once more, eager to reclaim sovereign power over a galaxy ripe with tribute.
    Once, they ruled from Irem, a planet trapped in a realm between the Underworld and reality (Neter-Khertet). Commanded by a senate of powerful necromancers, the Shan’iatu, the empire enslaved many civilizations, both living and dead.

    Notes:
    • In their true form, the Ancients are souls bound to skeletons of arcane machinery made to store and harness Sekhem. Most are also built with a sigil to disguise this form in something more familiar to viewers--though such forms often hint at their undead nature, in some way.
    • True Mummies more easily fall dormant the further they get from their stasis-tombs. Special forms of geometry can be used to endow ships with a tomb's ability to channel Sekhem, thereby keeping an Ancient out of hibernation.
    • A special form of "scarab" can be used by True Mummies to enslave a living creature and/or communicate with it. Such figures are able to see past Sekhem-based illusions, and also act as ambassadors between factions and their master.
    • Affinities are akin to upgrades built into the True Mummy's exoskeleton. Even with ones like "Beast Companion", signs of the Affinity mark the Ancient, whose mechanical body converts the nature of its Pillars to into arcane building material.
    • Unlike terrestrial Mummies, the Ancients are vulnerable to being permanently destroyed if unable to reconnect with the soul-network. Usually, when a True Mummy "dies" his identity is transferred into said-network until a new body is manufactured. This signal can be blocked if the local area, planet, or solar system is out of alignment with. Once the alignment is restored, however, the signal can be sent, provided the memory unit is still intact.

    ----

    The Shan'iatu made a pact with an entity known as the Devourer, who bargained for the souls of countless slaves to ensure each of the god-priests ascended to become the 42 Judges of Duat. To maintain their empire, they created the Rite of Return, transforming their most loyal servants (and the entire populace of Irem) into the first True Mummies. In but a single action, the Nameless Empire fell to its knees... in exchange for the certainty of its return.

    Sitting atop the Basalt throne of Duat lies the God Azar, who divided the Judges among 5 decrees of the soul. For each Arisen that came through the Rite of Return, they would be anointed with the patronage of a Judge, and the aspect of 1 decree, thus defining their values within the Empire.

    Ab (heart) -- The Lions: Those that retain the most emotion of the Deathless, they are best with negotiating with mortals.
    Ba (spirit) -- The Falcons:
    Ka (essence) -- The Bulls:
    Ren (name) -- The Snakes:
    Sheut (shadow) -- The Jackals:
    -----
    Each of the Arisen were part of a caste system of 6 guilds, one of which was destroyed by the others. Of the remaining 5, they were (and are) as follows:

    Maa-Kep: Slave Drivers and Spies -- Whilst still above the slaves, the Second Hands were tasked with serving the more mundane needs of empire, in public. In truth, they were also the eyes and ears of the Pharaoh, a guild of unseen slave drivers and secrets, desiring humility over the glory best given to their masters. Now they work as infiltrators and spy masters for the Empire, manipulating foreign powers via numerous false-fronts, and convoluted webs of intrigue.

    Mesen-Nebu: Alchemists and Craftsmen --

    Sesha-Hebsu: Scribes and Lawmakers --

    Su-Menent: Priests and Physicians --

    Tef-Aabhi: Architects and Engineers --
    -----
    Hierarchy
    Each of the Deathless was interred either within a shared or personal tomb somewhere within the material galaxy. Each of these tombs were built with advanced geometry and arcane science to channel the raw life force of the planet into refined Sekhem, thereby maintaining the stasis-tombs and their defense systems. None of the True Mummies were of nobility. That was for the Shan'iatu alone. However, they were still considered the elite or most beloved of the Sorcerer Kings, and so were often blessed with gifts in the form of relics, weapons, companion-like servitors known as sadikh, or even skybarges with which to traverse the stars if need be.

    Often, these tombs emanate with a hypnotic pulse that attracts figures of similar mindset to come across it, the figures oblivious to the growing power of suggestion they feel towards the mummy within. This is intentional. Known as the Subjugation protocol, the tomb subtly guides would-be explorers deemed appropriate to the Arisen's decree, guild, and judge, deeper into the ancient halls of its owner, till the figure does one of two things. If the planet is astrologically disconnected to Irem, the target will be tempted to manually override standard reactivation sequences, enacting a ritual to awaken his or her would-be master. If the tomb is infested with hostile forces, the figure will be swayed to somehow relocate or destroy them (usually in a manner appropriate to their personality). This is not a surefire of reactivation, as the protocol is only a weak suggestion. If the target resists long enough, the tomb will attempt to reassess possible proxies. If successful, said-proxy will either become a loyal cultist to the Arisen, or the first sacrifice to the Judges of Duat.

    Even with the empire crippled so, the Guilds of old still maintain a rank and file scheme to things. Each guild was obligated by imperial law to construct the stasis-tombs of their members. The more important or respected, the larger the tomb would usually be. These grand necropoli sometimes connected with equally large tombs (even creating whole tomb worlds, though rare to be sure). Generally, these immense structures would act as cities for the guilds to command as they rebuilt the Nameless Empire, with the largest being close to Irem, or built atop worlds important to some enigmatic scheme.

    Unfortunately, as time went on, it became impossible for individual guilds to maintain such superstructures without leaning onto the other guilds for support. This meant that new political figures would have to be made to mediate the wishes of all parties involved. So were born the Phaerons, planetary governors that would oversee the great cities till they could one day coordinate with the Pharaoh of Irem. While some of these Deathless took to their positions like philosopher kings, more than a few became tyrannical, proclaiming themselves the right of becoming the next Pharaoh. From there, they created dynasties not of blood, but through allegiance and power plays. As there came no signs of divine punishment from such proclamations, the seven leaders of each guild began to consider this idea, of sponsoring such a king or queen to ensure their dominance over their rivals. So long as the laws are obeyed by such dynasties, they believe the Judges will tolerate such a bid for the throne. For what is the Pharaoh but a conduit between the Empire and the Gods of Duat?

                                                  On the Sadikh, I'll just share a conversation I had on another forum:

                                                  So I'm looking over more stuff in the Core book. Regarding Sadikh, how would you describe the Rite of the Engraved Heart changing them? Given the mechanical nature of the True Arisen, would it be like them being injected with nanite-scarabs that transfigure the flesh and basic DNA structure? Would they or the Facad eventually become something akin to the husks from Mass Effect?
                                                  Kapten: Let's see...

                                                  * Sadikh look human, but their memories are shambles. While their master his hybernating, so are they, but their memories sculpt themselves so that they think that they're alive and awake, and their past memories are inherited from ancestors. I'd say if there was a way to scan their minds, someone would be able to register that there is something funky with their neural activity.

                                                  * Sadikh take damage like a mummy and ignore wound penalties. This could make them appear very "Terminator" in a sense.

                                                  * Finally, a Sadikh can call upon their master's Utterances. Maybe they've Iremite tech embedded in their brain or spinal chord to channel their master's power like a lighting rod?



                                                  Me: So partially cyborg-esque? I could see the same magic that hides the heka exo-skeleton being embedded in such "implants" to avoid suspicion.

                                                  Kapten: To represent the fact that a mummy can only have one Sadikh, how about this: The mummy must remove a piece of its exo-skeleton and fasted it to some part of the Sadikh, corresponding with the mummy's decree (Ab-Heart etc). The piece of the exo skeleton bonds with the Sadikh, releasing a nanite swarm, that infuses the Sadikh with power. The Heka-swarm would be more subtle, yet not as powerful as the mummy.

                                                  And since the Sadikh is made from a piece of the exo-skeleton, the mummy has some means of always tracking and communicating with it's Sadikh. Proprietary tech and all that.

                                                  Me: Well, the ritual is called the Rite of Engraved Heart. Rather than worry about biology, we can say the tech imbeds itself into said-heart (all the better to help circulate nanites through the bloodstream). Depending on Guild and decree though, defines what shape the "injector" takes. Perhaps with Ren, it becomes a snake, burrowing into the flesh. Or perhaps minor cosmetic affects happen during the ritual. With Ab, the sadikh gives out a thunderous roar as Sekhem briefly glows beneath the skin?

                                                  Kapten: I like it The nanites are shot into the heart. Maybe after the nanoi-injection the decree manifests as a tattoo depicting the animal associated with the decree? That way people could identify a Sadikh if they knew where to look for the tattoo?
                                                  [/quote]


                                                  Me: Right! Said-symbol might even be disguised to appear like a birthmark, or appear on a particular place on the sadikh's body (Ren = head, Ab = Chest, Ba = Limbs, Ka = Torso, Sheut = Back). Hell, maybe the tattoo's appearance is defined by and symbolic of the strength and/or nature of the sadikh's patron... or would that be over doing it? :-\

                                                  Kapten: Oh, I just noticed something about the Sadikh. Their master's decree doesn't really affect them, since it is a personal (defining really) thing for the mummy. However, every time a mummy's pillar reaches 5, the Sadikh gets an additional supernatural bonus associated with said Pillar. So instead maybe this is how it happens: Rite of engraved heart (ie the exo-skeleton nanite bonding) happens as we discussed earlier. At the time when one of the Mummy's Pillar's reaches 5, the Sadikh gets the "birth mark" you described above. (Check out page 190 for more details on the Five Pillars Strong).
