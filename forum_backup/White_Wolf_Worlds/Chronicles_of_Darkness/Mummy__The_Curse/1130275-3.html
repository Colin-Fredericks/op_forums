                I can agree with Deceived being a bit fidly-- Its hard to keep track of their true forms sometimes during intense game sessions when WP would be bleeding out like from a sieve, and their Utterances are really awesome but honestly hard to remember and keep track of (blessed of dead stars, btw, is impossible to convert to freeform diceless RP). The way I handled the Temakh is a bit more like Werewolf, or hopefully 2e Geist. I instructed my players to begin play as the Temakh, mentally speaking. Unlike Arisen, who are damn near blank slates when they wake up, I told the Deceived player to imagine themself AS the Musician, full of wrath and hubris and talking like he remembers the primordial nights and only has a passing grasp on actual human life. Then, like an arisen, as Memory increased, he started having flashbacks and memories of himself as a distinct person, and even began struggling so much against his Temakh that he is trying to ally with the Arisen and find the Dreams of Avarice. I do adore the Deceived, but it would be nice to see all mummies keep the Descent of Power and Ascent of memory, without having a billion variables and things to keep track of and (ironically) remember.

                It could be simple things, like a space on the character sheet for room to tell each Utterance's prerequisites so you can remember when they're inaccessible during descent, or if they have the Pillar requirements for the first and third tier but not the second, what specific pillar costs are involved.

                    In broad strokes...

                    1. While all of the books were well-done, I felt that a lot of effort went into Deceived that could have gone into exploring a lot of core story and mechanical elements of the Arisen. Cults deserve their own book, expanding not only on the mechanical elements, but the relationships between Arisen and their cults, and the role of the cults in a society where many of the Arisen in an area will slumber at any given time, and the utilization of grasp and reach. In some ways, the cultists of the Deceived have received more attention than those of the Arisen. There wasn't much development of the Shaunkhsen and human antagonists (though the latter is expanded in non-Mummy books).

                    2. Some of the mechanics seem to exist independent of one another (probably as a consequence of being introduced across multiple books). I think that the Arisen are balanced between two mechanics in tension with each other: Memory and Sekhem. That's a great idea and it works well in many regards, but I think there's a lot of opportunity to pull in the mechanics of relics, vessels, cults, and architecture to address the tension with some effort. The options currently available (affinities like entombed glory and utterance of the silenced vessel give some options to overcome dwindling Sekhem, while architecture can buttress memory or power) are there but feel more patched on than part of a coherent effort by an immortal seeking to grapple with the limitations of its condition (or embracing them to better serve its masters). I'd like to see something like the Vampire 2E linking of banes with Humanity, save in this case, it's a matter of mittigating (or amplifying) the tension between self and power.

                    3. More consistency in terms of the affinity and utterance mechanics. Interesting and evocative, the powers demand a fairly idiosyncratic understanding of how they work. I think some of the streamlining in terms of how they work in 2E would be advisable. The Mutapa chapter of the Dark Eras Companion offers some examples of how they think of this working in the Clash of Wills language, and I'd love to see it expanded.

                    4. Pillars feel a bit tacked on, and I'd like to see them linked directly to roleplaying decisions made by players in a way they currently aren't, perhaps in a way reflective of the updates to Werewolf renown. I love the idea of having two different power sources fueling the affinities and utterances. However, it feels a bit odd to rely solely on willpower to fuel affinities, while access to them rests with pillars. Am I the only one who thinks that?

                    5. I'd like to see some thought to apotheosis as an alternative play style. It radically shifts the play style and themes, but is itself inherently interesting. While the Heretic is presented as something of a White Knight fighting against the evil of the Judges, I think there's a lot of variety in stories about someone who has regained a sense of self at the cost of much of their power. The potential problem I see is in trying to distinguish it from the circumstances of a Demon who has rebelled against the God-Machine.

                                    As a huge fan of 1st edition, I am glad 2nd ed is happening. Also hoping that 2nd edition does not diminish the material but rather serve as both an consolidation and update.                                     Guidelines for STs to create Relics and Vessels (and the difference between the two) would be nice; Reliquary was a fantastic book but between the from the previous edition and it is not useful in determining a crucial detail about relics, the associated guild. Something akin to the Amkhat creation rules (minus construction requirements for relics at least).

                                    More details on how the social structures work would be nice: guilds, nomes, menats and cults should be something the characters (most of them anyway) should be used too and the interactions between them should be relatively frequent. Even when the Arisen is in Duat, the cult still functions, carrying on their duties, which can and should include monitoring cults of allies (menat), performing support functions for their 'god' (guild duties when at rest) and representing their master's interest in the local area (nome). And that is before personal agendas and beliefs are in the mix.

                                    I don't really want to lose anything, though some things may need to change.
