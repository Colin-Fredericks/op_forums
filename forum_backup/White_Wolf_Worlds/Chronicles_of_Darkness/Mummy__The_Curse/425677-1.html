                Originally posted by atamajakki View Post

                "Everything is either from Irem and awesome or not from Irem and shitty" gets boring

                That's a value judgement and we're going to have to agree to disagree over it. Every other game line is about creatures that are scattered across the world in roughly even numbers with mysterious origin sources that may be fact or fiction and are, in either case, tied to no real world geography. Mummy is different. Irem is the grandfather of civilisation, the world's only great magical empire, and was ruled by demigods. It was also definitively situated in pre-Dynastic Egypt and steeped in the trappings of Egyptian magic. No other civilisation had the "advantage" of being ruled by beings like the Shan'iatu with a direct line to the very gods themselves, and the fact remains that the Rite of Return (and actions of the Deceived's Temakh) fundamentally changed, even impoverished, the world of magic for future generations. The setting makes it seem quite unlikely that anyone would ever even come close to rivalling Irem.

                As for the rest of humanity, some of them made relics. That's about it. The books show you these relics. A lot of them originate from more recent civilisations than Irem, and many of them were even made intentionally. As the books make it clear, what once could be churned out by formula by halls of craftsmen now takes single isolated occultists an entire lifetime to make. Irem was explicitly the greatest there has ever been, ruled by the children of the gods themselves. I don't know what there is about Mummy that gives you the impression that later civilisations would ever come close to those heights.

                Remember the themes of Mummy. Your heights are in your past. You are diminished now, in the present, and can barely even remember your own glory.

                                                            Originally posted by atamajakki View Post

                                                            I can't think of anything that would emphasize this more than the fear of being surpassed by a successor.
                                                            The problem here, right off the bat, is that you're choosing one particular side of the two parts of the old age metaphor. Either the past is lost and can never be recovered, or the next generation is rising up to replace you and forget everything you have ever done.

                                                            Mummy seems to have pretty conclusively gone with the former when it comes to magic. Once you have civilisations emulating Irem, even replacing it, you can no longer speak about what has been lost. And given that the central crux of all this is Memory, Mummy encourages a fear of loss a helluva lot more than it fears replacement.
                                                            Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
                                                            "We ruled the world... thousands of years ago." The Arisen cling to a delusion of being the nobles of an empire long dead and well beyond the grasp of Memory, Arisen and mortal alike. It's been a decline ever since the Rite of Return. You're a slave to a name buried for nearly countless ages; how hollow does it feel when you see something that makes you wonder if , just maybe, you have tied yourself utterly to something that can be matched?

                                                            I'm not asking for "the Inca created Deathless that are ten times cooler than anything Irem made, " nor am I wanting "the Nameless Empire sucks and the modern day has it beat." But hints that Irem isn't the end-all, be-all of supernatural mojo and human ingenuity is massively in-theme for Mummy; it makes the Arisen doubt themselves and the worth of their eternal servitude. The point of Memory as a mechanic is that the Arisen should never be sure of anything; why is the complete and total supremacy of Irem a sacred cow, when literally nothing else the Arisen 'know' is?
                                                            The modern world allows for a lot of very interesting questions to be posed, but they're subtly different from what you're describing. In many ways, the modern, mundane world has far surpassed Irem. What was once mighty magic known only to the Sesha-Hebsu (sending messages across vast distances) can now be done by anyone with a phone, to take one example. The world has many ways to make a Mummy feel small, awed, obsolete. However, it does this without removing Irem from its position as the "sacred cow" of magic, which allows the themes I described above to come into play.
