    Seeds of life 3 and Trans issues

    10-23-2014, 08:00 AM
    SORRY WRONG FORUM, COULD ANY MODERATOR SHIFT THIS TO MUMMY FORUM?


    What happens when an "heterosexual" coupe with an operated transgender is under the effect of the last unleash of the Seeds of Life Utterance?

    If a MTF couples with a man, is it considered an heterosexual intercourse for Seeds of Life? What effects would it take?
    If a MTF couples with a woman, is it considered homosexual and thus doesn't work?
    And vice versa

    I asked this because what we choose to define someone for ethical reasons, is not necessarily what something ancient and monolithic like Sekhem magic would define. I mean, ethically, we call a MTF a woman because that's what she chose to be, but I don't personally think Sekhem magic would consider it a woman.
    To consider her a woman by magic, I think it would take a supernal Life and Prime magic spell, or a shift into her supernal symbols.

    What's your interpretation on this?

                  That's a tricky question. One one hand, magic in mummy isn't "Supernal"--it's largely a palpable, material force. The Utterance also specifies sex, not gender. We could substitute gender for sex as suggested, but this isn't necessarily a politically restorative act, because not all trans identities reject birth bodies' common biological roles in childbirth--transmen have carried pregnancies to term and transwomen have impregnated others without compromising who they are one bit.

                  However, later Egyptian culture did accept adopting gendered symbols as a magical-political act, along with the notion that there *is* a symbolic form of gender beyond personal gender identity. For example, Hatshepsut wore a false beard, but male counterparts *also* wore one. So neither self-identification as a man nor male biological attributes were the same as divine-Pharaonic gender.

                  So really, you can take it a number of ways, relying of course on players with trans identities to help define the parameters for you.

                              I don't really think that the Utterance is intended to create a biologically-functional uterus or ovaries/testes where there are none existing (or never was one, in the case of the Utterance reversing vasectomy, hysterectomy, or tubal ligation). So if a cis woman has underwent menopause, the Utterance will reverse that process (which is just a byproduct of aging and loss of a finite resource), basically I guess using magic to empower the woman's ovaries to make more eggs (leaving aside the fact that the ovaries don't make more eggs after a certain stage in fetal development...). For a cis man who is "shooting blanks," the Utterance would basically fix whatever is wrong with his testes and allow them to manufacture viable sperm. And I think it's important to note that none of the magic of Irem really cared about what the individual wanted (unless the individual was a member of the Shan'iatu), but instead followed cosmic metaphysical rules. I think it might be a little out of theme for Mummy magic to prioritize an individual's mental needs/wants for their body over simply reinforcing the existing fleshly pattern which is much more of a "known quantity" for the magic to interact with.

                              You do raise an interesting question, but here it might be instructive to reference Hebeny: at low Memory, Hebeny appears much more masculine, while as his Memory rises, the sadhu takes on the characteristics of "remembering" the physical body as it was thousands of years ago, which was quite busty and feminine. I can't imagine the frustration and dysphoria that must result when Hebeny is at high Memory, knowing that there is no way to permanently change his body short of becoming Twice-Arisen (which he seems to find heretical to do for such a "trivial" reason, if at all). Basically, Hebeny's body manifestation is directly affected by the ancient magic of the Rite of Return, presenting the physical factual reality of the distant past over longstanding desires of the mind (and soul - though the soul would seem to be betraying Hebeny at high Memory, forcing an unwanted form, but I digress).

                              For a very intersexed individual, I think it would depend on what biologically-functional organs were present. I think this could get quite complicated (I am not near enough familiar on the real-world medicine to hard a guess on what is possible or has been medically documented) and possibly even result in a person who could both sire and bear children. I think this is territory where it's best left up to the player and ST to decide what works best for the story in this case.

                                                  Yep, pretty much my thoughts on the subject. The first Tier is pretty neat. Snake-making, resistance to arrows and spears and other wooden weapons, and the ability to cure point all for one Pillar point. If you're running a historical setting that predates gunpowder, I'd almost go so far as to say that the first tier is good enough on its own to account for all the others. In the modern day, it gets scaled back to "situationally great".

                                                  The bonus the second tier gives in social situations to those who eat the fruit is neat, but if you compare it to other social abilities (Dreams of Dead Gods and Word of the Amanuensis, namely), it's pretty lacklustre by comparison. Those utterances don't have to deal with the issue of getting someone to eat food you grew before they can have an effect, either.

                                                  And, as you say, the third tier is actually kind of laughable. Short of playing a chronicle that jumps decades at a time, you're never going to see the impact of it on the world. And even if you do, well, the only benefit it could logically provide would be a way of producing a large cult made entirely of Witnesses.

                                                  It's not as bad as Waters of Life and Death, but it is one of the very small handful of Mummy powers that are decidedly unimpressive.
