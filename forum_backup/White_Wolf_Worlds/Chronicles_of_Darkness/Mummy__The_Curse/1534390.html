    How compatible is 1E and 2E lore?

    04-18-2024, 07:07 AM
    Hello everybody,

    I am new to MTC, coming from VTR, and I started with the MTC 2E Corebook and am now also through the Book of Lasting Death. Since my group is playing something different atm I am in for a long time of waiting till I get to run MTC.

    In the meantime I would like to read more. Since, as far as I know, there is nothing further in the works for MTC 2E, I shifted my gaze towards 1E supplements.

    But before I invest time and money I wanted to ask how much of a difference there is from 1E to 2E? Strictly speaking lore and fluff-wise. Mechanics-wise I dont really care a lot. As a comparison, after I read the VTR 2E core I read the 1E Clanbooks, which I found very evocative, even if some things between editions don't mix and match completely.

    Will I have a a similiar experience with MTC ? And where should I start?

    Thanks you in advance

      The fundamental difference between Mummy 1st and Mummy 2nd is Timelessness. It was only added with the new edition, and in the previous books, the Arisen lived their Descents in order - no time travel involved. There are also lesser differences: Mummy 1st lacked three of the minor guilds, Shuankhsen were more playable (but still not intended as PCs), lesser Immortals weren't offered as valid character types etc.

      What you may notice, is that early supplements and adventures didn't understand the ramifications of their own setting. For instance, Guildhalls of the Deathless was written as if Mummies were awake most of the time, and had a hierarchy where some worked under others' orders. The default adventure treated death of Arisen NPCs as something easy and lasting, where it should be the opposite. There are still cool bits in there to repurpose for your games, such as philosophies and morally dodgy plans Mummies may follow, but you must read those books with their shortcomings in mind.

      One of the most important books setting-wise was Book of the Deceivced. It gives a deep glimpse in the sixth Guild, who are thematically brilliant antagonists, and it also reveals a (biased) history of Irem and Shan'iatu. It has great things in it, but on the flipside, some sections of the book are so poetic, they become icomprehensible. The subfaction writeups are most culpable of this. Lore of the Deceived is a collection of powers and Relics. They're intended for the Deceived, but you can still use them for your game and the supplement is free, so you should just go ahead and grab it.

      The closest Mummy has to Requiem Clanbooks (in-world documents) is Dreams of Avarice. It's written from the point of view of the Heretic - one of few Mummies to free themselves of the Judges. It offers a (biased) mythology of the Judges and Gods, and Heretic's life experiences. If you liked the Clanbooks, you should give this one a try.

      Cursed Necropolis: DC and Cursed Necropolis: Rio, are setting books placed in the aforementioned cities. The contain plot hooks, NPCs, Relics, Utterances and other stuff useful for game masters. I remember reading and enjoying the latter. Sothis Ascends is similar, but offers historical settings at the time of Sothic Turns. You get to see first-hand what the Sha Amkhata (from Book of Long Death) were about.

      Curse of the Blue Nile is an anthology of stories.

              I'm happy to be of help!

              Be aware that as far as Sothis Ascends is concerned, the earliest possible Sothic Turn is in 2371 BCE Egypt - still 1461 years after Irem. Cultural differences between the two societies are a prominent point of that setting. If you want to focus in Irem itself, Book of the Deceived and Dreams of Avarice are your best choice.

              There is also some Mummy material in Dark Eras books, but I don't remember which of them fit your requirements. The Fall of Isireion (from Dark Eras: Companion) contains some seeds of the modern setting, so I assume this one. There are also:

              The Ruins of Empire (Dark Eras)
              Princes of the Conquered Land (Dark Eras: Companion​) - Mummy/Mage crossover
              Rise of the Last Imperials (Dark Eras 2​)​
              The Scandinavian Witch Trials (Dark Eras 2​) - Mummy/Sin-Eater crossover
              The Reign of Terror (Dark Eras 2​) - Mummy/Demon/Vampire crossover​​

                      Hi Spacedigger. So I'm going to answer this question coming from the perspective of an old grognard that really liked 1e as a whole a lot and has overall felt fairly lukewarm towards 2e supplements. Don't mistake me, I think there are some very good products coming out of second edition, but if I'm running a Chronicles game I'm using 1e's system and adapting whatever I need to from 2e.

                      For Mummy in particular the difference between the two editions is breadth versus depths. 2nd Edition Mummy presents fairly robust amount of material for a limited page count- in addition to the major Guilds and Decrees we get some nice options for Mionor Guilds, rules for sorcerers, the incorporation of characters from Wod: Immortals, more details on the denizens of Duat, a closer look at (some) of the Judges and rules for how they manifest on the Earth, etc. There's a lot of ground covered in that Book, and especially with regard to the Judges and sorcerers and underworld horror 2nd edition really delivers. But I tend to regard it as an expansion of 1e material rather than a replacement. The main reason for this is that due to page count in general, delays in new supplements and no sign of more material on the horizon any time soon 2nd edition sought to cover a wide range of topics for the setting and had a fairly limited amount of time to discuss each of those topics individually. First edition in contrast discusses fewer topics but goes into greater detail about what it does discuss. Guilds feel like the ancient societies whose traditions have withstood the test of time that give a Mummy that much needed sense of familiarity against the alien world in which they arise. Relics are presented with a unique history that gives a sense of importance to the object beyond a campaign mcguffin. More Affinities and Utterances are on offer. And while I continue to appreciate the exploration given to some of the Judges in second edition the downside is that the majority of them aren't mentioned at all. First edition offers only a brief description of each Judge, but it covers ALL OF THEM, and as a bonus offers the player an Affinity favored by that Judge for free. A similar situation applies to Immortals. Need rules for incorporating them into the Mummy setting? 2e has you covered. If you'd like more depth regarding each of those immortals and a couple extra ones Mummy has not yet featured, check out the 1e book focused on them.

                      There are some changes between editions, some I like, some I don't, most I'm indifferent about. The Judges being able to pop into the real world and cause an eldritch horror style mini-apocalypse was easily the best thing 2e introduced. The minor guilds are all right but nothing I'm over the moon about. Sorcerers were always hinted at but never given proper rules in 1e (mostly being a threat due to their ability to create and control with some difficulty Greater Amkhata). Speaking of, Amkhata in 1e were treated somewhat differently- there were no spirit-esque ranking systems involved, just lesser Amkhata (created as abominations born from the reckless destruction of a Relic) and Greater Amkhata (created from a specific blasphemous ritual of a sorcerer like a ghostly frankenstein's monster). I really didn't care for the way 2e turned them into another semi-spirit denizen of Twilight that can reproduce on its own- I infinitely prefer them to remain the products of foolish occult accidents and reckless half mad sorcerers conjuring up abominations. Shades didn't exist in 1e, any interactions Mummies had with the spectral dead were presumed to be with the default ghosts of the Core book. That's another thing I find disappointing about 2e- they really shied away from involving the restless dead to any significant degree, including stripping out a lot of the necromantic-based Utterances and the extra Numina ghosts had to play with. Mummy the Curse took a lot of inspiration from old WOD Wraith the Oblivion and Orpheus and it was disappointing to see the role Ghosts play in the setting being pushed aside to the degree it was (I guess rules wise only the Sin-Eaters gets to play in that sand box going forward). This is now officially nit pick territory- I'd have liked an expansion on the ghost mechanics for Mummy, and if anything use them rather than the Amkhata as the basis for representing Lifeless horrors from Duat.

                      Anyway this is officially too long winded. As a brief comment, I'd say don't worry about the way the setting is presented in the city books and Guildhalls of the Deathless. I've never really agreed with the opinions of some here that those books don't fit the setting given the constraints Mummies work under. Guildhalls says from the start that the focus of the book is on Nomes where the Guilds play a central role and the mummy population is compartively large (large being a relative term, Washington DC had maybe twenty mummies living there, but considering each Mummy tends to have a few dozen to a few hundreds cultists attending them at any given time the impact they have on a region is far broader than their population would suggest). 1e broke Mummy Chronicles down into a series of frameworks, one of which involved a single mummy and various members of his cult, another involving a multitude of Mummies in competition, another involving them in cooperation, etc. I would suggest viewing Guildhalls and some of the other supplements as discussing Nomes and regions of dense Mummy populations, where competing cults, merets and the ambitions of the local Guildhalls inspires cultists to perform the Call more often than they would if left in a remote area with just their sleeping patron to worry about. Likewise, Timelessness was not layed out precisely as it appears in 2e, but there were rules for having 'Flashbacks' to previous descents that could be played out in real time during the session. Also, Sothis Ascends, the book focusing on the various Sothic Turns that Mummies experienced introduced optional rules that more or less laid the groundwork for what became Timelessness. Immortals and Sorcerers are introduced into the 1e setting in Dark Eras Fall of Iserion. There's really very little between edition that is wholly incompatible in that regard- what 1e did as a touch upon option 2e fully expands and fleshes out.
