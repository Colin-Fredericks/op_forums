    Judges and the Arisen

    06-20-2015, 07:23 PM
    I'm fuzzy on the relationship between a mummy and her Judge. Is she the one being judged, or does she seek out those who deserve judgement? Have I simply overlooked the part of the core book that talks about this?

      The book isn't as explicit as it could be, but I believe it's pretty clear that the Arisen seeks out those who deserve Judgement. When an Arisen experienced the Rite of Return, she descended into Duat and stood before the Judges. Each of the 42 tested her, shredding her soul with every encounter. The Final Judge touched the core of her being, her Defining Pillar, and deemed her worthy of being its servant for all time.

      So, in other words, the Arisen has already been judged. One of the ways of earning a Descent reset is to do something your Judge approves of, and generally speaking exacting a harsh judgement on someone who has committed a sin against your specific Judge seems like a fine way to earn that reset.

          The Mummies are the servants of the 42 Judges. Mind you, its left deliberately vague who's the actual Judge they're following - could be old gods, could be the ascended Shan'itu (spelling?). But the Mummies are definitely the servant-slaves who are tasked with world domination through recreating Irem.

          In a way, Mummy is a game where you're playing a zombie raised by a necromancer and sent to do their bidding, like any other fantasy necromancer-zombie relationship save for the fact the necromancer is a godling. There's the temptation to play the necromancer-god on a smaller scale yourself with the cult, and the potential to become free again through Apothesis.

              I think Mummies are mostly a little fuzzy on the relationship, too, as they tend to be about most things.                       Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
                      I like the idea that the Arisen serve a Judge whose crime/sin they're guilty of, but that's just one interpretation and I would never try to say that it's canon.

                      I think that's a good way to go with it. After all, there's got to be some reason why a particular Judge chooses a particular Mummy; they don't seem like the kind of beings that act randomly or on a whim. Perhaps when the other judges accuse a Mummy of a crime of which he is guilty, it tears at the Mummy's soul because he feels remorse at what he's done and accepts his punishment. When he meets his final judge, however, he stands accused of a crime which he doesn't believe he has committed, or which he refuses to repent, and he instead makes his decree in an effort to justify himself.

                      For instance, a Mummy passes before 41 judges, before at last finding herself standing before Akhi, the Roar. It accuses her of giving voice, during a fit of rage, to blasphemous curses against one of the Shan'iatu who had overseen her guild. Though exhausted by her ordeals, she hardens her heart, telling the Judge that her passion was what defined her and gave her strength, and that she will not apologize for it. Akhi offers an enigmatic nod of his terrible head, and before she can figure out if he approves or disapproves of her defense, she is jarred into wakefulness by her first Descent.

                                                      Personal Cosmology Warning - what I say below has to do my head cannon, nothing official.

                                                      While I don't quite define Duat, I do define the Judges as the Old Gods, similar to the ones kicked out of the Supernal by the Exarchs. As of 1e, the Supernal was a critical part of the flow of souls between the material world, and the spiritual. When someone was born, their soul came from these heavenly realms, lived in the flesh, died, and then their soul traveled back to the Supernal. Duat, as the land where souls traveled to, was something related to this cycle. As well, the Supernal is big on the whole True Name thing, which is a fairly big theme in Mummy as well, with how Utterances work, and all. Destiny is also tied into the Supernal, different from the Wyrd and God Machine's version of Fate.

                                                      So, the sorcerer-kings and Judges are the Old Gods. When the Shan'iatu tried to become Judges, they ended up becoming corrupted things, as if tainted by the Lower Depths or Abyss. Thus, the failing of their powers over time as it was sucked out, until they effectively became twisted demon lords known as the Judges of Death, who are hungry for control of the world.

                                                      This ties into the theme of Mummy - power is slavery, as their lords and master are effectively slavish archdevils.
