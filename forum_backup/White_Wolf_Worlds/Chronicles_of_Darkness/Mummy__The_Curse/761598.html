      We don't know what the Judges are, but there could be two sets of them.

      Anpu is one of the gods. He was ruler of the dead until Azar took his throne; he now acts as a sort of shepherd for those on the way to Duat.

      Not sure which maw you're talking about.

      MtC is a game that plays very well with cosmic horror; individual insignificance against the magnitude of the ages (to say nothing of the stars) is a major theme in the game. As for specific monsters, we have someone who sounds very much like Nyarlathotep appearing alongside the Herald of Forgotten Stars, we have the Ochre-Masked King arising every thousand years, and we have the Black Huriyah as angels who steal Sekhem and orbit dead black stars.

          The Judges are a mystery yet to be answered, although the "original" Judges (unless they're the only Judges, as atamajakki says there is a possibility of there being two sets but it's hardly set in stone) are emanations of Fate and Law. The Book of Going Westward is something I would commit terrible sins to get. Maybe we'll get a glimpse in Dreams of Avarice? Personally, I like the theory that the original Judges are the same ones the Arisen think they serve, but that the Arisen's leashes are actually still held by their former Guildmasters in some manner. Certainly, a few quirks of the mechanics make it seem like the Shan'iatu are in charge as much as, if not more than, the Judges.

          Anpu's existence actually raises some extra questions. He and Azar are the only gods we have "first person" witnesses to, and only Anpu has been independently verified. For symmetry's sake, you would assume that this means that the other four Nameless Gods (Re, Esit, Ptar, and Sutek) exist, and yet Esit appears as a mortal human and the Shan'iatu self-identify as Sutek, although whether that is because they are associating themselves with the myth of Sutek in terms of a parable or whether they mean it in a more literal sense is entirely up in the air.

          The giant maw seems to be Ammut, or something physically connected to her.

          There are so many Lovecraftian horrors in Mummy's sourcebooks that, when I started making up my own and throwing them at my players, they thought I was pulling them out of the Storyteller section or a book they hadn't read yet

                  1) The original Judges of Life are the the living shadows of the Law, the natural order of things. The Arisen may or may not serve them. They created the Shan'iatu to teach humanity the ways of the five powers and of the Law so that they may find their way into A'aru where the Judges dwell. No one knows for sure if they're in charge now or not, since the Shan'iatu went out of their way to fuck things up, ending with 35 of them trying to become Judges of Death and 7 of them becoming Judges of Life and Death. Creation's a mess, so we don't know whether the Judges of Life are running around or if it's the Shan'iatu.

                  2) Anpu is one of the five Nameless Beasts, assumedly the highest of gods within Iremite mythology (and assumedly, the entire fallen world and maybe more, based on when you think they came into play and how they scale on the matters). Specifically, he is the Nameless Jackal, divinity of the Sheut(shadow), who once ruled over Duat until the Shan'iatu fucked everything up by killing Azar, God of Life, and sent him to reign over the dark lands(and possibly over the rest of reality-Nomenclature is a bitch). Now he is a psychopomp, the Opener of the Way whose dominion is ghostly Twilight.

                  3) The maw was a manifestation of Ammut with which the Shan'iatu made payment to receive her assistance for the Rite of Return. It worked-the Arisen do not suffer the loss of Sekhem in the way others do. Downside is, Ammut still gets to eat with her sacrifices turned Shuankhsen.

                  4) The Lovecraftian things are demons and divinities in the Iremite culture, things assumedly subjugated by the Shan'iatu time and time again. They fit in with the rest of the cosmic horrors that Mummy deals with.

                          Originally posted by Arcanist View Post
                          I think the Deceived in the first chapter of Book of the Deceived outright says that when the Remet asked them "are you our gods" they knew that the other gods were beings who had 'strayed from Anpu's herd'. So that implies that the other gods may exist.
                          "Anpu's herd" refers, I have always assumed, to the human souls he shepherds into the afterlife. Remember that we have multiple sources stating that, before the Shan'iatu, the people who lived by the River practiced ancestor worship and were ruled over by ghosts and demons. Note also that the Shan'iatu answer in the negative when asked that question, at least in part, because they're a whole lot mightier than the afterimages left behind by dead humans.

                          As for whether or not the other Nameless Gods connect in any way to the cosmology of another game line, that's not something I'm inclined to speculate on. I have always found it odd though that the Nameless Jackal is certainly a thing, and a thing that predates the Shan'iatu, whereas the Nameless Falcon is "named" for the mortal lover of a Shan'iatu sacrificed to send Azar to the underworld. It doesn't quite ring true that only some Decrees would be represented among the gods, but there is no evidence for any of them besides Anpu. Weird.

                          But on the subject of how screwed over the world is though, let's bear in mind that the djed, the Spine of the World that connected the Judges to the mortal world, was inside Azar and dropped into Duat with him. That the original Judges of Life may have become something darker as a consequence doesn't seem unlikely.

                                          Originally posted by reseru View Post
                                          The lesson here is that, unlike detractors would have people believe, there are mysteries in the game's mythology and not every question gets a thorough answer despite their claims that there's too much backstory (which they mistake for metaplot)
                                          I could understand that kind of belief when it was just the core book (or maybe the core book + Guildhalls) when it did read a lot like the Storyteller section and the Pale Brood's write-up were suggesting that the Shan'iatu just became the Judges. I do feel like anyone still holding that opinion just can't have read the Book of the Deceived though, because it punches so many holes in so many theories that I sometimes have a hard time deciding on what I think it all means anymore.

                                          I think, based on what evidence we have now, that the Judges in the core book are indeed the Judges of Life described by the Deceived. Even the Shan'iatu thought their parents were cosmic horrors, after all, and reference is made to the Judges of Life descending to Duat to judge the Remet. However, certain pieces of evidence (most notably, that Arisen who served the same Shan'iatu can't wield magic against one another) suggests that the original Shan'iatu hold the chains of the Arisen, not the Judges, and that the Shan'iatu have taken Irem into Duat where they use the Sekhem of the relics sent to them to preserve their kingdom down there. That ties up the cosmology about as neatly as I can manage, but even then there are still flaws. Stupid Pale Brood write-up.
