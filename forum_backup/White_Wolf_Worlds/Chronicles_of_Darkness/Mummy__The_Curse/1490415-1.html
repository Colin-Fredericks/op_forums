    So we can get some hype up, let's give a run down of what is in the book and what's my take on it.

    As a broad explanation, the Book of Lasting Death is mostly a wide variety of alternative characters, including enemies but more often in the 'frain of deliciously swiss army knife for casting, all of them handily filling in as foil, adversary, and ally (Yes, even the Deceived) as well as being imminently playable. There's definitely some welcome world building, and definitely some new toys for people to play with, but I would still by and large look at it from that inital point of view, and I would still hew it being a hair bit more for Storytellers than Players-not that players can't or won't get anything out of (again, pretty much everything here is playable), but it's definitely more useful in the toolbox.

    Chapter 1 is dedicated to the Minor Guilds, and I will go over them in brief.

    The inclusion of the Akhem-Urtu, aka the Decieved, into the category of the Minor Guilds has always been something that causes me to squint in consternation, but while the argument can still be made that doing so downplays one of the best organized dark-mirror antagonists of Chronicles, most other concerns can be put to rest regarding their entry here. Sure, it doesn't have the space to serve diehard fans the way they might want, but it very neatly and summarily reintroduces the Restless Stars in a very satisfying way that communicates their Big Deal-ness as much as humanly possible in the circumstances. If that sounds like a backhanded knock, it's not, it's a legitimately really good reintroduction of the middle class liberal arts major boogeymen, and in some ways it's actually an improvement, particularly when it comes to mechanics. Showiness is not a virtue for an antagonist defined by subversion of cosmic desire, exploitation of memory, and in general being the boogeyman, but if there was something lacking from the Decieved, it was the power of iconics in their reveal, and BoLD delivers very well on that front. The new Utterance, Color From Void, may hew a little toward's the Painter's verbiage, enough so that people who know what I'm talking about probably wonder how it works for any of the other six Temakhs, but it's otherwise a very good power to evoke the Deceived, and the Nameless Things of the third tier in particular is just utterly delicious as a minion type antagonist. Wielder of Names is positively elegant for how simply and directly it addresses the Ren-Hekau and how it's used to make a big deal out of the Deceived, and I don't say that as a slam towards previous attempts, it's just honestly the best delivered take on the subject. Even the affinity As Whispers In The Night, this edition's Face of Endless Lies, feels much more brand appropriate, since the edtion's overall move from the "mind is the body is the mind so all Mental Flaws are accompanied by Physical Flaws" means that this one can make it more like an affectation of the name, of the way one gets the information about a person, rather than simply be a doppelganger power, and that it twists Fate to protect the Embodiment of Vision so casually goes a long way to make them feel powerful in that low-key, behind the scenes way.

    I also had some hesitation about Military Guild Kher-Minu, because the way Mummy handled the subject of the Nameless Empire's military appealed to me on a lot of layers and in a lot of different ways, and making them some kind of Guild, let alone Arisen, threatened that, but worry not--the second-string engine of the empire not only retains the form of address that made them interesting (and true) previously in their capacity, but also make for an interesting and fickle character type, whose bloodlust and only-in-some-ways well-earned bitterness towards the Major Guilds contrasted with a thorough understanding of their role and the reasoning makes them a really vivacious character type to read and implement in a number of ways. Their powerset is also just very deeply satisfying, with Dust Beneath Feet and Guardian Wrath being innate just being solid decision making, Butcher Demon Transfiguration is, as mentioned, the best kind of anime bullshit, and in an instance where I'm glad systems glitched so I could read Korogra, Tread the Crimson Field Eternal is also the best Doom/boomer-shooter-esque bullshit as well. Honestly enjoyed the military way more than I expected to. Certainly the justification to have chararcter who takes "Mercy is a mistake/ Peace was never an option" as actual response as interesting character, whether fucking hey-shit-you've-got-a-point or (as I take more often) fucking shit-why-are-you-like-this.

    We'll pick up tomorrow, shall we?

                Per 2e core, the temakh is "an alien and corrupted beast of fluid form," and the mechanical change in how Sybaris works in general has translated the Deceived's specific twist on it into imposing a two-dice penalty on rolls to resist Sybaris in their presence as people sense the strangeness about them; the Maar-Kherit have picked up the slack on particularly overt supernatural trouble, being mechanically incapable of inflicting Sybaris less intense than Terror Sybaris.

                Likewise mentioned by Arc, my two PC Decieved are a Musician and a Philosopher. Would they still use a visual medium for the Decieved Utterance? Or could a recording of music or a spoken arguement work?
                The first Tier of Color from Void, at least, affects a Crafts or Expression roll to create a work of art, and the way it works is such that you can probably substitute the appropriate sense for "see" in the Tier's description. The other two Tiers are more specifically visually-oriented, but the Deceived's 2e mechanics are less particular and granular about the artforms they deal with; their single-point Pillar recovery method just involves inspiring another character to create something, and the sidebar on Temakh Concepts mostly suggests using the temakh's artform to inform goals and the types of people they surround themselves with.
