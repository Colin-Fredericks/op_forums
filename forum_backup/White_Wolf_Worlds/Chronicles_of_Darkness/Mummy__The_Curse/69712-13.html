            I think what's frustrating about Mummy isn't the fact that it's late - any book can have bad luck. It's that the community appears to have zero effect on development. Honestly, that's how most games are developed, but I've gotten really used to how the other Onyx Path games are made.

            I can make all sorts of suggestions on most other Onyx Path projects, and be reasonably sure that the devs will consider them. They might reject them, or even decide that they need to make the counterpoint even stronger, but we have a collective effect in open development. With Mummy, the community isn't usually asked anything at all. I know RichT isn't into pushing an unwilling dev into open dev, and I respect that.

            Maybe the reason for the secrecy is just the nature of Mummy's design, where the storyteller knows all the secrets and the players learn as their characters memory raises. I don't really know.

            I have high hopes for Deceived, though and if it ends up being good, I'll make some noise, do what I can to spread the word.

                                                First off, let me say that Mummy stuff is in the pipe, though there have been hiccups as there often are not only with individual lines, but the significantly escalated business of Onyx Path itself. Part of this ramp up has included the announcement and release of a bunch of new games and books in rapid succession. Mummy looks submerged, but keep in mind we're a year and a half in, just as other lines are ramping up. Beyond that, CAS is a pretty busy guy who works with other games, fiction and music.

                                                I don't want to put words in CAS' mouth but my feeling is that Mummy's combined releases are really designed for a unified look at a work of epic scope with immortals, a lost civilization and its gods. At the same time CAS believes strongly in collaboration, took in a huge amount of input at various stages from you, and it informs the collaboration that takes place between writers, but all with the aim of making something as uncompromising about its core ideas as possible. Maybe in a perfect world Mummy would have been released in 2015 in three 800 page volumes of a single, titanic work, but nobody can afford to produce or really purchase that and besides, this would make the option of taking what you want from the game a tad more difficult.

                                                            Originally posted by reseru View Post
                                                            This is something I intend on being very conscious of, with the first few sessions to be very corebook-only. My PC isn't going to have any character creation options beyond what was initially presented, and the plot is just a basic relic hunt. The core book is absolutely usable, while admittedly thin, but my entire campaign arc was formed before Guildhalls or DC were released.                                                             This is an attitude I can't wrap my head around. As an antagonist book, the Book of the Deceived will naturally be somewhat different, but there are elements in Guildhalls of the Deathless that a high Guild Status player character should know before the game even begins. There's no reason such a character shouldn't be manifesting Guild-specific Affinities or Utterances from the get-go, and I don't see how letting them do so in any way detracts from the game or its themes. I can't think of any such powers that "fundamentally" alter the game either, or in any way dilute the themes present in the corebook (Talismans do somewhat, but that aside...).

                                                            The additional elements presented in Guildhalls add depth to the most basic elements of Mummy society, and I feel should absolutely be taken into effect when constructing a nome. And if it makes sense for a character to have access to them, it feels natural to allow it.
