                                          There's so much awesome in here.

                                          Conceptually, it makes sense that the Sixth Guild would focus on art and inspiration, given the very material focuses of the other Guilds. It makes it seem like the Sixth Guild was almost the heart and soul of the Nameless Empire, investing it with meaning.

                                          I LOVE how much personality the Temakh seem to have, especially compared to the Judges, who aren't really fleshed out all that much (presumably because Arisen don't really interact with the Judges until they Descend). The Temakh really come across as characters in their own rights, to the point that it's surprising to see the apparent antipathy between factions in the Deceived (the Dream-Eater seems to REALLY not like the Philosopher).

                                          Man, the hints in here about what was really going on in the Nameless Empire, and why the Sixth Guild was cast down, are intriguing. It looks to me like the Shan'iatu leading the Five Guilds conspired to set up the Judges in place of the existing gods of Duat, possibly with the promise of being able to live forever themselves in the afterlife, and that the Shan'iatu of the Sixth Guild had a real problem with that (except maybe for the Philosopher). Fascinating call-backs to Mage, if that's true!

                                          I see vague homages to Changeling in here as well. The mummies of the Dream-Eater appear to want to foster true inspiration, and then consume the fruits of that inspiration such that the world loses out on a potentially great work of art (presumably the Dream-Eater consumes the inspiration as sustenance or something). That's awfully dark. It gives the Deceived a nice bite; I'm glad that they apparently are themselves monstrous.
