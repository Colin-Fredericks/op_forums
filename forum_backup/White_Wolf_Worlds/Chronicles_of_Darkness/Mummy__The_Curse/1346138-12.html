                    Originally posted by TyrannicalRabbit
                    It's somewhat why I was worried about the constant reference to things like the meteor shower Utterance in lead ups. Utterances are more self contained than some other powers in the cofd but right now mummies just feel like the earthquakes and meteor shower splat and that carries a lot of problems for a number of reasons.
                    Actually, I need to nitpick the Earthquake power. Because for me it is by far the biggest letdown. The text pitches this as a miraculous power of vast destruction, which is certainly is, unfortunately the time to cast it is measured in DAYS. Even with good rolls you are looking at a 48 hour period to cast this Utterance (which, IMO, kinda goes against the Words of Power feel of Utterances). Not only that, but you have to be at the center of the quake, so there's no using this to shake down an adversaries lair from afar.

                    Powers like this worry me because of how punishing they can be for a player who picked them. Having to wait out your character's big Utterance moment can be really disappointing, especially when the other players are calling down meteors, driving people insane with the winds and reviving Ghengis Khan. Seriously, I have run CofD for ten years now, hundreds of hours, dozens of stories all with different scales and tones, and I cannot think of a single time at which the story or characters could stand sit still for the 48+ hour cast time.

                    This power has got to be the poster child for the "breaks on" feel of Utterances.

                        Originally posted by TyrannicalRabbit
                        I can't speak for others but I dropped my pledge down for the time being and I am seriously considering bowing out entirely at this point.
                        I'm leaving room for Mummy to surprise me, but as it currently stands, I am hovering around here. I would say that for my money, I'm disappointed at how conservative the changes to Mummy in general have been. Your mileage may vary, of course, but I considered Mummy to be one of the weakest of the 1e gamelines, despite respecting some bold choices it made like the unique relationship of the Arisen to their Sekhem. The nonlinear relationship to time is a cool new wrinkle the new edition adds, and I respect the added attention to cults and the demolishment of the wall between player and Storyteller knowledge (and, arguably, Storyteller and developer knowledge) 1e had, as well as the addition of fiends from the Duat that seems to be implied by the Appendix. But I think the basic structure of the Mummy template and experience needed a reenvisioning and focused overhaul on the level of Geist 2e's Bound or moreso, and what I see so far is more of an update and a little polishing. Some nice appetizers added to the menu, but little change to the recipe of the main dish.

                        I don't really expect this to be a popular position on the Mummy subforum, of course, but I just felt one post of feedback was worth being heard.

                                    In this regard I think they had a difficult job in front of them. A newly awakened Mummy must be terrifying enough that no group of mortals would ever dream of standing their ground against it, while not so overwhelming that a plucky Brendan Fraser & Rachel Weisz cannot mount a daring escape. That's a tough line to walk. Most 2e lines resolve the Mortals vs Monsters problem by rewarding ambush tactics, the far off sniper for the mage, the house fire for the vampire, etc. This straight up does not work on Mummies, whom for the most part are unkillable by mortal means (save fire, and even that'll at best knock them into a cycle). So in that regard Mummies are fairly special, they won't necessarily kill you on contact, but you absolutely cannot stop them.

                                    Edit: Thaaat said, I certainly wouldn't complain if the devs decided to rip off the brakes and make the Arisen outrageous (like Gauru form. 1e: heal a lethal each turn! 2e: HEAL EVERYTHING! EVERY TURN! AND PEOPLE JUST DIE! RAAAAAWR!!!)

                                        Originally posted by TyrannicalRabbit

                                        I disagree in parts and agree in parts. Themetically it wasn't the weakest game line. It knew what it wanted to be and the collective books under the at the time limited book run format realized it's internal mythology.

                                        Even the understandable complaints about the insularity of Mummy were not really about Mummy's world itself but about trying to use it's bits with other things. In comparison things like Werewolf and Vampire laboured under the shadows of the WoD before breaking out spectacularly in 2e. I admit some bias. After all it was one of the if not the favourite 1e line for me. But I think at times criticisms of it fail to touch on problems of it's infrastructure rather than the clash of preference and presentation.

                                        Where I think 1e Mummy had it's pitfalls are elsewhere. The well mentioned st player knowledge separation. It's tendency to talk about in world things and never following up on them like the Scroll of Ages, Cosmic keyword, Sekhem Sorcery, etc.

                                        Some of the above was mechanical in addition to being setting retalization failures. But even beyond them there were mechanical issues. Vice and Virtue from 1e fit Mummy extremely poorly. Memory being both it's own thing and a morality stat. The power of Affinities and Utterances fluxated wildly with a few having three straight tiers of "Why ever take this." etc. Even Decieved which continued the corebook and Guildhall's fantastic flavour work for things like Utterances and Affinities had a few. Or in some cases had things like Affinities that looked like something that should have been core utility effects for all mummies that were cut for space from the core. And I'd still argue an incredibly restrictive fuel stat in Pillars that gets spread far too thin regardless of if you are newly awake or about to sleep. And even where we see potent powers done in a relatively satisfying way
                                        It tends to highlight even more so where it trips up. In my experience this can lead to players feeling like things are xp sinks and traps.

                                        2e is bringing some amazing things but where it is tripping over itself it feels like it's following in the footsteps of where 1e did.
                                        I would ever so slightly disagree on the variance in Affinities and Utterances. There were a couple of Utterances I would never use, but in the context of Chronicles of Darkness games, 1e and 2e alike, that number was honestly perishingly low. Mummy 1e did an excellent job of balancing its grand powers I think, making very nearly every Utterance a compelling choice. Affinities were more hit and miss, but still erring towards the solid I thought.

                                        But yes, I agree that where 2e appears to be going wrong is that it is mostly reproducing the more niggly problems from 1e, and compounding that by having perishingly few new ideas of its own. I have a player from my Mummy game who has been pretty excited to hear about the new character creation options in 2e, and I... honestly feel like I have to tell them that they could frankly be better off building a character with the 1e Corebook. You get more Utterances to choose from (let alone once expansions get factored in), some of them are honestly stronger than their new versions.

                                        It gets worse from a meta perspective. In 1e, where the other splats were tamer, Arisen felt dramatically more impressive than they do in 2e. I'm looking at what a Sekhem 10 Arisen can do upon waking and generally feeling like a new Werewolf or Promethean character could be built to cover most of the same bases. Sure, they won't be throwing meteors or summoning Earthquakes, but those are just two powers on the list and what if you don't want "city wide destruction" to be the only selling point of your splat?

                                        This is a random tangent, but are we meant to be impressed by the meteors from Secrets Ripped From Skies doing Sekhem in Aggravated damage to structures? Won't buildings typically have well into the double digits in Structure? I can't help but feel like this number would need to be a great deal higher to actually level a building.
