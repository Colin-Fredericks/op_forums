                        Oh look. Atamajakki dismissed an entire concept out of hand. It must be Tuesday.

                        I do apologize, though. That last paragraph may have confused you. Let me clarify. The concept behind the Chronicle isn't about Irem returning per se. Its about the threat (or as some Mummies would view it, the hope) that it would return. The Shan'iatu are certainly desperate to break free from Duat. Hounded and tormented as they are, they still have enough power to influence the mortal realm in small ways. So they call to their servants, those still willing to listen. They find unlikely allies in the Shuankhsen, for the Devourer has something to gain by Irem's return as well. The Chronicle is about the realization, through encounters with creatures from the deepest reaches of Duat and the restoration of Iremite ruins (one pillar at a time) that someone is calling Irem back to life, in the process causing the doorways into Duat to swing open and let out horrors from the dawn of time. The mummies will need to decide whether to listen to their Judges and put a stop to these groups or aid them in their efforts.

                        If the vampires ignore the signs and do nothing, the Strix will continue to nest and multiply around their Havens until one night every vampire in the city is nothing but a host and plaything for them. If the werewolves ignore the signs and do nothing the idigam will warp the Shadow and the material world to suit their alien natures. This chronicle is no different in that regard- there is the threat of a significant game changer on the horizon, and the PCs have an opportunity to prevent it.

                                        My beef with your proposed Irem Chronicle is that Mummy is, at its core, a game about clinging to the past. The Arisen fancy themselves the divine servants and heralds of the greatest civilization in history... when actually they're the stooges working for an empire that (while powerful) has been dead for five thousand years and completely forgotten. Their gods do not speak to them, their Judges only interact with them by punishing them in the abstract, they've lost the ability to create the Relics that formed the core of their Guilds, and are essentially doomed to spend the rest of eternity as amnesiac slaves going through the motions of something they barely remember but think is important. That the "good ending" of Mummy - Apotheosis - involves casting off the shackles of Irem's legacy and finally becoming a free agent with your identity intact is telling as to the core themes of the game; letting go of the distant past and learning to be yourself once again.

                                                How do I put this. I think you have some valid points with regard to your interpretation and I appreciate how closely you've examined the themes of the game. I just disagree with several of your conclusions.

                                                I think the lessons of the game are very much NOT that the past is invalid. If anything, I think the premise suggests that one should abandon blind faith in favor of truth and knowledge- accepting the past as it was rather than what one idealizes and wishes it had been. The game suggests that this pursuit is not easy. In fact, it suggests it can be quite lethal. Pursuing the past means violating ancient tombs and awakening the vengeful Arisen. It means calling down curses from buried artifacts and being driven mad by Sybaris. And of course, for the Arisen pursuing memory endangers and shortens their time on Earth. Like the roots of the Mummy horror story and the Cosmic Horror of HP Lovecraft, the game offers a theme that the past holds alien horrors waiting for the modern age to stumble upon them. But far from being invalid, I would say Irem remains extremely significant today despite being forgotten by the outside world. Cults great and small still pursue the agendas of the Judges (or use their knowledge for their own ends). Mummies still walk the Earth, carrying the old magic with them. Worse, the servants of the Devourer are still active upon the world, as are the rituals to create Amkhata. There's also unknown factors at play here- does the Sekhem no longer flow to Duat? Does the Devourer no longer taste the souls of those Judged unworthy? How about the nameless demons waiting for their Purpose as per the Dreams of Avarice? Or the other races and things from the dawn of time sprinkled throughout the line? The past isn't depicted so much as irrelevant as incredibly dangerous to the modern world. But unlike Lovecraft (but still in keeping with the Mummy horror story) knowledge of the old past isn't futile (though it can break the mind) but essential. Mummies, having lived in those times, are the best equipped to deal with the terrors from those eras (the Amkhata, for instance).

                                                I don't see the idea undermining the setting. I see it as an extension of already existing elements- from the Purpose the Judges assign to the threat of the Shuankhsen and Amkhata. Also, and apologies for scratching at this particular pet peeve, but could we lose the whole 'superhero' characterization? First off, the game as is has the characters battling Shuankhsen and Deceived already, and there are bound to be plenty of instances in which a mummy is called up to protect the cult or save someone or something important to his followers. Those are perfectly valid stories to tell (and indeed are much more the focus of other WOD games like Werewolf, Geist, Changeling, etc). Second, let's be frank- the WOD isn't about superheroes, its about horror. The grim dark secret is that those two genres overlap in several ways. Confronting the strange and bizarre with the intent to put an end to whatever its goals are and protect the victims of its depravity? Yep, common ground. The difference is that in horror you usually don't choose to fight your battles. Rather than being the hero that goes out and confronts evil, its the evil that comes to confront you. Your choice becomes whether to die or to survive- and survival means beating the thing that is hounding you. Mummies OFTEN find themselves squaring off against things without any choice, either because it entered their tombs and awoke them or because their cult called them to battle. Pitting your characters against some other horror is not the only way to tell a scary story, I'll give you that. But I submit it is neither an invalid approach to horror (I would argue it is very much a classic of the genre) or antithetical to the setting of Mummy: The Curse.

                                                    I think there's a big difference here. The game is about the past, rediscovering it and confronting it. There's a gulf between a Mummy unleashing an ancient demon in a sand-buried tomb and then saying the City of Pillars itself plops itself onto the earth as the gates of Duat flood open and the Shan'iatu openly bark orders at the Deathless.

                                                    The first scenario is about how desperately the past tries to be relevant. *cough* one of the erm...Dark Eras for Mummy *ahem* is a little bit about how much just used to be around that just isn't in the modern day. The Deathless and relics are all anachronisms lost in a sea of people that don't get what the big deal is. The theme of Apotheosis is the Deathless beginning to wonder what the big deal is, too, because the powers that created them are distant, lost, imprisoned, or apathetic.

                                                    The second scenario, that you're proposing, makes those powers relevant. Don't get me wrong, that can be a really cool campaign, and honestly it could be a cool effect that happens in my Sothic Chronicle proposal - Sothic turns are the crazy time periods where, quite literally, the stars are right. Still, the game now is about how the Deathless wonder why they bother and if the powers they serve care or, hell, even exist anymore. Your proposal is a giant fucking Yes, they do exist and they're right fucking here on Youtube. That's a stark contrast - not a bad one, but a very big one.

                                                    So while admittedly interesting with its own themes and questions it raises, as the game is presented right now your proposal does seem out of theme
