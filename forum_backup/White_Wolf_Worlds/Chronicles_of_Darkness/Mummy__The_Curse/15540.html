          Mummy is about secrets. Your past is a secret. Your mission is a secret. The way out is a secret. Relic-hunting and cult management is the everyday experience, at least to start, but the meat of the game's character focus is in discovering who you were and are, what you've done, what's been done to you, and what you can do about it. It's about secrets to the extent that half the book is recommended not to be read by players.

          The titular curse of Mummy is the condition of the Arisen: the same rite that grants them eternal life traps them as eternal servants of selfish, parasitic gods. The demands of Memory are at odds with the demands of the Descent. There is a way out of this condition, to seize control of the rite for yourself, but it's a personal journey to seek.


          Personally, it's not my bag and I would pump money into the Demon Kickstarter instead, but I think that's a fair shake, thematically.

                                          the game could be encompassed by describing how it is sort of a metaphor for old age. you remember that you were once glorious--you long for the olden days, even if you don't remember them. you reminisce, trying desperately to grasp at fading threads and vanishing memories. many of the people you loved are already dead, or will soon die, including other immortals. when your friends are slowly fading and your what legacy will you leave behind for your followers? where will you lay your body to rest? do you trust those around you to take care of you when you are weak and can no longer support yourself? are you the same person, now, that you used to be? did you accomplish anything important that youve forgotten? have you done things that you now regret? what can you still do, now, to make a difference? is the afterlife you have been promised, been waiting for all your life, really there, or something you want? where did you put that thing you loved so much? how do you deal with changes to the world you just can't understand? and, really, with all of this to worry about.. was it worth it to live so long, or would it have been better to simply have died long ago? sucks getting old, doesn't it.

                                                      Your Guild Hierarch is in a vulnerable state, out of favour with the majority but supported and propped up by a clique of high ranked individuals. They have begun lashing out at anyone who seems to be building popular support or threats of a coup to remove them. Things have grown to a head of late as rumours of a senior Scribe coming to your Nome are circulating, the incumbent is worried this is a precursor to an attempt to send them to Obloquy.

                                                      This process has been going on across the span of a century and your PCs have found themselves awakening within it, unsure of their previous involvement, which side they were on, who supports what position, etc. You have to find some way of untangling the threads of politics and power and where you and everyone else fit in before someone else casts you as friend or foe to either side takes action to shatter any powerbase you may have.

                                                          One huge specific story element ive been using a ton is flashbacks. Hell, because of the playstyle and storyline of the Mummy crossover ive been running, the majority of the events are actually flashbacks as his Memory grows. Setting events in the past is perfect for Mummy, because a) you can be killed, youll just come back, so there is still danger.. and b) with faulty memory, you dont really need to figure out how much your character does or doesnt know at the time. Mummy is great for flashback scenes to important plot points or NPCs--so long as they are appropriate to his Memory score and to the mystery of forgetfulness that gives mummy its horror edge.
