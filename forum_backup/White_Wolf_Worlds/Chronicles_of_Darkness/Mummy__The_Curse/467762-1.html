    Originally posted by Piff View Post
    In point of fact, archeologists do have a pretty good idea where Ubar was, as well as several major cities that made it up (such as Saffara and what would be modern Moscha).

    Even if you don't accept that research about a specific location as valid (Ptolemy's map is pretty iffy in many respects and Ubar is called by another name), Ubar is a distinctively Arabic myth that is local to southern Saudi Arabia. The mythical city of Ubar is an old tribal legend about a ruin that supposadly is in the empty quarter, which is... again... in southern Saudi Arabia, nowhere near Ki-En-Gir.
    Ubar has been "discovered" in multiple locations, so I felt pretty free to place it generally in middle or southern Arabia, just as I felt perfectly fine changing the extent of a fictionally supernaturally-dominated Sumer/Ki-En-Gir when describing it during the sketchily understood Ubaid period, which is contemporaneous with late Irem. I mean hell, Ubar is supposed to *be* Irem, but in Mummy Irem is in Egypt. Honestly, pointing to archaeological sites as the source of this or that legend is such a dodgy practice to begin with. For instance, archaeology at times and places contemporaneous with the Bible has been significantly interfered with due to assumptions that it would be a matter of confirming this or that.

    In other words, yes, we who write this stuff, do research this stuff. It doesn't mean we'll fictionalize them the way you would have, though. Just as Irem extended beyond Egypt proper, its closest rival had a somewhat longer grasp than its historical counterpart, and due to other ahistorical factors, a much longer reach.
