            Originally posted by nothri View Post
            So an odd question. In second edition all immortals, including body thieves, gain a dot or more of Sekhem as part of their template? Does this happen immdiately upon invoking the ritual, and does it apply to the anyone whose mind is swapped (in the event the ritual switches minds rather than simply destroying the psyche of the victim, of course)? I'm especially curious about the results of an oddity mind swap such as the Brown Acid described in WoD: Immortals. I'm picturing a scene in a nightclub where a Shuankhsen gives the tainted substance to party goers and then stands back and waits for the drugged up, newly disoriented from trading minds buffet line manifests to chow down.
            Per Mummy 2e, the body abandoned by a Mentally Talented Body Thief almost immediately dies, while Magically Talented Body Thieves' theft is temporary unless they kill their old body or are transferring to a body without a soul, which indicates to me that having your mind swapped doesn't give you the Body Thief template any more than a mage casting a spell to swap your mind with somebody else's would. Oddities in the vein of the Brown Acid or Death.com have their particulars handled at Storyteller discretion; some of them might not have the template's common features.

            Having enough of a substance that creates Oddity-type Body Thieves to be able to hand it out for the sake of harvesting Sekhem feels less like an isolated scene and more like the sort of thing that kicks off and/or rounds off a storyline, particularly given that in Mummy's metaphysics there's almost definitely Sekhem already in that stuff to begin with unless some very particular ritual is going down that the dosing is the culmination of. (A power hallucinogen containing or being used to generate the energy roused to escape Suffering is, of course, very apropos.)

            At present it's firmly in the "figure out what you want it to be for your game" category, but an easy enough explanation is that rituals and other circumstances that produce a form of immortality generate the new immortal's first dot of Sekhem in the same way that a mummy pursuing Apotheosis in 1e only has Sekhem 1 — it's the Sekhem of their own soul, concentrated in such a way as facilitates their immortality as long as the conditions that enable it are maintained. For Body Thieves, that means stealing a new body before the one they're in dies, which means they necessarily have one as soon as they've performed a swap with whatever method has given them transitional immortality.

Basically that game heavily involves ghosts and the underworld, and include a drug called Pigment that lets you see ghosts and turns you into a ghost if you die while taking the drug. The plot involves a batch of Pigment getting laced with arsenic to create a large mass of ghosts that are then attacked by Spectres (which are akin to ethereal Shuankhsen after a fashion, which serve a powerful entropic force called Oblivion that parallels Ammut enough to make it easy to adapt).                                 Originally posted by Ipergigio View Post
                                Maybe this was already debated, anyway, if an Arisen loses his original body and need a new one, the new vessel has to be a living person?
                                Invested ephemeral cultists explicitly can't be used to resurrect unless they've Claimed a host, and reincarnating into a vessel that isn't one of your canopic jars requires Investment, which requires the subject to be a part of a Scorpion Cult and swearing loyalty to the mummy that they mean on some level.

                                Merging with/consuming/overpowering a cultist's soul inflicts the Memory Bleed Condition, and since the soul departs after death that necessarily implies the character must be alive for the mummy to take over their body. A case could be made for incarnating into the body of a vampire or other Invested monster resulting in no mechanical difference from taking over a mortal body — it's how the Unchained do the same basic thing calling in soul pacts with monsters — but the Memory Bleed Condition is pretty definitely written under the assumption that you're wrestling with the memories of someone that had Integrity.

                                                            Originally posted by cybirddude View Post
                                                            Can multiple mummies share a cult or can there be only one mummy per cult?
                                                            Multiple mummies can explicitly pool dots in the Cult Merit.

                                                            Also, if a mummy is subordinate to another mummy, could the superior mummy gain the benefits of their subordinate's cult mechanics wise?
                                                            Scorpion Cults' Dominance rating adds to their Arisen masters' Social rolls with members of their own guild, which makes it easier for mummies belonging to powerful cults to persuade their guildmates to direct their cults to do stuff on their behalf.

                                                            Guild Status independent of your cult's prestige isn't so much a thing anymore, so being "subordinate to" another mummy amounts to having a less powerful cult and belonging to the same guild as another mummy who says they're your boss and can back that assertion up in some way.
