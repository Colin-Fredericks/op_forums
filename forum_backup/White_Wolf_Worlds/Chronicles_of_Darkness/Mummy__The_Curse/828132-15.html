    Duly noted. Anyway, here's a work-in-progress write-up that concerns Kindred politics in the Black Land.

    -=-

    Strife Among the Damned

    The Per Henet have courts throughout the Black Land. But the Hill of Relics remains the greatest seat of their society. Here, Mekhet and Ushabti from the 42 nomes regularly convene in assembly at the necropolis, to discuss matters of the Hungry Dead as a whole. These gatherings have dictated the policies of their kind since the days of the First Dynasty, when Narmer had united Upper and Lower Kemet into one kingdom.

    However, a schism gradually forms in the last days of the Fifth Dynasty. Having built up their influence for centuries, the Kindred of Inbu-Hedj have grown strong enough to challenge the authority of Abdju's elders. They clamor for the nomes to send representatives to their city, so that they may better shape the fate of the Black Land. By the time Unas ascended to the throne, these Hungry Dead have swayed their fellows in several nomes to their side, and thus hold assemblies of their own.

    It is this state of division that the Kindred are in at the onset of the First Sothic Turn.

                        Finished writing up this little sidebar section for Abdju.

                        -=-
                        Servants of the Foremost Westerner
                        Mortals were not the only followers of the cult that sprang up in the Hill of Relics. Many of Kemet's supernatural denizens also come to revere Khenti-Amentiu, and later Azar and the Arisen in the same manner as the remet.

                        Bound to the Nameless: Many of the city’s death priests are Bound to nameless akh, souls whose Ren was lost in Duat. Rather than fade into oblivion, though, these shades forge pacts with the living to survive. And in doing so, they grant their hosts mastery over the dead that inspires awe and fear among even the Awakened of the priesthood.

                        Meetings Beneath the Hill: Since Narmer united the two lands, Kindred from all 42 nomes have met in council at the necropolis at Abdju. Their assemblies brought about the rise of the Per Henet, and laid down the laws which all Hungry Dead follow throughout their Requiems. With the schism caused by those at Inbu-Hedj, though, the House of Repose finds itself on the verge of a civil war at the onset of the Turn.

                        Those Great of Magic: Abdju’s sect of Weret-Hekau stand apart from their peers, as they hold custody of one of Kemet’s greatest Mysteries. They sensed the magic bound within their city’s sacred mummies, and their divination rituals hinted at the coming cosmic event which would animate them. When the Sothic Turn does arrive, many of these sorcerers pledge allegiance to the Arisen who come to rule their city..

                            Not sure if this was more applicable in Arcanist's Sundered World Chronicles. But I'll put it here anyway, on the assumption that the Werewolf sects from the Sundered World still persist even after all this time.

                            The Loyal (Umfinthimma) struggle to keep the memory of Pangaea alive in the centuries that followed the unthinkable. Urfarah is dead, and the ancestral home of the werewolves died with him. As the only ones among his descendants who remained faithful to the end, it is their sacred duty to bring together their scattered race. The world must be claimed by the Uratha as one people, and the kinslayers who tore down their paradise are to answer for their crime.

                            The Cull (Kazithaga) continue to curb the spread of humanity, as they had when the Border Marches still stood. With Urfarah and Pangaea gone, though, their hunts are driven by both vengeance and a desperate attempt at preventing further damage on the symbolism of the world. Theirs is a losing battle, however. Mortals have prospered in the wake of the Sundering, and their numbers continue to grow despite their efforts. Faced with this fact, many find allies among the Loyal as the two sects' goals align with one another.

                            The Maw (Gathua) fractured into smaller sects since the Sundering. They could not slay enough of their own kind to restore the Great Predator's soul. Many blamed themselves for his death, as they did not act fast enough to slow his decline. These werewolves eventually chose to end their lives, whether at their own hands or those of others. Some, however, come to believe that their sect's purpose must change. Urfarah may be gone, but the Uratha continue to live and grow in number. They must continue to hunt their own, regardless of whether or not it might somehow bring him back to life.

                            The Shepherds (Sahenhar) weathered the worst of the aftershocks of Pangaea's destruction. And in the countless years that have passed, they have found a place amongst the ever growing herd of humanity. In spite of her rejection, some still use what little they have of the moon's power to bring about more of their kind. Others are more preoccupied fighting the Gudthubak over stewardship of mankind.

                            The Inheritors (Ifila) still fight to fill the void left by the passing of the Great Wolf. They still believe that it was necessary to kill him. But with the Firstborn having fled deep into the Shadow, and the Warden Moon having taken back her blessings, many grow convinced that Urfarah's death was a terrible mistake. A large number of these Uratha end up gravitating to the ranks of the Loyal. Those who remain keep true to their sect's new goals as their packs delve into the Shadow in search of the Great Predator's first children.

                            The Devourers (Tesfurfarrahu) fared poorly in the new world. Their efforts to devour Urfarah's soul amounted to nothing when the Sundering struck. Denied of their goal, and devoid of a purpose, many degenerate into savage creatures not unlike the First Pack. Some fell so far into their spiritual halves that they ended up slipping through the Gauntlet into the Shadow. Those that manage to balance their natures either redirect their attention to their fellow Uratha, or somehow convert to the other sects.

                            The Mourners (Athdursa) have persisted over the last thousand years, but now exist as a shadow of what they were. Pangaea is gone, yet creatures of flesh and spirit still find ways to cross through the Gauntlet that rose wih Urfarah's final howl. Many of the sect have drifted into the ranks of the Inheritors, taking on their progenitor's duty of enforcing the divide between the two worlds. The few that remain, though, grow darker and more radical in their beliefs. The Great Predator's death did separate Flesh and Shadow, but it falls to them to finish the deed. To that end, they develop rites to strengthen the Gauntlet's influence in their territories, and some even go so far as to seek out the children of Spider and enlist their aid in achieving a common goal. These Mourners do not realize the consequences of their actions, and the other Uratha may be forced to turn their fangs and claws against them.

                                    Just read this little article here, and it's definitely prompted me to try my hand at making a write-up of Khmun (Djehuti's cult center) similar to that of the cities mentioned in Sothis Ascends. Expect me to post it soon.

                                    EDIT: Here we go. Still a work-in-progress, though. Critique and additions are appreciated.

                                    KHMUN, THE EIGHT-TOWN (HERMOPOLIS)
                                    One of the more prosperous cities in Kemet, Khmun is the capital of the 15th Upper nome and center of worship for not one but nine gods. Eight of them are the Tepi-Aui-Un, primordial divinities who bore witness to the birth of Re and all of creation. The ninth is Djehuti, whom the remet-en-kemet honor as the scribe of the gods and foremost assessor of Ma'at.

                                    ---=---
                                    The Eightfold Pantheon:
                                    - The eight deities of the Tepi-Aui-Un (or Ogdoad, as they will be more commonly known) are divided into four male-female pairs. Each represents the male and female aspects of one of the four concepts of primordial chaos. Together, they embodied the primal, fundamental state of the beginning.

                                    • Nu and Naunet - Water
                                    • Amun and Amaunet - Air
                                    • Kuk and Kauket - Darkness
                                    • Huh and Hauhet - Eternity
                                    ---=---

                                    Khmun is a great city matches only by Iunu and Waset. Like the former, it is a place of learning, with Djehuti's priests practicing the same sorcery as the solar priesthood. Some even claim that Khmun's magical tradition eclipses that of the Place of Pillars. Yet despite the rivalries between them, the magicians of both cities stand together as allies when Kemet is plunged into civil war.

                                            The Lonely Watchers - Wardens of the Black Land

                                            From the boat people who navigate the Nile to the lonely watchers of the Red Desert, Kemet counts a number of Wardens among its Immortals. Tied to minor gods who dwell in Neter-Khertet, these men and women cannot travel far from the sites they are anchored to without risking the loss of their gifts. Nevertheless, their influence extends far through their dealings with the other shadow peoples, and it is said that the eldest among them may possess knowledge on the predynastic empire that the Black Land was once part of. This is often enough of a reason for occult scholars to seek them out.

                                            As it is in modern nights, Wardens of this era favor a life of solitude. Some gather cults around themselves to keep them company throughout succeeding generations. Others are fortunate enough to befriend another kind of Immortal like the Eternals or the Purified, or at least a long-lived supernatural such as a Beast or one of the Awakened. Most of the time, though, a Warden is alone. His immortality and the price it exacts ultimately push him away from his mortal loved ones. And most supernaturals who know him eventually move on for one reason or another. The only being that can truly be considered the Warden's constant companion is the god that rules over his sacred place. But even then, such a bond might be rather impersonal at times.

                                            In exchange for such a lonely existence, Wardens are empowered by their patrons to protect the locations they are anchored to. Those tied to the river could become one with its waters, and rose up to drown those who would seek to defile its sanctity as neutral ground. Similarly, immortal nomads in the Deshret could similarly turn into shifting columns of air, and move at blinding speeds to assail those unworthy to uncover the desert's secrets. Such powers give some of them the might of a Claimed, while still retaining much of the free will and individuality they had as mortals.

                                            Many of Kemet's shadow peoples cultivate ties with one Warden or another. Uratha and Weret-Hekau approach the boat people to seek an audience with the river gods of the Nile. And the Cult of Sutek counts among its allies Wardens of the Red Desert who keep them informed of anything in the dunes. Many of these Immortals were thus among the first beings to encounter the Deathless and Lifeless as they emerged with the coming of the Turn.
