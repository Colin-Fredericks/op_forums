    What's the benefit of this compromise, the purpose of this connection? I'm not trying to be flippant or condescending here, I'm genuinely wondering. Since the main purpose of introducing character types other than Arisen is so that PCs have something to contrast themselves with, and since we already have two other types of mummies tied to Irem, its gods and the Rite, wouldn't any further additions to the roster serve this purpose better if they were even further removed from the experience of the Arisen? Since confronting the legacy of Irem (either by upholding it or rebelling against it) is such an important theme to any Mummy game, wouldn't it shake things up more to confront players with something superficially similar but not tied to Irem in any way? What would the Arisen do upon learning there are dwellings of the gods other than Duat, beings that grant immortality beside the Judges? Could this be true, or are these just some Lifeless abominations with delusions of grandeur? How will the Judges react to the Arisen who choose to interact peacefully with such beings?

    EDIT: I was writing this in response to reseru's post, but my Internet went bonkers so it didn't register before BigDamnHero's response.

        Originally posted by Morangias View Post
        The actual point was more along the lines of: The Arisen are (proto-)Egyptian mummies specifically. If you want non-Egyptian mummies, make them from scratch instead of trying to shoehorn them into either the mechanical template or the cosmology of MtC, because doing that would be insensitive towards all those cultures that preserved the bodies of their deceased through completely different methods stemming from completely different cosmologies and belief systems and aimed towards completely different goals than the Egyptians did.
        Yeah, yeah, yeah. I understand the argument perfectly well. I just don't see the need for this level of caution. Okay, so you want to show respect of an ancient civilization. Great! Maybe you want to also raise awareness about their history and traditions and offer up a compelling and accurate depiction of their society. I think that's a fantastic goal! But beyond that and an effort to weave an immersive story the way its already been done with the Egyptian theme, I think we're being too timid when we're talking about avoiding adapting the existing template to other regions. For one thing, the racism issue...people, we're already sorta pissing on Egyptian civilization with this game. Their god of the storm and divine adversary to Osiris is treated like a hero, their Judges of Order and Law are self-serving monsters, and generally the goal was to ensure that the departed made it to Osiris (or whoever the favorite deity was at the time) and have a peaceful afterlife....NOT recalling them to perform menial work thousands of years later. Several aspects of this here game are all modern elements we attached to mummies in our stories and films. I am going to argue vehemently that a MUMMY (now speaking of the horror icon) has a few key aspects to its nature that make it what it is. I believe our goal should be to define those key aspects (including the core game mechanics we're using to play Iremite mummies right now) and continue to tie those into any new Mummy templates we design. It will save all kinds of headaches and allow the authors to focus on other asthetics to help make the non-iremites interesting and unique.

        Or to put it simply- I don't think a simple "rename everything" approach is acceptable either....but I'm okay if what we deem to be the most essential parts of what makes a Mummy a Mummy are largely untouched. I think right now we have the makings of an excellent set of "bare bones" for a Mummy PC here. I think it is possible to strip away the Egyptian elements and create a new creature (by all means, with a purely unique culture, gods, afterlife, whatever) but still keep the mechanics familiar enough not to disenfranchise the players and STs.

            Here's the thing: not only is mummy the horror icon firmly Egyptian in origins, the actual MtC template is very much its own thing, cribbing the aesthetics from the classic horror movies (somewhat) and the Brendan Fraser movies (mostly) but changing the origins almost completely to make them a reasonable protagonist choice in the context of World (Chronicles) of Darkness.

            The only commonality the Arisen have with mummy the horror icon are the Egyptian aesthetics and the biblical scale of their magic, the latter cribbed firmly from the Fraser movies.

            MtC tells a very specific story: you were a favored servant of dread necromancers from before Egypt was known as such, remade into an eternal servant of their horrible death gods.

            Meanwhile, the only commonality between all the other things we call mummies is that they're preserved corpses - that's literally the extent of it. The method of preservation varies from place to place, the reasons for the preservation vary from accidental to elaborately deliberate, the status of those preserved varies from criminals to ritual sacrifices to priests to kings to saints to random people who happened to be buried in the right place for their bodies to dry up rather than rot.

            Frankly, there's no common ground to tie them all into the same template, even if the template was more generic than it was. Mummies as undead aren't a common belief of the cultures that mummified their dead - they are what European culture gave us over several bouts of Egyptomania. Frankly, it's kinda the opposite - elaborate burial rites were invented to placate the dead and make sure they don't rise up to bother the living.

            So, what I'm saying is that there is no generic mummy template the same way there arguably is a generic vampire template or a generic fae template. Trying to make one is misguided, trying to model it after the ultra-specific systems of MtC is plain wrong.

No reworking of culturally tied mechanics necessary.
