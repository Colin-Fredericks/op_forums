                            Originally posted by YeOfLittleFaith View Post

                            Except True Names can't be divined at all. And come now, several very scary things the Deceived can do don't even require true names. Just given names. I wasn't particularly complaining from the perspective of it making mummies less powerful. I really liked the previous paradigm of sealing a mummy's True Name, and all it implicated. It added to their uniqueness.

                            Not to mention that the fact the true names of the Arisen can still be discerned and used in such ways, in my opinion sort of defeats the point of sealing them as was presented in the core book - allowing only the Judges to hold that kind of dominion over their Arisen servants.
                            I can certainly see where you are coming from. The main source of information on True Names prior to this was the Lost Brush of the First Scribe (or something like that, the 5 dot Text in the core), which allows the wielder to learn a user's True Name. The mechanics for having this knowledge are, from memory, "any number of story-level effects", and Lore of the Deceived seems to agree with this. True Names, capitalised, seem a lot more dangerous than true names, which merely make the target eligible for certain spells.

                            I will admit though that I wish the writers had found a different, ahem, "name" for that intermediate stage between "use the target's given name to target them" and "know their True Name to rewrite their very existence". I get the desire to have certain spells that require the wielder to research their target prior to using them, it's cool and adds a nice extra layer to the effect. That said, I'm sure they could have found a less confusing term for that design space.
