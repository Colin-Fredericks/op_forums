    Magic items and cursed artifacts are a key element of Mummy. The game requires that the prepared Storyteller has a few Relics and Vestiges ready for his players and npcs, and at times that he make new stuff up on the fly. Even for the most creative of Storyteller's, it can tax the imagination. Why then not lighten our collective burden by sharing our homemade Relics, Vestiges and Curios? I'd be a terrible person if I didn't kick things off, so, without further adoo...

    THE ST VINCENT COLLECTION PLATE (•••)

    This old tarnished silver collection plate once belonged to the St Vincent church in Massachusetts. The church was built by a Puritan community hoping to escape English persecution, which got burned down by the Redcoats during the War for Independence. Incredibly, the collection plate was one of the few items that survived the fire. When the church was rebuilt, the old collection plate became a symbol for the preserverance of the parish and a point of pride for the churchgoers. Each Sunday, the priest would mention the story of the silver plate just before the deacon would make the rounds, and then community would place its hard earned money on the plate, and soon the pride felt shifted from proud of a shared history to pride in who could place the largest, most generous donation to the church.

    In the 1920s the plate was stolen and for decades it was in the hands of various private collectors, who all took pride in retelling the story of how the plate had survived the fires that had claimed the old St Vincent Church, feeding the legend and feeding the pride of the owner.

    The silver plate has become tarnished with age, and its round edges have been marred by bumps and dents. An image depicting Jesus and, curiously, the Devil, is engraved in the plate. The art probably depicts their meeting in the desert in the New Testament. Probably.

              VIZIER'S TONGUE (UTER ••)
              Durability 2, Size 1, Structure 1

              Though today it is a petrified, hard, brownish black lump of dried corpse-flesh, this tongue was once smooth as silk. It had belonged to the Grand Vizier of the Second Pharao of Irem. As the chief advisor the Grand Vizier held much undue influence, and could be argued to have been the true Pharao, since the king followed his every advice. The Vizier got too bold however. When he tried to poison the Pharao against the Shan'iatu, the Pharao instead turned the vizier over to the sorcerer priests. The vizier was dragged into the chambers of the Su-Menent who pulled his tongue from his mouth and consigned him to the life of a slave at one of Irem's pleasure houses from then on. The office of Grand Vizier was abolished by the Shan'iatu after the incident.

              Power: The Vizier's Tongue grants the bearer the same smooth gift of words that the Grand Vizier had once possessed. When activated, the Vizier's Tongue grants its bearer 3 dice to all Social Skill rolls for a scene. The bearer must spend a point of Willpower to invoke this effect.

              Curse: The Grand Vizier was a master of falsehoods and pettiness, and the Uter reflects this. After having been used for a scene, the next time the bearer manages to fulfill his Virtue he replenishes no Willpower. Doing good, selfless deeds simply seem foolish and pointless to the bearer. The Su-Menent are less affected by this curse, and regain half their lost Willpower points when fulfilling their Virtue.

                  THE OATH-PLAQUE (TEXT •)
                  Durability 3, Size 2, Structure 3

                  The Oath-Plaque is a heavy, rectangual, slate stone, about an inch and a half thick and about a foot and a half in height and ten inches in width. Carved into the slate are ancient Iremite characters saying "BY THE GODS BY THE JUDGES BY THIS COURT I SWEAR TO SPEAK NO UNTRUTH." In forgotten Irem, witnesses were made to swear by the Oath-Plaque before giving testimony. Those who were found lying before the court were punished to different degrees depending on the severity of the crime being investigated. The plaque seems to radiate a quiet intense malice.

                  Power: The bearer of the Oath-Plaque spends a point of Willpower and makes a Presence + Intimidation roll (add +2 to the roll for Sesha-Hebsu). The person that the bearer wishes to use the relic's power on must have touched the plaque at some point during the scene for the power to work. For each success rolled, the wielder can discern one untrue statement made by the target. The Oath-Plaque doesn't reveal the truth. It merely exposes the lie. Each time the target lies in the bearer's presence, he displays some physical symptom of his lie. One might weep blood, another might suffer boils across his skin. The physical marks disappear once the target has left the bearer's presence (or the bearer's Tomb, if the Oath-Plaque is used as a centering relic).

                  Curse: The bearer of the Oath-Plaque becomes increasingly distrustful of those around him. He develops the Suspicion Derangement (WoD p.98). If the bearer already suffers from Suspicion, it increases in severity to Paranoia. Over longer periods of time, the bearer usually ends up trusting only those who have sworn on the Oath-Stone in his presence, and even then they usually have to jump through several other hoops to prove that they're trustworthy. The scribes of the Sesha-Hebsu are immune to this effect.

                          From the Thread in the old forums I only made five in my project, one of each type and level.

                          Sutekh’s Opal Knuckle (1 Dot Amulet)

                          Durability 3, Size 1, Structure 4

                          This relic can easily be confused for a large unpolished, yet quite fetching, rock. It is a sizeable chunk of opal, with harsh lines and jagged edges. In one of the crevices one can make out distinct hieroglyphs from a culture swallowed by the sands millennia ago. It is said to be a knuckle from Sutekh that evokes the wrath the war god showed his brother when he ripped him apart at the bank of the river Nile. It is often worn netted in a leather necklace, or if its owner is more aware of its history embedded into a glove or gauntlet.

                          The Roman soldier who brought this back home after retirement from his stint in Alexander the Great’s army however claimed it to be the knuckle of Mars the God of War. He was told by a priest in Alexandria, not the one that housed the famous library but one of the lesser Alexandria’s named to honor the strapping young conqueror, that it was a knuckle bone of Ares that he lost in his famous boxing match against graceful Apollo. It was believed to have broken off when Apollo baited the fierce god into rabid assault, only to deftly avoid his mighty blows until he struck a gigantic cliff face, where this bone broke off and was lost in the newly formed valley. With it even his untrained son, named Alexander after his victorious Macedonian hero, could fight like an experience pugilist and in the hands of a veteran it one’s fists could shatter skulls.

                          Power:

                          A war god’s skill in killing extends not just to perfection with weaponry on the field of battle but also with the fundamentals of sublime use of the fists. Having this relic on one’s person and kissing it activates it for a scene. For the scene treat the character as if they had 2 dots in the Boxing merit, if they already have that merit treat them as if they had two more dots than normal. If they mastered the merit then treat the technique Brutal Blow as if every willpower point spent makes the next two attacks deal lethal damage, such is the familiarity of a War God’s Fist’s with the blood of their foe. The Bearers of the Engraved through greater wisdom of Amuletcraft may treat the maneuvers of Body Blow and Haymaker as requiring 1 less success.

                          Curse:

                          Both Sutekh and Ares were given over to their darker urges, and the curse of this relic reflects this upon the character. Once the character kisses this relic for the rest of the day they cannot regain willpower for supporting their Virtues, and their vice becomes Wrath for this time as well. The Maa-Kep have tempered their emotions through their mastery of the Amulet, and may treat only the first scene of the curse as being effective, after which they shake off the undue influence. Any dramatic Failure from a brawl maneuver results in shattering the bones in one’s striking fist, or foot, and thus does lethal damage rather than bashing and results in the incapacitation of the limb. The Anger of an offended War God often gets the better of them.




                          Mother Scorpion (2 Dot Effigy)
                          Durability 2, Size 1, Structure 3

                          This Effigy is of a large stylized scorpion made of gold. It seems to be in excellent condition however the joints and head appear worn and polished. The head of the scorpion seems to have what appears to be a human face, apparently female, emitting from it, with only polished grooves remaining for what originally was likely an intricate depiction of a mouth nose and eyes. The statuette is rather light considering it is made of solid gold, and this is likely a deception caused by it having long thin limbs and a bloated abdomen depicting a brood of eggs.

                          This effigy was first known to be in a possession of a Roman cult sometime in the first century, carried as a symbol of Selket and used as a charm against poison as they moved through foreign lands with every generation or so. When the Cult’s patron arose to discover the appropriation of this relic she put it to proper use placing it within the bowels of her Tomb every time the cult moved to breed a host of living defenders. Eventually the cult was all but destroyed when they stumbled upon a great spirit composed of vermin in the lands on the outskirts of the Indian subcontinent. The smoke from the basil and the brood of scorpions were all that could save the remaining half score of cultists after the demon slew their patron. The cult survived and relocated their tomb and patron, but the relic was lost in a blaze started in the battle. Since then it has been changing hands from wealthy collectors to clandestine cults throughout the centuries eventually travelling from India to Britain and the last rumored whereabouts placed it in a Chinatown in Sydney Australia.

                          Power:

                          To make use of this Effigy it must be buried in horse dung along with basil and then burned. From the smoking pile a generation of scorpions comes forth to make their home in the location of their genesis. These scorpions will not harm the individual who caused their birth, but others they come across will be treated with hostility. When the fire is burnt out the effigy is unharmed, softly glowing with a white light. Previous owners have used this to seed rooms with deadly scorpions as traps or deterrents. The smoke from the burning pile can be used as an equipment modifier for resisting venom (+2) for the first 2 minutes of smoke. Some savvy owners have learned they can induce the genesis of desired breeds by placing remnants of them in the room before activating the relic, this causes 1/3rd of the scorpions to be of this breed. Otherwise it generates the closest local breed of scorpion.

                          Curse:

                          The rooms where the scorpions have made their nests have a tendency to retain scorpion inhabitants. Even when removed of all lifeforms, gassed and immaculately cleaned, scorpion eggs seem to somehow remain and hatch over the next few years. This leaves a small population in the cracks and crevices of the room, or greater structure. There are five potential years worth of spontaneous scorpion generation for each activation. But each success on a thorough cleaning/poisoning roll removes a year.

                          A stranger downside is basil left unattended for more than a week may spontaneously generate scorpions that nest underneath the leaves or that spread to live within nearby jars. This becomes a simple fact of life for as long as the Mother of Scorpions stays under ones possession. Some have viewed this rather optimistically, and at least one cult of Scorpion Eaters have formed over the reverence of the sacred of the sacred Tulsi form of basil, combined with the remenants of belief in Selket.




                          The Heavy Mouth (3 Dot Regium)
                          Durability 7, Size 4, Structure 11

                          Forged in the lost days of the Nameless Empire’s height this massive slab of metal is an alchemical wonder. It is a circular shield almost large enough to cover the entire body and weighing close to half a ton. The center is cast in the relief of a vast fang laden mouth in imitation of the Devourer. It appears the bright rouge of some unnatural copper that reflects the desert heat as some burning red sun. Everything besides the depicted teeth is stained jet black. The complete components of this strange metal is unknown but what is known is lead and copper were used in its creation, as well as certain rituals involving displacing mass and weight into Neter-khertet. The handle of the shield is composed of two straps of a leather like weave of metal that is soft and comfortable to grip.

                          The priest of a cult that cared for this relic before it was lost in the 3rd crusade claimed to receive a vision from a specter of lost Irem in a dream, claiming that the relic was alloyed from the teeth of some monstrosity from the Duat. His Arisen patron later theorized it may have been the Devourer itself which the dream described.

                          Plundered from the Holyland during the Crusades this relic was ripped from the wall of a hovel where it acted as a door. The Knight was said to be an occultist and even a worker of magic and desired to rip the alchemical secrets of its ancient design. It was lost during its transport back to France, and disappeared from all known accounts. Rumors of it persisted, with claims of its appearances everywhere from the plains of Mongolia to the New World. But it was finally confirmed in an incident near Kabul in 2006 where it was said to have survived an RPG attack upon a truck. Its current whereabouts are once again unknown.

                          Power:

                          When activated the vast majority of the Heavy Mouth’s weight is shifted into Neter-khertet, though enough of it still remains to require a strength of 3 to be used effectively. This shield works much like a ballistics shield from page 178 of Armory; it has a nearly 3 foot diameter, and part of acts as a one way mirror allowing its user to see through the open mouth of the shield. Bashing with the shield are penalty -1 weaponry attacks, and the character receives a +2 defense bonus when wielding the shield and an additional +1 if the other hand is free. All other attacks while holding the shield are at a -3 penalty. The soft yet strong metal of the shield often works to catch and eat bullets, digesting them into a state of twilight.

                          The surprise property of the Heavy Mouth that has led to its name is the fact that any attack it defends against results in the offending weapon being stuck in the shield as the metal indents and forms a soft lead like mouth grasping the weapon. An instant action must be made with a resisted strength roll against the shield’s wielder to free the weapon. Fists stuck in the Heavy Mouth receive 1 point bashing damage, and if forced free receive an additional 1 point of lethal damage if not wearing an armored gauntlet. The Shield’s wielder may reflexively drop the Heavy mouth forcing the wielder of the weapon disarmed unless they can lift one ton, and if they can they must still succeed in a reflexive Strength Roll with a -3 penalty.

                          Curse:

                          In order to even pick up the Heavy Mouth without possessing immense strength one must feed two points of lethal damage to the shield either in the form of blood, or self-inflicted damage against its teeth. The metal itself hungers like the gluttonous beasts of the underworld. After this point the Shield becomes light enough to wield, though still wide and unwieldy compared to more mundane shields. If ever released the shield must be reattuned with blood. If the wielder of the shield is one of the Mesen-Nebu they remain attuned to the shield for a lunar cycle. A Willpower point must be spent to activate the reflexive grasp of the shield. The Mesen-Nebu however may receive their Sekhem rating in grasps per willpower point spent.




                          Seed of Erudition (4 Dot Text)
                          Durability 7, Size 5, Structure 12

                          The Seed of Erudition takes the form of a massive oblong around the size of a punching bag. It is quite heavy but not so much as if it was a solid piece of stone. The outer shell appears to be seamless obsidian but pressure applied to either end reveals radial dials and moveable hidden cogs. The outer layer is merely a shell of with the internal layer an advanced array of marble clockwork and nodules. Those who have solved its puzzle may open it to reveal a pristinely preserved scroll of brass with silver inlaid squares each about as thin as a leaf. They appear to be some form of primitive yet stylized pictograms, however about 7 yards in some of the squares depict some form of Iremite Hieroglyphs, with more and more showing up the further into the scroll one gets. The Scroll can be unfurled to seemingly impossible lengths, with varying lengths being recorded, with the shortest being about 33 yards though rumors persist that properly solving the puzzle can cause the Scroll’s final length to be double or even triple this length. It has not gone unnoted that the first visible pictogram depicts what is obviously an ibis.

                          Rumors persist of the nature and origin of the Seed’s writings. Some have speculated that it seems to be a massive ledger or record of some sort of data. Others have claimed that deciphering it at its fully extended length reveals an ancient Prophecy or perhaps a biography of human knowledge which extends even to future ages. And more recent owners have speculated that the length of its writings actually grows over time, or possibly over each use.

                          The First recorded and confirmed discovery of the Seed of Erudition was at the final destruction of the Royal Library of Alexandria. However it was immediately made obvious that this relic has far more ancient origins. Much speculation exists upon its history with claims both proven and dubious have reported the Seed’s discovery in numerous locals such as at the grounds of the Xiyang Palace’s State Archives after its great fire, under the Library of al-Hakam II in the City of Córdoba, and the collected Maya Codices in the Yucatan in 1562 after their destruction by the Conquistador and Franciscan Monk Bishop De Landa. Their last recorded discovery was under the Library of Congress in 1808 in Washington D.C. before the structure was burned by British Forces. Rumors of use of the Seed have been many and contradictory over the last two decades. Records of research into the history of the Seed of Erudition have been rescued from the destruction of the Egyptian Scientific Institute in December of 2011. These records show an Arisen using the name Mukhtar, obviously a pseudonym for a Sesha-Hebsu, supposedly discovering her own memories of finding the Seed under the library of Ebla sometime around 2250 BC. Supposedly these memories revolved around a discussion with a mysterious individual known as the Arrow of Diamond about a possible connection between this Tablet and a continent lost under the waves. Arisen have speculated that this may be a reference to its origins in the Nameless Empire.

                          Power:

                          If the Seed of Erudition is buried at a location chosen to be a center of learning or the gathering place of knowledge it can ensure success and greatness in this worthy endeavor. The burier of the Seed treats all rolls to design, build, or aid a center of learning or knowledge, such as a library or school, on the site as if they spent a willpower points. Everyone else treats all rolls to aid in building as if they had a specialty in the task, this does not stack with other specialties. Anyone researching or studying on the grounds of this site receive a similar specialty bonus dedicated to this site (Sesha-Hebsu may spend a Pillar and a Willpower point to instead receive the willpower bonus for the entire scene.). It seems as though fate itself aids this vision of knowledge and the site becomes a confluence of stored knowledge as it receives donations and retrieves artifacts and writings irreplaceable to mankind to be stored within its walls and storied vaults. So naturally assume such a library, museum or private collection becomes a success, barring external factors, over the years. If attempting to appropriate relics of knowledge or history treat the institution as having a 1 point discount on the appropriate merit needed to collect it (such as resources to win an Auction, or Status to cut through the red tape needed to import a historical artifact from its homeland.)

                          Last there is one potent power that the Seed’s history has revolved around. One may unearth the Seed of Erudition, open its clockwork seal and discover any Knowledge they desire that was ever kept at any of the locations the Seed of Erudition was buried under. Roll Intelligence + Academics (Add ones Ren Rating if they have one to the roll) to find the information they desire. Give a -3 Penalty to the roll to represent finding the information in a language they can understand somewhere in the far end of the scroll, but negate this penalty if they know Iremite. Each success can give any information from the last structure it was buried under. Two successes can give access to ancient knowledge such as those found in the shelves of Alexandria, or the lost scrolls of ancient emperors and kings. With 4 successes information from before the 1st Dynasty can be discovered and if your storyteller is willing this can include knowledge from Nameless Empire, Atlantis, or even other stranger civilizations. This Roll can be made only once after each unearthing, with the roll taking an entire week of investigation of the Seed of Erudition’s secrets, however Arisen Scribes only take need single night for their roll. After the Roll is made all writing found within the seed becomes cryptic and indecipherable. Naturally each unearthing has a terrible cost.

                          Curse:

                          In order to plant the Seed of Erudition a required sacrifice of a unique piece of knowledge is necessary. This can be anything from a scavenged metal figurine from Pompeii, to the journal of a deceased artist. Whatever the sacrifice is it must be buried with the Seed of Erudition. Many have taken to heaping multiple sacrifices and doing so they actually attract the attention of ghosts interested in these sacrificed objects, who find themselves anchored to the site. These ghosts could be of any nature, wicked, greedy, or calm and generous. Either way they either try to guard this site of learning if given to a surely nature, or they try to aid in the collection of knowledge if given to a scholarly or philosophical bent.

                          In more modern times many have taken to burying the Seed of Erudition in formal Time Capsules at the founding of Schools or Libraries, only to have the official record of such projects disappearing. When unearthed no matter how sacrificed the Seed absorbs the sacrifices, resulting in them deteriorating, eaten by worms, or damaged through water rot.

                          The Seed must be buried for at least a year under a center of knowledge before they can be used research its lost lore. Some prefer to bury it in a site and build new institutes of knowledge, while others simply find existing structures that suffice, and bury it under them. The problem arises when the Seed is unearthed. Unearthing the Seed unleashes a doom upon their related structure. Destiny calls for those with the position to destroy such sites of knowledge. Destiny fans the flames of religious zealotry against heretical knowledge, or even the paranoia of Emperors to control the dissemination of knowledge. The planting of the Seed actually attracts a spirit called by destiny, whether a power ghost with a passion for ruin, or a spirit of arson or war, or even more enigmatic spirits called from the Duat or elsewhere. Unearthing the Seed unleashes the spirits sealed within and like dominoes fate starts a chain reaction that ultimately levies a terrible doom upon the structure. This doom may be instantaneous or it may take years, but it will happen within the decade. Through heroic action however the knowledge stored within may be partially preserved, and in fact the Arisen believe Alexandria has planted the seed perhaps four times within its grounds. In fact the planting of the Seed under the doomed structure is a strong tactic for preserving and repairing its losses. But ultimately the knowledge gathered and preserved is doomed to be sacrificed and lost to the Duat, to fill the Libraries of the Dark powers in the lands of the Dead.

                          Placing the Seed within the Tomb of the Arisen has an interesting effect. It becomes the seed of the Arisen’s personal Library, allowing them to buy the Library Merit at half price. The catch being that if the Seed is removed from the Tomb the Library is destroyed or stolen and reduces a similar amount of merit points from merits tied to the Tomb. So this may be Tomb merits itself, or similar merits dots such as Reach from the Arisen’s Cult, Relics stolen, or Resources lost.



                          Beast of the Shades (5 Dot Uter)

                          Durability 4, Size 4, Structure 8

                          These mummified creatures come directly out of ones nightmares of what lurks in the dark. These withered forms are hunched over with paws crossed (holding strange black weapons, such as a khopesh, two batons, or a one-sided axe). They are recognized as a real life depiction of a Sha, or Typhonic Beast, the totem animal of Sutekh. Their heads are covered in stylized gold headdresses to accentuate their triangular ears and tapered lipless snouts baring offal tearing teeth. They seem almost humanoid as if some ancient taxidermist perfected their art taking arm bones from some slave or ape and attaching them to stitched together limbs, and head from various canines all atop a massive chest of a nameless beast. This mummy both unnerves those who see it, but at the same time they impress a feeling of astoundment and wonder at the thought of such a predator ever existing and deep wish that such a fantasy is impossible. They must be kept out of the sunlight or wrapped heavy cloth because they seem to smoke up when exposed to the abominable eye of heaven.

                          When kept by those wise to its secrets these mummified monstrosities are often favored as peerless tomb guardians, or dauntless midnight hunters. When roused into activity to they are bound to the duty placed before them. Their withered muscles stretched taut over their dry bones return to a state of boundless vitality as they stretch and fill the area with their azure colored Ka visible as torch-bright waves of spiritual fire. Their chosen ebon weapon grasped in mighty clawed hands and their bandy legs ready to spring forth upon their prey. Their mouth curls back into a mirthless snarl showing their layers of deadly fangs. Straight and three forked, their tail stands at attention, clothed only in a golden kilt and headdress their pupilless eyes, lustrous even in the gloom of a crypt, appear as almond shaped sapphire jewels showing no sign of mercy, and knowing naught but the duty to which they were called forth. These rare and powerful Uters were first discovered, as far as the Arisen know, buried beneath massive goldfaced seals baring the authority of Ra and calling upon Horus the Avenger to punish the sins of Set. They are symbols of Set’s sins made manifest.

                          Power:

                          The power of this Uter is in bringing these creatures to life. Even a mortal may revive one by ripping out a human (or any intelligent being’s) heart and sacrificing it to the creature. The mummified Uter accepts the Ab and returns to life, next the mortal may spend a Dot, not a point, of Willpower to deliver a command to the Beast in exchange for free it. This willpower dot is gone until bought back with 8 experience. One of the Arisen however could spend a Pillar of Ab instead of sacrificing a sentient being, if they so choose. They still need to sacrifice a point of willpower to declare a duty; however they can regain this Willpower back after a month’s time. A member of the Su-Menent however can just spend a point of Willpower rather than a dot.

                          Each of these minions of Set would perform a duty given to them until they accomplish it, or until a year and a day pass. After which they view their obligation as being concluded. If they are an Arisen they leave upon dark roads on the next moonless night, and return at the next new moon in their mummified form in the Tomb of the Arisen that revived them or barring that next to the Arisen herself.

                          Beast of the Shades
                          Attributes:
                          Intelligence 1, Wits 6, Resolve 6
                          Strength 7, Dexterity 6, Stamina 7
                          Presence 6, Manipulation 1, Composure 4
                          Skills:
                          Academics 1 (Desert), Investigation 3 (Prey), Occult 2 (Desert)
                          Athletics 6, Brawl 6 (Bite), Stealth 5 (Shadows), Survival 6 (Desert), Weaponry 6 (Divine Arms)
                          Animal Ken 1 (Frighten), Empathy 3 (Fear), Expression 2 (Snarl), Intimidation 5 (Packs)

                          Essence: 10
                          Age: 5
                          Virtue: Faith
                          Wrath: Wrath
                          Initiative: 12
                          Defense: 6
                          Speed: 20 (23 if chasing Duty)
                          Armor: 1 Tenebrous Hide
                          Health: 13
                          Supernatural Powers:

                          Smell the Heavy Heart: They can perfectly tell if one has succumbed to their vice within the last Dusk/Dawn cycle.

                          Divine Hunter’s Peerless Tracking: They can track with all their senses, and they add 3 dice for tracking by smell, and they add 3 more dice if they are tracking their duty bound prey.

                          Dauntless Hunter: On all fours they can run without tiring even while holding their weapons. They have a species Factor of 7 and add 3 to their speed when chasing their duty.

                          Creeping Darkness: They may see perfectly in Darkness, and having bodies composed of their own corporeal Sheut they can travel full speed on vertical surfaces or even upside down upon the ceiling. They leave no tracks, and they are as silent as shadows.

                          Apep Fueled Veins: being entities of twisted malice they may spend Essence (Max Pool of 10) to heal. One essence point heals three bashing damage, or 1 Lethal. This may even heal lost limbs.

They may even perform pack tactics if the Storyteller desires, adding 3 dice. They communicate silently and instinctively with pack mates.

                          Divine Arms of Ebon Shadows: Each Beast of the Shades has a weapon formed of their own Sheut, and like their limbs they may reform them if lost. The most common weapons for them are twin batons, a stylized one bladed axe, and a khopesh. These weapons glow with the azure flames of their Ka and each is treated as a + 5 to equipment for attacks. The claws on their hands or feet do lethal +1 damage, their terrible fangs do 3+ damage and don’t need a grapple to bite.

For Arisen they leave for merely a cycle of the new moon. No Duty may last longer than a year and a day. Simple tasks are best, and if the task requires too many sentences to declare they may interpret it into a similar form which they can complete. The most common task is for them to hunt down and slay a target, the 2nd most common is to guard a Tomb. They do not speak and only snarl. The one to awaken them can be understood roughly even if they are not speaking a language they know.

                              This relic has been bouncing around in my head for a long time.

                              The Locket in Spun Gold (Three Dot Amulet)
                              Durability 1, Size 1, Structure 1

                              Born in the fires of the alchemists, this little locket was created for love. The alchemist who forged the small jade locket spent hours crafting the stone into the shape of a human heart (a literal heart not the hallmark version). The final touches after days of work was surrounding the heart in gold shaped to look like olive branches. He presented his gift to the woman he loved, but was scorned when she took the locket but left him for another man. The alchemist was wracked with grief but instead of killing her in a fit of passion, he decided to use the alchemist method of revenge. He stole the locket back in the dead of night. Next he begged the Shan'iatu for some of their darkest magics. After a full night of invoking dark spirits and demonic beasts, the Locket in Spun Gold was born. The alchemist returned the locket before his love ever woke up. Inside the locket, he wrote her name. A year later, her beauty was long gone and she now appeared to have aged a hundred years. She died in bed choking on her own vomit.

                              This relics true purpose has long since been lost to time and its shape has been altered by jewelers to make it more appealing (it's now shaped like a more modern heart though the gold still remains due to the care of its previous owners). Though it has changed, this relics dark past will never leave it. It has been given or sold as a lovers gift every time. And every owner it has ever had has died the same death. It is now held by a young woman who owns an antique store. The locket was the last gift her father ever gave her and she refuses to take it off. She placed a picture of her father and she makes sure to look at it every day. A mummy was sent to take the relic back to Duat but after hearing her story and being enraptured by her beauty he decided to let her keep it despite angering the judge. What he didn't know, was that the picture of her father was taken on a family outing. In the background of the picture you can barely see the woman sitting under a tree. She is making plans to go to the doctor because she has been feeling quite weak recently.

                              Power: If anyone's name is written or a picture is placed inside the locket, that person will slowly become enfeebled. The targets skin becomes gaunt and sunken, with black veins showing through his or her pale flesh. Every week, the target of this curse will lose 1 Willpower. They only regain 1 Willpower from acting upon her Virtue just like when she acts upon her Vice. Once the targets Willpower has reached 0, she take 1 Aggravated Damage every week as long as the targets Willpower remains at 0. Once the target regains at least 1 Willpower she stops taking Aggravated Damage, though the previous Aggravated Damage remains and cannot be healed by normal means. If the picture or name is removed from the locket, the effects of this relic are removed and the Aggravated Damage inflicted by it will heal by 1 Dot of Damage every week.

                              Curse: Though this seems like a powerful relic, the user must beware. The user can now only regain Willpower through acting upon his Vice. When the target takes her first Dot of Aggravated Damage, the locket burns a mark into the users skin over his heart. The curse restricts the users heart and his Strength, Dexterity and Stamina are reduced by 1 Dot for every 2 Dots of Aggravated Damage the target takes (Attribute Dots cannot go below 1). When one of the three Attributes is reduced to 1 Dot, he takes 3 Dot of Lethal Damage and the mark begins to bleed a black sludge. The world isn't without some justice.

                                          The Mermaid's Tooth (Two Dot Uter)
                                          Durability: 5, Size 1, Structure 1

                                          "What the hell is this?" The mummy cried in ancient Iremic. She held the petrified fang in her hands, spinning it in the light. It was an ugly shade of yellow and had jagged razors along its side. The tooth curved at the end, making a hook shape. Whatever it was, it wasn't pretty and it wasn't appealing to the builder. She slipped the fang into her pocket and promptly forgot about it. This is one instance where the mummies lack of pain is a detriment. The tooth hooked into the dead flesh and dug its way in.

                                          Power: This Relic only effects the arisen. If the hook end is close to dead flesh the relic will gain a mind of its own and dig into the flesh. The tooth draws the Sekhem from the arisen's body, not enough to actually cause alarm. As long as the tooth is affixed to the arisen, whenever they make a descent roll it's treated as if they have 1 less Sekhem. So if the player has 5 Sekhem, he rolls as if he were at 4 Sekhem. The seal is almost impossible to break and physical force is shrugged off by the relic. The only way to remove the tooth is to willingly reduce your Sekhem to 1. Only then will the tooth fall from the arisen's flesh, fully sated in Sekhem (this only works if the arisen WILLINGLY reduces their Sekhem. It will remain if the arisen is reduced to Sekhem 1 naturally).

                                          Curse: Whether it actually is a relic or not...well that's up for debate. Unfortunately so few arisen know or care of the existence of this particular relic that its presence has gone unchecked. It's origin? Well most records that actually mention the tooth say it came from a sailor. He was lonely at sea and a mermaid came to him. The sailor abused the mermaid and she bit him to escape, losing her tooth in the process. The sailor kept the tooth and his anger and darkness infected it. Someone unlocked its powers and now it is a bane to all arisen. This relics curse is that is will not return to Duat. Why? No one can say. Maybe the relic has been rejected by the judges. Maybe it's curse is so rooted inside the relic that it just refuses to leave. Either way, the tooth has made its way around the world more than once. It has always been a thorn in many arisen's side.

                                                      1.Breathe Catcher (Two dot Effigy)
                                                      Durability 2, Size 2, Structure 4
                                                      An intricate model of a river ship composed of innumerable miniscule reeds; the Breathe Catcher is imperceptibly light due to its fine construction and may be lifted easily with both hands. A fine silken sail, a rare luxury material available to few in the days of Irems Glory, is prepared to catch the Ka within one’s breathe. With a breeze born of sacred lungs the vessel upon which the effigies owner resides travels swiftly on invisible winds even on calm waters. It is through this relic that the Tef-Aabhi known as the Give of Truth traveled beyond the edges of the known waters to reach far off continents entire Sothic Turns before the great ages of exploration revealed the new world to the hungry empires of the globe.

                                                      Power:
                                                      The effigy is placed upon basin of water large enough to float upon. The Arisen then fills her lung with a silent pray and then exhales upon the Breathe Catcher filling its sails with her Ka all the while remaining motionless upon the water. This uses one Pillar of Ka from the Arisen and the ship the Arisen travels upon will travel as though on fine and strong winds as long as it’s sails are up, ignoring the weather, whether a lack of wind or strong opposing currents it matters not. The Arisen roles Stamina + Occult, with one success the vessel will travel slightly faster than its maximum speed for successes in days, with an exceptional success the vessel will travel greater than double its finest speed. No crew is needed for the ship to travel as long as the sails are at the ready. The Arisen’s desire controls the direction of the winds. The Arisen may also use this relic offensively on any ship she can perceive, filling their sails past bursting or cursing the winds to blow in the direction she chooses. Only one vessel may be affected by this effigy at one time, and each use, whether as a blessing or a curse, requires one Pillar of Ka. Humans may perhaps use this relic but they must continuously inhale and exhale over the effigy for as long as they want to propel the ship. They must also spend one willpower per roll and must roll once an hour.

                                                      Curse:
                                                      As long as the Arisen’s breathe is propelling the ship she is struck mute as any air summoned to her lungs vanishes into the mystic currents propelling the sales. If a living being someone manages to activate this Relic, all stamina rolls are reduced by three dice as their lungs struggle for air while exerting itself. If this reduces their stamina below 1 then they must slumber for the day If the Arisen is a member of the Tef-Aabhi however she may draw enough breath to speak in whispers.
