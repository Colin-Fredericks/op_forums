      My Mummy group is quite looking forward to the first bit of content for them since Guildhalls of the Deathless. They're mostly just hoping for new Affinities/Utterances to pore over. As their GM, I would quite like expansion on certain antagonist elements. Overall, I think my personal big three wishes are:

      1. Relic Architecture. I think DC promised to explore this in a future supplement, and my Tef-Aabhi player would kill for it.
      2. More Bane stuff. Bane Relics in particular. I have one written up as the crux of a central plot point in my current game, but more exploration of this idea would be great.
      3. Rules for mortal sorcerers. This is in last place for me, because worst-case scenario I can use Seba to approximate that effect. But given how many hints the game line has regarding these sorcerers, an expansion on who they are and where they stand in Mummy cosmology would be nice.

                                          As I said, if you want something spooky to throw at the players that is outside of the norm for their game line, that is what blue book monsters (or other game lines) are for.

                                                      Except we already have that. Most Arisen don't know what the Shuankhsen are, and Ammut ensures that lack of knowledge is perpetuated. One of the theories bandied around by the Arisen is that the Shuankhsen are failed attempts at emulating the Rite of Return by later cultures, and the fact that Shuankhsen appear in whatever corpse is nearby helps add almost a degree of authenticity to that. If you want to run a story about a Mummy having their faith in their Judges shaken by the actions of one of the Lifeless, well, you've already got an antagonist type that would take great joy in doing just that.

                                                      And I don't think making the Arisen query the Judges on the grounds of metaphysical superiority is actually in keeping with the game's themes. Arisen should spurn the Judges because they want self-determination and freedom from what is tantamount to mystical slavery and enforced dementia, not because their gods might not be as powerful as some other gods that also made magic slaves.
