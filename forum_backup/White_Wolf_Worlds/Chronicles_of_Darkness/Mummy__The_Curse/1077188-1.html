        So, as far as we can tell the chief difference between an Arisan and just an undead is that A. The arisen have some kind of connection to and control over Sekhem and B. The arisen serve Gods in some direct way.

        If the rite of return was performed in other places at other times, it was presumably for the same reason- to create powerful servants by killing mortals and rebuilding them as vessels for the divine.
        The differences would come in A. what kinds of bodies they can return in and the practical effects of that, B. what gods they actuall serve and what they demand and C. What effects their old culture would have on the way they were raised.

        Mechanically, would be thinking like the lesser vampires in Wicked Dead. They;d use the same ruleset- Sekhem, Pillars, returning from death, utterences and affinities, the decent- but in different ways

        For example- the Judges are forces of control, punishment and tyranny, created to cement the rulership of an empire. The Decived were only slightly different, serving the Tekhem (? I don't have the spelling on me) as works of art, immortality vessels and, later, instruments of vengeance. The Shasushken were created as sacrifices pledged to the abstract concept of destruction and decay. The three groups use the same basic mechanics and are, on some level, "the same type of thing", but they are very different from each other.

        The other types of arisen would be made on a similar level. What about creatures creates to serve dark forces as the last act of vengeance by a dying culture? Or perfect warriors created to promote the rise of an empire and serving elemntal forces of destruction? Or philosopher-angels ensuring the knowledge their home nation found lasts forever in the service of the lords of the Astral? They could be very unique while still mechanically being clearly "the same type of thing" created by the rite of return

                                    I've always thought about translating the Cabiri from Resurrection as an example for a bastardized version of the Rite of Return. You know, some weird beings created by sorcerers which tried to achieve immortality by investigating the Arisen, only to get a poor reflection of the Rite's true power and getting hunted by the Judges and their earthly agents.

                                    As for "why" to have non-Iremite mummies, it is for a number of reasons: first, to emphasis that Sekhem is a global force of nature- after all, if it is a global force of nature, other cultures or beings should have found exotic ways to manipulate it, including creating all kind of undead/deathless/lifeless/other abominations. Second, it is to give mummies something to deal in its own cosmology- vampires shouldn't depend of the Tremere from Mage to have as magical antagonists, and Demon shouldn't depend on the qashmalim as agents of unseen, divine power. The different gamelines are able to crossover with each each other, but each should still function on its own. Just as Mummy's sorcerers shouldn't just be Awakening's mages or Second Sight's taumaturges, their own failed replications of the Rite shouldn't be wholly depended on you searching for other gamelines to find new antagonists. And finally, adding non-Iremtie mummies would make the game feel much wider- it shows that the Arisen's world view is not set on stone, and that are things they can't really explain. This is why it is such a great material for Night Horrors- as one of the recurring themes in those books is things which not only threat the protagonists physically, but also endanger their world view. They don't know anything, even in their own area of expertise. Things which are familiar to them, yet they bend- or even break- the rules of everything they know about reality. It is a wide world out there, and even if you think about yourself as a god king, even the gods could be horrified from looking at that darkness.

                                                        Originally posted by Azahul View Post
                                                        Which is why I think trying to parallel the Arisen set-up would be a terrible mistake, and why ultimately why I feel this is a bad sub-forum for this discussion.
                                                        Fine, but since the moderators have said this is the last chance for a thread like this, please could we dispense with the usual round of arguing over whether it should exist?

                                                        Originally posted by LostLight View Post
                                                        As for "why" to have non-Iremite mummies, it is for a number of reasons: first, to emphasis that Sekhem is a global force of nature- after all, if it is a global force of nature, other cultures or beings should have found exotic ways to manipulate it, including creating all kind of undead/deathless/lifeless/other abominations. Second, it is to give mummies something to deal in its own cosmology- vampires shouldn't depend of the Tremere from Mage to have as magical antagonists, and Demon shouldn't depend on the qashmalim as agents of unseen, divine power.
                                                        Also, Mummy is a game where the main protagonists spend a lot of time "off," while the world stubbornly insists on having things happen without them. New manipulations of Sekhem should definitely factor into that.

                                                        However, I think the common thread should be "manipulations of Sekhem," rather than mummies per se - if it produces mummies of a sort, it's not because the sorcerers in question necessarily wanted mummies, but because mummification is a necessary component of Sekhem-fulled immortality (fitting for a magical force that draws little distinction between spirituality and materiality). More cultures would be producing relics outside the remits of the five Guilds than are producing mummies that are neither Deathless nor Lifeless - and of course non-walking-around mummies make pretty good uter.

                                                        For mummies themselves, the first question is why? As in what is the purpose of their immortality? For Andean mallqui, this is likely to be some combination of serving as an ancestral focus or serving as messenger between the divine and the Earth - which in game terms would shake out pretty well to what the Arisen do for their cults - but as an end, rather than a means. For those hard core Buddhist monks who mummified themselves, the point would be a search for enlightenment. If any later Egyptian mummies were intended to walk around, it was probably just to protect themselves.
                                                        (In contrast, I think if the Pazyrik Ice Maiden or the Lindow Man got up one day, they should be revenants rather than quasi-Arisen).

                                                        What they have or don't have can follow from that.

                                                        Mallqui under this model could be summoned up by their communities into something pretty much like a Descent cycle (though the things that hasten or slow it might be different). If their function is to help their communities, I'd give them something like Kepher senses, but applying to the problem their people are facing rather than relics. Since they weren't able to prevent the conquest by forces that didn't have mummies, I'd say there aren't many of them - maybe a dozen - and they're not as powerful as the Deathless (let them have Affinities - not necessarily the same ones, but that type of power - but not Utterances). Summoning would also be the only way they can wake up - if their people are gone, the next time they fall asleep will be for good - yes, this means a mallqui will have been created by the cult/community/ayllu it serves.
                                                        (This is also the option that would be easiest to generalise into something that's not always mummies. An ancestral ghost that serves as a family genius might be summoned into possession of a family member, accompanied by a burst of Sekhem, for example).

                                                        Meanwhile, the monks would start off in what the Deathless would consider apotheosis - and may be able to stay awake indefinitely at Sekhem 1, unless some outside party trashes them (which might be a permanent kill). Their numbers would be even lower than for mallqui - perhaps only one or two have ever accomplished this. On the other hand, they could be epic - Himalayan myth credits monks and sages with feats easily comparable to Utterances - but they'd have build up their Skehem for that, somehow, and it would bleed off quickly (think of it as a Descent where you're going against your purpose, with no way to slow it down).

                                                        Like Shuankhsen, I'd give other mummies only one Pillar - or at most two (one derived from personality, one derived from their cultural purpose).
