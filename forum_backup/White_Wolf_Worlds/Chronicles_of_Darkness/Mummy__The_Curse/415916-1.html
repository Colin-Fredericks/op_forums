        In the New Kingdom Egypt built an empire on the vassal system- all the occupied kingdoms (with the important exception of Nubia and surrounding lands) were left to their own devices as long as they gave proper tribute to the Pharaoh and sent their children to Egypt for education (Re: allowed them to be used as hostages by the Egyptians, to be executed should the vassals attempt rebellion). I imagine this makes the most logical sense for the Irem Empire as well.

        On a more Lovecraftian note, I've long assumed that Irem encountered several rival empires in its time. Most of them were petty human kingdoms that fell easily to conquest. One was a society based on magic wholly different from Shaniatu, and there were many terrible wars and treaties with the Atlanteans. And then there were....other empires. Empires more ancient than humanity, ruled by things that defied description. Empires who used humanity not even as servants or slaves but as raw materials...twisting body and mind and soul into "better" forms more pleasing to their masters. As terrible as Irem is, the world does owe them this much....those empires it could eradicate, it did so without mercy or hesitation. Those it could not....it stole away names to send its denizens into realms beyond the Earth. Those that resisted both of these were put to sleep, their lands scoured and poisoned so that none but the most insane would dare to venture there. Alas, as the Arisen awake in cycles, so too are these enchantments tied to the stars. One day the forgotten empires will rise again.

                                Atamajakki makes a good point about tin; it's significantly rarer than copper and very desirable for making bronze. Arsenical bronze is easier to make, since many bronze ores contain arsenic anyway, but has serious health drawbacks... not that the Shan'iatu necessarily cared. Nevertheless, I could see the Guild of Alchemists having a vested interest in seeking the stuff out, and the nearest viable sources are pretty far away. If early Imperial roman sources are to be believed, the Greeks were aware of Cornwall at least as early as the 4th century BCE, when the local Britons traded tin and bronze ore with "foreign merchants."

                                If we suppose that some Iremites were willing to travel a similar distance in order to acquire tin, it's possible that some would have followed the west African coast southward. If they devoted a lot of effort to exploring that coast, they may have discovered the "volta do mar" navigational technique, which involves sailing out into the Atlantic rather than closely following the coast, in order to take advantage of favorable ocean and wind currents. I'm not sure it's really possible to take advantage of that technique without the lateen sail, but lets assume for the moment that you get some advantage even with a single square sail like the Iremites would presumably be using.

                                If the Iremites were using the volta, and they kept at their African exploration (perhaps after discovering that west Africa was quite rich in gold), then they might just have stumbled across Brazil in the same way that Pedro Álvares Cabral would many thousands of years later. And then, if they decided there was something they wanted in this new territory, they might have explored more of the Americas.

                                That's a lot of ifs, but it's about the most plausible scenario for bronze age trans-Atlantic contact that I can think of, besides "a wizard did it." The Iremites could theoretically also have done what Thor Heyerdahl did with his Ra II and crossed further north by taking advantage of the Canary Current, but Heyerdahl had the advantage of knowing not only that there was something on the other side of the ocean, but also exactly how far away it was. Without some kind of really accurate divination, it would be next to impossible to plan a similar voyage.

                                    I've said this before, but if you are willing to put aside reason and embrace more imaginative narratives, there is some evidence to support a colonial Irem:

                                    Scota - Wikipedia
                                    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scota


                                    https://sites.google.com/site/ancien...s-grand-canyon

                                    A more "historical" idea would be to match the Nameless Empire with the common practices of actual Ancient Egypt. And historical Egyptians didn't like to go beyond the heartland, and the Nile was so prosperous they had little need to. When they did want to contact foreign lands, they usually employed an intermediary like the Phoenicians (who are credited with the first circumnavigation of Africa at the behest of Pharaoh Nekau II, for instance). So perhaps a mysterious third party of merchant seamen is called for if we want to lengthen the reach of Irem?
