    I'm just dropping in because I've noticed a tendency to exaggerate some of the difficulties of Memory and Sybaris, and as the designer of both I'd like to point the following out:

    1) At Memory 3 (see p. 78) you know your guild, decree and in rough terms, your last Descent. To clarify, "knowing" your guild (or decree) means more than seeing the word "Su-Menent" on the back of your hand--you know what it is to be a member of this guild. Your mind also possesses information you have learned from others and experienced yourself, but you have trouble weaving them into a coherent narrative, or telling the difference between secondhand knowledge and experience. There are blackouts at points where your Memory may have been higher (so you don't "remember remembering") but by and large, you're a fair bit better off than Guy Pearce in Memento, though for various reasons you might make the same mistakes by, for instance, misremembering a rumour of someone plotting against you as having *witnessed* this plot. But this does mean that yes, mummies can form coherent social structures. It's only at Memory 1, when conscious knowledge of the guild is lost, that organization is impossible--though at 2, when the mummy *is* operating at Memento level, it gets tough.

    2) A typical Arisen generates an Unease Sybaris of -7 (3-10) at the start of his Descent--no more than that! That's why the rules specify -8 at the point where the people go, as the ancient necromancers put it, kind of apeshit. -7 to -5 kind of casts a Gothic pall over the mummy's surroundings. People find refuge from a sense of nihilism in church and state, and mummies, who sort of represent *both* in an occult sense, enjoy a bit of an edge. But crazygonuts scrawling in inspired Iremite really happens when Memory 2 or less Arisen come back. People may unconsciously insert the odd King in Yellow-like reference into cricket bat spankings and other closed society initiations, but it's the kind of thing that would pass casual scrutiny.

    So in short, to have a bunch of mummies hanging out in an organized fashion you don't absolutely need special gimmicks, though it does help. The rules are specifically designed to permit a certain degree of social organization for mummies and for the most part, make mortals act strange within the parameters of a "modern Gothic" atmosphere.

                        I've enjoyed the book tremendously so far, but one thing struck me as worth mentioning. For a species of immortals beset by a number of supernatural enemies, including those interested in the grisly and repeated consumption of their flesh in exchange for greater power, the Arisen of DC display a remarkable dearth of capacity for protecting themselves. One assumes that this is being left to their cults for the most part (though how secure this is against the Shaunksen is unclear). Where evidence is available of the capacity to have it out with an opponent, it tends to be haphazard, with a token nod to combat ability absent much support. The only ones who tend to break the mold do so because of an unusual overall amount of capacity in all facets of the character, and combat simply seems to benefit from this (Sedge and the Jackal come to mind). One example in particular has an Arisen with the Guardian Wrath affinity and no brawl skill. Am I missing something about how they function in their environment? Does the Jackal come out to play every time a Shaunksen stalks the city? Are the cults key to combating supernatural threats in a way that hasn't been discussed? They've always struck me more as an Arisen's interface with the mortal world, than a monster squad.
