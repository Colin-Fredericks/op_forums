                                                            Originally posted by TyrannicalRabbit

                                                            Not being a maths and crunch fella my curiosity is mostly along the lines of how drastic of a difference it makes on success or failure. Beyond that I share in not getting it.
                                                            I was actually curious myself, so I spent some time running the numbers (having just spent a bit of time earlier to add Advanced Actions to my WoD/CoD/Exalted 2e general dice probability calculator).

                                                            My assumption here is that there's three things that matter in a dice trick, in descending order of importance. First, how it affects your odds of rolling at least one success (important for succeeding at any action at all), second how it affects your odds of rolling at least five successes to get an Exceptional Success, and finally how it affects your mean successes rolled (important for extended actions, contested rolls, resisted rolls).

                                                            Here are the results for those three actions, comparing a normal roll, a roll where 7s count as successes (referred to as TN7), and an advanced action roll.

                                                            Each sheet has a number of dice from 1 to 20, either the odds of hitting the specified success count, or the mean successes gained from the roll. Further each sheet has the two dice tricks' effective base dice (how many dice you would have to roll without dice tricks to get the same results)/expressed in terms of base dice (so a relative value of 1.4 means "as though having 40% extra base dice"). This analysis assumes no other dice tricks are used).

                                                            Based on that we see the following:

                                                            For 1 success, Advanced Actions give a much better result than TN7 does. TN7 is equivalent to adding approximately 44% dice to your dice pool. Advanced Actions, in the meanwhile, just straight up doubles your dice pool, make it equivalent to the Rote Quality (for this purpose at least).

                                                            For 5 successes, Advanced Action is slightly better at very low dice pools than TN7. However, the advantage of TN7 climbs quickly past that value, doing better from 3-18 dice (thus the vast majority of rolls even for very potent mummies). Finally, at 19 dice and beyond Advanced Action beats out TN7 again.

                                                            For mean successes, Advanced Action initially does better than TN7. However, TN7 benefits from the fact that it gives a very constant return in the extra number of successes (always adding 1/3rd) whereas Advanced Action has sharply diminishing returns (since the more dice, the less likely you are to get a result that's very far from the mean). Starting at 8 dice, TN7 does better than Advanced Action.

                                                            (Personally I am very happy with the removal of Target Number manipulation, since my location ensures I can only ever play online and adding extra flags and additions to dice rollers for a very limited group of things is a hassle and a half).
