    The Sothic Chronicles.

    Supernatural occurrences are typical in any age, but the Sothic Turns are like a flood compared to a high tide. For beings that literally predate history, how does this fourth Turning challenge them in ways new and unexpected? In an era more socially and technologically connected than ever, when mortals connect to those on the other side of the world, threats are everywhere. For the Arisen it's a time of loss as man turns his back on traditional mysticism, but for their enemies it's a time of more: the Shuankhsen feed more, the Deceived re-unite with each other more, amkhata pierce the veil more.

    The fears of Y2K didn't end. Mortals dream, and the brooding fear of the end of the world lurks in all their hearts...

    more.

    Strix rock the boat of the Damned's carefully balanced politics. Idigam are the unknowable prey to the world's best hunters. The Fallen World is an endless mystery to the only people who see the truth. The Sothic Turn is a period where those who have the longest memories are blindsided by a world that's outgrown them and changed in ways they didn't expect; it's time to either get busy livin' or get busy dyin', and that's a whole lot said for Mummies

            One thing I'd like in the 2e, if it keeps storyteller/player divide. I liked the idea that from almost turn 2 until Napoleon and Ruins of Empire, mummies hadn't really a good idea where they were from. They remembered the River, but.. with Memory 3, a lot more should be hidden from Players. They shouldn think of Guilds directly as the job you had in Irem--its the mystic Guild that gives you purpose. I don't want my players saying "I was a banker in Irem" until their memory is high enough. Similarly, a LOT of back story of Irem could be hidden from players. I wouldn't mind a starter mummy thinking Irem was in china, only to find out (through memory) that they are second arisen, or a truly wild and rare thing (a slave traded from china to irem who got into a guild somehow). I would rather them see Guilds as mummy archetypes (rich and powerful, memory keeper, plain immortal, lovecraftian cultist, forward thinker) rather than Jobs right away. I'd also like to see a bit more on "starter" mummies--knowing only the basics of your character sheet, plus a little back story from your last descent, while leaving the rest of it alone. Me and my players got so caught up in Area that we practically skipped the whole 6000 years between--player section being explicitly without much info on Irem let's the ST introduce it naturally, and prevents "I was a the if in irem, who became a tax collector" mindsets, and puts them clearly in that foggy mindset of a mummy. "I know I am Mesen Nebu, and believe it. What did I do in life? Don't remember" should've been what popped into mind for my players. If the Wall is gonna be there, make even the core meatier with it. PC section as basically the same as a newborn vampire with amnesia, talk about skipping through time, faking your memories, all that. But move the history of iarea and guilds into ST only.

                                    Slightly off-topic, but I was looking at the Ruins of Empire recently and I've become even more convinced that they need to do a setting blurb for San Jose/Silicon Valley. They actually mention Harvey Spencer Lewis and AMORC (The Ancient Mystical Order Rosæ Crucis) as possible antagonists/frienemies for the period. They mention that the Order, despite seemingly having no real inkling about Irem, the Arisen, or Sekhem, acquire numerous relics and vestiges (not to mention other artifacts that may be of interest to Arisen for one reason or another) during and after Lewis funds an expedition to Tel el Amarna. For the most part, these wind up at the Rosicrucian Egyptian Museum in San Jose.

                                    Rosicrucian park itself could make a great base for a cult, either Arisen or Deceived. It's got an Egyptian-style temple, a private research library, a planetarium, and even a reproduction of an Egyptian rock-cut tomb. It's also become a bit of a tourist trap, which could lead to some amusing difficulties for any of the Deathless who establish tombs there.

                                                        There are a lot of weird ways to extend your Descent, but I realize now that my NPC mummies dont even last long enough to finish a full descent--they get into fights with enemies, they question the gods, shit hit the fans, and a descent roll robs them of months of life. Granted, as its a crossover game, and I dont have many PC mummies, the NPCs do sorta fill that role of loss--the others watch mummy friends decline, then suddenly die of circumstance. Sure, Ive run into the problem of trying to have mummies vaguely in the same historical period interact, but then the math shows they couldnt have (sothic turn 1, im looking at you, you nightmare of math).. but I still say thats working as intended, and that relic devouring is sorta like cannibalism in Werewolf. In 1e theres was almost no temptation to do it. In 2e, my werewolf npc literally HAD to do it (10 harmony, 0 essence), and i think relic devouring is the equivalent temptation. in a crossover game, at least, the story seems suited to having short lifespan. In a non-crossover game, the desperate desire to extend your life even a few more months also has a big place. Ongoing with the old age metaphors, you could picture a human cultist PC having moral issues over just what the Mummy is willing to undergo just to live a LITTLE bit longer.
