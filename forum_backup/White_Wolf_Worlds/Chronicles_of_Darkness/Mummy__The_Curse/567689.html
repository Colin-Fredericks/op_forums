    A Dumb Question - What was in it for the Shan'iatu?

    06-25-2015, 08:08 AM
    Let me preface this post by saying I've really only skimmed the Corebook but as it specifically states that no-one who undertook the Rite of Return ever arose during the time of Irem... what was the Rite of Return for?

    The Arisen are hugely powerful servants that were never called upon throughout whatever calamity brought the Nameless Empire to an end - this in itself seems unusual. The Rite of Return was supposedly the greatest magic of Irem and yet neither the Empire nor its Sorceror-Priest-Kings seem to have gained any utility from it. What gives?

    I'm assuming that there is an answer in the text or hidden between the lines of the text but my (admittedly cursory reading) has not turned it up.

                                                          Well, I personally think that, somehow, the Shan'iatu and Judges (assuming they're different) have dreams of global domination. They want to build a society that funnels the energy of the living world to them in their "afterlife." A global theocratic civilization, all with the cults and worshiping.

                                                          I still think that the Judges that were part of the Rite of Return were actually the Shan'iatu after they ascended to godhood. Time gets a little bit wonky when we're talking about beings that exist outside the standard flow of time (which Du'at apparently does). So, that answers what the Judges got out of the Rite - their ascension in the first place!

                                                          I equate the Shan'iatu to the fantasy necromancer lich kings trying to take over the world, and the Arisen as the leaders of the undead hoards.
