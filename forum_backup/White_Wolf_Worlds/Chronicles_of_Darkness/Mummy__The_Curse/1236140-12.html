                        Originally posted by Banu_Saulot View Post
                        -What is the purpose of the Arisen? I mean, serving their Judges yes. But is there a general goal they usually have beyond recovering their memory?
                        The directives baked into the Rite of Return instinctively draw the Arisen to retrieve vessels of Sekhem and offer them to the Duat. This is the fundamental quest from which most action flows, and it is the root of their eternal slavery. Beyond this, each Judge charges its Arisen servants to seek out particular sins and punish them, each Guild shares its own philosophy of what is the best means of upholding the glory inherited from the Nameless Empire, and a heretical mummy could always choose to turn her back on the Judges of Life and Death to pursue a personal apotheosis and escape their eternal Curse.

                        Originally posted by Banu_Saulot View Post
                        -What would you consider the biggest differences between the Arisen from The Curse and the Undying from The Resurrection?
                        The Undying, unlike the Arisen, are principally modern souls mended by their union with the vestiges of ancient spirits.

                        The Arisen, unlike the Undying, are evil.*

                        * Deliberately hyperbolic, but with an underlying truth.

                            Originally posted by Banu_Saulot View Post

                            -What is the purpose of the Arisen? I mean, serving their Judges yes. But is there a general goal they usually have beyond recovering their memory?

                            -What would you consider the biggest differences between the Arisen from The Curse and the Undying from The Resurrection?
                            1. Somewhat. Recovering vessels of Sekhem has been mentioned, as has hunting to remember who they were. A big third one would be killing entities like Amkhata and Shuankhsen that directly hunt the Arisen. Some like to manipulate the construction of their cities to promote the flow of Sekhem according to the designs of Pre-Egyptian architecture, since such things often yield power when correctly aligned. Others promote the crafts of their Guilds, generally encouraging the building of relics under assumption that the more monkeys with typewriters the greater the odds someone will get it right. Some punish the sins sacred to their Judges. And others interact with the affairs of immortals and Lifeless creatures like Ghosts, craving allies and enemies that can reliably last from one awakening into the next. Guilds also have their own agendas...one of which is TO TAKE OVER THE WORLD! Sort of, anyway.

                            2. Too many to name. It reminds me of Changeling: The Lost...a game that takes many of the setting ideas from its previous incarnation and then goes in hard and dark on revisioning the setting. Resurrection was a game about finding some hope, salvation and forgiveness after a life of vice and failure. The Curse is about suffering through the servitude of cruel, lovecraftian gods that have chosen you to be their middle managers for all eternity. I think it is in the end superior to Resurrection, even though I miss a lot the themes of Hope in the original game (also, non-egyptiant mummies). Really, while it takes inspiration from Resurrection this game is more a spiritual successor to the 2nd edition of Mummy in the old world of Darkness. It takes a lot of themes of being burdened by history and memory loss and refines them, with in my opinion a much better society than any of the Mummy games in the OWOD provided. But its definitely darker in tone by a mile.

                                    There are ways to get a lot of inspiration from M:tR into the Curse, if you look into it. For example, while Geist seems much more to be a spiritual successor of Ressurection, MtR isn't entirely absent. For example, in MtC 1e there was a MICH stronger leaning towards Iremite mummies being a vast majority,while in 2e they outright say in the book that much fewer mummies still have their original bodies intact from Irem. Because of memory bleed and memory in general, I take that to mean that many mummies are indeed an MtR style combination of Old and New. Even the sample character creation is a mummy who took over the life of a Chinese detective, and that the memories it harbors are a mix of the new host and the old soul. While MtC doesn't share the open mindedness if the actual ORIGIN point of Mummies, 2e goes out of its way to address mummies from south America, China, bog mummies, and even a lovecraftian lost city in Antarctica. In the days of 1e I made a post about various mummies that are no longer "Egyptian", or never even were exactly.

                                    As for Hope, that still exists, though still viewed through CoDs darker angles of things. In 1e, I played a mummy bereft of hope that had given up by 1999, attempted to destroy himself, was tormented by his memories, and was stripped of his high rank in his guild. 2e made Timelessness an interesting twist, with him Ascending in 1912 and his closest memories being of his degraded state in 1999. Now, he holds onto hope that he can find enough memory to both escape his plight AND help all of humanity become "good enough" for A'aru.

                                    Hope is very strongly tied to Memory, as is regret. While in 1999 he wanted to forget everything, his next life in 1912 gives him the hope that he CAN avoid his fate, that if the Shaniatu did something terrible to him, that maybe he can undo it, if he finds a way to be true to both himself and his Gods.

                                    Either way, the Curse still holds true to the idea that your current self can be better than who you used to be. That Heaven IS real, even if it's painfully hard to get to. Touchstones and Memory help anchor you to that hope even as the Judges do their job at.. well, judging everyone as not being good enough. You made a Decree, you ARE good enough. You don't get to go to A'aru but you've got an eternity to find out how to do so. As bad as this Descent was, or as found wanting you've been every time before that, maybe THIS lifetime or the next will be the one when no Judge finds you wanting, or Azar or Sutekh lifts their dead hand to silence the Judges, smiles,and allows you to finally move on.
