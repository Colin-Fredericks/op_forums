                        WARNING: The following contains spoilers from the Book of the Deceived. I should have marked the entire thread a Storyteller Thread, I apologize.

                        They very definitely did not become the Judges. I don't know what that person was smoking. There's a difference between Life and Death, and that aside, the Judges were...Well, not quite the daddy's of the Shan'iatu, they definitely preceded them in an inviolable way.

                        As for the Deceived....Well, I'll say this-The Shan'iatu on the whole got deceived twice, and the Deceived got it thrice.

                        The third time has to do with the Rite of Return. The Restless Stars were the last to go through the entire affair, and they figured the other guilds were going to use them to ascend to Azar's side and then leave them the lowest, so they figured they'd come in as the Judges of Life and Death(That might be where your friend was confused, as the Shan'iatu were out to make themselves a second set). Turns out the other guilds, quite probably mostly the Su-Menent, fucked that up for them, and now the Restless star guildmasters are riding their guild.

                        I'm mostly right on that. I'll need to go over the Judges things again, although I know for a fact that the Shan'iatu did not become the Judges we know.

                                I just finished reading Book of the Deceived (except the SAS at the end). I liked it a lot, but I'll need to re-read it one or two more times before posting a review.

                                Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                My knowledge of it's mostly secondhand and I'm probably ignorant of a common inspiration, but I'm seeing shades of DtF history in the temakh's situation.
                                There are definitely a few parallels, although BotD has the advantage of not adapting one of the most well known myths (and classic literature if you factor in Paradise Lost), so the story has some unexpected twists and a few more layers of ambiguity.

                                At first I thought that BotD made it clear that the Shan'iatu became the Judges, then I thought it was clear that they did not. Now I think that we don't know what happened to the other Shan'iatu. Irem and the Shan'iatu were pulled down into Duat after the Rite of Return, but since no one knows what happened to Azar (aside from what Anpu says), it's hard to say whether they successfully became a new set of Judges. Seriously, I'm impressed that the the book was able to reveal so much about the Shan'iatu and Irem while still making the ultimate truths deeply ambiguous - it raises as many questions as it answers.


                                I do have one rules/setting question (so far). Has anyone figured out where it says how to divine a person's True Name? A lot of the Utterances and Affinities reference True Names for additional effects, but I think I missed the section on how to determine that.
