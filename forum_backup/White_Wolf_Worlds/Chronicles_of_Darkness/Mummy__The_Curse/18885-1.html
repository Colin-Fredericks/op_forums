    Fair enough; like I said, I don't really know anything about gadgets.

    As for the relationship between Essence and Sekhem, they strike me as very different things. Essence is an ephemeral product of emotions, actions, and events taking place in the physical world - a fear spirit feeds on the essence produced when people (and possibly animals) experience fear, while a car spirit feeds on the essence produced when a car is driven. Werewolves can gain essence by eating the flesh of humans or wolves, but I suspect that the essence comes from the act of eating and the emotions experienced by the prey, rather than being something present in the flesh itself. Likewise with human sacrifice.

    Sekhem, on the other hand, appears to be a sort of life-energy that suffuses the physical world. Unlike essence, it's physical, albeit invisible, and, when concentrated, can even by detected and measured by currently existing scientific tools. Where it comes from, exactly, is something of a mystery, but living things don't seem to generate it; they're suffused with it, and when they die it is released and (at least in some cases) sinks into Duat. If I were to compare it to anything, it actually seems conceptually most similar to vitae, and It's tempting to suggest that vitae is actually a specialized form of Sekhem that vampires and the strix are able to "digest."

        Originally posted by StSword View Post
        Where did you get the idea that sekhem comes from the gods? Sekhem comes from here, life itself, and is sent to the judges, not the other way around.

        The gods created life and the world. And traditional Egyptian mythology describes the universe based on the ebb of the Nile- lifeforce, like the Inundation, is seen as a blessing from the gods that is given and renewed regularly for the living. Many myths portray one god or another breathing life into mortal bodies. The myth of the Aten goes even further and portrays the light of the Sun as a daily revitalization of all living things. Some said that lifeforce literally flows into the body as you draw in a breath, and leaves with every exhale. Credit for all of this- the wind, the Nile, and the rising of the Sun...this is all credited directly to the Gods. Sekhem isn't like Glamour or Essence. It reverberates through the living, sure. Also, through the stars and the Earth and geomantic patterns. It isn't emanating from living beings, though. Its more moving through them and between them, cycling endlessly between the real world and wherever Duat is in the cosmic scheme of things.

        That's my metaplot answer. My out of game answer is that magic items are really kinda a Mummy's "thing". Vampires dance there way through social encounters, enslaving the minds of mortals with ease. Mages unlock secrets with preternatural sights and reshape magic in a thousand tricky ways. Werewolves kick the crap out of their enemies. Changelings excel at the art of dreams and promises. So here we are with Mummies. Dealing with magical artifacts are basically what they are all about. But necessity, really. They literally depend on magical toys to return to life (okay, toys and sacrifices, but mostly toys). Their whole society, the secret clubs they hang out in, all of those are literally based around the creation and study of a particular sort of magical doohicky. These guys spent their entire lives getting schooled in the art of making objects of power, and have spent the majority of their several thousand years of unlife hunting down said creations. So I ask you...if there was EVER a time that a supernatural entity should be able to mess around with a magic item usually off limits to all but one specific category of supernatural....who would that be? Okay, yes, probably Mages. But if there were TWO who should be able to do that, I defy you to say the second one isn't a goddamn Mummy!

                The thing is, though, that mummies aren't interested in "magical items" in a general sense; in fact, their goals (or, rather, the goals imposed on them by the Judges) are very specific. Their job is to send Sekhem to Duat, and they do this by (from their own perspective) reclaiming the works of lost Irem and those crafted by later people using the stolen secrets of the Nameless Empire. Artifacts, fetishes, tokens, and the like don't concern them, because they're only superficially similar to the relics they're actually interested in.

                In the World of Darkness, "magic item" is a pretty arbitrary category. Artifacts, fetishes, and relics are all inanimate objects that have useful supernatural properties, but each type of item has an independent origin and operates according to different principals. Saying that mummies are interested in magic items is like saying that astronomers are interested in things which produce visible light, and suggesting that they therefore probably know as much about lighting fixtures and bioluminescence as they do about stars.

I don't think that even holds up given the items in the core book specifically identified as Relics. Sure, some are from Irem, and some maybe could have been from some unknown splinter of Irem lore....but definitely not every last item. The Keening Box of Karl Von Becker has a specific origin that doesn't involve Irem or Egyptian folklore, for example. There also an Effigy of a toy car that seems to have had Sekhem latch onto it as a result of trauma and fate rather than design. The playing field is simply wider than you say it is. I say no harm in making it wider still. Sekhem favors first the reanimated bodies of the Arisem, second Relics designed (often deliberately, sometimes by coincidence) to house that magic, and finally other items of power. Remember that Sekhem doesn't just lie dormant in an object. It flows. The LIfeweb is an obvious manifestation of this. Less seen is the way Sekhem flows between the living, passing in and out like a breath. So too can it flow into and out architecture. That was the whole point of Irem's geomantic pillars. So, what reason is there to say it does not flow through more unique objects of power? Indeed, I would say any such object by its very nature creates permutations in Fate (after all, whatever its intended effect is going to have some impact on reality, big or small, and thus will shift the tides of Fate in the aftermath). Sekhem is drawn to such things like iron to a magnet.

                                    Look, at the end of the day we will all play the game our own way. For me personally, I look at all those WOD books teeming with their various magical items and I can almost hear them whisper "Hey,I'm a potential adventure waiting to happen!" And for my part, if I see a great adventure waiting to happen I'm not going to be discouraged from telling it just because some uppity wizard or wolf thinks it should stay in their little clubhouse. Doubly so because its a simple window into crossover stories if the ST is in need of one.

                                            Originally posted by nothri View Post


                                            'Those crafted by later people using the stolen secrets of the Nameless Empire', huh? I don't think that even holds up given the items in the core book specifically identified as Relics. Sure, some are from Irem, and some maybe could have been from some unknown splinter of Irem lore....but definitely not every last item. The Keening Box of Karl Von Becker has a specific origin that doesn't involve Irem or Egyptian folklore, for example. There also an Effigy of a toy car that seems to have had Sekhem latch onto it as a result of trauma and fate rather than design. The playing field is simply wider than you say it is. I say no harm in making it wider still.
                                            I'm well aware that not every relic is literally built on the secrets of Irem (though the most potent ones tend to be), but that seems to be what the Arisen believe. They tend to have a pretty narrow and warped world view, which tells them that all human civilizations are inheritors of Irem's lore and imitators of its glory. All human crafts were originated or perfected in Irem, and as far as they're concerned that means that all man-made objects that are genius enough in their construction to channel Sekhem are the rightful property of the Judges.

                                            Their definition of a "relic" is therefore quite broad, but it is also fairly specific. Relics are products of human artifice and are powered by Sekhem. If an object doesn't meet those two qualifications, then it isn't a relic. Vestiges, on the other hand, are often created by chance, and I would generally agree with the idea that some or all of the momentos prized by Sin Eaters would also qualify as vestiges. As for the toy car effigy, it's something of an exceptional case, and it's noteworthy that it's creation involved what might arguably be called a human sacrifice, and that a Mummy was present and using its powers at the time, so Sekhem was probably flowing through the area in more than the usual quantities.

                                            But you are, of course, correct that anybody can run the game any way that they like. I personally don't really see the need for Mummies to pursue the magical items used by other splats - genuine relics don't seem to be that rare in any case, and they're unique in that pretty much anyone can use many of them - but if you disagree that's fine. Having Mummies go after artifacts and fetishes in the like would obviously be a good way to build conflict between them and the other supernatural beings in the setting, so I could see it being an attractive option if that's the kind of game you want to run.
