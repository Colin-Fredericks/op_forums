            I am pleased to see that my favorite line that I wrote for the book made it in, speaking to the absolute immortality of the Deceived. Hope you all enjoy the implications.

            "Should the world be scoured so clean of life and remnants thereof that no trace of humanity remains, then the eternal echoes of the mummy’s True Name throughout the cosmos shall determine where the sahu must resurrect again and again until the end of time. Some future epoch may see lonely Restless Stars rise upon the burning sands of Mars, staring up at the red giant filling most of the sky as they struggle to hold onto fading recollections of Earth like worn tombstones scoured of epitaphs."

            Of course, on the same track, consider what sorts of ways the Affinity Eternal Legend might replenish a Deceived's cult if humanity were to go extinct. "It has not yet come to pass that no possible cultists could be recruited by Fate within the usual time intervals for the Affinity. Should that tragedy become so, providence is patient and inevitable; it will, in the fullness of time, find replacements somehow." Yes, that means that one mummy manifesting this magic could potentially ensure that sapient life evolved anew upon the planet to eventually worship her. Or it could also mean that the magic could drag alien explorers from other worlds to unearth and give worship to a dead god of a dead planet.

                                        (As a disclaimer of sorts as I've only read Chapter 1 as of this post)

                                        I kinda like the ambiguity of Chapter 1 in regards to the use of the term "Judges" actually.

                                        The 42 Judges mentioned in that chapter as the "mothers and fathers" of the Shan'Iatu are celestial beings seemingly on par but distinct and different in nature form the Gods. They reside in the paradise that mortals go to after Duat has purged them of sins.

                                        The 42 Judges that the Arisen seem to be bound too dwell in Duat and at least from a human viewpoint don't really seem to be living in paradise.

                                        I think the whole thing leaves it nicely "up-to-the-ST" about IF the Judges of Duat (the realm of the dead) are the same Judges as that the Shan'Iatu served, or the result of the Rite of Return (which by the way it being sorta "timeless" makes a lot of the head scratching over "when" it happened make a lot more sense now) and the Shan'Iatu getting to have their cake and eat it too. In so far as they finally got to get into the afterlife and got take Irem with them.

                                        I'm also kinda interested in what's up with the other Nameless Gods now. Anpu and Azar are accounted for nicely. There is also that line about Sutek. Still leaves the Nameless Lion, Bull and Falcon to be accounted for. Hmmm...wonder if that will be covered by Dreams of Avarice or if its another thing left for the ST to decide.

                                        Liked that Anpu and not Ammut seems to be in command of the Demons of Duat. I had a pet theory they served either the Judges or the Devourer. I did not see them serving Anpu so its a pretty awesome surprise for me. Does make me wonder how Shezmu fits into all that then? Is he Anpu's "dragon" so to speak?

                                        Also, am I the only one that got a big idiot grin when the last bit about the Heretic was mentioned? Considering that Mummy is a pretty dark game, its nice to have a "big good" in the background. Plus his quest for Apotheosis and the results of him achieving it seem to be far more far reaching then I maybe first envisioned "he's an artist" indeed. Honestly, he's starting to get up to Count F***ing Dracula and Verney levels of cannon NPC popularity for me now.

                                        "There is no God in the Badlands." - Sunny

                                                Thundercats makes so much sense now.....Thinking over it I really am wondering now just who the Judges in Duat are and what the hell happened to the other Shan'iatu, what are the Nameless Gods and what is the nature of this Fate that has so strong an agency with mummies?... What has really impressed me about the book is the interlocking pieces of creepy that all help to make up just how the Deceived are these boogey-men in the World of Darkness without it simply being body horror squick. From being eternal monstrosities of Fate, whose terror can evoke worship, the Cthulhu F'tang! of Al-Runihura, to the way new powers like Ever-Dying Name, Horror Among Horrors, Unspeakable Name, Word of Life, their version of Blessed is the God-King, Call Down the Black Huriyah, Forgetting the Name(throwback!), etc. Everything just wraps together to make the Restless Stars SCARY even to things that go bump in the night. Wow.

                                                The Arisen do not fear much in the World of Darkness, nor should they.
