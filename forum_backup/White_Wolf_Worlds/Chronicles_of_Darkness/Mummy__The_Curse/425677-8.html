                These are my thoughts as well. The initial blurb given about the book promised loads of relics, powers, and antagonists but an initial skim is just showing a lot of words saying, "Here's what the Guilds are up to."

                Even the beginning of the book states that Sothic turns are periods where all kinds of magic and Fate-inspired events take place, but (again, initial skim) the only chapter that really seems to uphold that is the first one, which of course has all kinds of cool unique hooks in it because it's the first one. Other than talking about the three phases of the Turn, there's a very subtle sort of campaign structure or story in the first chapter, too.                 The SAS seems long, too.

                                                        Just finished the book and I'm pretty pleased overall. It's not the same level of crazy good that Book of the Deceived was (though Dreams of Avarice may well match up), but it's still a damn good book.

                                                        The settings are the bulk of the book, and they range in quality. The First Turn is definitely the best of the three, as it rather clearly lays out several plot threads and provides a very cinematic view of a time far removed from our own while still being very playable; it makes me want to start my next chronicle during. The Second Turn is probably the weakest, throwing out a lot of shallow info about the mortal world while not really offering enough to be compelling as chronicle material. At times, it feels downright textbook-y, which is a death sentence for historical content to just about anyone but a dedicated student, and I feel like it could've benefited from a tighter narrative focus. Turn Three is interesting in that it shakes up Arisen culture and offers a whole host of weird factions that tie in well into history and all act as satisfying threads for a story, but doesn't ever lead it to a real point, instead just offering a Storyteller a lot of things to extensively build off of.

                                                        The section on Storyteller advice is essential and should've been in core; it's incredibly well-written and offers tons of pointers for running a game as complex as Mummy, in terms of flashbacks and setting weirdness. The structure of an "Interwoven Chronicle" and the suggestions that the Arisen don't operate linearly with time are great inspiration that will see a ton of use.

                                                        Overall, the book is very light on mechanics, offering a handful of NPC statblocks and one new Utterance that I can only see either being banned or required for a chronicle. I wasn't terribly fond of the final bit of the Avarice Chronicle, but I haven't loved the rest of it either, so results may vary.

                                                        The lack of lore revelations surprised me; for a book that pulls back the curtain on Memory, there was unexpectedly little in terms of facts that really shake things up or even hints towards mythology.

                                                        Sothis Ascends is solid; not amazing, not bad. You'll either use everything in it or probably ignore 90% of the book, but if the premise interests you at all, grab it.

                                                        Remi. she/her. game designer.
