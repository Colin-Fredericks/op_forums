        Session 3

        Oh man, 2015 has not been a good year for gaming. For either of my groups. From illness to concussions to work schedules, I think we had to reschedule this game four times in the last week. Ah well, we're back at least, and we ran for a solid seven hours to make up for lost time.

        We picked up right where the last session ended, with the characters still seated at the round table along with their cultists. They had pronounced their decision to focus on Kotira when a chill passed through the room. A moment later the room's only empty chair was occupied by a fit, healthy woman who appeared to be in her sixties, wearing the same gold-and-green outfit seen in the glass cabinet behind her. She bowed her head respectfully towards the two Deceived, and told them that she knew of a dark secret at the heart of Kotira's cultists. Alongside the lord Siranuthis, she had been responsible for discovering the bloodthirsty appetites of certain highly placed members of the cult.

        Imhotep was transported back to the past. He remembered standing on a rooftop, looking down at a street below. The sound of waves was behind him. He was watching a hunt unfold, a hunt with mortal men as both predator and prey. A dark-skinned man was struck down from behind by a pale, European fellow. Unfortunately, the memory ended there, without any other context.

        Dalila, meanwhile, asked the newcomer her name. She said she was known as Yuda Alamsyah, a loyal servant of the Temakh when she had been alive. Those words caused Imhotep to do a double-take, but Dalila pieced together the situation pretty promptly. Clearly a ghost, and probably anchored to that outfit in the glass casing. The other cultists seemed largely unperturbed by her presence, so they took her advice. Cultists were dispatched to discover whether this family, the "van Cleeks", were still employed among the ranks of Kotira's cultists.

        While they waited to hear back, Yuda explained more about her role in Imhotep's prior life. Generations ago, Imhotep had befriended an important Dutch businessman, a high-ranking cultist of one of the Arisen. He had hoped to milk the man for information, but it quickly became a lot more interesting when they discovered that this man, van Cleek, harboured a secret desire to commit acts of murder and torture. It transpired that the van Cleeks were believed to have a family curse. Once a generation, one among them would be driven to kill. Imhotep fostered these desires, pushing the man to murder locals and slaves, working his way up to a white man or woman before he could reveal the monster to the rest of Batavian society and thus throw the reputation of Kotira's cultists into ruin. Before he could quite graduate to that level, however, van Cleek committed suicide.

        The story told, the group wanted to make the most of their power while they remained at Sekhem 9. They checked over their files once more, and decided to throw themselves at Mot-tan. Given their monstrous visages and their ability to disguise their true appearance with Face of Endless Lies, they figured they could launch a massive attack on the isolated Mummy, do a lot of damage, and leave any survivors with the belief that they had been assaulted by Shuankhsen.

        Disguised in heavy clothing and accompanied by four cultists, carrying a mix of blades, guns, and magic (one of them was a dancer disciple of Dalila's, with access to the Phantom Arsenal Numen), they approached Mot-tan's riverside slum. They were confronted by a group of guards near the entrance, but Dalila bulled her way past them with a solid Intimidation roll. They tried to navigate the twisting maze of the slum without much success, when they were ambushed while squeezing through the gap between two makeshift buildings.

        The cultists fought off a wave of Mot-tan's thugs to the group's rear, while Imhotep grappled with a big, muscled enemy cultist who blocked the way forward. Trapped between these groups with nothing to reach, Dalila boosted her Strength to 9 and surged up the side of the building to reach two skinny young men who were using the high ground to bombard the group with concrete blocks. The fight took some time, with Imhotep being badly wounded by his opponent. Their Lone Star guards took a few wounds from the blades of their attackers, but Dalila was largely unscathed by the time she hurled her final attacker from the rooftop into the midst of the guards, who promptly dispatched the man.

        They continued on, relying on their cultists' sense of direction to find their way through the slum to Mot-tan's home. When they were only a short distance from the building, Imhotep used Name the Extinguished Star to summon up a vision of Mot-tan. They found him in the building just ahead, rallying his cultists to defend him. He was an Egyptian man of medium build, wearing an iron mask that ended just above his chin where his goatee was adorned with little occult trinkets. He was wearing a white cotton robe with details picked out in gold thread. With him was a slender European man they recognised as Haig, Mot-tan's Sadikh, and a young Indonesian woman. At least six enemy cultists were there with them.

        The plan was simple. One of the Deceived's cultists had a molotov, which would be used to slow down Mot-tan. Dalila would remove Haig from the equation. The other cultists would be driven off by a mixture of Sybaris and their Lone Star guards. Dalila and Imhotep made sure their Stamina was at 9 and used Face of Endless Lies to grow to Size 6, and then they charged the door. Dalila, flames dancing across her fingers from Power of Re, smashed down the door in a single blow and they ran inside.

        The two groups squared off. Imhotep led the charge with a howl, an unearthly, garbled sound from his deformed throat, as he tore his mask away to reveal his undead flesh. Sybaris overwhelmed Mot-tan's defenders in an instant. Five of them fled without a word, and the sixth fell to his knees and begged for his life. Dalila dodged around the mortal, slipped under the gauze curtain between her and her target, and hit Haig hard enough to wind him. One of the Lone Star guards tore down the curtain a few moments later, and a molotov sailed through the air to splash across Mot-tan. His robes caught fire and engulfed him in a pillar of flame. Bullets flew, and the Lone Star dancer ran past to attack Haig.

        For their part, the Mummy and his Sadikh seemed entirely caught off guard. Mot-tan struggled to cast an Utterance, taking two turns to perform Blessed is the God King. He manifested as an enormous man with a roaring lion's head, and bulled into Lone Star cultists. He knocked one man down and tore out the guard's throat, but he was coming apart far faster than he could heal. Then Imhotep dodged in and shut down his ability to heal with Kiss of Apep moments before Haig died, skull crushed by a blow from the Lone Star dancer.

        At this, Mot-tan yelled to the young woman, "Atya, run!" She had been seemingly unaffected by the Sybaris, but at those words she bolted. Looking at the situation, Imhotep figured he could afford to go after her. Telling Dalila to finish Mot-tan off, her chased after her. It wasn't a long chase. Imhotep ran her down at the edge of the river, pinned her to the ground, and dragged her back to the building a few minutes later.

        The fight hadn't lasted much longer. Dalila used Power of Re to quench the flames burning on Mot-tan, and ordered her cultists to stand back. She was pretty sure he was on the verge of death, only alive thanks to the power of Sealing the Flesh, and that just meant she had to slow him down until his Pillars ran out. She used Whispers to My Body to become like a liquid, and wrapped herself around Mot-tan's body. Unable to reach the cultists, and unable to harm Dalila, Mot-tan collapsed only a few turns later. Her Temakh's blessing filled her immediately, granting another Descent reset as she plucked a relic from Mot-tan's corpse. It was a simple gold wedding ring, but it was more than that, more than even a relic. Holding it, she knew it was a Talisman, containing the Blessed is the God King Utterance. Of the relic's powers, she could get nothing beyond the knowledge that it held fairly little power (2 dots).

        Imhotep returned with Atya and threw her to Dalila. He then walked over to Mot-tan's cowering cultist and shot the man in the head. He dropped to Memory 2, avoided a derangement, and gained a Descent reset for himself in the progress.

        Dalila interrogated Atya. She was sixteen years old, and Mot-tan's wife, being groomed to be the next hierophant of his cult. When asked about her master's goals in Jakarta, she said that Haig had told her that Mot-tan had been driven from Europe by the duplicitous lies of other Maa-Kep, and he was living in Indonesia in order to collect himself and meditate on his failures in peace. Imhotep dragged the girl to the side and killed her then, something they regretted a few minutes later when they realised they could have asked her for the location of Mot-tan's tomb.

        Imhotep ordered their guards to collect the body of their fallen comrade, and the group made a beeline back to the waiting car. They were a lot faster heading out than they were going in, and reached the edge of the slum without any further trouble. They left, the only survivors of their attack driven mad with Sybaris.

        They returned to their mountain retreat still brimming full of power. Imhotep had the cult arrange for a private session at a nearby shooting range, where he could practice his skills without interacting with any of the range's staff. Dalila, on the other hand, conscripted most of her cultists to finish construction of a project she had began decades ago. The building of an Amkhat pit. If she could finish it and gather the components in the next 24 hours, she could put her Sekhem into an Amkhat rather than allowing it to waste away. She slaved away at it, drawing blood from cultist after cultist, and managed to finish the hieroglyphs before the day was out.

        The cult was able to procure both a snake and a falcon for her in that time, but the closest they could get to a jackal was at a nearby zoo. Dalila waited until the sun set, slid into the zoo through its gutters and piping, and reformed near the jackal enclosure. A brief encounter with a guard left him Sybaris-wracked and trapped in Twilight. She found an isolated jackal in its cage for the night, draped it in herself, and the combination of Whispers to my Body and Grip of Death left it unable to fight back and unable to make a sound. It died quietly, and she was able to drag it out of the zoo without alerting security.

        On her way out, however, she realised that there was something undoubtedly geometric in the layout of the zoo. Not only that, but near one of the dealings she smelt something... something that reminded her distinctly of the pit she had spent all day building. Someone at the zoo, she figured, had had much the same idea as herself.

        She returned to the retreat, to the pit. A frenzied few hours followed, building the creature as her Sekhem dwindled ever closer to its threshold. She completed it on the verge of descending, breathing the creature to life. A jackal's head and paws, a serpent's body and fangs, a falcon's wings, and hive's worth of dead bees, lay stitched together in the pit. In those final moments, Tenh Tetabeh, "the Winged Poison" to its master's followers, was born.

                    Session 4

                    Imhotep and Dalila were now Sekhem 8, and thus able to assume human forms. Imhotep ditched most of his covering garb, but chose to keep a heavy coat. Something about it felt right to his Temakh, and he was eager enough to please. Dalila, meanwhile, chose to stick with the traditional Islamic clothing she'd been sporting, at least for now. They spent the rest of the night meditating to restore pillars and Willpower, and preparing for another trip to the city.

                    With Tenh Tetabeh following in Neter-Khetet, the group came down from their retreat and entered Jakarta. They decided to investigate the Amkhata pits Dalila had discovered at a zoo on the edge of town. The cult purchased them entry tickets, and they slipped inside. Imhotep used Face of Endless Lies to disguise himself as a bland-looking Indonesian man, while Dalila made a few subtle changes with Whispers to My Body to prevent recognition. There were quite a few staff around, surreptitiously keeping an eye out for a certain missing jackal.

                    In daylight, the zoo was quite intriguing. It had apparently been moved to this location in 1966, and there was a definite sense of Iremite geometry in its construction. Sandstone paths wove between exhibits in patterns that, if seen from the air, could form archaic hieroglyphs and networks. Squatting among these paths away from the exhibits, appearing a bit out of place, was a large white building. Behind it was a large enclosure, fenced off with a tall concrete wall topped with razor wire and monitored by security cameras. Through the glass door the players could see a reception area, staffed by a pleasant-looking secretary. The enclosure was where Dalila had smelt the rotting flesh and drying blood that reminded her so forcefully of her own experiments in Amkhat generation, and every now and then the characters could almost taste the aniseed and honey smell of Sekhem.

                    They found seats in the shade and sat down to watch the building. Before long they spotted a group of men and women, some wearing white lab coats, leaving the building. Imhotep absorbed that they were wearing identification badges with the company name "Killwarden Memorial Conservation" on them. Satisfied, Imhotep and Dalila moved on. They placed a call telling their cultists to research the name, and went to begin work on their other project.

                    Similar observation spots were set up outside the glass-and-steel skyscraper that formed the headquarters of the Batavian Maritime Banking and Insurance Firm, Kotira's cult. The group was keeping an eye out for anyone that could be one of the van Cleeks, when they received a call from the cultist responsible for the Killwarden Memorial Conservation research. He told them that, after a bit of digging, it turned out that a group called LDI was the principle donor to the fund. This pleased the Deceived greatly. The enemy of their enemy was manufacturing Amkhata, and they saw no reason to interfere.

                    They spoke briefly with their driver, an aspiring singer named Daryat, until they spotted a woman leaving the banking firm's offices. She had a look that resembled the man Imhotep remembered, the old van Cleek. They let her pass unremarked, but had their cultist take her picture to ensure that Dalila would be able to replicate it later.

                    After a brief expedition to collect clothing that looked close enough to pass master, they returned and Dalila changed shape to match the woman. She went inside, and found herself momentarily stumped by the invention of the card key. After claiming that she'd forgotten something upstairs and left her card in the car, Dalila was able to persuade an attendant on duty to let her in. She saw that several of the floors in the elevator were labelled as the offices of the bank, and decided to explore.

                    The first floor she went to, the uppermost, was unlit and quiet. No one was in evidence. A statue of Anubis holding a set of scales dominated the lobby. A brief exploration turned up nothing beyond several lavish offices with computer equipment, so she descended to the second highest, the third floor of the building.

                    A few people were still working here. Walking through the office, Dalila saw a name plate labelled "Marianne van Cleek". Guessing that it was the office of the woman whose form she wore, Dalila ducked inside and rummaged around. She learned that Marianne was an accountant dealing with some of the biggest accounts in the firm, but there were a lot of private correspondences that she couldn't read on account of them being written in Dutch. She saw an email on the woman's computer from a "Dietrich van Cleek", and a sly chat with one of the other workers revealed that Dietrich was Marianne's brother. He was also much more important, and had left about half an hour previously to speak with a "Pierre" in his private apartment elsewhere in the building.

                    Dalila figured that this was a good thing. She'd been lucky that Marianne speaking Indonesian apparently wasn't a big deal, and she didn't want to be addressed by someone who might expect her to speak another language. She decided to leave rather than press her luck, returned to the car waiting outside, and relayed this information to Imhotep.

                    Imhotep, in turn, promptly used Name the Extinguished Star, Tier 2, to spy on Dietrich van Cleek. He turned out to be a blonde man in his 40s, a lot paler than his sister, and sporting the beginnings of a pronounced beer gut. He was reclining in an expensive-looking couch, part of a truly high class apartment from what Imhotep could make out, while a thin, older Indonesian man paced back and forth in front of him. Presumably, this was the "Pierre" the woman had referred to earlier.

                    Pierre was in a panic. He didn't think the fighting could continue. Ever week they would lose a few men, and they couldn't stop the attacks. They would die where they thought they were the safest. When Dietrich expressed his faith that the Mistress would protect them, Pierre looked doubtful. And when Dietrich calmly stated that, if worst came to worst, they could always raise up "Aqhat", Pierre seemed downright shocked and taken aback. He finally stormed out of the meeting, muttering that he hoped Dietrich wouldn't be among the dead himself.

                    Imhotep continued watching. Dietrich was growing visibly uneasy from the observation, and went to his computer. Imhotep couldn't read the files, and resolved to study them through his vision in order to learn Dutch. Dalila, meanwhile, ordered Daryat to tail Pierre. They followed him to a local restaurant, where she slipped inside after disguising herself as a different nondescript woman. She sat down within earshot of him, and several minutes later he was joined at his table by a handsome man, perhaps of Italian or Greek descent, who introduced himself as "Mike".

                    Pierre was clearly anxious. He asked that they confirm, again, the terms of the deal. Mike, speaking Indonesian with a thick American accent, blithely assured him that he would have everything in return for the information he had asked for. And he showed him the contents of a briefcase he'd brought. Dalila managed to sneak a look, and saw a large syringe containing an amber-coloured liquid and several mechanical devices she didn't recognise. There was a whispered exchange that she was unable to hear, and after that the conversation turned deliberately innocuous. Mike asked about places to see while he was in the city, Pierre offered some token suggestions, and the pair went their separate ways after a short meal.

                    This exchange gave the Deceived a lot to mull over, but they weren't really sure what to make of it. They decided they'd keep an eye on Pierre the next day, but in the meantime they decided to investigate the nightclub that, according to Adit, would contain a clue to the location of a newly spawning Seba crafted by the Singer.

                    The nightclub, Novus, was very exclusive. The queue outside dragged on for hours, but the group was patient. When they finally got inside, they found it had two stories. The first was a matte black dance floor, with a mirror placed above it. The second floor was reserved for the VIPs, and after asking about they found two important things. A singer named Jemi was due to perform, and that only truly attractive individuals tended to get invited to the top floor. The only way up was to be a VIP, or to be personally escorted one. Well, that was too easy for the group. A moment in the bathroom and the use of Face of Endless Lies, and both Dalila and Imhotep waltzed out sporting the most attractive bodies and faces they could conceive.

                    It was minutes before an overweight man invited Dalila (and "your friend too, if he wants to tag along"), to the top floor. They found that, from above, the mirror was actually one-way allowing the VIPs to look down on the dancers below. Minutes later, the music died down and Jemi stepped up to perform. She was a young local nobody that the owners had apparently hand-picked to give a performance, but she was meant to be quite good. And she was... but she didn't make much of an impression. Imhotep was too busy feeding on her performance to rebuild his Ren, and few people gave any real notice to the song. As it ended, he also had a brief vision. He saw the stars and sun whirl across the sky, showing the passing of four days. He saw the rooftop of the Shangri-la Hotel at sundown. And he saw the manifestation there of a Seba, built from the songs of ancients.

                        Ok, so after a few months of back and forth, we have our newest Deceived character!

                        Rakhen-Roh, the Hidden, bearer of the Painter's Temakh. The Deceived of Jakarta are grossly outnumbered by the Arisen, so they must make up for that difference in guile. Rakhen-Roh provides this in spades. Where Imhotep leads as the commander of their little cadre, and Dalila fights at the front as the star-sworn warrior when battle must be joined, Rakhen-Roh is the scout and spy who provides the information they need to act. He is a short, plain little man with a light complexion, although his arms and hands have been marked with horrific burn scars at the behest of Tutkepertanu. He prefers long sleeves and gloves to keep these marks hidden.
                        He is also an initiate in the Shattered Crook, and has all the hallmarks of that order's paranoid secrecy. Every time he awakens he takes a new name for himself, making Rakhen-Roh merely the latest in a long, long line. Jakarta's Arisen population is dominated by the Su-Menent, five of the city's twelve Mummies belong to that Guild, and so he came here at the behest of the secret masters of his Movement to prove himself. He sees hidden signs and signals from others of the Shattered Crook wherever he goes, telling him what he must do in order to advance in the Movement's ranks. When it finally comes time to act, he prefers to do so from among a throng of minions and lesser creatures, a distraction so that his own actions might go unnoticed.
                        His Affinities are Face of Endless Lies, Beast Companion, and Voice of Temptation. His Utterances are Words of Dead Hunger and Words of Dead Glory. He also bears three Seba, To Paint with the Blood of Man (2 dot), A Thousand Woven Stings (2 dot), and Of the Dark Beyond Time (3 dot), which I have detailed below:


                        To Paint with the Blood of Man (2 dot Seba)

                        Power: Near every culture has a tradition in which the human form is considered a canvas. This Seba plays upon these ideas. Man is an imperfect picture waiting to be redrawn, perfected, or ruined. The bearer has the power to enact these changes in but a moment, so long as he knows the name of his subject. He can call upon the fluids of the human body to provide his paint, peel the skin for his canvas, shatter bones into more pleasing arrangements. The target must struggle inside their own mind to remember their true form, or else be broken beyond all recognition by the divine vision of the bearer.
                        The bearer may make a touch attack. If it hits, they can reflexively roll Manipulation + Expression, minus the target’s Resolve. Success indicates that the bearer was able to force the desired changes into the target’s body, with every success inflicting a point of damage. Blood seeps from the victim’s eyes, or wells up from their pores, scars and open wounds ripple across their body, even as bones change shape and shatter under the pressure. When attacking mortals, instead of inflicting damage the bearer may choose to impose a single Physical Flaw instead. This Flaw is permanent.
                        If the bearer knows the True Name of his target, the damage is aggravated. Otherwise, even against Mummies, the damage is lethal.

                        Curse: Inflicting change upon another opens oneself to change as well. For the rest of the turn, the bearer of this Seba does not benefit from their defence, armour, or Sekhem for the purpose of resisting attacks, either physical or magical.



                        A Thousand Woven Stings (2 dot Seba)

                        Power: The Scorpion Banners of Irem flew at the head of one of the world’s first great armies. To the primordial tribes that stood in their path, they bore with them an unstoppable wave of violence inevitably ending in the tribe’s capitulation and slavery. To the slave-soldiers who marched beneath them, they represented the mystic might of their Shan’iatu masters and the certainty of their eventual victory.
                        And to the artists who made them, they were a work of magic in their own right, a ritual to birth new magic from the stars.
                        The bearer of this Seba is a living banner, a symbol to his allies of their impending victory. When he strikes at an enemy, all his allies gain +1 to their rolls to attack that enemy. Whenever he strikes the final blow on a foe, his allies regain a point of Willpower.

                        Curse: Enemies innately sense that the bearer of this Seba is a rallying point for his allies, and may well prioritise the bearer over other, more threatening targets. Should the bearer fall, his allies shall be devastated. Should someone strike down the bearer, all his allies gain a Phobia of the one who did the deed. This phobia is permanent.




                        Of the Dark Beyond Time (3 dot Seba)

                        Power: The Seba created by Tutkepertanu in life were known as visions, the mystical by-products of his artistic expression. They allowed their bearer to change the world through visual art, to make real what was portrayed in colour and shape.
                        But everything has its other, its opposite, and so it was with the visions of the Painter. In the great palace of Irem, a child was born in the dark. And there they were kept, for all of their life, until the day they died. When light was brought to the chamber, it was found that the child had become an artist in their own right, but none who witnessed that work would ever see light again, nor be able to describe what it was they saw in those moments before their sight was taken. From this child’s masterpiece was born a vision of the absence of colour, the absence of shape, a vision of the universe from before the age of light and a vision of what the universe shall be once the reign of the stars has ended. This darkness, this terrifying nothing, can be gifted to others by the bearer of this Seba.
                        The bearer can take an instant action to pass on this “gift”. He spends a point of Willpower and rolls Manipulation + Occult + Sheut, resisted by the target’s Stamina + Composure + Sekhem. If the bearer succeeds, the target is blinded for the rest of the scene.

                        Curse: The legacy of its creator still haunts this Seba. Shadows seem longer, the dark deeper, an infinite variation of black upon black that draws the eye in increasingly hypnotic circles. The bearer suffers double the environmental penalty for taking actions in darkness.

                            Session 5

The Arisen Sesu Ra, Aquila, Sekhet Bastet, and another largely unknown Mummy named Akenakhi were all served by this cult, making it a prime target.

                                                    False Ka Made True (2 dot Seba)

                                                    Power: This Seba may be sacrificed during the construction of an Amkhat in order to imbue the creature's form with an unnatural vitality beyond anything normally possible. It has five Pillar dots, one in each Pillar, and may spend Pillar points like a Sekhem 1 Mummy to boost Physical Attributes (even Dexterity, which it retains the ability to increase at a 1-for-1 rating) or Seal the Flesh. However, due to the inhuman form of the Amkhat, the maximum ratings in each Attribute that it can be boosted to is 10, not 5. It can restore these Pillars by killing mortals (one point per life claimed), and gains one at the dawn of each day.

                                                    It may be possible for the Amkhat to increase these Pillar ratings beyond 1 by feeding on Arisen. And, if it is very lucky (or the world is very unlucky), it might even be able to gain Affinities in the process.

When it is hurt, the Amkhat bleeds a viscous golden ichor that bears the distinct honey and aniseed smell of Sekhem. Any Amkhat in the area when this Amkhat bleeds may feed into existence, counting every point of damage it has suffered as if a human had just died in the area. Lesser Amkhat in particular are drawn to the creature, and a fight in which it is injured may see swarms of smaller monsters appearing. The Amkhat will determine whatever seems the easiest prey in the area (mortals before the Amkhat itself, the Amkhat or one of the Arisen is kind of a coin toss) and seek to feed on the chosen target immediately.

They were structured as a mystery cult, layers upon layers of seemingly pointless intrigue and mystical paraphernalia obscuring any clear command structure. It made it frustratingly difficult to extract information, but Lies was confident that he was among the more trusted of Rekhenaten's people.

Imhotep smiled at this news, relishing the thought of confronting his enemy again. The Tef-Aabhi mason had woken for his Sothic Turn and met with his Guildmaster, Sesu Ra. Lies hadn't yet seen Rekhenaten in person, but he had met with the Arisen's Sadikh, Tobi. He had taken on the identity of a security operative or bodyguard in this life, and had taken up rooms in the Chilankang Country Club where Rekhenaten made him tomb.

Rekhenaten had woken concerned about the presence of some unnatural beings dwelling in the sewers beneath Jakarta. Tobi was tasked with their destruction, and he had asked Lies to be one of those involved in the operation. Lies had called on the Choir of the Dark Stars to probe a little, extracting information from the Sadikh about the beings they would be fighting. He had discovered the reason for Rekhenaten's concern. It was the Master of the Pit who was responsible for the births of these wretches.

                                                            Documents in Lies' possession made Rekhenaten's position as a Flesh Mason clear to Imhotep. He had been breeding populations in Jakarta for generations now. First he had used the Japanese occupation and the Second World War, and in more recent times had used a mixture of high society and people smuggling. By combining traits in certain groups, he had hoped to increase the amount of Sekhem among Jakarta's population as a whole. He had succeeded, but with unfortunate side-effects. Scrolls written in Iremite that Lies had discovered and read revealed secrets kept by the Su-Menent Guild. How this information had come into Rekhenaten's possession was unknown, but they spoke of legends in which demons and other spawn from the stars mingled with humanity before the coming of the Shan'iatu. These bloodlines had a power of their own, and Rekhenaten's actions had caused this power to manifest itself in rather more visual ways. The resulting offspring, deformed and visibly twisted by their demonic heritage, had literally been driven underground by Rekhenaten's cultists.

They agreed that exposing the mutants would bring Rekhenaten's standing into disrepute among the Arisen, but first they wished to see what creations the Flesh Mason had created. They had their cultist Deriyat drive them to Jakarta where they found a tunnel entrance not far from the maintenance access point Lies had said Rekhenaten's people would be using when they mounted their expedition.

The dry tunnel stretched on, although the sell rising from it was a little appalling. If they couldn't find their way to the demon-children, they would bring the creatures to them.

Imhotep's powerful voice never entirely ceased his chant, although he chose to translate his words to Indonesian in case the creatures had developed a negative association towards the tongue of Irem due to their treatment at Rekhenaten's hands. Rakhen-Roh was the first to spot the markings on the wall as they wandered, lost and nearly blind. Faint scratches were dug into the concrete walls, looking for all the world like marks left by fingernails. Beneath them, Rakhen-Roh noticed what seemed to be words written in ink. They were in a cipher he recognised, a code developed by the Shattered Crook.

The outsiders needed to speak to Rile.
