    Originally posted by Cleverest of Things View Post
    True, but it's likey the Maa-Kep and Su-Menent already have their eyes on such a Mummy, and are very eager and willing to earn a reset by mulching a potential heretic.
    Which is why it makes sense to me that mummies who are starting to entertain heretical thoughts would start withdrawing more and more from mummy society. Because of the way their society is structured, it's almost impossible to differentiate "intentionally withdrawing" from "has been busy/not awake", so it's pretty difficult for the secret police to keep tabs on every potential heretic unless those heretics are stupid enough to keep checking in to update everyone on their heresy. Basically, they built a huge security flaw into their culture, in that, if they don't see you then as far as they're concerned you're not doing anything. It is absurdly easy to drop off of the radar as a mummy without anyone even realizing that's weird.

    None of which is to say that you can't get caught. Accidentally running into a Maa-kep while you're pursuing some heretical piece of wisdom, and having that Maa-kep suddenly realize that she earns a reset every time she fucks with you...is the stuff stories are made of. I just think there should be a difference between "you could get caught" and "you will definitely get caught, you would be stupid to even attempt this"

    After all, it's implied that The Heretic (capital letters!) was a high-ranking scribe before he went AWOL. I'd set my bets on him being a Guildmaster of his Nome, wherever that was. If even a guildmaster can fall off the radar long enough to achieve Apotheosis, it's doable for everyone else.

    Otherwise, I basically agree about how most mummies have historically gotten through their unlives.

            Reading the part based on the Call, I was able to figure about the gist of the mechanics of a call. When a cult decides to awaken a mummy, they need a relic that belonged to the mummy, with a bunch of vestiges that would help the call along. The key point being that the relic needed to have belonged to the mummy, probably during his or her breathing days. It then goes on to say, that if they do not have a relic that a mummy had, which is a semi regular occurrence, then they can sacrifice a foreign relic to make the call. However, they said that sacrificing a relic to wake a mummy makes about as much sense as burning down a house to fix a roach problem.

            So, that would indicate to me that...only mummies who posses relics can be awoken on a constant basis of one call after another. If the cult does not have a relic that belonged to the mummy, then they need to get hold of one and sacrifice it to make a call. Given the rarity of relics, this would be a desperate measure.

            In addition, every time a mummy gets awoken their memory drops by one. So... I wonder if too many awakenings in a short period would make for a ravenous beast.

            Another point. To really be awoken on a regular basis, a mummy would need to have made an investment in both relic, and cult. I noticed that, in the 5 pre generated mummies that were given in the supplement, 3 had a cult, one had a relic, and one had a vestige, but not one of them had both a cult and a relic.

                Here is my interpretation of the Call. Amon-tep is a member of the Maa-Kep. During his days in the Egyptian empire, he was an overseer and his prized possession was a beer mug which he would use to drink with his buddies after a hard day of whipping slaves to build the pyramids. The Egyptian empire was one of the greatest civilizations on Earth due the invention of beer (actually, the term those days was mead). His greatest joy was to gather with his friends and drink beer near the Nile.

                Eventually, after he became a mummy, this beer mug became a relic. It was infused with Sekhem and had supernatural power. If someone held it, it would magically fill with the same mead that was invented during the Egyptian empire (or, if it was cursed, would fill with Molson Canadian). It was his prized possession and his cult would use it to awaken him during the centuries. Every time he awoke, he could remember drinking beer in the hot sun overlooking the Nile. Sometimes, his cult would need to issue multiple Calls. Not often, but there was sometimes an emergency situation. The relic would refresh with energy because it was his beer mug.

                Then, one day he awakens and the beer mug was….GONE! When he asked the high priest what happened the priest responded that, during his sleep, another mummy stole his relic and stole the Sekhem so that he could stay awake for a few more weeks. In a furious rage, Amon-tep sought out the offending mummy. Unfortunately, the mummy was asleep, but at least Amon-tep made sure that the cult of the interloper was slaughtered to a man. That is what you get for taking his beer mug.

                Now, every time his cult tries to awaken him, they need to sacrifice a relic or vestige to do it. The cult was able to steal a relic quill for a future awakening…but it is not the same! That was his beer mug! Nothing in the world will ever replace it and now it is gone forever! The quill will work for one Call and then it will be gone forever.

                Somewhere out there, a mummy scribe of the Sesha-Hebsu guild is brooding. Someone stole his quill that his cult has awoken him with for centuries. He remembers drawing rude and obscene pictures on papyrus which were highly amusing to the other scribes, and now some bastard has stolen it. He will find that mummy and make him pay for stealing his prized possession and probably wasting the Sekhem.

                There will be blood, things were going to get really ugly...
