                    Update: I have looked into the Mekhet Clan Book. Here's my take, if anyone's interested.

                    For the purposes of my chronicles, the Hollow Mekhet understanding of their reflection as the Ka is just how they framed it for themselves in terms of the Egyptian beliefs of their time. These beliefs are not the same as Irem: centuries have passed and the meaning of certain concepts has changed. I don't see a solid, useable connection between the Ka pillar of MtC and the Ka of the Hollow Mekhet, so they're just different things for me. The loss of reflection may have some interesting connection to the Mirrors books of WoD, but I have not looked at those yet and probably won't unless I start planning a Mekhet heavy VtR chronicle. There are many weird ephemeral creatures with unique rules in CoD, and the Hollow Mekhet's reflection is just one of these. The Iremite framework used by Mummies and the arcana system used by Mages are not sufficient to embrace or discern all the different permutations of these phenomena, so each creature focuses on those things they can perceive and interact with in a significant way.

                    I do postulate, however, a connection between Shades, Striges and vampires in general. My current idea is that Striges are Shades that have managed somehow at some point to leave the Twilight and enter the physical world and stay there. They have similarities to the Shades (interest in blood, shadowy appearance), but the move from one dimension to the other has also made them different. One important difference is that while Shades remember being a person, Striges have forgotten who they were and where they came from, or have confused ideas about this. Given the Underworld's connection to loss of memory and identity, that's not hard to imagine, even without coming up with a specific reason like having drunk from a particular river. I wonder if, since ghosts become geists by drinking from the rivers, shades might become striges instead. Need to dig deeper in the Geist material to see if it makes sense.
                    Vampires are another evolution of Striges, once they manage to take a body in a deeper way than just possession, but this deprives them again of memory, hence why vampires don't actually remember having connections to the Striges. But yes, they have a type of Strix inside, whose identities and memories have been drowned by the deceased/vampire's own. If we say Striges are Shades who have drunk from the rivers, like Geists are ghosts who have, then vampires are something somewhat analogous (but with plenty differences) to a Sin-Eater.

                    There's a story of the early Mekhet wandering through the Duat, getting lost and trading blood to its denizens in exchange of directions, and thus becoming vampires/Mekhet. I may use that as a way to explain how some Shades became Striges if I don't use the drink from the rivers idea, but I have to define the details of that. Once Striges entered the world through these lost travelers and the deals with them and the blood exchange, they became a feature of the physical living world, and produced the other clans in different ways.

                        One of the characters in the Halifax setting, according to rumor, "obsesses over vampires whose Ka have abandoned their bodies."

There's a type of God-Machine-related demon in Hunter's Mortal Remains supplement that is described in terms that visually and behaviorally evoke a subtype of Strix. Werewolf 2e Core has bird-spirits in Tokyo that sound a lot like the Strix in terms of probable shape, ability to possess corpses, and antipathy for vampires, but the color of the eyes is wrong (red instead of gold) and werewolves have the senses to identify if something is a spirit.                                             Correct. It's their ghost, in all the mechanical and thematic senses.

                                            See above: the Ka that haunts the Hollow Mekhet is a ghost. The book the Hollow were introduced in has its sample Hollow's Ka summoned for direct conversation by a character with a published statblock (Catesby from Shadows of the United Kingdom) that includes Death and Prime but no Spirit. 2e's mechanics for the Hollow in Thousand Years of Night explicitly models the Ka as a ghost, which jives with Hunger in the Black Land's statement on how ghosts are generally perceived in terms of soul-anatomy.

                                            The Hollow Mekhet's Ka behaves in a way consistent with the more horror-story-toned ghosts that the setting is generally concerned with as the more visible divergences from the quieter "Rank 2 person who happens to be dead" and all the references to the Ka as it correlates to hunger concern it as an entity separated from the body.

                                            The Ka of the Arisen is a Ka in a nominally-living body made eternal; the Obrimos have the hardest time learning the Death Arcanum used for ghosts despite being identified with the Ka, so evidently the part of the soul that distinguishes a living body from a dead one doesn't work the same way in living bodies as it does in dead ones.

                                            The story in VtR2e Core is part of a larger pattern of fairy tale-styled origin stories pretty directly riffing on the tale of Snow White — the equivalent stories for the Nosferatu, Gangrel, Ventrue, and Daeva evoke the seven dwarves, the huntsman, the prince, and the titular princess (notably, the Nosferatu, Daeva, and Ventrue stories cannot all have the same princess). The Mekhet story is evoking the witch with her magic mirror, in no small part because mirrors are an evocative narrative device in general.

                                            There is no similar origin story in the Mekhet clanbook, which simply uses mirrors in the described capacity for showing the Ka's manifestations and the signs of its absence.

                                            Setting aside that the word "Duat" doesn't appear anywhere in that story, I get the distinct feeling if they were referring to a specific underworld in the Chronicles cosmology it wouldn't be the one where the river and oases are made of blood.
                                            We see this element of the setting differently, and have a different take on some passages. Given the nature of CoD and nWoD as a toolbox for STs, no big issue. And I believe some things have been deliberately left undefined, while others may even be intentionally contradictory so that their mystery can't be solved in a definite way. It leaves STs more room, it stimulates discussion and curiosity, and it leads different players to develop different categories to make sense of what's in the CoD - case in point, the meaning of Ka and the nature of the Hollow Mekhet's Reflection. The clanbook says the Reflection is a ghost with some unique characteristics (that have little to do with ghosts). I read that as: it's an ephemera, with the rules of ghosts (like other ephemera) plus its special exceptions (like other ephemera), so it's not an actual ghost, but something different. You read it as "it's a ghost." I find both acceptable readings, though I prefer mine.
