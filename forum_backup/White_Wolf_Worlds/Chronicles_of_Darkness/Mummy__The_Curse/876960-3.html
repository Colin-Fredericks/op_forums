        Originally posted by reseru View Post
        I don't think a non-Iremite Mummy should seek relics. They might have a Purpose (or not) but it doesn't have to be about seeking out lost treasures or enacting vengeance.
        Mummies of the Andes were sacrifices, meant to represent their people in Hananpacha, or messengers between Hananpacha and Earth (which can be conflated easily enough) - others were meant to impart strength and guidance to their communities, which could be considered tribal cults in Mummy the Curse terms. As purposes, those are close enough to the Deathless experience for some commonality. I'd suggest they're called up by their people to deal with problems, and have a sense similar to Khepera, applying to seeking out solutions rather than relics (interpreted as wisdom from the gods). Apotheosis wouldn't be a goal to such mummies, but a salve for those whose communities are gone. I'd tone their power down too - analogues of Affinities, but not Utterances - and maybe the ability to empower their people in some way (maybe burning Huaca to do it).

        It would be easy for the Arisen to conclude that such mummies were made via some version of the Rite of Return, more successful than other copies. Only whatever spirituality they're connected to doesn't appear to be the Judges. And they could just as easily be wrong.

        I'd change the Pillars in this case, though I'm not certain how. I'd give them a trait analogous to Sekhem, which would be able to do most of the same things.

                            Originally posted by Ben Linus View Post
                            There is a retread. There is room for other people to have copied Return Rite, which prepares the way for something for the mummies that ascend or almost amount to judges. In the rules and record would be the same, would soon be almost like playing with the Requiem with Dead like Wicked with Kindred as Pijavica.
                            Things like Pijavica work in Requiem because a) the origins of vampirism are unknown in VtR, and b) there are different legends of the hungry dead from all over the world that despite all their differences bear enough similarities to each other that running them with a slightly tweaked vampire template makes sense.

                            The same cannot be said of MtC. Origins of the Arisen are well-detailed, and a lot of game considerations stem from their specificity. More importantly, while many civilizations throughout the ages practiced mummification, only one of them gave rise to legends of mummies as undead monsters hurling horrible curses at those who disturb the peace of their tombs. The Arisen are modeled after a mixture of Universal horror movies and pseudo-Egyptian mysticism in a way that permeates every aspect of the game, from aesthetics to mechanics to story considerations.

                            There's a place in Chronicles of Darkness for many other types of immortal/undead beings, be they Andean mummies or Taoist immortals or Sokushinbutsu or what have you. It's just that Mummy: the Curse is not the game to model their mechanics after. Why would a Chinese Immortal suffer through Descent cycles? Why would a Sokushinbutsu rise from his meditation to recover relics? Trying to shoehorn such beings into the Arisen template makes them look like cheap knockoffs.

                                    Well, pretty much any dead body that rises through magic qualifies as Lifeless, so there's that :P

                                    In all seriousness, though, I'm not trying to discourage anyone. In fact, I'm quite interested in anything people might come up with, because when it comes to Chronicles, I have the "the more the merrier" approach and am never shy to dig up some obscure critter for my games just to mess with my players and their futile attempts to categorize the world into neat little boxes

                                    In fact, part of the reason for my rant is this desire to not have things neatly categorized. I really hated it when oWoD Mummy introduced Taoist Immortals as the same kind of being as the Amenti, it felt extremely shoehorned to me, a wasted opportunity to shake things up and expand the cosmology.

                                    To go back to the Vampire comparison, MtC is more like Masquerade than Requiem, in that it has a very specific origin story for its protagonists. Due to that, I'd prefer it if non-Iremite mummies were handled more like Kindred of the East were handled in Masquerade than like Asian domains are handled in Requiem. Something different, something that gives the Arisen pause when encountered, something bearing certain similarities but recognizably different.

                                            You could crib Beast's Satiety mechanics and refer to it literally as a Sekhem battery: basically, the non-Arisen mummies are the product of early scientists/naturalists/occultists who encountered Sekhem, and found some way to empower themselves or a target in a sacrificial rite.                                                 Meh. We should keep the terminology the game left us with and adapt it to the situation. If the mummy is going to have some access to Arisen abilities it will need some equivalent of Arisen stats. Better to tweak the existing concept than to go Changeling: The Dreaming here and create an entirely new entity that vaguely resembles your original game creatures for any given region of the globe.

                                                "For the record, I'm wholly against Arisen being anything but proto-Egyptian:"

                                                I continue to disagree with this view. Diversity in playable options and ST concept fodder isn't a bad thing and keeps the game from getting stale.

                                                "it's not just boring"

                                                This statement floors me. If diversity in a monstrous concept is boring then White Wolf done fucked up since the very beginning! Vampires should only ever be from Transylvania, werewolves should only ever be victims of a cursed bite and uncontrollable transformations, etc. Its true that Mummy has gotten a surprising amount of mileage out of the Egyptian-esque (blended with a more modern Gothic/Lovecraftian horror asthetic, of course). But that simple fact right there says it all, really. There isn't a single reason that you could paint as rich and atmospheric a tapestry using the culture and mythology of any ancient culture on the Earth as this game has already done with the Egyptian culture. Yes, that's probably too ambitious a thing to ask for, but I suppose that's what I'm getting at here. If you are willing to put the effort and research into the project I see no reason whatsoever a mummy from say Scandinavia or Central America would be an inch less interesting than one from Egypt. Also, less sterotypical

                                                "but borderline racist since it says, "All cultures that preserved corpses in a ritualistic fashion are basically interchangeable," and saying something like, "But we've renamed the mechanics" is not good enough to differentiate them."

                                                .......
                                                .......
                                                ........

                                                O.........kay. So....mummies arising in various societies from around the world. BAD AND RACIST! But mummies arising solely in this one tiny region of the world because their magic was just so much more awesome and their gods so much more real than everyone else that they are the only ones who could ever pull it off. GOOD AND RACIALLY PROGRESSIVE!

                                                Nope. Sorry. Does not fucking compute. Maybe I'm just still stuck in Changeling: The Lost mode here, but I do not consider a game that allows you to make a character based around a few quintessential core mechanics and a lot of open ended options you can base on the vast of array of myths and societies we have available in this wide world a bad thing.

                                                I dunno. Much as I love the specifics of the setting, maybe second edition should include a section (in storytelling or antagonists) that helps an ST "decouple" the core mechanics from the Egyptian motif in order to create a more unique antagonist. Preferably in a way that forces the PCs to do what your normal mortal protagonists have to do in a good mummy horror movie- learn enough of the entity's history and culture to be able to combat it effectively. In fact, I would argue that's the best reason to include a non-iremite mummy right there- it keeps them guessing. Here's an entity similar enough to them to be frightening (like, say, not having the courtesy to stay dead when it gets put down) but different enough that the usual tricks just aren't enough.
