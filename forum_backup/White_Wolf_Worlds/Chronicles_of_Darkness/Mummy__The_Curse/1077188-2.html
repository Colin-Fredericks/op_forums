    Originally posted by Azahul View Post
    This bugs the ever living heck out of me. Every culture on the planet makes Relics. Not every culture had Shan'iatu desperate to achieve godhood through the usurpation of the underworld with the aid of the manifestation of entropy. It took a specific confluence of events, ambitions, and inhuman power to lead to the creation of the Arisen even being attempted.

    Mummies are not the "baseline" component of their own cosmology. They are the exceptional, the weird, highly specific creation that arose from a corner-case application of Sekhem's metaphysics. If it weren't for the fact that playing as inanimate objects is difficult to pull off, they would be more likely to feature in the Night Horrors book for Relics: The Curse.
    yes, every culture can create Relics, and every culture can make Amkhat, but what about things which are unique to the cultures which birthed them? Other cultures should make their own, unique creations, and give alternative readings to how the cosmology is built. Sekhem should feel wider- but right now, it feels that everything which is important or interesting about it comes from that same desert empire which was drown beneath the sands millenia ago. I want other cultures to make interesting things with Sekhem, and see how it is seen through different eyes. Non iremite Sekhem enhanced beings could be used to advance that view- as why on earth only one culture in the whole world should be the only one to make something exceptional with Sekhem? Why can't other cultures make their own interesting, exceptional, weird and highly specific creations? I'm not saying that every culture on earth should have made Sekhem enhanced beings (I avoid the word mummies because I don't care about them being mummies), but some should have.


    Well, after Book of the Deceived came out, I ran Sekhem Sorcerers as the creators and/or wielders of particularly arcane Seba. Before that I did much the same but for specific relics (e.g. an Amulet that grants Telekinesis). Similarly, I have used the Revenant rules printed in the Mummy Core Book to create an array of different undead. Notably, if you combine Old Age, Lucid Dead, Stolen Pillars, Regeneration, and Revenant or Intruder together you end up with a being that very closely mimics a weak Mummy, again within Mummy's existing cosmology.

    But that aside, the idea that Mummies are lacking in things to deal with internally is pretty laughable.
    I do not understand that argument. I haven't said that Mummy lacks antagonists. I've said that a concept that comes from Mummy should use Mummy's rules. The concept of bastardized version of the Rite comes from Mummy, and as such should be used through Mummy's tool box. I have no problem with what you are saying- I have a problem with saying that Mekhet or Osirian are the only example for failed replications of the Rite. I don't understand how giving an example, even unique, antagonist created through a failed attempt to recreate the Rite using the rules for Revenants and their like is a problem of any kind.


    I don't disagree. Non-Iremite Mummies would make the game feel a lot wider. I've been railing against that very idea. Mummy is situated within an extremely confined cultural context for what I consider to be very good reasons. Trying to broaden the game by plastering a new coat of cultural paint onto an idea rooted in the specifically Egyptian variant of mortuary culture makes me supremely uncomfortable. And this fixation on non-Egyptian mummies as the specific medium through which to achieve this "widening" of the game never fails to strike me as weird, when they relate as much to Arisen as an urn full of cremated ash.
    Maybe because it is the only gameline which is so specifically tied to a single culture. Maybe because it feels like it gives Egyptian mythology a special treatment which makes it feel weird. Maybe because through variant non Iremite mummies or similar beings, people could see the game from different angles, and not only through egyptian-like eyes.

    Notice- I don't argue that new mummy like beings should be copy-paste of the Arisen with some cultural paint. I argue for the existence of new, Sekhem based lifeforms created by other cultures, be them Lifeless, Deathless, Undead, living, just dead, ephemereal, spiritual, infernal or any other kind of subtitle you would feel comfortable with, which could either be created independently, spontaneously or by people investigating existing rituals and products of Iremite creations. I think that saying "this one culture is the only one that matters as no other had divine help" makes me much more uncomfortable than saying "look, other cultures can make mummies of some kind too!".

    In sort, I don't want a copy paste of existing Arisen with new aesthetic. I want other cultures to make their own unique creations.

                        Originally posted by LostLight View Post
                        I think that saying "this one culture is the only one that matters as no other had divine help" makes me much more uncomfortable than saying "look, other cultures can make mummies of some kind too!".
                        Especially since when you add Last Dynasty International to the mix, it kinda becomes "this ancient culture with divine help and modern white people." But anyway.

                        In sort, I don't want a copy paste of existing Arisen with new aesthetic. I want other cultures to make their own unique creations.
                        One option that I don't think has been considered is using the different Great Crafts to create analogous achievements to the Deathless. Say the Deathless themselves aren't just works of necromancy, but also works of uter in the relic sense of the word. The Shan'iatu were already great necromancers, and incorporated it into Rite of Return for the sake of efficiency (and maybe because they had just enough heart to allow their servants to keep at least their bodies for themselves) - and that is why the Sahu forms around the mummified body - another reason to do it the easy way would be that the Shan'iatu were doing some other pretty sustantial magics at the same time.

                        But it's not impossible for the Sahu to form independently of the Deathless-alike's material tie. Going back to the Andean mallqui, mummies were often wrapped up in bundles of cloth, along with other goods - and "textile" is sure as hell a fitting class of relic for the Andes. So maybe it's the bundle that's the important thing, not the corpse per se - supernatural mallqui rest inside them when it whatever they have in place of henet, but when called to action form a body apart from the mummy bundle - they have a greater tie to the Sekhem of their communities than the Arisen would have to their cults, which might be what makes that possible. That way, a mallqui's physical mummy would be more analogous to an Arisen's Tomb (balancing being easier to move with worse consequences for damaging it).

                        Further afield:

                        Amulets by nature implant knowledge and proclivities - which gives them tremendous vehicle for possession. Could the souls of a person be taken and physically shaped into a small object as the carrier for their Sekhem?

                        Effigies are larger figures and works of architecture - and more than other relics, they display what can almost be called agendas. Could a version of the Rite bring someone back as an ushabti, or write them into a building or earthwork as a genius loci?

                        Regia are a bit more difficult. The key element - the bit that would have to be emphasised as a work of Sekhem - is more process than object. How would that manifest?

                        Texts are back to the simple - writing souls into texts is a trope thats been around since writing began, most likely. Instead of their mummies, later Egyptian Deathless might have lived in their funerary texts. Could they make temporary bodies, or influence the people who read them? Would you have the King in Yellow as a PC?

                        Seba - becoming a living idea - could be the ultimate in apotheosis. Or even Apotheosis. Those hard-core monks I mentioned might become enlightenment itself, only making new bodies when they need them.
