    Exotic Mummies

    03-28-2014, 04:03 PM
    One of the larger complaints about The Curse is that it has a strong focus on Egypt and Egyptian mummies. While this is, broadly speaking, true, the canon leaves some wiggle room that can be used by a creative player and generous storyteller to create some "non-Egyptian" mummies. To test how flexible it was, I created a handful of such foreign Mummies, using existing setting canon and fluff. None of these mummies were "made" by their culture, but they ARE defined by it, and should be roleplayed as such. Anyway, here they are (Not statted out, and left broad enough to be used as PCs or NPCs without being pregenerated)

    The Chinchorro Mummy

    He does not know when he arrived in the Andes. His memories of Irem are scattered--he recalls having been a Su Menent, tasked with traveling the known world in search of cultures that practiced Mummification. While its unlikely that he was originally entombed in South America, he does recall having been there since at least 2000bc. The Chinchorro Mummy spent years, carried about by his nomadic cult, searching out the rare and unusual native South American mummy that rises again. While he has yet to find any proof that the local people created true Arisen, he has encountered Uter Mud-Mummies that rise and move, as well as Lifeless monsters more akin to Shuanksen and Nenitu than Arisen. His cult is an ancestor cult that has kept their history through Blessed Dead, some far older than the Egyptian mummies. Some of the Uter-Mummies have been claimed by him as allies and possessions. Over the millenia, the Chinchorro Mummy traveled as far as Mexico, and has memories of many of the Pre-columbian societies of Mexico, Central America, and South America. While the arrival of the European Mummies forced his cult into hiding and reduced its power, the Chinchorro still has spent more time in South America than almost any other Mummy. One particular ghost, a mad and powerful ancient thing long-since condemned to the depths of Neter-Khertet, even claims that the Chinchorro arrived in South America ALIVE, using sorcery and relics to communicate with the Shan’Iatu and to report on the native mummification techniques, and was eventually mummified and returned to South America. Though his story is unlikely, it is not impossible.

    The Taoist Immortal

    He was a philosopher, a monk, and a practitioner of the ideas that would grow up to become Daoism, Confucianism, and Zen, as well as ideas from India that would give rise to Buddhism millenia later. He lived in the growing cultures around the Yellow River, in a rural village. While Irem never conquered India or China, trade between the cultures occurred, and occasional slave raids were made. He is unsure of how he came to Irem, only recalling that his enlightened knowledge proved valuable to one of the Guilds and he was granted immortality. While he spent most of his adult life in the Nameless Empire, he was born and buried in China. To this day, he upholds the Judge’s decisions and will there. He can speak both major dialects of Chinese, as well as Hindi and Japanese. He is known to utilize Taoist and other Asian terminology over Iremite (For example, he refers to Ba as Yang, and Sheut as Yin). He dislikes a sarcophagus, preferring instead to meditate as he dies, resembling a self-preserved monk. While he is still faithful to the Judges, his peaceful nature often conflicts with their violent urges. He has secretly started researching a legend among Asian mummies that Eight Immortals fled the Empire and attained Apotheosis, long before the Heretic ever dreamed of avarice. He hopes the rumors are true, and that he may find a way to move beyond this endless cycle and onto final peace.

    The Great White Mummy

    Few Arisen who meet this Mummy believe that he was born, lived, and died in this very body. Most assume he is a Twice Arisen with a misunderstanding of his own memories. This is not the case--The Northman was once a powerful war chief among the wandering nomads of the Steppes, north of the Black Sea. His people fought the Iremites in the Caucasus mountains, and prevented their spread through the Black Sea into central Europe. Even still, this particular Northman’s prowess and hunger for power were legendary, and drew the curiosity of the Shan’Iatu. He was eventually swayed by their promises of blood and power, joined the Guilds, and earned his Immortal status. He was entombed in the frozen Northlands with a dangerous relic, to keep it as isolated as possible Thanks to the migrations of the Mummy in the millenia, the Relic and its tomb were lost. The Great White Mummy has a stubborn, warrior Norse mindset, mixed with the ruthelessness of the Nameless Empire.

    The Argonaut

    He spent his life sailing the Mediterranean and the Nile, exploring the reaches of the known world and seeking adventure. His mother was from the tribes of the Greek Islands, while his father was an Egyptian mix. While he did love Africa, especially the lands of Kush, in death he grew far more enamored with the Minoan and Macedonian cultures. He watched as Alexander and Rome rose to power, almost as if Irem was being reborn. He has stayed enamored with Western culture through the millenia, and was especially fond of the Renaissance. His cult seems on the surface to be a mere college Greek style fraternity. Under the surface, it blends a Masonic Skull-and-Bones conspiracy with Greek Mystery Religion. While he is a successful and influential Tef-Aabhi, he sometimes acts like a party-loving spoiled Frat Boy. He does not have an accurate grasp on his own history and memories, and has likely blended and blurred events in his own life, perhaps even including historical research and truth together in a western-biased way.

    The Bog Mummy

    How did he get here, in this peat? It is no tomb, there is no ritual. When Sothis smiles upon this Mummy, or when intruders disturb his corpse, he awakens lost. His memories are damaged, and his body feels unfamiliar--he is very likely a Twice Arisen. But how, and why, was he here haunting this lonely bog? Was he protecting, or seeking, a relic lost to its depths? Was he ritually sacrificed and laid to rest here by his cult, to raise him with greater power later on, and simply forgotten? Did he fight some enemy, and fall into the bog upon his defeat? If it was a Relic, he cannot feel it anymore. If it was his cult, they’ve long since died out. If it was an enemy, he has no idea who. All he remembers is the bog.. The stench.. The peat.. And that haunting loneliness. His past is a mystery that drives him to dig ever deeper into himself, and into the bog that was his grave.

    Saint Victoria, the Modern Mummy

    Little Victoria was about ten years old when she died. She was considered a Saint by the cult-church of her hometown, and was especially beloved by the immortal Saint that protected it. Unfortunately, her sacredness could not prevent her own illness. Though renowned for her ability to heal others, she could not heal herself, nor could her Patron. Unbeknownst to Victoria, the immortal Saint that protected her village was destroyed by a powerful enemy. The Saint’s sacred knowledge was too valuable to be allowed to die with her, and little Victoria was too precious to be allowed to pass on, either. And so, the ritual of the second breath was performed, and the Mummy rose again through little Victoria, blending their memories together. While she has yet to recall the important information she was saved for, Saint Victoria still remains a valuable treasure to her village, and to the Church. Her body rests in a display in the local Cathedral, protected by glass and petitioned by pilgrims. Though little Saint Victoria is famous for the remarkable preservation and the alleged healing powers, only her Cult knows that she could be awakened in a time of great need.

      Nice work! I've toyed with the ideas of 'fake' Mummies in my head since release, as I'm sure we all have, although yours are mostly Arisen who identify with other cultures

      Only the Nameless Empire sired true Deathless, though, and if any successor cultures attempted to mimic the antediluvian Rite of Return then they have only created Lifeless. I think the key themes with Mummy antagonists are 1) a sense of ancient timelessness and 2) pride an arrogance - either the Arisen have a superiority complex over them because they are 'true' Mummies, or the antagonists have a superiority complex because they know something the Arisen might possibly not remember

          Yes, tried to ensure that there wasn't any bending of the canonical creation of true Arisen, even if for some of the Mummies it became a big stretch. I'm pretty sure we will get some interesting Lifeless in later supplements, especially the Sothic Turn book. It will be fun to see a lot more depth on the actual history of Mummies and what all a high memory Arisen could recall. Either way, even without using the stretches I have, Twice Arisen and movement during the Diasporas can easily lead a Mummy to a given area without having been made by the culture or be associated with it in life (as most of my previously listed Mummies were).

          On a side note, I am working out the details of the Mummy-Uter. The idea was originally for an Uter that was an animal-mummy that could actually return to a Lifeless state as its power, giving the Mummy in question a unique companion that is as old as she is. After I went into the idea of the Chinchorro mummy, I figured that it could be extended to human remains. Granted, I wouldn't make the human-mummy-uter much more than a zombie slave, with little left of its humanity or memories (if anything). At best, it'd have a hint of the style of a Sadikh, mixed with a bit of Nenitu for flavor.

              I like how you were able to use an inherent flaw in the template (the unreliability of Memory) to give some of these creatures really keen backgrounds away from Irem. Some of the cultures aren't EXACTLY accurate for the time frame of Mummy: the Curse's origins, but who the hell cares? There've been people in Scandinavia and China for a long time, despite whatever cultures those lands eventually hosted. A judicious hodge-podge of slippy personal narrative and acculturation to a new land make these characters wonderfully believable without violating the seemingly iron-clad 'canon' of the game... Not that there's anything wrong with that.

              Keep us up-to-date regarding the 'animal-mummy' idea, too. I highly appreciate awesome rules hacks, and these are excellent examples!
