    Hunter: Mortal Remains

    04-30-2014, 06:18 PM
    Hunter: Mortal Remains is out and it has a substantial Mummy chapter with two new antagonist factions of Hunters for mummies to contend with. While the book does not use the words "Arisen" or "Deathless", the authors make it quite clear that these "undying ones" draw from and are inspired by Mummy: the Curse.

    The Faithful of Shulpae, those who eat monsters as an act of Devotion, have been discussed here previously, so I'd like to mention the new Compact: Habibti Ma. This is an Egypt based group of humans who rescue people who have fallen into cults of all kinds, but they find that they are most effective when countering the cults of charismatic god-figures.

    I strongly recommend this book for people who need some mortal enemies for their mummies and their cults. It even has an excellent section discussing the psychology of cultists.

                                              I have mixed feelings on the whole argument about the Faithful and if they are actually eating the Arisen.

                                              The fact that each game line does (if the ST wishes it to be the case) run in its own setting can mean that Hunter Mummy's and the Arisen are two separate things. The Mummy's in mortal remains are the "hunterverse" version of the Arisen basically.

                                              Alternatively, each game line can cross-over with others and does have some support for that idea and material to support it. I've voiced the opinion before that Mummy is the most "isolated" of the game lines in its fluff. Which used to bug the hell out of me but DC has gone some way to fixing that I feel, hopefully later supplements will as add more such content.

                                              Regardless you could use Arisen in a Hunter game and you could use Hunters in a Mummy game.

                                              The fact that CAS took a look at the Faithful preview posted on the Onyx Path website after a fan drew his attention to it on another thread and then basically said "nope not actually the case in Mummy" is simultaneously a good and bad move I feel.

                                              Its good because it upholds the idea that crossover rules relating to a major splats interaction with another gameline should only be detailed in the major splats books. A precedent Dave B has talked about in the past with the Mage/Changeling crossover rules situation that I support the idea of.

                                              It also points out an odd quirk of the Mortal Remains book. Some chapters were written by or with consultation from one of the other game lines devs or experienced writers. For example:

                                              Matt McFarland wrote the Promethean chapter, he oversaw all the Promethean splats, so he knows that setting and has a love for it and has said he'd be head dev on a Promethean Chronicle book if Onyx Path did one.

                                              Rose Bailey wrote the Demon Chapter, she's the head Dev for Demon.

                                              From what CAS has been saying he seems not to have been consulted on the Mummy chapter and the writer (while doing an excellent overall job) is not a Mummy writer veteran. Normally I would not stress this so much, but CAS has in the past mentioned he wants very particular writers for each Mummy project.

                                              On the bad side, CAS saying "nope, these guys can't be doing that to the Arisen" kinda undermines the idea of the Conspiracy a bit and does invalidate it within the scope of Mummy the Curse as a group that people might think have a "meta" validation in some way.

                                              Basically, I have the issue on a more in-theme front that Hunter Endowments are often weird supernatural powers that let people do the impossible. So why is it so hard for CAS (and by extension some fans) to imagine that an Endowment might allow for the negation/subversion of what is frankly and minor template trait? Considering their is already a president for a cannibalistic absorption of power in Mummy.

                                              It just rubs me the wrong way a little. It feels like a knee-jerk reaction/call based on OOG issue colouring an in game call.

                                              Obviously, this is all redundant anyway as each ST can make their own call on the issue thanks to the toolbox approach of the NWoD.
