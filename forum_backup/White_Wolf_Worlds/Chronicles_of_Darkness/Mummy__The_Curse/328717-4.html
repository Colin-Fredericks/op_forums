                                                Session 19

                                                Imhotep needed to restore the tattered remnants of his memory. He spent some time alone, away from his war against the Arisen, and managed to climb back to Memory 2. A memory of his last descent returned to him. He remembered watching the invaders, marching beneath the banner of a rising sun. It was a symbol he found endlessly amusing, every time he killed them. They didn't even know how close they were to the secrets of magic.

                                                He remembered a closeted meeting. Many of his cult's warriors, most of them Dalila's dancer-disciples, were gathered together. A foreign man named Adam was speaking. The general opinion was that he was a spy, an emissary from the West, working to destabilise the Japanese forces. He had valuable knowledge that he shared willingly among the groups resisting the occupation, whatever their motivation. The Deceived's cultists were simply one such group he had chosen to work with.

                                                Imhotep remembered learning many things from this man. The generals of his time had not had much time for asymmetric warfare. Principles like shooting to wound, rather than kill, would have been considered a weakness rather than a strategic tool. And yet there was much wisdom to be found, and useful tricks to be adopted in the secret wars.

                                                The memory blurred, and Imhotep remembered the ambush. His men were outnumbered and outgunned, many of them carried machetes stolen from the plantations rather than actual firearms, but Adam's lessons stood them in good stead. They had taken the patrol by surprise. The invaders were turning to flee, despite the exhortations of their officer. Imhotep strode across the battlefield untouched, content merely to observe, when he felt the presence of magic. In that moment the Japanese officer flung his hands out wide. Light poured from his hands, eyes, and mouth and swept across the field. Where it touched the fighting men their skin boiled and blistered, and Imhotep was overcome by the searing pain as it reached him. Mortals, friend and enemy alike, burned alive around him. He sealed his flesh, repairing the damage being done to him as quickly as it could be done, and took aim with the sidearm he had chosen for himself. His eyes began to boil in his skull, but he was able to fire a single shot at the source of the light.

                                                The pain stopped, the light vanished, and the officer swayed on his feet as a red flower bloomed across his chest. He collapsed. Imhotep stood and saw Adam approaching from the other direction, apparently unharmed. All around them lay the unmoving bodies of the fighters. Adam looked at Imhotep in shock.

                                                "How are you not dead?"

                                                Imhotep sneered. "As if such a trick could kill me. And I could ask the same of you."

                                                Adam didn't respond to that. Instead he turned towards the officer's body. Imhotep felt his occult senses alerting him to the manifestation of a Seba, and saw it rising from the dead man's body. They each realised the other could see it.

                                                "I must take it," Adam said. "We are being watched by the heavens. Habiru-Nut, the Thief Star, a great light is coming for this world. It comes to devour us, consume the light of the sun. But with this, we can appease it, buy us a little more time."

                                                Imhotep considered the words, and knew them to be true. He had never heard of this entity before, but the sound of its name filled him with a kind of nameless dread. "Take it," he said, and the vision ended.

                                                When he emerged from his tomb, he found the retreat in a frenzy of activity. Umar sent a messenger to collect him and Rakhen-Roh. Imhotep arrived first, but refused to listen to what was happening until Rakhen-Roh arrived as well. He didn't want to have to listen to the explanation twice. The moment the other Deceived walked through the door, Umar launched into the explanation. Daryat had gone missing. Upon reviewing security camera footage, it was apparent that at least three, possibly four Arisen had been present at Novus. Daryat had been put in charge of managing the operation there during the last six months, and had apparently gone to his office to discuss business matters. He'd never emerged. The Arisen who followed him did not appear to have ever left the building either, for that matter, but they were clearly gone now. Reviewing the footage, Rakhen-Roh was able to identify Aquila, Sekhet Bastet, Sesu Ra, and Akenakhi as the culprits, although the latter appeared to have spent most of the time drinking and had left by the usual exit.

                                                Imhotep ordered his cultists to memorise the appearances of those responsible. If they ever showed up again, he did not want even the lowliest initiate to fail to identify them. Then he summoned the diviners, the cultists whose job it was to work in the retreat's observatory, and ordered them to scry for Daryat's position. One of these individuals, a man named Abdul, was able to immediately pin-point his fellow cultist. After a bit of work triangulating his position, it was clear to the Deceived that he was being held underground at the headquarters of Asa Corp, the cult serving the meret responsible.

                                                Imhotep used Whispers in the Earth to discover that the sewers ran quite close to the cell where Abdul indicated Daryat was being kept. He and Rakhen-Roh went in alone, each disguising themselves with their magic. Imhotep became a beautiful young woman, figuring that no one would ever connect him to that form, while Rakhen-Roh was able to make himself into a passable copy of Yama-Het.

                                                Once they were close to Daryat, Imhotep used Whispers in the Earth to create a tunnel through the wall to Daryat's cell. They went through, and Daryat leapt to his feet in confusion. Imhotep made a veiled reference to how he lost his arm in order to identify himself, and Daryat understood. They were fleeing back through to the sewers when a guard burst into the cell and ordered them to stop. Imhotep replied by shooting the man in the knee. He fell to the ground, and the tunnel closed behind them before any other pursuit could be made.

                                                They ordered Daryat to return to Novus. He might have been compromised in some manner, and they didn't want him to lead their enemies to their bases in the city. They instructed him to stay put, and ordered Novus to be closed while greater defences were put in place. The next time the Arisen showed their face there, the Deceived planned on there being a deadly trap to greet them.

                                                During the discussion on how best to prepare, Rakhen-Roh's sarcasm crossed a line for Imhotep. The Forever Scornful demanded his fellow Deceived show proper respect. Rakhen-Roh attempted to divine Imhotep's true name, and pronounced the word he discovered in triumph. It simply confused Imhotep, for the divination had not been successful. When he realised what the other had done, or attempted to do, he was furious. He knew less about Rakhen-Roh than the other knew about him, he realised, so he could hardly expect success if he were to do the same in turn. Instead, he calmly and pleasantly declared that there was no need to quarrel like this, he merely asked that good manners be shown in future.

                                                Inside, of course, he was fuming.

                                                Word then came that the scryers had located another manifesting Seba, this time outside of the city in the Thousand Islands. Imhotep drew on his Tomb Legion resources to have a helicopter prepared, and rallied three heavily armed cultists together to join Rakhen-Roh and him on the attack. Abdul was rewarded for his success by being put in charge of this team, and Rakhen-Roh bestowed upon him a great gift in the form of one of his Seba, Of the Dark Beyond Time.

                                                The helicopter put down on a densely vegetated island in the midst of the archipelago. They left one of their men with the helicopter, and proceeded on foot towards the Seba. Rakhen-Roh and Imhotep could sense the imminent manifestation themselves now. Rakhen-Roh sent his snake ahead to scout, and deeming the way clear, they closed in on the Seba. They walked straight into an ambush.

                                                Four figures emerged from the undergrowth. Two of them, Imhotep recognised. One was the overweight woman with the blank, unchanging expression, the other was the frail, petite woman, both individuals he had encountered alongside Dalila on a previous Seba hunt that had won them the False Ka Made True. They had two men with them as well, a giant of a man with a bare chest covered in tribal tattoos, and a badly scarred diminutive figure whose age was hard to determine, but clearly advanced. This dwarf giggled and capered as he rushed at Imhotep, plunging his hands into the Deceived's chest. A foul rot began to spread from the point of contact, quickly overwhelming Imhotep's ability to repair his body. At the same time, sibilant chanting came from the direction of the great giant, and a blast of light came from the direction of the women which burned a hole in Rakhen-Roh's shoulder.

                                                The Deceived struggled to fight back. The dwarf wriggled his hands deeper into Imhotep's body. Rakhen-Roh tried to tear apart the women with a blast of sand, but he felt malevolent magics at work curbing his ability. The cultists accompanying them opened fire, driving the large man to take cover, and Abdul came to Imhotep's rescue by wielding Of the Dark Beyond Time to blind the dwarf. Moment's later, Rakhen-Roh's snake struck and delivered its poison into his veins. The man fell to the ground, giggling and frothing in turns.

                                                That seemed to take the fight out of the remaining three. They began to fade, just as Imhotep had seen them do before. Rakhen-Roh coughed up one of the ghosts he had devoured, the Preacher from the burning ruins, and ordered it to pursue and destroy them. The ghost returned to him soon after, saying he could not lay hands upon them. Rakhen-Roh told him to seek them out then, and report back when they were found.

                                                Their prize, the Seba, waited for them then. Imhotep stood back and allowed Rakhen-Roh to claim it as reconciliation for their previous argument. Carving the appropriate sigils into the ground in accordance with his tradition, Rakhen-Roh sealed Dark Prism Marks the Gate to his soul.

                                                    And the new Seba:

                                                    Dark Prism Marks the Gate (1 dot Seba)

                                                    Power: It takes a long time for a star's light to reach the world. This Seba seems more like an afterimage, a leftover remnant of a much greater magic that dates back to the dawn of prehistory. Whenever the bearer fulfils their Virtue, they will see from the corner of their eye a black obelisk, taller than a man and dark enough to appear like a hole in the very fabric of the universe. It will appear somewhere where those who cannot perceive it are in no immediate danger of touching it. The bearer has about a minute to approach the obelisk and touch it, otherwise it fades away quickly. Those who cannot perceive the obelisk will not notice the bearer as they do this.

                                                    When touched, the obelisk grants an insight. This usually takes the form of advancing the bearer up a "level" on the relation chart to a particular Seba for the purposes of Kepher. If the bearer knows of no unbound Seba, it will select one at random from one that will manifest within a relatively convenient timeframe at a location the bearer could reasonably be expected to reach. These Seba are invariably cosmic in nature.

                                                    Curse: The magic of the obelisk is old, maybe impossibly so. After touching the obelisk the bearer must immediately spend a point of Willpower or degenerate to a more primitive mindset. If they do not, for the next scene their Intelligence falls to 1, they lose the capacity for human speech, and their ability to respond to events is largely limited to fight-or-flight responses.

                                                        Session 20

                                                        The memory of his past flowed across him again. He remembered the cold, the burning flame, the convergence of stars. His lost friend counselled him, shepherding him inside in time to meet the spirit in the flame. The creature asked for a sacrifice, the life of every child younger than fourteen winters. To his everlasting shame, he accepted and condemned them to the fire.

                                                        "This was a mistake."

                                                        The Arisen, a Su-Menent named Akenakhi, turned to see the Prophet of Java standing there. In the background, the memory continued to run its course. The creature in the flame told him to sunder his heart and shadow to break the chains binding him to his Judge. He shuddered.

                                                        "How could you ever believe that ascension lay in breaking your soul, in acts like these? The Mentaar requires a complete soul, fully restored."

                                                        The Prophet counselled the Arisen to seek out the one-armed man, to seek sanctuary among the Lost Guild. The members of the Arisen Nome would have turned against him for what he had done, his betrayal of one of his fellow Su-Menent. And then the vision was over.

                                                        Together with his meret, they had encountered a one-armed man who could not be broken by even mystical means. The man's name was Daryat. Akenakhi had encountered him at the nightclub Novus. He gathered his relics, canopic jars, and personal belongings, clearing out his tomb, and abandoned it to whatever Fate had in store.

                                                        Novus was quiet and still when Akenakhi slipped in through the front door. There didn't appear to be anyone about. He was wondering how to proceed when Daryat entered the room behind the bar. The two stared at one another, and Akenakhi asked to speak to the secret masters. "The Lost Guild, I know you serve them."

                                                        Daryat turned to flee. Akenakhi vaulted the bar and landed in front of him with a surge of supernatural skill.

                                                        "I'm not here as an enemy. I just want to talk."

                                                        Daryat backed up a few steps and called Imhotep. In a deliberately circumspect manner, he informed his master that there was an Arisen in Novus, one who knew of the Deceived's existence but who wished to speak with them rather than fight. Imhotep ordered him to watch Akenakhi, and said he would be right there.

                                                        Imhotep and Rakhen-Roh entered Novus with Rakhen-Roh disguised beneath Face of Endless Lies, masquerading as a cultist. There was no sense in tipping their full hand, they figured. Imhotep demanded to know Akenakhi's name. Feeling intimidated by the presence of the other Deathless, whose existence he hadn't truly believed until he saw them, Akenakhi gave his name and explained the situation. He was a seeker of Apotheosis, and was being hunted by the Nome for his actions against one of his meret. At least, he assumed that was the case, he hadn't had any contact with other Arisen since his attempt to destroy Sekhet Bastet's tomb went awry and she had killed him.

                                                        Privately, Imhotep was quite pleased with the servile manner in which Akenakhi addressed him. He was not quick to trust the Arisen, however. He wanted information, everything Akenakhi could share on the Arisen in the city. Daryat found them some chairs, a table, and paper, and they began to explore what Akenakhi knew and what could be valuable for them.

                                                        Much of what Akenakhi had to share the Deceived already knew. A few of his unexplained incidents were ones they had instigated themselves. They were greatly interested in some of what he had to share. The presence of two Maa-Kep in the city, Mekshonat and Benerib, had not been known to them before this, although they did explain the presence of strange Arisen in their attack on the city's Temple. The existence of the creature called the Coils of Apophis was intriguing, as was the considerable amount of information Akenakhi had on some of the city's Shuankhsen. But most intriguing to them was this strange place called the Vault of the Stars, which had led Akenakhi to the Seba known as the Sickle of Eternity. They decided a good test of his information would be to have him take them to the Vault of the Stars immediately.

                                                        Akenakhi led them to an abandoned slum in the city's east, where flooding had forced even the homeless to abandon it. They were able to reach the site of the Vault's entrance on foot. It was contained within an abandoned hotel, badly damaged by the floods. Four strangely carved pillars rose around a vaulted archway made of small, gleaming stars. This latter object was greatly intriguing to Akenakhi. It hadn't been here on any of his previous visits.

                                                        One by one, they stepped through. The world melted away until they were surrounded on every side by the stars and the great vastness of the cosmos. All three of them immediately sensed the presence of a Seba, and looked up to find an unbound Seba floating just over their heads. Akenakhi realised with wonder that he hadn't been here since he had discovered the existence of Seba and gained the ability to perceive them. It must have been here on his every visit.

                                                        Imhotep examined the Seba and found that it allowed its bearer to treat the Vault like their tomb. They could even move its entrance from place to place, allowing them to steal it away from its current location, known to the Arisen. Rakhen-Roh and Akenakhi were each staring at something he couldn't see when he went to share this with them. Then, slowly, a woman manifested out of Twilight. Her body was made of stars. Akenakhi had forewarned them of her presence, a slave he had sacrificed thousands of years before on his first visit to the Vault.

                                                        She appeared shy, hesitant before the Deceived. She seemed to know of them, calling them the Restless Stars. Akenakhi was glad that she didn't appear to focus on him. Instead, she drew their attention out beyond the Vault, towards a light in the distance. The Radiance was approaching quickly, its light crawling across the stars as it surged towards the Earth. Akenakhi and Imhotep each noted that there were a swarm of smaller lights rushing ahead of it, barely visible to the naked eye.

                                                        The Radiance had tendrils anchoring it to the Earth, the ghost explained. They were pulling it closer with every passing day. She could offer no help in finding these tendrils, the Earth itself was beyond her sight, but they needed to be located and destroyed.

                                                        Before they left, Imhotep ordered Akenakhi to claim the Vault of Stars. They needed a safe place to keep him, particularly as he carried relics which could be sensed by other relics, and hidden inside a Seba seemed like an ideal place for him.

                                                        The Deceived then consulted with Akenakhi, sharing a little of what they knew of the Radiance. The Dreamland theme park seemed to be one of the most obvious places where its servants had a physical presence, so they decided to rush there and investigate. A year ago they had met a boy or young man named Imat who had spoken to them of a Watcher, a hidden man who blackmailed many of the staff into performing strange acts around the park. They decided to seek him out again, locating his old lodgings. Rakhen-Roh, now deigning to show his true face to Akenakhi, picked the lock to Imat's quarters and they filed in. The place was much as the Deceived remembered it, and it looked lived in. Imat wasn't around, but it surely wouldn't be long before he returned.

                                                        Imat eventually did appear, shocked to find the three immortals squatting in his room. Imhotep explained that they had returned to locate the Watcher and end his activities in the area, and asked Imat if he still received messages from the mysterious man. Imat replied that yes, he did. There were many more of what he called the "rotten" zones, places where light seemed to play tricks and create illusions and mirages of things that weren't really there. Things had only been getting worse over the last year.

                                                        The group decided to investigate the place where they had first encountered the illusions, the corridor with the spiked pits. They found the corridor easily enough. The illusions were gone, the camera at the end of the hall was dead, and the pits had been filled in or their spikes removed. In the level below, there was no longer any sign of the crates of disintegrating worm-like creatures, and the ritual room was barren. Akenakhi used Palace Knows Its Pharaoh to reactivate the camera, pointing out that it might catch the attention of the Watcher, but it seemed to create no significant reaction. He hadn't been able to properly restore his pillars since the incident with Bast, and now only had a few remaining to fuel his powers.

                                                        The group sensed a Seba nearby then. They rushed outside in time to see a pale light streak across the heavens, heading towards an Atlantis-themed aqua park nearby. They followed as fireworks began to explode overhead, a part of the resort town's evening activities.

                                                        The Seba came to ground in the stands around a large pool, a place where sea mammals could be brought to perform for the amusement of the daytime visitors. It was deserted at this time of night. Rakhen-Roh identified the Seba and claimed it for himself, snatching it quickly lest they be attacked in such an exposed position. The three spoke quietly among themselves, trying to work out what this meant, when Imhotep's head turned slowly skyward.

                                                        The fireworks had finished, but the sky was still full of light. It was a meteor shower. A thousand falling stars rippled across the heavens, bright flashes of light that burned out before they hit the ground. But to the eyes of the Deceived, and Akenakhi, there were dozens of Seba woven among them. They were scattering across Jakarta, but many were converging here, in the theme park, no doubt drawn and trapped by the arcane geometries of the Radiance's servants. Imhotep recalled the bright lights they had seen rushing as a precursor to the Radiance and realised that these were the same, Seba fleeing consumption.

                                                        The foremost priority seemed clear. Claim the Seba before the Radiance's missionaries could get to them. And then came a hissing sound from somewhere in the stands, and they realised that they were not alone...

                                                            The group discovered two Seba over the course of this session, found below:

                                                            The Vault of Stars (4 dot cosmic Seba)

                                                            The Vault exists outside of the world, in a space dedicated only to itself. It is the perfect observatory, its sacred halls open to the light of every star in the sky. Those who enter the Vault find it to be a perfect sphere made of an almost imperceptible material, suspended in the cosmos where the Earth should be. The sun, moon, and stars can be seen on every side. It functions to all extents and purposes like a Tomb with Geometry 5 attuned to the bearer of the Seba. In addition, the Vault has the following effects:
                                                            -Any Seba that manifest inside it remain until they are claimed, including those possessed by a Deathless who returns to henet inside it, as per .
                                                            -Any rolls regarding Astronomy or Astrology made inside the Vault gain a +3 bonus.
                                                            -The bearer of the Seba can create an entrance to the Vault anywhere there is an existing work of Geometry in place. Doing so takes an instant action and a point of Willpower, and replaces any existing entrances. This entrance is visible only to those who can detect Seba and appears to be a vaulted arch rimmed in starlight. It always faces the viewer no matter where they stand in relation to it.
                                                            -There is no visible exit on the inside of the Vault, but anyone can will themselves to depart as an instant action.

                                                            Note: The Utterance Palace Knows Its Pharaoh has strange effects inside the Vault.
                                                            Tier 1 casts the user's mind into the darkness of space, making them aware of the universe. This causes the user to experience an immediate vision of a Seba manifesting somewhere they could reasonably be expected to reach in time, but the knowledge that comes with it of the vast and terrible expanse of the universe makes their mind shudder and cringe. They must make a Memory degeneration check as if they had committed a Memory 1 sin.
                                                            Tier 2 allows the Mummy to grasp at the stuff of the firmament. It allows the user to craft shapes out of starlight, sometimes simple and crude lines, at other times strange and beautiful illusions. An extended Wits + Crafts or Expression roll lets the caster build images and illusions out of the pale light of far-off stars. Alternatively, they can wield this light as a weapon. Make the normal Telekinesis dice pool +3, and do that number of successes as lethal damage ignoring the target's defence and armour.
                                                            Tier 3 is dangerous to wield within the Vault. Doing so will work as if the caster had created the portal at Sekhem 8+ and left the destination up to Fate. These doorways invariably send those who enter them far out into space, to experience different worlds and vistas important to Fate. Cosmic Seba may be found at the other end, or else some part of Fate's great plan may be uncovered.

                                                            Curse: The Vault is open to the void, nearly defenceless against the malevolent intelligences that lurk beyond the confines of this world. Strange, cold fingers brush against the walls of the Vault, stroking and playing with the minds of those inside. Anyone who meditates within the tomb must roll Resolve + Composure. Failure sends them into an immediate Fugue state, as per the derangement, during which they act out the will of an alien being of inscrutable purpose. When they regain consciousness, they are often somewhere unexpected or find themselves to be the perpetrator of some abhorrent act, or whatever else the Storyteller deems appropriate.
