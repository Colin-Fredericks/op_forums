                  Yeah, we have to separate modern ideas of race versus ancient Egyptian and thus Iremite ideas of race, and again modern from Victorian/Orientalist.

                  An Iremite would ask "Are you Tjemehu or Byblos?" An Orientalist would ask "Are you Negro or Semitic?" And a modern scholar would ask "Are you Hebrew, Arab, Hittite, Sumerian, etc".

                  Our categories are different from theirs. It pretty much came down to "Us, the southerners, the easterners, and the westerners" at the time. Of course, if we look at a modern understanding, it gets a lot more complicated, partially because we have to consider what racial-ethnic-cultural divides would have changed between Irem and our knowledge of history. We know that there are the four major groups, so we know that Egyptians and Libyans and Syrians are all considered separate. But within those groups, there's a lot we don't know: where are the Hebrews? Are the Arabs divided into North and South? How different are the Ki-En-Gir from the proto-Assyrians, from the proto-Medes, from the proto-Hittites? The same kinds of questions can be said for the Libyan and Nubian super-groups, except our modern knowledge is lacking in these areas, other than the basic connection of the Libyans with modern Berbers/Amazigh.

                  This is also one of the reasons that I prefer to extend the borders of Irem far further than in the core-books, to allow more diversity. Why should Irem be limited to the heights of Ancient Egypt? Why not extend Irem far along the North African coast, as far as the Atlantic? Why not extend it south to the source of the Nile? Why not allow it to encompass the proto-Hittites? Encompass Arabia, the only issue is when you get to the Ki-En-Gir, who one can't really shift from Sumeria without them stopping being the Ki-En-Gir. Thus one can fully include most of the four Egyptian race constructs, and opens up new borders for the odd character; one can have a proto-Cretian character who had contacts with Irem's Anatolian wing.

                  Edit; One really good question would be; how would an Iremite see todays ethnicities? How would they adapt to a new way of thinking about types of people?

                                          Here's the thing though: you don't have to assign any of the groups above to modern racial groupings, all of which try to claim the Ancient Egyptians as part of their own (mostly in response to asshole colonialists claiming that they had to be white because no black people could have created such monuments, see also claims that white people must have created the Mississippi Mound culture and other similar racist bilge). But outside of the need to give your current political project a link to a 'more glorious past', does it really MATTER the colour of their skin? What matters is how they divided the world, which you can argue was just as much geographical (those to the east, west, south, and centre) as it was based on skin colour.                                               There really isn't much of a controversy about the ethnicity of ancient Egyptians, which covered a range of appearances common in North Africa. Ancient and modern Egyptians share a strong genetic relationship, though Egyptians, like most people, have ancestors from other places after thousands of years of history.

                                              However, there are explosive political aspects obscuring much of this, especially in the popular imagination. Early racists claimed ancient Egyptians were whites. Modern Afrocentrists have made claims in the modern period. Arguments that modern Egyptians share little to no kinship with ancient Egyptians has been used to push various political stances as well.

                                              One thing to note is that to the ancients, the closest thing to "race" would really mean "some characteristics we associate with a nationality partly out of fact, partly out of cultural tradition." Some of the distinctions made in the Table of Nations might be invisible to many of us, and the Egyptians may have exaggerated them in art for effect, just as we produce exaggerated skin tones and features in often-problematic art now. It also means that if someone came from beyond the regions currently known but generally looked like someone from such and such a place, they'd be slotted into that category, though maybe seen as an extreme type (by Egyptian/Iremite standards). If they looked a lot different, they would be considered some sort of unclassifiable foreigner. The root of the bias would be nationality, not really ancestry after the fashion of modern racism. So if you're from past Ki-En-Gir they'll still say you have that "type" as long as you vaguely look the part.

                                              As for what Arisen think of modern ethnicity? Virtually none of them would be "white-passing" in their original bodies, they almost all believe their African nation was the greatest civilization that can ever exist, and they practiced bias based on national political rivalries, so their attitudes are probably at right angles to any opinion anybody's had for centuries. Basically, they know there are actual gods, so everybody who doesn't is sort of an idiot for not following them anyway.

                                                  Some other cultures that might have been very influential in the Iremite world -

                                                  Proto-Dilmun : This verdant paradise of wetlands (now some of the most desolate places on earth, hmm) was so pretty that the people in Ki-En-Gir describe it as paradise and envision the Gods retired here. The legend of the Garden of Eden probably comes from this region. This legendary civilization is on the Persian gulf, right next to Ki-En-Gir.

                                                  Proto-Ubar : Ubar is NOT the Ki-En-Gir. Not sure what happened here?... but I assume the mummies misremember, or something, because Ubar is ACTUALLY on the southern edge of Saudi Arabia and is likewise extensively involved in the trade of exotic gums (particularly frankincense). Ubar is actually quite some distance from Ki-En-Gir, separated geographically by the proto-Dilmun civilization.

                                                  Proto-Indus : The pre-Harappan culture was producing a wide variety of art and fine ceramics at this time. The Indus Valley represented the eastern end of the 'Gum Road', which saw impressive amounts of trade given that people were using essentially overland routes and reed boats. By following monsoonal winds, merchants had a seasonal way of getting too and from western lands like Ubar and Irem.

                                                  Proto-Punt : Also gum traders (the incense thing was huge in the ancient world, much like spices), the civilization of Punt... mythical to the Egyptians themselves, explored by Hatshepsut in her famous expidition... would have factored large in the lives of Iremites, assuming they didn't outright just conquer them, which historical information would suggest they did not bother to do.
