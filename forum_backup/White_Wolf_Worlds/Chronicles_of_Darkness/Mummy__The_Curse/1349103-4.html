                    Originally posted by Exthalion View Post
                    So the broader question is: What is she getting out of this deal that actually makes it worth her while to keep up her end?
                    Ending things is what she does, and she's good at it and enjoys it a great deal.

                    Her endgame is The Complete Cessation Of Everything Forever, but the world is too structured for her to manifest directly anymore, the Judges are useful to her purposes by their being so prepared to compromise and drive the rest of Creation to her embrace directly or indirectly, and everything that exists inherited Will from Sutek, who inherited from Atum, her opposite number in the big three primordial forces of Irem's creation myth.

                    So she's stuck playing the long game; to borrow an analogous scheme from Changeling's Morphean the Rogue Sister, she's down to making deals to kill other deals. Without the legroom to just sweep in past the horizon and crunch the rock in one go, she's settled for subverting Creation to end Creation one step at a time.

                    She has eternity to play with, after all — once a relic's Sekhem is consigned to Duat, it remains there until and unless the Judges deign to send it back, which puts it on the playing field again for the Devourer to consume.

                            To answer this more directly with a quote now that I'm not on mobile:

                            Originally posted by The Judges and Shan'iatu, Translator's Introduction
                            We should also note the role of Iremite theodicy. Suffering drives life to change, act, and adapt, and is part of the basic makeup of the Judges’ cosmos. Creation legends say that Ammut must be appeased or she’ll consume everything. The Judges were willing to simply consign a portion of Creation to oblivion, but Sutek asserted that only beings capable of disobeying the Law of Suffering are appropriate sacrifices. If they choose the path the Heretic calls “Will,” they can rise to a place beside the Judges, in heavenly A’aru. Otherwise, they go to Duat.

                            Humanity needed to be educated and brought up to the task of choosing, but the Judges didn’t want to make it too easy to choose A’aru, lest they starve and enrage the Devourer. Thus, note that some of the commands given to the temakhs are paradoxical, or even hypocritical. The Judges command them to deceive humans but punish liars, for example. Some of the Judges issue straightforward directives, but many seem to demand that their servants actively tempt mortals to commit sins worth punishing. Perhaps this explains why, after becoming the Shan’iatu of Irem, the temakhs pursued the cruel, despotic rule the Heretic remembers.

                                            Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                            [Idigam or Old One?] Neither.

                                            The Judges are a step (or a step and a half) below omnipresent cosmic forces like creation, destruction, and manifest existence, second only to the defiance and unpredictability of all things. They are foundational to ordered reality as experienced in Mummy: the Curse, and it is this closeness to root divinity that likely makes their described appearances and physical manifestations so terrifying.

                                            Derived from this, they are a set of forty-two THOU SHALT NOTs that Irem's legacy of civilization is built upon, serving as channels for the Devourer to receive her tithe of souls, because they do not care for the world they judge except insofar as they are compelled to by their place in the cosmos. They are cruel and their place in the cosmos specifically includes "humans are important, because they're the only things we're allowed to feed to Ammut so she'll let the universe at large continue to exist," which I am given to understand is kind of the opposite of how that power dynamic usually manifests for the Great Old Ones.

                                            They are likewise not terribly close to the shifting proto-symbolic entities that are the idigam, who can certainly have a passive impact on the world around them as spiritual beings, but who lack the occult grounding one expects from something reality itself is built on; the idigam's creative approach to reality stems mostly from attempts to define new concepts, which makes them extremely late to the game as far as primordial beings go.

                                            The Judges are sharp edges on the rock of coherent reality, and the world theoretically dances to their tune because the song they're playing is stimulus-response that they refined into specific forbiddances at some point thousands of years ago. They're not disinterested in our world, and if their emergence from chaos has any truth to it it's so far in the relative past as to be irrelevant to anyone not banking on an objective and eternal order to the cosmos.
                                            There are two fundamentally terrifying sides of the coin of horror: the thing that is very interested in you that you do not understand, and the thing that is very DIS-interested in you that you do not understand. (Assumption: you are impacted in both cases. Otherwise, there is no conflict and thus no horror.)

                                            Acknowledging that the Judges are side 1. What do we have in CofD cosmology that most closely fits side 2 and thus either are or could be scaled into the Great Old One eldritch horrors of Lovecraftian fame?

                                            (Mummy's emphasis on cults can induce senses of Lovecraft, but cults could show up in any gameline, so let's set that aside.)

                                                Originally posted by Khanwulf View Post
                                                Acknowledging that the Judges are side 1. What do we have in CofD cosmology that most closely fits side 2 and thus either are or could be scaled into the Great Old One eldritch horrors of Lovecraftian fame?
                                                It's a pat simplification in general, but the Annunaki presumably have a similar relation to the world as the True Fae do: just like not all of the Gentry manifest their Titles as Keepers and other forms intent upon some stolen element of the waking world, much (inasmuch as "much" means anything in a place where quantity doesn't really exist) of the Abyss seems unconcerned with material reality unless it bothers them; that the hostility of the Abyss appears to have increased from the perspective of linear time as the Phenomenal World's history has moved on could be sampling bias or it could be indicative of the reality-hating elements of the Abyss getting more of their tendrils in the Tapestry, but for the most part the default state of an Abyssal entity in its normal state seems to be indifference to the world generated by the Supernal.

                                                By a similar tone, albeit at a reduced scale, the chthonians of Geist explicitly don't care about the ghosts around them unless they've stumbled into a hunting ground somewhere on the Rivers or elsewhere, being largely content to just eat each other and filter feed on the Essence carried down from the living world. No verdict yet on whether the Chthonic Gods (should they really exist) have any direct relation to the never-born "natives" of the Underworld, but the directive they impose upon Reapers is sufficiently interested-in-people that it somewhat outweighs their mythic scope.

                                                And then there's Weird Shit In The Anima Mundi, for which "psychic superbeings from the dreams of the world itself" initially sounds comprehensible until you remember that it's the source of a couple particularly worrying Legacies like the Unforgotten Scions and the Morphean Continuity; there's things in the Dreamtime that humanity only has tenuous terminology for, and many of them are content to go about their business beyond the Omphalos without ever seeing human contact.
