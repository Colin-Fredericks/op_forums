    Scroll of Ages and Mummy inaccessibility

    05-04-2015, 02:21 PM
    First of all, I really like the central concept in Mummy of waking powerful but oblivious to your past, and growing less powerful but more wise as time goes on. It's a great idea to structure any plot consisting revelations and unfolding mysteries around. I also adore the Utterances, the cults, the Shuankhsen antagonists. I like how Sothis Ascends makes a campaign setting for the game and gives you reams of locations and eras to set your game in with hundreds of plothooks.

    But...

    My main issue with Mummy is I find the lore inaccessible. I think it's in part a language thing. I'm dyslexic, but I'm not a stupid guy, but with every new book there are new terms being thrown out and I'm struggling to understand them all. It really confused me with Book of the Deceived, as half of the book is too dense for me. Like the writers were referring to such heavy mythology they couldn't explain anything in a straightforward way. This probably makes me sound dim, but I get every other WoD game, and honestly I couldn't read Deceived without having to put it down constantly realizing I'd not understood half of what I'd read. It was like reading course material for an exam on a subject I knew very little about.

    I've spoken to other players who have the same problem and none of them are dyslexic. It's a copout to call them stupid too. They don't want to play Mummy because it's too dense despite the excellent premise. It's not a game you can pick up and play. It's a game you need to study and interpret. This is probably why it gets nominated for awards but I can never find players. It's like an art film. A small group will love it. I love elements, but the game as a whole takes a lot of chewing.

    This takes me to the title of the thread. The Scroll of Ages. In the opening to Mummy it mentions a Scroll of Ages that'd be updated regularly with a lexicon, and other useful features to help us with our games. This would be such a perfect resource for Mummy. It's a little ridiculous that any game needs a reference guide, but it would help a lot of people understand what's on the page.

    Will this Scroll of Ages ever see release? It was something promised when the game was released. Who has the responsibility for developing this feature? Is it something the community were meant to develop as part of the wiki? Is it something C.A. Suleiman was supposed to be doing but never got to?

    If anyone can help me with my queries about the Scroll of Ages, or even if they have the time and will create a lexicon right here on the forum, it'd help me and many others gets a grip on this game that seems to put up barriers for new players.

    One more thing. It probably seems like I should just try harder to get it, but I thought about that and realized this is meant to be a game, and surely anyone should be able to have fun with a game. Just because I'm not the most able reader when it comes to complex language or historical terms with no context doesn't mean I shouldn't be able to play, and again, I've no problem with any other RPG. There's something about Mummy, it's terms, its writing, that seems incredibly inaccessible. Have you found this?

                                                      Duat is where the Arisen rest between Descents. Nothing ever allows them to remember their time in Duat, not even high Memory. During a death cycle (when they're killed in the middle of a Descent), the Arisen go to Neter-Khertet (Twilight, the sort of invisible realm overlaying the mortal world, where unmanifested ghosts hang out) and sometimes meet with Anpu/Anubis.

                                                      The Shan'iatu were the sorcerer-priest-kings who ran Irem. Each of the Guilds had seven Shan'iatu guildmasters, who led the populace to observe the word of the Judges. Book of the Deceived suggests that they tried to usurp the Judges using the Rite of Return, hoping to become gods; the Arisen were to be their immortal unthinking servants.

                                                      The Judges are the 42 godlike entities that the Arisen exist to serve. The Shan'iatu brought the worship of the Judges to Irem and hoped to join their ranks with magic. Given that they're roughly aligned to the Five Decrees (each of the five are tied to a Nameless God: Re for Falcon-Headed, Esit for Lion-Headed, Ptah for Bull-Headed, Sutek for Serpent-Headed, and Anpu for Jackal-Headed), we don't really know too much about how the Judges connect to the Nameless Gods other than some king of relation. The Nameless Gods are more powerful than them, I believe. Azar is the king of the Nameless Gods.

                                                      Temakh are the shattered spirits of the Shan'iatu who ran the Lost Guild, who were Deceived and denied ascension. They're basically insane ancient ghost-monsters who share mental space with the Deceived, kind of like super-powerful super-crazy geists. They usually want worship and to exact revenge upon the servants of the Judges. The Arisen know nothing of them and almost none of them believe the Lost Guild still exists.

                                                          Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
                                                          Re for Falcon-Headed, Esit for Lion-Headed
                                                          Other way around, actually, as weird as it seems to anyone who would assume "Ra" is the falcon.

                                                          Other than that, excellent summary. The only things I would add are the following:

                                                          The Shan'iatu are, according to the Book of the Deceived, not human. They didn't need the Rite to become immortal, because they were already immortal. Evidence suggests the Rite was intended to empower them to take up the mantle of godhood (at least on par with the Judges), an ascension rather than a bid to longevity on their part.

                                                          Azar is the king of Duat, so the 42 Judges are subordinate to him. We don't actually know if he is the chief of the Nameless Gods. He is the one the Shan'iatu placed above the others in their own rituals, however, and was the patron god of Irem.

                                                          The nature of the Judges is very complicated and we have some conflicting evidence on what they are. The 42 who rule the Arisen may have been products of the same Rite that created the Arisen, or they could be the original 42 Judges "of Life" who were the parents of the Shan'iatu as described in the Book of the Deceived, manifestations of divine law.
