    Mummy Cult as antagonists in other gamelines

    11-21-2021, 11:51 AM
    I've got a Changeling setting where rare books are going to be a big deal, and the idea of a cult with an inactive (for now) Arisen dedicated to seeking out rare texts (Relic and mundane) came to mind. I'm thinking a division of the Library of Congress that operates in secret with a surreal twist like the British Library of the Read or Die anime. It'd explain some fanatical bibliomania, and I can see myself portraying an agent going to extremes against the Motley, all the while yelling, "Give me that book!" a la Yumiko Readman.
    City Design: The city of Buena Vista was built on a peninsula, in the shape of a pentagon as reference to the five Pillars for Sacred Geometry reasons. I was also thinking the cult would be in possession of a model of the city with some kind of Effigy power. Hints would be some kind of unusual civic design choices or strict zoning laws the cult put into place.
    The Archive: The unofficial name of the cult inside the Library of Congress that takes an interest in McGuffin books that'll be showing up in Buena Vista. I'll be looking over Cult Creation and Merits for ideas.
    So, any suggestions on the hints I could drop, how to run the cult members, and so on? I'm in that phase where I feel like I'm missing something but can't put my finger on it.

      My casual thoughts:

      How deeply do you want to delve into Mummy Lore? If you want to tie your Mummy to a Guild, you can make them Sesha-Hebsu. They're the ones with affinity for Relic Texts, so it would make perfect sense. It would also add context of making your Mummy a Scribe from the Nameless Empire, which can inspire you as to what goals the Cult may have. This brings me to my second point. What is the Cult's purpose in collecting writing? Relics are simple - they can be used for their power, or offered to the Judges. Why do they care about mundane texts? How does it tie to the Judge's agenda and which Judge do they serve? That choice will focus their actions against a particular type of wrongdoing. For instance, the First Judge presides over maintaining divine authority over the world, and punishing crimes of Immortality. That would work pretty well with Read or Die, but other Judges could give you a different spin on the concept. Basically, you have to come up with Cult Doctrines. If you have that, the rest should be easier.

              Alright, so the Cult is meant to protect rare texts from destruction and misuse. The next step is to determine how to best make them antagonistic against Changelings. The choice of the Judge will play a role in that.

              • Initially, I suggested the First Judge because of the antagonists from Read or Die. As I read the premise, I understood them to be sort-a Immortals who wish to rule the world. You may replace them with True Fae. The Cult would see the Gentry and all their works (including Changelings) as abominations before the Judges - they twist rules and steal Mortal souls away from judgement. Such a cult would be most fanatical and antagonistic of all presented choices.

              • Heraf-Het judges over transgressions of fine distinction, so his Cultists would have to constantly question themselves. That would make them most likely to negotiate with Changelings, unless either PCs or Cultists make things personal. If you want peace to be an option, this is an easy choice.

              • Worshipping Neb-Imkhu or Sekhiru (or both) would force the Cultists to assess who is meant to have the knowledge, and who doesn't. That could instill a sense of elitism. On the other hand, Changelings are already part of the supernatural, and the Cult could find common cause with them. That places this option between the two former ones, when it comes to odds of peaceful resolution.

              Find out what characters your players intend to make. That way, you will be able to fine-tune the antagonists to be most appropriate.

                      You seem to be homing in on your goal. When it comes to surrealism, I can suggest the following:

                      • Curses of Text Relics tend to affect minds, so they can cause delusions, hallucinations and strange behaviors. Tokens demand odd actions and have weird drawbacks, so the same thing can be said of them.

                      • There's an idea I like where Fae phenomena hi-jack human creativity. As a result, they can manifest as living pictures, sentient TV channels, an opinionated voiceover narrating the characters' actions, a predatory ecosystem made of lost toys, and so on...

                      • Both Mummies and Fae phenomena have a wonky relationship to time and fate. Changelings find their loopholes, while Mummies cut through them like through butter, being beholden to something grander. Time anomalies, visions of the future and the past, anachronistic objects, destinies of varying seriousness - each can play a role in your game.

                      • Faced with something baffling, an average Changeling can be uncertain whether it's something Fae, Mummy-related, or merely a sign of cracked Clarity. You can play off that uncertainty.

                      I there anything else you'd like help with?
