    All I can really say is "Wow!" The Deceived are not at all what I expected, and all the more awesome for it.

    My only problem with the book is I still really don't get what seba actually are. They're confluences of Fate that manifest as great works of art? So Beethoven's 5th is an expression of seba, say?     Glad we wowed you. Seba are patterns of numinous Sekhem that are created sometimes by masterful art and other times by the Sekhem of the cosmos itself. You attach them to your soul and they become part of you, giving you the curse and power associated with them. Think of them as post-it notes stuck onto your true name.

            Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
            This gets more complex and interesting when you add in that CAS has mentioned several times that the Judges of Duat are not just the ascended Shan'Iatu. However, the end result might have been the creation of a separate group of entities by the Shan'Iatu, as effectively a by-product of them sacrificing Irem (and possibly themselves) to Ammut. In a more liner way, perhaps getting new "true names" changed the Shan'Iatu more then they expected and instead of becoming the same old beings in new forms they became new beings full stop.

                            The fact that there are 42 Judges (regardless of if you believe in a single set or two different sets) does pretty much make it seem like the Shan'Iatu did not manage to pull off their "enter Duat as Gods" thing. However, since magic has to do "something" when it enacted (its an active force after all and a sacrifice was made) she kinda "fulfilled" her end of the bargain and the Judges are the result. Hence because the Judges deal with her has a place in Duat for all 42 of them, 42 Judges popped up.
