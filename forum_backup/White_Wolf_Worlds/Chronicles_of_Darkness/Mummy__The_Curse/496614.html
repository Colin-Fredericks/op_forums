    Share your Arisen!

    04-29-2015, 03:48 PM
    The title speaks for itself; I would absolutely love to hear about your precious Deathless babies and what misadventures they've been up to.

    Nakhte is my main man, a Su-Menent (who, for the life of me, cannot choose between being Lion- or Falcon-Headed; help me) who was the chief of a barbarian tribe in the time of Irem, conquered and converted to the priesthood. He was a bloody force of nature, filled with rage and zealotry to compensate for his former lack of understanding. He's mellowed out over the long ages, becoming less wrathful and more of a nomadic warrior-poet with a love and longing for his lost Empire.

    He has too many titles to list and incorporates images of both the hieracosphinx (lion body, falcon head) and the color scarlet into his personal mythos. Nakhte's cults have been everything from rogue Medjay mercenaries to Minoan refugees and Phoenician explorers, from Mithraic officers in Rome's legions to heretical Templars, members of the German Afrika Korps to a modern British PMC. Military structure brings him great comfort; he longs to have a fighting force of his own to take territory for Irem once more, and only awaits the Shan'iatu to command him as they did in days long past..

    Nekheb-Mewet, an apotheosis-seeking Sesha-Hebsu, has been his lover and hated foe countless times over the march of history, with low Memory driving them to crave the other and remembrance bringing them back to hideous feuds that end in mutual destruction.

    Remi. she/her. game designer.

      Misadventures, you say? I've got an entire topic about a set of Arisen having misadventures:

      http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...my-actual-play

      I don't have a player character written up myself, although I would lean towards a senior Sesha-Hebsu (probably Ren, but I adore the Ba decree as well). That said, I've done a lot of work on the backstories of my players' Arisen, so I do have a lot of information on the player characters in my games that their own players are not aware of. It has to be one of the parts of Mummy that I most adore, because it gets both the GM and the player invested in the player characters and deeply interested in who that character is. I've included a brief overview of the various characters in my chronicle below.

      Players in my games and people interested in not having possible revelations in my Actual Play thread being spoiled for them shouldn't read these, although I'm trying to avoid touching on things that haven't come up so far:

      Sesu Ra

      The Unbending Spine, a Tef-Aabhi Guildmaster of the Ka Decree, has spent much of the last six thousand years toeing the line between heresy and orthodoxy. Tef-Aabhi guild interests mean that the Descent is a perpetual frustration, removing him from overseeing his most important projects for years at a time. The Ka Decree simply compounds that, making him wish the Descent allowed for the kind of fortitude and staying power his inclinations would prefer. And so his experiments in the mystic arts over the last six thousand years have been a long chronicle in finding the best ways to prolong the Descent. In some lives he has drained relics, in others he has taken every opportunity to adhere to his Judge's wishes, mounting crusade after crusade against the Lifeless and mercilessly hunting down those who transgress against his Judge, Set-Qesu. At all times he has pushed forward the agenda of his Guild, using Talismans to ensure he remembers his long term objectives, working on projects to slow the Descent for all Arisen in the cities in which he dwells. At times he has come close to considering the value of an enslaved Amkhat to bring him tithes of Sekhem so that he might last for ever longer periods, but he has not yet fallen to such a low. Similarly, the concept of Apotheosis is one that he has yet to entertain, he values his position in Arisen society too highly to risk such blasphemy.

      Through it all, he has been dogged by a Shuankhsen whose presence scratches at the wall in his mind. The creature seems to provoke memories in him that are better kept buried. Others who have been with him through past Descents remember him burning out and replacing Talismans to destroy the memories preserved within, deliberately sabotaging his own Memory to destroy the revelations of who this being is and what it wants. He has no memory of these events himself, and his century-old Sadikh says he has been commanded not to speak of them, so whatever dark secret lies buried in the depth of his mind remains undisturbed for now.


      Aquila

      The Sphinx of Rome, a Mesen-Nebu Guildmaster and member of the Ab decree. She forsook her own birth-name to adopt a nickname given at the height of the Empire. She kept to Rome long after its fall from grace, but through every life for the last few millennia at least she has fought for the poor and destitute. She sees value in the mortal soul, value that cannot be found anywhere else. After all, all Dedwen flows from humanity, and thus every human is a potential source. By uplifting those worst off in a society, the society as a whole must surely increase in value, as shall all things within. Lead into gold as evidenced by the transformation of the desperate. She has fought in revolutions and slave uprisings, but her approaches through history have been a vicious cycle as her idealism gets ground down after repeated failures. In times of high memory, she remembers the failure of peaceful reformation and gives in to the temptations of violence and brutality to bring her goals to fruition. Doing so erodes her memory, and eventually she is left with little more than her emotions and a burgeoning sense of idealism grows once again.

      She has memories of barbarous bloodshed that stretch from the Third Servile War to the Boxer Rebellion. Her desperation only grows as she realises that near every cause she has supported has failed, crushed by the empires of the time. And yet she takes hope from the fact that personal freedoms seem to be growing, that the world is, in her eyes, a little more civilised today than it was in the past. Others in her meret claim not to see the difference, but she sees the rise of democracy and her vague knowledge of certain UN charters as evidence of her private philosophies.


      Sekhet Bastet

      She Who Is Power, a Su-Menent and member of the Sheut Decree. Her own memories of her past are scant, but she knows herself to be a fanatic in the service of Her-Uru. She upholds the Judges' mission above all else. Her First Purpose comes before any other consideration, and from there she will seek to gather as many relics for her Judge as she can. Unlike the rest of the meret, she has no long term goals and little interest in planning for the future. She feels in her soul that she belongs to Duat, and her time in the mortal world exists for nothing more than to further the mission of the Judges.

      Although she has little in the way of recollection, her campaign has taken her from the cities of Rome to the temples of Cambodia. She has claimed relics from the ruins of South America, hunted thieves across the ocean, and thrown herself against the Shuankhsen again and again in the hope that this time, finally, the creatures would stay dead. She remembers her greatest triumph, in the building of a cult of mortals given over entirely to the will of the Judges. She remembers her greatest failing, allowing a fellow Su-Menent to be corrupted and become that which she hates. Still, she takes faith in the knowledge, in the absolute certainty and inevitability, that the Judges shall triumph.


      Akenakhi

      The Fog of Torment, a Su-Menent of the Ren Decree. The traitor, the heretic, and the seeker of enlightenment. He is on the path to Apotheosis, with a Memory that nears complete restoration. He has sought a way to free himself from the Judges since nearly his first Descent, or so he believes. He has a vague sense of the horror he felt in the first Sothic Turn, when he realised the nature of his condition. And yet he has, at times, proven to be thoroughly selfish and uncaring in his quest. Mortal lives have been sacrificed without a thought, and the trials he has subjected his cultists to in order to obtain the knowledge he wants and needs go beyond the pale. Only in the revelation that freedom could be won through memory has stayed his hand at times. He is less the noble and kind bodhisattva, and more the slave who would willingly slit his companion's throat if it gave him a chance to crawl to freedom. Whether that opportunistic bent will be his undoing is yet to be seen...


      I also have a group of Deceived lying about somewhere, but that's another story entirely.

          These are a few sample npcs I used in my games:

          A particularly charismatic Falcon-Headed Tef-Aabhi gets it into his head that this degenerate age needs the true guidance of a firm-handed leader, someone imbued with the wisdom of ages. Someone like him.

          The Judges punish his attempts at reaching out with his cult, grasping the local infrastructure and subverting the culture like crazy, but his message rings true to like-minded Arisen, many of them followers of the Ba like him, as they start creating the first empire ruled by the Arisen. Many opposing Arisen cults are simply swallowed by the collective might of their secret societies.



          The so-called Heretic isn't the only of his kind. One of the Jackal-Headed has recently gained notoriety as well as the enmity of the Su-Menent for her heretic dogma. This Arisen believes that the number of Judges of Duat isn't necessarily fixed, and that it might well be possible for individual Arisen of sufficient dedication and ruthlessness to ascend to their numbers.

          The Judges are, obviously, opposed to her beliefs, but not to her methods. The heretic claims that, in order for a Deathless mummy to ascend to the ranks of the Judges, she has to destroy everything that made her human. Only a mummy with no memories of human existence (Memory 0) could ever transform into a god.




          No Arisen has spoken to Hsekiu, Arisen of the Tef-Aabhi, in centuries. He lies, like so many of the Deathless, dead but dreaming. No cultists revere his image (and if they do, then in secret). No Arisen utter his name, even as a whisper.

          The guilds do not precisely remember why they are so afraid of calling him back to life. There are faint memories of blood .. pain ... fates worse than death, especially for the Deathless. His tomb remains undisturbed, and if the guilds have their way, it shall remain so forever.




          Rekhetre was once one of the most popular of the Su-Menent, renowned for being a death priest of unusually high compassion. The Rite of Return, the most powerful feat of magic ever performed by men, wasn't strong enough to change her caring nature, and it was to noone's surprise when she declared her decree to be that of the Heart.

          Her experiences in Buddhist India gave her compassion focus and guidance, as she refined her beliefs and learned about the suffering inherent in all life.

          Millenia later, Rekhetre, still a priest of death, brings everlasting peace to those who need it - and, in the end, everyone does. She still means well, as she always did, which is why her methods of passing the sacrament of death on to others are gentle, but neither she nor her cult of extremists are particularly discriminating as to who they murder in the name of compassion. Sooner or later, they'll figure out some way of using modern weaponry for the purposes of sharing their sacrament with hundreds or even thousands of people all at once; and on that day, Rekhetre, a Bodhisattva who chose to live and feel and suffer for all eternity, so that others don't have to, will weep with joy for bringing peace to so many people.






          A recently awakened Sesha-Hebsu, confused and lacking in Memory, has started to believe the dogma of her own cult - in which she is revered as a goddess incarnate.

          This in and itself wouldn't be a problem, if the cult wasn't powerful and/or well-connected; but it is. It also has excellent marketing.

          Very soon, cult members and indeed even the mummy herself start having public appearances on local TV and radio stations, spreading the word and gaining new followers by the hour. After only a couple months, the cult is a trend religion, to which all the cool modern kids belong to.

              Well, there haven't been many sessions, the four Arisen that our group has created are very memorable.

              The first of the "meret" is Tnemei Ruhak; The Chronicler of Ages, a Ren member of the Sesha-Hebsu. This Scribe is particularly ambitious for being part of a Guild known for it's ambition, for he has made it his immortal mission to catalogue as many events as possible. Thus he's actually up surprisingly often, and has alluded to possessing massive libraries of information that he has collected over the millennia (As for remembering where any of these cashes of cultural and historical accounts may be.. that's a completely different story). Personality wise while he is ambitious, he possesses a very dry and analytical view of the world that leaves little room for emotional attachment save for cataloguing particularly exciting events, thus in comparison to the others he appears to be much more stunted( and mummy-like) when it comes to interactions with other characters. Nonetheless, he's easily the most intriguing of the PCs.

              The second and third are also rather interesting due to them believing themselves to be the same person, so I will explain both of them together. These two Arisen are named Aahmas, a Sheut member of the Tef-Aabhi and Iskander, an Ab member of the Mesen-Nebu. Both of these mummies believe that they either travelled with or actually were Alexander the Great during the time of his campaign of conquest. What is interesting is that Aahmas is very heavily implied to be a woman, though low Memory has made it so that their Sahu is most definitely male, albeit a rather young one. Iskander is very much sure that he indeed is the true Alexander, though he is also the first to admit that he could also be completely wrong. What makes this even more complicated is that both possess faint/ tattered memories of the same events regarding Alexander's reign of bloodshed, leading to a very interesting identity crisis for both of them.

              The final member of the "meret" is the most amusing one, but also possesses the least depth, not that it's particularly a bad thing. Kek is a Shuet who belongs to the Su-Menent, though somewhere in his long immortal life he had fallen astray, becoming very self-referential and indulgent. While he is a strange mummy indeed, one of the things he is incredibly invested in is his Cult, which he regards with a rather reverent care. The humans who belong to it are slaves yes, but he has a almost grandfatherly affection towards the mortals he lords over, being one of his most redeeming qualities when not acting maniacal or emulating Imhotep from the Mummy movies.
