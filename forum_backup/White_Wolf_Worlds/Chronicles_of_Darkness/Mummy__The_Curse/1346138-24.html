        Originally posted by Neall View Post

        That only removes artforms from the world's artistic paradigms. It's an incredibly blunt hammer, when what you need is the soft and insistent shaping of mummies and cults providing consistent artistic, imperial, and cultural pressure over centuries and millennia.



        This is the crux of the misunderstanding, I think: no, the Judges do not want their servants to rediscover Memory and a sense of self. The Shan'iatu, in creating and encoding the Rite of Return, recognized that a certain amount of Memory could be situationally preferable to the raw might of Sekhem, especially when it comes to that all-important human interaction that is necessary for shaping artistic paradigms in the mold of Irem. They didn't anticipate Apotheosis; they knew that any recovered Memory would be eroded by the waves of Sekhem after the conclusion of the Descent. But the rising and falling pattern of Memory is a feature of the Arisen, not a bug.

        The Arisen are thinking slaves kept in rising and falling states of ignorance for eternity, not perfect automatons, and that's by design.
        As I mentioned earlier, my impression from 1e was not that Memory was intentional on anyone's part, but that it was simply not possible for the Rite to not provide a way out. Remember the fundamental laws of magic in the Mummy setting, and that they are built on Sutek's sacrifice to ensure beings can evade doom in Ammut's maw through their own Will. Of course the Rite can't entirely erase a Mummy's Memory, the very laws of existence itself mandate that they must have a chance to be free.

        All this stuff about the Shan'iatu wanting a certain amount of Memory present doesn't ring a bell to me, sorry. Is there some chapter or quote I can read about it? Mummy has always mapped to the binary of Memory=freedom/rebellion while Sekhem=slavery/power, and the idea of the Shan'iatu embracing a happy medium is new to me. The closest I can recall is them being taken aback that Ammut might use the Shuankhsen as an investment instead of a one-off payment. The arts and template of Irem has always seemed hard coded into civilisation itself, Mummies acting as stewards of it has always seemed more in the vein of making it more likely that they'll be nearby when a relic is made than necessary to perpetuate the existence of those arts.

            Can Wanderer's Clay assume the form of complex items? Can it be reinvoked to make a different object or is it fixed once used? (I recall there were one(ish)-use regia like that wine that could only be recovered by pressing it out of a body.)
            Wanderer’s Clay can’t be used to make particularly complex items. You could mold it into the shape of a revolver, but it wouldn’t function as one. It isn’t a one use item, though.                                     This is what I would do, and this is entirely house ruling, just to be clear.

                                    Iremic Sorcery/Magic/Whatever-You-Call-It is not a kind thing. Anything you want, must be paid for. You're either paying the cost up front, or something else is paying it for you, which means bad times down the road. Unfortunately, there's likely only one kind of coin that will suffice for payment. And so I'd likely go along the route of the Sadikh, requiring a person loyal to you willing to sacrifice themselves for the creation of the benevolent Amhkata. And honestly, I'd probably have that willing sacrifice actually become the Amhkata. I'm sure there are extra things that could be done to make stuff interesting and worthwhile as additional Tomb infrastructure, such as the body of the willing sacrifice needing to be interred within the Tomb of the Arisen, and so forth.

                                    But yeah. Something needs to be sacrificed, bare minimum. After that, there are the logistical issues of keeping the creature fed, etc.

                                    Edit: Yeah, I'd model it off of the rite for Sadikh, including hefty cost, probably pissing off the Judges a bit, etc. But probably also allow you to have more than one, while requiring a dedicated Relic to empower each Tomb Guardian that has to stay within the Tomb's Web. Not being able to interact with people like a Sadikh can is a pretty good disadvantage for them.
