    Questions from a newbie

    12-08-2015, 06:13 PM
    I'm new to the World of Darkness in general as well as Changeling: The Lost, and thought I'd ask for advice and ideas while I'm looking for a group in my town. I realize people have their own ways of playing, so I'm hoping for a variety of answers so I can get a good feel for the game.

    1. So, I'm fresh out of the Hedge. What should I put on my To Do List?

    What comes to mind for me, in no particular order:
    1) Discretely check on friends, family, and Fetch. If my Fetch is actually well-adjusted and liked, move on with my life because I don't want to be a jerk by killing him. If not, investigate what I should do to protect everyone from him.
    2) Find a freehold to join, get a grasp on the courts in the region. I'm not sure I grasp the full problem of being courtless, but I know it's bad and carries the suspicion I might be a Loyalist.
    3) Make connections and pledges with normal humans. Keeps a Changeling sane and topped off with glamour.
    4) Get an orientation on the ins and outs of being a Changeling as well as info on any other supernatural beings in the region.
    5) Get in a Motley with the other PCs and get to know them so we can work together.
    6) Get a source of money and a safe place to sleep.

    2. I know the Hedge is dangerous. What are some good reasons to go there, and how do I survive there?

    One thing I'm contemplating with my character is the notion that he's referencing Brer Rabbit: He managed to put an exit from his Arcadian realm in a briar patch, leading into the hedge. He got himself punished in a manner that let him use that exit. Brer Rabbit got himself thrown into a briar patch to escape Brer Fox and Brer Bear, since he's at home in the briar patch, so I'd like ideas on how to be more 'at home' in a place as dangerous as the Hedge. One thing I'm thinking about is the Chimera Kith for the social bonus it provides for dealing with Hobgoblins.

    Other reasons I know of: 1) There's the Goblin Market, where handy stuff might be. 2) Goblin fruit and other possible treasures. 3) Hollows for interdimensional homes, escape routes, shortcuts, and the like.

    3. I don't plan on spending my life hiding under the bed. I want to make the best of being a weird and wonderful creature, but where should I draw the line on being too reckless?

    I think my Seasonal Court leaning would be Spring with this attitude, and I might wind up as the guy who gets into trouble on a more regular basis this way. Hopefully, I can at least get out of trouble with quick wits and guile. I want to keep the game interesting, but I don't want to be the bumbling sidekick who's always in need of rescue, or look like I'm causing trouble for its own sake. He'd probably spend some of his time researching how magic works now that he knows it's real.

    4. In the "Gotta Catch 'em All" thread, I mentioned my character concept for Ziggy. He's a game designer who got abducted as a "rescue" from Development Hell, and escaped from Arcadia by turning himself into one of the collectible monsters from his game. The twist is, he's a "glitch" Beast like MissingNo from Pokemon. What are some ways I could express that glitched nature of his existence, in terms of game mechanics, descriptions, and roleplaying?

    Mechanics: The Gremlin Kith seems appropriate, since it'd give him the ability to cause glitches, but he was abducted to design his collectible monster/survival game, so I might need to find other ways to do that, like Contracts.

    Descriptions: I was thinking he'd have "Tron lines" in his fur as markings or tattoos on his mask, but they'd change when people aren't looking, or they'd move out of sync with his body, like they were lagging, or they'd extend beyond the "borders" of his body. His fur could be patchwork if I go with the Chimera Kith (representing having too many tasks to do as a game designer), with black, gray, and white regions slowly drifting underneath his markings, possibly in pixellated junk at the edges.

    Roleplaying: When woken up, he blurts out humorous or ominous lines from video games. Produces unnatural digital sounds when he tries anything musical, like whistling.

    Equipment: He'd have some glitched objects that look like real life "sprite garbage" or odd chimeric machines. Provided by a willing Storyteller or built himself. Some would be just useless stuff to collect, while others could be MacGuffins. "Is that a rifle or a trombone?" *click. Door unlocks.* "Long range key."

    Other: One thought I had was that his True Name is "missing" in some fashion, like he'd produce the sound of a dial-up modem if he tried to say it. Wyrd: "This changeling has performed an illegal operation and will be rebooted." Ziggy suddenly finds himself waking up in a very different location with no idea how he got there.

              Hope this is helpful, not sure.

              1) Talk to your GM. It really varies by game. I've run two games (one is an AP on the forums here) and the answer varies. Usually food and shelter are a start, if you really are starting out just out of the Hedge.

              2) Those are good options. Note too, again, depends on the game. My current group does not go that much, but another game run within the group goes there ALL THE TIME, they spend almost as much time there as the real world.

              3) A more mischievous character can work, and wits and guile are good traits for Changelings, which hinge more on Finesse.

              4) Wizened or Beast both sound good. If you double kith, Gremlin plus something could be good...Coyote is a mischievous Beast kith, and Riddleseeker could be someone more intellectual. If looking to Gremlinize, Artifice has excellent powers for that, and Animation can work, both are for Wizened. We actually have a Gremlin in my Xover game (Golden Eagle in my Sig) she uses both heavily.

              I know a lot of that is depends on GM and group, but it really often does. if making the character purely for you (which is fine) some questions you will be answering as their next steps will be what interests you.

                  1 - Your first reaction doesn't seem realistic to the setting. You've been abducted by something who as far as your concerned, may have been a god and then mind raped for an unspecific amount of time. When you escape, anywhere from several minutes to several years have passed, and you find one of those... things... is impersonating you and living among your family. For all you know, they could be planning to steal another one of you when the time is right! The first thing on your mind is probably going to be running to friends, family or any authority figure you think may be able to help with this. Only to be intercepted by the Freehold who attempt to calm you down and explain things before you blow their cover.


                  2 - Goblin Markets, Goblin Fruit, and Hollows. Changelings often have need of obscure and bizarre tools and resources only the Hobgoblins can provide, while Goblin Fruits offer a variety of useful effects they can take advantage of. Hollows serve as a base of operations for the Changeling and their Motley.


                  3 - As for not hiding, you only have to worry about that if you're in the Winter Court. However, the Spring Court is also hiding in its own way. They use pleasure and indulgence to block out the painful memories of their time in Arcadia. They are, for lack of a better word, in denial, pretending that everything is nice and happy even though they are suffering by the effects of what's happened to them.

                  The Summer Court are the fiery rebels who decide to start throwing punches, most of the Summer Court is made of Beasts and Ogres for a reason. They're the warriors of the Freehold. The Autumn Court are the scientists and sorcerers who believe in a "fight fire with fire" approach to the Gentry. They study magic and conduct experiments on others to find their weaknesses and then exploit them when they need to.


                  4 - As somebody who's played the original Red and Blue Pokemon and seen what happens when you stupidly catch a MissingNo. I would suggest making him a Mirror Skin, due to their ability to disguise themselves as other beings. In the game, MissingNo had a tendency to change at complete random into many different types of pokemon. I remember in one instance it "evolved" into a level 255 Snorlax, who then evolved into level 1 Golduck.

                  The primary glitch that MissingNo is known for is duplication, whether that be items or other pokemon. So a shapeshifting Mirror Skin seems the most appropriate for that theme.


                  Of course, this depends on which version of the game you're playing. If it's first edition, then your selection of Kiths are going to be limited by your Seeming. If it's second edition, then any Kith is available for any Seeming, so you're free to choose whatever.

                      In regards to the Hedge, sometimes you also don't have a choice. When your friend is kidnapped by a Privateer and takes him into the Hedge, you follow them to free him. When you accidentally insult a hobgoblin at the local Goblin Market, you might need to find the right goblin fruit to compensate him before you and your Motley are banished from the Goblin Market for eternity. When the Sheriff of the Summer Court asks you a favor to investigate an odd murder, because you handled an investigation in the mundane world, you are not likely to say no. When you are boxed in an abandoned warehouse, two angry vampires hot on your trail, getting into the Hedge might be your only way out of Dodge.

                      In my current campaign none of the PCs are big fans of the Hedge, and they try to avoid it. I don't even have to work all that hard though as a Storyteller to get them to enter the Hedge though. It is simply too easily to slip into, and it has way too many benefits despite the dangers. And belief me, my players still talk in horror about their near death experience with a pack of briar wolves or how one barely managed to escape from a band of red caps
