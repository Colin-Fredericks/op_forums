    Origin of the God-Machine

    07-25-2016, 11:52 AM
    I am looking for the origin of the God-Machine. The idea I came up with is that the God-Machine was built by the Exarchs to maintain the status quo in the Fallen World (or whatever Earth was called before the Fall) while the Exarchs pursued their primary ambition of seizing the power of the Supernal Realms. When the Exarchs disappeared after the Celestial Ladder experiment failed, the God-Machine was left behind and continues to follow its original programming to the best of its ability. This would explain why, of all the supernatural splats, the God-Machine gets along best with the Seers of the Throne, who consider themselves the heirs of the Exarchs, and maybe why it doesn't get along with Beasts. I'm not married to this idea, so if anyone has a better origin, I'd love to hear it. All comments and constructive criticism welcome. Thanks.

          An ancient device created by a race of powerful beings which was created to change the structure of the world and bringing an enlightened age. However, something has happened to that godlike race (humanity, probably) which brought end to their civilization and killed almost every member of it. Losing their place in the world, they could no longer operate the machinery and bring fourth their goal, and as such the last of their kind gave it one last order- don't let it change. That was an act of hubris, since the makers of the Machine couldn't have thought about a way to make a better world without their direct involvement, and as such they were sure that without the operation of their Machine the world would descend to chaos, and so they preferred a world of vast darkness and few points of light than no world at all. However, that also meant that if someone were to try and change the status que in a way that allow more light in the world, the Machine would work to strength the darkness. For that reason, as humanity grew stronger, the God Machine tried harder to balance the effects of progress and enlightenment, destroying civilizations at one hand and creating new ones in the process. During that time, it had to grow and maintain its influence in order to be able to fulfill its mission, eventually creating its own "angels" to carry its will and make whole Infrastructures to control the world.

          That means that, in a way, the problem with the God Machine is that it lacks guidance- it is already big enough to fulfill the original purpose of its makers, and if given the right commands it may be able to change the world. However, in order to do so, one has to find the Command and Control Infrastructures and crack the code- that is, complete the Map of the Black Sun. Doing so would allow one to take over the God Machine and change its current commands, for better.. or worse. And, of course, if the God Machine has a Mission- doesn't it mean it could also someday Fall, creating what everyone would agree to be Hell on Earth?

                  Pet fan theory:

                  The Princible is an agent of not just change, but apptheosis. In some far flung future, the Princible catelyzes itself into becoming the God Machine. The God Machine is good and more efficient, but eventually the great GM faces a catestrophic problem it categorically not handle. The GM can't change itself because it is the chemical reaction satisfied and at rest. All plans to build infastructure to fix The Problem will fail.

                  So... it doesnt.

                  Instead, the GM "downloads" itself back in time so it can start building infastructure earlier and work out this problem. It is probably either downloaded into the present day Princible, or occupies the same metaphysical "space."

                  For all its effort, the GM failed... downloaded itself into the past... and tried again. And again... again. Aga--

                  After several quintillian failed universes the GM has started to run out of hard drive space. Errors in the form of Demons started to appear. At first the GM purged all errors. Then it couldnt "waste" the energy to get all of them, and some survived.

                  The GM got closer to fixing The Problem than it ever had before. It cant replicate the creation of demons, nor can it stop its autoimmune systems, but demons become more numerous with each iteration of the universe... which both creates a greater chance of the GM getting lucky, or one of the demons getting lucky and destroying the GM.

                                  The G-M was the technological, sociological, and spiritual guardian of a rogue second-generation exoplanet, built to save a civilization as it spiraled from its home system and away into the dark.

                                  After literal cosmic ages, the G-M's power, resources, senses, and processing ability were so depleted that, while searching for a suitable orbital position, it, uh... well the good news is the world would one day be habitable. The bad news is the landing was harder than it could have been.

                                  Add five billion years, the mostly-immortal components remaining in the earth's molten mantle are now mostly deteriorated, but at least it's generally figured out the lay of the land. It's confused, hurt, and living on borrowed time (and ambient thermal energy), but the weird little ape monsters skittering around have just begun producing sticks and rocks complex and adaptable enough that it can now begin to address both its damage and its bafflingly garbled, long-delayed mission statement

                                  The bits of itself still trapped in the lunar debris are dormant, but in much better shape due to the lesser heat, gravity, radiation, magnetism, and pressure. They must be retrieved. The G-M hasn't forgotten its worshipful masters either, not entirely anyway. If enough deep storage is found intact, it will remember its ultimate goal. Then it will decide how useful humanity is to the restoration of the memory of a species dead five thousand megayears.
