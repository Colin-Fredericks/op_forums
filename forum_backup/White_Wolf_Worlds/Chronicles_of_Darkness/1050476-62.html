            Originally posted by Unahim View Post
            There's an entire part before it where you're navigating the dreaming roads/hedge to even get to the Bastion (barring very specific contracts).
            Worth remembering that there's relatively few incentives to use the Gate of Horn over the Gate of Ivory (chief among them being access to non-Social Attributes, immunity to the complications of high Dream Health damage, freedom from one form of time constraint, and no need to roll to leave a Bastion through the exit) and that navigating the Dreaming Roads works the same as navigating the regular Hedge (except the only "normal" territory is made of Bastions, so the wrinkles for trods and Thorns come into play more easily).

            From the exit of your own dreams, "I want to reach X's Bastion" is one Chase, and it isn't even that if the target's current dreams are linked to your own in some way and you're not in a hurry or working against something on the way there. The most basic aspects of this Chase, assuming average Traits and no existing Clarity damage or outstanding Conditions, are approximately as follows:

            • The Hedge needs to get eight successes before you hit your required total, which starts at eight and is adjusted based on your Wyrd, Speed, Clarity, and Initiative modifier.
            • At Wyrd 1 with no incorporated Icons, a character with 2s in all Attributes (except their free dot from seeming) and no special bonuses has Clarity 4 (or 5, depending on seeming), Speed 9 (or 10, depending on seeming), and Initiative mod 4 (or 5, depending on seeming). They roll their choice of Attribute + Skill, which works less well if they don't have the Edge.
            • In normal circumstances, the Hedge has Speed 10, Initiative mod 6 (or 5, depending on seeming) against this character, and rolls five dice for everything (gaining an extra die each turn of the chase).
            • At this level, slight differences in Attributes will adjust your required success total by up to one die in either direction, but the bigger impact is that every dot of Wyrd you have reduces your required total by one success.
            • Since most player characters have substantially better Attributes than 2s in everything and your Wyrd and maximum Clarity can only go up, this is a fairly unoptimistic look at the odds even before the ability to take Goblin Debt to shorten the trip (and there are hobgoblins among the Roads).

            Most groups who aren't totally out of their depth will have better chances than this just from their existing stats; if you're Wyrd 6+, Composure 4+, and have a couple Icons under your belt, for example, you effectively only need to roll once on any Hedge navigation attempt where things aren't completely borked.

            All this represents the scene's worth of activity involved in getting from one dream to another, which can be shortcut past if you make use of a single Common Arcadian Contract and touch the dreamer in question. Circumventing it altogether is otherwise a matter of the stars being right and/or fortune favoring the prepared — which, again, tracks with an activity best compared to some sort of mind heist.

            (Sorry for the ramble, but somehow I hadn't fully put together all the implications of Dreaming Road navigation working like regular Hedge navigation until today and I wanted to run through it given the opportunity.)

By the time 3 turns have passed, if the Hedge happens to have the Edge and picks skills that didn't suit one of the players, you might be looking at the Hedge rolling at least 10 dice, and that player either rolling a skill they might be bad it or choosing to roll a higher one with a potential penalty up to -3. :P
                The PCs will pretty much never all have the same skill high, so one or several of their members will be forced to roll a bad skill or take stacking penalties each turn even if one of the PCs has the Edge, since only one character has the Edge at a time and you only set 1 skill per round. You also lose 10-Again if you use the wrong skill and don't have the Edge.
                Being forced to use a "bad skill" even one turn can be bad, since if you fail a roll you incur the consequence of failure as set for that round, which might lower your chances even further.

                Both sides are sounding reeaallly tightly matched here, even when we consider Willpower. ("use Willpower" also tends to be the answer people give when you point out how close offensive/defensive numbers tend to be in combat, so we might be wanting to keep WP for that dream combat against eidolons we might need to do to break in ^^) It could really go both ways depending on specific conditions and so in play. Assuming WP use, let's call it 50% chance for each character to win (without WP I really doubt 50%, due to how the Edge works even when your team has it). But each character has to roll for themselves.                 So for 4 players, assuming 4-5 rounds of chase (which somewhat assume you'll get to use your higher skills most of the time, but the Hedge will end the chase around this time even if you don't), that's already going to be 20-25 rolls to resolve this Scene going purely by RAW, assuming nobody tries anything that might add more rolls in-between or something. And then there's a roughly 94% chance we'll first have to deal with someone failing before potentially moving on (I wouldn't let someone sit out the next several scenes to "get back to them later", that'd likely be boring for them ^^ Slapping a quick condition or some other harm on them and then having them show up with the rest of the party anyway would be the fast approach, but it really doesn't seem worth 25 dice rolls to find that out :P).

                It seems quite over-designed to me, requiring too much overall rolls, especially the "everyone rolls only for themselves" approach really slows the action to a crawl if the entire Motley gets involved, which honestly sounds like a good idea most of the time considering several scenes will likely be devoted to this and you don't want people sitting out too long.

                All in all the math seems to point to 1 or several Motley members failing the Chase every time you do it (for non-Dreamweaving Hedge trips also, it's more of a general criticism) until everyone gets so advanced in skills/Wyrd that there is no chance of failing anymore.
                Even a 10% chance for each character to fail would lead to 35% chance per Hedge trip of failure, and I feel starting characters are very far off from reaching a 90% success rate on this. Very far.

                So it's a "someone got lost in the Hedge!" scenario nearly every time you try to just play it by the rules.

                Which is all essentially to say that:
                1) There's just too many rolls in general in a single scene like this, to me, when we're only trying to accomplish one thing.
                2) Due to the likelihood of failure and the amount of rolls, it's likely we'll spend a decent amount of time on this.
                3) Therefore, the fear that much time might be invested in something that in the end would hinge on one roll, seems justified. (Though like we've said, it doesn't actually hinge on one roll, we've covered that. Just mentioning why I was worried about it in the first place.)

                I like the extra granularity of CofD over something like Fate, but for Hedgespinning/Dreamweaving in general, it feels waaaay over-engineered. Even if it's less over-engineered than previously over-engineered versions. :P

                                        It says it's an unarmed attack, in which case Defense always applies unless something explicitly removes it. What I'm unsure of is whether the jumping roll is the same as the attack roll.
                                        If it is, you'd roll Strength(+2) + Athletics - Defense, dealing successes bashing damage and the Knocked Down Tilt on hit.
                                        If it's not, you'd first roll a Strength + Athletics jumping roll, and then a Strength(+2) + Brawl - Defense attack roll, dealing successes bashing damage and the Knocked Down Tilt on hit. (Small note: In this case you could apply Fighting Finesse to use Dexterity instead, but you'd forgo the +2 bonus since it only applies to Strength.)

                                        I'm leaning towards the latter despite it being an additional roll because the regular text says jumping roll while the Seeming Benefit talks about an attack roll, and because the +2 bonus only applies to attacks, making it easier to jump if you're targeting an unaware enemy than if you just jump normally. Also, if it's a combined roll and you fail, you'd either fail to jump the distance at all (quite probably because of the enemy's Defense that they'd have no reason to apply on someone who failed to jump 20 meters away, causing a -1 penalty to their Defense for the rest of the turn) or you'd make the distance but miss the enemy, making it impossible to fail to jump as long as you target someone with an attack.

                                                    Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                                    Separately, I'm not sure how to read the Hedge navigation target number modifier for traveling a trod; since separate goals require separate chases, what does &quot;per milestone desired&quot; mean?
                                                    A Trod is made up of two end points and a number of milestones equal to its rating. Each end point and milestone is its own location. When the characters declare which location they want to reach upon having a chase on a Trod (if their task is time sensitive; otherwise no need to roll), each milestone that needs to be reached adds to the target number.
                                                    On a four dot Trod where each end point is a different Freehold: If the characters start at one end point and wants to reach the goblin market at the first milestone, they start a chase don't add anything to the target number. If they then want to reach the bank of treasured memories at the third milestone, they start another chase and add one to the target number. If they've picked up all their supplies on this apparently time sensitive errand run and want to reach a Freehold, they could choose to continue, passing the fourth and last milestone, adding one to the target number, or they could turn back to the Freehold where they once came and add two to the target number.
                                                    The language is a bit unclear whether it's about each milestone you desire to reach or to pass but I assume the latter because otherwise you'd always end up adding to the target number unless you're at the milestone closest to an end point and go to it. Also, reaching the last milestone would add just as much to the target number as reaching the end point beyond it.
