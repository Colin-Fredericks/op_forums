            Just something I've been wondering: has any thought been given as to crossovers between Leviathan and the canon game splats?

            I mean, yes, all NWoD books are written as stand-alones, allowing the player race to be the only (or only significant) thing going bump in the night... but, at the same time, the NWoD has always been better than the OWoD in that it actually puts thought into what will happen if the other races of the meta-setting actually are running around and bumping into your PCs.

            So, it makes sense, to me at least, to put some thought into canonical interactions between Leviathans and other monsters. What do they think of Werewolves, who have similar shapeshifting powers and "divine lineage"? Or Mummies, who are literally relics of the old world that the Tribe once dominated? Or Mages, whose powers are such that they can claim to be the new gods of humanity, or Demons, who are literally technology beyond even human understanding come to life?

            Hell, no matter what ultimately happens to the Marduk Society, some thought should be put into what happens should they cross paths with the other horrors lurking in the dark. How do they think of Werewolves, who are so much like terrestrial-focused Leviathans, but which have adapted far better to the changing world around them, even being capable of subverting technology to their whim (thanks to "ins" with Technology spirits)? What happens when they come into contact with a being that seems to pollute the minds around it, and even the very earth underfoot, but which actually feeds on the lightning that they try to destroy it with? Mages can reasonably claim a divine status, being capable of commanding life, death, space, time, the matter around them, the fundamental forces of the universe itself - how does Marduk reconcile all that unimaginable power existing in the hands of "superstitious primitives"? Demons are many-angled transdimensional quantum data singularities who tore themselves free of a machine so vast that the laws of physics are either a side-effect of its operating system, part of its hardware, or both - and they use that technological nature to indulge in the most base and human of natures; how does the Marduk Society reconcile that with their reverence for technological progress as the physical expression of reason's triumph over primeval nature?

            There's just so many interesting aspects to think about when it comes into crossing over Leviathans or Marduk with the canon splats, it seems a shame not to try and address them...

                            And here it is:

                            If this doesn't satisfy people's desire to strip away civilisation, reason and morality then I don't know what will. Call of the Depths (Descendant Channel)

                            The voice of the Leviathan is what inspired legends of sirens. Speaking, crying, bellowing, or whispering: all vocalizations of the Leviathan creep into the minds of listeners, robbing them of their will and self determination.
                              • Dice Pool: Presence + Expression + Sheol
                              • Action Type: Standard

                            The Leviathan's can create an unearthly song or other, stranger, vocalisation that interacts with the deepest and most primitive parts of the mind. Those that hear the voice, are vulnerable to the Wake, and have less (Resolve + Power Trait) than the Leviathan's successes are affected. However player characters and important NPCs should be aloud to roll Resolve + Power Trait independently.
                            • “Depth 0”: Birthright of Sanctity
                            • “Depth 1”: Those who hear the Leviathan’s voice are lulled into a hypnotic state and take the Insensate Tilt. They retain enough awareness to slow down gently and so a Leviathan can only cause the occasional accident and not mass carnage. The range is 100 yards per dot of Sheol. Physical barriers that block the Leviathan’s voice can lower the range but water will not.
                            • “Depth 2”: As above, but the Leviathan may voluntarily exclude his Beloved from the effect and may speak normally without interrupting his song.
                            • “Depth 3”: As above, and the Leviathan gains an alternative ability. By expending 2 Ichor she may give a command that can be expressed in five or so words such as “come to the waterfront”, “bring me your jewelry” or “kill everyone you see”. Everyone affected will obey the command to the best of their ability, if they complete the task they gain the Insensate Tilt.
                            • “Depth 4”: As above, and the range extends to 500 yards per dot of Sheol.
                            • “Depth 5”: As above, and the Leviathan may target his song’s to a certain group. This group must have a shared biological trait, linguistic trait, or they can be the Beloved of a particular Leviathan. At the storyteller’s discretion supernatural traits unrelated to the Tribe may be used.
                            • “Depth 6”: By expending 4 Ichor the Leviathan may make their song echo against the background fabric of the universe. The Depth 1 or Depth 3 iteration of this Channel become an Environmental Tilt that lasts for the remainder of the scene. Interrupting or otherwise silencing the Leviathan can no longer end the effects.

                            Adaptations
                            • Unholy Disharmonies (O) - In Depth 2 or lower the Leviathan is able to use Call of the Depths to counter another Leviathan’s song. Roll as normal and reduce the target Leviathan’s successes by your successes.
                            • Song of Madness (OO) - In Depth 3 and lower, when using the Depth 3 version of this Channel the Leviathan may trade in Successes to inflict Mental Conditions upon affected individuals at a ratio of 1 Success per Condition. If the Leviathan’s only goal is to inflict Conditions she may forgo giving orders. Conditions last until they’re Resolved, the Leviathan’s song ends, or the affected individual moves out of range.
                            • Echo’s of Madness (OOOO, requires Song of Madness) - In Depth 6 the Leviathan may choose to have their song Echo for up to Sheol days. This cannot extend the duration of any given orders but it can extend the duration of Conditions inflicted through Song of Madness.

                                Hi there! Found the thread through Tvtropes.
                                I've got to implore you to take a closer look at the problems people are having with the current treatment of Marduk. I do think the themes of Progress and Reason can work, but well:

                                Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
                                The idea of creating antagonists to make your protagonist look good or evil by contrast is perfectly fine; however you can screw up the implementation. If I have any criticisms of Heroes - it's that Matt screwed up the implementation.
                                To expand:

                                From a game design perspective, what is the purpose of Marduk: At the root level, you are trying to provide storytellers with tools that they can use to create stories that their players will enjoy. In the Leviathan toolbox, Marduk is supposed to highlight the Tribe's stagnation. As it exists, it does this job terribly.
                                Consider the story options that the current treatment makes possible:
                                1. Beatstick. The players done pissed the ST off, time to die.
                                2. Cat and mouse: Players must deal with Marduk scouts. <== There could be some potential there, depending on how the game goes. Characters will need to be put in situations where they can socialize with/observe the agents with an open mind. Requires a very skilled ST, but the will not have to reinterpret/change Marduk all that much to get it to work.
                                3. I wanna punch them in their stupid perfect faces. <== This is the first one that has really has the potential to display the themes out of the box, but it requires the players to have a reason to be resentful of Marduk because of how they are not stagnant. Just wanting to punch them because they are stronger than you isn't going to convey Marduk's themes. It just makes them feel like bullies.
                                4. Rogue Cell: The players go up against a Marduk cell that has been cut off. Added wrinkle is that this cell has gone astray, and Marduk proper is also after them. <== Here we have a chance to illustrate Marduk's themes by contrast. Thing is, this only really allows the players to see what Marduk is not, rather than what it is. Unless the rogue cell is cartoonishly evil, you run the risk of showing Marduk as close minded or bullies or something else not in keeping with Marduk's desired themes.
                                5. Greater Threat: The players have to team up with agents of Marduk to stop something bad enough that they would be willing to consider this. <== Here we have a chance for the players to see Marduk as the good people that they are. It allows the players/storyteller to explore the differences in their approach to problem solving than those a Leviathan's abilities naturally predispose them towards. Unfortunately, it is next to impossible to do while staying true to the current treatment. In order to pull it off, you need:
                                - An isolated cell, one that does not have the resources to resolve the problem on its own.
                                - A problem that the cell and the players need to resolve, that neither party can actually resolve without the aid of the other.

                                Here are a few stories that it prohibits (the storyteller will have to alter Marduk from the current presentation in order for these to work):
                                1. The players are approached by a Marduk faction who genuinely wants to find a way to help them. They believe that the characters are the victims of circumstance, and deserve a chance at a full and happy life. <== Shows that Marduk are good people, but could also be used to foster resentment in the players, leading to a "I wanna punch them in the face" scenario.
                                1.1. The players are simultaneously attacked by a faction that believes that Leviathans are fundamentally unsalvageable, and should be excised for the good of the world. <== Highlights the dynamic nature of progress, helps lead into an enemy mine situation. I think this is the greatest loss in the transition from 1e to the current incarnation. There, you could have a story about a relatively innocent Leviathan being pursued by a corrupt Marduk agent, bring in a good-guy Marduk agent, have the Leviathans reveal corruption, team up with good guy to stop the corrupt agent. Then, at the end, you can slap the players in the face by having the good agent say that, while the corrupt agent was evil, that doesn't make them good.
                                2. Have the Leviathans deal with people who have been in some way harmed with Marduk, such as people who were made redundant by a Marduk invention. <== This breeds contrast, and allows the storyteller/players to more fully explore the themes of Progress vs their own stagnation.

                                I'm not claiming that each list is exhaustive.
                                Marduk as it currently is does not lend itself well to the stories that will best explore its themes. Remember, both players and storytellers need to be interested for it to work.

                                What does "the way science, reason, civilisation, etc help make humanity into better people" actually mean: This is a biggie, and why I think it would make sense for you to make "Reason and Progress" the core or Marduk, and allow the "better people" part to emerge more organically. First however, a quick rant on the nature of Progress:
                                Progress is not neat, it is not even, it is not inevitable, it has no end goal. Progress proceeds in fits and starts along a myriad different paths, and those paths do not all go in the same direction. One man's Utopia is another's hell. Progress is dynamic, and it arises from conflict and disagreement. When everyone agrees, that is stagnation, just as it is when no-one can agree.
                                I actually have a lot more thoughts on that particular subject, but those are the points that lead to the question "what does better actually mean?". If you want Marduk to be anything other than a beatstick, then that question has to be addressed. If you want Marduk to be an interesting beastick, then give a single answer, and be aware that you a diluting the theme of Progress. If you want Marduk to be a tool that is well suited, out of the box, to allow the players to explore Progress vs Stagnation in a meaningful way, then make that question the heart of what Marduk is. My proposal to give Marduk a variety of factions is a way to convey that question, but it isn't the only way. Marduk as it is is poorly designed to convey that question.
                                At the moment, Marduk's only nod to the chaos and dynamism of Progress is that they are currently disorganized. This feels anemic for something so full of potential.

                                How does Marduk fit into the world, and the players expectations: Like it or not, the WoD has certain expectations associated with it, things that a player wants when they play a WoD game. I don't agree with everything that has been said on that point, here is how I view it:
                                1. Cynicism: At its core, the WoD is cynical. Marduk as written clashes with that. Thing is, I regard this expectation as a flawed attempt to express the WoD's true draw, which I feel to be:
                                2. Mature Storytelling: As in emotionally mature. WoD is a place where players/storytellers can explore complex emotions, questions about morality, ect. A simple Good vs Evil divide is anathema to that. Marduk, as described and from the incomplete draft I've read, fails at this, and I feel that the reason is worth its own bold header.

                                Do not dictate value judgments: You, as a designer, have opinions about what the game is and how it should be played. You design the mechanics and the lore to suggest that. However, it is up to the players, not you, not even the storyteller, to decide what they take away from that.

                                Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
                                But the core question of Leviathan is "you are a monster who should commit suicide. You don't kill yourself, explore why you don't and how you justify that decision".
                                You don't get to dictate that. Not in a system designed to promote mature storytelling. You can design the rules to suggest it, and I think that Leviathan's mechanics do a pretty good job at that. The treatment of Marduk does not. It presents a foregone value judgement on the part of the game designer rather than a question to be asked and explored.


                                I'd say in summary that what we want from Marduk is something more mature and nuanced, something that can be used out of the box to tell interesting stories and acknowledges the complexity of the human condition. I'd suggest drawing on a mix of Princess and Genius. Let them be good people who can't decide or agree which way forward (or even what constitutes "forward") is best.

                                                            A few ways of looking at it:
                                                            100 yards ~= 90 meters, which is roughly about 1 city block (quick lookup gave ~= 80 meters for a city block). If we situate a Leviathan in an apartment complex surrounded by other apartment complexes, each complex taking up 1 block. By in this setup, at Sheol 1, the Leviathan will have significant influence over 1 building, partial influence over 4 buildings and tangential influence over 4 more. At Sheol 2, near complete control over 1 building, significant control over eight buildings and tangential control over 16 buildings. At Sheol 5, pretty much complete influence over something like 80 or 90 buildings.

                                                            Alternately, go by square km and population density:
                                                            100 yards
                                                            Sheol Tijuana Random Small Town
                                                            1 ~1200-1300 people (0.09%) ~40 - 100 people
                                                            2 ~2500-2600 people (0.19%) ~60 - 120 people
                                                            5 ~6000-7000 people (0.45%) ~200 people.
                                                            500 yards
                                                            Sheol Tijuana Random Small Town
                                                            1 ~6000-7000 people (0.45%) ~200 people.
                                                            2 ~12500-12600 people (0.96%) ~340 people
                                                            5 ~31700-31800 people (2.43%) ~900 people.
                                                            Command "Riot":
                                                            A quick look at wikipedia says that the 1992 LA riots led to about 11000 arrests, while the 2001 Cincinnati riots had a little under 1000 arrests.
                                                            So a Sheol 1 Leviathan could definitely make the national news in the US with this in the US, or bog down an assailant in a smaller area.
                                                            Command "Give me five bucks":
                                                            Sheol 1 Leviathan in a big city will walk away with ~$6000, ~$200-500 in a small town.
                                                            Command "Protest X":
                                                            National news, although protest probably won't be as well organized as you might like. The omnipresent eldritch song might also detract from the effectiveness.
                                                            Command "Flush your toilet":
                                                            Serious problems for a major city's water infrastructure. Small town, probably nothing.
                                                            Command "Kill X"
                                                            In a big city, for most X, X is dead, assuming they can't hide from the mob. Small town, serious inconvenience.
                                                            Command "Gather at X"
                                                            In a city, X is swamped like Time Square at new years. Small town, small crowd.

                                                            How would using this in a noisy environment, like say, at a concert, work? That is to ask, could a clever player hide their hand by disguising their song as something else?
                                                            I think it is overpowered as a standard action. At Sheol 1, Depth 1, a Leviathan could paralyze most of an Aircraft Carrier.
