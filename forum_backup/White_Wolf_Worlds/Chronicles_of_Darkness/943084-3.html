    Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
    Again, not there, but in the spirit of what 1E had to say on the subject:

    Healthy Minds do not seek the Divine Fire.
    Excluding, of course, all the sane Alchemists who are apparently in full control of their faculties despite using and containing Pyros.

    Edit: That was a tad too snarky, sorry. Let me try a more reasoned approach.

    Why? Why don't healthy minds seek the divine fire? Does tapping pyros demand madness? Clearly no, as the text admits there are wholly sane alchemists. Do healthy minds have better resorts? Sometimes, but I know enough scientists to say with certainty that if Alchemy worked, reasonable people would pursue it and not for exclusively selfish reasons. And if walking fonts of Alchemical mystery were walking about, dammit, those sane men would absolutely seek them out. To study them, to protect them, and, yes, to dissect them.

    Honestly I'd be happier if all Alchemists were unhinged, if the requirement to wield divine fire be intrinsically tied to desperate obsession. Sometimes the text says as much, then sometimes it contradicts that with things like White Alchemists who Prometheans apparently never meet. It just doesn't add up, and when I can't rationally reason my way to how a set piece came about (such as why all the Alchemists that seek Prometheans are dead bent on tearing them apart) then the whole thing starts to seem absurd.

        Originally posted by atamajakki View Post

        Imagine you're a Promethean, a few years into you Pilgrimage; you understand some things about humanity, you know what sets you apart from it, and your days are spent striving towards becoming it. You come across a human being - a real human being, born into glorious normalcy with a genuine soul - who wants to kill you for the fire inside you. Somehow things reach a point where the two of you talk about things, and when you ask them why they want to steal your flame, they explain that they want to become more, become stronger and smarter and immortal and to surpass the circumstances of their birth.

        I don't know about you, but I would find that infuriating. Here's some jerk who has everything you've ever wanted by no virtue or effort of their own other than a luckier origin than you're own, and they're not happy with it. They want more. They want to leave being human (magnificently human!) for another state of being, and worse, they want to kill you to fuel it.

        You don't need "Pyros drives them hopeless maaaaaaaad!" for that to be an incredible point of ohilosophical contention in a game about the beauty of basic fundamental humanity.
        Keeping in mind that this is coming from the perspective of a human? I'd recognize some "grass is greener" going on. I might well be interested in working together - I'll help you in your research on transcending humanity if you help me understand what it means to be human. But, of course, if he seems intent on killing me, I probably wouldn't trust him with such an arrangement, nor that he'd really have much understanding of humanity if he's so eager to leave it behind. I'd probably pity him for being born with the gift of humanity, but understanding it less than I even do. I'd definitely be afraid of him. Hating him would seem misplaced, though.

                            Originally posted by Darksider View Post
                            "I will see this person as an object as I vivisect them for scientific advancement" is pretty textbook insane. "I will strive to achieve this goal, even to the point of destruction of myself or everything I hold dear" is also textbook insanity. "I care only about my well being to the exclusion of everything and everyone else" is also another example of insanity.
                            By what textbook? Not the DSM 5, that's for sure.

                            If you define horrible acts as insane, then yes, people who commit horrible acts will be insane by that definition. But such a definition ignores the fact that perfectly mentally healthy people can just as easily commit those acts. It ignores the fact that there are legions of mentally ill people who would never dream of committing such acts. It paints the world in terms of good people and madmen, and that's not a healthy attitude to have. I would say that vivisecting people in the name of scientific advancement, self-destruction in pursuit of a goal, and extreme lack of empathy are all awful things that "sane" and "insane" people alike are equally capable of.

                            Originally posted by Darksider View Post
                            These are the viewpoints and attitudes you see in the Alchemists that pursue Prometheans as a means to an end. This is why they are considered insane while alchemists that wisely avoid Prometheans, the ones that know the dangers of interacting with Prometheans, are considered sane. I don't see one group of alchemists as being inherently "eeevil" and the other "bastions of moral goodness". But I sure do see one group as being more conscious of the repercussions associated with their actions and behavior, and that is very much an indicator of one's sanity or lack thereof. I don't see sanity being conflated with morality here the way some others do. If anything there seems to be a large dose of amorality on the alchemists' part.
                            Some Alchemists are wise enough to put their own safety above pursuit of their goals, some are not. Some Alchemists are altruistic enough not to want to kill people in pursuit of their goals (and not to rationalize Prometheans as acceptable to kill because they're "not really people") and others are not. Some Alchemists fall victim to Disquiet and others do not. It would be nice if these were not dichotomous categorizations, and if they were not associated with the Alchemists' Sanity.

                            Originally posted by Darksider View Post
                            Insanity is measured by degrees, it isn't an all or nothing proposition, but the alchemists in the antagonist section seem to be the people who are well on the extreme negative end of that spectrum. These aren't people that would just like to be a little better physically and mentally than other people, these are people who are absolutely driven to cast aside humanity along with the weaknesses they believe it entails, and don't care about how they achieve that end. These antagonist alchemists are like heroin junkies who don't care about how they get their next fix, just that they get it. I've specifically dealt with people like that, they most certainly were not sane.

                            ​So yes, you can count me in the camp of people who are just fine with the antagonist splat not being suitable player options straight out of the core rules. Focus on the Proetheans in the core book about Prometheans, leave playable antagonists for later projects, if ever.
                            Yeah, I get that the Alchemists detailed in the Antagonist section are naturally the ones that are prone to hunting and killing Prometheans - that's what makes them antagonists, and the reason they were able to justify having wordcount dedicated to them that could have gone towards Zeka. In a game about Prometheans striving to become human, human antagonists striving to shed their humanity make compelling additions to the story, where more reasonable Alchemists don't really add much. Indeed, I think the right call was made in that respect. However, I don't think Pendragon is wrong to feel that the commentary about why non-antagonistic Alchemists are unlikely to show up in Promethean games are misplaced. I also don't appreciate the associations between antagonists and "madness".
