                See, the idea that there's no stick to discourage people from being as abusive as possible to feed ignores the fact that not wanting to be an intensely abusive person can be more than enough incentive by itself. The assumption seems to be that every Beast wants to feed as much as possible, and that just isn't true. Many Beasts feed because the alternatives, the Horror feeding on its own and eventual starvation, are worse.

                In fact, I would argue that the mechanics incentivizing more brutal feeding adds far more weight to choosing not to do that. It's easy to resist the temptation to do something nasty when it doesn't further your own self interest, taking the high road when it hurts you is far more interesting. I can see why people would be more comfortable with a more direct mechanical penalty for cruel behavior, but from where I'm standing it would take away from the narrative. How many games have binary morality choices that fall flat because the evil choice doesn't make things any easier, it's just evil for the sake of evil? Being good *should* be the difficult path, otherwise where's the significance in choosing it? There's no meaning in resisting a temptation that isn't tempting, that's just balking at rotten carrots.

                And that's not even considering that Low Satiety comes with some pretty hefty benefits that give you good reason to not want to go above it all that often.

                Of course, for some people the game serves as an unrestrained sadist fantasy. If that's the game they want to play, and it stays confined to the game? I'm glad they have an outlet.

                Really though, every Hunger has options for feeding in ways that deal minimal harm or even benefit the people around them.

                        Low Satiety feeding generally ignores the need to cause significant suffering. At low Satiety, pretty much anything that fits the Hunger works. A Collector that takes stamps can just steal stamps form the post office at low Satiety instead of having to find and steal rare and collectible stamps that are going to impact individuals that own them. A Tyrant can flash a wad of cash to get the best seat in a restaurant to show off how awesome they are and feed off the reactions at this. A Predator just needs to hunt something like taking a .22 and shooting a squirrel since challenge and even being human doesn't matter to one at low Satiety. A low Satiety Nemeses and Ravagers aren't as easy, but they can easily deal in short term sufferings that don't result in lasting trauma. Burning a drunk driver's car while he sleeps is punishment or destruction enough for low Satiety here, but for most people it's still a minor inconvenience via insurance and rentals.

                        If your Beast prefers to hover between Moderate and Low Satiety, they can easily stack on minor bonuses for 6-8 dice, which is more than enough to move from Low to Moderate in roll on average.

                        So one feeding where you're at Low Satiety, but you take extra time (+2 or +4 bonus), perform the act a few times during the scene (+2), it's oriented around your character's preferences (+1), and deals with a current Aspiration (+1, +2 if you fulfill it), you can do something pretty minor and still have a nice feeding roll out of it.

                                Power: It's pretty easy to spin a Hunger for Power into an "I want to be the very best" type of journey, and there's nothing saying the power you get has to be abused or unearned to qualify. Climbing the company ladder could mean backstabbing, but it could also come from merit and long hours. Becoming an MMA champion means punching a lot of people in the face, but they accepted that risk when they entered the ring. Power can also mean the strength to defend those weaker than yourself. One of my all time favorite characters, Drake Thorn, is an Anakim who joined the Summer Court. Changelings have a lot of powerful enemies, so Drake is rarely without an opportunity to demonstrate his considerable martial prowess. Protecting Changelings from Arcadian kidnappers is pretty damn heroic, I'd say.

                                Hoard: This one is kind of weird, because a lot of the examples in the corebook aren't really things most people value. Roadkill and cracked safes are two examples, which lends to the idea that some hoards have value only to the owner. But, even sticking to things other people do value: Grace is an incredibly competitive athlete. She'll try any sport if there's a trophy to be won, and when she can't find trophies she'll make bets. Whatever someone is willing to wager, as long as she can win it fair and square in a competition. If she's starving she's willing to bend the fair and square bit, but for the most part she earned each item in the vast pile of trophies in her lair.

                                Punishment: Pretty easy, and even straight up legal in many cases. The prosecutor who makes sure the guilty are convicted as such, etc. Anybody who works through legal channels and does their job strictly but by the book has plenty of opportunity to feed without causing undue harm.

                                Prey: Straight from the Core "The victim needs to be at the Beast's mercy ...[but] the victim doesn't need to know it." Killing is not required (some Horrors are exceptions but that's the general rule). See the Reaper from my earlier post. Just about every night she deals with people on death's doorstep, but she saves every one she can. It's enough that they know she was the only thing keeping them alive, and if they live more judiciously because of it so much the better

                                Ruin: This was the hardest Hunger for me to paint positively until I remembered how sometimes fire has to purge old growth before new growth can begin Some things need to be broken, either because they're toxic and people won't acknowledge it, or because they need fixing and nobody sees it yet. Ravagers can be the ones to lance the boil and force a painful but rapid healing in place of lingering decay. TJ works with the Dawn Court to promote change and growth. Sometimes when the municipal government is dragging its feet with funding for social programs a select bit of arson or vandalism can convince them there's a problem to be addressed, and TJ's the man for the job.

                                            Heavy Arms You know, it really irritates me the way that you go through my post sentence by sentence instead of as a coherent whole. Each dice bonus on the Satiety roll represents something you actively have to work at. If you don't consider that a mechanical incentive, then that's just something we're going to disagree with.

If you're at 7 Satiety, there's really very little incentive to feed in the first place. It's possible to get three successes on five dice, but difficult. You could also spend a Satiety on the hunt, which drops you down to 6 but doesn't increase the Satiety roll passed 10. If I'm understanding right (I might not be), you can also just do a small petty feeding that murders someone. A Collector with an Aspiration of "get a new watch" killing some rich man over his watch. Provided you have 7 dice, you still roll Satiety. If you disagree on whether you need to be a dick, that's really just a quibble. The description of shock certainly spells out that you have to in some way effect someone, though, and most people would argue that abusing power, stealing, enforcing your own morality, chasing, or destroying are all "being a dick".
                                            Although, as an aside, we're now... took me all day to write the post, 146 posts. If someone hasn't made up their mind by now, nothing anyone says for or against Beast is likely to change that.

Harmony, lopsided as it is, helps underscore the fact that you have competing natures. Beast doesn't really have mechanics that punish you. Satiety is easily managed, and Heroes aren't created by Beasts. People tend to ignore or unintentionally forget non-mechanical drawbacks, like "feeling bad".

                                            That's why it's dissonant. That's the only story you can tell. As written, a Beast is not able to feed "safely". It's not like Vampire where the parasitism metaphor can be stretched to the point that you do very little harm and feed safely from your willing blood dolls. You have to do harm. And since you need to get Potential Satiety over your current Satiety, that often means doing a lot of harm. That you do harm and justify it with "this is for your own good" is why a lot of people see Beast as an abuse metaphor. And it would be nice if you could move beyond that. Consider for a moment "shock":
                                            Originally posted by Feeding, page 108, bolded for emphasis
                                            Beasts feed by satisfying their Hungers. Doing so can be as simple as a Tyrant shoving a victim up against a wall and screaming in his face, or as elaborate as a Ravager slowly dismantling a victim’s life, piece by piece, until he’s either completely ruined or he breaks off contact with the Beast. In either case, the moment of feeding happens when the victim experiences the shock.
                                            The shock happens at the moment where a victim realizes what is happening. If he learns a lesson, the shock is the instant in which it crystalizes. Beasts liken the shock to the moment when one awakens, startled, from a nightmare — a moment that every Beast is well familiar with, having experienced it many times before the Devouring. This comparison is not completely apt, however; a victim can also experience the shock at the moment of death.
                                            Shock is not dissimilar to a breaking point for Integrity (see p. 155) insofar as it impacts the victim’s soul. A shock can be a breaking point, but it doesn’t need to be. The important thing is that the victim’s unconscious response impacts the Primordial Dream, strengthening the Horror.
                                            Now, let's put aside how reasonable a conclusion it is whether or not "lock your doors" constitutes a lesson, as if all Beasts were members of DeadRed Cell, testing out security weaknesses by staging fake terrorist attacks. Shock is a moment of profound realization. Why does this have to come at the result of harm? Why does only negative emotion strengthen the Primordial Dream? Yes, yes, it's the Darkbad Shadowlands of the Temenos, but that's part of the problem. It's kind of arbitrary. Why shouldn't a Beast be able to work with their Horror when they change their outlook and work towards actually doing good in the world? If the Beast can potentially learn positive, helpful traits (even if they really only benefit those they have Kinship with), why can't they alter their Beast to not feed from misery? Maybe an example would be in order.

                                            You're a Beast. You buy into the notion of teaching Lessons. You could try to teach a lesson against assault by assaulting someone. It's big, bad, harmful, gorges the Beast, and says "see how you like when it's done to you". That's more or less what the book encourages you to do. But let's be honest, in the real world if people having bad things done to them ever actually taught them a lesson, we wouldn't live in such a crappy world that isn't a WorldChronicle of Darkness. More often than not, the lesson people get taught is "no one cares about you" or even "this is normal, so it will be okay when you do it". There's a reason abused kids grow up to be abusers. It's because their parents taught through example that abuse is an acceptable response.
                                            But, what if instead of beating someone up or raping them or stealing their shit or abusing your authority over them, you instead make them empathize? What if instead of tormenting them you make them feel for someone else, instead of for themselves. What if you do something like this ad to cause shock? What about making someone realize the gays are just like them? The book would have you teach a bully a Lesson the old fashioned way, bullying them right back and making them to afraid to retaliate, but why can't you feed from the shock of showing them that the people they torment are just like them? I mean, I know the reason; "teach a Lesson" was thrown in at the last minute to appease people because Beast's first draft was received negatively. It's not a bad idea, but by necessity as a last minute addition, it's pretty much tacked on.

                                            Hopefully in a Player's Guide or STG, we will see ways to feed that don't have to directly involve harming people. Even if it was harder to feed this way, it would at least be something working towards that theme of teaching a lesson.
