    I've made the edition. Please look and see if it's everything alright or if something needs tweaking.
    Making a correction on Demons and Cover: Demons can absolutely get Covers from other demons, whether through transfer pacts, bills of sale, or Cover Trading, all of which are detailed in the Demon supplement Interface, on pages 125-127.

    Cover Trading is simply "demons can make pacts with other demons, though they can only provide each other with Cover elements representing whole dots or whole Covers and they can't provide any other benefits they're be able to give to or as a mortal." Transfer pacts are made with one party left unspecified to be signed for later, whereas bills of sale are gadgets (created with Academics) that demons can make to change out their name on a pact for that of the demon who signs the bill (though it can't transfer Cover elements that have already been integrated into a Cover).

    General correction on Dreams: With the exception of mages, hunters, Beasts, and deviants, no published non-human non-mortal splat has soul-based access to the Temenos and instead requires outside help approaching it more as another world to be reached with world-hopping powers; vampires in particular are dead while they sleep and their dreams are not restful. Per the Clade Companion, Deviants' access to the Temenos is starkly limited, typically to Realms relating to the means of their Divergence and their Conviction.


    Re: the new question 8, getting the simple ones out of the way: Hunters, mages, and demons have Virtues and Vices that work like normal humans' do, though demons' Anchors may be unusual in the same way as angels' can and mages may gradually shift their Virtue and/or Vice as their Gnosis increases to reflect their Obsessions and Aspirations (see "Increasing Gnosis," Mage 2e Core p85-86). Any splat with a Virtue can recover all spent Willpower up to twice per chapter by taking meaningful action in accordance with it, while any splat with a Vice can recover a single point of spent Willpower once per scene by acting in accordance with it to fulfill it. All three splats also dream and recover Willpower from restful sleep. Mages also recover Willpower whenever they get an exceptional success on a spellcasting roll (most easily accomplished with their Praxes), may gain an additional Vice through one of the Conditions brought about by Acts of Hubris, and may directly grant Willpower through moderately powerful Mind spells.

    Deviants have Touchstones tied to their Conviction and Loyalty, which provide a single point of Willpower once per scene spent pursuing the drive or a full refill of Willpower once per chapter when risking danger or suffering in pursuit of the drive; the single-point and full-refill methods are mutually incompatible within the same type of Touchstone and which way they're distributed depends on whether the character is a Renegade or a Devoted; Renegades gain single points of Willpower from pursuing their Conviction Touchstones and full refills from taking risks for their Loyalty Touchstones, while Devoted do the reverse. With certain Merits, they may also have limited access to a Virtue and/or Vice (typically needing to succeed on a Resolve + Composure roll to get the Willpower an act would earn; see the Bleeding Heart and Blood on My Hands Merits on p96 of DtR Core).

    Vampires do not recover Willpower from sleep and must instead rely on their Mask, Dirge, and Touchstones (and, in the case of elders, ritualized behaviors called Anachronisms as described in Thousand Years of Night p18). They may also derive Willpower from the Inspired Condition like any other character, and the Predatory Aura's ability to impose Conditions that lower the exceptional success threshold on certain types of rolls without cost when targeting mortals means they are also incentivized to play toward the Monstrous, Seductive, and/or Competitive Beast (likely taking one of the three mutually-exclusive one-dot Merits that gives one application of the Aura 8-Again while removing 10-Again for the other two).

    Mages, Prometheans, changelings, hunters, mummies, Beasts, and deviants all explicitly or implicitly recover Willpower from a full night's sleep (or seven hours of meditation in the case of the Arisen) in the absence of effects that say otherwise, typically notated in their copy of the core system rules under the section describing the acquisition and/or expenditure of Willpower; Promethean does not have the direct statement that the other lines do, but the Vivid Dreams Merit (PtC2e Core, p117) has an effect on Willpower recovery from rest that doesn't make sense to include in a Promethean-exclusive Merit otherwise.

    Werewolf, Geist, and Demon's corebooks do not explicitly state whether or not their characters recover Willpower from restful sleep, but demons in Cover have human bodies and generally dream (in addition to having Virtues and Vices, as described above). Werewolves are alive, are connected to a dimension of runaway vitality, are ludonarratively the most incentivized to participate in the general system that most eats up Willpower for non-specialists (combat), and dream consistently enough that prophetic dreams are the signature power of one of the five main Auspices (and their default Moon Gift's Sacred Hunt-modifying Facet provides an option that prevents the prey from recovering from rest) in addition to being able to recover single points of Willpower when acting impulsively or rationally in line with their Blood or Bone respectively (increasing to full Willpower recovery if they involve or impede the hunt, submit to Death Rage, cause conflict within their packs, etc.) and likewise gaining single points of Willpower from reinforcing their bonds to one of their two physical and spiritual Touchstones (increasing to full refill if the defense of this relationship put the character's life or pack on the line).

    Sin-Eaters can regain one or all of their spent Willpower points from defending their attachment to any of their up-to-three Touchstones (requiring a significant obstacle for one point and serious risk or major cost for the full refill) and from either of their Root and Bloom Anchors (the former from prioritizing the dead over the living, the latter for the opposite; single point once per scene from tending to one community and ignoring the other, full refill once per story from risking your life or status among the opposite community for the corresponding community or protecting one from the other at great risk or cost to yourself), and may also regain one point of Willpower once per scene from participating in an action with their krewe that fulfills its Creed and/or regain all Willpower once per chapter from participating in an action with their krewe that fulfills its Virtue.

                Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                From what you said, it's my understanding that Transfer Pacts and Bills of Sale are "legal" instruments to deal with contracts, but do not alter an already active Cover. Am I right?
                Partially correct; a transfer pact is a normal pact-making option that allows a demon to e.g. make a soul pact and then give the pact to a different demon to call in, and a bill of sale allows a demon to do the same with a pact that they didn't create with that capacity.

                On the other hand, I understood that Cover Trading is a contract signed directly between two Demons, dealing in active Covers. Am I correct?
                Also correct. Limitations on what they can handle aside, they work the same as ordinary pacts.

                And finally, the main point is: as far as I understood, a Demon cannot get the demonic identity itself of another as a Cover. That would be the main meaning of "taking a Demon as Cover".
                Correct, due to the systems for pacts for Cover involving trading pieces of one's mundane life.

                If I correctly understood dreams, Vampires, Werewolves, Changelings and Mummies have "normal" Oneiroi and connect to the Anima Mundi, but not to the Temenos? But the others are as I stated (Mages, Hunters and Deviants have normal, human access to the three strata, while Promethean, Geist and Demon have none, and Beast is Beast)?
                It is a general point of lore that the Temenos is not accessible to most "monstrous" splats through the soul due to their having lost or never possessed humanity; this is why deviants have limited access to it (and narratively have the strained dehumanizing relationships that they do with humans, partially mechanized through their Touchstones) and the Begotten have access as semi-natives in a manner closer to the sideways routes used by other monsters specializing their powersets to do so (e.g. demons with the Rip the Gates Exploit or, in 1e, Prometheans with the Crane Athanor introduced in the Astral Realms book).

                It is unconfirmed whether the above psychic "disconnect" still means monsters with souls have Oneiroi, particularly given the typical restrictions mages face on when and where they can meditate into the Astral (with the primary exception, the Astral Adept Merit, only working through the interior of one's own soul), and deviants essentially need the Astral Travel Variation to enter the Astral realms mentally or bodily (and their own souls are more difficult to navigate the more powerful their Scars are). The fact that the Oneiros typically does not practically exist unless it is being experienced by somebody further complicates matters.

                Prometheans access the Azothic Memory as the collective unconscious of all Created, providing them with Elpis visions and basic knowledge of their condition (with Extempore's one-of-a-kind nature requiring them to join an alchemically branded throng of other Prometheans to use powers that tap into Azothic memory), and it may be that other occult frameworks serving as the repository of culture for a given type of monster similarly disconnects their interiority from conventional systems for Astral access to the Oneiros, as deep supernatural trauma and drastic alterations to one's mindset may preclude navigating and altering a mind through metaphorical actions and magical exercises without specialized kit.

                Most splats dream unless stated otherwise in ways that create a Bastion on the Dreaming Roads, per The Hedge (demons being an explicit exception, though the Demon Storyteller's Guide answers the question of whether they dream at all by the "The Labyrinth" item in its Urban Legends chapter, also establishing that even without a soul, being killed in a dream gives them the Harrowed Condition, which is mechanically identical to Soul-Shocked).

                Finally, thematically speaking, anything with a soul (and several things without one that are nevertheless part of the world) has a connection to the Anima Mundi, though the nature of access to the Astral means this doesn't necessarily imply the ability to get there without supernatural assistance like a Primordial Pathway or Rip the Gates.
