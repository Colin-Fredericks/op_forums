    Werewolf Ethics

    03-09-2014, 05:13 AM
    I was struck by an interesting thought while refreshing myself on the Ridden. They are currently the antagonists in the vampire game I'm running (I know odd, but they want to play around with Blood Tenebrous), and I've just started playing an Ithaeur in a PBP. Well, enough about why the thought happened... lol, onto the idea.

    So, if, as a werewolf, you knew a non-Uratha (Wolf-Blooded, or just any reason for them to be important to the Uratha), would it be ethical to make a deal with a spirit to inhabit the significant person in order to save their life? What would it take for that line to be crossed? Or is it even a line?

    Almost immediately I could see arguments for both sides, and pros/cons.

    Then of course it got more complicated. What if the werewolf in question was one of the Pure?
    Would this be as ethical as making a Fetish? (I've played a Theurge (yes, 3rd edition) that had a huge issue with making fetishes, only a side note, and I've also played a Theurge that was 'Ridden' as well. Creepiest character I've ever played! From these two past perspectives, debating this in my own mind becomes an endless mute point.)

    I've decided if I get the chance I'll explore this concept through role-play. I would also be very amused to see other players' thoughts on this.

    A few of my own thoughts about it:

    Against:
    1) It places the spirit on the wrong side of the gauntlet, and into one of the flock.
    2) It disrupts the order of things, which the Uratha are to keep.
    3) It is fundamentally altering the individual, it would be saving them by making them an abomination.
    For:
    1) With a trusted spirit, and the right type of spirit, the changes will be minimal, and save the individual's life, and even better it.
    2) What if the spirit used is the pack totem? A Wolf-Blooded would be elevated, and the Totem would be far more potent in assisting the pack. Plus loyalty is already a thing.
    3) Not all spirits are bad, and spirits being on the material side of things do happen. How is it any different than making a fetish? So long as they are kept under control through proper spirit deals.

    Of course, none of these are full-proof, and are just simple first thoughts on each side. But I am super curious to see arguments on both sides that I didn't think of.

                  I personally never liked the idea that all spirits always make the world a worse place from a human prospective. It seems that if that were the case, annihilating the shadow realm would be the appropriate response, not tending and cultivating a balanced shadow ecosystem of compatible spirit presences.

                  In the story of Pangea, we find that Father Wolf didn't just put up a wall except as a last resort when his ability to police things was going to be cut short by his death. He demanded that spirits not overstay their time in the material world, but didn't tell them to never show their faces on the wrong side of the border. It seems to me that there are positive things that humans and spirits can get by interacting with one another that would be beneficial for both sides, including some forms of claiming.

                  It'd certainly be frowned upon by the orthodoxy, but that doesn't make it ethcially wrong. You just need to make sure to serve your proper role as mediator and ensure that neither side is getting fucked over in this deal.

                          I personally never liked the idea that all spirits always make the world a worse place from a human prospective. It seems that if that were the case, annihilating the shadow realm would be the appropriate response, not tending and cultivating a balanced shadow ecosystem of compatible spirit presences.
                          According to tribal lore, the Red Wolf, Sagrim-Ur spent his every waking hour asking Father why the rain fell, why the mountain stood, why the wind blew… For every question, Father Wolf’s answer was the same. “They do, and it is good. Nothing more need be known.” But Sagrim was never satisfied with this answer. He asked because he wanted to change things and watch what would happen if he could undo some of the rigid equilibrium that it was Father Wolf’s duty to maintain. He would never have advocated Father Wolf’s death, but neither did he act to stop it when the fateful attack came. Even as Father Wolf lay dying and snarling in impotent rage, Red Wolf stared at him in hidden wonder, imagining how things might be if they were allowed to proceed unchecked. But it was Sagrim himself who was changed when Father Wolf caught him and held him down under one weak paw to speak to him. “Things will not be as they ought,” he told his son. “Note well how they go, and remember what I told you was good.”
                          If the shadow is in balance, the different broods of spirits will police itself like a food chain. Introduce a new predator species and that balance can go out of whack. Picture the shadow like a Darwinian hellish nightmare. Introduce Claimed or Urged, who develop their depredations in the material realm (every spirit is a predator, yes, even a bunny spirit will cannibalize other bunny spirits) and their actions -will- fuck the balance up.

                          Spirits you can "trust" are the ones which benefit to keep the status quo, (or the ones vying to change it into something you'd prefer) and this means normally the Jagglings, which are at the top of the food chain. If things aren't going well for you and it's only a matter of time before you get consumed, some spirits may gamble going into the material realm: where there aren't nearly as many competing predators. Others might chew up on inadequate essence, becoming magath, gaining power at the expense of any sanity or inner balance that the spirit displayed.

                          Even Uratha's totem spirit, from Pack, Lodge or Tribe need to get something out of the relationship (copious amounts of essence, usually). But if you're allying with a race of monsters sworn to keep the balance of the world, it would be pretty stupid to take a leak on that notion when they're bringing the bacon to you. Nothing is free.

                          Most spirits know their place. A combination of discouragement from nearby Uratha patrolling both sides; and/or by the difficulty to cross the Gauntlet (without a specific numen the locus needed to cross the gauntlet has to be of a similar resonance to that of the spirit's; hence why you'll be hard pressed to see, say, a bear spirit of low rank -IE, which only resonates with bears- taking a joy-ride. This usually requires the bear to be Jaggling, and say, has taken say the additional aspects of Endurance).
