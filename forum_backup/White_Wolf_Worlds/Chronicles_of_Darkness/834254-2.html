    Originally posted by milo v3
    Wow, that'd put only 16 changelings for the region my group was going to be in and that region is the third most populated in the state (and since it's australia states are not small).... Can't believe someone would want even less than 1:20 000 considering how small that makes supernatural populations.

    edit: Wait... No 16 Major template supernaturals..... Well there goes werewolves and changelings....
    First, the 1:20.000 is per splat, in average, so all the 10 splats accounted for makes it 1:2.000. So, yeah, it would make for 16 changelings in average for a region with three hundred, twenty thousand people. And sorry but, even if it is big for your state, it isn't that big at all.

    Second, I presented the two numbers (1:2.000 and 1:20.000) precisely because you can play with them. Shrink a splat and you will have space for more of the others. That without stretching the supernatural population.

    Third, supernatural societies in the CoD are meant for metropolitan areas. At least as "complete" social structures. They aren't meant to be like this in smaller regions.

    Fourth, do not forget the Minor templates, unique supernatural creatures and generally aware humans, like blue-book PCs. You should both take into account their numbers in the region and use them in your favor. You don't need every NPC to be a "pure M-Splat".

    Fifth, you can simply ignore all of this for your game without stretching credibility. You should only think of the consequences. A region with 320.000 people and 320 vampires have to account for the consequences of 1 bloodsucking predator for every 1.000 people. This does have consequences. The community should be affected in various ways for so much vampires. And this can be carried over to other supernaturals. Just think how they affect people around them, and extrapolate to a situation of "overcrowding" Changeling population.

                                            Originally posted by ShadowKnight1224 View Post
                                            My eyebally rule of thumb is to have a number of tiers:

                                            Tier 0: Limited number in existence (Mummy).
                                            Tier 1: One per hundred million (Promethean).
                                            Tier 2: One per hundred thousand (Sin-Eater, Demon?, Beast?).
                                            Tier 3: One per ten thousand (Changeling, Mage).
                                            Tier 4: One per five thousand (Vampire, Werewolf).
                                            Tier 5: One per thousand (Hunter, Minor Template Mortal).

                                            Take New York City. The latest census estimated 8,491,079 people. Let's round that to 8.5 million for convenience.

                                            This gives us:

                                            As many Mummies and Prometheans as the ST deems fit.
                                            8 Demons?
                                            85 Sin-Eaters (and Demons? and Beasts?).
                                            850 Changelings and Mages.
                                            1700 Vampires and Werewolves.
                                            8500 Hunters and Minor Template Mortals.

                                            All of this adds up to 11143 people "in the know" about the supernatural, the vast majority of which have some supernatural capability of their own. This is 0.131% of New York's population. If we narrow it down to Major Templates only, the number is reduced to 2643 creatures, or 0.031%.

                                            I am personally quite happy with these numbers.
                                            I'd personally I'd divide most of those numbers by 10, and fill the gap with minor templates. 850 Mages, for instance, seems ridiculously high, as does nearly 2000 Vampires. 8 Demons seems right, but I'd bolster the number of stigmatics considerably. Personally I tend to favor a 1:10 or more big splat to minor version ratio, then toss in a bundle of the splats primary antagonists/plot-devices so they have something to interact with, so that'd look like:

                                            Vampires: ~200 Kindred, and ~2000 combined Ghouls, Revenants and Blood Slaves.
                                            Werewolves: ~100 Uratha, and ~3000 combined Wolf-Blooded, Spirit Touched, and weird kin.
                                            Mages: ~25 Awakened, and ~1000 combined Thaumaturges and Proximi (waaay more Thaumaturges, like 100:1)
                                            Prometheans: ~5 (possibly more since New York is a big hub and many Prommies may see it as a place to learn about humanity), and ~100 or so Alchemists, Pandorans, and other weird Principle things.
                                            Changelings: ~150 Lost, and ~500 combined Fae-Touched, Earth Bound Goblins and Fae (lots more in the hedge)
                                            Sin-Eaters: ~15 Bound (being as their fluff favors Urban Areas), and ~1000 or so psychics, ghosts and abmortals.
                                            Mummy: Maybe one or two, and ~100 or so mummy spawn and weird creatures who's names I don't currently recall. Cultist count may vary considerably.
                                            Demon: ~10 Unchained (again owing to the urban zone), and ~1000 Stigmatics and offspring (skewing stigmatic 100:1)
                                            Beasts: ~25 Begotten (once more owing to the city as a gathering point)

                                            Oh, and since everything with a supernatural ability is a Sleepwalker...somewhere around 5000 of them.

                                            So that'd come to about 500 Major Splats, and 10,000 minor splats and offspring. Which, IMO, makes a pretty huge supernatural population, even for New York. But as others have said, the real answer is however many you feel is right for your story.

                                                    Originally posted by Epimetheus View Post
                                                    Considering salem has about three times that many awakened and apparently that many more. I'd probably make the populations of the major ones about 200-300 larger. I feel like these numbers are a lot lower than they should be.
                                                    So here's the thing about running large populations of Major Splats, it's fucking hard.

                                                    I'll use Mage as an example: Lets say one of the PC characters gets in some trouble and has to do some big, bang-bang spell slinging to get out of it. Makes a huge supernal boom, registers on the unseen senses of all those around, you know, cool climactic stuff. Now lets say the story has ~150 Mages packed into an area like Salem, it'd be fairly reasonable to assume that maybe 10%, no let's make it 5% come to check out what happened (Mages are, after all, a nosey folk). That's 7 characters, big name NPCs with real Awakened power, with obsessions, virtues, interests, opinions and fears to work out. And it won't always be the same seven.

                                                    Now the storyteller could always just handwave it, fade some or most of them into the background as irrelevant to the current story. But I'm not terribly fond of sweeping a dozen borderline demigods under the rug. And as the story and setting grows it gets harder and harder to juggle all those balls, especially if you're going for some big climax. The players will start asking things like "hey, why don't we get those other 15 Arrows to come help us quash this Abyssal incursion" and things like that. Even in little things players are likely to wonder about hitting up random NPCs for help and advice, dragging more and more characters into the plot. Now that's not necessarily bad, but it is difficult to manage as a storyteller.

                                                    Hence I favor smaller populations, ones that I can reasonably manage without lumping 90% of them into "background randos".

                                                    Edit: There's also the character saturation problem: With 150 Mages in one area, even if that's only a small population, it will likely result in the players interacting primarily with other mages. Enough that even if it that is a small drop in the population bucket the impression will be that there are mages bloody everywhere, and they are the only characters of interest. Small populations maintains that feel that the PCs are really special, the 1% of the 1% of the 1%, which I find encourages player agency.
