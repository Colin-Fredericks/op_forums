    And time to review one of the new compacts and our old and loved church hounds!

    Again, full disclosure, the Abbey was my favorite compact so I may be too critical towards SWORN. On the other hand, as I am not American, much of the cultural reference towards SWORN would be lost to me. I do hope I'll like them, but I'll do my best to try and read them from the point of someone who didn't feel included culturally wise, and that it answers their way to connect to the game. Can't promise that I would like them, but I'll try to be as neutral as possible.

    And here we go!

    SWORN- Well, it at least defines the acronym.

    Ok, so some events that I have somewhat heard of but was never very familiar with. At the very least, I would learn something about american history in here.

    Feels somewhat Union like, well as much as the Night Watch felt Union like.

    I must say that it feels much more like the 1e style of writeups, where the compact was very focused around a specific case which founded it. Not that it is a bad thing, of course- especially for a new compact.

    While I would have liked a Keepers of the Weave reference (or even Wardens of Light), but at the very least it describes why there isn't- those traditions were lost with the Relocation. People being stuck out of place and their culture broken apart is indeed a tragedy from which compacts rise. The whole world is in chaos, and candles are being lite to guide them through the darkness.

    I personally think they could have left out the overly detailed cell structure, but it does not harm the writeup or anything.

    While I get the focus around the foundation of the compact, I would have liked it if the writeup would have focused more about who SWORN are beside being a compact for Native Americans- you know, what that differs them from any other cell of Native American hunters.

    I do like the Enemy section, which actually gives some more character to the group. Their division between "natural" and "intelligent", as well as "invasive" and "hybrid" monsters gives some interesting twist towards how they operate their Vigil.

    I think that the main issue with having a cultural specific group in the core is that it somewhat limits who you can play in the organization towards a certain ethnicity. Again, I am not saying that those groups should not exist, but they make more sense to me as things you gain in supplements about specific regions and such. I mean, I can play a Native American who is a part of the Null Mysteriis, for example, but you can't really play a Jew who is a part of SWORN, for example. On the other hand, I am not sure how well you can play a Native American who is a part of the Union, and I am definitely sure you can't play a Muslim who is a part of the Long Night, so what we think as a mainstream is heavily tilted by our own culture. However, there are Christians all around the world while Native Americans are found only in, well, the Americas. But most of the people who fund the Kickstarter seems to be from the US, so... Yeah, there is no right or wrong in this subject, but if it helps some people who love Hunter to feel included then why not?

    Ammm- the Unyielding Hearts Society actually helps SWORN feels more global in terms of focusing less about the circumstances for SWORN's creation and more about how their classifications and more "ecological" view of the Vigil. I think I would have liked it if the writeup were to focus more about that part of the compact and less on the specific death which brought it into existence, like how the Null Mysteriis's talk about the group's founder and focus more on their views and ideologies. A compact which is all about "protecting Native Americans" feels a bit too niche for a core, IMO. A compact which is about preserving the natural order which was lost due colonialism and the lose of ancient traditions is something I could connect to.

    In general the Factions actually enrich the writeup in a few levels, presenting both the more militant and the more open faces of SWORN- and reinforced that no, you are not the good guys.

    Fighting nun art is awesome.

    Yep, the Inquisition is here.

    Seems that the MM now has a strong focus around witches. Interesting.

    Ah- here is the vampire hunting part.

    Now, that's actually really cool! I remember that some people complained about why the MM were given that name when they hunt for vampire instead of witches, and the writeup now gives a very good reason, IMO.

    So traditions versus the need to change. I wonder if that means that the day for them absorbing the Long Night is closer than ever- I mean, if those suspicious bastards would stop thinking that the Catholic Church is the devil :P

    Baudolino is still alive! Great!

    And he still have his dinners with the Lucifuge

    OVerall, I like their Enemy section.

    So the Orders are unofficial? I was sure that those divisions were intended by the organization.

    I'm surprised that there was no tension between the orders as it was presented in C&C.

    So they are still rigid Catholics, Endowments wise- now I wonder how it works with them trying to share knowledge and resources through other religious groups.

    The fact that Benediction requires you a connection to the Pope (meaning, Baudolino) in order to work is an interesting plot hook. I mean, if you are a good Christian, you can just handwave it by saying that God exists. Otherwise- the requirement to be a part of a large infrastructure to preform miracles kinds leads towards the wrong God...

    So it seems they have scrapped the Saints Calendar for a more simple Integrity dependence system- healthy mind leads to healthy spirit. The fact the Theban Sorcery operates in a similar way does rise some red flags- no wonder that Baudolino tells no one about the Lance.

    Yep, the whole thing stinks from Theban Sorcery- but as Benedictions are not related to Vitae (and to be fair, so does Theban Sorcery), what exactly powers it? Heck, what exactly powers Theban Sorcery? The God Machine? The Judges? God Himself? How does Baudolino maintains that ability to grant someone the blessing of Benediction by serving as a divine channel for SOMETHING?

    Again, notice that up until now, every Endowment requires some sort of spiritual metamorphosis to work- the AO need to learn how to transform poison to Elixirs. Cheiron needs monster parts to be transplanted into them. The Council marks with with the sign of Death. The Lucifuge are born this way. And now the MM must be blessed by the Pope and commit themselves to the conspiracy. Gaining Endowment changes something in the Hunter- my personal view is that it serves as a "stabilized Divergence", something which does not crack your soul, but perhaps leaves a scar upon it. That is, every Endowment once started from a Divergence, but if the method becomes stable and reliable enough it turns into Endowments. Again, just my personal theory on the subject- and we are still wondering how Advanced Armory will work with this (they do have that chip inside of them, however).

    Yeah, that's basically Theban Sorcery for mortals.. Damn it, what if the Lance is actually right and there is a God out there in the CofD, who is just not very nice?

    And they can still bring people back to life! Exactly what I wanted from Hunter- to mess up with everything other supernaturals know about the world :P (I wonder what would happen if they would try to revive someone just as they are being Embraced..)

    And again we see the rogue cells are a HUGE problem for conspiracies. I have some very interesting plans for the Order of St Jude, and they tie well with that plot hook about those hunters who hunt for "non righteous hunters"..

    Overall, I really liked the MM's writeup, and I found SWORN more interesting than I expected them to be. Only two more previews to go before we can finally go deep into the next chapters of Hunter!

                            Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post

                            I still do not see the reason why MM is called this way and they still hunt vampires. ( Beside 'they boss is previous ghoul' ) Conspiracy is called 'Hammer on Witches', for Popes sake! ( Vampires relation are still not open to this. ).
                            Because Baudolino used the witch paranoia as a tool to motivate the Church to hunt vampires. His claims were that all the things you think that witches are doing are in fact the actions of vampires, and he utilized the already existing christian hunters which were driven by the fear from witches and directed their actions to something which was more useful. From his point of view, vampires are the main source of evil in the world, and as the voice whispering in the Pope's hear, he constructed the organization to fit his own zeal and hate towards those who abused him.

                            So the MM use their name because when they were founded, they were built upon the witch paranoia, and Baudolino told them that what they think is witches was actually vampires. It is even possible that he didn't knew about witches at the time, and believed that vampires are the actual source of the claims- he knew that vampires were realm, and thought them to be the source of the claims about witches while innocents suffer. IIRC, the Catholic Church actually viewed the belief in witches as heresy, so claiming that someone was a witch could have ended up with your getting the stake. Vampires, on the other hand, were definitely real, and as such it was better to hunt them than some poor women who were just too old or acted weird.

                            In short, the MM doesn't gain its name because it hunts witches, but because it was founded on the fear from witches. That was their concept since 1e, and 2e have reinforced it through the writeup and grounded it with history. In modern nights they are hunting witches and demons, but vampires are still their main target because Baudolino is still the power behind the scenes, and he still hates the damn monsters (not to mention that he need to keep his lifespan somehow)

                                    In Doubting Souls it gets into some of the origins of the Malleus Maleficarum, and it does seem like Baudolino didn't exactly start out corrupt. They originally hunted vampires and demons, but he thought vampires were too canny to get caught by the witch trials, and sought some help for identifying and hunting them.

                                    While it's easy to dismiss Ambrogio Baudolino as a corrupt and cackling evil mastermind, it doesn't really indicate that at all. Looking at the witch trials, and aspects of the church itself and the way nations and ideas shift it would be easy to recognize that if you start a group hunting them in the 1500s, it will be gone or shifted in focus by the 1700s. Keeping one person in charge of a conspiracy is an easy way to give reason for that conspiracy lasting more than the lifespan of that person. And sure he can be an evil bastard who adopts all the puppies in Italy just to kick them, he can just as easily be a dude who made a hard choice because he believes the Malleus Maleficarum is objectively bettering the world. I'm not of the mindset, but anyone who's been in the Vampire forum has likely seen people saying things like 'you could get rid of all vampires and the world would be a better place.' Baudolino may believe the same, and think its worth his soul to make it happen.

                                    My own opinion, I really like the SWORN write-up, but the Malleus write-up is kinda bland, like reading the various MM sections from the books and writing a summary.
