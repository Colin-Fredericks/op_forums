                    The thread is for simple questions.

                    You're asking whether a version of a thing designed as an out-of-context problem in its own gameline that is weaker than any that the book bothers to talk about can be taken as a familiar using a spell that explicitly doesn't work on spirits of the same Rank as the weakest specimens of that thing ever encountered.

                    Insofar as a literalistic reading of the text is concerned, that nobody has encountered an idigam lower than Rank 3 despite there having been idigam on Earth before the return of the Moon-Banished most practically suggests that either they don't exist, they can't survive long enough on Earth to become relevant, they stay out of the way enough that their being a protean blob of Essence doesn't draw attention, or they can't get off the moon at that level of power.

                    Rank 3 is broadly marked as the point in a normal spirit's evolution where it's surpassed the conceptual bounds of being the spirit of a singular instance of the thing it represents; a dog spirit strong enough to join a descant is no longer the spirit of a specific dog, but a spirit of Dogs. Below that level, spiritual society broadly regards Gafflings as children and animals. Idigam aren't naturally the spirits of anything and develop proper consistent spiritual qualities in a process that also increases their Rank and gives them massively disruptive outward control over Essence.

                    Much like how Geist's mechanics for ghosts becoming geists does not strictly forbid a Rank 1 ghost from drinking from the Rivers and somehow surviving to become a Rank 2 geist despite most geists being Rank 3 or higher, the books do not provide anything to support a Rank 2 idigam beyond a lack of explicit forbiddance and so any interaction you come up with for such an edge case is firmly up to you to figure out the broader implications of.

                    You're likewise free to imagine what Rank 7+ idigam can get up to, because an Earthbound or Moon-Banished as strong as a tribal totem is running on plot-device levels of power approximately systematized in Imperial Mysteries, but that has more textual support than a shapeshifting blob of spiritual ephemera with no Influences, no natural capacity to manifest, and the ability to constantly alter its traits being viable as a familiar in a way predictable enough to have answers that aren't tailored to the story this situation was contrived for.

                    Pangaeans in the context they're introduced in are beings you can go out and encounter and are loosely incentivized to seek out. The idigam are a broad category of shapeshifting weirdoes who may most reliably be encountered on the Moon, where most Chronicles of Darkness games are not set. They had their first rules published in a Night Horrors book. They're sufficiently on the edge of consistent mechanics that asking about a never-before-seen subset of them is not going to get much more than speculation for the same reason you're not going to get hard answers on what you can find in the Abyss.

                    If I sound hostile it's because you sound insistent for answers that aren't established. I have provided my take on how one might answer the question. That is all you are going to get for something this far into hypotheticals about an outside-context problem from another gameline. Further answers will need to be come up with in a different thread than the one for asking simple questions about the setting and mechanics, because this is not the Brainstorm About Primordial Ooze-Spirits thread.

                                                    Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
                                                    Do Legacy attainments using Advanced Duration benefit of from the +X die for Clash of Wills?
                                                    They're treated the same as natural phenomena (the specific example being described as as easy to negate as a similar ordinary instance of the same phenomenon) and can be used as frequently as their casting time allows (metered further to "as many times as you have points of Mana" for the ones that have a Mana cost).

                                                    They're narratively and mechanically things you can Just Do, and their reliability comes at the cost of the flexibility and scope available to a spell cast using the same principles; Mage Armor and Greater Utility Attainments fill the role that made it prudent in First Edition to have a Legacy's First Attainment be based on the spells for Mage Armor and Mage Sight, so much of the "defensive" applications of a Clash are gone for Legacy Attainments, particularly when you also retain the ability to replicate the effect as a spell if you need a stronger version.

                                                    Given all of that, in the unlikely event a Legacy Attainment with Advanced Duration conflicts with a supernatural power in a way that isn't bypassed by the Attainment not being a supernatural effect (e.g. attempting to compel a character with Unbound Fate whose Withstand is less than the Potency the Attainment can muster with the non-Duration factors, whether because it's Potency-primary or the target's Withstand is very low), yes, the Duration adds dice as normal.
