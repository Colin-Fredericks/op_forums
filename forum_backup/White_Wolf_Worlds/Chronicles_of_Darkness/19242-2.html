    This story begins with a kind girl. A girl who wanders into the forest, meaning no harm.
    What she doesn't know is that this land belongs to Him, is roamed by the not-humans that he calls 'family.'
    They look like people, but they aren't really, are they? They belong in the world of fang and claw, wing and hoof. And it's not hard at all to reveal their true forms, if one only knows how. (The girl learns this, much to her sorrow.)
    But the Beasts are not cruel. They let her stay with them, working for food and board, if she agrees to protect their secret. And if she is careful to stay away from Him- their patriarch, their master, their god.
    For it is his fault, you see. He cursed all of them, long ago.
    And they will never be free.

    Fruits Basket!

    For some, being taken is a blessing. That is how it started for this young boy, a slave. They saw potential in him, one they could mold to their higher purposes. They trained him, taught him their ways. He did well, and was told great things. He was the chosen one, the one who would bring balance. Soon, he started wanting more, and the dream turned to a nightmare. His masters held him back, lied to him, betrayed him. When the time came for his escape, he did it by force, ridding the world of his former masters, or so he thought. His escape did not come without cost. His body was blackened, and forced into an artificial shell. Now he is the master, and hunts down the remnants of his former master's influence, and discovering they they may not be as banished as he had thought.

        Gather 'round, children, and let me speak of the story of the Grieving Guest, a most caring friend. A special friend, with crows feet for hands and a funny hat, with the grin of a bear. It's his only expression-the Guest's body isn't his real one, it's his way of talking to you, in that lovely little sound by which he gets his proper name He loves you, you see-he so wants to know people, the bits that remind him of his empty home, his broken home. He loves you, because you can break just like his his home does, under stress, how you react to sadness and despair, and how he can live in you, too. That's what he really wants, to be let into that dark place left behind when someone loved is gone, and to curl up there, and never leave-not that he does otherwise. He only leaves when his new house is utterly broken,and then he takes the house with him to add to his own bleak estate.

        He can't leave, now-what was once his house is now his warden. Her little child was too clever for the Guest, too prepared, and his house wasn't taken by the grief like he is. He's stuck-feasting on worms, moldering in her basement, truly homeless. But he lives, and he still craves a home-for if it's a word, or if it's a book...

                        Black Swan?

                        Emmet was just like everyone else. He liked the shows everyone liked, he listened to the popular music everyone listened too, he cheered for the local sorts team like everyone else. Then one day after work at the construction site, he fell into a hole in the building they were demolishing, and suddenly everything changed. Whisked across worlds by bizarre beings who saw things differently than he. Where he had conformity, they had creativity, where he saw objects, they saw parts. When he finally returned to his own world, he was hunted by the same people he used to be like. No longer normal, and not entirely like the others, it was by walking the line between he truly found that he was the greatest, most interesting, and most important person of all times.

                                Deep within the Hedge and into the walls of Arcadia lies a place that even other True Fae speak of with hints of fear. A mansion that is clean and calm on the surface, but hides secrets that could only have been conceived in the worst of nightmares. The Gentry which resides and rules there has an air of a well read gentleman, but should you peer behind the Mask, you will see brief glimpses of shining blades and hear the wet squirming of bloated leeches. This gentleman insists his methods of experimentation are based on science and reason. He is eerily polite, even as he transforms you inch by inch into a monster. He has a whole legion of abominations under his command, but every so often, he chooses someone who he can’t break to wear his Prop. Said “special” subjects are pitted against the legions of monsters under the promise of being granted freedom should they provide satisfactory data. That day almost never comes, however. And night after night, broken bones after spilled guts, these victims make their way through the mansion and back to the real world. The Prop never comes back with them, but some grow a mad fondness for the power the artifact gave them and learn to create copies. And thus, the Splatterhouse grows.

                                (In game terms, the Terror Mask is a Fae Title in Prop form. It belongs to a True Fae with Wyrd 9. Treat someone who is wearing the Mask as a True Fae Actor, with all the powers and weaknesses that come with that, such as spending glamour to heal wounds, dream warping and invoking new Contract clauses on the fly. You can create Hedgespun items, unlike other True Fae. The Computer, Expression and Empathy Skills do not suffer limitations, and your creativity is unaffected. Apply the following characteristics, which overwrite supernatural templates as long as the Mask is being worn. The Mien Blessings may vary. You gain Bloodthirsty as an additional Vice.)

                                Seeming: Ogre

                                Attributes: Power 8, Finesse 6, Resistance 7

                                Bonus Merits: Ambidextrous, Gunslinger, Quick Draw, Disarm, Demolisher 3, Frenzied Assault 5, Hedge Duelsit 5, Heavy Weapons (All Weapons) 5, Two Weapons 4, Danger Sense, Direction Sense, Striking Looks +2, Fast Reflexes 3, Fleet of Foot 3, Iron Stamina 3, Harvest (Emotions 5, Goblin Fruit 5), Lethal Mien, Charmed Life, Armored Mien 1/1

                                Wyrd: 9
                                Contracts: Darkness 6, Stone 6, Separation 3, Moon 5, Artifice 5, Animation 3, Fleeting Autumn 1, Goblin (Shooter's Bargain, Delayed Harm)
                                Max Glamour/Per Turn: 50/10
                                Frailties: determined by the Storyteller

                                Mien Blessings: Improvised Mayhem (Bloodbrute), Obdurate Skin (Stonebones), Weapons (Soldier)

                                Fae Aspects: Immortal Flesh and Ruled By Passion

                                    They say you can never really go home again. That was certainly his case. They came in the night, calling him friend, and in the morning, he went willingly under a contract. But friends they were not. They were haughty, cruel, and obstinate. They would march him to his limit, then continue for hours beyond. Yet horrible as they were, he grew to find loyalty to them. He had opportunities to go back, but could never do it. "I can't leave," he thought, "I've signed a contract."

                                    Eventually, his term of service was over, and their task complete, though not without cost. Some among their number perished, as did his innocence. His body returned, but his mind was ever elsewhere. He kept the tokens of his journey ever close at hand, and longed to hear them summon him once more. To hear them say, "We need our burglar."

                                                        He lurks in the dark corners of a glittering city, where legions of smiling, hollow people act out his gaudy technicolor fantasies. If you're lucky, you'll never see his face, but he knows yours well, and he wants to make you a star. He knows that you have what it takes, if only you didn't have so many things holding you back. He'll take your worries, fears, and regrets, and lock them away in a shiny blue box. From time to time he may take a little more than that, but surely your memories are a small price to pay for a life of glamour.

                                                        Of course, there are those ungrateful souls who refuse to play the roles in which they have been cast. The man in the shadows is nothing if not patient; he sends his loyal marionettes to show these uncooperative few the error of their ways - gently at first, and then with a firmer hand. In the end, however, he knows that even he makes mistakes sometimes; to those who resolutely refuse to follow his direction he at last bestows a key to match their box of troubles, and watches with righteous glee as they destroy themselves.
