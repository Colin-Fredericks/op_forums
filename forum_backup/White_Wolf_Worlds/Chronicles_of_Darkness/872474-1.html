            There was a fictional character who's Vice was described as "He always does the right thing, even when he shouldn't". The character's sense of right and wrong was defined by a written code, with no consideration for mitigating circumstance or degree of sin. For example a thief is a thief, no one cares if he stole bread for his starving children or food from starving children the punishment is the same.
            ​On the other end there is the Robin Hood character who does wrong for the greater good, at least as he sees it.
            Personally I like the idea of Virtue and Vice being two sides of the same coin, it makes it easier for a normally virtuous character to engage his Vice. One of my players used Ambition, on the one hand it represented the drive to work hard and put themselves out to achieve great things. On the other it represented a willingness to engage in questionable acts to advance their agenda.

                    This combination of Virtue and Vice reminded me a lot of Dr. Gears from the SCP Foundation, who is an extremely intelligent and dutiful scientist at the top ranks of the Foundation with an unexplained, almost total lack of any emotions. Except that Gears has a second Virtue of Stoic + the Advanced Virtuous Merit, and his Vice is closer to Callous.
