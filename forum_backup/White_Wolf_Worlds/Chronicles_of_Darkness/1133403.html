    Fighting among Family: Beasts vs. Other Splats

    08-24-2017, 12:07 PM
    Since we have stuff like Vampires vs. Werewolves, I thought it might be interesting to see how and why Beasts could come into conflict with other splats, and how that might play out.
    Of special interest: Which abilities, Lair traits etc. could be utilized to specifically counter their Cousins?

    Werewolves and Beasts seem to be likely to come into conflict because Beasts provide nourishment to Spirits of Fear at the very least, but likely also for others like deceit, pain, murder and betrayal. They are likely to upset the balance, and might even ally with hosts - those are Kin as well, after all. Tyrants and Prey especially might clash with Forsaken and run with Pure like the Predator Kings. Let's for now assume it's a Brood that simply attracts too many negative Spirits, and thus are deemed to have to disappear. So how would Pack vs. Brood come down? In my opinion, there is one almost decisive factor: If the Pack initiates a Hunt and prepares, the Brood will have immense trouble. Uratha are made to hunt and kill, and unless the Brood pulls back into their Lair, will likely fall prey to their cousins. Both have access to different realms, and the Brood might surprise the Forsaken by following them into the Shadow. On the other hand, following the Begotten into their Lair might prove hard for the Forsaken, and an ambush from the Primordeal Realm would be an effective strategy - which loses in effectiveness the more the Forsaken have learned. Their family-sensing abilities might thwart attempts to scout out in human or wolf-form by an incautious Pack, but it is more likely they will at least expend the effort to stay hidden, possibly forcing a Clash of Wills. However, going Garou is a gamechanger, and forces unprepared Begotten to spend Satiety to cause meaningful harm.
    If the Brood gets to start the war, edged on by Hosts maybe, they could expend time on learning more about the Uratha and are likely to make use of silver, however, I'd peck their advantage gain as lower than Wolves that go full on Siskur-Dah.

    Which abilities and traits would help the Begotten survive or even take such a fight, though? In my opinion, one of the most valuable abilities would be the Lair Trait Rotting: reducing their regenerative capabilities is taking away one of the Uratha's greatest boons - especially in Garou. Stench and Thunderous are two additional Traits that would seriously hamper the Forsaken. Thin Air and Heavy seem also useful in disencouraging the use of Dalu, Garou etc. Suffocating or Noxious Gas might help a lot, too, and Slick or Cramped would also be very useful.
    An anti-Werewolf Begotten would probably have Razored Silver in his Lair, but that would be incredibly specific.
    Limb from Limb is an obvious Atavism here, as is Unbreakable and Relentless Hunter. Looming Presence might be of surprisingly good use, not just due to the health increase, but the Rote quality for Intimidation.
    If the Brood is more likely to try and find a trade-off, Alien Allure and Siren's Treacherous Song could find great application. The latter might also help in disrupting a pack's team work a bit, by Deafening them, which might be a nice set-up for an ambush.

    Kindred might feel threatened by a Brood or single Beast's lack of concern for the Masquerade, and decide they need to disappear. The Begottens' existence could also be seen as too upsetting the balance of power, and that does not even take into account that Beasts might clash with Vampires due to their Hunger: Nemesis punishing abuse, Tyrant trying to control the city, but runs against the Kindred's chosen puppet and proposes changes harmful to their plans, the prey reducing their own herds, the collector going for their teeth, the ravager striking fear at the hearts of people and thus attracting the attention of Hunters to the area.
    In a direct confrontation, I'd give the average Kindred a slight advantage due to Blood Buffing themselves, especially combat-specced Kindred like Sworn of the Axe for example. However, In addition, at their chosen game of manipulation, Beasts could pose an unexpected and very real threat thanks to being able to pass as a vampire and attacking from within: a Begotten who manages to get into Elysium or similar could possibly manipulate his surroundings to call down his Lair Traits, which can be far more threatening to Kindred than Uratha. Mimir's Wisdom and Eye of Heaven are powerful tools in Kindreds' favoured realm of conflict, and can provide the Begotten with most essential information, whereas Alien Allure and Siren's Treacherous Song help them fight back: maybe not as good as a Kindred, maybe not good enough to win here, but good enough to cause problems in their plans. Thwarting plans and causing chaos comes almost naturally to Beasts, and though Dominate is more powerful, especially in the long term, You must Obey! and Fear is Infectious make for a horrifying short term combination, especially when aimed at the mortal underlings. And short term is often all you need to take apart a carefully constructed conspiracy. In addition, Beasts don't feel the need for subtelty, and thus are free to go viciously overt in their attacks, which Kindred will (at least initially) try to avoid, and are quite likely to attack during daylight (not even on purpose, simply on accident as they are not bound to the night as Kindred are) - where vampires are most vulnerable (not helpless!).
    On the other hand, Kindred might well use their far reaching webs and construe a narrative that attacks these new terrorists, with a good chance of attracting Hunter or Heroes to do their handy work. Lacking universal access to a different realm might be a decisive advantage Begotten have over their cousins here.

    Dragonbreath is the most obvious Atavism to chose here, but Mimir's Wisdom is quite essential to this fight to better see through the false information Kindred spread, especially when chosing to fight the Vampires on their turf (which I definitly wouldn't recommend, though, despite the seemingly optimistic write-up above). Eye of Heaven is also rather useful here. Looming Presence might be used to trigger a frenzy-response, which, depending on the circumstances, is a good thing. Unbreakable and Relentless Hunter of course are good to have in case of a fight.
    Maybe unsurprisingly, what works well against Uratha is not as helpful at all against Kindred. Suffocating/Noxious Gases and Stench especially are practically worthless, and things like Razored would be less dangerous. The obvious choices here are of course Burning and Blazing Light, which are easily enough to dispatch a room full of Kindred at once.

    One Lair trait you absolutely do not want to have when facing Prometheans is Electrified, but Burning and Dragonbreath would work here, too. Maybe even more effective would be making use of Disquet and social warfare, using Alien Allure/STS to instigate a mob to hunt the Created. As for why the Throng might attack the Brood: Vigilante seeking to stop the violence, Titan looking for a challenge and Quicksilver tagging along to learn more seem like realistic possibilities.

    Demons obviously have the upper hand, seing how they are one of the most powerful splats from creation on, and Beasts better be well prepared to even think about launching an attack.
    That said, the reasons they could clash are manifold: destroying that which you do not understand and which therefore is wrong comes to mind. But also the fact that Beasts pose a great unknown, a unpredictable variable that might prove hazardous to your own ends could drive Unchained to seek conflict. Beasts preying on those they made a deal with could also lead to conflict: either the Demon wants to protect his investment, or the Beast instigated the deal in the first place: free me from these nightmares, and my [x] is yours! Could however, happen on accident: the guy the Beast started hunting a week ago is not a guy anymore, as the Demon came to collect his due. Suddenly, the Demon finds himself under unknown supernatural predation - this could lead to compromising the new Cover! It has to stop! NOW! Finally, it could be simply that the Beast made mistake of preying on someone the Demon holds dear.
    Honestly, the best way the fight could go down is for the Begotten forcing the Demon to go Loud, then run into the Primordeal Dream (preferably by , and let Angels and the G-M handle this. Otherwise, it would be down to clever use of abilities and lair traits, and pure dumb luck.

    Mummys? Open a Primordeal Pathway, and Run.
    Unless it has been around some time, and is greatly reduced. Then dispatch it. Again, and again, and again. Keep an eye out for the cultists, too. Eradicate the cult, if possible (not necessarily killing them all, but destroy the cult as an organization/religious group. Which, honestly, might be harder to do.)

    What's your take on this?
    And yeah, I've left out Mages and Changelings. You tell me what goes down there =P
    Feel free to contradict or elaborate on already-given examples!

                                      If you build a combat monster out of a Rahu Blood Talon and an Anakim Nemesis with 0xp, and focus purely on the physical encounter in a purely white room scenario, you'll still have to question the aspect of effective range, mobility, and DPR, and survivability. Now you know the most effective way to handle a werewolf is silver, but you don't have reliable built in access to silver as a beast. You do, however, have access to Aggravated Damage from two atavisms, Limb from Limb and Dragonfire. The most effective weapon against beasts is Hero's ability to put a bane on you, so the wolf has no access to that. He does, however, have the ability to literally eat the beast for Aggravated damage.

                                      The Beast has access to at least one form of regeneration that can even regen ag, and one form of armor against even ag damage- this time I suggest the latter, to turn werewolf damage into chip damage. Looming Presence, especially in conjunction with Advanced Giant, will help reduce the wolf's attack pool. There werewolves have constant slow regen, and if they are willing to Death Rage, they have access to a boatload of regen.

                                      The real answer to this fight is going to be the beast getting the werewolf to rage early, then CCing him down somehow. And that's where the beasts' last big ability come's in- the Lair. Extreme Heat can force the wolf into War Form to survive, while Engulfing will help keep him snared. To build the Anakim to fight a wolf, I'm suggesting Looming Presence and Dragonfire, to reduce the wolf's ability to hit, and provide yourself some good offense, as well as Extreme Heat and Engulfing. Run Away and You Must Obey are your best starting nightmares here- primarily because they aren't stamina primary, and we've build two deliberately combat focused characters. Tactics: Use the dragonfire to bring the heat, call down the engulfing trait. Looming Presence can give you extra movement speed to stay ahead even if the engulfing fails at some point.

                                      For the Rahu, I'd probably take Slaughterer and Primal Strength myself. (Note: both are Purity Renown). That gives you some good offense, and with Killer Instinct and Warrior's Hide to help bump your hp. Your built in survivability and DPR are pretty good, and your powers and rahu nature bump that up to a solid rating. You have to worry about the Basu-Im timer, so you're best tactic is to mix it up close and personal with Urhan/Urshul, then Garu and eat the Begotten- literally.

                                      If you white room it, I think the Beast has a slight edge here- primarily because he will try to force the Kuruth, and then play keep away with him. Once the Rage ends, the wolf is helpless. Granted, surviving 10 minutes is a long time, but with a lair trait in play, you could lair hop out, then come back when the hard part is over. An Alternative tactic the beast can use is to enhance his grappling capacity and attempt to pin the raging wolf- but that's really not easy, especially since the werewolf wants to be up close and personal.

                                      ***

                                      If I wanted to really work this out, I'd need to build 6 of each splat- a stand alone who is well rounded in all arenas, and 5 'group' members who would fight together and can afford to specialize more. Each of these would be built base at 0xp, then have tiers of say, 15, 30, and 50 xp, where each character is saved in multiple sheets. Each tier, both 1v1 and as a group, would fight 4 matches- One that is a stand up fight, one as hunted, and one as hunter. The final bout would be a purely social manipulation scheme. A lot of work, but with an interesting pay off.
