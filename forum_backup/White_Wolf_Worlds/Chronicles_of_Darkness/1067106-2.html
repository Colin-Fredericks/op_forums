            Originally posted by MCN View Post
            Ah, but the problem is that Mage themes shouldn't ignore the themes of Changeling either. Why should Mage's themes ignore Changeling's? Or should Awakened magic be able to ignore rules more than a Demon's?
            Awakened magic is very specifically themed around ignoring more and more significant rules with greater mastery of the Arcana — again, by Fate 5 you have the ability to make substantially more improbable things happen than the appearance of another hole in the colander on top of being able to twist a loophole into any mystical bargain you can get your head around and having the ability to Clash any attempt to fetter your will.

            "The Hedge keeps hold of the vast majority of the people that find themselves within it unless they have supernatural aid" is both not a theme of Changeling in any meaningful sense and not actually contradicted by "The Hedge can be induced to open a gateway to the mortal world by one of the most powerful forms of supernatural aid available to characters that the system normally bothers to represent with game statistics."

            Demon's "rule-breaking" powers work on weird lateral applications of the principles that angels use, which themselves manage to be applied to material reality by demons without Clashing with the Machine on every other use. It's not a comparable phenomenon to meddling with the volatile fundament of reality itself to temporarily make the improbable happen.

            As I said before, in 1e, no one could open a gateway out of the Hedge. Not changelings, not the Gentry, not the goblins.
            1e had tokens that enabled outright direct travel to or from Arcadia. The primary means of getting them was to trade one from a Stranger.

            1e had Hedge gates that consisted of rituals that worked in very broad swathes of location, as well as trods between Earth and Faerie that were both wide and short.

            1e made a point of specifying both that the Gentry always knew where They were going and that the Wyrd contrived circumstances that enabled Them to play out Their Legends.

            1e and 2e both have made it plain that several goblins and other contents of the Hedge don't leave because they cannot exist outside of it.

            1e's stipulation was that you had to use a gateway to get out of the Hedge and could not simply teleport out with Space alone; this is not a limitation unique to the Hedge, and the spell under discussion does involve using a gateway.

            A mage could, in theory, open a gate, but they'd have to beat a Clash of Wills against the god-level being who's established that Contract in the Hedge, and good luck with that. We have precident for this kind of thing as well (re: altering angels).
            Again: compelling an angel to forsake its mission has consequences that do not exist for allowing the most powerful variety of mage short of an archmaster to affect entry or egress to or from the Hedge. Nothing about being able to get out of the Hedge inherently disrupts a godlike being's intricate ten-thousand-step plan and reforges a lesser divinity into a rogue agent.

            This is to say nothing of how you're interpreting "entering the Hedge is an oath" as "a discrete godlike being swore an oath with the Hedge to make it inescapable and cares if somebody does like fairies do to get around it."


            Consider: a character who can freely open a portal into or out of the Hedge affects the setting by their inclusion if they make use of that ability in a manner that traces back to the larger narrative of a chronicle. What does the freehold do if they hear of multiple accounts of Lost getting out from deep within the Thorns after an encounter with a mysterious figure? What about the motley who takes in a stranger and finds their Hollow door opening to a place none of them have ever been? What about the privateer who witnesses a small household thrown upon the briarwolves' mercy after offending their surprise dinner guest? What about the influx of otherworldly visitors brought to Earth by the tit-for-tat nature of the Hedge interacting strangely with the sudden hiccup in the number of gateways in a region? What about the newly-posted job offer/bounty at the goblin market for a wyrd-twisting willworker as broker/barker/slave? What about the Stranger who's had their interest piqued by the activities of a fate-shaper who isn't protected by the Bulwark? How do you attempt to deal with any of that?

            Masters have access to powers on the lower end of plot-device level, and as mages they tend to conduct themselves with an according level of care or severely disrupt the natural order of their surroundings until they get a hold of themselves or self-destruct in the course of impatience and/or overextension. Their ability to circumvent the normal strictures of the Hedge no more ruins Changeling for their interaction than a Master of Spirit's ability to create a Place-That-Isn't ruins Werewolf crossover.
