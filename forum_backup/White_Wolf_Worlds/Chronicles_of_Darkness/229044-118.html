    Traits: Flooded, Unfathomably Deep, Non-Euclidean.

    Unfathomably Deep: R'lyeh lays at the unexplored and unexplorable Depths of the oceans, where the Pressure is normally lethal too mortals, Those operating without support or a time too adjust will suffer a -3 penalty too any Physical action until they can become accustomed too the pressure (Roll Against Stamina once a Scene while exposed, To grow fully accustomed you would need 10 successes over an extended action) Once Accustomed you may operate without Penalty except for single Turn Transitions.

    Non-Euclidean: Why does that Corner reach that side but... it doesn't travel the distance in between? That Space inside is too Large, And that Edge is too small to contain that door-frame.                         Me, I just realized "physical body of the Dark Mother" is an awesome final boss.

                        She even has her own phases in the form of evolutionary states; her reborn form is initially a puddle of prokaryotic slime (the first life form capable of deliberately preserving its own existence-the first thing to feel fear), and as she devours nightmares from across the world she keeps on increasing in complexity until she stabilizes at the life form most capable of feeling acute and complex fear; humanity.

                        I can't help but see her as a "demon lord to be resurrected by her loyal cultists", existing normally as a creature of living dream and sapient instinct, with the proper rituals allowing her to take physical form and finally eat her fill. Said eating of fill involves driving somewhere between a country and a continent insane from fear, and the birthing of new and powerful monsters into the world.

                        It's nothing personal, honestly, she's just hungry. And she doesn't hate you for trying to stop her; prey fighting for its right to exist is natural, and she can't hate people following their instincts without feeling like a complete hypocrite. Honestly, she respects you; bravery is the ability to face fear after all, and she is fear.
