        My question is about the intentions of some of the suggested numbers. Why are werewolves by far the most numerous of them? Why are Changelings and Unchained second to that, and still by that metric still vastly outnumbering the likes of mages?

        I think perspectives on approximate sizes for populations of supernaturals relative to one another, and to the world at large, should be modelled on things like the kinds of activities they undertake and the sorts of societies attributed to them, as well as what might be expected from their origins. I wouldn't buy mages as one of the smaller populations because they have one of the more elaborate and internationally connected social structures. Nor would I think Demons and Changelings are so numerous, both because it runs counters to their particular themes of living in secrecy even within the occult underground, and undermines the credibility of what they had to overcome to be at large in the world.

        Uratha I actually do think myself of as the most numerous, but not to that extent where it seems as though they'd be crowding one another out a bit and, importantly, where the rate of incidents where any of them rage out of control seems as though it should have a big visible impact.

        20% might sound sufficient because it's a minority, but I think it's worth breaking down just how big of a minority it is. Like, just think about what it would mean if statistically any given visit to something like a shopping mall means you're sharing the space with dozens of supernatural people.

        Conversely, the fact that the overall population is huge means that you can get by with all supernaturals combined constituting a much smaller percentage overall and still have a very large number of people.

                Has this question popped up again?

                I've spent time thinking about this, mostly for fun. It's almost never going to be relevant in a game (unless you're doing something like a Werewolf: the Apocalypse end times scenario), but thinking about rough proportions can help me build settings.

                Like vampires congregate, and have traditionally needed decent numbers to keep up their organisational structures. So I estimate that anywhere important enough to set a Chronicle probably has one vampire per ten thousand people, even if the actual global population is lower by an order of magnitude or more.

                Werewolves? Who knows. There's somewhere between thousands and millions worldwide, and the number fluctuates wildly over the decades. There's a handful of packs around here, but that's all we know.

                With Mages global population is more of a concern, as they can communicate across the world even in ye olde dayes before the telegraph. At which point theoretically reasonable numbers like one in a million start to give you far too many mages. There are likely maybe a few thousand in the world, and people are currently awakening proportionally less than they did but there's still more total mages than before.

                Changelings? Up to hundreds per freehold, and they pop up in the weirdest places.

                Deviants? Nobody knows, but the Renegades in your country probably number in the tens.

                Mummies? Maybe as few as a thousand in the entire world. Today most have been moved to the US or China, but not every Cult wants to risk moving the death god.

                Hunters? Kind of a moot question as someone is always taking up or dropping the Vigil. But one in a million is not a bad guess for the worldwide population.

                Other splats? I don't own those books
