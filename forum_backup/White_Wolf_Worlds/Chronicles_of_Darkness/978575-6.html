        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
        It didn't occurred to me, but indeed. The sharing of blood could be the device behind so many disparate creatures becoming so alike. Specially if you consider that even a single clan in Requiem doesn't have to actualy be a single clan IC. That could make, with passing time, the Cainites turn more alike the Requiem's Kindred.

        But until then, they would have a tough time. Thin-Bloods and Caitiff would certainly look for protection among the Requiem. They are far less concerned with their blood weirdness, and can even consider it an asset. Their society simply have room for those vampires by default. It is a win-win situation for both sides, since the Cainites are bringing information on this new Kindred Clan. I don't think Cainite society will endure much in the long run as a distinct thing, with both Camarilla and Sabbat clueless about the Requiem Ones. Requiem Vampires simply have a far more stable (if insular) society, with more room for adaptation to this crisis, and even to incorporate all the Cainite without any significant change at all in their basic structure. Even the several distinct belief systems of the Cainite vampires (and diverse lineages, even the Laibon) would simply slip in as new Covenants, something the Requiem Society already saw numerous times.

        The same can't be said of other CoD splats. If a conflict ensues between Ascension and Awakening, I would bet on Ascension. It isn't a likely outcome at first (just Tech vs Seers), but if done, they're screwed on Awakening's side. Ascension Magi have, as noted, far more experience with war. They're kind of balanced in power, so this experience would make the difference. Even if Seers and Pentacle and FC ally against this threat, Traditions and Technocracy are as able to do so, as they already do in some circumstances. Both can create their war machines, but Ascension simply already have a lot of them done, a lot more in production, and the blueprints to keep this going on. The Arrow is about conflict, but mostly on a philosophical way. Ascension not only have a lot of warriors, every single one with more than two years as willworker is a guerrilla survivor.

        Werewolf is dangerously close to actual conflict being deflagrated between the "lines", given the nature of the Garou Nation as a whole. And at first, the Nation would have far more power as an organized front. They actualy are one, something the Forsaken lack. To make things worse, the Pure wouldn't have much of a problem allying with the Nation (if not with unanimous consent), whyle the Forsaken can't say the same of the Wyrm (on most cases, at least). The advantage of the Forsaken, though, would came out with time. First, they are better at guerrilla as independent cells, since they work far better without any support above the Pack, whyle the Nation is a cracked monolith. Second, they can muster far easier the help of mortal institutions personaly, most of them being in good terms with the Wolf-Blooded. By the way, 2nd Edition Wolf-Blooded is an awesome asset, since they can convert any mortal supporter into a wolf-blooded with time and exposure. The Nation have to rely on Kinfolk, and I'm not sure if the Kinfolk will not try to change sides in this conflagration. Third, the Forsaken doesn't have any of the infinite number of prejudices of the Nation, so they're not against allying to anyone else in particular as a rule of thumb. They can approach in very ameanable ways to other Fera, less harmfull Weaver-Spirits, and even vampires and other creatures.
        I wouldn't dismiss the Awakened so easily. For one, the Traditions don't really have a reason to pick a fight with the Awakened. In fact, it seems likely they might form an alliance in an "enemy of my enemy way." The Pentacle and the Seers do have common cause to work together though and the Seers have Ochemata backup. While the Technocracy might have an edge, the fight is far from one-sided.
