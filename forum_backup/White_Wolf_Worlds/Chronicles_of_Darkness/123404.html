    In which I read Mortal Remains

    05-01-2014, 05:01 PM
    So I'm going to try my hand at something I've seen around here a couple of times - a lets read. I'm aiming for a chapter every couple of days, and in between that you're welcome to discuss, review my review, make bets on what I'll think based on my "infamous" opinions or whatever.


    Introduction

    The introduction was straight forward and introduced the book. I don't have anything to say about it, good or bad.


    Pieces of Flesh.

    Ah, Promethean. The flawed masterpiece of the WoD. The section begins with a bit on xenophobia, “they are not our people” which is very appropriate as Prometheans are very much the “other” in “fear of the other”. I could probably do a whole post on how Promethians embody this theme, and how it contrasts & compliments Genius' fear of the new, but I shall resist the temptation and press on.

    One thing I noticed is that the xenophobia was tied into alienation and paranoia. At a guess this was an attempt to tie it into core hunter themes, though I'm not sure this was the best choice. Xenophobia can also unite people, sad but true, and in Promethean that's what it so often does. A community affected by disquiet are all on the same “wavelength” - and unity/teamwork are Hunter themes too. Still, this is a small niggle in an otherwise themic and appropriate opening.

    The Enemy: Mad Science and Old Legends

    Next we open up into an overview of what the created are. This bit is functional and solid, it covers how the Created interact with humanity, and why that causes appropriate problems for Hunters to solve. This is followed by a section on common misconceptions that hunters might fall into, which is frankly a great idea. If this was a full length book having a list of common misconceptions would, in my humble opinion, be a bit limiting to the sheer diversity of the Promethean concept. Night Stalkers did include far more than just the Kindred. But with Pieces of Flesh's laser focus on the reanimated corpses of the Created corebook it works great.

    One thing I was very surprised to see is that it says there is less than Created alive at any one time. This seems bizarre, considering that a typical game of created includes 3-5 PCs travelling together and plenty of NPC Created. One of the sample adventures includes 20 Created all in the same place, are we to assume the world's entire Created population is in a single region?

    Now we get to a section on Lineages. This is again straight forward and functional, having already read and played Promethian I'm sure I'm not the target reader for this section so I'll quickly move along.

    Following that we get a selection of different themes for Promethean: Body Horror, Obsession and The Creator. I think this section really suffers from it's limited word-count, themes are a tricky thing and this came across as more of a list than guidance on how to use them. Then again, if they're deftly woven into the later material then it won't matter.

    The three themes presented naturally not a comprehensive list of Created's themes, and I was surprised by the omission of divinity. Not only is it a core Created theme it also an extremely appropriate one to focus on for Hunter – xenophobia was the opening to the whole chapter, how entwined have xenophobia and religion been across history? And how many Compacts/Conspiracies are religious in nature.


    We now come onto mechanics for the Created; which being mechanics I shall skip over. Save to notice that disquiet is significantly rarer, and requires actual interaction or a supernatural or hostile display from the Promethean. Wastelands too. The advice for presenting Disquiet is also damn good – though the advice under “Show don't Tell” doesn't quite match up with the types of disquiet mentioned earlier.

    The response

    Compacts opens with the logical conclusion that the rarity of the Created is a big problem, it also points out that Mary Shelly's Frakenstein is not a howto for monster hunting. The next bit seems a bit less logical – that each organisation will have at least something on the Created, with so few it requires some suspension of disbelief. Requesting we leave our disbelief behind is probably fine, but I can't help but think a larger Created population would work better. The third suggestion I didn't really buy; that is, that it might make more thematic sense for hunters to be wrong about the Created. Mistakes are always in theme, but I can't see why this is more appropraite for the Created rather than, say, the fae.


    Ashwood Abbey – This starts with the implication that a Created is a better human than the Abbey, sensible. The primary theme of this section is that the Created's potential for redemption is a threat to the Abbey's thin justifications. This is a good angle, especially since there's not much room to link the Created to the Abbey's theme of so called “class”. Also – how on Earth did Ogilvy survive the ending fiction?

    The Long Night – The section opens badly, by saying that The Long Night think inhuman monsters like vampires deserve only death, this directly contradicts Hunter core. It's also an odd choice of opening for this section as the distinction, and where Created's potential for humanity places them, is quickly declared irrelevant by the Long Night not knowing of the pilgrimage. I think this section could have been done much better. On the closing fiction – how can the Long Night not believe in Ghosts when they're the most common supernatural creature?

    The Loyalists of Thule – This one I liked. It's a bit straight forward, of course the Loyalists will be cerebral, interested in secrets, and curious about how Promethians are created. But with three paragraphs there's not much room for more than straight forward.

    Network Zero – I feel about this the same as the Loyalists. It's straight forward but with so little word count what else could it be. That said, I never liked the “viral marketing” excuse. I much prefer willing disbelief as Net0's arch-enemy.

    Null Mysteriis – Since this is Promethian (and the word count is expanded) I expected a lot from the Null Mysteriis section; it did not disappoint. It began with a very strong opening “Null Mysteriis has discovered disquiet” (though the Loyalists haven't even read the publications?). The next section goes onto their speculation about the nature of disquiet, they're wrong but in a way that's both plausible and clever. I tip my hat. I particularly like the way Imp was wrong to think so little of O.R.A.S.

    The Union – We have the longest word-count yet this fits the earlier pattern of straight forward and solid. The ending fiction made me think of surfer-dudes. Surfer dude union is a hilarious image that's lightened my evening with giggles.


    Closing thoughts – “Straight forward and solid” summarises my thoughts quite well. Aside from a misstep with The Long Night, these are for the most part exactly what I'd expect from Hunter – Promethian interactions. Network Zero films them, but no one believes them. The Union defend their turf, and only deal with Promethians who make trouble or disquiet.

    Null Mysteriis is probably the most important compact here, given it's thematic ties to Promethian so I'm pleased to say it was the one I liked the most. Honestly I would have liked to see more of what it did – talk about the non-obvious stuff connected to the obvious interactions. I can figure out for myself that Null Mysteriis will study Promethinas, and while I probably could come up with some interesting theories for them, I wouldn't have come up with the ones in this book so I'm glad someone else did.

    Finally. I know I said I wasn't going to talk about the fiction, but I have to say I loved the Promethian's journals. Is it a bad thing if the Promethians are better than the Hunters in a Hunter book? Probably not


    Conspiracies

    Agis Kai Doru – The gist of the this section is that the closet thing to Relics asssociated with the Created are Azothic objects and dormant Pandorans; but that the AKD don't particularly focus on either. This is probably the strongest link you could make given the contents of the Promethian line, but I can't help but feel this is a missed opportunity. Since the AKD aren't too interested in the existing relics, I felt, as a player, a similar disinterest – so why not use the space instead to talk about evocative Demiurge mad mad science relics that you'd get excited to collect for the AKD?

    Orpheus' Eye is a solid Endowment, but apart from it's origin story it has little to do with the Created.

    Ascending Ones – We've had the scientists, now we're onto the Alchemists. Nothing about this captured me quite like the creative look at disquiet under Null Mysteriis; but I did like the merciful angle and their positive view on the new dawn is very fitting. I would have liked more about the Southern Temple and alchemists in general; but I suspect the Knife of Paradise and protection would be easier to fit into a word-count. I would describe this as a good start, that the deeper links could be based upon, which is why I did dislike the explicit mention that their knowledge is limited, I would rather that the knowledge was contained in “advanced texts” that most AO hunters just aren't interested in reading. (It could be read that way, but it wasn't the impression I got)

    Gentle Mind – an anti-disquiet Elixir. I like the concept but I'm not too sold on the price, for such a narrow concept I'd say one dot. Also it only helps against ties, my guesstimate on the Disquiet rolls is that mortals will typically have a better pool, but you roll so often that you have to watch out for the occasional freak roll. Protecting against ties doesn't really fit that paradigm. I'll probably change this to a single auto-success.

    The Cheiron Group – We're now back to straight forward presentations. I agree that TCG is are the ones likely to realise the underlying biologial similarities between Lineages, I wonder if they feel the same about the Unfleshed? The lack of a Cherion are mad scientists angle was surprising though.

    Vitriol Pump – a nifty idea. I was surprised this doesn't cause side effects, but then Cheiron has already used more dangerous stuff than the Divine Fire (like the Infernal fire).

    Lucifuge – I felt underwhelmed by this section. We see one of the rare peeks at the broader gameline, this being the qashmallim-angel / Lucifuge-demon connection which was clearly too tempting to ignore. But we have Lucifuge directly confronted by angels and... they decide to tread carefully and consider the created on a case by case basis? In Created the Qashmallim, even when they weren't actually that impressive, still felt awesome. In the Hunter core the Lucifuge mentions Qashmallim, and it makes it clear that meeting one is a massive deal. Here they seem to be closer to “eh that's interesting.”.

    Gulf of Hades is a neat one. It ties into the Promethian and Mad Science angle well. I'm less fond of the lethal damage it does to humans, it doesn't really fit the theme of draining energy. If it shut down machines, drained Pyros from Promethians and Willpower from Humans; that would be perfect. I'm not sure what I'd prefer in Hunter's mechanics though.

    The Malleus Maleficarum – So once again we have the Inquisition assuming the worst and burning people. No surprises there, I think you could do a lot of interesting stuff with the juxtaposition of “undead monsters” from their point of view with Angels, but alas. Word Count.

    The Peace of St Joseph is a good Benediction, it's a little odd seing a Benediciton with a penalty against Prometheans in the Promethian section but I quite like it.

    Task Force: VALKYRIE – the first sentence opened badly. Not having to do their own legwork, getting orders, to me that's should be the defining difference between a Compact and a Conspiracy. I know the line didn't go that way, but still: The Malleus, Cheiron Group, Ageis Kai Doru and VASCU should get quite a lot of legwork done for them. VASCU often don't hit the field until a sheriff called in with lists of killings, modeus operandi, witness statements, etc. I really like the fact this is talking about TF:V as professionals, not four dudes with big guns doing their own thing, I just dislike the fact it's saying that's unusual and unique.

    The middle bit about Disquiet making it personal is fine but the bit about the Brass not giving proper instructions is just silly. I can accept that TF:V wants Prometheans alive (though what's that “mysterious forces” that is mentioned in a throw away line?) but sending the troops out with no info is a way to loose your men and a extremely rare and valuable opportunity to take a Promethian alive. You'd be far better off giving all the tactical information you know, and trusting your men to obey thir orders. Give them incendiary rounds, normal bullets won't take a Promethean's leg off but fire will. A Promethian can survive without it's legs, it can't run without them.

    Gleipnir Restraints are a neat idea. The maths works out too.


    Wandering Monsters

    I don't really have much to say about these. The three Promethians presented felt like typical examples, they illustrate the game well without pushing it's limits; but when they're responsible for diaries that good, who cares? The exact same applies to the three story hooks, though I was pleased to see ackowladgement that multi-Compact/Conspiracy teams are uncommon.

    I notice that the Sources and Inspiration section does not include The Golem and the Djinn. I've only just started the book, but I can say it really should be on this list.


    Conclusions

    Apart from a few sour notes I felt this was solid, but I think the word-count really constrained it. This section tends towards straight forward portrayals of hunter-Promethian relationships, while omitting some of the richer thematic parallels between the underlying themes (which admittedly need more words): Hunters faith and Promethian's Quashamalim. Mad Science vs Hunter's reactionary streak. The conflict between base urges and higher principles/Principles. Even xenopohbia barely appeared once you got to The Response. The best part though, was clearly the fiction which was basically sublime.

    I remember when Mortal Remains was in early development Matthew asked what we would like to see; one of the suggestions was an organisation of Rabbis. Matt was hesitant to write a group based on Judaism and I encouraged it; having seen this I think he made the right call to omitt it. There's so much you can do with Judaism-Promethians – old to young, tradition to blank slates, promised land to pilgrims, and that's before you get to the Rabbi of Prague. I don't think it would have been be a good idea to do it without enough word-count to do it justice.


    And that's it until next time. Feel free to discuss, flame me, tear apart my attempts at Promethian Hunters while saying stuff about glass houses. Tune in (probably) on Saturday for Changelings.

      All is Lost

      The chapter opens with the theme of paranoia. This is an interesting choice, much like Promethian's xenophobia. It's a theme shared by both the Hunter and the Changeling gameline.

      The reason I say it's an interesting choice is because the better a fae is at hiding among humanity, the less threatening it is to hunters. Fae doing nasty hunter-disaproved things tends to damage their sanity, and often in particularly faeish ways. Obviously most hunters won't know that, but it makes it an unusual choice at least in my opinion. If only because vampires are better at being evil and hidden.

      Following on we have a brief couple of paragraphs about fairy tales; this includes the suggestion that the Lost run by rules of an alien reality which is good; the suggestion that faerie tales are advice on how to survive that reality â€“ I have mixed feelings. Faerie tales were cautionary tales, but for how to survive in our mundane world. It does make sense to assume that in the WoD they really are designed as cautionary tales for the supernatural, though that would (according to the rules in Changeling: the Lost) imply that WoD faerie tales are either really badly designed cautionary tales or completely different to the ones we read. Either way could work well, but it would have benefited from a discussion on the implications.

      Closing the section of is three rules; the final one (don't make deals) I think was actually a mistake. Firstly because the actual rule in faerie tales is usually “don't break deals”, consider The Three Spinners for example. Changeling too follows this theme. Make a deal with a vampire and it will most probably break it's word and suck you dry the moment it feels advantageous to do so. Make a deal with a Changeling and 9/10 times it won't break it. In other words, if your deal with a vampire goes bad you shouldn't have made a deal with a vampire. If your deal with a fae goes bad, you should get a better lawyer.

      The second reason is that deals with the fae are interesting stories. Because they don't break their deals the fae are a great resource for someone who wants something from the supernatural (other than it's hide on a platter). The Hunter who only wants the leprechaun's pot of gold has a lot of precedent, it would be a shame if he wasn't covered in this section.


      The Lost

      As before I shall not dwell much on the section about Changeling: the Lost, it seemed like a competent summery, but targeted at people who – unlike me – haven't played Changeling so I have nothing much to say.


      The Response

      Ashwood Abbey – The general gist is that most faeries aren't impressive enough to hunt, but some are and others make good suppliers. This is good, but the bit about hunting through the Hedge was weird. The hedge is supremely dangerous, and being psychoractive makes it more dangerous for the Abbey than most people. The Abbey aren't known for their sense of restraint, but natural selection should have removed that idea.

      The Long Night – We start with a Christian lens, which is only natural for the Long Night, based around the biblical Nephilim. I'm not the Nephilim in the bible were ever considered evil, but they were fallen angels. As the Long Night believe that the True Fae are trying to corrupt humanity – the more powerful and fae-like a Changeling is, the more corrupt it is. My biggest gripe with this is that The Long Night agree with each other. It's a good theology and I can certainly see a cell of the Long Night preaching it, but I've always understood that every long night Cell had their own spin on the core doctrine and sometimes vastly different interpretations on specific points (Wikipedia's page on faeries lists quite a few Christian interpretations)

      The Loyalists of Thule – The first two I liked, with caveats. This one I don't really like. The summery is that the Loyalists believe that the True Fae are gods (a simplification, but correct) and fear that if provoked they might attack and destroy humanity. Caution is the party line for the loyalists, but being the best academics of the core compacts they should at the very least have figured out that fairy tales are instruction manuals for interacting with the fae, as said in the chapter's opening section, which in turn means that if it was easy to provoke a genocide it would have happened already.

      I'm not saying that this idea is a bad one. Rather, it needs more explanation than we got. Why did they favour this piece of evidence over that and come to this conclusion? I also think that since the PCs are clearly the younger Loyalists mentioned as having defied the order the limited word-count might be better placed on what they think – what theories are popular, etc.

      Network Zero – You know, I had completely forgotten Radio Free Fae existed. I think this is a great way to work with limited word-count, we all know that Network Zero will want to put faeries on youtube, so pointing out these more obscure links between the settings is more useful. I'm a bit surprised Radio Free Fae don't know who Net0 is though, I thought Net0 advertised just about everything but their names.

      Null Mysteriis – And another one I really didn't like. Take this quote “When first hand reports surface, Null sci-
      entists write off the unexplicable,”. This is exactly what Null Mysteriis don't do. That's what separates O.R.A.S. from other scientists. They might be less interested in dream manipulation than hedge gates or an Ogre's super-strength but they would not write it off without testing it.

      The section from the archives even reads like a scientist trying to rationalise away obvious evidence by furiously making up nonsense justifications. Trace drugs do not noticeably affect nearby people. You do not need a control group for hedge gates – control groups are designed to minimise confounding variables. There is are no confounding variables for Hedge gates.

      The Union – This is pretty much what you'd expect. Union cells tend to notice when lots of weird strangers come into their neighbourhoods and if the fae keep quiet they don't really care.

      Aegis Kai Doru – Another one with few surprises. The AKD want the fae's relics, and if a fae can make relics they'd want the supply. The fae are not the AKD's primary enemy but sometimes a hunter confuses them for a Witch or a Werewolf or interprets their vow broadly. I didn't like the bit about “an all-out war between the Conspiracy and a Freehold”. The AKD is a sprawling ancient organisation with access to literal WMDs. Freeholds are small, chaotic, and disorganised. In an all-out war it should be a decisive victory for the AKD; Tier 3 vs Tier 2.

      The Silver Key is a straight forward relic that gives access to the Hedge. I particularly like the way it's explicitly something the AKD has lots of, a few more of those would be a good idea. The whole AKD and the Hedge could have used more expansion though, the Hedge is insanely dangerous, deliberately impossible for humans to navigate. So the AKD would still be enamoured but might have a more nuanced view that would be worth expanding on.

      The Ascending Ones – So how does a cell of Ascending Ones manage to take down a True Fae more than once? They don't mind Changelings keeping to themselves. They don't like True Fae kidnapping. That all makes sense, it's classic Ascending One motives. But what makes them so successful? Apart from that it's solid, covering their views on Changelings, True Fae and the obvious use of Goblin Fruit in Elixirs.

      The Hound Mark is a good Elixir, it's use is duplicated by Red Resin but both work slightly differently. Hound Mark is short range but specific, Red Resin is long range but vague. I would have liked some explanation for why the Fae are easier to detect, the Mask is supposed to be one of the strongest illusions. Mentioning that it's made from goblin fruit (it probably is, but it doesn't say) would do it.

      The Cherion Group – This section is mostly history. So it raises a few questions like how they found the fetches they experimented on, but on the whole it's a sensible history. Also mentioned is that they really want an ability to telepathically communicate and influence people (changeling's dream powers) and that they're getting a supply of hedgebeast and hob bodies from somewhere mysterious. (The story then reveals that it's Goblin Markets, I guessed privateers but both make sense)

      Cranial Cortex Augmentation; so lost brains give you the ability to physically transform into a dream-being? Actually, this is faeries, that makes as much sense as anything else. It's not stated why Cheiron wanted dream powers so badly, but given Field Research and Recruitment shenanigans I'm sure players can think of a good use for this.


      The Lucifuge – I always felt the Lucifuge should generally be better at telling what actually is a demon, but since a True Fae disguised as an angel or demon should have not trouble fooling a hunter, so I'm fine with the idea that the Lucifuge going after “demonic” True Fae. On the whole they're concerned with this world and not the hedge, and consider Changeling’s to not be making too much trouble. But they also believe the True Fae are powerful enough to be cosmologically significant and wonder what's up and the Lady has some vague goal that requires knowing more about the True Fae. All in all, standard Lucifgue.

      Unholy Aura opens with the oddest statement possible. “Better to reign in hell than serve in heaven, and the Lucifuge agree”. Um, no they don't. They hate what they are. Apart from that this is a cool concept, the royalty of hell getting some general social bonuses because, we'll they're royalty. I like it, I don't like the justification for this being extra effective against the Fae but I do like the fact True Fae pay extra attention to anyone using this Castigation.


      Malleus Maleficarum – I think that if you're in the core 12, you need to have support for PCs going up against any kind of monster. While Changelings aren't vampires, there's plenty of angles that could justify it; to start with two of the groups disagree with Baudolino's monomania for Vampires. It also mentions that the Malleus has no position on the fae so it's hunters fall back to whatever they got from their own upbringing. If the MM doesn't have anything why not fall back to the Church, they'll have something due to the church's tendency to syncretize just about everything everything. If you want to play a faerie hunting Irish priest, I think this section needs to do a better job of supporting you.

      Saint Collen’s Clarity is basically an improved version of the True Sight of St Able. It works on both active and passive powers, instead of just passive, and it automatically beats everything. I felt that the True Sight was better balanced, I'm not fond of powers that work uncontested. I'll probably rework this into a power that undoes active deceptions and has no effects on passive ones. That would give both it and the True Sight their own useful niche.

      Task Force: Valkarie – So TFV is basically working with the faeries. TWILIGHT just spy on them and conclude they're no threat to humanity. FORT study their magic, and ADAMANSKI works with radio free fae to keep the fae under cover. This isn't as bad as it sounds, TWILIGHT is right that faeries aren't a threat – and with the ability to supply legitimate ID they have a lot of room at the negotiating table. However it could really benefit from more support for actual combat, what will convince TFV that this particular faerie is a threat and let some PCs break out guns.

      Compound Rounds are a great idea conceptually, I'm actually surprised that they weren't in the corebook. But like Saint Collen's Clarity we have a power that, as written, always works. I'd have preferred it if it only works on monsters vulnerable to one of it's component materials.


      Closing thoughts: Is it possible for me to think that hunters are too nice? Virtually nobody here actually hunts Changelings; now on the one hand I always felt that the “xenophobic burn-em-all hunter” was a stereotype that needs to die. That it sells the diversity and richness of vigil short. At the same time, the diversity of Hunter does include some good-ol' xenophobes.

      Either Clare was too damn nice to have humans shooting at Changelings after what happened in Arcadia or maybe there wasn't word-count to cover both the cooperative side and the antagonistic side; but either way I felt the lack of support for hunter vs changeling quite strongly.


      Wondering Monsters

      Coming up with only three Changelings to illustrate the breadth of Lost is a hard challenge, but the three here do the job. You have the someone who's more like a consequence for faerie tale rules, someone who's more of a normal person, and someone who's a bit more of a player in the occult game with his Keeper hanging over his shoulder.

      It promises that each one can be an ally or an enemy, a welcome move after the last section, and again it delivers. Tensare Custos, (the rules consequence) basically kills people who hurt women. Reading this made me realise something; I know from reading lost that his targets will be picked according to a legalistic criteria so you could have a great story your hunter cell kills a few evil witches and now have this guy bearing down on you; or some other case where by a human (or at least, your) moral code he's the enemy. I don't think that would be obvious from reading this section alone, so that could have been added.

      The three story hooks were also good, providing a nice diversity beyond here's a monster kill it. Though I have to say, how did Conspiracies get so big and so old without knowing about Goblin Markets already?

                  Ghostwalkers

                  We open with an overview of Sin-Eaters and Sin-Eater history. The general gist of it is that Ancient Egypt’s obsession with all things death points to the fact Sin-Eaters were highly influential at the time. Afterwards a cultural change makes Sin-Eaters harder to notice.

                  This is probably wrong - I suspect Ancient Egypt got it’s death obsession from Irem; and being a very long time period full of death studying mystics might have gotten into a self-reinforcing loop (E.G. create Mekhet, oops, better learn more about death to do something about that). Plus, there couldn’t be a unified Sin-Eater culture before modern communication.

                  However it is a plausible mistake for hunters to make. And I can easily see five year old Krewes going “Yeah, we totally stretch back to Ancient Egypt. Praise Anubis the god of the dead!”. So on the whole this was a nice bit of history. It’s the sort of mistake I could see hunters making, sin-eaters making, and sin-eaters pretending to be true because it’s totally pimpin.

                  After we’re done with ancient history we come to recent history and this section is gold. I was pleased that the short WWII fiction fit exactly how I said a WWII Hunter book should go (you’ll notice quite a bit of WWII origin stories in my Hunter work), and basically comes with a lightning tour of cool places to set a Sin-Eater game: Victorian england occultism, World War battlefields, Eastern Europe in the aftermath of the Wars, communist Russia. Seriously, pretty much all of Europe is bound to still be dealing with the ghostly aftermath of the World Wars or Stalin. Geist needs more on the subject.

                  We conclude the history with a brief section saying that in the present Ghost Walkers are rare and the hunters don’t know much about them. This fits the Geist books, but much like the Geist books I always had to ask myself - what stopping Sin-Eaters going on TV?


                  The next section is a long one about the relationships between Sin-Eaters and hunters, with several angels presented. There’s nothing I particularly want to mention, other than I very much enjoyed this section. Obviously it was constrained by space, but it made full use of the words that were there.

Most of the sections here try to draw a link between one aspect of the Compact/Conspiracy with one aspect of the Sin-Eater line; with some more general material on the side.                                           The picture – utterly utterly brilliant.

                                          I'm not really sure if these guys are a Compact or a Conspiracy, I'm leaning towards Compact since I don't think they have that much in the way of numbers or organisation.

                                          I don't really have much to say other than I like them, and can the next group trying to gain magic powers be less evil please.


                                          Story Hooks – These two I wasn't a big fan of, they seemed straight forward and thus didn't grab me.

                                          Wondering Monsters – We only get one Mummy, and not a very good one. The problems are two fold, firstly there's no cult. I don't think you can have a Mummy without a cult, literally, don't Mummies radiate a “cult creation aura” while they're asleep. Even if you could, this is the example Mummy so rare oddities like not having a Cult shouldn't be included.

                                          Secondly is the use of modern gender ideas. Mummies are quite strongly defined by having an identity that's broadly speaking, bronze age. It's entirely possible that a Mummy would be genderless, but it not look anything like modern ideas of genderlessness. I do find it ironic that in a book with chapters for Promethian, Changeling, Geist and Demon – all games which strongly support characters exploring their gender identity, it showed up in the one game where it doesn't fit (at least in a recognisably modern form).


                                          Conclusions – This chapter was one of extremes. What it did right it typically did very right. The Agis Kai Doru was brilliant and most of the other Conspiracies were at least very good. The new organisations were both done very well.

                                          The Compacts on the other hand, I ended up not feeling the idea of portraying them entirely as Storyhooks. I would rather have seen advice on where they'd likely go after they learned about Mummies, or what they'd think about Mummy cults in general written from the perspective that they don't know about the Mummies at the heart. The Malleus pinned itself entirely on a theological issue that under catholic scripture, really should not be an issue.
