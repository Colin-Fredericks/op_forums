That is, again, as I understood it, the point in saying that the person can find a way to access the money.

                            OsuNeko, social Merits aren't so straightforward, either. Nor is Sanctity of Merits.

                            First, imagine this: a character can decide to spend a lot of money going to clubs, bribing club guards, and stuff, just to get used to the club scene and be known enough in the scene. The character effectively changes a dot on Resources for Barfly. The character can donate money in a regular basis to an organization, or otherwise use the money to financially back-up them, effectively changing Resources for Allies. Or put a lot of money into the right hands for information and change Resources for Contacts, or even bribe the way through the meandres of the organization to change Resources for Status.

                            But Sanctity of Merits isn't about this, either way. Changing Merits around is an option that the ST can and should grant only with certain care. Preferably between stories, not just between chapters. Maybe not at all, because this isn't the core of Sanctity of Merits. The core of Sanctity of Merits is that "nothing that happens in the story should reduce the ratings on Merits of the character sheet". It isn't about versatility, but about protection. The player is guaranteed to keep all the points spent. So, if something in story happens to somehow destroy something from the character sheet, the player have the right to be refund on every XP point that Merit was worth to spend anew. The most common thing in the sheet to be destroyed are Merits, and the simplest thing to repurpose the XP are also Merits, and overall is recommended that the ST gives preference for the points being repurposed in the same category. Hence the name Sanctity of Merits.

                                I think I've completely lost track of the discussion here. What are we talking about? Cause it looks like there are two or three completely separate things being discussed at once here and it's causing a lot of confusion.

                                Re: having consistent income and not having disposable income. Yes, this is entirely possible and quite common in real life. There are plenty of people with jobs that provide them enough money to pay their bills and feed themselves and/or their dependents, but little to none left over after that to spend on things they want for themselves. In game terms, this is what having a consistent source of income and no dots in Resources looks like. Remember, Resources doesn't represent any and all money, it specifically represents disposable income. Money you are receiving on a regular basis, in excess of what it costs to keep your basic needs met. Money that you can freely spend on shot guns, rock salt, antique ritual knives, and all the other weird shit characters in Chronicles of Darkness end up needing.

                                Re: Resources vs. Cash. Resources, as mentioned above, represents money you can regularly spend every month on things beyond keeping up with your recurring expenses. Mechanically, this is represented by an ability to acquire a specific Availability worth of equipment and services each month (or each chapter). Cash represents money you have that is not part of your regular income, and can be spent once to acquire a specific Availability worth of equipment and services.

                                Re: Sanctity of Merits. This is specifically something that exists to protect players' experience investments. If you spent 5 Experiences (in other words, roughly 6 game sessions worth of character advancement) to get Resources 5, representing your character's job at a fancy law firm, and then the law firm gets blown up as part of the plot and your character loses their job, you as a player don't lose that 6 weeks worth of experiences. You no longer have those Resources dots, because it doesn't make sense in the narrative to have disppsable income from a job that no longer exists, but you get those 5 Experiences back and can spend them on other Merits. It does not allow players to exchange one Merit for another on a whim.
