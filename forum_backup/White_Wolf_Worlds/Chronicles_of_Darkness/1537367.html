    Welcome [Again] to Atlantis

    06-14-2024, 07:05 PM
    So a while back, I've decided to considered making a Mage fantasy setting which is inspired by the Darksword series by Margaret Weis based around Atlantis. While I it only remained at the pitch level, every once and a while I return to think about the setting, and lately I decided to finally sit down and start working on it- and finally, I finished the introduction and the history section (which wasn't short). As appropriate to the game, I mostly focused on Awakening's elements (mostly from Tome of the Pentacle and Imperial Mysteries, just to make things feel "weird"), but I have also added my own personal homebrew while also adapting concepts from Ascension and other CofD gamelines, such as Vampire, Hunter and Mummy. As such, it won't be a standard take on Mage and may feel somewhat "corssovery", but I do intend to focus on Mage in terms of rules and and themes, while adapting the concepts to fit the system (mechanics are still work in progress). As we have all kinds of changes to the canon and such, I think I should first start with the intro, show the "roadmap" to the project, and then the history section as it is currently written. Feel free to give suggestions and such.

    Introduction
    Welcome to Atlantis.

    A magical utopia, a land of wonder, the land of wizards where magic roams free and unbound. Ruled by sorcerer kings and divine queens, it is the land where humanity triumphed- where it bound its own gods to serve its will, where it transformed the sea, land and sky to reflect their ideal world, where the population thrives with all the wonders that magic can offer. Atlantis is a perfect civilization, founded as a safe place for the Awakened- the willworkers who have escaped the World of Sleep which resides beyond the Abyss to find solace in their bubble of light. Each person has their role to play within this utopia, to maintain its integrity under the watchful eye of the Orders. Atlantis is perfect, without flaw, ideal, as it always was and always will be.

    That’s a lie, of course.

    There wasn’t always Atlantis. The Ancients didn’t simply forge it one bright day after crossing the Abyss. The Watchtowers which shed light upon the proud city states weren’t found by them- they were made, created with struggle against the Old Gods and the original mages of the land which still linger in the shadows of the utopia. The enlightened tyrants which rule Atlantis, the Exarchs, weren’t crowned by the masses out of gratitude- they took their power by force, at the cost of blood and souls. The class system which separates the nobles from the commoners isn’t a part of the “perfect utopia”- it is a tool of oppression, a product of the Nameless Wars which almost tore Atlantis down and were only stopped when the Diamond Orders and the Ministries of the Seers made an alliance in a face of a age of darkness.

    There won’t always be Atlantis. While the Diamond and the Seers share bread and wine in the public, in secret they plot against one another, trying to usurp each other’s power over the multitude of city states. While they claim to rule over the Supernal World, the Nameless Lands are filled with dangerous folk, be them hot headed revolutionaries, exiled and cursed wonderworkers or left handed practitioners of the darkest of magics. While they say that there is no threat within the land for the Old Gods are bound, dark mysteries lurk in the shadows of the city states, waiting to devour careless souls. While they say that they live in a magical utopia, Sleeping Children are constantly being born- only to be stolen away, far from the public’s eyes.

    Atlantis is a perfect- and like all perfect things, it is merely an illusion. It is a dream, maintained through the hard work of both the Seers and the Diamond. It is a smokescreen, a veil, a lie- a practice which keeps the people of Atlantis sedated and happy, unaware of the sweet scent of rotten which lurks beneath the performed surface. It may be a matter of years, months, weeks, perhaps even days, but eventually Atlantis would collapse upon itself, lost forever to the Abyss… unless something would change.

    Welcome to Atlantis.

    Enjoy it while it lasts.

    The Mysteries of Magic- the differences in the magic system due to the more limited Abyssal influence and Sleeper population, including ephemeral entities and how they interact in Atlantis.

    Lay of the Land- the city states which compose the Awakened city and its Watchtowers, the craft cities of the Nameless Lands, the desolation beyond them, the dangerous Ocean Ouroboros which lurks at the edges of Atlantis and the over loom Gate which separates Atlantis from the World of Sleep.

    The Broken Dragon- the Atlantean Orders, which includes the Diamond (composed of the Silver Ladder, the Adamantine Arrow, the Walkers in the Mists and the Masters of the Forge), the Seers (including Ministries and servant races), the Mysterium (which is composed of the Keepers of the Word, the Pancryptiates and the Taftani), the Guardians of the Veil and their factions and the Regalia of Heaven (the noble dynasties of Atlantis)

    The Nameless Lands- describing the powers which rule the lands beyond Atlantis's rule, including the Free Council, the Disparate Alliance, the Elemental Masteries, the Technocratic Union, the Sorcerer Kingdoms of Lemuria, the Roses of Eden, the Celestial Chorus, the Traditions which worship the Arcana themselves and the Keepers of Vedet which broke from the Tremere following their banishment from Atlantis.

    The Banishers- detailing those mages who are dedicated for hunting down other Awakened for variety of reasons, such as the Keepers of the Source, the Dragon Slayers which hunt down the Old Gods, the exiled Shield and Spear, the Promethean Brotherhood which runs out of time and the terrifying Timori.

    The Accursed- presenting the most foulest of the Awakened, which turn to forbidden magics. This includes the Vebrena who serve the Old Gods, the soul devouring Tremere, the mad ravaging of the Bacchanal, the Abyss worshipping Ziggurat, the Circle of Red which united the different infernal cults of the Burning Tree, the secret eating Logophages, the flesh eating Faithful of the Feast, the shadow mages of the Followers of the Dark One, and the death cults of the Euthanatos.

    The Ones Outside- those nations who leave far outside the influence of Atlantis and utilise strange and heretical powers. This includes the Blood Cults of Phobos, the Iremite Order of the Craftmasons, the Healers of Cheiron, the merchants of the Hototogisu League, the ever expanding Deva Infrastructure, the strange Order of the Auric Chalice, the foul Winter's Norns, the poisonous Iltani, the roaming pirates of Leviathan, the dangerous experiments of the Onachus, the followers of the root race ghosts of Thule and the Tenth Choir which aims to kill all divine authority.

    And that's it! Let' start with the History posts, shall we?

      History of the Awakened

      The history of Atlantis is filled with misdirection and misinformation. According to the Atlantean Orders, it is the Eternal City, founded by the Ancients from raw and primordial magic, giving a sanctuary which stands as a bastion of human will. According to the Nameless, the Supernal was full of rich and proud magical traditions long before Atlantis was founded, with the Ancients enforcing their tyrannical rule while eliminating any competition. According to the Banishers, Atlantis is a hubris- an act where humanity claimed more than it should have had, and as such it is a threat to the natural harmony and stability of the world. To the Accursed, Atlantis is nothing but a doomed dream- a falsehood from which humanity will one day be truly Awakened, and only they truly know the true history of magic. Still, behind the layers of agenda and lies, there is a truth about Atlantis’s origin- one which may be discovered by those who dare to patch the differentiated tattered folk stories, censured texts and magically encapsulated memories, though by doing so they are sure to enraged the forces which rule the Awakened utopia.

      The Pre-Atlantean Era
      • Pre Humanity: the Supernal forms from the chaos, the Old Gods are embodied from the raw force of magic, ruling openly over the untamed world, while the Root races build their own early civilizations.
      • The First Crossing: following the whispers of magic in the World of Sleep, the first of the Awakened finds the Supernal World. There, they seek to establish a safe place for themselves, able to practise magic far from the Curse of Quiescence.
      • The First Dragon Wars: faced with the influx of invasive human souls, the Old Gods quickly rise to enslave and bind them to their will. The Wise, however, refuse to fall victim to the hungry deities. A long series of battles starts as the Wise carve a place for themselves in the Supernal, though the Old Gods still maintain their hold over most of the world.
      • The Cults of the Garden start to rise: while most of the Wise see the Old Gods as hostile powers, some are entranced by their mysteries and come to worship them as magic incarnated. While the Old Gods initially view them as nothing more than food and plaything, they slowly grow accustomed to the practice of worship, granting boons to those cults which appease them.
      • The Pact of Wolf and Bull: reaching the conclusion that nothing but further pain and sorrow would come from the conflict, the Wise reach to the less hostile members of the Old Gods and bargain for peace. Known later as the Pact of Wolf and Bull (as named after the most prominent of the Old Gods included in the treaty), the First Dragon War mostly reaches its end, with humans and gods learning to coexist alongside each other.
      • The Breaking of the Dragon Slayers: while most of the Wise and Old Gods accept the terms of the Pact, others on both sides refuse to operate on its conditions. Those Old Gods who go still prey upon human souls and exist far in the wilderness of the Supernal. Among the Wise, however, a group of zealots refuse to rest as long as the Old Gods exist as a threat. Known as the Dragon Slayers, they maintain their vigil outside of the Wise communities, though their numbers remain small due to the hostile environment and dangerous lifestyle.
      • The Tamers System is founded: with peace finally being achieved, the Wise start formulating magic through a direct meditation with the Supernal, seeking a balance between the physical form and the divine soul through personal and environmental harmony. Eventually, they formulate magic through an elemental system, finding enlightenment through elemental balance. As such, the Wise start to initiate into the system, creating the Paths for the Elemental Masteries.
      • Rise of the Verbena: as a side effect of the Pact, more and more of the Wise find it easy to worship the Old Gods, and more of the Old Gods come to enjoy worship. Those give power to the Cults of the Garden, which eventually are organised into the Verbena as a semi-cohesive faith system.
      • The City of Healers is founded: as more and more of the Wise learn to coexist with the Old Gods, one of the Garden Cults reaches out to the Old Gods of the Forest and bargain with them for their secrets of healing. The strange beings agree, and they crawl from their shaded sanctuaries with their flesh crafted creations into the temples built for them by the Verbena. This leads to the foundation of Cheiron, the City of Healers, where flesh is constantly weaved into perfection.
      • The Time of the Thousand Crafts: as each tribe and city state of the Wise lives in a relative isolation and association, they become more and more distinct, developing different practices and belief systems. This leads to an era of magical development and enlightenment, though tension slowly rises between different crafts of magic due to fundamental disagreements between factions.
      • The Period of Many Wars: with tensions between city states growing, the Wise eventually turn upon one another, starting a series of small strifes. Numerous crafts die out during those times, as mages of different traditions brand each other as heretics and threats. While the Tamers attempt to maintain peace, it is fragile and breaks down easily due to factionalism and isolationism. Many of the darker beings of the time, such as rogue gods and the Dragon Slayers, use this time to bring down tribes and destroy their magical legacies.
      • The Call of the Sea starts humming: a strange sound, peaked only by certain individuals, starts filling the Supernal. Those who follow it are led to the edges of the realm, to the shores of the Ocean Oroborus. The Verbena warn against following the sound, claiming that it is related to one of the Old Gods who was banished by the others in the early days of creation, though not all listen to their warnings.
      • Thule Rise to Prominence: while many of the craft cities start infighting, one of them, Thule, starts increasing with power and influence. Born of an alliance between the Wise and the pre-human giant tribes of the Supernal, the Rmoahal, Thule develops into a centre of magical knowledge, learning and trade, turning into a stable point in a tumulus time.
      The First Atlantean Age
      • The Second Crossing: seeking a safe haven from the World of Sleep, a second wave of Awakened refugees escape to the Supernal. They, however, do not use some delicate method, but utilise a forbidden relic, the Celestial Ladder, in order to break through the defences of the Supernal and allow an en masse escape from the terrors of their old world.
      • The Abyss envelops the Supernal: In order to stop whatever horrors which have caused them to escape to follow in their footsteps, the Ancients shatter the Ladder after the passage. This act creates the Abyss, a vast darkness which replaces the old barriers, yet the Abyss itself comes off as corrosive and dangerous. In order to stop it from corrupting the new world, the Ancients create the Gate- a powerful barrier that keeps the Abyss at bay while stopping any potential future Crossings from taking place.
      • The Sea is devoured by darkness: as the Abyss becomes the new barrier between worlds, the Ocean Oroborus is drowned in blackness, and those Wise who followed the Call are devoured by the storm which now isolates the Supernal. However, the Call still draws people towards it, humming beneath the surface of the Abyss.
      • Atlantis is Founded: seeing themselves as superior to the current denizens of the Supernal (both the Wise and the Old Gods), the Ancients decide on creating a utopia which fits their needs- Atlantis, a civilization founded upon magic. Carving it from the backbone of the Supernal’s magic through the creation of the five Watchtowers, the Ancients formulate their society around the symbolism of the Dragon- the Voice of the Dragon to pronounce laws and enforce judgement, the Claw of the Dragon to reap apart the enemies of Atlantis through military might, the Eye of the Dragon to find any signs of disobedience and heresy, the Wings of the Dragon to allow the Awakened to soar towards new height through innovation and invention, the Body of the Dragon to main the archives of the past, the Hoard of the Dragon to protect the precious treasures which were brought from the World of Sleep, the Hunger of the Dragon to hunt down the threat of the Supernal and harness their magic for Atlantis’s use, the Fire of the Dragon to forge new wonders from the Supernal’s raw magic, the Blood of the Dragon to rule over the city state and the Scales of the Dragon to manage the complex bureaucracy and religion which tied Atlantis together, making sure that the social order will be maintained while the commoners would follow the noble Dragon mages.
      • The Second Dragon War: seeing the foundation of Atlantis as an act of hubris which breaks the terms of the treaty, the Old Gods wage war against Atlantis, with the support of the Vebrana. The Atlanteans react with all of their magical might, expanding their sphere of influence and developing new methods to bind or kill the Old Gods, unleashing a new period of violence. The Hunger of the Dragon rose to prominence during that period, due to their capabilities as monster hunters.
      • The Thousand Crafts are Attacked: while not all of the Wise join the war initially, due to the involvement of the Verbena Atlantis comes to view all of the pre-Atlantean mages as a potential threat to its hegemony, unleashing war and conquest upon them. While some of the city states, especially those where the presence of the Tamers is the strongest, manage to maintain their hold, others either fall to the Atlantean armies or simply ally themselves with Atlantis in order to bring down their enemies.
      • The Daoine ally themselves with Atlantis: following the prophecies of their sorcerer kings, the Daoine (one of the most prominent magical traditions in the Supernal), choose to ally itself with Atlantis, believing that it would allow them to not only survive in those tumulus times, but to prosper as well.
      • The Hearts of the Gods are stolen: during the period of the Second Dragon War, the Ancients manage to bring down some of the Old Gods and discover a terrifying truth- that by reaping the hearts of the gods from their bodies, they could harness great magical power, and become like gods by themselves. As such, the Ancients start to massively hunt down the Old Gods, driving them to a defence while the Verbena and their patrons are pushed outside of Atlantis’s sphere of influence.
      • The Healers side with Atlantis: seeing the fallen corpses of the Old Gods as an opportunity for harvest, the Healers of Cheiron bargain for peace with the Atlantean, exchanging information about the Old Gods and their weaknesses as well as their knowledge in fleshcraft. While the Verbena sees them as traitors, the Healers become too powerful to be stopped by them, and the alliance allows Cheiron to prosper, becoming an important trading partner.
      • The Hunger meets the Theban: during their fighting against the Old Gods, few of the elite members of the Hunger of the Dragon are drawn to fight one of the ancient beings. However, instead of slaying the monstrosity they become enslaved by it, their souls eaten and replaced by a constant, yearning hollow. Through magical means they hide the holes in their soul, and return to Atlantis while claiming to have defeated the Old God- while they slowly spread the corruption of their curse within their Order.
      • The First Dragon Slayer Crusade: the city states weakened and many mages harmed by the war, the Dragon Slayers use the opportunity to incite against both the Old Gods and the Atlanteans as one, claiming them as threat to the Supernal. More and more city states join their goal, slaying many of the weakened Old Gods and waging a war against Atlantis, tearing apart the Supernal in their wake.
      • The Culmination Happens: while Atlantis fights at three different fronts due to the joining of the Dragon Slayers to the war, the Dragon Orders manage to hold their own, especially due to the tendency of their enemies to fall upon one another. However, all sides underestimate how far the Dragon Slayers plan on taking the war. By using polluted magic through the hearts of fallen gods, they unleash a wave of distorted magic which poisons both the magic of Atlantis and the Tamers. For a moment, Atlantis’s magical Watchtowers fail, and chaos spreads as magic dies out across the city.
      • The Alliance Written in Wine: seeing the threat that the Dragon Slayers pose to their magical utopia, the Atlanteans and the Wise reach and understand that the Dragon Slayers must be eliminated. By signing a peace treaty, they unite their efforts against the heretics, shattering their alliance and killing their followers, with those few who survive escape to the wilderness to live as barbarian witches.
      • The Nameless Treaty is Signed: with the Supernal still recovering from the damage and the Crusade over, Atlantis, the Tamers and the representatives of the prominent Craft cities sign an official alliance, dividing the Supernal between Atlantis and the pre-Atlantean Wise, forming the so-called “Nameless Lands” as a place outside of the Atlantean sphere for those who desire for it. Many crafts, however, are already absorbed into the structure of Atlantis, and the Tamers are pushed into the more hostile regions of the Supernal. The Verbena do not take part in the treaty, seeing it as a violation of the original Pact, and instead choose to maintain their Garden Cults far from Atlantis, devoted to protecting the surviving gods.
      • Fall of Thule: while many of the craft cities accept the treaty, Thule refuses to do so. Instead, acting out of pride and hubris, it launches a surprise attack against the Atlantean armies. However, while weakened, Atlantis easily withstand the attack and launches a short yet bloody campaign in which all of Thule’s giants are massacred. As no other craft city joins the fray, the conflict ends as easily as it began- with Thule’s remains left as a reminder to Atlantean power, though some remain loyal to its memory.
      • Death of the Wolf: Wolf, one of the great Old Gods which have supported the original Pact, have slowly grown weaker and showed lack of capacity or incentive to maintain the old bargain, and with the signing of the new treaty he showed a complete lack of interest in objecting to it. Seeing him as too weak and corrupted to lead the Old Gods, he is slain by his own children, with the help of his own Verbena cultists. However, with the loss of Wolf as a unifying factor, the surviving Old Gods scatter and the Verbena fractures.
      • The Disparate Alliance is Established: losing many of their members to both the war and Atlantis’s hegemony, the surviving independent craft cities seek to protect themselves from both the dangers of the Supernal and potential attempts to be taken over by Atlantis. As such, they make an economical and political alliance known as the Disparate Alliance, allowing them to support each other while not being dependent on the Tamer Orders.​​

          The Second Atlantean Age
          • Irem is Founded: with many people driven far into the wilderness, a group of refugee mages come upon a sacred site to the Old Gods. Communicating with the sleeping forces in the relics, they come in contact with mysterious beings known as the Judges, who guide them to build a city state dedicated to their glory under the name of Irem. Slowly, a society based around guilds of crafters takes rise, forming the Order of the Craftmasons
          • Rise of the Baalim: seeking to understand the forces which almost brought ruin upon Atlantis, an alliance of both Wise and Atlanteans gather around the ruins of the Culmination in an attempt to learn how to protect themselves from such power in the future. They devote themselves to the research of dark magic, becoming the first of the Baalim.
          • The Hunger grows Corrupted: with their souls hollowed from their bodies, the Hunger of the Dragon starts feeding upon the souls of its own members, presenting it as a part of the “true initiation to the mysteries”. While they manage to maintain the secret under cover, it becomes harder and harder to keep the majority of the members in the shadows, and as such they slowly turn to feast upon commoners and outsiders to Atlantis.
          • Rise of the Ki En Gir: while initially seeking to understand the Culmination in order to control it, the Baalim discover that the knowledge of the magic is corrosive to the mind, and originates from strange beings lurking beyond the Gate. While they maintain their sanity, it becomes a daily struggle. However, more and more mages are drawn to the mysteries of the Baalim and seek to join them, building craft cities around their philosophy, becoming known as the Ki En Gir.
          • The Heiromagus Prophecy is Found: while searching for signs of heresy within Atlantis, the Eye of the Dragon stumbles upon a strange cult dedicated for a “saviour” which is promised to save Atlantis from the “Time of Sleep”. While the cult is dismantled, the Eye keeps the prophecy as a secret, and starts studying it so they could prepare for the dread era.
          • The First Hototogisu League is founded: with the increase in the Ki En Gir’s power, mages in the Nameless Lands drift towards any power which might offer them support. One of those powers is a lone merchant mage who claims to represent a secretive patron known as the Hotototogisu. By trading with secrets and occult artefacts, he ties together numerous craft cities into a network of alliances, brokering peace treaties which turn them into a significant player in the Nameless Lands.
          • The Curse of the Lion’s Heart: with the Ki En Gir growing in power and influence, the Baalim start using their dark magic to force more and more craft cities to join them- or bear the consequences. One of them, Lion’s Heart, refuses the offer, and their sorcerer king is cursed by the Baalim, to have their magic limited and life eaten by the Void Beyond.
          • The Dark Mirror War: with the Ki En Gir becoming a threat over the Atlantean hegemony, Atlantis begins a military campaign meant to dismantle the Baalim. However, the mages of the Ki En Gir became well devised with the dark magic of the Abyss, showing themselves as a challenge to the Awakened City by subverting their magic. The Scales of the Dragon, on their turn, start a propaganda campaign meant to maintain fear within the population from the Baalim and proclaim their magic as heresy, while the Voice of the Dragon negotiates with the Disparate Alliance and the Tamers to stop potential alliance between them and the Ki En Gir.
          • The Daoine show their worth: as the Ki En Gir rely on their seers in order to outmanoeuvre their enemies, Atlantis utilises its own prophets, the Daoine, as part of the war effort. Working closely with the Claw, the two groups manage to gain precious victories against the Baalim, yet do not break the stalemate.
          • The Way of the Wheel is Formulated: with the rise of the Kin En Gir being viewed as one of the first omens for the Time of Sleep, the Eye of the Dragon starts working to keep the prophecy at bay- including ritual murder of those who are believed to be related to the signs of the prophecy. Slowly, the practice develops into a faith based around reincarnation and sin cleansing, with the Heiromagus being the promised soul who would be reborn to save Atlantis from the Time of Sleep.
          • The Stone Pilots Start their Journey: seeing the Culmination as an act which has corrupted the Supernal and puts all magic in danger, the Hoard of the Dragon decides to search after other potential worlds to replace it as the home of the Awakened. Acting secretly, they send a few of their members to the Gate, where they turn their own bodies into stone and send their souls into the Abyss beyond, meant to serve as scouts who would, hopefully, find a safer haven for the Awakened. Many of the stone pilots are driven mad in the process, yet they still ritually return to the Supernal to share their wisdom.
          • The Path of the Burning Tree is Found: seeking for a way to protect themselves from the horrors of the Baalim, many craft cities desperately bargain with the Old Gods for protection, allowing them a measure of protection. One of the Garden Cults, however, specifically that of the Bull, seeks to use their god as a gate for powers that would provide countermeasure to the Abyss’s magic- without the god’s knowing, of course. By using his hearts and using ancient texts about forbidden magics from the World of Sleep, they turn the Bull into a vessel of infernal magic, corrupting their god in the process into a foul being known as Moloch. This allows them to summon entities from below the world who indeed are able to fight against the armies of the Ki En Gir, allowing the Molochite to challenge the empire.
          • The Second Dragon Slayer Crusade: with no Awakened mage showing the capacity to remove the curse from the kin of Lion’s Heart, the desperate people turn to the Dragon Slayers, for they were the ones who caused the Culmination in the first place. Surprising to all, they agree, and use their foul magic to remove the curse. This leads the king to make the Dragon Slayers into his consuls and embrace their faith, while also starting a campaign against the Ki En Gir, which shows partial successes.
          • Irem Starts Expanding: following the will of the Judges, the Order of the Craftmasons starts expanding their influence, initially through trade and later through conquest. As the Judges seem to work towards a great ritual which requires slavery for both toil and as an ingredient, its power over the outer reaches of the Nameless Lands expands at a quick pace, pushing outside the Tamers and the Disparate Alliance.
          • Fall of the Molochite: while the followers of the corrupted Bull are able to fight the Baalim for a while, the constant demands of the infernal entities for sacrifice slowly drain the craft city, and eventually the Ki En Gir destroy them. The knowledge for summoning those entities, however, as well as the hearts of Moloch and the demons themselves, do survive, allowing other infernal beings to be summoned and the Molochite faith to be maintained, opening the Supernal to the so-called “Lower Depths”.
          • The Massacre of Shattered Pillars: with Irem growing in power and becoming a rival to the Ki En Gir, the Craftmasons start negotiating with the Ki En Gir over control over the Nameless Lands. The Baalim, on their end, view Irem as nothing of importance, and murder its emissaries as their answer. This instigates a war, and while the Baalim are sure of their supremacy, they discover that their magic nullifying abilities and occult prophecies fail against the Judges. Pushed between the Dragon Slayers and the armies of Irem, the Ki En Gir collapse and its people are slaughtered by the armies of Irem, their death is used as a sacrifice to the Judges. The site of the Culmination is climbed by the forces of Lion’s Heart, while survivors either blend with the population of local craft cities or escape to Atlantis, hiding their identities.
          • The Peace of Shaddad: wary of Irem’s ambitions of power, Atlantis starts mobilising its forces and patrolling its members, turning the Claw of the Dragon to the largest Dragon Order. Irem, however, reaches out and proclaims that they are not interested in further war. This leads to a peace treaty, a product of negotiation that as long as Atnlantis will not progress into Irem’s territory, they will not cross the Nameless Lands.
          • The Ziggurat forms: with many Ki En Gir refugees finding a safe place in Atlantis, they come under a strict scrutiny by the Dragon Orders for any sign of use of their old magic. While many try to begin a new life, others delve deep into the dark mysteries, creating a loose network of information. While they do not have a solid organisation, the Scales use the propaganda against them in order to paint them as a “ghost threat” known as the Ziggurat- only from the same dark mages to use the same propaganda to find scapegoats for their endeavours.
          • The Shadow Inquisition Begins: with paranoia growing with the public and the spread of the dark magic threatening to fulfil the prophecy, the Eye of the Dragon begins a hidden crusade to hunt down any signs of heresy and use of the Ki En Gir’s foul magic within Atlantis’s bounds, starting a long game of cat and mouse between them and the Ziggurat. Those dark mages who are found are either killed by the Eye in secret as a sacrifice for the Heiromagus or are brought to a trial before the Voice, where they are banished beyond the Gate to the depths of the Abyss.
          • The Hunger Infiltrates the Eye: seeing the Shadow Inquisition as a potential threat to their own secrets, the Hunger of the Dragon starts instilling their own agents within the Eye, stealing their souls in order to keep a close vigil over them. In the process, they learn bits and pieces of the Prophecy of the Heiromagus and the acts that the Order does in its name, yet they decide to keep this information as a leverage.

                                                  The Mysteries of Magic


                                                  Atlantis is the land of the Awakened. Magic burns deep through the land, being imbued in the soul of every newborn. Unbound by the Curse of Sleep and shining bright against the Abyss, the forces which corrode magic in the World of Sleep no longer hold to the same extent in the blessed land. However, while Atlantis is a beacon of magic, it is not to say that it is unharmed- Sleeping Children are being secretly removed from the population of the Awakened city, while the Nameless lands have a small yet growing population of those magic-less individuals. Being attacked rapidly by the followers of the Abyss and other accursed powers, the magic of the Supernal is slowly dying- and it will depend on the Awakened to find a way to save it, one way or another.

                                                  Paths of Magics
                                                  The world of Atlantis is a world of magic- all souls are Awakened by the mysteries in the Supernal (or at least, were, before the birth of Sleeping Children). As such, the people of the Supernal can easily command the forces of magic, and all hold a special association with one of the fundamental powers of creation since birth. Some, however, seek after greater affinity with the fundamental powers, though doing so always comes with the cost of losing affinity with another discipline. Still, in Atlantis proper, going through such initiation is considered as a rite of passage, the Mystery Play, which requires the mage to become a true part of the Awakened society. In the Nameless Lands rules are much more flexible. Still, regardless of place of origin, mages begin their training at a very young age- they need time in order to truly grasp the mysteries, and tests are performed in order to identify which form of magic they find natural- and more importantly, if they are, in truth, Sleeping Children.
                                                  1. Mages of the Supernal are much more trained compared to those of the World of Sleep. They begin with five additional Experience points and three additional Arcane Experience points with which they start character creation.
                                                  1. All mages begin the game with a single Arcana of their choice as their Ruling Arcana and no Inferior Arcana. At the cost of 3 Merit dots (either in the beginning of the game or afterwards), they can complete it into a Path by gaining an additional Ruling Arcana and an Inferior Arcana. The other Ruling Arcana must be of the complimentary type of the original (that is, if the mage starts the game with a Gross Arcana, they need a Subtle Arcana to complete it). The Inferior Arcana needs to be appropriate to one of the two Ruling Arcana in question and can have a rating higher than 2 dots when buying the Path. The choice for Inferior Arcana as determined by the Ruling is as follows:
                                                  Death -> Life

                                                  Fate -> Forces

                                                  Forces -> Death

                                                  Life -> Mind

                                                  Matter -> Spirit

                                                  Mind -> Matter

                                                  Prime -> Space

                                                  Space -> Fate

                                                  Spirit -> Time

                                                  Time -> Prime

                                                  Mages of Atlantis gain this Path at character creation for free if they choose to have one.
                                                  1. Path tools and similar Path related mechanics treat the original Ruling Arcana of the mage as their Path by association- that is, a Fate affinity mage would be treated as Acanthus for the purpose of their Path tools even if they gain Matter as their second Path, and can adopt Acanthus Legacies though no Moros ones.
                                                  1. As Paths are mostly ceremonial and flexible, the culture of the Supernal is defined by the Ruling Arcana of the mage. Use the following list for the common nicknames associated with the Arcana, which serve as the names for the “Path” of the mage in question-
                                                  Death- Necromancer

                                                  Fate- Witch

                                                  Forces- Thaumaturge

                                                  Life- Druid

                                                  Matter- Alchemist

                                                  Mind- Warlock

                                                  Prime- Theurgist

                                                  Space- Conjurer

                                                  Spirit- Shaman

                                                  Time- Diviner

                                                  Paradox in the World of Truth
                                                  Naturally, there is no Paradox in the Supernal- at least, there wasn’t. With the Abyss being kept in bay by the Gate, its touch couldn’t have corrupted magic to the same degree as it does to those mages who live in the World of Sleep. However, with the birth of Sleeping Children and the corruption of magic by the Ziggurat, magic became more vulnerable, which is especially prominent in the Nameless Lands. As such, Paradox rules are modified as follows-
                                                  1. Unless a Sleeper is present within Atlantis proper (which means, within the boundaries of the Pentagram, as defined by the Watchtowers), mages who cast magic within this territory do not need to roll Paradox at all regardless of circumstances. However, when increasing Reach in a manner which would traditionally be paid by Paradox increase, the mage must spend one point of mana per extra Reach instead. As Sleepers are mostly being purged at birth and aren’t allowed to Atlantean territories, it is very unlikely that Paradox will take place there, and generally would end up with mass hysteria and attempt to identify the purge the Sleeper.
                                                  1. Within the Nameless Lands, magic is less stable. Spells which are inured do introduce Paradox dice to the mage who casts them, and with a greater Sleeper population there is a greater risk for them affecting the spell (though many Sleeping Children end up gaining a Sleepwalker status through different methods). Due to the corruption of the ley lines, mages can gain Reach by gaining Paradox rolls (though they may still choose to pay with mana instead), yet they must try and contain the Paradox as the nature of the Supernal rejects the Abyss and attempts to contain the anomaly within its source to stop it from spreading.
                                                  1. In the Outer Reaches, near Ocean Ouroboros and near the Gate, Paradox is rolled normally- the essence of the Supernal grows weak enough due to the corrosive touch of the Abyss, making those worlds much closer to the World of Sleep in terms of how magic behaves.
                                                  Curse of Sleep

                                                  The ban of all magic is the Curse of Sleep- the process through which magic degrades when exposed to the presence of Sleeping souls. Until the Black Moon Tragedy, there were no true Sleepers in the Supernal, making Quiescence into a non-existent phenomenon. However, with the birth of Sleeping Children, while they are a minority which is culled at birth by the Guardians and the Seers within Atlantis, the Curse of Sleep becomes a more and more frequent problem for mages, slowly corrupting the process of magic within them. Still, the Supernal is a world of magic, making it much more resilient to the effect that a few Sleeper souls has on it… for now, at least.
                                                  1. Sleepers in Atlantis might provoke Paradox, depending on how close they are to Atlantis (see above). While they suffer from Integrity breaking points from witnessing obvious magic, they do not forget the existence of magic, as it is an undeniable truth to them. At Integrity 6 and below, Integrity no longer drops because of exposure to magic, yet the roll is still conducted, causing tremendous pain and manifestation of Conditions which are appropriate for their mental, physical and spiritual state. Due to those rules, simply being able to remember the existence of magic does not qualify for making one into a Sleepwalker, with the merit reducing the need for rolling for breaking points.
                                                  1. Sorcerers, gutter witches, psychics, thaumaturges, followers of the Celestial Chorus and similar individuals are not considered as Sleepers though they were born as such. However, not every Sleeper can adopt the different initiations- Supernal Merits are relatively rare, and generally require special initiation rituals, knowledge in the Occult, resilience of the soul and repeated exposure to magic while maintaining high Integrity. Most Sleepers simply do not hold the affinity to any of those paths.
                                                  1. Within Atlantis proper, even if a Sleeper is exposed to magic, Dissonance does not take effect- the light of the Watchtowers is too strong to be extinguished even by all of the Sleepers of the Supernal united.
                                                  1. Within the Nameless Lands and the Outer Reaches, Dissonance can still happen- though it is very much more tamed. Spells are weakened naturally in the presence of Sleepers, yet Supernal Entities and Demesnes are unaffected. Abyssal Entities are still strengthened by it normally.
                                                  1. Near Ocean Ouroboros and near the Gate, Dissonance works as normally.
                                                  Sacred Places and Broken Hearts

                                                  It is said that in the World of Sleep, magic was rare- needed to be drawn from special, hallowed fonts of power. In the Supernal, however, magic is common, and is spread quickly among all of those who were to desire it- at least, that’s how it was before Atlantis was made. Now, Atlantis drains the vast majority of the Supernal’s magic, usings infrastructure made out of the hearts of the Old Gods to do so, leaving the magic of the Supernal depleted and waning.
                                                  1. Atlantis is considered a 5 dot rating Hallow free for any mage within its bonds, though they can only draw mana from it once per day. It is also considered to be a Demesne for all Arcana, though only towards mages belonging to Atlantean traditions.
                                                  1. In the Nameless Lands, magic can be harnessed once per day as if it was a 1 dot Hallow, a benefit open to all mages.
                                                  1. Craft cities of the Nameless Lands are considered to be Demesne, though only to a specific Arcana and only towards mages of the craft city’s tradition.
                                                  1. The dwellings of the Old Gods are considered as both a Verges and a Hallow with a rating equal to the Rank (or equivalent) of the Old God in question.
                                                  1. Soul stones can be used to create Demesne as normal. In case that it is built within an existing one (such as a craft city), the effects do not stack, but do overlap (that is, the craft city would grant the bonus for its ruling Arcana, while the Demesne might grant benefit to another Arcana, giving benefit to both). In Atlantis, however, the benefits do stack.
                                                  1. Hallows still exist across the Supernal, though not inside Atlantis.
                                                  1. Each of the Old Gods has special hearts which can be either given freely to be harvested from their corpses by force, which could in turn be used for building Demesne or creating similar supernatural conductors. They follow the rules for the omphalos stones of the Pangaeans, as described in Dark Eras.
                                                  Forms of the Old Gods

                                                  Long before humanity set a foot in the Supernal, the Old Gods thrived in the primordial lands. Unbound by the bargains of the Wise and the spells of the Ancients, they lived truly within their desire, roaming free and manipulating the fabric of magic to shape the world as they saw fit. After the Second Dragon War, however, the Old Gods were banished from their thrones, being driven to the Outer Reaches unless they somehow managed to bargain for freedom and worship in the Nameless Lands. Still, many seek out the Old Gods even in modern times, recognising that they have a lot to teach the petty mortals who took over their land- all for a price, of course.
                                                  1. The Old Gods is a term referring to the collective of inhuman spirits and horrors which dwell in the Supernal. As such, they do not have a lot in common, though it is important to note that those who use Ephemeral Entities rules are, in the Supernal, naturally materialised and do not suffer from Essence Bleed while in this form (though they can still revert into Twilight if they wish to, including all other Manifestations which they can naturally access) as long as they are found within their appropriate territory or within their dwelling.
                                                  1. The Realms Invisible are still accessible to a variety of degrees, though they all require a Verge of an appropriate Arcana to do so. The dwellings of the Old Gods with a Rank or equivalent of 3 or above are always Verges related to a realm appropriate to their origin (such as spirits leading to a Shadow-like realm, while small gods of death would lead to an Underworld like reality).
                                                  1. The most common of the Old Gods are Supernal Entities. Their dwellings generally do not lead to a different realm, but do create their own “pocket worlds”, known as Emanation Realms. They can still be summoned by mages as detailed under summoning rules. They are generally found within the Supernal region which fits their Arcana (such as Shades being found in Stygia), though all can thrive in Atlantis.
                                                  1. Abyssal Entities are relatively rare in the Supernal- they generally only manifest under rare circumstances in the Nameless Lands and are violently rejected by the Watchtowers of Atlantis. As such, unless summoned by a Scelesti, they will not be found naturally in the Supernal proper. They do have a presence near Ocean Ouroboros and the Gate, however.
                                                  1. The Root Races (see Signs of Sorcery) have a relatively small presence in the Nameless Lands, though they have significant numbers in the Outer Reaches, where they maintain their pre-human civilizations.
                                                  1. The Ananke exist only within Atlantis, though they can under rare circumstances leave the Awakened city to the Nameless Lands. They act as the messengers of the Oracles. The Exarchs, on the other hand, have their own personal Ochemata, which are similarly confined to Atlantis.
                                                  1. The Pangaeans do exist within the Supernal, and are often found in a region appropriate to their Arcana. While they aren’t bound to a certain region, they are commonly found in the Outer Reaches due to the Dragon Wars.
                                                  1. Spirits are mostly found in the Nameless Lands, though many have transferred to Atlantis (sometimes against their will). Their dwellings connect to the Shadow. Magaths are relatively rare in the Supernal, though they do emerge, mostly by rogue spirits who attempt to survive in Atlantis.
                                                  1. The Idigam, the Geryo and the Void Spirits are relatively rare in the Supernal, often dwelling in the Outer Reaches. The Idigam do not suffer from Essence Bleed across the Supernal, while Void Spirits must maintain their presence to Ocean Ouroboros in order to do so.
                                                  1. Ghosts are found all across the Supernal and do not suffer from Essence Bleed. However, Geists and Reapers are often viewed as signs of ill omen and are contained within graveyards. Their dwellings lead to the Underworld.
                                                  1. Kerberoi and Chthonians rarely emerge from their dwellings, but they are present. Kerberoi exist mostly in the Outer Reaches, while the Chthonians exist in the Ocean Ouroboros.
                                                  1. Like in the World of Sleep, Goetia have limited manifestation ability in the Supernal, though they can possess and even Claim individuals. They can materialise in Atlantis without suffering from Essence Bleed. Their dwellings lead to the Astral. Insatiable are also present, yet they are fully material beings who do not emerge from humans but are created spontaneously. Due to their dangerous behaviour, they are often confined to the Outer Reaches.
                                                  1. Shades are present all across the Nameless Lands, though they can’t enter Atlantis. All of the Amkhata in the Supernal are unholy creations, found only in Irem and produced by the Order of the Craftmasons. Fiends exist only in Irem, where their dwellings lead to Duat. Alien horrors, sucvh as the Visitors, are only found in the Outer Reaches and near Ocean Ouroboros.
                                                  1. Hedge Ghosts, hobgoblins, True Fey Titles and Huntsmen are only found in the Great Forest region of the Nameless Lands. Huntsmen can leave the Forest, but can not enter Atlantis.
                                                  1. Eidolons can exist all across the Nameless Lands, yet are not present in Atlantis. Their dwellings lead to the Great Forest.
                                                  1. Infernal Entities manifest all across the Nameless Land, yet are unable to enter either Atlantis or the Gate or Ocean Ouroboros. Their dwellings all lead to Inferno. Wounded Spirits are treated as Infernal Entities for this purpose. Beings who are related to other Lower Depths (such as the Hounds of the Cwn Annwn) act similarly, yet lead to different Lower Depths appropriately.
                                                  1. The strix are not necessarily bound to a specific region, yet are found almost entirely at Phobos.
                                                  1. Angels and Exiled are only found at the Deva Infrastructure. Unchained demons are not bound to specific locations yet there are no Infrastructures outside of Deva. Echoes are not limited by location, yet only form when Angels or Unchained erase a soul through a Soul Bargain.
                                                  1. Qashmalim rarely manifest in the Supernal. When they do, it is generally in response to efforts made by the Celestial Chorus and the Auric Chalice. Djinn have a more constant presence, however, and are often confined to special dwellings in the Nameless Lands and Outer Reaches, which are often controlled by the Chorus or the Auric Chalice. Their dwellings lead to the Empyrean. Valkyries are found in the Outer Reaches, with their dwellings leading to an icy hellscape.
                                                  1. Natural Hallows in the Nameless Lands often have sentience and manifest a guardian spirit of some sort. They use the rules for Sentient Places from Hunter: the Vigil 2e.
                                                  1. Leviathans are treated as Horrors who are found only in Ocean Ouroboros.
