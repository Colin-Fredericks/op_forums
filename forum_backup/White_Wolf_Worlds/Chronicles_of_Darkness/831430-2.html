            It looks great, and I'm eager to see the finished book.

            Changelings definitely received a power bump like vampires and werewolves in their second editions.

            I do have a few questions and comments:

            1. The iron explanation and distinctions definitely still need some work to avoid confusion and arguments, particularly any differentiation from steel if that is the intent. While I understand that most people don't have usable steel swords and similar archaic weapons, items like steel pipes, crowbars, hunting and work knives and axes, shotgun slugs and shot, arrow broadheads, etc. are common, and items like steel chains and police handcuffs that seriously impact changelings inherent escape capabilities are ubiquitous.

            2. If you simultaneously Embrace the Wyrd and scour your Mask, does the use of contracts still benefit from the Exceptional Success bonus?

            3. I believe Reticent is a fairly harsh and long lasting Condition and a little inconsistent with claims that Harvesting doesn't actually harm the subject and is not necessarily predatory.

            4. Does Clarity provide a bonus to resist all magic and supernatural effects or only those that affect a changeling's perception?

            5. For Kenning. what constitutes the "presence of magic" in an area? Is this like Mage 2e's Peripheral Mage Sight which only detects 'active' supernatural effects like the use of Vampire Disciplines, Werewolf Gifts, and Mage spells, or does Kenning also automatically attempt to detect the mere presence of anything supernatural in nature or origin like the mere presence of a vampire or werewolf? If the latter, are mages without active spells considered inherently supernatural?

            6. The Character Creation spoiler twice lists Experience costs for Morality. Is this a placeholder for Clarity?

                        Copied/reworked from my comments, just so it's here, and a bit more:
                        • I think Dreamwalking should use Dream Form rules like other gamelines it's been mentioned for, at least when mentioning non-changelings. A universal baseline would be nice for crossover, or even just for being better able to transfer between games. As it is, it seems very, very difficult to handle a situation where both are involved.
                        • Character creation seems all good, except the template document mentions a free specialty but the character creation doesn’t mention that at all, unless I missed it?
                        • Really like that you built into the core that other supernatural senses can beat the mask by default, rather than using the somewhat difficult mask piercing system from 1e. Mask strengthening does imply that only fae can use the clash of wills, though, is that intentional?
                        • I love the mechanics for scouring away the Mask and having that enhance your fae side a bit in the form of contract bonuses, as well as causing you some hardships with gates and implementing the Bewitched condition (which I also love). I do think the distance on the drawbacks is a little high though.
                        • The scar bit is a nifty, flavorful tidbit, I like that.
                        • I like that cold iron has more of a bite on Changelings now, doing agg to them in addition to true fae.
                        • Caging a Dream is awesome, simply put, and well made to not be an instant I Win.
                        • Embracing the Wyrd seems a little potent, but it’s hard to say for sure since it does have a drawback and likely needs to be seen in play for me to be sure.
                        • Incite Bedlam is much cooler, but I think it should come into play earlier than Wyrd 6; that always felt like an unneeded prerequisite.
                        • The Reticent condition seems like it’s a bit too potent of a drawback to characters targeted by a harvest; it hardly seems like nobody’s harmed if they’re losing all willpower and keeping it off, especially since it lasts for so long. Almost makes them worse for a community than Vampires (at least high humanity rarely-kill vamps). Too many changelings? Suddenly have a town of zombies...
                        • Clarity’s benefit against all magic seems to be a bit high to me. I think it might be better if you use the same perception chart and have that modify the willpower bonus (so, Clarity 6 gives +4 or -3).
                        • By contrast, I do love kenning being reflexive, usable by all, and costless if you’re at Clarity 5+.
                        • I went through merits and Contracts too. Only thing that lept out (other than liking the majority of them a lot) for merits is that Rigid Mask is a bit much as an auto-win against non-supernatural attempts, at least for the cost. Contracts I'm going back and forth about, mostly that their power levels don't always seem to match the dot rating, but that's the sorta thing I'd have to playtest.

                        As a note, I do want to try all of this out. I need to see if I can convince the ST for the Victorian Lost game we’re starting up soon to let us use these rules, so I can give some actual playtest feedback, instead of just blabbing about my at-a-glance opinion >_>.

                                    Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
                                    I'm not too keen on the definition of iron. Under the old rules, you had to intentionally acquire iron. That meant that a Changeling with an iron weapon is making an important statement, and a Hunter had to do research and identify their target. When every melee weapon is iron by default you loose that. My suggestion would be to use a more narrative definition rather than a chemical one. If a metallurgist would call it iron (including things like "pig iron") then it counts. If they call it something else, like steel, then it doesn't count. You say you don't know anyone with a steel sword; but I'd wager your friendship circle contains few Changelings or monster hunters.
                                    A metallurgist (and a great many other people) would make little distinction between iron and steel, except to note that steel is basically iron with fewer impurities. That's precisely why in a lot of fantasy literature the fae are affected by steel, a ubiquitous substance in the modern world.

                                    The differentiation of "cold iron" as being wrought by hand without magic or mass-production is far easier to understand narratively or mythologically than any apparent difference between iron and steel.

                                                    All right.
                                                    First off: David A Hill is an awesome guy, and for this, he deserves a holiday dedicated to himself. (DaveB also deserves one, but it should be darker and scarier. Like Halloween. But that's neither here nor there.)

                                                    Secondly, I have a few things I'd like to comment about some of the Contracts! In general, they are just... amazing. I love them. But some of them sparked questions in me, especially the catches. I'll try to list them here.

                                                    Blessing of Perfection is the one that I have the most issues with. The Catch hinges on two rather rare things happening at once. That you do this for someone you have just met, will not happen often. Doing something in exchange for a small favour, is also not going to happen very often. Now, this Catch hinges on you doing something with an object that belongs to someone you just met, AND that you do it in exchange for a small favour. Either one or the other is rare enough, I'd say.
                                                    Second nitpick: Why does the Catch only work to alleviate half of the uses for the Contract? There is no way to fulfil the Catch if one uses it to boost oneself.
                                                    Third nitpick: Exceptional on four successes? I thought de rigueur was to have it on three? (This one, I admit is whiny, but shouldn't the game try to be consistent?)

                                                    Mirror Walk, I don't have as much a problem with, as I'm unsure as to how it works exactly. Do you have to know that there is a reflective surface you aim at? Can you 'scan' for reflective surfaces near where you want to go?

                                                    Game Master's Table lacks an Exceptional effect. Does this mean to say that there isn't one, or did it accidentally get cut?

                                                    With Portents and Visions, once again the Catch confuses me. Does the Catch mean a picture of the target AS a child, or does it require a picture the target MADE as a child? (Also, how does this apply to things that were born adult? I got the impression that Oneiroi just spawn fully grown out of dreams. Same with Huntsmen, True Fae, et cetera. I have no problem with them being un-Catchable, I am just curious.

                                                    Hidden Reality. Now... This is a Catch I think is rather pointless. Isn't one of the big things with Changelings that they can't be confined? Or does it simply mean that this grants them more options to escape? In which case, it's not pointless, and I am stupid.

                                                    Finally, Chrysalis. I simply don't understand how the Catch works. It might again be just me who is stupid, but perhaps the phrasing could use some stupid-proofing?


                                                    Apart from these things, I simply loved everything that was given out. It's simply glorious. Thank you David for doing this. And of course, thank you all writers who contributed. (You can't let David get ALL the glory for this, can you?)
