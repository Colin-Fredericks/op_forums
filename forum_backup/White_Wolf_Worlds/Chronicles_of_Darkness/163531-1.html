                            I've always played my Uratha having been chosen for two reasons in particular.

                            1) If there are specific genetic markers in the human body that can be pointed to in the World of Darkness as "the werewolf gene," then that creates a whole mess of problems I have to explain away as to why hunter agencies don't snuff out wolf-blooded with impunity after a little routine bloodwork. I'll add the caveat that this one only matters if you're running a game with organized human hunters though.

                            2) Given the statistical likelihood that everyone shares a common ancestor as far back as 300 BC then it becomes a lot harder to find someone who didn't get a little contribution from a Pre-Sumerian flirtation with Father Wolf's children, much less from time immemorial before that. Naturally, the gene might not carry forward, but it still makes any one person you pick out on the street a very likely carrier.

                            Beyond that, I simply find the moment more evocative when a finger from on high points at you and says "You. You will bear this yoke. You will wear the night and moon as your cloak and shackles. You will be Uratha." Werewolves in the World of Darkness are driven creatures of undeniable instinct, so making the Change an epiphany just fits for me. Whenever I get to play through a prologue with players, the moment is painted in fractured visions of Luna and his purpose for her child.

                                        @Satchel: Guess the quotation markers around the "the werewolf gene" didn't convey what I was getting at. I'll give you the long version, then. If breeding and legacy influence the chance of an Uratha Changing, then you've got potential data that says "persons who are related to X werewolf have a higher-than-average possibility of being werewolves themselves." The wolf-blooded merit presently implies this much, that a lineage close to Uratha makes you more likely to become one. Hunter Team Alpha takes out a werewolf, takes a sample, and puts that into a bank of known werewolves. Hunter Agency checks that against genealogical records and test results from said bloodwork to find near relatives of that person, since, as you pointed out, many Uratha drop off the grid and may impregnate someone without leaving a record. Methodical Hunter Agency will flag these individuals and keep an eye out for them. Crazy Hunter Agency disappears the ones that are too-closely-related. Look up Operation Twelfth Night in War Against the Pure, and you've got hunters who are driven enough and have enough backing to try these kinds of shenanigans.

                                        Thus, I tend to run that lineage doesn't meaningfully-influence Changing.

                                        As for my second remark, that's, indeed, the point. My implication, then, becomes that the ability to trace your lineage to a point where it was touched by the spiritual influence that seems at present to be necessary for a werewolf is moot. Thus, the more meaningful mechanism becomes the Chosen aspect.

                                                        Originally posted by Tilting Clock View Post
                                                        As for my second remark, that's, indeed, the point. My implication, then, becomes that the ability to trace your lineage to a point where it was touched by the spiritual influence that seems at present to be necessary for a werewolf is moot.
                                                        Yes. Hence my pointing out of notable ancestors as a greater focus of attempts to trace Uratha lineage - the strength of the wolf's blood in you appears to vary based on more than familial factors - there's a chicken-and-egg thing at work where "wilder" people seem to have a stronger spiritual footprint and a greater likelihood of giving birth to werewolves than more "normal" children-of-children of werewolves.

                                                        It's not "trace your lineage back to a Celestine in prehistory" that's important. It's "you are made from the stuff that produced this, that, and the other renowned skinchanging wolfperson of legend, and you bear some of the same traits that made the spirits view them as worthy of power." There is no singular "the" spiritual influence at play here to a meaningful degree.

                                                        Most of those records are likely to be recorded in the First Tongue in Uratha oral tradition and are unlikely to mention the werewolves by anything but their deed name. Most of the people who become werewolves become werewolves and thus fall off the grid after the Change. Killing off wolf-blooded is going to accomplish precisely nothing useful thanks to the aforementioned nearly-everybody's-a-carrier thing; doubly so now that the Uratha can safely breed amongst themselves.

                                                        Thus, the more meaningful mechanism becomes the Chosen aspect.
                                                        In case it wasn't clear, I thoroughly disagree with the premise that the Uratha are "bred rather than chosen" in anything but the most meaninglessly broad sense of "having this degree of lineage makes you more likely to be seen as worthy of the curse of lycanthropy, for all that 'more likely' means anything when Luna is involved." As has already been pointed out, strictly-breeding-based werewolfism is nearer to Apocalypse's bailiwick.
