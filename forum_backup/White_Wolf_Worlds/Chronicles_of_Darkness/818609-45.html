    We should probably agree on Theme before we could actually start making the new system though. Then we could pound it into the readers head with a jackhammer.

    I propose Obsession. Genii are all, to differing degrees, obsessed with their Magnum Opus. Bardo/Shard Worlds/Splinters are Obsessed with true existance. Inspiration is Obsessed with the advancement of humanity. Humanity is Obsessed with holding on in an increasingly chaotic world. Manes are Obsessed with getting Mania. And the proposed Edisons (probably should get different name though) are Obsessed with Wonders.

    Obsession can drive people to ever greater heights and, if left unchecked, can also lead to ever greater atrocities in pursuit of it.

        We kind of already got into the themes. The idea of there not being anyone in charge, the tension between the future you want and the blood and tears that pave the road to it. Genii can see that the rules of society are only rules in so far as people are willing to follow them, and that being a Genius is about rejecting those rules, and maybe substituting your own if you can get around to it.

        Obsession is present, but it's not obsession with a mystery for its own sake, like Magi. Genii don't get obsessed with the Well of Souls, from which all life force flows, because it's an amazing mystery that could rewrite everything we understand. They get obsessed with it because they want to know how they can use it to build their perfect future where all mankind is connected to a collective consciousness. Their obsessions are practical, rather than academic.

                For me, the tragedy of the genius, and their main motivation is this: They're smart enough to see the glaring faults of society with greater clarity than the average person, while at the same time they see the amazing potential of what that society could be. It's the conflict between the vision of a utopia that their inspiration brings to their minds and the clash against the sad crushing reality of our world that their eyes see. A world populated by a majority of people that are too afraid of change and too trapped in their routines to dream.
                The irony is that the very same intelligence and inspiration that provides them with their amazing abilities and perspective also shows them just how impossibly difficult their task is. Their probabilities of success are really low, and even if they succeed their solution will surely have unforeseen, and probably disastrous, consequences. No matter how smart someone is, nobody can see all the variables or predict all the outcomes. This is of course without taking into account if their utopia is really that perfect, their arrogance blinds them to their imperfect vision, turning their idea of paradise into a hell for everybody else.
                However, the genii won't give up, oh no they won't. Despite everything, they still hope. It's a hope born out of the desperation, and the joy, of seeing pieces of that dream of utopia taking life in their lab.The genius keeps working while they murmur "If only they could see...", and that's why they persist. Because if only the world could see people would believe in their vision of utopia, and no matter what they will make the world see because it's a dream worth fighting for.

                                                    Originally posted by Beriorn View Post
                                                    Given the theme of failure, I think that there shouldn't be some kind of "win condition" like the New Dawn. No matter how smart a Genius is or how well they master the Axioms, they will never make the world a better place.
                                                    I'm sorry, but that was one of the things that I hate the most about first edition genius, and I couldn't be happier that they're ditching it. What's the point of having those amazing skills if you can't do anything with them? Hell, what's the point of playing a game in which nothing I do matters? I already talked about this on my first posts.
                                                    For me, genious is not about failing no matter what and dealing with the desperation that comes with the realization that you can't win. For me, genious is a game about leaving behind the arrogant attitude of the genius that makes him think that he, and he alone, can change the world for the better, solve all its problems, and create a perfect utopia.
                                                    Change, the kind that truly benefits the world, takes a lot of time, hard work, teamwork, and it won't solve all the problems. The genious that reaches victory is the one that internalizes this truth and learns that to succed he must work with the world, not against it, to create a positive change.
                                                    The amazing abilities of the genious are a beacon of light that ignites the spark of inspiration in the people around him, and with that he plants the seeds of belief in a better world. The people, fileld with that newfound faith in a better tomorrow, will work to make that vision a reality.
                                                    The world won't become the amazing utopia that the genious has envisioned, but that's not the point. The point is to make the world a better place, one piece at a time, till one day utopia becomes real.
