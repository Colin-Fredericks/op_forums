                  I'm on lunch, so let's break this down a bit.

                  First, as Arc said, Integrity and Morality and not the same. Further more, Humanity is not the same as either, although mechanically it is more informed by Integrity than Morality.

                  Secondly, it is important to understand that the violations are not alone in this matter, it is important to take the Humanity score itself into account as well. When your Humanity is very high you are extremely tied to the concept of humanity as a central pillar of your sense of self. When you commit a sin that is high on the list then you are shaking that very pristine version of yourself in your head. If you fail then you realize that you aren't as human as you thought and it breaks you a bit. If you succeed then you remain blissfully unaware of how you are losing your Humanity. If you get an Exceptional Success then you are managing to not only retain the facade but actually reinforce it. This makes sense sense it is easier to reinforce while you are still very sure of it (but also the simplest things are triggers because it is such a false state).

                  When you are committing a far more grievous sin like murder, you are likely to be shaken far more and have a much harder time having a positive reinforcing effect from the act. If you have low Humanity then it is even worse because you no longer even hold the delusion that you retain your Humanity so why would committing a heinous act ever reinforce that image except in the most extreme lucky circumstances.

                  As a third point, Exceptional Success on a Humanity roll are not the only epiphanies that arise from testing your Humanity, they are just the ones that reinforce the human self image. It is also an epiphany to realize just how monstrous you have become and forsake the idea of ever going back.

                  Similar, but different, logic follows for Integrity and the other 2e equivalents based on what those stats are trying to emulate.

                  Edit:

                  Originally posted by Prince of the Night View Post
                  I know but it being more likely with laughably petty indiscretions like "Witnessing a supernatural act the first time"
                  Didn't see this.

                  So to talk more about Integrity specifically. It is a stated part of the setting that the human psyche has a tendency to ignore or rationalize away the supernatural. Given that, this particular example makes sense since an Exceptional Success shows that the character not only ignored the supernatural ( and thus maintains their world view, also known as Integrity) but the rationalization is used as a mental model to convince themselves that they understand the world BETTER now and thus reinforces their Integrity.

                  The lower Breaking Points represent things that would shake your world view more and are harder to rationalize away.

                          First of all, the way Exceptionals work doesn't mean Integrity or Humanity is done backwards. The core of Integrity/Humanity is that it's harder to achieve a success when exposed to something more traumatising/dehumanising, which is completely realistic (as far as vampires and the supernatural are realistic) and works as intended. That Exceptional Success is more common with more trivial breaking points is a side effect of the underlying dice system.

                          Unfortunately there's no real good way of changing how it works mechanically.
                          The first alternative would be to make more horrible breaking points easier to succeed at, which just doesn't make any sense.
                          The second alternative would be to introduce a new mechanic to replace the Exceptional Success. Perhaps by changing the Exceptional threshold depending on the level of the breaking point, or introducing a second roll that is somehow inverted to the first roll. I would not recommend either because from a game design point of view they just add clutter to an otherwise pretty streamlined system.
                          But what you can do without messing with the dice system is to narratively redefine what an Exceptional Success is. Rather than an epiphany that should arise from more dramatic moments, an Exceptional Success could instead mean that the character takes the breaking point in stride, without even being shaken by what they just experienced. I think that works perfectly because that would be more common with more trivial breaking points.

                              If you're interested, we can discuss how to make revelations from "minor" incidents something easier to grok instead. Religion, philosophy, and science are actually full of amazing realizations born from incredibly small seemingly meaningless things.
