    Beast: Question

    06-27-2015, 07:30 AM
    This has probably already been discussed, and I post on here very rarely, but even though I have helped kickstart Beast to show my support for new venues I still have reservations. In particular how does the myth of the Dark Mother and all monsters being connected "help" the World of Darkness as a whole?

    What I mean is this: when I heard about Beast I was very excited with the concept, you play a human who is not a human who is in fact a monster of ancient myth and the only people who understand you are other monsters in a little bit Addam's Family meets a little bit of Clive Barker's Nightbreed where mostly you just aspire to feed your needs and avoid Heroes. That sound's great. But then this idea crept in that somehow Beasts have the real absolute inside tract on where they come from and this humoring big brother mentality regarding other supernaturals. Except the nWoD is designed with a decentralized mythos in mind, there is no one true ideology, all myths are equally plausible from the perspective of the one spouting it with their own onus of supposed proof. A mage is going to think mage lore is the answer. A vampire is going to think vampiric lore is the answer. And, most importantly to me, a werewolf is going to favor its own lore.

    This is important because when Changing Breeds came out I experienced a lot of people trying to force the Breeds into Forsaken like they were classic Fera when definitively they are not. The Fera are nature's fecundity made manifest. The Forsaken (and Pure) are ancient and storied descendants of a spirit god with a shared language and culture that defies boundaries. They're two different things and, given The People come into conflict with hosts at regular interval, I've always advocated the standard Forsaken response to a human that claims to be a royal ape should be to identify it as a strange kind of host and kill it.

    Now we have Beasts who seem to have an implicit "better than thou" mentality and a mandate to "fill in the blanks" of the world of darkness in such a way that the Beast story supersedes all other subjective beliefs of other supernaturals. That strikes me as a major mistake. But maybe I've just misread the situation. Would someone like to set me straight on this?

          I suppose that is true enough. However, in my experience the newest breed of tabletop gamer (Post 3.0 D&D and younger) is woefully narrow minded in their game scholarship. It's a different subject but there seems to be this propensity to either adhere blindly to exactly what is in book or be completely blind to things that are not expressly written down. For instance 2nd Edition Forsaken states that the Forsaken chose the name and call themselves Forsaken of their own volition. That's patently wrong. No one calls themselves Forsaken of their own free will. Their enemies proclaim them to be Forsaken. It is only centuries later with the Forsaken claiming the term from their enemies and making it their own that they would self identify as Forsaken, as if to say "I am Forsaken, but unlike you I can be redeemed." But there are those who, seeing the text say one thing will just blindly assume that is true. If Beast specifically says the Beasts are both from the dark mother and know the truth of the nWoD some people will assume that is true rather than being an assumption by the Beasts as a response to try and understand their existence. I'm not looking forward to being the one who has to remind people constantly that the nWoD is not a monolith but a continuum of in-character beliefs.

                              I agree. I mean really. I could tell tale of some of the stupid that has come out of the mouths of perfectly rational people who could only see through narrow boxes. The thing is that in my observations part of the reason for the pervasiveness of the problem is writers unintentionally taking for granted that their readership is neccessarily as schooled as it should be in its own subject matter. Using D&D again as the meta example, 3.0 and to a lesser extent 4.0 (An entirely different series of major mistakes there) didn't expressly prohibit some older more nuanced material from 2nd and 1st Edition D&D. There were game concepts hard learned and spread by word of mouth through the player base that everyone knew were part and parcel of the older editions that just didn't make it into the newer ones, and so newer players assumed they were nonexistent or had never been there. Its the same mentality that looked at cWoD and nWoD and said "Well they're both WoD, they must be the same", when we know they are very much not. I would just not want to see Beast suffer from misunderstandings due to a lack of clarity that seems to imply things which it really does not.
