    How would you sell the Forsaken Koolaid to Cubs?

    10-14-2016, 07:14 PM
    So i running this cubs game in werewolf. Pc started as freshly change werewolf and im running their first hunt. The pcs are normal people (a lawyer, a teacher and a bouncer) who a month ago transformed and have no idea of the setting (so no tribes yet) and i had planned a sort of "moot" for the players to get introduced but i kind got myself into a problem.

    As i was thinking on how the elder werewolf would explain the "setting" to them i ran into the problem that well being a forsaken kind of sucks. Not from a player perspective but from a character perspective. So how do you sell the duty of the forsaken to newly changed cubs?

    Because as i was thinking it, all i would could think is

    "So hey, you guys are werewolves and you have to leave your human lives aside and help us do this spiritual thankless duty. Spirits will hate you for it, other bad werewolf will hate you while coming to kick you out and then take your stuff because you doing it. And from time to time we also will come and kick you out and take your stuff because you arent doing it as good as we do"

    I kind of write myself into a corner here also because in my game im running the pure as the sabbat, in the sense that they represent the werewolf who schew their duty and live "free" because one of the players has a trigger with the "hate group that comes and burn you and take your family and reprogram you" interpretation of them. So i kind struggling how to sell the setting to the characters.

      Are you playing first edition?

      Because in second your duty is the hunt. If you neglect it, you weaken and lose control and fall into Kuruth at the drop of a hat. And you don't have to leave your human lives behind. You can if you want but it's not necessary. This is why there's a Lodge of the Shield, and the Pack has packs formed from a band, a software programming company, a fire house and a hospital. They didn't turn into werewolves and decide, 'Well I suppose I should become a police officer now.' You do have to recognize, or at least you should, that you aren't human anymore, however. And spirits may not like you, but many will at least respect you, providing you've earned it.

              You might not want to portray it as 'favorite prey.' As that sounds like, 'Yeah we love killing these.' Instead they believe they are the most dangerous prey, and thus need to be hunted. But the tribes have other aspects to them than just their sacred prey. Storm Lords are about strength, fortitude, perseverance, the Ithaeur are about wisdom and knowledge and secrets.

              But struggling on how to sell the character the forsaken faction. Even if i were to change their duty to hunting for huntings sake or else degeneration. The pure have easier prey, and the spirit already respect them.

              I guess my main question is. The pure have a great selling pitch. What would be a great forsaken selling pitch?
              The duty of the werewolf is to hunt. It's not for hunting's sake. It's because they're part 'spirit of the hunt.' They aren't human. They are a living, breathing Predator. If they do not perform the Sacred Hunt, they will make Breaking Points towards the Flesh. They will lose Essence. They will not be able to change willingly, to heal Lethal wounds, to use their powers. They will be triggered by passive triggers beyond their control, simply their moon being in the sky even if they're inside and asleep. This is not changing anything, it's how the game works. Your pack gets to choose what they hunt, what they think is the most dangerous, what they think is worth it, what they think deserves it, and what they think is so wrong it needs to be eradicated.

              I like the Pure. The spirits don't respect them any more than the Forsaken. They like using them. They like taking advantage of them not giving a damn the way the Forsaken do, but they don't respect them unless they've earned it the same as the Forsaken. And the Pure are the ones who have to leave their humanity behind. Two of the tribes, at least. The Predator Kings cannot use things made by humans. The Fire-Touched cannot let lies stand. There's a reason they live in the wilderness and the Hisil, respectively.

              Between that and 'Lets just burn that Renown off you,' I don't know why you get the idea the Pure have a great selling pitch. Their methods of conversion include torture and brainwashing. I could understand if you said your characters were backwoods survivalists or hellfire and brimstone preachers but are they really the type to fall for the Pure? Some people want to destroy the world as it is, and that fits for the Pure, but the path of the Forsaken doesn't involve destroying humanity and civilization as we know it.

              You shouldn't have a hard time with it. A character explains the history of the Uratha. Urfarah, Mother Luna, Pangaea. He weakened, was overthrown, the Uratha were exiled from their home, cursed with madness as a result. Eventually the Uratha earned back the grace of the spirits, the Firstborn totems and Luna. She marks them with Renown so that spirits will know she watches over them. The Firstborn give them a path so they can become what they're truly meant to be. She gave them the Oath of the Moon, so they can know how to find Harmony and self-control over their ever-shifting natures.

              The tribes should be open enough to fit them. If for some reason they don't like the tribes, they can remain a ghost wolf. The introduction wouldn't really have to even mention the Pure, though mentioning that some wolves want to tear it all down or serve twisted alien gods probably isn't out of the ordinary.

                                              The Forsaken sales pitch is that they can teach you to cope with your whole turning into a 400 pound snarling death beast a few nights out of every month problem. That happens because your Harmony is borked, and the Forsaken can teach you to balance your Harmony. The Wolf Must Hunt, and the Tribes can show you the best ways to turn that need to hunt to good use - better you hunt the Werewolves that can't control their rage, or renegade spirits, or Claimed, or the Hosts, or hell, even bad people than to hunt who or what ever happens to be closest to you when you lose control. And the Forsaken can give you the means, the know-how, and the opportunity to channel your Primal Urge safely. Sure, the Pure aren't bound by Luna's laws, but those laws are there for a reason. Breaking them pushes you further from your spirit nature and puts you at greater risk of losing control. Sure, you can counteract that by abandoning civilization and running wild as a King of Predators, or by abandoning the world of flesh to Touch the Fire of the spirit world, or by abandoning your humanity in pursuit bleaching your Claws the Ivory of racial purity. But those paths all come with great sacrifice and great risk, not to mention being morally dubious. It's order and discipline versus freedom and chaos. Do you join those who know how to carefully moderate their dual natures, or those who throw themeselves headlong towards alternating extremes with reckless abandon? Do you maintain a careful balance or risk fluctuating wildly?

                                                          It's kind of a minor problem with the setting IMO.

                                                          The setting assumes the Pure and the Forsaken are the majority (specifically, the Pure). Both factions sound bad from a purely human mindset, IMO especially if the human in question is very 'neutral' and "live and let live" or just "undecided."

                                                          So why not just go Ghost Wolf? I feel like the real reason a werewolf wouldn't go Ghost Wolf is that they'd feel lonely and most Ghost Wolves are disorganized and not necessarily present in their area. So they'd be alone... really alone! It would tear at their heart everyday. They'd be confused. They'd be vulnerable to the things that go bump in the night, which they feel the need to hunt. Werewolf lore is ALL about the pack, even the nonsense Twilight movies. So alone... But the two factions? They would introduce you to the world of wolves. Sort of a tutorial and potentially the place where you'd meet your pack or be pointed in the right direction.

                                                          The other reason is that since the Pure are more numerous (esp in 1st edition) they are very forceful about recruitment. The Fire Touched straight up won't take no for an answer and would attempt brainwashing. A Predator King interested in your strength would kick the shit out of you, schoolyard bully style, complete with torture. Ivory Claws are just kind of assholes, but if you're related to them or their extended pack in anyway, they wouldn't let you go Ghost Wolf easily.

                                                          Edit: I just read the part about one of your players not wanting to be exposed neither to brainwashing nor to torture. Unfortunately, you've kind of written yourself into a corner in the conflict with such limitations. I feel like all 3 Pure tribes would be willing to do it to a potential recruit. I'd have the Ivory Claws be the main Pure tribe of the area, as they'd be less prone to such issues.

                                                          The problem then is that your characters are all already cubs, technically Ghost Wolves, at the moment. They already have their pack and know each other? So loneliness is hard to play up.

                                                          The easiest way to sell them on the Forsaken is to portray the Pure as villains. Or if they are willing to give the Pure a chance, then let them see that they are evilish (and hopefully your players are more neutral/good-aligned).

                                                          However, much like the current election where both candidates try to sell themselves as "I may be bad, but at least I'm not the other guy," fear doesn't inspire as much as actual inspiration. In addition to making the Pure stereotypically evil, you could make some of the Forsaken in the area stereotypically good. For example, maybe the local Pure don't care about the local Beshuli infestation and are even rumored to allow them to fester, as they want to combine both worlds and bring back Pangaea. Cue the local Hunters in Darkness or Iron Masters, who valiantly sacrifice their lives to protect their turf or to protect their local humans and community.

                                                          Or maybe have the local Pure be incompetent, while the Forsaken are competent?

                                                          If you want less stereotypes and want to have more neutral leaning Pure, show off some of that infighting you mentioned. Hard to do without physicality because of your sensitive player, but maybe you can just show off the Forsaken as being more 'together.' Maybe the local Forsaken are less alpha-oriented or are even democratic. Maybe there's a local Ghost Wolf with red brands who used to be Pure and severely regrets becoming Pure.

                                                          2nd Edition is *much* less about being spirit police. If that aspect of the game bothers you, you could have the local Forsaken be heretics who let the occasional spirit trespass as long as they have proven themselves valuable allies and don't harm the local human ecosystem. Basically give more incentive to be Forsaken, even if it means abandoning some of the usual rules. It also sets up a fun encounter much deeper into your campaign if out-of-region Forsaken visit and cause a fuss.

                                                          Since being Forsaken *or* Pure can suck hugely, you could let them remain Ghost Wolves. Try to play up the feeling of loneliness, maybe their first totem acquisition fails as the spirit judges them to be fake wolves since they're not Pure or Forsaken. Play up that they don't really know what they're doing. Drag them into wars they're not fighting (local spirits or hosts come to kill them, confusing them for another pack) Give the Cahalith dreams of them all dying alone, confused, hunted by all manner of things but notably hunted by both the Pure and even the Forsaken (foreboding!) Give them reasons to join up, although no need to railroad.
