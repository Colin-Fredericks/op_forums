    What skill for mathematics and statistics?

    06-23-2016, 04:09 PM
    I had hoped this would get handled with the new CofD book, but, alas, I cannot find anything. So, I am reaching out to you all, in hopes that someone was able to find something in the text. If not in the text, the perhaps someone knows of an OPP/WW employee/writer/etc that might have spoken on the matter?

    In the CofD core book, there is a reference to economics in Academics, but that's the closest it gets to math. Science, meanwhile, includes several fields of science (biology, physics, etc) which doubtlessly use statistical models to explain data. My gut says that this would be up to the storyteller, and up to the player to define what "sort" of math they are using. But, do you all know anything different?

    Thanks!

          Don't bother finding a skill for all mathematics, just use the mental skill that makes most sense in context. Calculating important engineering stuff? Crafts+Intelligence.               My games tend to use a lot of Science. Mostly to determine what's happening when you're looking at a particular phenomena, or coming up with the lexicon used to describe something that's happening.

              I second math-as-Science rather than Academics. A good rule of thumb is that Academics are the "psychological" sciences, such as study of group behaviors, history, art theory, color theory, and so on - Academics will tell you that green's complementary color is purple and that many New Age cultures see it as the color of life, as well as its etymology and that some cultures have the same word for blue and green. Science will tell you that green is between 490 and 700-something nanometers light wavelength (one of the broader frequencies), and that it's the most efficient available type of natural chlorophyll for plants in regards to our sun for absorption of light, and that most public laser outputs are in this spectrum because they are neodymium-doped yttrium.

                  I'm not an OPP employee or freelancer, but I'm a fan who likes to think she has a pretty good handle on the rules. There's really no "Mathematics Skill", but as you observed, math is involved in a couple of other mental Skills. Academics, Science, and Computers, all involve math to some degree, and it can even be useful in Crafts and Investigation. I would say depending on what the player is trying to accomplish, pick the Skill that seems like the best fit for the situation. If you've got a player who has a character concept to which exceptional mathematical aptitude is an important detail, consider taking Mathematics as an Academics or Science Specialty, along with the Area of Expertise and Interdisciplinary Specialty Merits.

                      Remember that by forcing all Math related things into Science, you are kinda encouraging the model where characters are required to have 1 dot of Science / Academics to perform basic academic tasks. While it's one way to do things, I feel that CofD moved away from 1 being "bare minimum" to "wow, you've got a 1 in this skill, that's pretty nice, you can do a lot with it!". Like, you couldn't portray field I'm studying without literally handling every of our students 1 dot in Academics (for the "soft" side of it), Science (we deal with math, probability, simulations and other stuff too, yo), Investigation and Politics. And a dot of Medicine and Brawl too, I guess.

                      Which in CofD has a side problem of making people like this into Sherlock Holmes level of dillettantes, because mechanically, there is no difference between taking Science for Quantum Physics and for taking it for doing math. I feel that it's easier to portray a "field" by using one of the big mental skills, and applying more and more penalties as it goes away from it's "core".

                                      I'd say that it probably falls into Academics more than Science.

                                      Yes, raw calculation would be an Intelligence + Science roll... but I can count on my fingers how many times I had to do difficult calculations for my higher level courses.

                                      Take, for example:

                                      (1) Solve c²uxx + utt = 0 for the initial functions u(x,0) = p(x) and ux(x,0) = q(x).
                                      (2) Let E/F be a Galois extension, and L an intermediate subfield, such that L/F is a normal extension. Prove that the restriction map ρ:AutF(E) → AutF(L) given by ρ(τ) = τ|L, is a surjective homomorphism.

                                      (1) would be a Science roll to solve (and should be easy enough for any engineer in the audience), while (2) would be an Academics roll.

                                      [algebraist_neckbeard]And (2) is real math, not engineer scribbles like (2).[/algebraist_neckbeard]
