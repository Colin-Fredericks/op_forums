                                    Session LXIV

                                    Gawain’s Memories

                                    November 23, evening

                                    Now that was a nice way to spend an afternoon. Not quite what I expected when I went to talk to Lori after breakfast. We had Brussels waffles this time which were a little, you know, crispier. They maybe weren’t perfectly accurate - I didn’t, you know, by a different waffle iron for it and apparently Brussels waffles are usually a slightly different shape - but the ingredient part was right. The powdered sugar worked nice too, but sugar usually does.

                                    But I’m getting distracted. In between discussing the relative aerodynamics of peoples’ cars - see, Dominika had come by last night to make sure Monica was, you know, still welcome which of course she was, as long as she didn’t threaten people with cars, you know? It was kinda unfortunate she felt she had to ask, but De la Roche said it was probably just cause she felt guilty and all. Anyway, Calamity mentioned something about helping Gloria and Desiree with moving and IKEA furniture and stuff and that got me thinking about wedding presents in a weird sorta free association thought string.

                                    So once Julia went off to paint Oleander in her business suit church clothes (though assuredly not like on of her French girls) and Calamity snagged Lori for working on their new act, I interrupted just a little to ask if an armoire that could magic up whatever outfit you might need would be an OK wedding present. And by the time I’d said half of it, she was all but doing the little lip trembly, wide eyes, hopeful smile thing and I’d, you know, promised to make her one.

                                    It took me pretty much all of the afternoon until it was time to get ready for the show. Carpentry is, you know, a kinda new thing for me, but I had a good idea of what to do for the Hedgecrafting part -silken moonlight, the velvet of new growth, finely tanned dragon hide and the clash of metal on metal. It should be fun - I really do like the wavy, weird quality of the whole Hedgecrating process. It’s nice to have a project too.

                                    The shows went well, the past couple of nights. Calamity rocked it both times, of course. She’s gotten really good, not that, you know, she wasn’t always awesome. We started telling folks about the Thanksgiving feast last night too. Moon, who was totally smooth chatting up Hailey last night, will probably come, to get out of a second awkward Thanksgiving with Shi. Apparently last year he had invited hir to his family gathering, and zie’d realized halfway through that no one there actually knew Shi. He’d just, you know, stalked them enough to be able to talk about all the things they’d done together recently.

                                    Hailey was pretty shocked that De la Roche was doing Thanksgiving stuff at all. So was Monica actually and even more so that De la Roche went to Disneyland and enjoyed it. That was, you know, more of an amused shock though. She’d showed up tonight - Hailey hadn’t. I guess Dominika coming by yesterday gave her warning, you know? She was pretty bummed, Hailey was, that Monica hadn’t even let her know she was back safe in town.

                                    I wish there was something I could do to help that whole situation out. I think I managed to not make it worse at least. It did get a little, you know, awkward when she mentioned being a drama factory. I guess it was mostly just still awkward from when I tried not to mention anything about Leanne while sharing gossip.

                                    Andromeda was also there tonight, sans Joan. And as soon as Lori got down off the stage, Andromeda walked over and kissed her. I guess that’s how they’re dealing with the whole, you know, eye fucking thing? They seemed happy though and she and Lori and De la Roche were talking most of the the night.

                                    Calamity made her usual rounds, and the unusual one of checking on Insincere Marguerite in the catwalks. She didn’t seem upset going up or coming back down though, so I’m sure it’s alright. She wound up spending a lot of time with Becky, Princess Joli and Desiree - apparently the Princess is going to join us for movie night tomorrow, you know, as Desiree’s date. It should be fun. Maybe I’ll make something special for dinner as well tomorrow. Unless she just really likes takeout...


                                    Lorelei’s Diary

                                    It’s Monday morning, and I haven’t written anything in this diary since Saturday evening just before the show. I wish that I could say that this is due to the fact that I’ve been existing in a state of blissful peace in which nothing worth recording occurred, but sadly this is not the case. The truth is that I’ve had a pretty bad case of the doldrums lately, and haven’t felt much like writing.

                                    In any case, on Saturday night I went through one of my standard acts, backed up a few of our other headliners, and then made the rounds of the audience as per usual… well, maybe a little quicker than usual, in truth. Oleander was there, and I quickly sought her out, planted myself firmly in her lap, and draped my arms around her neck. It had been nearly a week since we’d seen each other at that point, and it was clear that we were both hungry for the contact.

                                    “I’ve missed you angel,” she said simply as I leaned up against her shoulder.

                                    “I’ve missed you too, honey.” I murmured the words into her ear, and then chased it with a light kiss.

                                    “So I know you need to stick around here for a while,” she said with a twinkle, “but I was wondering… are you going to drag me upstairs to your room tonight, or are we heading back to my place?” It’s always kind of a difficult question in Oleander’s case, since I share my room with D and the two of us heading up there would essentially be tantamount to kicking her out to fend for herself.

                                    “I have church tomorrow morning…” I began.

                                    Oleander smiled. “My place is actually closer to your church, you know…”

                                    I returned her smile at that. “Just let me pack up some clothes… I probably shouldn’t wear this to church,” I indicated the low-cut sparkly evening gown I’d donned for my last number.

                                    Oleander shrugged. “Might give the congregation a thrill,” she opined.

                                    I laughed a little. “Why don’t you come upstairs with me? I wanted to talk with you privately about something, and we can take care of that while I pack.”

                                    She agreed amiably enough, and a few minutes later we headed up to my bedroom. On the way out, Dominika caught my eye and motioned that she wanted to talk to me (and D, and Gawain and Calamity). Turns out she and Monica are back in town (obviously) and were considering stopping by the Dream House the following evening, and Dominika wanted to make sure Monica was still welcome here in the wake of the… unpleasantness. Of course we assured her that both she and Monica would always be welcome at the Dream House… but it made me a little sad that she felt she had to ask in the first place. D opined that our friend is just embarrassed about her actions and trying to work through it the only way she knows how.

                                    In any case, a few minutes later Oleander was sitting on the messily rumpled bed D and I share while I carefully folded up my church clothes and put them in an overnight bag.

                                    “So what did you want to talk to me about?” she asked curiously. “Nothing dire, I hope…”

                                    I shook my head. “It’s a good thing,” I assured her, “it’s just private. I wanted to tell you because it’s important to me… and so are you.”

                                    She blushed furiously, but didn’t say anything.

                                    “So, you know the church I go to every Sunday morning?” I asked. She nodded. “Well, I go there – or at least I started going there – because the preacher is my father.”

                                    Oleander took it in stride, though she was obviously surprised. “That’s… wow,” she said after a moment.

                                    “Yeah,” I replied, “I guess ‘wow’ pretty much covers it for me, too. The real news is that he now knows I’m his daughter. D and I told him last weekend, and we explained the facts of Changeling existence to him as well. He took it… much better than I expected.”

                                    Oleander’s face lit up. “That’s great, angel,” she said fervently. Then her brow wrinkled a bit. “Does he know you’re…” she began hesitantly.

                                    “Gay?” I asked, leaving the somewhat open-ended question of my own sexuality aside for the moment. “Yeah. I actually told him about that a few months ago. He’s been very supportive.”

                                    I took a deep breath and put my hands on Oleander’s shoulders. “I’d… I’d like you to meet him, if you wouldn’t mind joining me at church tomorrow morning,” I said softly. “I haven’t told Dad that I’m polyamorous yet… though he can be annoyingly perceptive at times. I’ll probably introduce you as a very dear friend. But he’s met D and Marguerite and Julia already, and I want…” I trailed off vaguely and twirled my fingers in the air.

                                    She leaned in and kissed me gently on the tip of my nose. “I’d love to meet him,” she answered softly.

                                    We headed back to her place after that and proceeded with our evening together. I’m sure Oleander noticed that I wasn’t quite as bubbly and enthusiastic as usual, but she didn’t say anything, and did her best to make me feel accepted and loved, which was pretty much exactly what I needed. We made love, of course, but mostly we cuddled and kissed and slept.

                                    Church was once again nerve-wracking but otherwise pleasant. Oleander, like D, put on a suit rather than a dress, and like D, she can really rock that look. I had a brief solo during one of the hymns today, so she got to see me perform in something other than skimpy, spangly clothes, which I’m sure made a nice change. When I introduced her to Dad as a dear friend she was very stiff and formal (I think she was worried about giving too much away, actually), but he took one look at her and greeted her warmly with a firm handshake and kind words. Did he read more into that introduction than I intended to give away? Who can tell…

                                    We both headed over to the Dream House for breakfast after church. More waffles. “Brussels Waffles,” apparently, which I can only assume are more specific than regular old Belgian waffles. They were tasty, of course, but I hope Gawain works this out of his system soon. Lorelei cannot live on waffles alone (not if she wants to maintain her girlish figure, at any rate).

                                    Julia sort of tentatively approached me and Oleander and asked if she could talk with us. That surprised me, since she’s been a little distant lately (though she’d obviously been impressed by the sight of Oleander in a suit). Turns out she wanted to offer to paint a portrait of Oleander for me, since she’s already painted all of my other girlfriends. I was so touched by the offer that I had to gather myself for a few moments before I could answer. Oleander looked at me questioningly, and I was eventually able to mutter out that I’d love to have a portrait if she was willing.

                                    The two of them went off to make plans. I think they were originally going to set something up for Tuesday, but as Oleander had the time right then, they got started immediately instead. Gawain approached me as they left to ask for my opinion on an idea he had for a wedding present (whether for Joan and Andromeda or for Marguerite and Julia, I’m not sure). He described a Hedge-crafted armoire which could produce tailored outfits on demand, and started going on about the esoteric ingredients he wanted to include, like moonlight and the velvet from new leaves and such.

                                    I confess that my mind got sort of hazy as I considered the possibilities inherent in such a treasure. “My birthday’s in February,” I said nonchalantly when he paused. I’d intended it as a joke, but he got this sort of goofy grin on his face and asked if that was the sort of thing that I might enjoy having myself, and I just couldn’t turn him down.

                                    I spent most of the early afternoon rehearsing with Calamity, working out new steps and moves for our “Bloody Creature Poster Girl” number, which is shaping up nicely. I think we should have it as close to perfect as anyone could reasonably expect by its debut next Saturday. I’d sort of absently noted that Jacob and Marguerite had left earlier, but actually called a halt to our rehearsal when they got back, as Marguerite was looking unusually grave. She asked if she could speak with me, and of course I agreed.

                                    It turns out that the two of them had been to visit Felicity. Jacob had once again agreed to act as “chaperone” for Marguerite, for which I’m grateful, even if it doesn’t seem strictly necessary anymore. This visit was not romantic in nature, however… Marguerite had wanted to ask Felicity about Rowan since they’re both Day Court. And while our neighbor didn’t know much about my newest admirer… she did have a few concerns about her house.

                                    Yeah, that’s right, her house is the concerning issue.

                                    Turns out there’s no record of the house ever having been bought or sold… or built for that matter. It seems like it’s always been there. There are no records of any major renovations or repairs. The house’s owner and sole long-term occupant has always been a single woman for as far back as the records go, which is more than a century now. And Marguerite said that Felicity reported a strange feeling of being watched while she was in Rowan’s house.

                                    What could this mean? Is the house itself some sort of supernatural creature, like a Gentry or a Hedge Beast? Could this be some sort of haunting? I tried to remain calm as I asked what they thought I should do. I got my phone out, thinking that I should call Rowan to warn her of this potential danger, but Marguerite stopped me. “If she is in her house when you call… something might be listening,” she said delicately. “Felicity has already offered to approach her at work tomorrow, to see if she has more information.”

                                    “She shouldn’t go back there,” I insisted. “If it’s dangerous…”

                                    “It has done nothing to hurt her in two years,” Marguerite soothed. “Felicity has offered to allow Rowan to stay with her if she likes.”

                                    I chewed on my bottom lip for a minute. Rowan is my kinda-girlfriend, so I really feel like I should be the one to put her up if her house becomes inhospitable. But on the other hand, I don’t want to tip our hand early if there’s anything we can do to rectify this situation. And besides, guest rooms at the Dream House are running short, and I don’t want to just cavalierly invite her to join me and D in our bedroom.

                                    I mean, if she would be into that, sure, but…

                                    In the end, I decided to sit on this information for now. I’m still really worried about my poor selkie, I admit, but I’m not sure what I can do about it until we have more information. I’d resolved to try to subtly tempt her into staying with me that night if she’d turned up for the show… but she didn’t.

                                    Calamity and I mutually declared the rehearsal over after that, and I headed up to see what Oleander and Julia were up to. It looked like Julia had just about finished her rough sketch, and Oleander was sitting off to one side relaxing. I walked over to her and leaned up against her shoulder, worried and distressed and still a little depressed for no reason I could easily describe.

                                    My honey picked up on it immediately (Oleander, I mean… Julia was engrossed in her art). “What’s wrong, angel?” she asked in a whisper.

                                    Not wanting to disturb Julia, I led her back to my bedroom. “I want to ask you something serious, sweetheart,” I said after a moment. She raised an eyebrow, silently giving me the go-ahead to continue. “Do you like me?” I asked when I couldn’t think of any other way to put it.

                                    She took my hand in hers. “What kind of a question is that?” she asked softly.

                                    “I know you love me,” I explained hastily. “I don’t doubt that for a second, I promise. But do you like me? I mean… I’ve been thinking about this a lot recently. What do people see when they look at me? I’m pretty, because I was made to be pretty. I’m a good singer… but again, some of that is because of what was done to me. And I have so many lovers that I’m actually having difficulty scheduling time to be with all of them. But is that all I am? Take away the singing and the sex, and… is there anything left?”

                                    “Lori,” she said, squeezing my hand, “I want you to listen to me, okay? You’re a good person… one of the kindest people I know. You make things better just by being there. I’m not explaining this well, but… I like the person I am when I’m around you. You’ve changed me for the better. When other people talk about me, they say that I’m cold and distant and a little creepy. You don’t agree with that description of me because I’m different when I’m with you. I like myself better when you’re around, and that has nothing to do with the sex… though the sex is good as well.” She gave me a wry grin at that.

                                    I pulled her into a kiss. “Thank you,” I said softly, “I think I needed to hear that.”

                                    We rejoined Julia and Marguerite in the attic – just in case Julia needed more reference material, we joked – and it wasn’t long before D returned to the house, having met Joan for ice cream to discuss what was going on with our relationship. D looked at me seriously and said “I need to talk to you privately for a few minutes.” Then she turned and gave Oleander a friendly smile and added “I won’t keep her for too long, I promise.” Oleander nodded good-naturedly and helped me up.

                                    D kind of sighed as the door to our bedroom shut behind her. “Word is spreading that you and Joan are an item,” she said seriously, “and Joan, of course, insists on shouldering whatever blame she thinks there is. I tried to tell her that everyone involved is an adult, that no one would care about this ‘indiscretion’ as long as she didn’t make a big deal out of it, but she’s convinced that she’s somehow soiled your reputation… and Andromeda’s, too.”

                                    I sort of goggled at that for a minute. “Ummm,” I said after I’d processed it for a bit, “she knows it’s a little late to close that barn door in my case, right? The cows are already out to pasture. And I’d never say anything against Andromeda, but…”

                                    “She’s been around too, I know,” D nodded, “but try telling Joan that. She’s convinced that there’s been some sort of transgression here, and she’s trying to take all of the blame on herself.”

                                    I sat down on the bed and put my head in my hands. “What am I gonna do, D?” I asked rhetorically.

                                    She sat next to me and put her arm around my shoulders, just above my wings. “Only thing you can do in my opinion is just keep moving forward. It’ll blow over when people find something else to talk about. You and Andromeda can help Joan get past this. I know you can.”

                                    She kissed me on the temple. It’s one of those oddly sweet gestures that she does all the time when we’re alone but denies hotly when anyone else is around. I started to melt a little, and it suddenly occurred to me that I wanted her opinion on the things I had previously discussed with Oleander. I didn’t open by asking if she liked me this time, though… I knew well enough how she’d respond to that sort of a question.

                                    “Am I a stereotype, Maria?” I asked after enough time had passed that I felt I could reasonably change the subject.

                                    She snorted. “Of course not, Jules. Where did that come from?”

                                    I shrugged noncommittally. “I dunno. Just thinking about my life, I guess. Sometimes I think all anybody sees when they look at me is how much sex I have, and how many partners. I want to be more than that.”

                                    Her face turned stony. “Did somebody say something to you, Juliette?” she asked seriously. “Is this about what Augustine said? Because he still hasn’t made that apology like he promised, and if I need to rearrange some teeth…”

                                    “No,” I said with a soft smile. “It’s not about the Viscount. It’s not about anyone else, really. This is coming from me.”

                                    “Coming from our patriarchal society, more like,” she grumbled, and my smile grew a little more genuine as I regarded my ranting love fondly. “Nobody hangs offensive labels on a man who has a lot of sexual partners, but women in the same situation become pariahs. It’s bullshit, and I don’t want you to obsess over it.”

                                    “I just… I dunno, I just want to be more than just the ‘Pretty One,’ I guess,” I said after a minute. “I don’t want that to define who I am. Maybe I should try to focus more on my medical studies. Even if I can’t be a real doctor, I could at least maybe make a name for myself in the Freehold as a decent healer.”

                                    To her credit, D let my self-deprecating remark pass without comment. “You are an amazing person, Jules,” she said seriously. “Nobody in the motley thinks you’re one-dimensional. We love you, and we like hanging out around you… and we respect you. You just keep sleeping with as many people as you like. Keep singing like you do until someone important hears you and signs you to a label. Study medicine and become a brilliant neurosurgeon or something. Do whatever you want with your life. And if anyone ever tries to tell you that any of that defines who you are… fuck ‘em.”

                                    So I was completely useless for the next five minutes or so as I composed myself. What did I ever do to deserve two such amazing women in my life? And that’s not even mentioning Julia, Marguerite, Joan, Andromeda, and Rowan.

                                    Anyway, after I’d cried myself out, I got cleaned up and started preparing for the show. Oleander opted to stick around for the performance, and I started to form the beginnings of a plan at that point. All four of my closest lovers were there, after all…

                                    Monica showed up, as expected. Halley, having been forewarned of her ex’s plans, opted to stay away for the evening. I think Monica and D are okay now… not that I ever thought any bad feelings would linger for long. In any case, Monica and Dominika talked up Hawaii quite a bit, and I admit that it does sound like a wonderful place. Monica even started ribbing Gawain a bit about how he ought to take Calamity out there for a weekend.

                                    Andromeda showed up stag, which I think surprised just about everyone. As soon as she walked in the doors, she located me as I walked the floor and made a beeline for me. I was a little worried about what she might say, I confess. I had thought that she might be a little upset about how my actions had affected her relationship with Joan.
