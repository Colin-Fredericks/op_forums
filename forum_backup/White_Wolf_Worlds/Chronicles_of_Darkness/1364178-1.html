    Originally posted by HelmsDerp View Post
    I think it's worth remembering that a lot of people's first impressions of Heroes are from the kickstarter draft, and because that initial impression was so poor many of them didn't bother to follow up and see how things changed in publication.
    Is it?

    We're getting close to the 3 year mark on that (by close I mean in less than a month).

    I'm pretty comfortable with the response to anyone that falls back to this position being, "If that turned you off the game, please move on instead of coming into a space for discussing the game and expecting your opinions to be valued when you haven't updated your knowledge of the game in this long."

    I'm not saying this is true of the OP, because they haven't actually done this openly to my knowledge. But I'm done caring about this excuse. We have had a revised manuscript, and multiple supplements, expanding the game and improving it significantly from its flawed start. If people don't care about the game enough to know that, they don't actually care about things like what playable Heroes would actually be about either.

    Even for those that did, well, first impressions are important for a reason, they stick with you.
    But.... first impressions of Heroes don't count?

    The Kickstarter draft isn't exactly kind to them. It repeatedly makes it clear that they're violent hypocrites. Many Beasts might be capable of repenting but don't, while becoming a Hero means tossing the idea that you'll have to repent for anything you do out the window. The original draft makes it clear that Hunters usually think that Heroes are Slashers because of how far gone Heroes are to their fellow monster fighters.

    I'm not surprised if people consider Heroes the lesser evil, but I presume it's because they didn't read the text to have that impression. If you actually read it, even the old preview, and came off with that impression? Then yeah, I'm surprised because there's less nuance about them being anything but dangerous lunatics that gladly kill innocent humans in their quest to kill Beasts than their is in the final text. That version doesn't present Heroes as complex people once they become Heroes that all goes away.

    Originally posted by Caedus View Post
    ....like....beasts are meant to be hypocritical...they are full of shit and the world doesn't need them.
    "The world doesn't need" is an empty phrase. The world doesn't need humans either. The world would continue on if we all poofed out of existence tomorrow.

    Beasts are actually, by default, not very hypocritical. They're presented as knowing what they are, the danger they represent, and acknowledging their desire for survival over the comfort and safety of others. They know their attempts to temper their needs via Lessons is an artificial philosophic position with no weight behind it to generate consequences for Beasts that don't serve this purpose. As the book notes in crossover with Hunter, one of the best weapons Beasts bring to conflict with hunters is their honesty about what they are, and hunters frequently not being so honest with themselves about what compromises they make.

    Beyond "need," it seems worth expanding on an important part of the metaphysics of Beast:

    Humanity as a collective is what makes Beasts and Heroes.

    Our primordial fears create the Horrors that find dreamers to bond with to form the Begotten. Our unwillingness to confront those fears in a healthy fashion and instead seek comfort in civilization to alleviate such fears (which isn't really possible) spurned Oracles and Sages to fall from mediators between the manifestations of our fears and ourselves, into the debased glory hounds Heroes are.

    The world doesn't need lions, but they exist. There's lots of ways killing a lion is justifiable for humans seeking to protect themselves or other humans. But trophy hunters there to kill a lion just to kill a lion aren't doing it to make the world better.

It just... is. Because low integrity. That was somehow self inflicted and deserved, unlike other kinds of trauma.

                                                            Here's two other possibilities I can see for sane hero.

                                                            First, moderation and principal. Much like a Hunter, a Hero could cleave to an idea. Their Hero senses tell them when someone has suffered by a beast's hand. They try to cause more good than harm. They don't unduly risk others. They're not glory hounds by nature. And perhaps, even, they allow a Beast who is trying to minimize the harm they inflict to live, perhaps even seeing them as a person. The one that just ripped an arm off that high school student? Yeah, he's ending up six feet under.

                                                            Second, the Hero as a judge. A Hero possibly who tries to convince Beasts to moderate their actions. To be as human. And they strike if they refuse their advances. A Hero could even be an ally to a Beast who's been trying to moderate their actions. Perhaps a Hero even visits the Beast's own depredations upon them if they insist, but the Hero isn't quite ready to swing a sword... unless, of course, the Anakim needs to pushed ahead of them. Perhaps even a system for asserting their authority over a beast to get gifts. Or at least, perhaps, the Hero can recover Integrity for free that way, as the idea they can peacefully get that.

                                                            Or, as one more idea for a system change? A Hero who works peacefully can recover Integrity over 10, and perhaps gain powers the higher it goes. Heroes that slay Beasts are like Heracles or Odysseus, strong but fatally flawed. Heroes of Integrity are different. They may be a little more than human - they might have had to slay an unredeemable Beast. But they more than make up for it with their Wisdom. They're more than able to see past fear. Nightmares have no purchase on them. A Hero of Integrity is David facing Goliath - throwing warning shots before their faith or wisdom delivers a killing blow. They tame dragons, rather than slay them.

                                                            In short, you have Beasts that cling to their Life and Beasts that revel in their Legend. Why not have Heroes who act the same way?
