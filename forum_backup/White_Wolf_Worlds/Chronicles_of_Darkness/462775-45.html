                        11-28-2015, 08:22 PM
                        Once a spirit has decided to cross the Gauntlet and Claim a being, it has decided to no longer act by the restraints of its concepts. This would be a coyote that would be not just a really good and effective coyote, but be able to control and possibly even create other coyotes. And since it no longer holds back, it has no reason to fit into the 'natural' cycle of things, so it wouldn't stick to being just an ordinary coyote that would run from man, it would act erratically, attack pets and use its Dread Powers to expand its influence.

                        If you've ever seen a 'when animals attack' type horror movie, that's basically the situation you have at hand. An animal makes the particularly un-animal-like decision to not take it anymore and organizes action against the status quo. They don't always have to go out and attack small towns with a massive horde of other animals, but they're likely to supplement their Essence needs with some flesh of man. Some werewolves might ally themselves with them, but if it was with the Forsaken it would always be an uneasy alliance, as werewolves are generally going to be more powerful than any animal, even spirit-enhanced ones, that you would find in your standard territory and the Claimed would always know that it's only surviving as long as the werewolves let it, which wouldn't put it in a comfortable position. Something like that is probably more common in the other groups like the Pure or Ghost Wolves, as well as humanity. The sample K-9 Officer in Tales from the 13th Precinct has a dog, Kita, that is claimed by a dog-spirit.

                                Don't forget that spirits eat their own kind as part of the Shadow's natural order of survival. So a coyote claimed isn't just going to be hunting normal coyote prey, it's also likely to prey on other coyotes for their delicious coyote flesh that tastes so right to it.

                                Regardless of what animal, plant, or concept a spirit resonates with, all spirits are predators whose primary instinct is survival. More powerful spirits gain nuance, but the core remains. For 99.9% of spirits, this instinct translates into some variation of 'consume the weak, bring low the strong, gain strength and power'. Some ally themselves with Uratha to gain this power. Others live the laws of the Shadow and take their chances. And some break the rules and take their chances with the soft, fleshy creatures on the other side of the barrier.

                                Plus, once a spirit has broken a couple of rules (being on the wrong side of the Gauntlet and possessing flesh), breaking subsequent and greater rules becomes easier, and more damaging to everything around it.
