    No, that's Dream Sorcery. Maidens are sources of stability. The Maiden merit is currently as follows...
    Maiden (Varies) A Human Mortal who’s company and opinion is of importance to the Oroboros, and can prevent them from falling too far into Guilt or Hubris with Praise and Admonishment respectively. This Maiden might be a simple one-dot friend who knows something of the character’s exploits, or a several-dot companion who is able to deal with all the crap they go through on a regular basis, perhaps even with some supernatural ability of their own, such as Spirit Sight or Telekinesis. The one requirement is that they ARE a human Mortal: no Supernatural Creatures.

    Because of this Mortality and weakness, Oroboroi tend to be very protective of their “maidens”, and (especially at high Hubris) may even resort to holding them against their will rather than lose them. Other Oroboroi can see this connection. In fact, they SMELL of Dragon, which tips off most entities with some sense of the supernatural that they are protected. While Oroboroi generally do not screw with each other’s Maidens as a matter of conduct, it is a GAPING weakness that an Oroboros cares about this person, and they MAY be used as leverage by the unscrupulous. Anyone doing so, however, had better bring an army, because that sort of behavior tends to piss off a bit more than one Oroboros...

    I'm gonna be fair here, I'll have to recheck Second Sight on what happens if your dreaming form gets imprisoned in the Dreamtide. This just seemed reasonable... But no, the Dream Sorcery Merit is currently as follows...
    Dream Sorcery Requirements: Occult (3)OR be an Oroboros



    The Dreamtide may be removed from the Waking, but it is not inaccessible, even to mortals. The character who possesses this merit understands the forces at play between this reality and the one beyond the miasma, and is able to affect it with various rituals. Most often, these require what these occultists call“Materialized Dreamstuff” and “Implements”. Materialized Dreamstuff is a potent material from the Dreamtide which has gained full presence in the Waking, such as the blood of an Oroboros in True Form (1 Lethal), and Implement is something which can directly interact with non-material Dreamstuff, such as an enchanted or Jadeite Daggar.
    1 Dot - Dream Sense - for a single point of Willpower, the Sorcerer can activate Dream Sense, allowing them to track down and see dreamstuff and dreamers in the Waking, and allowing them to better navigate the Dreamtide itself.
    2 Dot - Dream Manipulation - For Willpower, a caster can hack new elements into a Dream or alter Dreamstuff. Problems that can happen include the dreamer resisting, the dreamer noticing, or the wrong thing being hacked in.
    3 Dot - Object Permanence - By coating an object taken from the dreamtide with Materialized Dreamstuff, it will become permanent, and will stay in existence even when not in sight or mind.
    4 Dot - Dream Portal - The sorcerer knows enough about the Miasma to open portals to the Dreamtide. There are many ways to do this, but the most common two involve a dreamer or materialized dreamstuff. (like the blood of a True Form Oroboros) Either way has it’s own risks associated, as the portal not only allows the sorcerer and their companions to enter and exit the Dreamtide, but anyone and anything else as well.

    Entering the Dreamtide requires the enterer to know what they’re doing, either through training or instinct (like the Oroboroi), to suspend all disbelief of what is happening, and to spend a point of willpower to bridge the gap between them and the Dreamtide.
    Through a Dreamer - To enter the Dreamtide through a Dreamer, directly into their Dream, only requires the Sorcerer to draw a series of concentric circles around where the Dreamer’s head is using Materialized Dreamstuff. Once everything is in place, anyone who knows how can simply walk in and out of their Dream as if it were a staircase into their head.

    Direct Portal - Entering without a Dreamer is more complicated and dangerous. First, a wall of floor must be cleaned of impurities: covering it with good quality paper works. Next, the sorcerer uses Materialized Dreamstuff to draw a flat ovoid with pointed sides and, inside that, a near-perfect circle. Finally, the sorcerer takes their Implement and uses it to carve a line straight down the center of the circle. Entering the Dreamtide is achieved by slipping through the crack.

                        Very well. I have the GMC update and I read it in its entirety; most of my aversion to character creation was founded in the changes to merits.

                        I'll take your challenge and do Blazing Dragons. I'm going to build off of the video game, since it had a more focused story and I'm less familiar with the television show from whence your internet search yielded its quarry. I also don't have access to your document (You didn't share the google docs folder in the thread), so I'm going to wing this. Feel free to correct mechanic misunderstandings or to harvest concepts for new player options.


                        First, some groundwork. Keep in mind I'm embellishing for the purpose of putting the setting into Chronicles of Darkness; the source material isn't nearly this grim:

                        Camelhot
                        Centuries ago (more than a few, less than a bunch) a group of mining serfs working and living in a secluded valley unearthed a vein of buried Orobori Hearts, consumed them, and became dragons. They used their new-found power to overthrow their leaders and drive them out of the valley. The leader of the group took the title of King and a new name: Allfire. He declared that he and the others would found a new nation of gods in that very valley, and only dragons would ever be allowed to dwell there.
                        As a mockery of (then) popular culture, this new kingdom was named CamelHOT (as a jab against King Arthur Pendragon) and Allfire declared the other dragons would be his Knights of the SQUARE Table (where everyone is equally spaced from each other... unless you happen to be seated at a corner).

                        Centuries passed and the little valley of Camelhot remained isolated from the rest of the world. Allfire's knights would incinerate almost every human who wandered into the valley; those who survived the assault were presented before the king and either offered to swear fealty (and given a Heart from the mine) or--if they refused--were roasted like chickens. Eventually the population swelled, the secrets of the mines were obscured, and everyone forgot that they used to be human beings. Only Allfire remembers, and his encroaching senility risks the doom of their entire settlement. To that end, he's begun the pursuit of a successor king, promising his lands and the hand of his daughter Flame in marriage to the most worthy knight in his kingdom.

                        The Orobori of Camelhot subsist entirely on coal, to such an extent that they consume nothing else for their sustenance. This has also lead them all to the false belief that they must eat coal to be able to breathe fire. All Camelhot Orobori are green and have six limbs: they are quadrupedal and do not possess wings. They are also deathly afraid of getting wet, and are no longer aware they have human forms. All of these are facets of the reimagining enforced by their constant exposure to their neighbors. Only Allfire remains aware of their real physical characteristics. Whenever a child is born, Allfire has it brought to the castle and feeds it a Heart from the mine; the villagers have come to believe the strange, tailless, pink babies they have appear as such because they have not yet been blessed by the king (and to an extent they are correct). Therefore, every dragon in Camelhot save Allfire and his first knights are Inheritors.

                        The vein of Orobori Hearts originally unearthed in the mine is a deep concern for the king; as he aged and researched his new power he became suspicious of the numerous quantity of Hearts--not a natural mineral deposit--buried in the earth of his valley. He has come to believe the mine was originally a mass grave or--more disturbingly--a trophy room for some unknown entity. He frets constantly about what would happen if the latter were true and its owner came back for its prizes. This is of greatest concern because Allfire unearthed a scepter within the mine with the peculiar property of being able to meld machinery with flesh.

                        Other Traits
                        Conviction [?] (Inklings of humanity)
                        Conditions:
                        - Leveraged (Allfire: serfdom)
                        - Reimagining (Denizen of Camelhot) - Flicker is green, has six limbs [four legs, two arms] and is wingless, like all other denizens of Camelhot
                        - Reimagining (Feeble, Brawn over Brains) - As an inventor Flicker is frequently mocked by the other denizens of Camelhot as being feeble and worthless; he suffers -2 to all Physical rolls but gains a beat upon succeeding.

                        Bio
                        Flicker, like other denizens of Camelhot, received his Heart directly from Allfire when he was just a baby. As such, he has no awareness of his human guise or his relationship to the world outside of the valley. He's an inventor on retainer in Allfire's castle, but his inventions go largely unnoticed because of the nature of the kingdom and its denizens. He has a knack for putting things together out of mundane components; even having built an automatic steam-powered dishwasher out of tea kettles, a candelabra, a leg warmer, a cup rack, and a mop.
                        Flicker is secretly infatuated with Allfire's daughter Flame, though he cannot interact with her in an official capacity due to his social standing. He loathes the notion that she will be a prize for one of the Square Table's knights and longs to find a way to spare her from that obligation.
                        His inventions have recently caught the intention of the court chancellor, who is conspiring with a group of humans beyond the valley to stage a coup and overthrow Allfire's reign.

I'd appreciate feedback on this, and remember that we're still open to changing things in the next 48 hours.


                                                Major Changes between Embers: 2nd Edition and Rekindled: Transitional Edition.
                                                (AKA, the most important parts of the book)

                                                New Focus
                                                Rather than being a game about a specific view of Leadership, Dragon Rekindled is a game about POWER in general:
                                                • Why you want it.
                                                • How you take it.
                                                • What you do with it.
                                                • And the monster (or savior) it turns you into...

                                                Also, we've added elements like...
                                                • Secret Lore which backs up what's going on and makes the whole thing much deeper and more mysterious.
                                                • Philosophy references from people who talked about power, like Nietzsche, Ayn Rand, and Anton LaVey.
                                                • Exploration of Fantasy from the meta-perspective of Concept itself: the kind of power the Oroboroi have is based entirely around bringing ideas into reality, both fantasies and hard facts.
                                                • More emphasis on themes like Power, Domination, Corruption, Hubris, Responsibility, Guilt, and The Inevitable Fall.


                                                New Merits
                                                • Maiden - A Mortal who knows the Oroboros for who they truly are, and can keep them from swinging too far down one path or another, as well as be a human accomplice in their supernatural dealings.
                                                • Cult - A group of Mortals who have some idea of what's going on, and follow the Oroboros as a divine leader.
                                                • PR Campaign - Allows an Oroboros to directly manipulate their Public Image for the Reimagining.
                                                • Contingency Plan - How well you've set up for the long haul: investments, identities, conspiracies, etc.
                                                • Dream Sorcery - 1 to 4 dots (I've posted it already)
                                                  • Oroboroi get it for free, but Mortals and other Supernaturals can use it as well.

                                                New Philosophies
                                                Methods are about how you take power, but Philosophies are about WHY you took it in the first place. They embody what is most important to an Oroboros when they first change.
                                                • Ash - Revolutionaries who thrive on the Destruction of whatever they don't like.
                                                • Blood - Honor-hounds who enforce their own morals.
                                                • Coin (new) - Materialists who believe that wealth and resources are the most important things in the world. This can mean many things, from a Banking Executive who hoards wealth, to the Communist revolutionary attempting to overthrow him, to the Charity Worker desperately trying to acquire the funds to keep the shelters running. All of them are accountants who see that wealth is what moves the world.
                                                • Sweat - Work Ethic, Industry, Inspiration, and Pride.
                                                • Bone - Symbols of stability, who are experts in holding things together.
                                                • Tears - Believe that emotions are really what matters.
                                                • Book (new) - Believe that knowledge is the greatest power, and that all problems can be solved with fact. They are occultists, scientists, thinkers, and teachers, and their signature Purview, Knowledge, deals with gaining, spreading, and secluding information.
                                                • Nihilism - Power without reason: they believe that there is nothing of worth in the universe, and have no reason to use their powers for anything but subversion of meaning itself. Not all of them are neccesarily bad, but among the highest leaders of the great organizations, they are considered a threat to everything an Oroboros should stand for.

                                                Note that schools of the 5 Evils have been shuffled back into the others, and the True Heart Schools are no longer relevant. (for now, at least...)

                                                Ablutions
                                                They haven't really changed much. The "odd-even" ones are now just boosts to both skills, and the new starting points are a little different:
                                                • 3 Points from the Common list
                                                • 3 Points from the Method list
                                                • 3 Points from any Oroboros list
                                                • 3 Points from the Storyteller due to Public Image

                                                New Ablutions are mainly following the rule that every list has...
                                                • A Type of Breath: Common is Fire, Slayers get Ice, Finders get a mist of Perception, Tricksters get "sleeping sand", and the other two haven't been fleshed out enough.
                                                • An Attribute-Booster which takes breath to boost the Method-Attribute for a scene.
                                                • Ablutions which are both positive and negative, because there was too much emphasis on Inherritors being good and Tricksters being bad. Now they all have potential for both.


                                                Changing Edicts to Purviews
                                                Functionally the same, and 90% unchanged, only more streamlined towards both positive and negative ends, and built to better reflect the Philosophies. "Due Process" is now "Justice" which allows an Oroboros to not simply hide a crime, but reveal it as well.

                                                Each Philosophy starts with a dot of its own specific purviews (as opposed to a single Edict you had to take) and one from any they chose.

                                                The signature Purviews for each Philosophy are currently...
                                                • Ash - Ruin, Agony, Hysteria, Rampage
                                                • Blood - Oath, Justice, Polygraph
                                                • Sweat - Industry, Agony, Property
                                                • Bone - Hippocratic, Assembly, Polygraph
                                                • Tears - Passion, Hysteria, Rampage, Trance
                                                • Wisdom - Knowledge, Polygraph, Trance
                                                • Materialism - Currency, Justice, Property
                                                • Nihilism - Void

                                                Heartstring replaces Furnace
                                                Power Stats represent the strength of the source of a character's supernatural powers, but an Oroboros's Heart doesn't actually change with them. The thing that does is how well they're connected to it: their Heartstring.

                                                Higher Heartstring means...
                                                • Larger breath pool, more breath per turn.
                                                • Stats can go above 5 once Heartstring does.
                                                • Greater Distance from the Heart
                                                  • Early on, you can maybe cross town.
                                                  • Later, you might be able to leave it on the other side of the county.
                                                  • At 10, you can go to the other side of the planet... assuming you don't leave the planet.
                                                • More potent Reimagining
                                                  • At 1 and 2, Ablution dots are on the table.
                                                  • 3, 4, and 5 add other physical characteristics to the chopping block.
                                                  • 6, 7, and 8 add skills, merits, personality quirks, and mannerisms.
                                                  • at 9 and 10, the very attributes and personality of an Oroboros can be altered.


                                                Oroboros Integrity: Conviction
                                                Conviction represents how the Oroboros feels about themselves, from the over-inflated extreme of Hubris, to the self-loathing Guilt. A Conviction of 5 represents an Oroboros who isn't going off the deep end of either extreme, however, each extremes have their own utility.

                                                Hubris has the benefit of strong Self-Image, which allows them to add every point above 5 against the Reimagining, but are prone to Hoarding, Rage, and other forms of Hubris. At the extremes of 9 and 10, an Oroboros has such a high image of herself, that she'll find it hard to resist the proposals of Challengers. Breaking points for this include anything that                                                 reinforces the idea that an Oroboros is a god, either through her own action or a reaction to her actions.

                                                Guilt has the benefit of desperation, and the Oroboros gains a free point of Willpower once per scene for any action which would trigger a breaking point towards Hubris (assuming she's drop below 4), but the drawback is the Reimagining becoming more potent as she spirals towards someone who may not even want to protect her own identity. At the extremes of 1 and 0, an Oroboros will begin seeking a new, better owner for her Heart, and will start looking for an Inherritor. Breaking points for this include any time an Oroboros fails to live up to the power she has willfully usurped from another.

                                                New Traits
                                                • Nutrition - Oroboroi need to eat enough to nutritionally sustain themselves before they can garner breath. This becomes more potent as Divinity rises.
                                                • Eating for Calories - The "refueling" roll is only required if an Oroboros consumes a non-food item for fuel, but not for broccoli.
                                                • Temperature - Oroboroi are now immune to extreme temperatures and fire, but can still be frozen solid.
                                                • Toxicity - Oroboroi blood is technically boiled every minute (that's how fast human blood circulates), meaning they're immune to anything that can't survive being supernaturally boiled, like most poisons and illnesses. That's just biology.
                                                • Heart Removal - Is no longer as big of an issue: an Oroboros can take it out to hide it somewhere for a day, or eat it again to keep it in their chest, but there's no huge emphasis on how "only a fool would take their one weakness and not put it somewhere where they can't keep an eye on it." An Oroboros can only travel a short distance from her Heart, depending on her Heartstring.
                                                • Immortality - Oroboroi are immortal. Period. They age relatively slowly, but always resurrect at the age they became an Oroboros at, As long as the form can be rebuilt by the Heart, they can resurrect at any time.
                                                • Weaknesses - Oroboroi are extremely susceptible to "Dark Magic" and Jade.

                                                And of course, the big new thing everyone seems to be asking about is...
                                                The Reimagining
                                                This is about the idea that others will always have some control how to use power, for better or for worse. Democracy, Public Opinion, Morale, Oversight... No man is an island, no matter how badly those with the most Hubris tell themselves.

                                                Here's how that works...
                                                1. Whenever an Oroboros feeds a point of Breath to keep the Heart beating, the Storyteller overlooks what their current "Public Image" is, to see what the potential changes are. Merits like Maiden, Cult, and PR Campaign can keep this in line.
                                                2. The roll is based on a combination (through addition) of 3 things: how many people believe this new image, how strongly they believe it, and how tangible it is (for instance, simply saying "I can imagine him as a pilot" and "I literally believe he has wings" are a 1 and a 5 respectively)
                                                3. The Oroboros resists based on Resolve + Composure + however far above 5 their conviction is. (So, Tyrannical Dragons tend to be better at resisting)
                                                4. If the Reimagining Roll - the Resistance Roll is more than 0, the Oroboros gets a 1-dot change to whatever was on the table. If it's an exceptional success, it's 2 dots.
                                                • Note that it becomes more potent at higher Heartstring, with low levels being ablutions, and higher levels being things like personality traits, HOWEVER, the Heart itself and things that are direct manifestations of the Heart (like the simple fact that they are an Oroboros) are permanent and cannot be affected by the Reimagining.


                                                Aaaand that's it for now. Expect a rough draft of the Transitional Rulebook in the next 48 hours...

                                                        Alright, here it is, the current draft of the Transitional Rulebook, with (almost) everything you'd need to move an Oroboros over from Dragon: The Embers - Second Edition. I'm not entirely happy with it, but we're not going to sit on it any longer. Expect some obvious holes in the draft now, including (at the worst parts) references to 2nd edition, and some patches in the coming weeks as we move onto the full, 10-chapter, rulebook, which will probably take a few months. We're open to any questions or comments on this right now, but please remember that this is neither a complete nor finalized set of rules, just a document containing a solid idea of the direction we're heading in.
