    As a general rule, I'd imagine mages to be the second most knowledgeable about any given metaphysical sphere, and beasts to be the second most knowledgeable about any given social sphere, just because they take an interest in such things. The idea that needs to go isn't about whether mages are knowledgeable or ignorant, but that being more broadly knowledgeable means they win in some way - if it's a competition, it's a competition only mages are taking part in, because none of the other care that much.

    At any rate, I imagine that supernaturals being known - and approached - for particular skill sets will be something that's addressed. The uppermost example in my mind is that Geist ceremony that lets you change the apparent cause of death of a body, right down to the forensic level. A sineater who's known to be able to do that will be on every supernatural's speed-dial in the city, especially since sineaters are often willing to work for nothing more outre than cash.

                            Who has had experience running crossover games with mages? And I mean more than just have a random supernatural show up in a Mage game - sure, mages are awesome at dealing with other supernaturals in a Mage-centric game. Lets talk about an actual a mixed party, or how about actual mages as full on antagonists in another game. Because I'm just hearing about a lot of theorycraft and guessing, and not a lot of personal experience on the matter.

                            I have run such games, and I've personally experienced a number of issues stemming from a number of different sources - thematically, mechanically, overlapping abilities that render other PCs feeling redundant, and so on. Introducing the Awakened into other games causes multiple problems from a number of angles, and mixed groups have niche issues that a mage can easily overwhelm.

                            I have talked with others who've done crossover games with mages, and the reports tend to be less than enthusiastic about mages being awesome in crossover games. In fact, the general consensus tends to lean towards disallowing mages more than anything.

                                        In 1st edition, I've been able to get Mage to play nicely with other games with a bit of house ruling (not even that much) and in general opening up options for other character types (allowing Vampires to make gargoyles and keep devotion creation open, let changelings talecraft, etc). Sure, Mages could generally twink a little better and had a wider array of tools, but it didn't ruin the game for anyone else.

                                        With 2nd edition, well, I can't see that working anymore, I admit. They no longer have just better utility; their powers are now superior at similar levels too (if they're willing to risk paradox), to the point of completely overriding other splats in the same field. In 1e, a Mage needed 4 dots of Mind to do what a Vampire could do with Dominate 2, but now they have a 2 dot spell that's far better than Dominate is (again, if enough Reach is spent), for instance, in addition to the half dozen other spells they get at that level (plus CT and attainments). Paradox is more of a risk now, but not enough to override the complete advantage they have in both breadth and depth.

                                        As someone who always loved including Mage in crossover in 1e, it does sort of upset me how the 2e developer mindset is "Fuck balance" ( / "balance is a myth" / however else they actually put it) with regards to crossover (and sometimes in the same game).

                                        Now, that said, that's a mechanical issue. From a story standpoint, I don't think there's any support for saying Mages are harder to integrate; everyone has a healthy distrust as the default, but I've never seen any evidence of Mages being moreso than anyone else. Yes, they're seen as arrogant. And vampires are backstabbing, and werewolves violent, and changelings xenophobic/paranoid. It's a stereotype. And not one that's any more universally pervasive, I don't think.

                                        So, my overall point, I suppose, is that Mage is a great game for crossover, and fits it in thematically better than many other games, from a story standpoint. It's just terrible for any PC-level characters of other venues involved, as they'll very quickly be overshadowed.

                                        EDIT: For what it's worth, I do like a large amount of the changes to the 'skeleton' of Mage's spellcasting, and a large number of other changes, it's just the actual balance and individual spells that I have issue with.
