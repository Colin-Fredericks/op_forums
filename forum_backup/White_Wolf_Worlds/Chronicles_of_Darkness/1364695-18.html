                                            Originally posted by Nicias View Post
                                            I'm pretty disappointed in the NM write-up honestly. I've never been able to understand the value of a (long-lived!) compact which is in open denial of what they deal with, and preferred as in Compacts and Conspiracies where they sound more like they just want to apply the scientific method to understanding the supernatural. But here we have a bunch of lines about we can prove everyone wrong and if NM succeeds than the Vigil can end (?!) and generally doubling down on the more ridiculous version of things.

                                            Is there some other way to see it? What am I missing? Is this just a fascinating satire of rationalism over empiricism or something that I keep not getting?
                                            From my reading of it, it is not that they want to prove that supernatural beings do not exist- what they want to prove is that they are not supernatural. That there is some theory or system or scientific way to categorize the darkness, and explaining how those species (not monsters!) exist. That magic is just a form of energy, and could be described through formulas and laws just like any form of energy. That other worlds could be explained through a system of equations. That the scientific method could be used even for the occult world, and by doing so explain it. And once you explain the supernatural as just a part of the system, you could use technology in order to keep up with it. You can develop tools and apparatuses and simulations which don't only explain how the darkness works, but can also predict it and utilize it. The Vigil will end not by destroying all monsters, but by showing they are not monsters- they are just a part of the natural world, and could be studied like any other aspect of it. And once the fire of knowledge is ignited, the fear from the dark vanishes. Oh, sure, those things you used to be fearful from are still there, but before the light you can study them, understand them, and utilize them. Once, lions and wolves and bears were monsters and gods who preyed upon humanity. Now, they are just one more species we need to protect from ourselves, for not only we understood them, we have also developed the tools to kill them, and the fear subsides.

                                            That is the end of the Vigil. That is the end of the supernatural. Whatever you were afraid of is still there, but through rational thought and scientific tools, you can understand it, and knowledge is the one thing that the darkness fears the most.

                                            At least, that's my reading of it.

                                                            Originally posted by mlvalentine View Post
                                                            Thanks, Primordial newcomer. The history of compacts and conspiracies began to change when the Dark Eras line was added, much like DE affected Geist, too. All of a sudden global conspiracies either needed to have more history than they did or they had to be newer; compacts could (but not always) be temporary due to historical events/tragedies and changes over time. Knowing this, I wanted Hunter 2E to be a corebook that reflected that change while not fixating on Dark Eras. There were tons of compacts/conspiracies created as First Edition progressed, and I felt it made sense they'd be absorbed or dissolved because there are more denizens now in the Chronicles of Darkness than there was when Hunter 1E debuted.
                                                            Problem is not only have you thereby created a metaplot consisting mostly of “Your fave is canonically gone now,” but one that, without the benefit of this post to know the actual reasoning, reads (IMO) a lot like someone got it in their head somehow that any compact/conspiracy without a 2e corebook writeup has to have an in-universe reason why it didn’t get one.

                                                            How much consideration was given to the option of instead making up new compact/conspiracy names to drop as the examples of ones that have come and gone over time?
