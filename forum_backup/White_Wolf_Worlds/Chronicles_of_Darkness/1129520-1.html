            I didn't get to try it, yet, but I am quite fond of this writeup nonetheless. More monstrousness, more focus on what being a monster entails.


            To make Vampires more heroic is less simply a matter of motivation and actions, in my opinion. Keep the monstrosity in, though, to add drama, tragedy, and give an additional layer to the characters.
            You will, invariably, end up with some sort of Anti-Hero, of course. Less hacks, more plothooks:

            The 'Friendly Neighbourhood Vampires' try to atone for the lives they've taken by patroling the area and stopping any crime they meet. Of course, this news will spread, and attract people that emulate them, fans that want to see them. How will they feel when the first imitators die in trying to be like their heroes? What will go through their mind when their fans get endangered, not by some thug, but by a member of the coterie frenzying?

            The Coterie feed exclusively on drug dealers, human traffickers and murderers - this way, they can keep telling themselves that they aren't bad people, that they are in control, that what they are doing is right - that they are not monsters. How long will they be able to keep the illusion alive?

            You could encourage this kind of behavious by giving them a bonus to feeding rolls when NOT drinking from the innocent, or a bonus to their Humanity safe roll, or a bonus to frenzy checks.

            You could make something like a True Love bond mechanic: drinking from your true love causes them ecstasy, and restores willpower. In addition, this blood is much richer in power - you need less blood, thus can feed more often on them. In return, however, , feeding from anyone else means you cause them incredible pain, AND you need more blood than usual: every feeding is fatal.
            Furthermore, your true love shares extreme emotions you experience, can always tell where you are, and spending a instant action, can see through your eyes. How will they react when their lover starts feeling joy upon killing evil-doers, how will they react when they experience just what it means to be, feel and think inhumanely? What will they do when frenzy takes them, too?

                    Originally posted by Wormwood View Post
                    I didn't get to try it, yet, but I am quite fond of this writeup nonetheless. More monstrousness, more focus on what being a monster entails.


                    To make Vampires more heroic is less simply a matter of motivation and actions, in my opinion. Keep the monstrosity in, though, to add drama, tragedy, and give an additional layer to the characters.
                    You will, invariably, end up with some sort of Anti-Hero, of course.

                    <snippity>
                    That's a very interesting set of hacks you reference there, and I'd love to hear if anyone has actually tried them or developed their thinking further in the ~1 year since posting. It's... rather extreme to me as hacks go and the OP references a desire to discuss hacks that veer toward the heroic side.

                    Ok. First of all, as others reference, drinking blood and flinging around powers drawn from base human impulses of domination, slavery and rape do not make for heroic characters. That can make for anti-heroes, however, and most players I think would rather view their own characters as anti-heroes within their own story. I'll just leave that assertion on the table, I've neither known a player pleased to run an unjustified moral evil for long, nor would I want to rub shoulders with one.

                    Key word here is "unjustified": vampires are excellent liars, especially to themselves, so their play is in part a matter of exploring to what extent they are willing to lie to themselves (let alone others) as they dance down the primrose path of utilitarianism before looking up at the ruins of all they care for and love. In other words "how many corners do you cut to be the hero of your own story?" Choosing on your first night to feed instead of walking into the sun is the first step on that path, of turning people into objects and paving your road with them.

                    To me, then, the first "hack" to Vampire is figuring out for the campaign and the characters what is going on that makes them heroes--or is it really a matter of "survive one more night in a world gone (personally, supernaturally) mad?" That kind of Tier 1 experience is great and can make for a tight story, or blossom into another style once the characters get a handle on themselves. The converse: Blade superheroes and even less introspective trenchcoat ninja monster-of-the-week action imply that the characters have (long ago) worked past their moral, supernatural lifestyle issues and decided dealing with the external monster is sufficient to justify their existence. If your table goes for that, then cool.

                    So what does "setting hacks" mean, then? Things that help shove aside the moral introspection built into the system? Things that veer the story toward teen angst and sparkles? Supermen with fangs? Marvel Anti-Heroic Role-Playing?

                    Anyway, here is reference to one suggestion on daywalking with a contribution inside it. The the net effect is to enable vampires to wander about during the day to one degree or another, which significantly changes many of the trope dynamics and can make them more like Superman-by-night/Kent-by-day if you like. I'm intending to try it a bit and see what happens. Otherwise, consider that so long as the players/characters can point to a bigger and meaner bad than them, they can justify or handwave around the morally reprehensible parts of kindred existence long enough to come up with a "fix" of some sort. That's pretty much the whole point of the Ordo Dracul, unless you're into knowledge-for-knowledge sake.

                    --Khanwulf

                                        Originally posted by Malus View Post
                                        For vampires to be heroic you basically need to take away all the elements of vampirism that make it a curse. Heroes, as they were first conceived by romanticism are infallible. Which turns them into Mary Sues. Which is why you get such a negative connotation out of this idea.
                                        Go further back, though, and you end up with tragic, curse-plagued and disaster-stricken heroes like Heracles or (more recent, maybe) Siegfried. Those would work well with a vampire looking to redeem himself, no?
                                        Or take modern human superheroes: Batman is basically a deeply-traumatized boy throwing a tantrum and enacting his own therapy by punishing people by proxi, Tony Stark is arrogant, vaine and an addict incapable of forming and keeping bonds, a realistic Steve Rogers would most likely have tons of problems adjusting to a bunch of changes due to having out-of-touch values, and Peter Parker has an enormous guilt-complex.
                                        Highlighting these qualities is enough to make them usable concepts, I think.

                                        Camilla You want a Hero story? Fine, easy enough. But don't go superhero - go for a more personal route. You wan't something that jives with Golconda? Okay, how about this: Go full on Campbell's Monomyth. Take a look at the Hero's Journey:

                                        Don't adhvere slavishly to it. But use this as a kind of blueprint.
                                        My take on this, with what little I know, would be: Start with your human characters, Call them to Adventure via the Embrace. Have them delve into Vampire Society, an exhilarating and promising, if scary experience, then the corners and edges of their new unlife show more and more and start wearing on them. They are not able to lie to themselves about the fact that this is a curse, that bad things can and will happen if this keeps one. Make them want to search out Golconda. How do they achieve it? How about not by doing great things, save lifes, but by acts of compassion, of courage, of humbleness, generosity, wisdom and honour: essentially striving for the best of humanity, not for heroics - take a note from Disney's Hercules if you want to. Want some heroic deeds? Go make them reduce Kindred influence on society, challenge those that prevent access to the knowledge of Golconda, rip out the parasite that is Kindred society wholly if you want to. But make sure that caring for that one victim, with warmth, with love, that ignoring their own goals for the benefit of that homeless guy is what brings them forwards. I'm sure you can fluff it up any way you want to.
                                        The important thing is: When they achieve it, they will have overcome their own monstrous nature and become better human beings. What higher mark of a hero than to overcome the darkness inside?

                                        And, to be honest, a powerful curse only makes the fight harder, and the victory that much sweeter in this case. But that's just my personal preference.
