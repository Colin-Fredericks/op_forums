                                06-21-2016, 11:16 AM
                                So I've got a lot of feelings about the review, and how people have responded to it, and the game itself. I'm going to try to write all of my thoughts up in a way that's hopefully concise and follows from point to point, but it's gonna be a long post.

                                Characters that you Hate

                                I don't know if everybody's familiar with the TV series Archer. It's a comedy about a bunch of spies working for a government agency. It's mostly about their interpersonal relationships and how awful they are as people. Anybody who thinks "Man, I'd love to be Archer/Lana/whomever." is either saying that they want to be a petty, vain, asshole or that they don't get the show. The creator himself has said that he wouldn't want any of the characters as friends. There are a lot of classic, popular shows like this: Seinfeld, It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia, Friends, the list goes on. Beast is sort of like that: The characters, more than almost any other CofD line, come across as the meanest, most petty. Granted, this is partially because some assholes subscribe to a "You're whoever you are on your worst day" mentality, so a Begotten is never treated as more than their most petty, small-time feeding. Or for whatever reason, people make a big deal out of their big meals.

                                The part of the review where I realized I wasn't going to agree with them is when they asked "Who would want to play as one of them?" and "Who could ever think a beast is a good person?" Because those questions miss the point.* And it surprised me because y'all are such big fans of the CofD, because none of the games are designed with the assumption that people are going to want to make sympathetic, good characters (even Hunter). The entire Chronicles of Darkness system is based around personal horror. Beast is the logical extreme of that assumption: these characters are assholes. The point of it all, the main draw of this game -- for me at least -- is that more than any other game, beasts are set up to be awful people. The beauty of the game is that I can do whatever I want from there. All of the Children I've made so far are people who have really unfortunate urges trying to leverage their urges in productive, or at least non-destructive ways. But nothing about the game says that I have to make them that way. I could also make a character who goes straight-up Patrick Bateman, Hannibal Lecter, or Pamela Vorhees. There's no judgment either way. And as a player, I think this is a great thing.

                                *The point being that not all games assume that people want to be the characters they're playing. I don't want to be any of the characters that I play in either my WoD or CofD games. Maybe my Scion character, though she's had to suffer all of her friends and family dying except for the other PCs.

                                Does the book act like Begotten are hot shit? Of course! So does Vampire: The Requiem 2E. It's like it was written by an Anne Rice fan who found out vampires are real and got Embraced and is dead and loving it. Werewolf makes it sound like being an Uratha is nothing short of high-octane awesomeness. It's the job of these books to try to sell the reader on wanting to play their subjects. But that's not the same thing as wanting to be the characters. The most relevant quote from the book is from the Introduction:

                                Originally posted by Beast: The Primordial, page 18, "No Neat Little Boxes"
                                Beasts are not “good guys.” They terrify people in order to feed their Horrors. Although their culture teaches that they do so in order to impart important lessons, their peculiar form of pedagogy is entirely optional. A Beast is capable of being a force for wisdom, even for “good” in the Chronicles of Darkness, but doing so is entirely up to the Beast. Likewise, a Beast’s primary enemy, the Hero, believes that killing the Beast is the right, objectively moral thing to do. The Beast disagrees. Neither of them is necessarily wrong.
                                People reading this book should be keeping this in mind even when the book is going on about how awesome Lairs are, and how Beasts are inheritors to awesome responsibility and legacy, and etc...etc....

                                No Metaphor Stands Up to Scrutiny

                                Hey, I get some people being uncomfortable about the LGBT subtext. But I can name some other popular franchises that act as metaphors for real-world oppressed groups: X-Men and True Blood. The vampires of the Sookieverse are quite obviously stand-ins for the LGBT community, but they're also literally blood-sucking monsters, even the ones who seem sympathetic at first. X-Men's classic movers and shakers, Professor X and Magneto, are stand-ins for Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. and Malcolm X. But that falls apart because Professor X raises child soldiers in a cult-like environment, and Magneto is a genocidal tyrant. But we can still borrow elements from the real world to help sell an idea, even if it's not perfect.

                                The closest that Beast as a game gets to saying "Beasts are an oppressed minority" is "The majority tries to put all Beasts in the "Evil monster" box because it fits their narrative, and some try to enact simple, violent solutions to the "beast problem." Beast's disagree." I get that some players will also disagree, that was made very clear to me when I read RPG.net's threads about Beast. And if that's the case, then Beast isn't the game for you. This isn't a bad thing, no game is for everybody. The issue I have is when people make bullshit claims like, "There's nothing redeeming about this" or "This is an objective statement about the game's quality, we're just being honest." when it's clearly an issue of preference.

                                Heroes, The Monomyth, and Subversion vs Aversion

                                So there was a line in the review where one of the hosts said, "You can't go on and on about the monomyth and then tell the Storyteller to just ignore it."

                                Why not? The whole point is that the monomyth has no bearing on the real world, and Heroes are just random assholes who show up thinking that Beasts need to die. There's no great narrative that Beasts are a part of. Same with Heroes.

                                Hey, let's talk about Subversion vs Aversion. Beast averts the monomyth by asserting that Beasts are more than just the "boss monster" for the Hero to slay. In play, you're just playing people trying to survive. Nothing epic or grand here. Antagonistic Heroes think they're part of a monomyth, and so from a Hero's perspective when they show up to slay the beast thinking it's all part of a bigger narrative it's a subversion. Hence the Subversion parts of the book. But from a Beast perspective, it's an Aversion because Beasts know that they're not a part of a greater narrative.

                                Speaking of Heroes, one of the issues the book has is shutting down the idea of friendly Heroes. But it also works into the "default Beast story", which I approve the game of having. Like, nobody's gonna stop you from having a Beast/Hero buddy cop story -- I made a post about the TV series Lucifer and how it could work as a Beast game -- but that's not the intent. There's nothing wrong with an author saying, "I want you to play my game in a specific way." Matt thinks that allowing playable Heroes and introducing sympathetic, friendly Heroes goes against the intent of the game. There's nothing wrong with that. The four example Heroes are fantastic because they're (mostly) sympathetic, but unlikely to be friendly. Even the MRA one is fine, because they wanted an example of somebody we could all hate without feeling too bad.

                                Put another way: Strix are supposed to be used as Enemy Within antagonists who tear a vampiric society apart from the inside thanks to propaganda and preying on Kindred fears. But they're powerful enough that honestly you could have a Strix who just possesses a Kindred, or an Uratha, or whatever, and works its way through Kindred society just murdering them all like Jason. The books give you advice on how to use them as subversive, guerilla monsters that tear things apart. The intent is to use them in a specific way. Same here with Heroes.

                                And again, that can be not to your liking: I know lots of people who hate it when games tell them what kind of games to play, or what stories to tell. But that's a preference issue.

                                Wrapping it Up

                                So yeah, this went long. But I hope it explains my disappointment with the review. The book probably needed another editorial pass: it's got some inconsistencies that need addressing, particularly when it comes to how the book treats Lessons. But saying that there's nothing redeemable and calling for people to boycott buying it is...it's not the objective "Let's talk about the real problems." review that you guys claim.

                                    For me personally, I don't need to play or have players play good guys. But the character has to be sympathetic. Beasts to me are not. They do evil things. Either because they were born that way, and then bemoan those who wish to stop them (and then we are told in a matter fact way Heroes are wrong - I am of the school of moral grey areas in all parts of CofD, since this is why I don't play WoD anymore). Or they chose to be a Beast, because they were ostracised for some reason from society, and so feel the right to then do awful things - which in turn to me makes them more awful and unsympathetic. And lessons is this thing tacked out to stroke those egos. As much as I like running games and playing through stories of moral choices and seeing the descent to evil, playing out and out monsters is not fun.

                                    As any long time listener to the show would know, they know the sorts of games we run and stories we run. And thus if they like that, and understand our position in reviews, then they should also understand the dislike of this game we have, and our dislike at how the rewrite was handled and the fanbase was managed. As a backer, I am angry more wasn't done to address those concerns. That is where the anger comes from.

                                    If however, you feel that the game is not for us, then our review still stands. If people like the types of games we like, then our review is valid for helping them make an informed decision. Not all games are made equal. The choice is still yours.

                                    If you then feel the need to sell the game to us still, and be a cheer leader for it, that says more about strength of your own conviction for the game. I fully acknowledge people dislike Requiem, but I will only do the hard sell if their dislike is from a position of ignorance, or worse some sort of edition war elitism, not if they simply say, that they prefer the style of the one over the other.

                                            Rather than have Chris speak for all of us, I'll chime in here myself. Hi, I'm Matt, and I did most of the heavy lifting on the Beast review.

                                            The problem with the “No Neat Little Boxes” Paragraph is that the rest of the book more or less ignores it. If Clanbook Baali opened up with a disclaimer saying that they were actually really nice people would that make the rest of the book any less horrific? There are no examples given in the book of a Beast helping someone out through their feeding. There are no examples given in the book of a person’s life being meaningfully improved by a Beast’s Existence. There are, however, several times given when Heroes are demonized. The book equates them to Gamergate, MRA’s, and Internet Trolls. It describes their mindset in absolute terms. More importantly it also states that they were born this way and actually changing their mind is a monumental task compared to just killing the poor fuckers. Meanwhile the “Good” Heroes are so amazingly uninteresting the book says not to use them and doesn’t provide rules for them.

                                            The reason why Beasts aren’t sympathetic is that the book doesn’t portray them as such. There isn’t really any evidence of Beasts fighting against their darker nature, they embrace it wholeheartedly. Instead being a Beast is described as the most amazing thing ever with only the slight downside of summoning acceptable murder targets whenever you don’t keep your hunger in check. (You yourself said that you loved the gamergate guy because you were ‘allowed’ to hate him). And Dexter is an example of a “Good Beast” But he’s a very narrow one. Also Dexter owns up to his own aberrant nature and monstrosity, and doesn’t once even hint at ‘teaching lessons’. He’s not putting the bodies on display, he’s not making examples of them, he’s removing them from the equation so that the rest of society can keep on turning.


                                            As for my assumption about Demon. Giving Beasts and Demon no mechanical interaction beyond enforced antagonism is a pretty good indicator that you don’t want them to interact. Which goes against the “No Little Boxes” thing yet again. Beasts hate Demons and Demons hate Beasts end of story.


                                            As far as Promethean’s the fiction anthology is fairly explicit that having a Beast in their life is actively detrimental to the Promethean. Beasts are cheerleaders of the inhuman, they want to drive Prometheans to become the world destroying monsters they were ‘meant’ to be. Sure that creates conflict and extends the game length but I’m not sure why, in character, a Promethean would continue to willingly associate with the creepy guy that keeps insisting you should go kill some conservative soccer moms who hate him. The fact that they’re immune to disquiet should be a giant red flag to the Promethean of just how inhuman they are.
