                    Here's the fluff and a mechanics explaining what Leviathan vs Leviathan conflict look like. (Note, these rules apply to Opheons but not Typhons).


                    The Tribe face conflict with each other all the time. Whether it's secluded waterfront property, Heirlooms, control over isolated towns, or the attention of Atolls, Leviathans share very specific needs, often the supply is limited. There's isn't enough room for two big fish in a small pond. Those who have must defend themselves from the have nots, and the have nots often find the easiest way is to take from the haves.

                    When they're not fighting over resources the Tribe fight over theology or morality. A Tranquil Leviathan may object to a tyrannical deposit or horrific occult experimentation upon cultists. Two equally monstrous Leviathans might find themselves disgusted by the form of the other's evil. Theologically, a contradicting view on the Progenitors can be the cause belli for war. It often doesn't matter if both Leviathans made it up a month ago, a Leviathan might let a slight against genuine primordial truth slide but feel compelled to defend a self affirming lie least they can no longer convince themselves.

                    Both the Leviathan's Bestial and Divine natures are instinctively opposed to the idea of Transforming and charging in a trashing maelstrom of teeth and claws, while the human nature is usually the least violent of the three. Like an animal the Bestial nature knows how to fight without escalating to any real risk of harm, and it instinctively prefers to loose than to risk injury or death. The divine nature also avoids direct combat, but for different natures. The Tribe's primordial divinity is expressed through power and dominance, the divine nature wants it's foes to remain alive and aware that they have lost, that they are lesser than it. Being so heavily dominated by their instincts conflicts between Leviathans follow predictable patterns and can be decided into three forms, with numerous subdivisions.

                    The first and simplest form is simply talking it out. If the Leviathans are on good terms they may meet in person, often mixing conversation with animal like threat displays or pitting their cults against each other in contests. If things are tense they may use telepathy, text messaging, send Beloved back and forth with letters or some other way of keeping things peaceful through distance. The more tense things are the more impersonal the conversation becomes.
                    • Use the social manoeuvring rules. Bonus dice may be applied if your cultists win the contest, your messenger is wearing ostentatious displays of wealth, if you've transformed into a more powerful body than your opponent. A more distant method of communication provides fewer or no opportunities for modifiers, but unless things are tense insisting on text messaging is likely to be taken as an insult.
                    • The looser may give the winner what they want, offer an alternative, or they may Escalate. Escalation resets both parties Doors, but the winner gains an extra Door for the remainder of the conflict. If a conflict Escalates multiple times, extra Doors stack and both parties retain any extra Doors until the conflict is resolved. Note that Escalation does not have to be to the next stage, nor does it have to wait until the final Door is opened. Any Leviathan can go as far as they wish, whenever they wish.


                    The second and most common form of Leviathan conflict is fighting through their Cults and spiteful sabotage. Beloved are sent to steal from the other Leviathan, to interfere with their plans, or sent battles. The aim of these is to say “look, I am stronger, don't let this turn into a real fight, because you'd loose”. Players who wish to get into their character’s headspace could try thinking of it as a particularly vicious divorce where the aim is simply to punish the other party.
                    • In this stage Leviathans open their rival's Doors by making a successful Fervour or Piety action to directly challenge their rival. If the two Cults come into direct conflict and the aggressor wins a Door opens. Alternatively if a Leviathan faces a significant setback to an Aspiration and their rival can demonstrate responsibility a Door opens.
                    • As before Leviathans have the opportunity to Escalate the conflict instead of admitting defeat. They may either move to the third and final stage, alternatively they can just advance through the subdivisions. Those subdivisions are:
                      • Non-violent but hostile conflict. E.G. Destroying that Heirloom your rival is looking for.
                      • Involving mortal society. E.G. Telling a Beloved's parent where to find their child.
                      • Non-lethal violence between cultists. E.G. Having your Beloved break into their Beloved's home and administer a beating.
                      • Non-lethal violence targeted against a Leviathan's sane friends, family, or favourites among non-combatant Beloved.
                      • Potentially lethal violence between your cults: E.G. Pitched battles in the streets of Insmouth. Your Beloved look for opportunities to knife their cultists in dark alleyways without the alerting the police.
                      • Potentially lethal violence targeted against a Leviathan's sane friends, family, or favourites among non-combatant Beloved.


                    The third and final stage is the simplest. The Leviathans want each other dead. A Leviathan may choose to Escalate to this level. However there are many ways Leviathans may find themselves here without wanting to: If a Leviathan inflicts (directly or via pawns) even a single point of Lethal damage during the same scene as a conflict, catches another Leviathan directing the Marduk Society or an Ahab towards them, or harming an Atoll then they instantly escalate to the third stage and take the Ultimate Enmity Condition.

                    Sidebar: What’s a Conflict

                    Unfortunately for the Tribe the term Conflict is broadly defined. If a Leviathan is thrown around during an Earthquake and their spiny body lands on another Leviathan that's fine. But if they were in the middle of an argument or a game of chess before the earthquake struck they take the Ultimate Enmity Condition. If a friendly boxing match results in wrap-around damage they take the Ultimate Enmity Condition.

                    The Tribe tend to play it very safe. As a rule, even good friends will remain two bodies length apart at all times unless they have a reason not too. If they are going to have a conflict they try to avoid being in the same building, you can play chess over the internet. Even arch-enemies often collaborate to avoid escalating too far.

                    Condition: Ultimate Enmity (Persistant)

                    Both your Divine and Beastal nature want another Leviathan dead, and nothing will convince them otherwise.

                    If you and the other Leviathan are in the same scene you must choose fight or flight or pay one willpower per turn you're doing neither.

                    If you choose flight you must run from the scene at your maximum speed, you may not use less instinctive methods of transportation like a car. It's running, swimming, or entering the Rift. You may also change to fight if you're cornered.

                    If you choose fight you may take no action except Transforming into a more powerful form or attacking your opponent with your natural weapons. You may use any Channel or Birthright that will help you in combat and you may give your Beloved orders. You may also change to flight if the battle is going against you.

                    If your rival is not present in the scene but you have an opportunity to thwart their plans, and you do not make an attempt to do so or an attempt to withdraw your resources further away from your rival, then instead of gaining a point of Willpower after sleeping you loose a point.

                    Beat: The conflict causes a serious hardship.

                    Resolution: One Leviathan dies. This requires true death. If a Leviathan reincarnates, time travels back to before their rival dies, or anything of that nature occurs, the Condition reappears.

                                                        I have no idea why it's called Sheol, the only definition for the word I can find is the Hebrew afterlife, so I assume there's supposed to be some connection. I can think of links, but the person who first suggested it gave no reason and it was accepted by the first contributes for some reason.

                                                        In practice though. Sheol is a Leviathan's connection to the Tribe. Any Leviathan can say they're a Leviathan and having divine power is neat, but it takes a lifetime of shared experiences, of commiserating over shared history, to truly build a connection. And since a Leviathan's power flows down through untold generations, the stronger a Leviathan's connection to the Tribe and it's history the more power flows. (No, this doesn't mean that the Tribe get weaker over time. This is a sympathetic link like Knowledge. In fact some Leviathans believe Knowledge of Tiamat and the Progenitors is the source of Sheol, but it's debatable at best).

                                                        A Sheol 1 and Sheol 10 Leviathan might both lack any strong sense of what being a Leviathan is. But the Sheol 10 Leviathan would strongly relate and empathise with all the other Levaithans, alive long gone, who faced similar issues to the ones he's facing.


                                                        If you want a real world example to help. Look at the Jewish community. That je ne sais quoi that let the Jewish identity survive both oppression and integration is a good example of Sheol. (Complete with endless questions about what does it mean to be a Jew/Leviathan)
