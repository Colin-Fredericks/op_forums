                    The use of a wooden stake through the heart was purely symbolic and owes most of its fame to the idea of Vlad the Impaler (emphasis on impaler) being destroyed by the same means he was famous for employing. Other myths required that an iron spike be driven through the heart, nailing the vampire to the ground and imprisoning (not destroying) him there. Another involved burying him at a cross roads so he wouldn't know which way to go and his confusion would prevent him from rising again. The use of holy water and wafers was associated with the power of god to purify and destroy evil. Flame is the same, consuming and purifying whatever it touches. The writers of the game just chose the most commonly known methods (thanks to Bram Stoker), then added the possibility of the others as additional banes taken by blood lines. The heart, head or whatever is based upon the symbolic beliefs of the people they come from. As for the head, medically it wasn't all that long ago they though the brain was just packing material, the heart was the focus of life. More people survived head injuries (including a four foot metal rod through the cranium) then damage to the heart.
                    It was a tamping rod, he worked for the railroad. It was his subsequent change in personality that led to the discovery that the brain was home to the way you think.​

                        The man you're talking about was named Phineas Gage, and the reason his case was so significant is that it effectively settled the cerebral localization debate that was still going on at the time. In other words, the midical community was well aware by that time that the brain was the seat of consciousness, they were merely arguing over whether all parts of the brain were equally important in all cognitive function, or if certain parts were responsible for certain tasks. The fact that Gage survived the destruction of a significant portion of his frontal lobe with seemingly little resulting imparment was in and of itself strong evidence in favor of the hypothesis that brain function is localized. But more importanly, his personality changed drastically after the injury, which indicated that the frontal lobe must be largely responsible for personality. This line of thought is what lead to the invention of the frontal lobotomy.
