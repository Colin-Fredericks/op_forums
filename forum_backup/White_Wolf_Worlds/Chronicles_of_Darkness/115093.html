    Help request : the Wilds

    04-17-2014, 11:57 PM
    Greetings all,

    I'll be imminently storytelling a game session which will involve the pack traveling deep into the wilderness of the Rocky Mountains, and their spirit wilds parallel. I have a few ideas of my own, but this seemed like a good time to see what recommendations the community had for some really evocative horrors that dwell off the map.

    I don't like to do straight-up help requests, generally; I prefer to present them as discussion threads to refine an idea - it has more long-term benefit to the community that way, at least in theory. So it is my intention to turn this into an Antagonist Collection -style thread once a few solid write-ups have been finished. I unfortunately don't have any new ideas developed enough to present right now, and I'll be starting this session on the 20th [its not like I'm drawing a blank, but I'd definitely appreciate help adding to what I have to really make it a dangerous, scary trip], so... it's just a help request right now. But I will be re-adapting this to a general thread soon(ish).


    My chronicle is a Forsaken one, but its open to the broader World of Darkness (we have a Changeling and a Thaumaturge as PCs, for instance). It is a pretty high level game (~625 exp). Tough antagonists and large groups, from any corner of the nWoD and fan ideas all interest me.
    There was some talk on the old forums [primarily by Papa Bear and Dreamweaver, as least as I recall] about introducing a type of Beast antagonist as a "fourth pillar" of Forsaken enemies (an animal-based enemy, to go with the human, spirit, and werewolf ones); that would be of particular interest to me, if anyone has done work on that, or on expanding Demon's Cryptids.
    The other type of suggestion I'd really appreciate are spirit choirs for the wilds: the kinds of spirit activity and alliances which occur in the dark parts of the mountains where there is no werewolf pack to curb their activity (and perhaps never was). In territories like the one I want to use, you can never have enough ideas for spirits like these.


    Thanks in advance for any suggestions,
    Seraph Kitty

          Ghods, I do not remember kicking around an idea for animal-based enemies, but it's something I hope I contributed to, because it's a good idea.

          I can definitely see some cryptids wandering around the Rockies. There's still a whole lot of open spaces, national parks, and mountain regions that no one wants to try to claim. And they would have to be a hearty bunch. The open, moderate temperature areas are going to be covered in farms and ranches, so they have to go to higher elevations to stay clear of people. Local versions of sasquatch and wendigo, skinwalkers are from this region, too. And, of course, what probably sparked the idea of the animal adversaries, there are lots and lots of legends of over-sized, overly aggressive versions of the local wildlife. Top contenders are man-eating bears, baby-killing coyotes and cougars, and even bucks and elk who have fought back against the hunters.

          As far as the kinds of spirits that are getting together, first thing is all the pain, isolation, desperation and death spirits. Every time some skier or hiker goes off the trail, gets their dumb-asses lost/hurt/killed, these guys all get together. And lemme tell ya, certain times of the year, we usually don't go a week without hearing about one of these Darwin Award Winners. You remember that movie about the guy who took his own arm off to survive? That was the 3rd or 4th time his fool ass had almost died while out hiking. Add to that all the nature spirits in the area: trees, snow, mountain, rocks, some grasses and flowers. The snow, mountain, rock and tree spirit are often in on these deaths, since getting caught under a rock slide, avalanche or just hitting a damn tree is what usually gets these fools hurt and/or lost in the first place. Then there's the small host of animals who'll feed of the carcases, who will have their own little ravenous spirit reflections tagging along. And that's all the potential spirits just for one lone idiot.

          Then there's the every day spirits hanging around. The animals (bears, rabbits, racoons, squirrels, deer, elk, cougars, fish, hawks, ducks, geese), the nature spirits (see above), and the different abstract spirits. You get a lot of different feelings when dealing with being in the mountains. Sometimes you get the Purple Mountains Majesty feeling of awe and beauty. Some people are looking at the high cliff walls and thinking about what a long way down it is into the rivers below. Some people are looking at them as a place to run and hide or to escape other people or to be free to do what they want.

                  I'm glad to help with this because, as a local, I know I can give more accurate info for everybody. Keep in mind, I was giving you just info on the more uninhabited areas, since it looked like that's what you're looking for. And while I have nothing against the gang at WW/OP, the Hunting Grounds: Rockies book was kind of misleading about a lot of things and I remember that when it first came out, I spent a lot of time of the forums trying to correct those impression.

                  For instance, the mountains do not just shoot up out of the ground 1 mile outside of Denver. You have quite a few miles from Denver to get into the foothills (which most Americans from east of the Mississippi would call "mountains") and a few more miles west of that, then they start that steep slope up to the 14,000 foot peaks. There are lots of little towns dotting the countryside, but there are usually some very big stretches in between, so your players could have some decent home base areas to fall back to, however....supplies will be limited. There won't be a local Wal-Mart, but there could be a general store, maybe an ammo store (could be the same place, too), a post office and at least one guy who's "Doc". Now, don't get me wrong, they still live in the modern world. "Doc" can patch some wounds and set a bone, and then he'll call Flight for Life to get them out of there.

                  That actually leads me to a big thing around here - distance and traveling. Not a lot of people out here blink twice at taking an hour to get somewhere. I drive about 20 miles and it takes about 45 minutes for me to go to work in the morning and home again at night. And I don't even live in the mountains right now. The next two over could be an hour by winding highway. In the mountain areas, there aren't a lot of direct paths to anywhere, since that would involve going over or thru the mountains, so the paths tend to go along the sides of the mountains. That means wolves can cover some distances faster than a car, but boy will their legs be tired.

In territories like the one I want to use, you can never have enough ideas for spirits like these.
                      I have run a high powered game and while I am sure my antagonists won't quite fit in with yours, I found that some of my most effective scenarios came not as a result of a big bad, but what happens when Uratha simply trespass into areas they are not made to go. While there might be a few strong spirits such as Greater Jagglings, they have massive choirs built up of simply gaffling level.

                      Again, that isn't really the point since otherwise it would be a straight dungeon crawl/raid boss encounter. The real trick is when the spirit wilds themselves make absolutely no sense to the players and everything there screams at them to leave it alone. For example, I had my players attempt to find the ban of a Greater Jaggling in the depths of the Shadow as a direct attack on it would be a horrible idea thanks to it having insinuated itself into the workings of nearly a dozen humans. If he died, they would too and that is obviously bad.

                      Their journey lead them into an ancient spiritual forest that overtime took on the resonances of silence, fear, mystery, lost, loneliness, isolation, and despair. Combined the resonances built up and my players rapidly became lost, separated, susceptible to Kuruth, and more than a little terrified by the fact that when fear, isolation, and despair start kicking in, hallucinations begin to manifest similar to how a parched man in the desert may see a tranquil oasis.

                      I had my players begging any and every spirit they thought they saw for mercy to leave all while never knowing if what they saw was a manifestation of their own terror, or a real spirit wanting to cash in for a favor. I never needed a single antagonist in that encounter and I brought an entire pack to its' knees
