      Knowledge of the theory would fall under Academics. In general, it seems more like using a lot of Wits and Intelligence rolls paired with appropriate skills, than relying on one skill.           I would recommend a mix of optional Skills for the Legacy. There are many different styles of military commander, but they should be mostly Mental and Social, obviously. Maybe with Survival thrown in. Maybe make it so the character must nominate their "style" at initiation and use the appropriate Skill then. Alternatively, just scrap the Skill requirement and create (a) custom Merit(s) members of the Legacy have to buy up instead of getting Skill dots.

          If I had to decide on resolving an entire battle as a single roll, I would opt for an opposed Intelligence/Wits + Academics for which lacking an appropriate Speciality levies a -3 penalty. In some specific circumstances this might be replaced with Investigation or Science, for example when the main element is logistics or figuring out a siege.

          That being said, I would think that a major battle is so important that a single roll simply does not do it justice, so then I would use a variation of the Chase rules or opposed Social Manoeuvring.

                  Originally posted by Caitiff Primogen View Post
                  I wouldn't rule out Politics, personally.

                  "War is the continuation of politics by other means."
                  -Carl von Clausewitz, Vom Kriege
                  Sun Tzu wasn't a fan of this sentiment, though.

                  "There are three ways in which a ruler can bring misfortune upon his army:

                  By commanding the army to advance or to retreat, being ignorant of the fact that it cannot obey. This is called hobbling the army.

                  By attempting to govern an army in the same way as he administers a kingdom, being ignorant of the conditions which obtain in an army. This causes restlessness in the soldier's minds.

                  By employing the officers of his army without discrimination, through ignorance of the military principle of adaptation to circumstances. This shakes the confidence of the soldiers. "

                  " Sun Tzu said: In war, the general receives his commands from the sovereign, collects his army and concentrates his forces. When in difficult country, do not encamp. In country where high roads intersect, join hands with your allies. Do not linger in dangerously isolated positions. In hemmed-in situations, you must resort to stratagem. In desperate position, you must fight . There are roads which must not be followed, armies which must be not attacked, towns which must not be besieged, positions which must not be contested, commands of the sovereign which must not be obeyed."

                  "The natural formation of the country is the soldier's best ally; but a power of estimating the adversary, of controlling the forces of victory, and of shrewdly calculating difficulties, dangers and distances, constitutes the test of a great general. He who knows these things, and in fighting puts his knowledge into practice, will win his battles. He who knows them not, nor practices them, will surely be defeated. If fighting is sure to result in victory, then you must fight, even though the ruler forbid it; if fighting will not result in victory, then you must not fight even at the ruler's bidding."

                  Basically, his sentiment was that politicians and political skills have their priorities wrong, and they meddling only leads to ruin.
