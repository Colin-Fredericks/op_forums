                Yeah, it'll be interesting to see if they preserve the great wall between GM and player content. They could certainly move some things around at the very least (I never quite understood why Relics, potentially starting equipment, were gated off beyond the Pale Brood's misleadingly spoilery description text).

                The core's biggest flaw to me was always the lack of end game content. I.e. unclear Apotheosis mechanics, little to no information on what remembering Duat might mean, etc. You can make that stuff up, sure, but I could never really think of an interesting "grand truth" to reveal if my campaigns ever got to that point. Part of that is me. I struggle to find the grand, cosmology-level lore to be terribly interesting. And I understand the intrinsic irony in both expressing that disinterest while calling its absence the game's "biggest flaw". For me, revelations about who the characters have been and details about their past lives are more human and a thousand times more interesting than The True Identity Of The Judges or anything like that, but I'm not so dismissive as to assume that a revelation on that level would have no impact or be inherently boring in any way. I just don't think I'm the person to try and write something like that.

                The easiest thing to do, from my perspective, would be to ditch the wall and have all the information about the game's setting be up front to the characters. Memory just becomes something entirely personal. But if they were to keep the wall then it would need a marked improvement over the first edition. It felt genuinely frustrating as a GM to get no clear answers from the game billing itself as the only Chronicles game to have a clear Truth. The core book in particular was somewhat notorious for keeping things ambiguous and with little more than clever innuendo and hints even in the "behind the wall" section. Never really bothered me, I wasn't inclined to be running chronicles delving into that stuff in the first place, but the discrepancy between what the book seemed to sell itself as and what was actually there stood out even to me.

Later supplements show it becoming more and more widespread as the years march on, with the Heretic no longer the only canonical Arisen to achieve it. It's presented in a pretty similar manner to the New Dawn, except with a cultural aversion to it instead of an inbuilt need to achieve it. It's just that the book gives you no real ideas on what to tell a player who achieves it (in terms of what they now remember about Duat), and no concrete mechanics for their most important new power (relic creation). Like the New Dawn, you by no means need to have it occur in any Chronicle. Like the New Dawn, there's the definite suggestion though that a not-insignificant number of Chronicles might end in it, or at least have it as their driving force.

                            This gap, to me, represented what felt like the first edition's biggest flaw. It isn't, however, a big flaw. This is my favourite game, I have to work hard to find things I dislike...

                            Edit: Just to clarify a little further, Mummy is the only Chronicles game to build itself around a player-GM divide. It calls this out itself in the core. It makes a really freaking big deal about it. But... there's practically nothing behind the core's GM "wall" that feels worth the fuss. Not every game needs something massive for the end game but Mummy very explicitly sold itself as having something. And I don't think it pulled it off well. That's why my original post was speculating on whether they'd toss out that particular concept, or redo it with a little more oomph.

                                                            I've been trying to measure the advantages of Mummy presenting an objective account of events, against presenting readers with historical revisionism and unreliable narrators. The former approach gives the player-base a common ground for discussion and makes in easier to invest into presented concepts. The latter approach enables GMs to choose between alternate truths, which they can tailor for their campaign and use them to actually surprise players, which synergises well with the concept of reclaiming Memory. I wonder what balance will the game go for.
