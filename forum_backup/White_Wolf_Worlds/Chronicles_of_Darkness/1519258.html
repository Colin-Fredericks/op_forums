    Fire Touched Roleplay

    07-31-2023, 12:23 PM
    I recently created a Fire Touched character for a mixed venue game. I decided to lean more heavily into the fervor and fanaticism side of things, and far less into the disease angle. I've devoted a large portion of my EXP into merits like retainers, staff, residential area, essentially creating a small village of fanatical followers bound under the leadership of my PC and his pack. Most of his time has been spent making it look like an insular, yet perfect community, that simply prefers not to interact much with outsiders unless its to bring them into the fold. My PC isn't powerful in his own right, but through sheer numbers and the ability to grant them mechanical advantages through various gifts, merits, and such, isn't a slouch, especially when it comes to manipulating various things through his network of allies, retainers, and amassed social merits.

    Essentially though I've hit a bit of a wall, as there aren't any other forsaken or pure PCs playing (mostly changelings and a smattering of other templates) so most of his actions pretty much go unnoticed, and most of the other characters are more or less unconcerned. Not having knowledge of werewolves, he more or less crafts whatever story he wishes when questioned by them (there are others of my kind who wish to subjugate and destroy spirits, his faction of werewolves are generally treated poorly, looked down upon, and even destroyed by them, etc).

    I guess I'm basically asking what are people's opinions and experience in regards to the Fire Touched, and Pure in a general sense, when interacting with other non-werewolf supernaturals? I'd like to find some way to begin poking at other characters in a sort of semi-antagonist role, rather than playing what is beginning to feel like an entirely separate subgame within the overall game. Also open to questions about my character, perhaps to inspire a fire to give me some ideas about how to expand on his roleplay.

              Well, let's see what we can do.

              The first particular thing I want to do is step back and get a sense of the overarching story and seeing if you, the other players, and the Storyteller are working with the same set of expectations. I can't infer what the overarching plot is from this, so I'll need some input to really comment on it, but it sounds like you have a character arc that is playing outside of the expected experience-if everyone else is playing a globetrotting treasure hunt adventure and you have a struggling orphanage owner who needs to spend time with the kids, then you have a story likely being left behind, as an example. By getting everyone on the same page about what you want out of your story, as well as their own, it's easier to get

              Getting into what we have, I do have to wonder what the arc of your character is supposed to be-it sounds like you're looking forward to being a semi-antagonist revealed, which can be fun, but the catch of the matter is that there's always sort of a catch of what happens when the player characters suddenly realize that you are actually being an antagonist. The main way that's relevant for the issue you're having is that it may be that the players and Storyteller don't realize what you have afterwards, and so may be operating on the assumption that confronting you is supposed to be endgame material.

              My next question is "Are you, as an antagonist, doing things that affect the other protagonists in terms they understand as a problem that needs fixing?" You mention that you're able to handwave a lot of the things you're doing as something that they don't understand by virtue of not being a Werewolf, but that kind of betrays the fact that you're trying to be an antagonist in a crossover setting in Forsaken terms-if you're going to have conflict, you have to be willing to have elements of yourself that make sense in the context of other supernaturals.

              For example, you mention that there's plural changelings in your group, so.....what is your community doing to impede the freedom and agency of the protagonists and their touchstones/family/friends? Is anyone in those changelings stories even living in your community? Do you stand in favor of the fetch's claim to life and strictly police the neighborhood to keep the changeling's out? Are the changelings in the community put upon by your character to fulfill roles and obligations they might not want to do or be but have to keep the sanctity of your territory? Have you demonstrated a willingness to rob them of the life they've built for your own ends? How are your deals with them rigged against them? What do you hold that they want that can only be gotten at cost?

              You get the idea-you need to play into the threats that the characters are already primed to act on if you want people to act on it, which goes into the last thing to ask-is the way you're currently being an antagonist a problem, or an inconvenience? THere are a lot of antagonists in fiction who are annoying and represent a low level of compromise that speak to the fact that protagonists are making it-think of Marcone from the Dresden Files, who is definitely on Dresden's shit list, but he keeps ending up in alliance with him on the various issues of Chicago and keeps pushing back the due date for dealing with him, a thing that Dresden has noted as costing him because Marcone is now much better equipped to handle him and his allies.

              This can matter because If you're just being a nuisance, then it may be that other players have opted to just ignore or otherwise deal with your antics-and that can be a particular issue to deal with if you are trying to handle the line between being part of the play group and being an antagonist with them. Going with another example, I had a game where another player's character was suffering from an active psychic degradation that, in theory, meant that they could be a threat to them at any point of time because their mind could literally change to "killing the other people in this party is neccesary to keep the sun rising, and will also be a lot of fun." The player, unfortunately, both way too rapidly expanded their psychotic language outburst and was never really willing to commit to being a physical threat, so it got to a point where the character had a moment where they yelled out "I WILL MURDER YOU ALL AND DRESS IN YOUR ENTRAILS"* and the rest of the player characters took a second of just looking at each other before continuing with the conversation they were having.

              So, yeah, if you want to have confrontation, they need to be taking actions that require confrontation.

              I suppose we could talk about making sure you maintain the balance of also being an actionable protagonist who contributes to the group, but the character's virtues as an asset are obvious, and currently it sounds like you are still being a viable aid to fill out the silent semi-protagonist included with the semi-antagonist goal, so we won't worry about that.

              I need ot actually start my day, so I'll get around to opinions on the Pure as Crossover Antagonists later.

              *I suppose it pays to mention that this particular player had a particular problem of "wanting to be the main character"-itis in not only this game but the one that preceded it, and never quite got it into their head that they should actually drive the action in a way that actually moves the story forward to the player's (ostensible) benefit if they wanted to be the main character. Scene hoggery has diminishing returns.

                          The overarcing setting is that of a semi-rebuilt post-apocalypse style city state, the lands outside of which are scarred and desolate, filled with things far worse on the antagonist scale such as Bale Hounds. My PC has established a sort of farming community of wolfbloods in the outskirts of the city (no other PCs present), the inner portions more akin to an early industrial late 1800s vibe.

                          As for the story as focused on my PC and the one I'm trying to tell, he very much wishes to protect those under his control, but demands absolute loyalty, enforcing this through powers to make the villagers fanatical in their devotion to him and the "Word of Fire". Heavy religious notes to it, for example, two portions of my staff merit being represented by the Clergy of Fire (which ensure social compliance) and the Choir of Fire (which is a more occult focused arm). He thus far mostly uses the carrot method, although a situation hasn't arisen where the stick has been needed either.

                          As far as interactions with the other characters, I've tried to bring plot my character has uncovered to them but they seem largely uninterested (despite some having obvious changeling roots), and while he assists them in problems that threaten the city as a whole (not just his own small corner of it) most of the time and effort of my character is spent beating back things that threaten just him. I came in with what I think is a decent enough idea, but it was under the assumption there would be more werewolf characters, so kinda got thrown for a loop on my plans when it turned out most folks ended up playing changelings, which I don't have enough information on to really develop a plan to be antagonistic in a way they understand. Without much knowledge of changeling, doubly so for how a Fire Touched would threaten them, the best I've come up with so far is a long term plan of slowly absorbing the outer portions of the city into fanatical followers, where much of the food and natural resources that supply the inner portions reside. Even at this point while he couldn't starve the city to death, he could certainly make them feel some level of pain as many other smaller satellite communities owe him for his protection of them (part of a carrot method to convert them fully). All good questions though in your fourth paragraph that will help with this, ones I plan to heavily consider leaning into as I try to salvage this concept.

                          The other characters aren't really aware he is an antagonist at this point, mostly because I've been unsure of how to approach other splats with that in mind (you provided good starting points though, much appreciated). I've tried to expose them to his little fanatical slice of heaven, inviting them to participate in seasonal rites that would align with many of their seasonal courts, asking for aid in dealing with problems, etc., but there has been no real interest in it. Could be I'm not pushing the right buttons, or they just are more interested in a changeling focused game despite the multi-splat venue. Cause at this point he wouldn't seem to be much more than a friendly werewolf that is trying to get them to work with him on stuff.

                          Its possible that despite it how I feel in regards to my character fitting into the setting established by the storyteller, he just doesn't fit in to the setting/story the other players are trying to develop. I'd like to try and salvage it, but it may just come down to him not being a good fit, or just needing to force the other characters into having to interact with mine.

                          Hopefully that answers most of your questions, admittedly was a bit much for me to digest, but I think I hit the high points.

                                          You haven't mentioned what the congregation actually believes in. What is their dogma and how in line with reality is it? The Fire-Touched Ban forces the Tribe to confront untruths, but most of them have a cultural blind spot where "Truth is what I believe in and if facts disagree with that then the facts are wrong". If you haven't drafted a philosophy of the congregation yet, then this is a great opportunity to draft one most appropriate for the game. I like Shadowcatx's idea where the werewolf would demand radical honesty in all things (something super risky for Changelings), and he could also provide unflinching protection to the truthful. The Fire-touched would start as a possible ally, and as the game progresses, he could become gradually more demanding and impractical (or mellow out through actions of other PCs).

                                                  In case anyone was interested, I did come up with something to build around as far as a system of beliefs or code. It is written from an IC point of view, and does need a bit more work, I think an introductory paragraph akin to a speech or sermon would be nice, bit of cleaning up on the wording and language, but the core idea I think is established:

                                                  The Word of Fire

                                                  “Very truly I tell you, everyone who lies is a slave to those lies. Listen to these words then you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free.” ~ Isib Ur, the Prophet of Fire

                                                  <Insert Introductory Paragraph here>


                                                  The Blazing Sacraments
                                                  Live the truth, this is our divine order.

                                                  Pay falsehoods with penance, burning it away with fire of the flesh and soul.

                                                  Stoke the fires of your soul, feed it the truth.

                                                  Honor the manifestation of this fire, treat it reverently.

                                                  Pity those who live in deceit, but rejoice for they can be saved.

                                                  Do not seek out, or bear witness to those who do not live the truth, except to bring them our word.



                                                  The Choir of Fire
                                                  The choir is the social arm of the faith, they travel amongst the people doing good works in its name. Leading by example, they live the truth and seek to strengthen it by the witnessing of their virtues.

                                                  The Clergy of Fire
                                                  T                                                  he clergy leads in the rituals of the faith, with which our fires are strengthened and forge our community stronger. Their ceremony is one of the foundations upon which we change the world.

                                                  The Templars of Fire
                                                  The templars protect our faith from those who would seek to destroy us and the truth, for some can only achieve absolution in death. They protect us through the strength of their faith, but their mercy we grant only as a last resort.

                                                  The People of Fire
                                                  Our people are our most treasured of the faith, they fight the true battle armed only with their belief in the truth. They live each day in service of our faith, and raise a new generation in service to our belief.



                                                  Still kinda considering how he'd work with/against changelings but one hand he would recognize their deceit being born of fear, something he could remedy as he offers protection to those who abandon lies and deceit for favor of living a true life. Even if as a werewolf he'd know very little about them or the True Fae, so believing he could protect them might not be smart from an out of character standpoint, but in character would be something he would believe however naïve it may be. On the other hand, changelings are creatures irreversibly changed by the True Fae, who would be a sort of being representing concepts that are the polar opposite to the ideas of truth. If they cannot abandon the trappings of falsehood instilled in them by the True Fae, they much like their masters, can only "achieve absolution in death".

                                                  Obviously got a bit more tightening up to do on the concept, but just wanted to say thanks to folks for giving me a bit more to work with, expanding my knowledge base on the subject, and helping by asking me the right questions I myself need to answer.
