                        Members of the faculty usually have their own private libraries (my supervisor has a few hundred books in his office alone on his area of research) and definitely their favourite vice (usually coffee, wine or beer). Having access to "secure" offices of their own usually helps too. Faculty members usually have access to staff time in some capacity and their students would count for that too! They have computers, the ability to avoid or join meetings at a whim. Depending on the University, they'd have access to resources due to status too. Faculty members usually have priority access to equipment directly related to their research (my supervisor for instance had priority access to the NMR spectrometer when he was actively running structural research). Staff members in general usually have 24 hour access to the campus buildings that they belong to, too - be it building keys or NFC keycards coded to let them in. Most of the faculty avoid carrying around much stuff because they've got storage space, they're likely to just have the basics when they're out and about like a phone and a small bag with an organiser, diary, access cards, car keys, etc.

                        PhD students have similar access to faculty, but far less "pull" - they're usually in a shared office (the entire research group under one supervisor is usually in one room), they quite often get all-hours access, they sometimes get junior honours/masters students that they have to mentor (but in exchange can act as staff for them too). They'll have less resources by far (most of it is by grace of the institution and their supervisors), but otherwise they still have significant access. PhDs/Masters students tend to get access to speciality equipment by their research and tend to carry around stuff they don't want left unsupervised in their common offices (have known more than a few of my PhD friends who don't leave their laptops in their shared office for exactly that reason).

                        Undergraduates are likely to be the people who carry tonnes of crap around - most don't get storage areas (or they're really temporary) so you'll find them with folders, notes, books, laptops, etc. as normal course of their day. They're also likely to carry around any hobby stuff they're doing, most likely to have snacks on them as individuals. They'll often have spare clothes (e.g. they're science students and have lab coats/glasses or they've got part-time jobs and need a chance of clothes for work). Things like printed bus/train schedules are pretty common, as are maps early in the semester.

                        Basically, treat anyone that's of faculty or PhD/Masters as someone who lives at the University - they've got office space and can and will store things there rather than carry them on their person. Comparatively, undergrads are basically permanent visitors - they'll always be lugging lots of stuff around and have everything they need for their uni day on them.

                        Edit:
                        On the subject of available staff, I'll list what I had access to as a PhD student and could request tasks/resources from - it's actually quite a bit! I'd also provide a guess of what skill they'd be appropriate for.

                        Junior Students (Science)
                        Research Librarian (Academics)
                        School Administration (Politics)
                        Laboratory Managers and Technical Staff (Craft)
                        Research Association (Socialise)
                        Campus Security (Investigation)
                        Counselling Service (Empathy?)
                        Medical Practice (Medicine)

                        A lot of these I didn't have access to as an undergraduate, so they're definitely something that you could make use of with higher status at a University, or by having explicit staff from the Merit.

                                If you don't mind looking at it like a player with meta knowledge, while still staying within the bounds of the reasonable, I would recommend three things no matter what role you draw.
                                ​1-A pocket knife, I'm talking a less than two inch long blade utility knife.
                                2-A multi-tool, yea they're crap compared to a dedicated tool but it would allow you to at least attempt tasks that require a tool.
                                3-A mini mag light of some sort. Never underestimate the usefulness, even in normal light, of having a small light source handy. After 40 you often need one just to see into shadows.
                                All of these are things 'real' people carry on a regular basis and while they might be hard to fit into the pockets of your blue jeans they will easily fit in jacket pockets or the standard backpack.

                                                        Originally posted by Seta143 View Post
                                                        i don't like the idea of having that much of an advantage over the rest of the group, from my perspective doing more with less usually results in more fun and commands more respect.

                                                        That and combat type characters excepts for ones like Joxer from Xena are usually less appealing to me.
                                                        It's not necessarily a net advantage, at least when you look at the other characters' abilities and resources. A campus cop may have a gun and access to campus buildings, but he's going to be lacking in academic resources. Even if they're actual police (which the ones on my smallish state university are), their authority is limited to law enforcement. They don't have much political pull with the administration or faculty beyond that scope, and they don't have access to the resources that faculty would in terms of equipment, a library, student assistance, etc. And even a student might have advantages in certain situations. Students are more likely to be more open when talking to other students than with faculty or police, and there may be parts of the campus that are known to students that the faculty and staff aren't aware of, or at least places they wouldn't think of going, such as rooftops and steam tunnels. There are also a lot of social situations in which a student would fit in better than faculty or staff. (Unless you're that rare "cool" professor, showing up at the homecoming bonfire would get you a lot of awkward looks as faculty, and you'd definitely be looking in from the outside as a campus cop.)

                                                        So being the only member of the group with a weapon is definitely an advantage, there are definitely plenty of areas where you'd be at a disadvantage, too.
