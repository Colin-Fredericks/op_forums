    Session 15 - The Clocktower

    Riley and Abram briefly looked over the Green Plains Bank. They found the interior to show signs, here and there, of the hard times striking the entire region, but no clues as to what might be triggering their sensitivity to the Azothic Radiance. Rather than speak to anyone, they chose instead to walk back out quickly. They had returned to their makeshift home by the time Elsie returned with her bottles of alcohol, which she proudly displayed to them.

    After some discussion, the group decided to focus on the leadership of Sour Springs. There was clearly something going on at the Town Hall, between its massively distorted appearance in the dream and the fact that they had been unable to get past the front desk and talk to the mayor. Abram suggested that they find out where the mayor lived and try to catch him away from the office, so they went for a walk around town looking for the nicest houses.

    Riley, for their part, spent this walk sizing up the men of the town trying to work out what Abram's "type" might be. They figured someone like Abram, tall, muscular, fairly good-looking, but sadly pickings in Sour Springs seemed to be slim. They were down by the railway, inspecting the exterior of a building best described as a small mansion, when Riley spotted a nice-looking young man walking towards it. He was dressed in a duster and hat that made him look like a cowboy out of a western. He made straight for the building they were looking for, and Riley persuaded the other two to stage a distraction.

    With the sound of Abram and Elsie laying into each other in a half-real, half-pretend row about parenting techniques providing cover, Riley was able to sneak up to the window. Inside they could see the young man, his back to them, talking with a corpulent fat man wearing the finest clothes Riley had ever seen. He was slumped back in his chair, coated in sweat, and the allure of his clothing was somewhat spoiled by the sweat stains on the collar.

    Riley wasn't able to hear anything, but they had some small talent at reading lips and were able to determine that the fat man was expressing his frustration to the young man, whose name was Mikael, that the mayor was unreachable. Mikael's replies were impossible to make out, but eventually the fat man shrugged and said that "Gant" would not be a problem.

    When Mikael left the house, Riley tailed them until they found him taking a seat in front of the town hall, staring up at it. Riley returned to gather the other two, and the throng resolved to just break in. They entered the town hall and confront the clerk. When he, once again, refused to let them in without an appointment, Abram and Elsie staged another row and Riley was able to slip past the clerk and through the door behind him.

    The corridor behind reception was a long, featureless wooden corridor that curved slightly to the right. Riley followed it until they reached a door at the other end, but realised before they opened it that it was the door they had just come through. They realised that they needed to back up, back the way they thought they had just walked, and open the door they found behind them.

    That door led through to a stone corridor, lit with flickering torches. Riley had an easy feeling that they were somehow standing on a physical edge of the dream. They stepped inside and started to explore, finding the stone maze of corridors beyond looped back in on itself in confusing, even impossible ways.

    When Riley hadn't returned within a quarter of an hour of departing, Abram got impatient. He threatened the clerk with violence, and he bolted. Elsie and Abram walked through into the corridors beyond. Through a fluke of luck more than anything they were able to find the entrance to the stone corridors, but after that they became separated and very, very lost.

    Riley, unaware that the others were trying to follow, had used their newfound Alembics from the Plumbum Transmutation to access the Azothic Memory and guide them onward. They found the base of a long staircase they hoped would lead up to the tower. They began to climb. The stairs went on for what felt like forever, leading them up for more than an hour. They reached a landing, at last. The sound of gears turning and grinding drowned out everything else. Ladders led up to the final platforms at the top of the tower. There, nestled among the gears, Riley saw It.

    It was a bizarre amalgamation of nightmare and beauty. Smiling, angelic faces peered out from flesh torn open into ravenous, fang-filled maws. Flesh merged with spokes and gears, twisting and shifting as the seconds ticked by. And Riley could suddenly sense the Azoth blazing like a sun. They raised their gun, prepared to summon a Firestorm to try and burn this creature away, when their access to the Azothic Memory told them what they were looking at. An Archqashmal.

    Riley ran. They raced down the clocktower at a breakneck pace, the stairs appearing to be a lot shorter on the way down than they had on the ascent. When they reached the bottom they began to parse their way through the corridors, finding their way back, when they ran into Elsie stumbling around lost. She told them that Abram was also lost in the halls, but dismissed Riley's idea that they should look for him. This horrified Riley.

    "Don't you want to be part of this family?"

    Elsie's reply was a cruel barb, telling Riley that she thought they were the ones who didn't want to be part of the family, since they kept running off to their horse. Hurt and reeling, Riley turned away and went to look for Abram, Elsie reluctantly following along behind them. They found him pretty quickly, only a few corridors away, and Riley then led their throng out of the dream halls and back to the reception area.

    When they stepped out into the room they found the sheriff and his deputies waiting for them.

        Session 16 - Jailbirds

        The sheriff and his men fumbled for their weapons as the Prometheans came through the door. Everyone froze, a tense standoff, until the sheriff remembered himself and demanded that the throng throw down their weapons. Abram's hand went to his pickaxe, he started to prepare his Pyros to burn and bring the entire building down, but Riley was faster. Their pistol was carefully placed on the ground.

        "Don't do it," they hissed at Abram.

        Once the Prometheans were disarmed, the sheriff's men moved in to arrest them. They were charged with threatening violence against a citizen of Sour Spring. Abram was arrested as the culprit, Riley as his accomplice. No one made any move on Elsie. Instead, the sheriff had a quiet word with her, telling her not to leave town as she was a witness but that as yet no one had suggested that she had anything to do with events. Not long after and she was skipping happily down the road, heading for home, entirely oblivious to the darkening skies.

        Abram and Riley were not quite so blind. They saw the storm coming from the north, an enormous front of dust and debris. The wind was already picking up, filling the air with flying grit. The sheriff's men marched them quickly to the town lock-up, where they split the two of them up. Riley went into an overcrowded cell with half a dozen black men, Abram into a cell with a lone, scraggly-looking white man.

        Riley attempted to talk to the other prisoners. They tried at first to talk about the dreams, but once again they were laughed down by people apparently unprepared to admit that the recurring nightmares were important. Then they tried to muster interest in a jailbreak, but the men were all local and didn't like the sound of being on the run. Finally, they asked about Malik. One of the prisoners, a man who had apparently been arrested only a few hours before for drinking, recognised the description. He'd gone West, Riley was told, not following the road or train. This was about three weeks back.

        Abram, meanwhile, began working at loosening the bars on their cell. A brief protest from their fellow prisoner was shut down by a blunt threat. He had pried two bars loose, creating enough space to fit through. Then, he carefully replaced the bars and prepared for sleep.

        That night, as the Prometheans slept, they found themselves in what looked like a medieval dungeon as they entered the nightmare once again. The sheriff appeared, torch in one hand, keys in the other, and began to unlock the cells. He told the prisoners to gather their weapons, for the hunt was about to begin. They rushed out, part of a mob clamouring for arms. Abram snatched up his pickaxe, Riley got their hands on a shotgun. They stepped out onto the streets of Sour Springs to find it beset by the storm. In the howling wind and dust it was easy for them to quietly separate from the sheriff's gang and make their own way in the dream.

        The storm seemed to be effecting the dreamscape sporadically, occasionally blasting away and making navigation almost impossible, then vanishing in an instant, restoring the dream to its normal state. Abram and Riley picked their way through, making their way to Peter's home to fulfill their promise to protect him. They found him cowering, alone. When asked about his wife, he said she had gone elsewhere to hide. They told him that they thought they had found a way to end the nightmare entirely, but they would have to leave him on his own. It took a bit of wheedling from Riley, but he eventually agreed. Before they left, Riley remembered the conversation they had witnessed between Mikael and the fat man, and asked Peter if he knew the name "Gant". Peter told them that it was probably Belladonna Gant, the closest person the town had to a doctor.

        They left Peter to go and explore the wealthy estate they had visited earlier that day, where they had seen Mikael for the first time. They found their way there only due to Riley's almost supernatural skill at navigation, the storm had rendered the nightmare even more confusing than normal. When they arrived, everything was still. The building was a gutted ruin, filled with the offal of a pile of dismembered bodies, the house's second floor torn almost entirely out to make it little more than a ruined shell. Riley and Abram glanced at one another. They'd found the lair of one of the beasts.

        They went inside to investigate. While they were in there, they heard heavy footsteps approaching, and went to hide. The massive arm of the largest of the three beasts, the bloated beast whose lupine features were almost obscured under its bodily fat, reached around the edge of a large gap in the wall. It hauled itself into view, filling the interior with the stench of its bodily musk. It looked around, but saw no sign of the Prometheans. Instead it seated itself in a pit of its own filth and began to scoop up body parts from the pile for it, shovelling them into its mouth. Riley glimpsed the head of the building's maid among the carnage. While it was occupied, the two of them were able to steal away.

        In high spirits because they had confirmed the identity of one of the beasts, the two of them headed back across town to the Green Plains Bank. They were about to go in and explore to see if there was any sign of an athanor in the dream, but Abram spotted the smallest, pale beast lurking in the building's shadow. He told Riley to run inside while he distracted it.

        Riley made a sprint for the building, hearing the beast howling in flame as Abram bombarded it with fire. They didn't look back. They rushed inside, and everything went very quiet. The bank was pretty similar on the inside, and they couldn't find anything of note. They went back outside, to find the charred remains of Abram and the pale beast fused together in the middle of the street. Then they heard the approaching footsteps of the elder beast, and ran.

        Elsie woke in the early hours of the morning. She had spent the dream hiding away, and woke up feeling both refreshed and itchy. The black blizzard still raged outside, and dust was seeping into the unsealed building. It covered her skin and clothes. She spent a few minutes petulantly kicking at it for ruining her momentary good mood, and then walked over to feed Baby, but the infant didn't want to eat. Sulking, Elsie hauled the child onto her back and stepped out into the dust storm, fumbling her way through the streets to find the corner store.

        When Abram woke he stepped out through the gap he had made in the bars and went to sit outside Riley's cell. There, to while away the hours of the night, Riley traced words in the dust to help Abram learn how to read.

        Elsie was back in the streets, heading for Mike's Roadhouse. She had a bag on her back, slung next to Baby, packed full of utensils and plates she had looted from the store. It hadn't even taken much effort to force the door open. She intended to take them to Mike and use them for a romantic dinner. That was when she sensed a presence looming over her.

        She looked up, into the distended jaws of Typhoeus. His voice cut through the storm, a gloating, liquid wave of sound.

        "I've been looking for you."

        Elsie smiled, always happy to be the centre of attention. "Hello honey!"

                    Session 17 - Answers

                    Elsie waited calmly for Typhoeus to stop shouting. It was some nonsense about her lying to him, running away, trying to trick him. She was just pleased that he'd cared enough to seek her out. When he was done she cuddled closer to him, using his body to shield herself from the storm, and told him the truth.

                    "Oh honey, I didn't want to run. It was Riley. They set everything on fire, and I had to get away from the fire. I didn't want to leave. I couldn't stop them."

                    The enormous sublimati seemed taken aback by that. For all of his immense strength, he seemed unable to overcome the full force of Elsie's indomitable personality. She demanded he follow back to her home and proudly led the way back down the main street, battling the wind and dust.

                    Terah did not take the arrival of the titanic monster well. The horse screamed and panicked, trying to pull itself free from its post and escape the small shelter in the back yard of the building. Elsie walked over and calmly untied it, letting the noisy creature run off into the storm. With peace and quiet restored, she had Typhoeus lie his bulk down so that he was almost covered by the roof of the rear porch. She sat down next to him, wearing her gas mask, and placed Baby between them. Over the next few hours she gossiped and chatted happily at him, occasionally inquiring into his past and storing the details away for later. At last she went to go and prepare a meal, but Typhoeus refused the food, instead, he asked for her arm.

                    Elsie weighed it up, and recalled that she had read magazines that told her wives should be willing to sacrifice anything for their husbands. If she wanted to keep Typhoeus with her, this seemed like a good place to start. She held out her hand, and Typhoeus took the whole length in his mouth. She then felt repeated stabbing, tearing pains running up and down the limb. When Typhoeus allow her to remove it, long strips and chunks of flesh had been torn away. Elsie cooed and beamed down at the damage, feeling a happy warmth in her stomach at the thought of having pleased her man.

                    Across town, Riley and Abram had broken out of their cells, beaten two deputies unconscious, retrieved their gear (and collected some new gear from the deputies, including a badge and a pair of revolvers), and escaped into the storm. They were intent on locating the home of Belladonna Gant, but they quickly became lost in the Black Blizzard. When they felt their throats begin to close, unable to cope with the dust, they stumbled up to the nearest door hammered at it until it opened. They found themselves in Mike's Roadhouse. They told Mike that they were looking for Elsie, that she was missing. He was curiously unresponsive to that, instead shrugging and leading them down into the speakeasy. An older man they had never met before was sitting in the company of several young men, all of them wearing dirty workmen's uniforms.

                    The Prometheans stayed a few hours, keeping up the cover by asking the men if they knew where Elsie was. They didn't, but the older man said he'd be happy to help. He was quite interested in meeting the young woman again, too. When they asked if they could wait out the storm in the Roadhouse, Mike told them that they would need to pay for a room. Rather than do that, they instead waited until they felt like they had recovered somewhat and plunged back out into the storm.

                    This time they were able to reach Belladonna's house. She was clearly expecting people to come to her during the storm, when they hammered on the door of her modest little townhouse it opened immediately, and they stumbled into a living room where a medical valise was already set up on the central table. Before Belladonna could do more than close the door behind them, however, Riley had drawn a gun on her and demanded answers. They wanted to know everything she knew about the dream, about the wolf-monsters, about why the nightmares were happening and what her role in it all was.

                    Belladonna appeared calm, even confident, in the face of the drawn weapon. She asked them to please put the weapon away, and in return she would tell them everything she knew. Somewhat reluctantly, Riley complied.

                    Belladonna explained that she had served as a nurse in the great war. In the midst of the battles she saw a man be shot half a dozen times in the chest and keep moving, pus flowing from his wounds in place of blood. The sight set her on a path that led to her discovering alchemical secrets, which she delved into in the hope of finding better ways to treat the sick and injured. Over the years she fell into the company of two other alchemists, the man named Mikael and his associate, a railroad baron named Boris Trammel (the Prometheans assumed this was the fat man in the opulent house they had spied on earlier). They studied and experimented together for years, settling down in Sour Springs, trying to discover new ways to heal and extend natural life. Belladonna always knew the others were in it for more selfish reasons than herself, but figured it didn't matter so long as it would help other people.

                    Then, one day, they encountered a creature like the one she had seen in the trenches. It was immediately apparent that its body was filled with alchemical power. Boris and Mikael captured the creature, intending to experiment on it. Belladonna objected, but she couldn't overrule the other two. Instead she split from them, continuing her own research on her own. The night of the creature's capture, however, was when the nightmares began.

                    Her own knowledge of the nightmares were limited. To her they were strange visions of blood and fire. She had tried to restrain herself from fighting after she found out that others were experiencing the nightmares as her prey, but fools sometimes sought her out and attacked her. Most nights, however, she simply sought to hide.

                    Riley and Abram decided to divulge to Belladonna that they, too, were Prometheans, creatures like the one she had described. Riley asked her to describe the Promethean Mikael and Boris had captured, and was relieved to hear that it had appeared as a young woman. They found out that Mikael had a house down near the bank, and suddenly the Azothic Resonance they had sensed there began to make sense.

                    Riley asked for permission to look at Belladonna's books. She refused at first, not wanting to trust her books to strangers, but Abram offered up some of his Pyros to her in return. It was an offer she couldn't refuse. She carefully extracted some of his vital fluids with her medical equipment as Riley poured over the books. Riley couldn't discern anything useful from the books, but Abram did get Belladonna to agree to help in any way she could. She was not interested in fighting, but she was willing to be on hand to tend to them if they were injured rescuing their fellow Promethean.

                    The storm still raged outside. They didn't know where Elsie was. But the two Prometheans resolved that they couldn't stand to let one of their kind suffer much longer. They began preparations to leave.

                                    Session 18 - Sleeping with the Beasts

                                    The storm continued to rage throughout the day. Elsie snuggled into Typhoeus's side and, when the wind allowed it, babbled at him about their future. She was able to worm out of him a few insights about what he wanted, and at the top of the list was children. That made her happy. Five, six years from now, she could picture them living together, eleven or twelve offspring swarming all over the place.

                                    In Belladonna's home, Abram was trying to work through her motives. He had encountered no genuinely selfless humans in his life, so he was suspicious and confused. He understood sacrifice and struggle when it came with a reward, but Belladonna denied that was why she wanted to help people. He understood wanting to help those in your family, and he understood helping those who could be of use to you, but Belladonna had claimed she tried to help people she had never met who would be of no help to her in return. In the war she apparently even cared for the enemy at times. Riley had an easier time grasping the concept, but even they weren't able to make the idea make much more sense to Abram.

                                    The two of them decided to stay with Belladonna for the night, and attempt to rescue the imprisoned Promethean the next morning. That night, when they slept, they found themselves in what appeared to be a natural cave. The smallest of the three wolf-beasts, the pale one they knew to be Belladonna, was there with them. Backed into a corner, it attacked. Riley ran, sprinting for the exit, while Abram held his ground. The beast bore him to the ground, but in a straight fight Abram's superhuman strength proved to his advantage. He slammed the head of his pickaxe into its stomach again and again. Then, raked by tooth and claw, he kicked the creature to the floor and broke its back with a final blow.

                                    Elsie had appeared in the nightmare alongside Typhoeus. She convinced him to show his devotion to her by bringing her the head, or heads, of the creature Riley had seen in the clocktower. They set off together, Typhoeus bearing Elsie on her back. When they reached the base of the clocktower they heard howling, and saw the two remaining beasts of the dream approaching together as if to protect the tower. Typhoeus loomed over the bestial creatures. He picked the largest beast up by its neck before it had a chance to create and tore it in two. The elder beast showed no fear at this. Its jaws yawned wide and a stream of corrosive bile poured out, spilling over Typhoeus and Elsie. Elsie felt it bubble and burn its way through her skin, dissolving her, but she lived just long enough to witness Typhoeus bite off the head of the second beast.

                                    Riley used the dream as an opportunity to scout out the ground ahead of tomorrow's events. They found Mikael's home. Unlike the lairs of the other two beasts, it was strangely normal in the dream. The house itself was blandly sterile and bereft of personal effects. After a few minutes' search they located an entrance on the outside of the house that looked like it would open into a coal cellar or basement. Instead they found a lab, overgrown with pulsating living flesh, lumps of the stuff arrayed on each of the room's three tables and interconnected with rubber pipes and tubes. Riley stared at the sight in horror, hoping that this wasn't what the Promethean captive had become. They explored a little further and found that a curtain at the back of the room opened into a dark pit several metres deep. A winch and cable allowed them to pull up the iron coffin that lay in its depths. When the coffin reached the top, however, a coruscating bolt of flux-like energy blasted out of it and struck Riley in the chest. The next thing they knew, they were waking up.

                                    With gas masks borrowed from Belladonna, Riley and Abram set out to find Mikael's home. They battled their way through the storm, managing to stick to the route. As they were setting off, Elsie asked Typhoeus to help her locate her friends. She told him that he wasn't to eat Riley, or Abram, they were her friends and belonged to her and even if Riley had separated them before, they weren't to be harmed. Typhoeus appeared to agree to this, on the condition that he still be allowed to taste each of them in turn. Elsie was at first a little insulted that her flesh wasn't enough, but Typhoeus claimed that he didn't want to wear her out by constant feedings. Her pride mollified, Elsie allowed him to lead the way. They talked about the night before on the way, but Typhoeus hadn't been able to get into the Clocktower from the outside. He had contented himself by preying on the townsfolk.

                                    Abram and Riley had found the building and were standing outside when they heard the heavy tread of massive footsteps. Moments later, Elsie walked into view. Then, before the others could speak, two massive arms settled onto the ground on either side of her, and Typhoeus's head came down to leer at them.

                                        Session 19 - The Family Grows

                                        Riley's reaction to seeing Typhoeus loom out of the dust was immediate and instinctive. "Elsie, run!" They took off, racing away. Elsie stood where she was, sharing an exasperated look with Typhoeus at her friend's ridiculous behaviour. Abram, seeing that Elsie was making no move to get away, and that she was carrying Baby, stood his ground and hefted his pickaxe. And Riley, realising that none of their throng was following, slowed and turned, pulling out their shotgun, and made their way back.

                                        Once Elsie explained to her dim-witted friends that no, Typhoeus was not going to eat them, he had promised her that he wouldn't, the other two reacted with a mixture of disbelief and curiosity. Abram bellowed over the storm at the Sublimati, demanding to know his intentions, to know how Elsie had hurt her hand, and why he was so willing to let them live. Typhoeus appeared bored with the conversation, but said that he would only dine on them with their permission, and that he intended to form a family with Elsie. He wanted children, and hoped to make them with her.

                                        During this, Abram and Riley realised that there was something wrong with Baby. The child was lying very still, listless and unmoving, not reacting to the dust settling on their face. Abram asked Elsie if the child was alright, but she refused to let them near it. She surreptitiously checked the child, but they seemed perfectly fine to her eyes.

                                        Riley was appalled. They turned towards the alchemists' house. The child was dead, that was clear. At least they could still do some good by rescuing the Promethean trapped inside. They checked the inside of Mikael's house and found it clean but barren, devoid of any personal effects, with no sign of the alchemists in sight. They then continued around the side of the building until they found the coal cellar-like entrance they recalled from the dream. By the time they reached it, the others had caught up. Typhoeus loomed overhead, making Riley's skin crawl. Abram asked what he was planning to do, and he looked over at Elsie.

                                        "Protect her."

                                        Abram smashed the door open with his pickaxe, and the Prometheans rushed inside, descending into the room below. Typhoeus, thanks to his enormous size, had to wait outside.

                                        They found themselves stepping into a laboratory that closely mimicked what Riley had seen in her dreams. Creatures rendered unrecognisable were splayed on each of the room's three long tables. Chemicals bubbled and boiled in beakers and tubes, distilling strange solutions. The very air stank of Pyros, joined with a sharp, chemical smell that pierced straight through the nose to the brain. Hunched over one of the tables, lit by a single gas lamp above their heads, stood Boris and Mikael, turning to face the source of the noise. Boris frowned and stormed towards them, while Mikael laid his hands on a foot-long object not dissimilar to a medieval mace. It was a round, heavy ball of metal on top of a short shaft.

                                        Boris stormed up to the characters, demanding that they leave, calling them trespassers and criminals and threatening to summon the sheriff. Riley held their ground, and shouted back that they knew what the alchemists were doing, that they had come to rescue the Promethean they were torturing. Boris looked like he was going to respond, but instead he doubled over in a heavy coughing fit. As he straightened he cast something on the ground. A flash of light lit up the dark room, filling the throng's eyes with dazzling colours, and through it they heard Boris's voice booming. "Leave now. Run!"

                                        Elsie felt an unnatural emotion creeping into her mind, a total, irrational fear of Boris. As the others went for their weapons, Mikael raised up some rubber tubes which made the room hum and shake. Riley shot Boris in his chest, making a bloody ruin of it, but impossibly the alchemist continued to move. Elsie fought down the urge to flee and threw herself at Boris, bearing him to the ground. Baby fell to the ground in the midst of her panicked frenzy, striking the hard stone and rolling beneath a table. She screamed out for Typhoeus, and the entire building shook as his talons carved out a larger opening to the room, big enough for his arms to reach inside. But with her on top of Boris, there was little she could do.

                                        Abram and Riley moved past the struggling figures to attack Mikael. The tall, slim man quickly swallowed the contents of a clear vial, his skin rippling as Abram advanced. The pickaxe came flying through the air to strike Mikael in the cheek. It was like hitting a steel pole. Mikael's head turned, a small sliver of blood visible through the grey scales covering what had been soft skin a moment before. Riley fired their shotgun again, but in their haste and panic the shot went wide.

                                        Mikael focussed on Abram. The Prometheans watched the muscles of his throat visibly rearrange themselves, widening his gullet, and he retched a stream of thick black bile onto Abram. The substance was corrosive and burned Abram's skin horribly, coating him from head to toe, though had he not been Tammuz with his own muscles and blood streams already saturated with such bile he knew he would have been far more wounded. Riley ducked past Abram, shooting Mikael in the stomach. He reacted more like a human should after being kicked in the stomach, not being shot with a shotgun at point blank range. Through the tattered remains of his shirt, they could see that his stomach was also now covered in the same dark scales.

                                        Behind them, Elsie was getting the better of Boris. He had thrown several punches, and she was bleeding from the nose and a split lip, but she had him on the floor with her hands around his throat. She ground herself down against him, excited to feel him struggling for breath. His eyes rolled back into his head at last, and she leapt up and jumped for joy on his chest.

                                        Mikael dove for cover behind one of the surgical tables. He raised the mace in his hand, and a blast of lightning launched out of it and struck Abram full on. Abram roared, frustrated that his blows couldn't break through Mikael's skin. He followed the alchemist, striking again and again with the pickaxe, each of his blows jarring his arms and unable to break through Mikael's skin. At last, feeling his body coming apart under Mikael's onslaught, Abram released his grip on the Soldier Role he had embraced for so long, diving into Stannum. The ground beneath and around them shook in sympathy as a wasteland flowed out from his body, flux rippling and distorting the world around them. Mikael paled as lightning danced along Abram's arms. He held out his hands, pouring his Pyros into his newfound power, but Mikael threw himself out of the way and the blast instead tore the wall behind him apart.

                                        Elsie paused in her excited bouncing when she felt movement beneath her feet. She looked down, curious, as a bloody hand burst out from the wound in Boris's chest. She was sent stumbling backwards as the alchemist's bloated form came apart, torn open by something emerging from the inside. It was a human male, an adult, naked and perfectly proportioned. The gore and fluids of Boris's body slipped from his form as if they could find no purchase on his skin. Long, blonde hair cascaded down to his shoulders, and bright blue eyes scanned the room. Elsie stared this new creature, excited. He turned towards Boris's body and snatched something from the broken corpse's pockets, moments before Typhoeus's hand reached down and wrapped around his waist. As quickly as he had come, the beautiful vision vanished, hauled up and into the storm.

                                        Mikael was backed into a corner. He tried to drive his opponents off by sending fragments of his stony hide blasting out at them, shredding his clothes as spikes and shards tore through them. They found no purchase in the Prometheans' skin, however, as Riley pushed Abram out of the way in time. He was bleeding heavily, his remaining scales scorched by lightning strikes, blood oozing between them after many shotgun blasts. He held his mace above his head, and the air around it hummed with electrical energy. It exploded out, the Pyros-tainted lightning scorching the flesh of each Promethean in the ruin. Elsie and Riley were blackened and burned, and even Abram felt his flesh scream in protest as the energy coursed through him.

                                        Riley raised their gun in trembling hands, trying to focus on Mikael, but the shot barely winged him as it went past. Elsie backed up, trying to get out of range of another lightning surge. Abram raised his hands to the sky, pouring his Pyros into a final blast. The lightning swirled around him, crackling and leaping as it surged into his chest, before exploding out in a beam of white-hot energy. It sheered through Mikael, tearing his scales and flesh apart, flinging the alchemist across the chamber and leaving him a crumpled, smoking ruin on the ground.

                                        The Prometheans took a long moment to get their breath back. Both Riley and Elsie were closer to death than they had ever been before, limping and clutching at injured limbs. Abram, the only able-bodied member of the group, moved to the crank mechanism Riley pointed out to him. The same pit, shielded by a simple curtain, lay at the back of the chamber. Riley called down, asking the prisoner to thump twice on the cage if they understood that they were being rescued and promised not to attack them. A long few seconds passed, and then the characters heard two distant thumps.

                                        Abram worked the winch and hauled an iron coffin up from the depths. They had to search Mikael's body to find the key, heated to the point where it was still painfully hot to touch. It fit the lock, however, and allowed them to open the coffin.

                                        The being inside was a ruin of a person. Their naked body was covered in deep, surgical wounds, crusted fluids, and weeping sores. They were so thin and emaciated that their gender was impossible to spot without diving between their legs. Their face was largely obscured by matted hair, but it shifted out of the way to show a single brown eye, bloodshot and full of anger. The rest of their face was covered by something like a gas mask, making it impossible for them to talk. They were strapped into the coffin, and Abram hurried to undo the straps while Riley shrugged off their coat. When the straps were undone the Promethean fell forward, landing in Riley's arms as they smoothly wrapped them in the coat. Lifting the prisoner into their arms, Riley carried them back to the surface.

                                                        Session 20 - You have twins!

                                                        The storm was still raging when the Prometheans returned to the surface. As they set foot on the ground above, however, they felt a massive surge of Azothic energy from the centre of town. There, somehow, they could see the Gothic spire of the Nightmare's town hall, visible through the dust. As they watched the peak of the spire tore apart, revealing the seething, twisted form of the Arch-Qashmal at its heart. The creature flared its wings, its jaws and faces turned to look at them, and it rose from its place within the tower. Then, as they watched, its body and the tower burned away, leaving only the storm. Each of the Prometheans felt Vitriol surge within their chests, and then all trace of the Qashmal vanished.

                                                        A wail caught their attention, and they turned to see that the beautiful man who had emerged from Boris's body was pinned to the ground with one of Typhoeus's claws embedded in his gut. The sublimati was slavering and barely restraining himself from wolfing down the alchemist. He looked in Elsie's direction and begged her to let him eat the man, saying he smelled so, so perfect. For once, no member of the throng had any problems with Elsie's callous disregard for human life. "Eat up," she said, and Typhoeus began to force the man into his mouth faster than he could chew.

                                                        They took shelter in Mikael's home while Typhoeus ate. They placed the injured Promethean they had rescued in a chair and quickly swept the house again for anything of use. Abram did turn up some bits and pieces, cloth and thread, that he could use as medical supplies. He set to work tending to the Promethean's words. Before he began he had to pry off the mask bound to the captive's face. It appeared to have been soldered or otherwise fused to their flesh, and required him to leverage both his considerable strength and medical knowledge to tear it away without taking most of the face away with it. He succeeded, and was able to remove the mask taking only a fairly minimal amount of skin and tissue with it.

                                                        Once the mask was off, he could finally talk with the Promethean. He set to work bandaging their wounds, and ask for their name.

                                                        "They called me Vessel," came the response. The voice was soft and very hoarse, with a strong lisp thanks to a mouth of broken and missing teeth.

                                                        "Oh. Urgh. Do you want to have another name?" Abram was more than a little disgusted, but Vessel just shrugged and said they didn't mind it.

                                                        Abram noticed small red-brown flecks of rust fell from Vessel's lips with every word they spoke. At around this point it also became clear that they were a she. He couldn't determine Vessel's lineage, but guessed that she was most likely an Extempore.

                                                        Once Vessel was all patched up, Elsie shooed Abram and Riley out of the room with some reference to "women's business" and told them to make themselves useful. Once they were gone she promptly ignored Vessel, who quietly passed out, and began to examine Baby. She finally saw what the others had been seeing, that Baby was very, very dead, and had been for a while. She sat there for a while. Outside the room, Abram finished clearing the rest of the house of anything useful, swept dust into the house so that he could lie down in his element without being exposed to the storm, and went to sleep. For the first time since arriving in Sour Springs, he didn't dream.

                                                        An hour or so later, Elsie came to a decision. She took the pearl bracelet she wore and all the materials she could gather from the lab in the basement and filled a large bucket with what she could find. She submerged Baby in the materials and poured her Pyros into the infusion. Perhaps, if she could make Baby a Promethean, no one would notice the difference?

                                                        Beneath the surface, Baby's body rippled. The flesh bulged and split. New growths appeared. Then, as she watched, Baby's body tore in two, splitting from the head and right down their spine. The two halves writhed and formed into twisted little bulbs of tentacles and hard chitin, foetus-like faces staring out at her. The two creatures burst from the bucket, hissing and spitting. They retreated towards the other side of the room. For a moment they appeared poised to attack, when a strange calm swept over them. Elsie took her final step from the Pilgrimage, leaving it behind, and asserted her Lordship over the newborn Pandorans.

                                                        By the time the others woke, her new offspring had spun their cocoons and began to mature. Elsie had packed them into her backpack and fallen asleep beside them.

                                                        Abram was the first to wake, but found that Vessel had risen before him. She had found Mikael's store of fresh water and was gently bathing her arms and face, washing away the filth and stains from her time in captivity. She was still thin to the point of being skeletal, but all of her visible cuts and wounds Abram hadn't yet bandaged had closed over and healed, leaving only scars to show where they had been. When asked, she said the lightning had healed her, and held a hand out to Abram. He took it, and energy poured from her into him, causing his own burns and cuts from the fight with Mikael to heal. He recognised it as an Alembic of the Electrification Transmutation, and thanked her for sparing the energy.

                                                        The throng then split. Abram had decided to loot the now-abandoned General Store under cover of the Black Blizzard. He sent Riley back to the house where they'd been staying, to secure their belongings, as he knew Riley wouldn't approve. Then, accompanied by Elsie, Typhoeus, and Vessel, he led the way through the storm to the store. He forced the door easily and soon his arms were filled with everything that could possibly be of use.

                                                        As he worked, he and Elsie talked. By their reckoning, with the alchemists and Qashmal gone, there was no reason they could not now set themselves up as lords of Sour Springs. If nothing else, the town owed them for their service. Once they had delivered their new belongings back to their lodgings, they decided that they would head across to the local bank and relieve it of everything inside its vaults. Elsie profusely agreed with Abram, even egging him on. Why not just drive out the ungrateful humans, those who had scorned them while they sought to end the Nightmare? Sour Springs could become their own private sanctuary in the wasteland, with only enough humans left to feed Typhoeus. This... Abram was less sure of, but he wasn't as opposed to the idea as he once might have been. Typhoeus had proven himself an ally, after all, far more so than any human in town.

                                                        They made the walk back up the street towards their abandoned home, ugly thoughts swirling in Abram's mind in a perfect reflection of the storm outside.
