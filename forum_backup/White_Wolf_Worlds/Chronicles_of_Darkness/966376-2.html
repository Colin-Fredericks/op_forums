                                        Threads like this make me think a Sad Puppies or Gamer Gate situation is inevitable for us, too.

                                        As to the original thread topic - I agree with Vent0. People aren't stereotypes*, but stereotypes didn't arise out of nothing - they often reflect real social and cultural phenomena (usually problems). Don't embrace the stereotype - that's a waste of a character - but if the natural progress of a character's development requires that they "go there", don't shy away from it. Everyone is flawed, after all, and forcing a character to be unnaturally perfect isn't good, either.

                                        Legacy-wise, most of the older Legacies are appropriate. The Fall makes it impossible to pinpoint an origin for any of the ancient Legacies, after all. The Dreamspeakers claim to be the original mages, older even than Atlantis, who first found the Isle of Dragons. Its canon that the Elemental Masteries are a worldwide phenomena, found among all the peoples of the world. And so on. With their origins lost, who can say what's "authentic" Native American magic, or African, or . . . .

                                        And its always fun to use the Fallen Pillar to smack pretentious mages in the face, when it turns out that that the "street bum" is actually the most powerful Thyrsus in a hundred mile radius

                                        The old World of Darkness has some good material, too. Vampires among the Aztecs, Greymere Glen is a "haunted" faerie stronghold accidentally created by fallen Vikings (and in North America, too) . . .




                                        *although (in real life) I've known people who thought it was funny to play to the relevant stereotype, just to mess with a prejudiced individual
