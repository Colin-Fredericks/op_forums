    How to create an Angel ?

    12-02-2014, 02:38 AM
    Currently for my game I'm trying to make a angel but I can't really find any rules in the book for it. I saw a few examples and they brought up some questions.
    So for example do angels have two forms like demons ?
    Because the book shows angel by the name of Mr Shiver but not once does it talk about what his true form looks like only his current one.

    When creating an angel is there any rule I need to follow ?

    Currently I'm making an angel that is going to exist with in a Command and Control Infrastructure. This angel is hooked into every camera and drone in the city made by the company and it is tasked to monitor and send angels out to locations of demon activity.

    So far I was thinking description wise it looks like a slim frail human being and it sits crossed legged unmoving as it is hooked up to millions of different wires. It has 4 arms that type on many different keyboards and has eyes all over its head to watch all the Screens.

      Like someone said in your other thread that started about the subject, the rules for ephemeral entities are in the Appendix II; the mechanical guidelines in Demon: the Descent start on p.338. The rules are for ephemeral beings as a whole - the rules absolutely work for angels, but the given Numina and other stuff are written to be very general so they can be used across types. That section will point you in the right direction, but don't be afraid to write something more specific if you need it for your game - I personally have some tweaks lined up for angels in my game.

      As far as I know, angels do not by default have two forms; angels that do so probably have the Mortal Mask Numina or a similar effect. I know some folks have argued that an angel can be designed to look exactly like a normal human and forgo the need for something like Mortal Mask, which accounts for all the human or mostly human looking angels in Demon/GMC without the Mortal Mask numina; it's up to you if you want to run with that or not.

      The concept you've listed is entirely appropriate for an angel, though it sounds like most of it's "effects" are plot devices rather than actual mechanics - which is fine! There's absolutely nothing in the rules that would stop you from creating that angel, and it's not a bad option at all. If I was in your position I might consider making that thing a Cryptid instead, but that's just an aesthetic difference. In any case, good luck putting it together!

      That should start you off, if you have any other questions let us know. I'm probably going to be putting together an opinion piece to follow this up right after I post this.

          Okay, soapbox time: I feel like it's important to point out some of the descrepancies between how the fluff treats angels and how the mechanics portray them; whether you accept the differences or work to reconcile them, I think it's worth talking about in order to skip a big source of confusion early on.

          In the Demon text, angels are treated mostly like Demons with a couple of extra limitations. They talk a little about how angels are tied to Infrastructure and that they have access to Twilight, but the majority of the text portrays loyalists as beings that are fundamentally indistinguishable from the Unchained, to the point that you should have no idea if the person you're talking to is human, angel or demon. Infiltration is presented as a high priority concern, and if hunter angels are sent after you they will chase you to the ends of the earth with no issue. Example angels are usually human or semi-human looking, and limitations for how long they can stay in the field are never discussed.

          The mechanics point a much different animal: an angel's ephemeral nature and their ties to Infrastructure have a lot bigger impact than the fluff would suggest. Infrastructure is extremely important to angels, as it's both fuel source and staging ground for Manifestations. Also, let's look at the practicality of infiltrating the Unchained by going over fundamental differences between angels and demons.

          • Demons are inherently physical creatures that can go wherever they want. Angels are ephemeral beings that have to use the Materialize, Possession or Claimed to walk the world physically, each which have their own issues. Looking just at Materialize as a baseline, an angel has to start in Infrastructure to use it (technically the Infrastructure has to be Open but a Rank 1 angel can achieve that with a single Essence), the Manifestation lasts for 1 hour per success and then it dumps you back into Twilight. Deep cover is nearly impossible under these conditions. Even in the short term, Mortal Mask is not infallible even considering the angel's likely high Finesse; by my reading, users of Mortal Mask wouldn't enjoy the penalty if a demon uses Aetheric Resonance, which isn't automatic but is a major issue if the angel is trying to pull one over on a paranoid demon.

          • Despite loyalists having the more ephemeral bodies, the Unchained's identities are much more mutable. Short version: demons can switch covers and change into their demonic form. An angel trying to blend it almost certainly lacks these abilities.

          • The Unchained can sense Aether, while angels can't. Any test for Demon-hood factoring that in will be almost impossible for an angel to pass.

          • Hell, angels don't even have the ability to lie perfectly. The problems of infiltration without that are pretty self-evident.

          I could keep going but as long as you're looking for it the issues jump out, so I'm not going to waste your time. The way I handle the discrepancies in my game? Well, honestly most of the time I accept that the differences are there and shift the focus to compensate. Angels in my demon games are going to be the familiar but ultimately alien Other, a host of ephemeral agents that serve the G-M from the shadows and stay within bastions of power for the most part. For horror and paranoia related to infiltration and betrayal, I won't hesitate to use regular humans or possibly some cryptids, but by and large my workhorse is going to be other Unchained.

          And for those times where I need an angel that works like the angels in the fluff? Well, I have some tricks up my sleeves...

                          I've run two short Demon games now and I just outright ignore the ephemeral rules. Each angel is a custom built A.I. loyal to the GM. Whether a cultists got that intelligence downloaded into their mind, a human body was built from scratch, a robot was made to house the angel or any other form it could possibly be. It is still an angel. some are impossible to tell apart form human or Demon, some can body jump, some live wholly on the internet as a living virus.
                          The only trick is defining what you want it to be(form), what it can do(numina/stats) and what are it's limits(bane/ban). I also make a big deal out of how it was summoned.


                          As an example:
                          Viso Born into this world time and time again through the reflected light of a far away star where it lies dormant between summons. the cultists differ each time but there procedure is the same. they catch angelic light in holy mirrors gifted to them by God. The trapped light loops impossibly in the reflection until a host body steps into the light (the perfect spot for angel-jacking). Viso enters the host it's blistering radiance whipping away all thoughts until only the divine words remain. Its mission is to identify and observe the unchained. in the host body Viso looks completely human unless it is using it's powers which manifest as bleeding light.
                          Rank 2
                          Power:3 finesse:5 resistance:3
                          Numina: Teleportation, invisibility, mind wipe,
                          Bane: Viso looses apart of it's self when it's host body comes in contact with a reflective surface.
                          Ban:it scope is limited to areas touched by the light of its star. at most a couple miles diameter from it's summoning circle, adjust for plot.

                                      Technically speaking an angel is perfectly capable of mimicking an Unchained's ability to switch covers via the Mortal Mask numen. It says, specifically, that it -can- be used during Manifestation, not that it must be used. It also doesn't specify that the ability makes the entity appear like a specific human chosen at entity-creation and the ability to perceive past this would be the equivalent imperfection for an angel that a Demon suffers via compromise. It's just when an angel screws up, God doesn't care (though the demons do!). The problem, however, lies within the essence conundrum where eventually hopping in this manner between individuals is costly on the essence pool.

                                      A more long-winded technique is to write-up generic NPCs (such as office workers, police, etc) that serve the role of possess-fodder and have hunter angels able to hop back and forth via possession much like an Agent from the Matrix trilogy. It is achievable via having each hunter have the Seek numen and to assume that there are many small pieces of infrastructure across the city. This is more mechanically trying, but since the mechanics happen in the background, it will create the image that the angel is able to just pop out from every door. Mechanically, of course, he is possessing a host, shooting some guns, leaving and popping into a new one and so forth. In light of the above option (and the fact that any Demonic Form ability or Exploit/Embed can be made into numen) it would seem better to have an angel with Mortal Mask and Urban Fluidity, but it works thematically well.

                                      It is therefore simple to give an angel a "demonic form" by giving it a horrifying base design and several images via Mortal Mask. When it wishes to assume its true form it simply turns off the mortal mask numen and gets creative.

                                      My personal rule for defeating the essence conundrum is to treat it like NPC willpower pools by having short-hand limitations. (This character has half of his essence in this scene, next scene he has it all back again, because he's assumed to be restoring it and using it off-screen).
