                                    I've always thought the overall setting, and the bardos in particular, needed some work. Here's how I'd do it:

                                    Geniuses first appeared in 1895 in central Europe, although they quickly began to appear all over the world. History since then is more-or-less our history. The physical universe is exactly the same. Mars is the sterile Mars we're familiar with, and non-Genii will ever see anything different. However, some Wonders include the creation of unique pocket universes called bardos, which run on the conceits of the Wonder. Every bardo created by a particular Genius is distinct and isolated from that created by a different Genius, no matter how similar the Wonders are. Two Geniuses who travel to Mars by astral projection will find completely different alien civilisations, and will be unable to visit each other's Mars. Another Genius who builds a zero-point energy spacecraft could find yet another kind of alien, perhaps not native to Mars. It's also possible that either Wonder would transport the Genius to the actual, real-world Mars. Bringing concrete evidence of bardos into the real world encounters the same problems as Wonders interacting with the public.
                                    The same goes for time-travel, which can reach either real or fantastic history. The Lemurians have cobbled together an almost infinitely baroque "secret history" of the world from their time excursions, but this is just the historical version of their overarching "scientific" theories. One thing bardos have in common is that they lack Geniuses. The hypothetical Needle Grail, the Axiom of Inspiration itself, is rumoured to allow travel to bardos with Geniuses, or perhaps with nothing but Geniuses.
