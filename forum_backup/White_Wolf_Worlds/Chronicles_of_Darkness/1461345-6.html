        Have a well-equipped Haven to end your night on. Cultivate Resilience. Dress like you don't want your identity discovered. Owe some favors to an Ascendant Dragon if need be. Pursue Disciplines and Devotions to let you telepresence your way around your flesh body being extremely flammable.

        Most people in-setting are going to have more free time and freedom of movement after dark, and vampires work just fine in crossover play with that in mind — the characters do not inhabit an abstract setting where stuff happens at all hours, considering that the Shadow is largely nocturnal, the Primordial Dream is nocturnal outside of Chambers and the Day, there are entire sections of the Hedge where the sun never rises, Infrastructure is frequently an indoor affair, the Underworld is the Underworld, and monstrous involvement in mortal society means folks like mummies (and, indeed, vampires) can contrive secret passageways around the city that only those in the know or obsessed enough to piece together the architecture would be aware of. Hunter's antagonist book for vampires puts an extensive warren of tunnels underneath a Philadelphia cemetery.

        Their weakness is that they're mortal and their powers run on fuel they can't get themselves. "Their book" is one page of notes and three pages of Merits they can take.

                A starting vampire character can dress for winter and take no damage at all, or cut that down to one point of lethal damage per ten minutes if for some reason they need to stand around in the middle of the day, but the sorts of Kindred who spontaneously combust under the daystar have been dead for at least half a century or otherwise been leaning hard into being an otherworldly monster whose "other world" is "institutional corruption" and/or "the night."
