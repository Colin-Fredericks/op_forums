                I feel my greatest irritation with Obligation and Restraint is that it encourages compliance and discourages Geniuses being weird misfits. The "mad science" stories of transgression/hubris and fallout/nemesis often preach about irresponsibility of the scientist, how their denouncement of their creation at critical moment doomed them and people around them. It could be great basis for Genius's Integrity stat, but irresponsible behavior is already easily punished by Havoc, attention of conspiracies, public, and Clockstoppers.
                I still feel what we need is more alien measurement system that tells when Saturday morning cartoon villain crosses the line into present day Joker.

                Genius 2e Integrity pitch of the month: Content, how much of the world, society, universe and themselves does Genius accept as is. Very few Geniuses could be found in complete zen state of acceptance since such nihilism stands almost anti-thesis to discontent that motives and drives most Inspired. The difference between 1 and 0 however is that Genius at 1 is at least willing to tolerate themselves enough to resist giving in into completely alien desire to vivisect reality itself and rebuild it by their singular vision.

                Breaking point to such Geniuses would be whenever the aspects of reality they are happy with would abruptly and completely change. What comes next is Genius either needs to seek closure, understanding and acceptance to what happened, or deny and bargain to try to change and force it back. This creates tension between all Inspired: pursuit of your future may thwart what little other Genius liked about our world.
                Discontent is Geniuses' breaking point, reality suddenly snatching their textbook from their hand and teasingly holding it above their head out of their reach.

                Each Catalyst, like Changelings' Seeming could come with Blessing and Curse or Joy and Frustration. Frustration is what automatically drains Geniuses Content and drives them to their drawing boards. Joy is what helps them find happiness in what they have.

                On this measurement tree Ford Pines would be relatively stable example finding happiness in exploring the weird phenomena of the world, while Rick Santchez would stay poster boy of rogues trying to hide his unhappiness in copious consumption and straw nihilism.

                                                    Another bi-montly, "Weird tries to reinvent Obligation" -post.
                                                    How about we go Blue and Orange? New Genius Integrity: Patience. The higher Patience brings bonuses to extended rolls, some social skills, and gives Paragon some contingencies against Havoc as Genius takes a step back to look at the bigger picture and think things through. Lower you go, the easier it gets to Kitbash, "reverse polarity", and leap to (often correct) conclusions.
                                                    Sins against Patience are spontaneous, reckless, all-in ad hoc actions. However when Genius is rewarded for taking time Player may choose to increase their Patience score by one (add Exp cost, maybe?), representing whether Genius decides to change their approach or not.
                                                    In addition Patient Geniuses have easier time to avoid turning Unmada but get penalties on Unmada checks after becoming one, risking Illumination. Reckless Genii are the opposite: penalties on Unmada check if not already Unmada, bonuses against Illumination.

                                                    Reason: Patience makes an interesting axis between classical Order and Chaos, while making only small suggestions about Genius's bigger picture morality. More unhinged Mad Scientists are often portrayed as extremely single track thinkers who put all their eggs in single basket, but are masters of improv and turnabout schemes. Running out of Patience seems like natural way to forsake their humanity. At the same time high Patience Geniuses are likely to fall for fallacy so common in real life, thinking of oneself as completely objective, forgetting that personal experiences and bias can still influence our "rational" judgement.

                                                            With Genius 2E, what I'm trying to go for is "Occult Science Horror." Think of stuff like Re-Animator. People using forces that aren't within mortal understanding, to do things that science says shouldn't be possible, usually with horrible results. A person finding a mathematical formula that opens a portal to hell, a roboticist who loses control of his creation, a painter whose paintings are eerily lifelike and occasionally swallow a person whole, things like that. Not quite science fiction, not quite fantasy, definitely all about weird, scary shit.

                                                            Another theme is actually sort of a condemnation of the scientism and intellectual arrogance that Genius 1E sort of unintentionally advanced. The Genii are intended to be, quite frankly, mostly assholes and lunatics. Advancing as a Genius is almost always going to be easier for Genii who disregard the well-being of others, and the detachment of Inspired society from humanity will generally be played up as a bad thing that the Peerage and Lemuria maintain for maintaining their own power. It's hard to be a good Genius.
