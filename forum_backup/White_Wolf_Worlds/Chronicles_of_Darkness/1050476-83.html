        Originally posted by Unahim View Post
        If it cuts in half extended actions, it should cut in half the time it takes to navigate to somewhere, too. As that is nothing but essentially a contest of extended actions in the Hedge. Yet, it doesn't for some odd reason.
        I will repeat: Hedge travel takes an arbitrary length of time. Making it go faster is accomplished by making it easier to accomplish your intent of getting somewhere fast by increasing your Speed a bit and giving you the Edge in the chase. There is no actual way to make it go faster otherwise, because there is no base speed to increase for a Hedge chase. You get somewhere quicker by being more likely to succeed at trying to get somewhere quickly, and if that means running down a trod at what hindsight will clock at thirty miles an hour then nobody will notice it in the moment unless they've got some sort of sense for temporal weirdness.

        And if you let it do it, there's also a lot of oddities there, what with people chasing you having any chance to catch you at all when you're essentially arriving twice as fast. It's a pretty confusing Contract overall, and "Well, Hedge weird!" only goes so far. The ways through which the Hedge does things don't need to be explained, but what effect it has on our end state kinda does, as does the way our characters experience that change... since we'll be moving our characters through that change towards the end state. :P
        It changes the passage of time. The primary effect of the Contract is its effect on the passage of time. Your ability to walk faster is negligible because it is affecting your personal time and that interacts with the Hedge in the way that interacts with any psychoactive space. This isn't Celerity, it's being able to spend what felt like ten minutes talking at the Goblin Market and come back to find that a whole night has passed. It's Arcadian Metabolism as a generalized toggle.

        Which is fine, of course, you're not forced to answer any questions or anything, but we'll just end up at "Ok, thanks, but I'll wait for someone to answer the questions asked" at this rate. ^^
        You're asking questions that don't have textual answers, which amounts to an opinion check. Nobody in this thread can help you find answers that don't exist; this is all interpretation.

        Specifically, out of those quoted, these have not been answered:
        - What happens when a group of Changelings travel together, and we apply the Contract only to a few of them? Or we apply opposing sides of the Contract within the same group?
        - Can they still travel together, or not?
        - What does it look like to the Changelings involved in these various different cases? What does it feel like to be one of the Changelings affected by the Contract or not, in that scenario? How would you describe it?
        They can certainly travel together. Nothing unusual happens, unless you're keeping track over a long enough time to see changes like fatigue or healing come into play, at which point you have an incongruity to observe but not much else. If they split up they may find that more happens to one set of them than the other before they regroup, but again, that's a long-term oddity. Maybe some characters feel ill — that sort of flavoring is part of what the book is getting at when it tells you to personalize the feel of your character's Contracts.

        So not pure mechanics or actual wording, but how would it feel to characters, look to them... if a few Autumn courtiers study the terms of the Contract and its precise effects, how do they word what it can and can't do, etc... ?
        That sort of thing.
        Shorter equivalents of Rip Van Winkle's infamous outing. Skewed day/night cycles. People getting tired from a relatively small amount of running or failing to notice that the seconds take longer to pass. It is easy to overlook if you're not paying attention to it, because it happens in reality in smaller chunks throughout a normal day.

            It's not like I knew there would be no answers when I asked, though! ;D

            I like to approach things like this from the perspective of writing clear rules that are easily understood by new players, and will make smooth transitions between different tables and groups easy. In the end I can always rule things however I like for my table. That's a foregone conclusion. I just wasn't sure if this Contract was really as wildly unclear and confusing as I thought, or if it was just me.

            Considering the responses, I think it's fair to say it's not me. ^^ People can't even say for sure what it does to Speed, a simple, hard mechanics question. :P

            I don't agree with your conclusions, but it's essentially clear that if I want a better defined Contract I'll just have to rewrite it myself, so that is Question Answered as far as I'm concerned.

            It has been rightly observed before that not everything with mechanics has to be or should be a Condition — "any effect that progresses over time does so at half or twice the rate, ending when the target leaves the Hedge or the sun goes down or comes up, and they don't get a Beat for either of those things" firmly shifts Flickering Hours onto the "not" end of the scale.
            It could be a Tilt, though. They say tilts "can" easily causes Conditions outside of action scenes, but not that they must. They also say: "Personal Tilts only apply to one character and include ways in which that character can overcome the effect." Well, the Contract includes a way for individual characters to overcome the effect placed upon them, and the effect is placed on individuals, not on a location or anything. So it could easily be a Tilt.
            They suggest not using Tilts outside of action scenes, but to use Conditions instead... but people use Tilts like Heavy Winds in scenes where we're not going by turn order (so, not an action scene) all the time, so that's a pretty arbitrary thing.

            In the end Tilts are really just shorthand for a collection of different rules effects that you want to be able to easily reference with just a few lines of text without having to type it all out again.

            Whether or not it makes sense to make this a Tilt thus basically comes down to whether you're going to use this same Tilt in other places or not. If you'll only use it once, might as well be in the Contract. As soon as it's two or more times... sure, make it a Tilt.
