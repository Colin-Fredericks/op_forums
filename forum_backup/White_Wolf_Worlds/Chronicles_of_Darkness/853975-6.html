The following is a 'family tree' of sorts, showing the hierarchy of beings. bear in mind that the 'true divines' and the 'primordial divines' could be the Olympians and the Protogenoi respectively, or they might be the Archangels and the Unmoved Mover, or they might be Bodhisattvas and Devas.

        Primordial Divines > Titans > True Divines > Demigods

        Titans > Titan Shards > Titanspawn

        Gigantes > Enkidu

        Titan Shards are very powerful things and are the equivalent of Idigam. On the bright side, each Shard has its own prison within the Drasil, as a result being one of the losers in the War in Heaven, the eternal conflict between the True Divines and the Titans. On the other hand, Titanspawn are powerful entities, although they vary in power. Titanspawn are creatures created by the Titan Shards with work towards freeing their masters from their entrapment. Some are simple monsters, some are, to all appearances, human. Needless to say, they can cause quite a bit of trouble in both the Drasil and the Phenomenal world.

        Gigantes are now basically independent beings who do not necessarily hate demigods but covet many of the same things, such as Gardr and Temples.

            It's not quite in the format you wanted, and it hasn't been aligned with the latest revisions, but here are my ideas for the followings: Following of Vassals (nickname: Lackeys)
            This following believes that Demigods have been selected by the True God (or the True Goddess, or True Gods, plural) to be their servants on Earth. Unfortunately for them, they don't have much more insight into what their gods may want than mortals do, so they tend either to become scrupulous interpreters of holy texts, or to wander other worlds looking for beings who fit the description they have in mind (popular choices have been powerful spirits, Astral versions of various gods, and the Divine Fire - the God-Machine has been less popular, as it usually grinds Demigods who offer to serve it into raw material for new angels). This latter option makes them the following which interacts most frequently with other supernaturals, although not always in a positive way.
            A majority of their members are of the monotheistic faiths, but there are also some who believe themselves to be servants of polytheistic deities. Their Cults and Temples almost invariably mirror their chosen faith, though there is something of a split between Vassals who found or latch onto relatively orthodox groups and those who start idiosyncratic sects who see a given Vassal as their primary
            Opinions on Second Ascension are also divided: most believe it to be the final reward for their service, with the polytheists among them believing that the True Gods promote worthy Demigods to their own ranks. However, a few believe that Second Ascension actually represents damnation or some form of demotion, and point to the fact that it is the Demigods who became most distant from humanity - and thus most distracted from their mission among humans - who are most likely to ascend again.
            Wings are the most common element tying together the numinous forms of this following, symbolizing their role as go between for the higher heavens and the mundane world.

            Following of Sparks (nickname: Fragments)
            This following believes that there is only one thing which is truly divine, faith itself. Demigods are the result when humans have a great concentration of Faith.
            Members of this following tend to collect faith from members of all religions equally, seeing each as a similar means for faith to express itself in the world. They seldom belong to an organized mortal religion themselves, but some may believe that the worldwide sea of Faith is itself conscious and thus be able to reconcile it with some form of monotheism or pantheism (there are virtually no polytheists among them - the Sparks believe that polytheistic religions originally followed Demigods who have long since Ascended for the second time and thus no longer have individual consciousness). The Following of Sparks is itself more like a religion than most of the other Followings: unlike the following of Reincarnates, they believe in a higher power which should be served, and unlike the Following of Vassals, they have reached an agreement on roughly what this power is and what it wants. Simply put, it wants more of itself to be spread throughout the world. Sparks accomplish this mission by spreading new Cults and founding new Temples, which tends to make them popular with other followings even if their reasons for doing so do not, for they create far more of these resources than they use themselves and are often willing to share.
            Sparks believe that Second Ascension is the process of becoming one with Faith again, and they see it not only the pinnacle and goal of their own existence, but of everyone else's as well.
            The numinous forms in this following tend to be fiery or to radiate some other form of energy, reflecting the belief among followers that they are filled with the energy of faith.

            Following of Reincarnates
            This following believes that each Demigod is the reincarnated form of some known deity from a known religion. What pantheon they believe themselves to hail from varies from Demigod to Demigod, but they have established techniques which they claim can verify a Demigod's original divine identity. Whether this actually works or not is of course a matter of faith, but true or not, the rites involved have led to this group's explosive growth over the past few decades, now that they have been freed from the embarrassment of having dozens of "Thors" and "Dagdas" at the same time, which once opened them to all manner of mockery. (Note: If there is eventually going to be a form of Rote for miracles, then I think it would be a good idea to give some to each Following to further distinguish them, and this would be one that pertains to the Reincarnates.)
            Reincarnates usually establish Temples and Cults based on the original religions of the gods they believe themselves to be, although they seldom place much emphasis on doing things exactly the same way as the ancients did. They consider themselves the ultimate authority within their own cults, and typically feel free to change things not only century to century but day to day as the mood strikes them. Occasionally a group of Reincarnates who believe themselves to hail from the same pantheon will create Cults and Temples which they share amongst themselves, in which case they tend to default more to the ancient example (as they understand it) as a compromise. Even more rarely, some Reincarnates may give space in their Cults/Temples to allies of their mythical persona whose reincarnation they've never met or who are believed to still enjoy True Godhood.
            Reincarnates are split on the issue of Second Ascension. Many believe that Demigods who vanish in this manner have returned to their otherworldly thrones, but others believe that Demigods who supposedly ascend for the second time actually find themselves starting over in a new mortal body. The rite of identity supports both theories: some of the ascended deities are replaced almost immediately, while other names never reappear.
            The numinous forms in this following are the most varied, since each Demigod comes to resemble the god they believe themselves to be rather than any unified template. Common elements are a basically humanlike form, and signature weapons. Perhaps because it is a religion which already admits the possibility of reincarnation, Hindu deities are particularly likely to be claimed by reincarnates, so blue skin and multiple arms are frequent as well

            Following of Heirs
            This following believes that the gods worshipped by previous religions either never existed or have died, and that Demigods are the creation of humankind's collective soul to fill the void. Some believe that Demigodhood is the eventual destiny of every human being, while others merely consider themselves shepherds of humanity's aspirations, a role they try to fulfill in different ways.
            They tend to be the most modern in their outlook of all the followings, and often dismiss older religions as steps (or even wrong turns) on the path that lead to the current generation of Demigods.They have no qualms about taking faith from worshippers or temples of other religions, but their own Temples and Cults are usually started in their own names or that of their personal ideals, and incorporate all of the improvements which a particular Heir sees in society since the major world religions were founded.
            Unlike the other followings, the Heirs are almost unanimous in seeing Second Ascension as a bad thing. They believe themselves to be empowered directly by the aspirations of humanity, and so deduce that losing Attachment would completely destroy those who have become more faith than human themselves.
            Numinous forms in this following are among the least flashy, since the Heirs see themselves more as "Humanity+" than as something dramatically different. Increased size, astonishing beauty, or unearthly grace are often the only things which distinguish their numinous forms from their regular ones.

            Not the daimon, just a namesake.

                                The major thing which comes to mind to create more conflict between Demigods would be to make Potentia more difficult to harvest, forcing them to fight over it. As it stands, it looks as if there is no limit to the number of different Demigods who can take Potentia from the same Temple. If we limit the number of times a Temple can provide resources based on its rating, it would incentivize more fighting over them, as well as making Gigantes more of a threat, assuming the Essence they draw from Temples counts against the same limit.
                                Since the power provided by Temples now comes from a root of the Drasil rather than directly from the worship that takes place there, I suggest that drawing on more than the limit be possible, but risks killing the root providing the Temple's value, which most Demigods would be reluctant to do. This would open the possibility of a group that kills these roots deliberately, trying to sever the world from the Drasil. It would be interesting if such a group didn't even see Demigods as much of a threat to be opposed - they just want to stop the Gigantes (and Titanspawn?) who occasionally invade the mundane world, and see inconveniencing Demigods as an acceptable loss. It would be unlikely ever to happen, but it they ever succeeded well enough at their goal to kill all the roots leading to the regular world, it might hurt the tree itself, with possible far-reaching results which no one would be able to predict. Something I like about this possible group is that it represents one of your themes - separation between the mortal and the divine.
                                Another group that could play with this theme is one that seeks to enforce the Masquerade as it pertains to Demigods. Their reasoning would be that mortals for some reason need to be free to doubt, and that by their presence and open activity by Demigods tend to stunt the ability of humans to develop faith on their own, without evidence.

                                P.S. Thank you for directing me to the correct location for the new halysis. I like the way it looks from the Demigod standpoint: the mechanical aspect is simple and the description provides clear guidelines for how mortals influenced by it may act while still leaving room for variety. It would be nice to have it written as a series of conditions so that it would be easier to use if Demigod NPCs are encountered in Mortal or Hunter game, but for Demigod itself that isn't really necessary.

                                                        Ok, here's the followings I've got so far (with help from the ever-wonderful Alastor):

                                                        The Following of Reincarnates
                                                        Believe that they are the reincarnations of ancient deities.

                                                        The Following of Heirs
                                                        Believe that they are the creations of mankind's soul in order to fill the void of the either absent or nonexistent gods.

                                                        The Following of Sparks
                                                        Believe that they are the creations of mankind's collective faith

                                                        The Following of Vassals
                                                        Believe that they are the servants of a higher being (aka God/ess) on earth

                                                        The Following of Descendants
                                                        Believe that they are heirs to a literal bloodline of divine beings from ages past (or more recent liaisons).

                                                        The Following of the Morningstar
                                                        Believe that they are beings which fled a 'higher authority', be that God, the Demiurge or some kind of 'divine dictator' to Earth, encasing themselves in mortal flesh and suppressing their memories.

                                                        The Following of Embodiments
                                                        Believe that demigods are a physical embodiment of their Purview, in a way similar to Spirits.

                                                        The Following of Ascendants
                                                        Believe that demigods are the next step in humanity's journey towards a perfect being

                                                        The Following of the Nine Circles
                                                        Believe that demigods are demons who have escaped Hell at the price of the majority of their identity and now have a chance to live free

                                                        Any other ideas would be appreciated, particularly for more 'scientific' Followings.

                                                            Sample Numinous Form: Symbols of leadership and power are common, such as crowns, staffs and swords.
