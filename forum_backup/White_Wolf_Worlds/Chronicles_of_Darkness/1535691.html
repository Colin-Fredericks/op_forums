    Shallow Graves - A Player's Guide to Deviant: The Renegades

    05-15-2024, 07:24 PM
    You may have heard that with the publication of Clade Companion, Deviant: The Renegades is now (or very soon will be) a dead game line. But these are Renegades we're talking about. You should never believe they're dead unless you see a body - and even then, you'd be within your right mind to wake up in a cold sweat some nights, wondering if perhaps they had Rapid Healing 5 or were a secret Myriad. If you create a Deviant and they go Renegade, you don't get to decide that you are done with them, because unless they are really, truly dead, you can be quite certain that they are not done with you!

    In case you don't know me, I was the co-developer of Deviant: The Renegades and the line developer for it after that. Because the IP owner stopped approving new Chronicles of Darkness projects, I have decided to continue the Deviant line on the Storyteller's Vault. My first offering, which is currently in 1st proofs, is Shallow Graves - a Deviant Player's Guide packed with more character options than you can shake a stick at (perhaps because if you shake a stick at it, it may break out of its cage and rip your arm off). I anticipate a full release this summer, and in anticipation of that, I will be sharing excerpts of the text a couple of times a week until release day - starting tomorrow.

    Today? Today is just a warning - this supplement affirming a few Touchstones before it begins the real work of bringing down the Web of Pain. Okay, actually, today is a cover reveal to show you what's coming:








    In addition to the excerpts I'm posting here, I've set up a Patreon page for anyone who may want even more Shallow Graves content before the official release.

                      Today, I'm going to spoil a little bit of the chapter fiction. Fiction in Chronicles of Darkness games has long served as a way to convey the mood of the game and the kinds of stories to be told with it. In some cases, it also showcases some of the new powers and narrative tools a supplement will put at a troupe's disposal. The Shallow Graves fiction is a novella in eight parts - one for the introduction (which you can read for free here), and one for each of the seven chapters.

                      Next week, I'll be posting some excerpts from Chapter 1, which is a deep dive into roleplaying a Renegade.

                      People Who Died

                      Part I

                      Mathias sat on the balcony of his house overlooking his pool. Beyond the tall privacy fence surrounding it stretched a small forest, and past that — just before the horizon — he could make out the Gulf of Mexico. The sun hung low over the water, not yet a sunset but the promise of a beautiful one.

                      He glanced to his left at the other chair. It was empty. It had been empty for years — ever since cancer took Julie. Mathias knew his wife would have enjoyed watching the sunset with him.

                      Gripping the arms of his chair, he hoisted himself to his feet. He was by no means old — not yet 50, with a bare pate covered by a Marlins baseball cap. His hair had been buzzed short his entire adult life, so shaving it once his hairline had begun to recede seemed an obvious choice.

                      Mathias picked up the empty margarita glass. There was plenty of time to get another drink. He went inside, set the glass on the beautiful granite countertop, and opened the stainless-steel finished refrigerator. He had replaced all the kitchen appliances a year ago, after a particularly lucrative consulting gig.

                      “Major Mathias Washington,” said a woman’s voice behind him.

                      He spun, brandishing a half-empty, plastic margarita bottle like a weapon. His guns were in his bedroom, locked in a gun cabinet. The front door had sturdy locks to buy time for him to get them.
                      If the woman framed in the doorway to his living room regarded him as a threat, she gave no sign of it. She was short and slight, with dusky features and long black hair. She looked to be in her 20s or early 30s and wore a black T-shirt, blue jeans, and sneakers.

                      Mathias deliberately set down the bottle on the counter next to the margarita glass. And the block of kitchen knives, in case it came to that. How had she gotten into the house?

                      “What do you want?” he demanded.

                      “Me? From you? Nothing.”

                      He placed the accent as Afghan.

                      “My friends, however…”

                      She made a small motion with one hand, and a circular hole ringed in black fire opened in the space between her and Mathias. A white man and a tall black woman, both of them in their 30s, stepped out of the portal. He didn’t bother taking in more of their appearance than that as he reached for the French chef’s knife.

                      “No,” said the man, and Mathias’s hand struck an invisible barrier.

                      “Weingarten,” the major said.

                      Then an unseen fist closed around Mathias, pinning his arms and legs and lifting him a foot off the floor. He wriggled futilely.

                      The man, who wore crooked black glasses, offered a small, cold smile. “I knew you’d remember me, Major. I certainly remember you.”

                      “Clarence Weingarten,” Mathias said by way of acknowledgment.

                      “Monty Weingarten. Clarence was my grandfather’s name.”

                      Mathias managed a shrug despite the bonds holding him. “Who are your friends?”

                      “And I knew you wouldn’t remember me,” said the black woman, stepping forward. Her right arm hung by her side, swaying slightly with the movement as though it were a dead thing. “Kenya Moses. One of your Tailored Xenetics buddies’ failed experiments. There were nine of us for every Monty.”

                      No recognition in the major’s eyes.

                      “Three hundred soldiers died, sir.” Her fury was thick despite the softness of her voice. “300 more of us ended up like this.” She gestured at her limp right arm.

                      “Which is why I shut the program down,” Mathias reminded them. When they didn’t immediately respond, he continued. “I assume your Afghan friend is another Tailored Xenetics success story?”

                      The black fire portal abruptly vanished.

                      “Yes,” the woman in the jeans said. “After they killed and maimed hundreds of Americans in their experiments, they killed and maimed thousands of Afghans, luring us with promises of giving us the tools we’d need to fight the Taliban. Then they sold those same tools to our enemies.”

                      “I had nothing to do with any of that,” Mathias told her. “And I had no idea they were in bed with the Taliban. Look, I’m not your enemy here.”

                      “You’re lying,” Monty cut in. “You’ve never met Zafir, but we know you’re still doing work for Tailored Xenetics.”

                      “I’m just a consultant,” Mathias stammered. “I know nothing about their inner workings.”Fear. The fear of the animal as the door to the trap slams shut.

                      “Ah, but we know you’re working on a project with Michael Swan,” Monty told him.

                      “You want to know where he lives?”

                      “We already know that,” Kenya said.

                      “We want the names of his friends within the organization,” Monty clarified. “We want what you know about his handlers.”

                      “No one can handle Swan but you, Weingarten. I wish you’d reconsider their offer.”

                      Mathias grunted as the invisible fist tightened around him until he could barely breathe.

                      Monty’s voice shook with rage as he spoke. “We’re not doing this again.”

                                              It's Tuesday and time for more previews of Shallow Graves. As with last week, I've posted one excerpt here, but a second is available for free at the Patreon in my signature (as is the full text of the first half of the chapter, for paid members).

                                              Chapter One explores the personal impacts of the Divergence to make it easier to get inside the heads of the Broken when roleplaying. Its first half unpacks Origin, Clades, souls, lost Anchors and Aspirations, and Acclimation. Its second half focuses on Touchstones, including more details about what constitutes "furthering a Touchstone", roleplaying guidance and systems for Faltering and reversals (i.e., changing a Conviction Touchstone to a Loyalty Touchstone or vice versa), and some ideas advice for choosing Touchstones (including several examples of some unusual Touchstones of each type). On with the preview!

                                              Origins

                                              Origins reflect a character’s state of consent at the moment of Divergence. While some Remade retain the same attitude towards their transformation now that they had then, many have evolving views of the net cost/benefit of their Divergence. If they had it to do again, some Exomorphs would have welcomed the power they gained when they were Broken, and no few Autourgics discover they regret their transformations. Origin should therefore be considered a starting point, not a reflection of a character’s current attitudes towards their Remade condition. Any Origin can lead a Deviant to life as a Renegade (or a Devoted).

                                              A character’s Origin often says more about the conspiracy that made them than it does about the Deviant themself. Moreover, a conspiracy’s preference of Origin can evolve over time.

                                              Below are 50 Deviant backstories that lead Remade to become Renegades. They’re organized by Origin, but most could just as easily apply to a character of another Origin. For example, the Exomorph might well have grown sympathetic towards the conspiracy that made her, only to run afoul of a bad boss or workplace rival who drove her to go rogue. Remember also that while all paths in these character concepts lead to Renegades, the roads to becoming Devoted are just as numerous and varied.

                                              Autourgic Renegades

                                              Conspiracies with a large population of Elect are often among the most ethical in the Web of Pain. This is certainly relative. These conspiracies must still violate their subjects’ bodies and minds in unthinkably awful ways to trigger the Divergence. But at least those they recruit go into the process with the risks clearly disclosed, even if they can’t truly understand what the experience will be like. Autourgic-heavy conspiracies rely on their recruits’ loyalty and dedication to a cause or ideal. Many are government-sanctioned groups that can wave the flag of nationalism to win over patriots, while others are religious orders that can rely upon their adherents’ zeal to ensure a steady supply of willing subjects. Elect-heavy conspiracies are also the most likely to know about and fight against hostile supernatural forces in the world, sharing this knowledge with potential Divergence candidates to inspire them to offer themselves as soldiers in that hidden war. A large minority of conspiracies begin with a Self-Made Autourgic as a founder.

                                              In times when the body count rises, or cultural factors make patriotism, zeal, and sacrifices for the greater good a less common refrain in the population, Autourgic-dominated conspiracies must often adapt. Many turn to recruiting existing Remade, either poaching them from rival conspiracies or seeking out Renegades or Pathologicals who might be stirred by the conspiracy’s higher purpose. Sufficiently desperate conspiracies, finding it impossible to meet their recruitment goals in any other way, might resort to deception or outright abduction to meet their quotas (producing Epimorphs and Exomorphs).

                                              On the other hand, a successful conspiracy with a large pool of Autourgic Devoted may unlock the secrets of producing Genotypals, laying the foundation for an important shift in its focus. This is by no means assured, however, since it depends on whether the conspiracy is able to isolate the root cause of one type of Divergence and reliably reproduce it.

                                              Bad Apple: Divergence offered you power, which you have long craved. You didn’t much care what the conspiracy’s true goals were as long as you benefited. For a long time, you did. You did your job, but you also enjoyed the power your Variations granted you outside of working hours. Technically, you signed a code of ethics or behavior or some shit, but adhering to it never crossed your mind. You were one of the conspiracy’s few success stories. You were irreplaceable, far too precious to discard over some venial violation, whether it was exacting petty revenge on old enemies or earning a few grand extra selling your unique services to outside parties. Then those bastards told you they weren’t going to let you work for them anymore. You were giving their whole organization a bad name, and finance ran some numbers with the PR department and decided you were costing them more money than you were earning. They…fired you. Like some incompetent custodian or a telemarketer who missed her quota one month too many. But you’ll show them they’ve made a grave mistake.

                                              Corruption: The conspiracy used to be something you could believe in — not perfect, but basically well-intentioned and filled with people who wanted to make the world a better place. You’re not sure exactly when that changed. Maybe it was the new CEO. Maybe it was the IPO. Maybe it was after the head of R&D was killed in that laboratory accident. Regardless, it isn’t the same place anymore. It isn’t a good place to work, and any good it might still be accomplishing is far outstripped by the harm it inflicts. So, you left. It wasn’t easy. You still have friends there, after all. But you know it was the right decision.

                                              Discarded: You chose Divergence, chose it knowing something of what it would cost you, knowing there could be no turning back. You woke with remarkable gifts. You woke with a confidence you had never felt in your life. Then you saw the frowns on the faces of the scientists as they tested you. You had gained impossible powers, but maybe they weren’t as potent or as useful as the conspiracy had hoped. Or perhaps the burdens you gained during the process were too disruptive or more severe than was acceptable. It’s even possible that your new abilities were too powerful, and the conspiracy feared it wouldn’t be able to control you. Whatever the case, they decided they didn’t want you. They might have flown you to an unfamiliar, faraway city and abandoned you, or perhaps you woke up one day and found that they had taken all their equipment and staff and left for places unknown. Either way, you don’t know how to find them, and doubt they would take you back even if you did.

                                              Extinct Conspiracy: The conspiracy was home to you when you had nowhere else to go and no one else to care for you. You liked working there. You liked your coworkers. You believed in its mission. Yours was the first name on the list when they asked for volunteers for a special new treatment. But then something happened. At the time, you thought it a catastrophic accident, one that in an instant took away everyone and everything you loved. You only survived because of your remarkable abilities. As you picked through the rubble, looking for survivors, you discovered evidence of foul play. Now you seek the ones responsible.

                                              Mission Accomplished: You volunteered for the Divergence out of a sense of duty or to achieve an objective that would have been impossible without the power it granted. It was an act of self-sacrifice you embraced at the time and didn’t regret afterwards. Whatever it is that drove you to choose Divergence, you accomplished it. That was supposed to be the end. You turned in your resignation — or your Progenitor released you from his service as agreed when you volunteered — and you walked away. But then you started to notice the black sedans waiting wherever you went. One day, a gentleman in a well-tailored suit got out of one and made you an offer: use your powers in the service of his employer. You refused him, but he and his associates have proven unwilling to take no for an answer.

                                              New Boss: You were satisfied with your life at the conspiracy until he arrived. Maybe he was part of a routine management shake-up, or maybe he forced out (or murdered) your previous boss. Maybe he still claims to be the same boss as ever, but he acts like a different person. The point is that he was trying to make your life miserable (or planned to get rid of you in a permanent fashion), so you took action. You may or may not have gone berserk at your office and left his body in a shallow grave before you left, but you’re out now, and there’s no going back.

                                              Self-Loathing: You volunteered for the process or ritual that led to your Divergence, but you realized almost immediately that it was a mistake. You hate what you’ve become, and you hate the ones who convinced you to go through with it even more.

                                              Truth Revealed: The conspiracy seemed benevolent, at first. You served it loyally, gave more of yourself to it than was probably wise. But gradually you became aware of some questionable practices. You brought them to your superior’s attention, and she assured you that these violations would be investigated, the perpetrators disciplined. Except that didn’t happen, so you started looking into matters on your own, which led you to a terrible truth. These awful practices weren’t anomalies; they were standard procedure. The more you looked, the more you realized your beloved organization was a cancer in the world. Most of those it Remade didn’t have the choice you had. You met some of them, heard their stories firsthand. You helped them escape, and together you work to bring down the conspiracy you once served.

                                              Unacceptable Order: You were a loyal agent of the conspiracy, obeying its commands unquestioningly until the day you received an order you found you could not obey. Maybe it was a suicide mission or a doomed last stand. Maybe you were ordered to report for a dangerous new treatment or for vivisection. Maybe you were instructed to hurt someone you cared about or perpetrate an abominable crime that was a bridge too far even for you. Whatever the specifics, you either fled rather than obey the order, or you carried out the instruction but were so shaken by doing so that you left the conspiracy soon after.

                                              Weary Soldier: You’ve served the conspiracy for as long as anyone can remember — before and after your Divergence. You have done unspeakable things in its interests. Maybe you could reasonably convince yourself that it was all for the greater good, or maybe you know full well you’ve spent your life furthering the goals of criminals and villains. In any case, you tired of that life and walked away. Perhaps you went with your conspiracy’s blessing and a nice severance package — or at least a retirement plan better than “one bullet in the chest and one in the back of the head.” Or maybe you faked your own death or carved a bloody path out of the conspiracy. Regardless, you’re out. Deprived of the protection they granted you, though, other conspiracies have come calling.

                                              Workplace Drama: It could have started with a small conflict — a disagreement at the weekly staff meeting, a tendency to chew with her mouth open, bad taste in music. Or maybe you had an instant dislike for one another. Regardless, it soon worsened until it started to interfere with both your jobs, and eventually the sniping, backbiting, and personal sabotage attracted the attention of HR. Despite their efforts at mediation, as it turned out, the conspiracy wasn’t big enough for both of you. Maybe you walked away while she stayed, or maybe you arranged a little accident that people weren’t convinced was an accident after all. In any case, you didn’t stick around to find out what happened next. The conspiracy is probably still looking for you, and it will be big trouble if they find you.

                                                  Today I'm previewing a bit about Touchstones and what it means to "further" them. The Patreon in my signature also has a free preview of what it means to Falter, including optional systems for handling those times when a Renegade doesn't live up to her own expectations (and for those who absolutely can't wait, the full text of the second half of Chapter One is available to paid members).

                                                  Touchstones

                                                  Deviant souls burn white-hot — like the reactor cores of nuclear power plants. Touchstones act as coolant systems that, as long as they are functioning, leech away the excess heat of the nuclear reactions. Without them, the fire of the Broken’s soul reaches a temperature that exceeds its metaphorical melting point. Unlike a nuclear power plant, however, his soul has no reactor shielding and no failsafes to shut down the reaction if it gets out of control. In the absence of steady coolant pressure, this meltdown causes further damage to the Remade’s soul (Instability) and can lead to a catastrophic meltdown (the End Stage Condition).

                                                  Not all Deviants rely on the same coolants or cooling systems. They have many different ways of expressing Conviction and Loyalty, and they have a wide range of options in whom (or what) they choose as their Touchstones.

                                                  Self-Expression

                                                  A Deviant does not need to express her hatred of a Conviction Touchstone with immediate and outright murder. Nor does Loyalty compel her to constant acts of self-sacrifice in the name of protecting her Touchstones from harm. In practice, both scenarios are rare compared to less drastic interactions.

                                                  Expressions of Conviction

                                                  Deviants aren’t necessarily murderers. True, they only heal major Instability by destroying a Conviction Touchstone, but even that doesn’t always mean “kill”. Remade need their Touchstones, and killing a Conviction Touchstone at the earliest possible opportunity is usually wasteful. After all, choosing and affirming a new Touchstone takes time, and eliminating all the Broken’s Touchstones permanently worsens their damaged state (making them Ferals). Unless the Deviant is suffering from extreme (major) Instability, small acts of defiance, opposition, and sabotage can soothe her soul’s injuries for a long time. Some Remade grow so dependent on a Conviction Touchstone for their Stability that they maintain their quiet animosity for years — regularly needling, harming, or inconveniencing the target of their rage.

                                                  If the Broken’s actions set off a chain of events that leads to the Touchstone’s death or permanent incapacitation, this still heals a major Instability, even if the Deviant isn’t the one who pulled the trigger or plunged the dagger into her heart.

                                                  The Deviant also heals a major Instability if he places the Touchstone into an apparent certain death situation, such that any reasonable person would presume they had died. If a Conviction Touchstone later comes back from the “dead”, the Broken may immediately replace an existing Touchstone (either Conviction or Loyalty) with the returning Touchstone, who is not considered Wavering, although this counts against the limit of naming one new Touchstone per chapter.

                                                  At the Storyteller’s discretion, the Deviant may also heal a major Instability if he completely transforms the Touchstone or irreversibly destroys his victim’s life. Forcibly subjecting the target to the Divergence or sabotaging her intended Divergence so that it does not bestow the desired benefits or imposes much worse disadvantages than expected often qualifies. Inflicting an injury sufficient to change the victim’s personality, or one that permanently bars her from her chosen vocation (cutting off the hands of a professional pianist, for example, or destroying the eyes of a sniper) may also suffice. Certain kinds of scandal can likewise drive a politician, financier, or government worker from their professional arena and prevent them from returning to it. For some Remade, alternatively, so thoroughly reforming a Conviction Touchstone or convincing him of the error of his ways that he wants to leave behind who he is and make amends for his past crimes can also qualify.

                                                  Below are 15 ways Deviants might express their Conviction without resorting to killing the Touchstone in question. Taking action to oppose a Conviction Touchstone should take some amount of gaming-table time to heal Instability. Locking a Touchstone in your basement and taunting (or torturing) him off-camera shouldn’t count — although it’s certainly something a Deviant might do — but foiling his escape attempt or convincing the cops not to come inside to investigate the screams the neighbors reported coming from your house usually will.

                                                  Bully: The Remade gains professional power over the target and uses this position of authority to make the Touchstone’s life miserable in ways small or large — from setting unrealistic goals to creating an unpleasant working environment to levying unfair punishments for perceived failures. While most bosses don’t wield life-and-death power over those who report to them, if the Deviant does and he uses it to get his Touchstone killed (such as a commander who can order a soldier on a suicide mission), it can permit the Broken to heal a major Instability.

                                                  Dehumanize: The Remade causes or forces the Touchstone to suffer some indignity. This often has a physical component (a bucket of pig’s blood poured on the victim, for example) but it’s primarily a psychological attack. It either makes the victim act in ways that are contrary to his self-identity (committing cannibalism, for example, or killing a friend in order to save his own life) or embarrasses him in front of either a large group of strangers or a smaller group of people he wishes to impress.

                                                  Discredit: The Remade targets the Touchstone’s professional or social reputation. Because of the Broken’s intervention, the children’s entertainer is shown to be a sex predator, or the academic’s papers are works of plagiarism, or the preacher is caught on camera laughing at how he has fleeced his clueless flock to make himself rich. This usually heals a minor Instability, although it can heal a major Instability if it leaves the target’s reputation so badly stained that he can never participate in a field or mingle in a social circle again.

                                                  Expressions of Loyalty

                                                  Although the mere fact that the Remade relies upon them for her survival often puts Loyalty Touchstones in harm’s way — forcing her to protect or rescue them — Deviants express Loyalty in many ways, any of which can restore their Stability. As well, the intensity and social acceptability of their behavior depends on the individual’s personality and often how much Instability they have accrued. Broken who have few Touchstones are more likely to resort to desperate means than those with many, as are transformed who have a lot of Instability.

                                                  Most expressions of Loyalty can look like perfectly healthy gestures of friendship and love. This doesn’t change the fact that they are critically necessary for the Broken. The Remade don’t just feel lonely or suffer mental and physical health issues if deprived of opportunities to engage with their Touchstones. They manifest increasingly terrible and numerous Scars until their powers go nuclear and kill them. Maintaining and deepening relationships with Touchstones is of deadly importance to them. Stripped of their Integrity, Remade are naturally less retrained in their behavior, so it isn’t so surprising that they sometimes take things too far.

                                                  Some Deviants can count on their Touchstones to initiate meaningful contact with them. Maybe her best friend invites her out for dinner and drinks almost weekly, like clockwork. Most Remade must engage in a bit of give-and-take. That same friend starts dating someone she’s really into, and while she’s sometimes free on the usual night of the week, the Deviant needs to make plans with her at other times to continue getting her fix. Broken who get desperate may resort to manufacturing circumstances to ensure that their Touchstones spend time with them. The Remade in the other examples, jealous of the one who she feels has taken her friend away from her, secretly sabotages the relationship or, in a worst-case scenario, kills him so that she can have her friend to herself. Oftentimes, jealousy is an expression of fear and neediness; the Deviant needs regular contact with at least some of her Touchstones and is afraid that if they forge bonds with someone else it might mean they have less time for her, or that they will abandon her entirely.

                                                  Similarly, some Deviants express Loyalty with no strings attached. They give of themselves because they want their Touchstone to be happy and healthy, seeing this as its own reward. Such unconditional love can leave them open to exploitation, however. Other Deviants try to get as much out of their relationship as possible. They trust in their Touchstone’s good nature or lack of less toxic relationships to ensure that they continue to meet the Broken’s needs. What little they give back they use as emotional leverage to prove that their Touchstone should do more to meet the Remade’s needs. Sure, he’ll lend you money, but whenever you argue about anything he’ll remind you of all the times he’s lent you money as evidence that you should let him have his way. Most Broken need to learn to balance these two approaches — sometimes giving freely, and sometimes expecting (or at least asking for) something in return — but this can be difficult for them, especially those whose shrunken number of Touchstones and/or rising Instability make them desperate for their Touchstone’s continued attention.

                                                  Below are 15 ways Remade can express their devotion to a Loyalty Touchstone. Each includes both benign and toxic versions. As with taking action to oppose a Conviction Touchstone, deepening one’s relationship with a Loyalty Touchstone should take some amount of gaming-table time to heal Instability. Going to bed with your Touchstone lover for the first time certainly counts, but sharing a bed with them for several uneventful weeks — and presumably having sex some of the time — shouldn’t. It isn’t that it must put either of you in peril or cause you significant injury (although Renegades can regain Willpower if they take risks or suffer harm in doing so), but that the event should further the story of your relationship or, at the very least, set the table for events that will threaten or alter it.

                                                  Accompany: The Deviant participates in a social activity with his Touchstone — anything from a fishing trip or sporting event to a stake-out or heist. The key is that it gives the Remade an opportunity to share an experience with the Touchstone. This becomes toxic if the Broken invites himself along on activities when his Touchstone doesn’t want him there, particularly if he stalks or stows away to stay close to them long enough to find an opportunity to insert himself into their activity.

                                                  Depend: The Touchstone takes care of the Deviant. This can range from tending her injuries or posting her bail, to picking up after her or insisting that she take better care of herself. This becomes toxic if she intentionally puts herself in harm’s way so that he can care for her when she gets hurt, or if she defers simple maintenance tasks until they come to his attention so that he can perform them.

                                                  Finance: The Broken gives the Touchstone money or pays her way. This can become toxic if the Deviant expects to be repaid or demands some non-monetary favor in return, especially if his “charity” is something that will place the Touchstone deeply in his debt and so gives the Remade leverage over her.
