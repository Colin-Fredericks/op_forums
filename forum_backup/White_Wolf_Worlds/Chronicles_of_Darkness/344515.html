    Glamour Gathering

    12-19-2014, 05:51 AM
    At the moment I am running my first Changeling chronicle and I am having some questions about glamour gathering about which I would like some second opinion/suggestions. The rules of glamour gathering are fairly simple and streight forward. The player select an emotion and a method, the storyteller assigns skills and a modifier, and the check is made with each success generating 1 glamour. From what I gather though the rules don't discuss those modifiers, only mentioning that quick rushes of emotions incur penalties (in other words: generate less glamour). It never even mentions how much time gathering glamour costs, although the mechanic again suggests it can be a matter of moments.

    Game mechanically this is no issue. It just means that in general glamour is easy to get. Since at the current powerlevel contracts are not particular powerful unlimittedless glamour does not really impact my game from a power perspective. It does clash with what is implied about glamour in other sections though. Why bother with the hunting ground merrit if you can gather glamour simply by acting like the crazy homeless guy on the streets (although that will eventually get you in trouble with the cops)? Why mention glamour tithes and rewards between lieges and followers? There are better things to gain compared to a measily 2 glamour per month. How does this work with the generic suggestion that glamour is somewhat rare and hard to get?

    Now, in my current game at least most of the players ignore the mechanics to some point. They make work to get a more permanent source of glamour through the story by finding the right kind of job such as for example working at a hospital (which is not easy for a newly returned Changeling). Still, others ignore story in this particular case and just use the mechanics, not because they are bad roleplayers, just because this aspect of the game is not their cup of tea. So any suggestions on how to penalize glamour hunting checks through modifiers that make that quick rush much harder, but not impossible?

      One suggestion that comes to mind is to ask the players, when harvesting glamour, whether they're actively causing the emotion or passively absorbing a feeling that's already there (I recall one of the books mentioning a changeling watching a city riot from a rooftop and soaking up the anger). Passively absorbing sorrow at a funeral or desire on a dancefloor might penalize their harvest roll. On the other hand, actively causing a rush of emotion might actually give them a bonus to the roll, depending on the circumstances; however, this comes with its own complications. You do your hardest to make a man at a bar lust after you, and now he won't leave you alone. You spook some normies coming home from a night out and the next day you're accosted by the police. Extra consequences I'd wager should most likely happen on either a dramatic failure (since that's the sort of thing a dramatic failure functions for) or an exceptional success (representing the overpowering strength of the emotion). It gives the players a choice to make every time they need a fix.

      The trickle of glamour from pledges is kind of weird, though I imagine that changelings dedicated to that approach just make a lot of pledges with the glamour boon. I also imagine that they're the sorts who either can't, won't, or feel like they can't interact with humanity on an emotionally-connecting level.

      Blood & Smoke has a section on running feeding scenes that could be adapted for Changeling with a little finagling, if that's your thing.

                                              Got to agree with Kat here; you don't need to be the monster to be in a horror story. Most often, quite the opposite. Changelings deal with a half-dozen kinds of horror before breakfast; their minds and bodies have been changed by alien experiences, yet no-one but a select few can see it. Reality itself might be a lie, and danger lurks around every corner. Every door, every window, every mirror is a possible portal through which the Gentry or one of their lapdogs or an army of goblins might come storming. Everyone you love is a potential weapon against you, but, if you don't keep them close, it becomes oh so difficult to remember just what reality is and isn't; so much harder to believe this is true.

                                              Changelings are victims as much as villains.

                                              Meanwhile, you have the ability to make Pledges, which grant a fair deal more power when dealing with mortals, and feed off of mortal emotions. Even if you don't induce the emotion yourself, you're encouraged to maintain it and, while this can be benign, not all Changelings will take that path. Miami explicitly has Winter Court members who basically use the drug trade to feed their habit.
