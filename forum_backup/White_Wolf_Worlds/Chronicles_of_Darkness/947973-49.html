                If the computer is an important keystone to the angel's mission consider having the angel use a Strengthen Influence on it, adding the angel's Influence dots as a penalty to any rolls to do anything unauthorised with that computer. Also, ask the player what he's looking for, and adjust the target number accordingly to regulate the difficulty/reward ratio. If it's just company information then that should be relatively easy to get for a PC hacker, but if it's anything relating to the angel's mission you could have the angel contest the extended action, potentially alerting the angel to the PC's location if the angel reaches the target number first.

                    Consider having the computer in question not be connected to any networks - it’s not on wifi, no internet or intranet access, not even bluetooth. If you want, you could make it so that it can access other computers via intranet, but other computers can’t access it owing to how the programming is done. That would require actually getting into the office to check it out, which would have its own foibles. You just want to make sure that you’re not making things too difficult or specialized so the other players can have something to do (which is admittedly a bit of a fundamental problem of Fungible Knowledge in the first place).

                            Setting aside that this is literally part of why disposable low-rating Covers are called "burners," going loud lasts for a scene. Destroying a Cover for power is a commitment to Being A Demon for that time; you assume a different Cover at the end of the scene if you can, and you're Hunted even if you aren't now one of the Burned.

                                Originally posted by Bunyip View Post

                                It depends entirely on what the scope of the angel’s mission is. A hunter angel sent to track and destroy demons is going to react with hostility. An angel whose mission is to ensure a certain switch is flipped every day at precisely 3 PM isn’t going to care much about demons unless they interfere with its switch flipping. It may note their presence if they make themselves known but otherwise they’re not its problem.

                                An angel on a broader mission, say to watch over the life of a human because they have some unknown but important destiny is going to be more flexible in its approach. It may ignore demons if it becomes aware of them incidentally, it may engage with them politely if they come within its sphere of interest, it may even manipulate them into doing work for it if this seems the best way to serve its mission.

                                Ultimately, if you want to know how an angel will react you need to understand what the angel does first.
