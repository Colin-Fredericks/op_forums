    Originally posted by mlvalentine View Post

    If you'd like to help shape these definitions that will be supported by the 2E rules, I think the forums is a good place to discuss what the iconic, human "Hunter" who has no access to the supernatural is. Then, take that same Hunter, and tell me what the heroic and villainous versions are. I'm also interested in how the definitions shift between a Hunter who has access to the supernatural, and one who does not.

    I'd like to reinforce that nothing is set in stone at this point, and I haven't made any clear decisions yet. These discussions are hugely important--especially prior to the outline phase. Looking forward to seeing your thoughts on this.
    for me, the most iconic "Hunter" is the best that humanity has to offer- how people draw strength from their hope and despair in order to challenge creatures who are more powerful than them in more than one way: beings who can torn them to shreds with bare hands, beings who can steal their souls with a whisper, beings who has control over the greatest individuals in society and enough resources to buy a nation. Hunters know all of that and they steal dare to challenge those enemies against all odds- and they actually succeed! They use splinters of information gathered from old books and not very trusty forums, collect some kitchen knives, baseball bats and maybe the gun of their parents and gather their faith and willpower to the endless fight against the monsters. Risking Willpower, Practical Experience and the Code all enhance that feeling, IMO.

    As an hero, that hunter would relay on her virtues and higher nature in order to hold on- she has faith in humanity, she believes her fight is meaningful, she is willing to risk her life for the greater good. She doesn't do it for vengeance or hate- she does it simply because it is the right thing to do. As a villain, she in a nihilist- she hunts because she fears, she hunts because she hates, she hunts because she's hurt. Maybe she doesn't hunt to protect others as much as she tries to protect herself, willing to sacrifice others for her own safety. Maybe she just seeks after vengeance, and she is prepared to do what ever it takes just to kill that specific monster. And then that one. And that one. Maybe she just does it because it is fun- she likes the rush, and doesn't care if people get killed in the way- or even if she dies by herself. All of those are forms of nihilism and fear, unlike those who seek for a brighter future- but at any case, she is still an Hunter, not a Slasher. She would fall into Slasherhood only when she would leave behind both her fear and hope in order to become.. something else, something which is no longer quite human as she once was. Even a villainous hunter may be able to light up a candle, but when you turn into a Slasher, there are only shadows around you.

    Now, let's say that our hunter survive her first few stumbles against the supernatural,and she learns a thing or two about the monsters. She joins (or even forms) a Compact. Now she has some more resources, more friends, more support. In her own turf, she turns into a power one needs to consider- not something too big, not enough to challenge a Covenant or a Lodge, but they are able to present a constant Vigil against random, lone monsters (or even a group of those!). She knows more about what hides in the shadows and how to deal with them- she no longer tries to shoot a werewolf with an iron bullet or thinks she is protected from a vampire just because she didn't invited it in. She can call for help when things goes bad and she has friends she can share with her experiences. She sacrifice some of her freedom, of course, but the chances are that the compact shares most of how she views her hunt. Devoted Christians would join the Long Night, killing monsters with mercy. Scientific researchers would cast their lot among the Null Mysteriis. Hedonists who just want to have fun would buy an Ashwood Abbey License, and so on and so on. An heroic hunter would find comfort in her new place in the world and devout herself to their ideals. She would fear the monsters less. She feel comfort from her friends and partners in the hunt.

    A villainous hunter, however, would have a different view on the world- the more she knows, the more is afraid. Believing that there are vampires in the government is different than actually knowing it. She doesn't see her cell mates as friends as much as they are tools- people die every day on the Vigil, it is better not to get attached. It is ok to send a cell or two to their death, because there are always new hunters in the compact and as long as the beast is dead everyone (she!) is safe. She won't rest as long as there are monsters in the world, no matter how many lives are needed to be sacrificed for that goal. And yet, she is still not a Slasher- only because she directs her efforts toward monsters. She is still sane enough to distinguish between human and inhuman, and through that she still clings to her own humanity. There is a difference between accepting death as an eventual outcome and actively pursuing it. After all, an heroic hunter is as much as in danger for falling as a villainous one- adhering into your Virtues may cause you to put people out of their misery, judging the guilty on your own accord or waging an holy war against anyone who is in touch with a monster- up to the point of seeing other people as monsters.

    After few years in the hunt and showing a surprising ability to survive, out Hunter has managed to join the top of the top, the best among the best- a Conspiracy. Either by birth or skill, she is now a part of a great organization, one of the movers and shakers of the occult world. If a lone hunter is the best that humanity has to offer, a Conspiracy is made by taking all of those small candles and turn them into a lighthouse, guiding and protecting thousands of souls from the monsters. She has enough resources to plan raids and operations against the things in the dark, and she sometimes laugh on the times she believed quartz would actually banish spirits away. For her, hunting monsters is not just a way of life- it is a job, something she is expected to do. She knows how monsters act, what types of monsters are there and how you can kill them, and sometimes she even knows things the monsters themselves are not aware of. Alone, she is weak in powerless- but supported by her organization, she can become something that monsters fear. And not only that, now she even has access to Endowments- supernatural or high mundane abilities which can become the wild card which changes the outcome of battle. Joining a Conspiracy also supports the idea of using whatever you got to fight the darkness mechanically- hunters benefit from their Status, Endowments are just a special set of Merits. The supernatural is a part of her, and she is using it to hunt it down.

    For an heroic character, it may not be easy to adapt to her new situation- Conspiracies are not the place of idealists. There is a chain of command, a way to do things. She is expected to do certain things and don't ask certain questions. She may need to make compromises, unless she follows their point of view with zeal- and even then she is required to restrain herself. She may even feel a bit of hypocrite in case of using Endowments, and avoid using them. She may try to rise in her organization in order to change it, make it into a better one, or she may adapt to it, agreeing that the way they do things is right. She already gets used to monsters, and may start to forget all of early fears and hopes- although she sometimes asks herself: is that the best humanity is capable of? This is truly what I've dreamed about when I've started? Those who stick to their hope may actually make a difference. Those who fall to despair usually vanish one day without trace, only to be found years later, holding a knife and wearing a mask.

    A villainous character may not be much better. Being a part of a conspiracy should be everything she ever wanted to- she is finally protected, she can easily kill her enemy and if she rise in rank she is able to command others to die in the name of her cause. Maybe she agrees with the organization's point of view and fully adhere to it for it makes her feel comfort. Maybe she is just using it as a tool, like she always did. Anyway, now she is a part of a big Conspiracy- yet she still doesn't feel safe yet. She looks around her and doesn't see friends- she sees people who wants something from her, who expect her to follow their orders without question. She sees secrets in every corner, and people who are not willing to talk about them. She sees Endowments and think to herself from where those powers come from and whether her organization is actually working with monsters- or worse, being the power behind the monsters. Some keep those fears to themselves. Others investigate their own organization. Few find answers which they don't like, finally losing hope on mankind. Some breaks, something cracks, the candle falls on the ground- and here comes the chopper!

    In sort, an Hunter is always an Hunter, whether she is an hero or a villain. Knowing about the supernatural may bring her peace, and it may drive her to madness. Not anyone can be an Hunter- only the best that humanity has to offer, and as we all know, it is not a lot.

        Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
        • serial killer: someone who has no problem killing.
        • ripper: a serial killer who is driven to kill.
        • scourge: a ripper who has literally become inhuman.

        In 1e, “slasher” meant “ripper or scourge”, and furthermore involved an Undertaking that put the slasher a cut above an ordinary mortal (if a ripper) or turned him into an inhuman killer (if a scourge). In some ways, they felt a bit like Hunter: the Reckoning's Imbued, in that even a ripper was empowered. In 2e, I'm hoping that the definition of “slasher” gets expanded to include serial killers, leading to something like a three-tier setup, with killers being the “Entry-level Slashers” who don't have any extraordinary or supernatural abilities. This makes it easier to slide over from Hunter to Slasher, since the distinction between hunter and killer is so easy to blur.

        I'm going to ask you a ridiculously challenging question then. With what you posted in mind: What's the difference between a serial killer and a hunter?

            Heroic Hunter: Head of an information network who keeps Hunters informed about notable supernatural happenings. Acts as a middleman between Cells, to help them maintain their anonymity. Earnings go towards the Vigil and related matters.

            Selfish Hunter: As above, only with a mercenary approach. Help is usually refused without payment - be it money, occasional shady service or stranger things. Earnings may, or may not go towards the Vigil. Uses leverage against their competition, to preserve their niche.

            Corrupt Hunter: The Hunter is now willing to play Cells against each other, just to advance their interests. Uses blackmail and coercion, hires criminal leg-breakers (possibly through different identities). May sell his competitors out to their enemies, mortal authorities and even Monsters. Likely benefits from a criminal enterprise.

            Extremist Hunter: Uses the tactics of a Corrupt Hunter to advance their vision of the Vigil. Makes hard choices that likely don't have to be made. Treats Hunters with different beliefs as enemies. May, or may not be open about their plans.

            Compromised Hunter: May build upon any of the previous examples. For whatever reason, their activities benefit a supernatural entity (or society). Keeps it informed about going-ons in the Hunter community. Distracts Hunters from targets that matter or uses them as unwitting pawns.

            Burned-out Hunter: Likewise, may build upon any of the previous examples. Assuming they don't just cease the Vigil, they become unpredictable in the eyes of other Hunters. The quality of their services likely drops. They may wake Hunters during the night with rant-filled phone calls, or show up on TV demolishing the local Police station. With all the information at their disposal they become a massive liability to the Hunter community.

                                        Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                        DaveB has mentioned in the past that humans in CofD have a tendency to shy away from the supernatural. That is why one of the five Breaking Point questions in the CofD Rulebook is “what [supernatural encounter] have you forgotten?” — the premise behind that question is that virtually everyone in the setting has had an encounter with the supernatural at one point or another, and nearly all of them have put that encounter out of their mind, willfully forgetting what they saw. The protagonists of the CofD core book are among these “ordinary people”, but have recently had (or are about to have) an encounter with the darkness that they cannot ignore. That's the framework of the “default” Chronicle of Darkness: ordinary people dealing as best they can with the horrors and nightmares that lurk in the shadows, despite their natural inclination to hide from it and pretend it doesn't exist.

                                        By contrast, a hunter is a human who willfully confronts the monsters on a regular basis. Whatever it is that causes ordinary people to ignore the monsters has no hold on the hunter; instead, he or she does the exact opposite and seeks out the supernatural. That's what the Vigil is: a reversal of humanity's normal instincts concerning the CofD's mysteries.

                                        That's the conceptual distinction between a “blue book mortal” and a hunter, as I see it. The mechanical distinction is almost entirely embodied in Practical Experience and its applications (in 1e, Risking Willpower is the only “template ability” that hunters have that isn't purchased with Practical Experience): as I like to think of it, Practical Experience is what a hunter learns from an encounter that a non-hunter would overlook or forget.
                                        That's my preferred answer as well. A person becomes a hunter and takes up the Vigil when the scales are ripped from their eyes and the darkness is laid bare.

                                        I'm not sure if this is an either/or question. To me, the blurry nature of the distinction between a hunter and a serial killer or slasher is part of the point. Hunters walk a razor-thin line.

                                                    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                                    A hunter has a problem with killing. He'll do it if he has to; but like a soldier or a police officer who kills someone, killing is still a Breaking Point for him. For a serial killer, there's no problem.
                                                    I don't know if that's necessarily true. Like I said in my comment on the devpost, a hunter's reluctance to kill is largely contextual and from my experience tied to how closely they can relate to the monster they're hunting. A very inhuman and unnatural creature seems easier to just kill remorselessly, the same way you or I might smash a mosquito but on a larger scale. Or go a step further and think about actual 'hunters' in the mundane world who cull dangerous invasive species. They most likely have no problem with their killing. The rub comes when, as a hunter, you start extending that attitude to increasingly broader definitions of 'monster'. The serial killer starts from the zero-mark, often seeing human life as having no more value than an insect or small animal; it exists solely to be used and destroyed for their satisfaction.

                                                    EDIT: To put a more mechanical spin on this; would you require a Breaking Point for a Hunter killing a particularly inhuman manifested Angel? Putting aside any Breaking Points for simply encountering such a creature, is there (or should there be) personal degeneration for what is essentially turning off a biomechanical automaton?
