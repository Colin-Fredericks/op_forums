        I propose that the 'demons' Marco Singe references in his vision are in fact the Shan'iatu. I don't assume Singe had a perfectly accurate story ; the authenticity of his sources must be questioned as must his own personal interpretations. Sometimes, he seems to confuse the Shan'iatu with angels, which can either be interpreted as 'of course' or ridiculous. That said, his vision fits pretty closely with Mummy as we've seen it.

        'The God Machine built our world as a resting place for its first children, whom men called angels or ancient ones. After a time, the ancients desired servants to dwell with them, servants who walked upright and had pleasing shapes, and who could speak. They sent the proper prayer-signals to the god-machine and were granted leave to do so. First the ancient Ones took the beasts of the field and granted them the knowledge to speak and walk. But these animals retained there wildness and did not make good servants. They grew wicked and violent, and were cast into the wilderness. They were the second children, whom men called demons.'

        Note - the text establishes two 'races' here, but they both look very similar. It's notable that the ability to speak is something inherent and critical to design (Names?). Likewise, the Shan'iatu... from the text... seem very violent and impulsive, even somewhat insane.

        Further evidence...

        '... and some of the men began to wonder, 'Why do the ancients spend their days in rest while we must toil without relief? Why do the ancients enjoy the pleasures of this city when it is our labors that built it? And so resentment rose among the Third Children. They made the proper calculations and sent the prayer-signals to the coordinates of the God Machine, saying, 'We are your children, too. It is not right that we be enslaved. Will you not look on us with favor?'

        And they recieved this answer - 'What rises must fall. What has fallen may rise again'.

        The men of the city debated for seven years. Finally, they decided that it was the will of the God Machine that they raise themselves from slavery. Determined to kill the Ancients and take their place as the favored of the God Machine, the men of the city plotted carefully. When the time came, they fell upon the Ancient Ones in their sleep, murdering them with there own weapons and devices in a single night of betrayal.'

        This closely follows the narrative of a Shana'Iatu betrayal in Book of the Deceived.

        And a final thought provoking quote...

        'The Angel then bade me to warn mankind of the hidden forces that seek to thwart the destiny of the Third Children. Their handiwork appears again and again throughout history. Regard the mighty sphinx of Egypt. Recent studies of the water erosion on its rocky surface indicate that the monument dates back to the days when the Sahara was green and lush...."

        ... it's heavily hinted that the Sahara was established in the battle with the first Amkhata, so this is a little timeline info! But, Singe continues on...

        '... Far older than the pyramids it guards, the Sphinx comes from a time close to the fall of the Ancients. The second children roamed freely through the world then, greater than man in power and knowledge. The demons shaped primitive man into a civilization that revered them as Gods. But their hubris was against the will of the God Machine, and they failed. The great civilizations of Egypt, Sumer and Babylonia rose later from the dim memories of that failure, revering Gods with the forms and features of beasts. The Egyptians called the proto-kingdoms that preceded them the First Time, or age of Osiris, and considered it the source of all wisdom and knowledge.'

        So my final theory IS!...

        The Shan'iatu are the second children. If one is to believe Marco Singe, they were initially 'uplifted' animals given human shapes and immense power/intellect. Interesting when one considers the fact they are unable to go where human souls go...

        ... and even more chilling, is it possible the Third Children (humanity) didn't properly interpret the God-Machine's reply?

        Consider the statement - 'What rises must fall. What has fallen may rise again'.

        Was the Machine helping them?... or merely telling them the inevitable future?...

        For Irem fell. And what has fallen may rise again.
