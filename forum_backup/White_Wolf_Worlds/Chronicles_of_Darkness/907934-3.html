                            And "the idea of an illusory choice" is only actually relevant in the abstract as part of the larger thematic framework of how the Begotten tend to think as a consequence of frequent exposure to the most desperate and deautonomized elements of the supernatural world.

                            The impact it has on playing the game is that most Beasts who've been around the block are going to recognize that there are a lot of things that can't be helped, many of them due to having already happened (or worse, being necessary to allow some sympathetic actor to live or suffer less), and that a significant majority of monsters did not ask to be as they are and do not have the luxury of actively pursuing a way out.

                            It's "metagame knowledge" of the same sort as "Luna makes people into werewolves in a fashion that their culture attributes to her being a capricious god of fate."

                            The nearest thing to your "the omniscient narrator said it so it must be objectively true!" bit comes as a rider on the line that essentially just says "a Beast can't not be a Beast" and frames the non-choice as "they're choosing to acknowledge what they are in full, not choosing to be a monster," not "whether or not they're Devoured is only in their hands as far as their elder sibling is willing to honor their refusal," which is part of the section talking about how the Devouring tends to happen in those cases where another Beast comes across a deep-dreamer by chance.

                            In-context the thing you're worrying about is a non-issue because every "caveat that impl[ies] you were always destined to be Devoured" is presented as an in-character personal/cultural view based in the fact that if you're Devoured, you undergo the Devouring. That's it.

                                                            Originally posted by ZealousChristian24 View Post
                                                            Personally I'm wondering if Satiety not having any &quot;Breaking Points&quot; like Humanity or even Cover have anything to do with it.
                                                            I always found this design choice interesting, as it implies that Beasts have either already fallen to monstrosity and become utter horrors whose only moral direction is that which they impose on themselves or that they are, in a way, the splat which is closest to true (real-world) humanity, in that they are free to decide what they are and will become. The thing is that if a Beast is a monster, it is because he chooses to indulge his darker side. If he is a murderer, it is because he chose not to satisfy his Hunger in a gentler way. If he goes mad, it is because his mind could not handle the stress of his life, not because some outside factor such as the integrity of his soul, his closeness to humanity or his balance between the spirit and human worlds. A Beast is, in all ways but the Devouring itself, what he has made of himself.

                                                            And I would like to say that I'm not getting involved in the 'choice vs Fate' argument with this post.
