    I don't think it was pointed out before, but Integrity by Attrition gives you a way to recover Integrity without paying Experience. This solves a common problem and critique about Integrity and Integrity equivalents: they can and will be lost through roleplaying, but cost you the same resource (XP) that is spent on permanent things, which turns losing Integrity into "too bad, you are now going to be 2 XP behind everyone else because they don't have to be punished for failing an Integrity check".
    This applies to most of the Integrity replacements in the game lines, too. "Recover Humanity through roleplaying event for no XP cost" is a common homerule. It's also pretty obnoxious in Mage, where you need to spend a whole story trying to up your Wisdom just so you can spend XP to bring it up by one, and you lose control of your character at Wisdom 0.

                    Integrity by Attrition is pretty much that, with added gameplay element that allows you to track it easier and have a sense of progress towards your goal. Looking at legacy of games (both video, board and tabletop), getting to mark a halfway point between regaining a point and not regaining a point tends to make the halfway point almost as satisfying as reaching the end, while increasing feeling of player agency ("I did a thing, and it made the bar towards my goal progress, I have a visual representation of my actions having consequences, so cool")
                    This is BTW "science" behind tons of facebook clickey games and addiction to achievments.

                            Yeah, Integrity suffers from the death spiral phenomena. The less you have, easier it is to lose it. So if you don't re-buy it fast, you are increasing your chance of losing more and more of it, costing you more experience. It also highlights one of Attribute system problems, where Composure and Resolve tend to do be worth more than other Attributes, simply because they contribute to a lot of subsystems. Specifically with Integrity in mind, they determine your pool for Breaking Points, which makes certain characters less likely to lose Integrity, so you can't expect all characters to fail around the same number of Breaking Points - contrast it with Wisdom in Mage, where each character has ~the same chance of passing Wisdom Checks, and math seems to be balanced that on a average, you will make enough Beats from Wisdom Breaking Points to restore it, and then have a net gain of one or two over course of the Chronicle.

                                                It does, and how you like your integrity comes down to the preferences of one troupe to the next.

                                                I personally like the death spiral of integrity in my games because of a few reasons:
                                                - I am sometimes pretty generous with Experiences and beats.
                                                - My games are long but often don't last a very very long time. Meaning I've yet to see anyone actually reach the int 0 (I've seen Cover 0 I have but that's totally different).
                                                - I'm also pretty forgiving on when to roll for breaking points.
                                                - And lastly, the easy slip in integrity is a big background part in almost every one of my games.

                                                The above means that my players tend to one of the following:
                                                1. They try hard to not lose integrity in the first place and so they wont more often than not.
                                                2. They try and fail to not lose integrity and don't bother with regaining that dot due to rationalizing the breaking point away and thus compromise their values if even slightly.
                                                3. They try and fail to not lose integrity but roleplay the breaking point and the loss of integrity and the desire for redemption or otherwise cannot rationalize it away which I would likely award special integrity beats for the roleplaying.

                                                I like it as a tool for the storytelling the spiral in my games. Half of my players are of the second kind and the other half are the third kind and both work deliciously as additions to the story.

                                                    CHAPTER TWO: INSTRUMENTS OF VIOLENCE

                                                    The chapter starts out with a list of general Merits that are designed for mortal characters but can be used for most splats, save from a few referencing mechanics that don't make sense for said splats. I'm going to go over the Merits from the book without revealing too much mechanical detail.

                                                    Air of Menace: Enhances the character's ability to intimidate others but also makes sure to address the fact that looking menacing isn't always a good thing.

                                                    Body as Weapon: Unarmed attacks deal more damage. It's interesting that it's Stamina-based instead of Strength-based, but I assume it has to do with hardened knuckles.

                                                    Cohesive Unit: This is a powerful Merit for the social leader type character. It gives plenty of benefits to a team under the character's command.

                                                    Defender: This Merit gives additional Willpower points specifically meant to be used to protect loved ones. I can't really address the balance of this one since my lack of experience with the game makes it so that only a single point of Willpower has been spent in my pbp so far, and that one was replenished quickly. This is a good Merit to have for the kind of games where you burn through Willpower quickly which I suppose a violence-heavy game does.

                                                    Empath: The way this Merit explains how you can identify other character's Conditions is something I'd think you'd be able to do with an ordinary Empathy roll. It's not really difficult to see that someone is Shaken for example.

                                                    Object Fetishism: Right. This Merit lets you create Negan from the Walking Dead. You associate a Skill Specialty with your favorite object and draws a bunch of additional Willpower and so forth. It's a very very powerful Merit for the character who has a favored weapon, like Negan's Lucile.

                                                    Peacemaker: This is a very powerful Merit and a must-have for the kind of empathic, social character that wants to play pacifist.

                                                    Punch Drunk: This Merit lets you keep fighting even after you should be knocked out. Very cool.

                                                    Scarred: This is an interesting one. It lets you take on a persistent Condition when failing a breaking point. The only way to get rid of the Condition is to also get rid of this Merit.

                                                    Support Network: This Merit lets you prolong an Integrity breaking point for some time.

                                                    Survivalist: You are more resistant to extreme cold or heat.


                                                    FIGHTING MERITS

                                                    The book changes the way Fighting Merits work from the rulebook by freeing them up and making them less structured. I like the change for the most part, but I think some styles have too strict requirements based on the limited number of dots you get at character creation.

                                                    I enjoyed the paragraph about the arguments over the best style. When I was in grade school, I was a judo kid. I thought I was really cool at the time. As I became a teenager, I had watched a bunch of kung fu movies and started training hung gar kuen, tai chi and sanshou. I learned how to flail around while thinking I was cool. Then when I got older (17-18), I realized that I was bowing to altars in a dojo and talking about chi like some weird religious sect. I suddenly found the whole thing completely ridiculous and stopped doing it. But then again, I didn't train "fighting". I went to a class where people imagined that they were learning how to fight. I learned to be a little acrobatic and how to make cool moves in the air. Put me up against someone who had trained "fighting" and I'd be crying and pissing myself.

                                                    Now let's look at the actual Style Merits.

                                                    Avoidance: It's completely unnecessary to have Manipulation 3 as a prerequisite for this style. If you really want Manipulation as a prerequisite at all, bring it down to 2, Athletics can remain at 2 and the Stealth 2 should be swapped for Subterfuge 1.

                                                    Insignificance: Okay, the first technique could use Stealth because you're trying to blend in to seem non-threatening, but I'd argue that seeming non-threatening could equally well fall under Subterfuge.

                                                    Whack-a-Mole: This technique calls out for a Manipulation + Persuasion + Avoidance roll against your enemy's attack. Once again, change it to Subterfuge. You're not persuading your opponent to injure themselves. You're tricking them into injuring themselves.

                                                    Play Dead: Finally a technique that uses Subterfuge without me having to house rule it.

                                                    Avoidance is a cool style, but I think I made it make more sense with my complaints above. It fits as a Subterfuge-heavy style, and should therefore require basic Subterfuge instead of Stealth 2 to learn.To have it require Manipulation 3 is just nuts.

                                                    Berserker: This style requires Stamina 3 and Iron Stamina 3. For the theme of this particular style, it works. You have to really build a specific type of character though through the Iron Stamina requirement. But this isn't the typical fighting style.

                                                    Bowmanship: It requires Dexterity 3, Firearms 2, Trained Observer 1. I think Dexterity 3 is steep. You don't have to be masterful to start learning these techniques. Dexterity 2 is fine. I'd argue that bowmanship isn't very dexterious in its requirement and execution at all. Composure could have been another trait that would have worked as a prerequisite.

                                                    Boxing: Very jack of all trades with the requirements. It works though. I like the techniques overall. It feels like a very powerful and fun style to have in game.

                                                    Chain Weapons: This style requires Strength 3 and Dexterity 3 which means that if you want to have it chargen, your Stamina must be 1. I hate that. My character is being railroaded into a certain way just because I want to have this particular style. Once gain, stop being to strict on the requirements. It's completely unnecessary. I'd suggest that the Strength requirement is removed completely. Dexterity can remain at 3 because of how difficult it is to control chain weapons.

                                                    Combat Archery: It makes sense to have Strength as a requirement here because of how exerting archery is, but I'm curious why it's so completely different in its requirements than Bowmanship. I would change these prerequirements a little, and not necessary "nerf" them. They are currently Strength 3, Athletics 2, Quick Draw (Bow). I'll lower Strength 2 and add in Dexterity 2 as well. It fits better here than in Bowmanship (which I suggest Composure for) because of the active nature of this style.

                                                    Falconry: It requires Wits 3 and Animal Ken 3, and you need the Bonded Condition with the bird. I'm fine with Animal Ken 3 but I question the need for Wits 3. I think Wits 2 or maybe Presence 2 would work here, but I don't care enough to actually house rule it.

                                                    Grappling: Lower the Stamina requirement from 3 to 2. Then I'm fine with it.

                                                    K-9: My comments on this style are the same as my comments on Falconry above.

                                                    Kino Mutai: I'd suggest swapping the Dexterity requirement for Stamina. I'm fine with keeping Resolve at 3 because you're actually biting people.

                                                    Martial Arts: The prerequirements are absolutely too high here. You can remove the Resolve requirement completely. Dexterity can be reduced to 2. Athletics and Brawl can remain as they are. If you really want to keep Resolve for some reason (you shouldn't), then lower it to 2. When comparing these tecniques to the ones in the rulebook, the rulebook are somewhat more interesting to me. I think these are solid alternatives though, and I'm fine with mixing them up.

                                                    Mounted Combat: Lower Dexterity to 2.

                                                    Powered Projectile: Same here. It's unnecessary to force Dexterity 3 on you to learn these techniques.

                                                    Spear and Bayonet: Lower Strength to 2.

                                                    Staff Fighting: Hmm. I've trained staff fighting a lot when I was a "cool" kid as mentioned above. Though it's not as difficult as chain weapons, it is very difficult to maintain control over the staff while in a situation where someone is fighting back. I'm fine with keeping the prerequirements as they are.

                                                    Strength Performance: Well, since this style focuses on strength feats, I'm going to do something crazy and not suggest to lower that requirement.

                                                    Systema: This is a fun style that I think you can play around with a lot in game. It has steep requirements, but the nature of the style justifies that it's a step above some of the other styles in its prerequisites. I'm fine with it.

                                                    Thrown Weapons: Throwing weapons is hard. I'll keep the prerequisites as they are for this one. Maybe even add Composure 2 as a secondary prerequisite.

                                                    Two Weapon Fighting: It doesn't need Wits 3 as a prerequisite. It doesn't really need Wits at all, to be honest. Change it to Dexterity 2. Keep Fighting Finesse, and keep Weaponry at 3.

                                                    Weapon and Shield: Lower both Strength and Stamina to 2. Lower Weaponry to 1.

                                                    I'm not going to comment on each of the Individual Merits, but I do like most of them. I especially enjoy Roadkill. When we were creating some characters for some of my friends, one of them wanted to play a trucker with high Drive. We debated a lot whether to get Roadkill and Aggressive Driving, both of which are Merits I've read before when we made those characters and both of which I enjoyed a lot. He decided not to get them (right now) because of the steep requirements in combination with some of the driving Merits from the rulebook he wanted instead.

                                                    Drone Control: I don't think this style needs Intelligence 3. It probably doesn't even need Computer 3. I'd argue that you should lower both to 2.


                                                    LASTING TRAUMA

                                                    I've been thinking about how to run this. I have my solo game that I've mentioned a lot before, in which the character isn't combat oriented at all. I'd probably be very gritty when it comes to violence in that game. I am going to storytell for a group IRL too, and those have differing opinions about what makes a roleplaying game fun. Though they all have experiences with older WoD games, they like active games where they roll a lot of dice. You can certainly do that in this game, but the focus of this game is on the storytelling. I'm not sure they'd go for the gritty combat. We'll have to see how it feel in game, I suppose.

                                                    There's this info box called Or Death (Optional) where it suggests that you can forego death and instead take on a lasting injury. When I storytell Exalted 3E, I do this, telling players that they can cheat death once per story in exchange for a crippling effect. We had this cool scene where the Twilight Caste was stabbed in the eye, and he's been wearing an eyepatch since. Now he's lost his other eye too and is going for this whole Zatoichi shtick. For a game like Exalted, that works. It's cool. The characters feel like badasses. And you can eventually let them heal their crippling injuries as the reward for an epic storyline.

                                                    In this game, though. I'm not sure. I kind of like the idea of putting the fear of death in my players.

                                                    I wish the new Conditions in this chapter would have been part of the Condition Cards I bought. I can probably whip something together myself though.


                                                    I really wanted to finish this chapter right away, but the Violent Skills part is too text heavy for me to have time to invest in it right now. So we'll leave it like this for now and pick it up again later. Now I'm off for BBQ.
