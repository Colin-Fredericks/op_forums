                With regard to other player-controlled characters, the target may choose to accept the Family Ties Condition, or she may decide the Beast must use Social maneuvering
                so this rule only applies to other player-controlled characters, and even then the Beast can overrun this decision by using the same rules as they do with everyone else. NPC don't have a say in Family Ties.

                Even if the target is willing, the two still must spend considerable time together, endure common hardship, hunt together, or otherwise become close before this Condition can be applied.
                I'll admit this one skimmed my first reading.

                As for the Family Ties Condition...it does grant the bonuses to both the Beast and the Character, but the only drawback (Beats for not playing your own game) only applies to the family member, not the Beast itself. This particular effect makes sure the game is all about the Beast rather than about cross-over play.

                As for Satiety Potential, it states that you can't feed from your family members as effectively as from non-members...but you're already feeding from mortals so I don't understand where's the drawback, since this is not Vampire where at some point mortals are not good enough.

                I also understand that a Beast can only get Kinship Nightmares thanks to family members...so why wouldn't a Beast run around and Family everyone?

                Passing Resemblance also asks for a Family Member, again saying that a Beast is better off getting as many friends as possible rather than a few close ones.

                To stay on track: I would have tried and make Beast a cross-over friendly game in the sense that it tries to get into everyone's business rather than getting everyone to care about the Beast's business.

                                    07-27-2015, 06:22 PM
                                    Beast is a game about cross-over play - and it succeeds in making sure every splat can get into the action. My point is that the game seems to have been designed with the idea that the PCs would always be Beasts. Again, if this was the idea, it succeeds. But since the Kinship Nightmares look like an easy way to get into other character's niche, since Beast's family mechanic is built with the Beast as the central character, since the Beast can cross dimensions without problem, etc... the game looks built to make sure the Beast always have an option and a say in the action - which again is fine if you consider the PCs are all Beasts.

                                    Let's take a coterie of vampires with a Beast in it. The whole Coterie is the Beast's family. As such, every player has a condition which states that the main character is the Beast (Bonuses if you share the spotlight with the Beast, Beats if you stop playing Vampire in order to feed the Beast)

                                    I honestly don't know how to solve this issue with the current game. It seems built around this concept and it would be hard to dislodge from there, but I believe that changing the way Family Ties work would fix a lot of it for me. Give The Beast incentive to care about his family rather than the other way around. If the theme is to choose your own family, make it so that choice is hard and interesting and asks a lot of questions, rather than having a condition you apply to someone to make him ruin his supernatural life in order to be with you. It looks more like a Vinculum effect than a Family Tie.

                                        Family Ties benefits go both ways. The bonuses encourage the Begotten and supernatural they've bonded with to share the limelight as a team. It doesn't really steal protagonism. The connection and other things the Beast can do for them give mechanical incentive for the recipient to care, but the Begotten has equally strong reasons to, with their own mechanical incentive.

Treat the narrative /supported/ by the mechanics with care.

                                                        Looking at all the times "Family Ties" comes up in the pdf, here's what I found.

                                                        Beast Benefits:
                                                        Can create Kinship Nightmares
                                                        Can use Passing Resemblance
                                                        Better Family Dinners
                                                        Easier to add Chambers created by the target
                                                        +1 to assist or be assisted by the target
                                                        Drawback: Worse at feeding on the target and their type of supernatural

                                                        Target Benefits:
                                                        Can use the Beast's Resistance Attributes when in their presence
                                                        Mother's Kiss is more effective
                                                        Get 1 Beat/session if they get in trouble for associating with the Beast
                                                        +1 to assist or be assisted by the Beast

                                                        I don't understand how any of this "makes the target feel like prey." Note that the target doesn't get Beats for "playing Beast" they get Beats when their relationship causes complications, which (to me, at least) is emphatically not the same thing. Also, pretty much all of the target's benefits equate to "the Beast is better at supporting me," so it really isn't a spotlight stealer.

                                                        As for why the Beast should get involved in trouble for the target's sake? To maintain Family Ties! The target can resolve the Condition at any time by "cutting ties with the Beast" (and get a beat for it)! From a kind of sociopathic angle, if the target dies, the Beast also loses the benefit.

                                                        Idk, man. I certainly don't see a problem with doing kind of a two-way Condition so that the Beast can get Beats, too, but it seems like overkill to me.

                                                            Originally posted by Magnum Opus View Post
                                                            If I read the subtext correctly, you're saying that the "mechanical" advantage of being friend with a Beast is....the RP advantage of being friend with a Beast? How is that different than being friend with a Demon?
                                                            Demons can't open negotiations with a supernatural third party from a position of appeal and their arena of strength is in the mortal sphere; knowing what strings to pull to get City Hall on your side isn't going to help when the Fae come calling or the Strix decide you need reminded what vampires really are, and for the most part integrating your Cover with supernatural beings is a tertiary concern liable to attract attention. Beasts have a touch of the silver-tongued devil in their ludonarrative toolbox that the fallen angels have to work at through less specialized channels.

                                                            The nearest things the Unchained have to pocket dimensions in common use are specifically there as hidey-holes that you don't want people to find — the sole concession to mobility they possess just lets you open your toolshed from anywhere and get out in the same place when the coast is clear. Beasts are world-walkers with a heightened capacity for infamy and a personal hub-world that they can rearrange and authorize to suit their security needs. The degree of comparability is kind of low on that front.

                                                            The space Beasts occupy in crossover is generally to be the guy who makes it really tempting to give up on the whole "blending into human society" thing by being better able to offer you assistance if you indulge your own supernatural nature more often and stick close to the places where they're more powerful (which may include places where you have personally lost some degree of humanity, depending on the particulars). That in turn brings that aspect of the struggle with monstrosity more to the fore if you opt to focus on it.

                                                            If the theme of the game is that you do get to choose your family, by all means you should be able to choose your family.
                                                            There are three themes listed at the front of the book. "You choose your family" is in fact the exact opposite of one of them.
