                                                        Sorry about the derail, but... need to say this.

                                                        Originally posted by SaulottheGentle View Post
                                                        The horror in the World of Darkness is reliant on the ST giving the story.
                                                        I'm going to disagree strongly with this statement. There's a degree to which, yeah, everything in a TTRPG relies on the ST facilitating things by cultivating this, putting that off limits, challenging you with this, pulling you with that.

                                                        But there are two kinds of horror, in my view: externally-generated, and internally-generated. The ST can only provide externally-generated horror: scary boojums that leap out of the shadows with lolling tongues and susurrations whispering you into insanity. Sure, and that's great. I tend to find that kind of horror bland, personally, so I don't give it a lot of thought. What I find interesting, and what I think that CofD as a whole does exceptionally well, is internally-generated horror.

                                                        Instead of describing that right now, let me paste something I wrote a while back:

                                                        Originally posted by Errol216
                                                        Originally posted by Other Poster
                                                        The way my group plays WoD is much closer to Urban Fantasy with supernatural societies, clashing personalities and territorial politics and power plays than slice of life and/or horror and I'm wondering how you've changed things to suit what you prefer.

                                                        In fact...I'm almost not sure what horror gameplay even entails. Can someone share what goes on in a regular session that they consider to be a horror themed game?
                                                        For me? It's having enough carrots that your player is willing to do something terrible, and then having enough self-reflection that they can look back, from an OOC perspective, and find their character's actions repulsive. The subjective-stat mechanic is just a way, IMO, to prompt that self-reflection.

                                                        Of course, the thing about horror is that it's supposed to leave you unsettled. That's part of the definition. So horror generally doesn't get placed at the end of things: it gets placed in the preparation and the lead-up, at most, and then the PCs go and clean everything up and make everything better and feel very good about themselves.

                                                        But really where horror belongs, in an RPG? Is as a constant, inexorable temptation. You should constantly be finding reasons to do something terrible. It should be obvious, convenient, easy. You should be staring down the barrel of the gun, knowing all the time that it'd be simpler to just pull that trigger and kill that fucker. Let the Paradox go. Let Kuruth consume you. Let yourself Embrace him. Let yourself betray your motleymate. Choose to let go of humanity, just this once. It would solve the problem. It would solve it quickly, and efficiently, and damn the consequences. Move fast. Break things. That's what a PC does, right?

                                                        That kind of horror can work at the session-level, I think, because all you have to do is find situations where there's a meaningful choice between something horrific but easy and something boring but difficult. And as a player, you get tired of doing the hard thing. Or you're bored of doing the right thing. So you take a dramatic failure. Or you choose to do the wrong thing. And you roll that subjective stat. You ask if it's worth it. Of course it is. You're bored; you're frustrated; you're willing to break a few eggs because this omelette needs to just be done.

                                                        Suggestion: have your players write a diary entry about every roll they make against their subjective stat. Three or four sentences is enough; it's not supposed to be an essay. But have them really get inside their character's head, and figure out what that roll meant, internally, introspectively. Piece those entries together after the chronicle. Talk about them together. Talk about what your characters did, whether it was the right thing to do, if it was worth it.

                                                        See if that helps you find the horror. Or, at least, see if it helps you find the self-justification.
                                                        IMO, the ST's job is much less about "how to horrify your players" and more "how to set up situations in which the players horrify themselves". And I think that that's an important distinction.

                                                        I call the Integrity-analogue the "subjective stat".
