        Having played a fair bit of Forsaken 2e this was not my experience.

        Let's take for example the time our pack fought a pack of Pure. We caused the Pure pack to all death rage near the start of the combat (by murdering their totem). They all took Gauru form, and we mostly stayed in Dalu and Urshul form. We managed to wipe out the Pure pack without using any silver. How? By focusing on one enemy at a time. We used our gifts, positioning, and misdirection to make sure that most of the Pure were chasing after shadows and not able to bring their strength to bear. We then focused down one enemy before moving on to the next. A Gauru form Werewolf only heals their health track in lethal each turn - for most Werewolves this is going to be about 11 damage. Dealing 11 damage across 2-3 attacks in a turn is not very difficult. One thing we found useful was that you can make characters with lethal damage attack first in the round to fill up those 11 boxes. Any characters dealing bashing damage can then attack later in the same round and it will count as agg regardless. It took us about 2 rounds to take down each of the Pure using these tactics.

                Originally posted by maryshelly View Post

                Aurumae, I find your posts extremely helpful in explaining the game. Could you elaborate how your Pack destroyed the Pure pack in Kuruth? I am curious about which tactics were used, as well as gifts and any tools
                It's been quite a few years so lets see how much I can remember.

                We went in with the knowledge that we intended to wipe out the pack. They had been causing a bunch of problems for us and were up to some shenanigans in the Shadow that we wanted to put a stop to. Our plan going in was to distract them while two of our packmates took out their totem. Their totem was a huge phoenix type thing, and was way more powerful than ours. It was also responsible for the issues in the Shadow - they were setting the whole Shadow in the area on fire and had some kind of cult of humans they were using to empower the totem.

                The Pure were set up in a church, and their totem was on top of the church. If I remember correctly, we actually attacked them in the Shadow. Our Rahu and our Ithaeur (group A) snuck off to take down the totem, while the Cahalith (myself) the Elodoth and the Irraka (group B) took on the Pure pack assisted by our own Totem. Before launching the attack, I used This Story Is True to empower our Elodoth who didn't have a high brawl skill, and Song in Your Heart to empower the whole pack. We had also called a Siskur-Dah on the totem and most of us were using our Auspice 3 abilities (the ones that give you big bonuses on the Siskur-Dah).

                Group B launched the attack first to draw their attention. My character had a really high defense (I had 6 dots in athletics thanks to our totem advantage, and 4-6 dots in dexterity depending on form, so up to 12 defense), so the plan was basically to get them to focus their attacks on me in the opening stages. The Elodoth had Memento Mori, so the idea was that he would hang back, and if any of us took a lot of damage he would siphon the damage away. I think we went in to attack in Urshul and Dalu form, and as soon as the battle was joined I used War Howl (which was also the signal for group A to go).

                I think the Pure pack were initially pretty cautious, staying in Hishu or just taking Dalu form. I was basically blocking the doorway to the church so that was where the fighting started, with the Pure focusing on me as intended. One of the Pure hung back inside the church and was using Gifts to empower her packmates, so the Irraka decided to use her batman powers to take her out. The Irraka had some absolutely obscene weaponry dice pool with knives, and our Rahu had blessed the knives using Perfection of Form before we went to the attack. The outcome was that the knives had 8-again and something like 6 bonus successes on the first attack. Combine that with the rote action from Eviscerate and the result was that one of the Pure went from perfect health to something like 1 agg away from death in a single hit.

                Meanwhile the group on the roof were closing in on the totem. The whole time the totem had been flying around, raining fire down on us, and our own totem had been staying hidden as best it could. The Ithaeur used Shadow Gaze on the totem and learned that its ban and bane. I think the bane was Holy Water, which would have been handy had we not been in the Shadow. In any case, the Ithaeur was noticed and started taking the brunt of the totem's attacks, but he was protected by Shadow Hunter from the worst of it. The Rahu though was still unnoticed, and we then had a really cinematic moment where the Rahu leapt from the top of the church in Gauru form right on top of this blazing phoenix and tackled it straight to the ground below. The totem was then surrounded, with the Pure pack bottled up inside the church and most of our Pack still outside around it on the ground. We basically dogpiled it and tore it apart (getting a decent amount of essence back in the process).

                The ST decided that this counted as a Kuruth trigger and had the Pure pack all make rolls. All but one failed and went into death rage. Interestingly, I think with the death of their totem they technically no longer counted as a Pack for the purpose of Gifts etc. but it didn't end up mattering.

                I had taken a few sharp knocks by that point and was now facing down multiple Gauru, so I backed out into the area in front of the Church and took Gauru for a turn myself in order to heal. I think I might have had 2 or 3 agg which the Elodoth helped by taking a point on himself. The Pure came barrelling out of the Church, and we used the fact that they had to attack an opponent within striking range each turn to control who they would go after. One thing to note is that Gauru form has +4 speed while Urshul has +7, so by being able to choose our forms we could easily stay ahead of them. Our totem helped out too and I think it ended up getting discorporated by the end of the fight, but for the most part it was able to zoom around at high speed (it had speed 20 I think) throwing out Blasts with the Gauru chasing after it. Inside the Church the Irraka finished off the wounded Pure pack member. She then joined the fight outside and was able to use Hit and Run on the Gauru to deal some serious damage to them and then avoid reprisal.

                We took the enemies down one by one, focusing on whoever looked like they were weak that turn while denying the Pure the opportunity to do the same to us. We took some hard knocks, but by the end of the fight the only one left in their pack was the one who hadn't death raged, and he ran off. During the fight we made full use of our Gifts - I used Primal Strength as did the Rahu. I used Thousand Throat Howl to Demoralize them. The Irraka used Stealth and Evasion Gifts to use Eviscerate again and again. The Rahu used Killer Instinct to get some incredible brawl dice pools. We made use of our Auspice Benefits - the Irraka used Closer Than You Thought to suddenly appear behind the Pure in the Church and nearly one-shotted her (which I think was crucial to us winning, we knew that Pure had access to some scary Gifts), and our Elodoth used his ability to pull one of the Pure out of Death Rage - thus forcing him down into a weaker form and inflicting the Stunned tilt, which we were able to capitalise on. Furthermore, we all took Gauru at one point or another in order to heal or do a big attack, but avoided the form otherwise and were able to avoid death raging ourselves.

                    Mechanical considerations aside there is also story reasons restricting the use of Gauru, such as character goals.
                    To drag out the tired Superman example again if Kal's goal was to find out who was responsible for the dastardly deed and then rip their head off, Luthor is a one shot villain turned corpse, but its not, the goal is to find out who is responsible for the dastardly deed and then take them down using the criminal justice system.
                    Here Kal can't use his super strength to fight Luthor's connections
                    (At the time I was having dinner with the Governor)
                    his organization
                    (All of your evidence is circumstantial, there is nothing here that directly ties Mr Luthor to the crime)
                    and finally plain old corruption
                    (What do you mean the judge threw out the evidence, what about the witness...Police Officer: we are dragging Hobb's bay, don't hold your breath.)

                    For Werewolves their goals almost always revolve aroung their territory, so if the goal is to fill it with ruined buildings, a fleeing citizenry and a glut of fear and death spirits then Gauru is the perfect tool. If the goal is anything else then a little more discretion is probably best.

                        Referencing the last 2 posts, evasion seems to be the key to surviving the garou form. Either dodging (which doubles your defense pool), having a higher speed and running away, using Evasion gifts ( Hit and Run seems useful), celerity, wall climbing, burrow, discorporate...etc. The Hosts even have a built in Discorporate power which is an automatic escape route if the hunt wasn't prepared perfectly in advance.

                        Also the social consequences of Garou can be severe. The book hints that a hunter compact formed in Detroit due to the lunacy. In addition, the Shadow Shadows in the Spirit section suggests that they form when the lunacy affects a community.

                        Many times it seems that the improper use of Garou creates more problems than it solves.

                            If what you are after is a way to challenge werewolves that is not immediately bested by Garou, then my experience has been the secret is not to attack the wolves but their territory.
