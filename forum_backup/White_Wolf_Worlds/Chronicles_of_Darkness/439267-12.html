            Preface: I have not played anything. This is pure educated white room guesses. Without play experience, I can't really be more positive than to say "I see no problem with this", so assume anything I don't mention raises no red flags. Also, I'm not really inclined to cross-check 13 pages of feedback to determine if my point is brand new or different enough to warrant separate mention. From experience, I'll assume I have something new to contribute with.

            Let's go:

            Cohesive Unit:
            Dot 2 could use some clarification. My first impulse was players attempting to justify why all group actions are team actions deserving of dice benefit. Since that would make the two-dot version the most potent of the bunch, I'm thinking that's very much not the intent.
            Suggestion: Limit to Teamwork actions, or require some pre-planning along the lines of Know the Territory before people get to benefit. Something to differentiate the Team from the Troupe.

            At Any Cost:
            The idea seems fair enough, but as written it goes too far IMO. Why would a Mental merit about trucking on in the face of pain prevent that foot snagged by a halberd from rendering you prone? Why would you see when blinded? Why are you able to counteract the effects of the environment? How can you escape immobilization? Etc. Is that what the Emotional tag is all about? I couldn't actually find a writeup on the tags.

            Defender:
            This one is...complicated. I'll attempt to sort my thoughts on it.
            1) It seems easy to abuse by designating other troupe-members as loved ones. Unless the intent is to effectively give you 3 extra Willpower for 3 Experiences in dangerous situations, which compares poorly with Specialties and their benefit. The experienced Storyteller likely dreading party splits, the drawback then becomes negligible. The extra bookkeeping involved with off-the-books Willpower is a concern too.
            2) Even without such abuse, the drawback seems to infringe on the Storyteller and his Story. Unless a loved one outside the Troupe gets killed or kidnapped as part of the narrative, the drawback will derail the actual Story in favor of this side-quest where one character has to hunt down her husband's killer or suffer severe penalties.
            3) It seems to me the risk of loved ones is a heavy enough topic that it benefits from coming as a surprise. So why assume it will happen without the Storyteller spoiling his narrative ahead of time? Why assume he is enough of a dirtbag to just improvise such a thing for the lulz? But without such, can it possibly crop up often enough to warrant buying the merit? In an analogy, where is the Arson Insurance merit? Should come up about as often.
            Suggestion: From its fluff text I would personally have reworked it mechanically from the ground up to actually enable protection of others. But going off its proactive/reactive mechanics, all I can think of is to at least limit it to one Troupe member or Storyteller character close to the action, plus all loved ones outside of it. That seems like a good middle ground where you get something for your Experiences, but not too much.

            Know The Territory:
            Two complaints here:
            1) I can't think of a narrative justification for the Speed increase during advantage 1. At its mechanical extreme, a few minutes of pre-planning will turn average joes into Olympic sprinters simply from knowing the terrain. Even traceurs only remove environmental penalties.
            Suggestion: Let it remove terrain penalties instead. The Initiative increase is potent enough as it is.
            2) I can't think of a narrative justification for the bonus only lasting a few turns. If a character knows the lay of the land well enough to reap bonuses as significant as this merit provides, a short-term memory retention of 3-15 seconds seems counter-intuitive.
            Suggestion: Either let the bonus persist throughout the Scene, or let the commander "recast" it by barking orders once the time runs out, with its own dice action. In return, force him to only apply each advantage once, without the powerful stacking currently possible. Giving a glass window Durability 6 against gunfire strains credibility in any case.

            Living With Death:
            Doesn't seem to be worth it when compared to spending a point of Willpower. As I read it, it adds +4 dice max, at a significant drawback. If your character has to be dry of Willpower and beaten within an inch or her life before benefiting, 2-4 Experiences aren't equal to one Willpower worth of dice, especially if it requires Defense sacrifice on top, as potent as that trait has become in GMC. Hell, a case could be made that a measly +2 dice bonus in return for all your GMC Defense is broken to begin with. All-out was barely worth it in 1Ed.
            Suggestion: This is the exact sort of narrative occurrence I thought Willpower was there to represent to begin with. Let players invest their Experiences in more of that.

            Power Object:
            Suggestion: Could use some clarification, as the fluff now implies contextual bonuses, while the mechanics make them omnipresent.

            Master The Mask:
            I have to imagine using this with Subterfuge and Intimidation would be infinitely more useful than with Empathy and Persuasion. Looking at Subterfuge alone, it seems you are paying 3 Experiences for +2 dice on most rolls -at the risk of feeling Guilty-, as opposed to paying 4 Experiences without drawback normally. But with the other three Skills, the bonus doesn't seem to be worth it. The rare occasion where lying will be better covered by Empathy than Subterfuge can't possibly weigh up for the former now having become a minefield to use.
            Suggestion: To be honest, the merit seems superfluous. Lying and social masks are covered by Subterfuge and Composure as it is, and this one just seems to switch things around while making it even harder to determine which social dice pool is appropriate for an action. I'm know I'm not supposed to second what others have said, but this seems like a perfect example of just adding dice to normal stuff without really making things more interesting.

            Dirty Fighter:
            Firstly, any merit that requires you have an Attribute value of 4 or 5 before benefiting from it should have that as a prerequisite IMO. Joe average could never benefit from this one.
            Secondly, the benefit compared to Willpower use spans from 0-13 dice, if my math serves me. During challenging and dramatically interesting one-on-one encounters, where you actually need a boost, the benefit goes from 0 to +2 dice. When it is a boring, result given, many-vs-one curbstomp battle, only then does the benefit kick in for real. This at the Experience cost of an actually useful Fighting Style, one that doesn't carry potent drawbacks. The problem with that should be obvious.
            Suggestion: I have rarely seen a merit better suited to impose Tilts on an enemy. Would be a shame to waste it. The mechanics would get more complicated than I'm willing to consider here, but if Willpower has to be the limiter on this merit, the benefit should be something more potent and interesting than mere Defense reduction. Making an All-Out Attack the prerequisite action would be another, possibly better limiter.
            As for the drawback, with the WoD being such a dirty, gritty place, it seems overkill for people to be so sensitive to some practical fighting. Hell, how many fighting styles wouldn't qualify under such a scheme? Better to give immunity or resistance to such tricks to familiar characters IMO. If there has to be social consequences for fighting dirty, that can be handled on an individual character basis. I have no trouble envisioning characters in fact applauding your character's pragmatic kill-or-be-killed attitude.

            Fair Fighter:
            1) This one could benefit from some clarity on how it interacts with Intent for the combat. The narrative justification for the effect is really thin to begin with, and breaks down if your character is trying to kill the enemy.
            Suggestion: In line with its fluff text, it should require a self-defense or non-lethal Intent on your character's part. I would also call for some social actions before or early in the fight, as holding back out of honor merely from watching the opponent's stance and etiquette seems rather unlikely. With fights lasting less than 30 seconds most of the time (10 turns of combat), the fact that the enemy is fighting an inspiringly noble character is the sort of conclusion the system would normally require perception checks for him to even realize, much less act upon.
            2) If indeed an enemy's efforts are hampered by fighting fair, why doesn't your character suffer the same?
            Suggestion: Scrap it in favor of the following: If the enemy is swayed, he becomes incapable of performing targeted attacks, non-grappling prone attacks, and lethal brawling attacks. Furthermore, he will pull his blows so that he can't gain exceptional successes on attack rolls. Your character will suffer the same. Adjust cost as needed.

            Otherwise, seems kosher and/or covered by others already.


            Phew. That's all for today. I may or may not tackle the fighting merits at some later point.

                        Originally posted by Griautis View Post
                        Non-Military drones WILL NOT have a self-destruct button in them. Unless we're going for some really high action movies stuff...
                        Well clearly they do, that's the whole point of the Merit. Once again I refer you to the quoted text:

                        These maneuvers work independently of the sophistication of the actual device. An RC car with a mounted pistol and a wi-fi receiver works just as well as a state-of-the-art military drone for the purposes of using this Style
                        Since you're rolling Intelligence + Computer I assume this is some kind of software wipe. If that's the case, the normal thing to do would be to write a wiping program (rolling at this time), test it, once tested and installed execute it. You wouldn't write a new program for every drone, you could reuse the old one, and executing a simple program shouldn't require a roll.

                        If this is actually some kind of explosive device, shouldn't this be a craft roll? And shouldn't the roll be made before launching the drone?

                        I'd be happier if the text read:

                        Kill-switch (••): Your character routinely installs self-destruct systems in her drones and can cause them to self-destruct on command, making any attempts to retrieve information off of it impossible to mundane means. This doesn't require a roll. Characters without this Merit can attempt to install such systems with an Intelligence + Computer for software systems or an Intelligence + Crafts roll for physical systems.

                                                Just ran a game where a player used Avoidance and kept asking to house rule to replace the Persuasion requirements with Subterfuge (probably because it was the higher skill for him) but also because it just made a lot more sense for the spirit of the maneuvers. I would suggest that Subterfuge either replace or be a possible alternative for some characters.

                                                He also tried using the third dot (Whack-a-Mole) and were having trouble thinking of ways this actually looks in combat barring having the enemy punch a wall. So a couple examples would help (though I blame our storyteller and his improv skills), and specifying that it refers specifically refers to brawl and maybe weaponry attacks (and could maybe be a leg wrack or knockdown tilt instead, depending on the method used?) - Unless it's intentionally left open and you meant to allow bullet ricochets, etc. (Which would be kind of cool, actually, but clarity would help).

                                                He used it specifically after dropping prone behind one of his packmates, so he was being kicked and stomped at, no real room for arm wracks when you're rolling around on the ground.

                                                Despite all those things, both the Avoidance style and the Dirty Fighting merits were huge hits with the group and enabled some really cool things. Our Irraka used it to hide and use his sneak attack gift against an enemy while they were all ignoring him. Emphasizing the first dots ability to allow for escape or stealth would really upplay one of the coolest things about the merit.

                                                            For whatever it's worth, I'm currently working on revisions. I know Punch Drunk had a few questions, so I'm revising it thusly:

                                                            Punch Drunk (••••)
                                                            Prerequisite: Willpower ••••••+
                                                            Effect: Your character’s resolve is unwavering, even when suffering wounds, broken limbs and lost blood. She can fight on past the point that her body demands she quit. When she suffers bashing or damage that would remove her last Health box, you may spend a point of Willpower to keep the last box, and instead upgrade damage in her other health boxes. This Merit has no effect on aggravated damage.
