    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
    (Note also that I didn’t mention Blood Bonding; that’s partially because I’m not terribly familiar with Vampire, and partially because what I do know of Blood Bonding doesn’t strike me as being the same sort of thing as the others: as I understand it, nobody gets immunity to Blood Bonding, not even vampires.)
    I could see some people being more resistant to Blood Bonding than others, but in general, it should be a very rare thing. Vampiric Vitae is potent when ingested. And from another point of view, it's less common than the Lie, Lunacy or Disquiet - you need to actually ingest the Vitae for blood bonding to take hold, whereas the other three effects happen every time a mortal human sees a Supernal spell, a werewolf's bestial form or interacts with a Promethean.

    Perhaps; I’m not averse to the notion of immunity being preceded by resistance.
    Outright immunity should be reserved for supernatually touched mortals, like Sleepwalkers or Wolf-Blooded. Normal people should be able to push through those effects with effort - Tier Two hunters with noticeably less effort than Tier One, in this case.

    Again, list the consequences of each option so the Storyteller can make an informed decision. And there are mortals who are immune to Disquiet (e.g., some of the luckier among the Redeemed); so it’s not like granting such immunity is without precedent.
    True, there are different views on Disquiet and the nWoD is nothing if not modular.

    Transitioning down the Tiers would harder to justify, though, as it would involve giving up options that had previously been available. Going from tier three to tier two play would likely involve losing any Endowments the hunters have, because Endowments are a Tier Three thing.
    How likely losing Endowments is depends on the organization in question; someone who leaves TFV without being court-martialled and locked away somewhere will lose access to its resources, and a Malleus Maleficarum member might suffer a crisis of faith that'll leave her unable to use Benedictions. But a Lucifuge member is unlikely to lose the Castigations.

            Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post

            Fortunately conspiracy design is not about humour. Obviousness isn't bad, and AKD's mummy links isn't just a possibility, it's an inevitability. The two groups have identical goals - acquiring relics, they're been fighting a large chunk of history. That is going to get into their collective culture.
            Wow. I woke up this morning reflecting a little on how lazy my response and thinking that nothing could be lazier, and then I read "Fortunately conspiracy design is not about humour".

            Well, I feel better about the initial statement. Granted, you did go and support it, but still..well, again, I think some of the point is missed. I think a lot of the point was missed.

            I mean, don't get me wrong: of course these guys are enemies. Of course they butt heads. But by and large, that's because their surface elements run similar to one another, as a matter of incidence.

            But as thematics go, mummies just don't tap into the entire 'paradise lost' thing the AKD have as their core, which is why their enemies are mages and werewolves as well as why their endowments are relics.

            See, that's the thing that's all over the place, this sentiment that we've lost heights unimaginable, and are left with naught but shadows, and all responsible will pay. Out of...well, all the conspiracies, really, the two oldest are the Aegis Kai Doru and the Ascending Ones. But where the Ascending Ones claim roots in one of the oldest civilizations of the world, the AKD claim older, and they have perpetually described the first world as paradisical. The fact the world isn't is where their oath comes from, and everything the AKD does reflects a broken world where they have to make do with the pieces. They don't have magic of their own, they have to play with the shards of magic found in stuff(relics)-which at least isn't tainted in some form or fashion, like just about every other form of Endowment out there in Hunter, but still, people lack inherent magic(innate sleepwalker status, if you will, tying into FWC), and the world is not as it should be, there are divides and wounds in the entire cosmology. And who sits pretty at the heart of the explosion?

            Mages, inherently. Mages still have magic, and boy isn't that unfair. Mages speak about the fractured divide between the heights and here and they talk about it being the fault of their ancestors,and the concepts they play with seem to speak to the divide. It doesn't take long interacting with them to realize they deserve all the shit.

            Werewolves are less inherent, but the way they explain things makes it just as clear: humanity's fallen state is their fault as well. THese guys are primal, and their stories just as much so, and the more they explain their duty and sense of morality and why they have it, the more and more it becomes apparent that they are moved, mythologically, by the destruction of the first world, and they claim their ancestors had to do it.

            On top of that, both sides seem to propagate the fracture. Spirit and Flesh weren't once so divided, but the Werewolves, on top of rending a gash in the seperation, actively seek to make things even more separate(from the AKD's point of view, anyways). Mages recklessly use their gifts, that they deny most of humanity, and in their recklessness they rip the fabric of reality and introduce dark things from beyond, tearing apart the world. Yeah, these guys need to be hunted down. They need to be punished, and ideally removed, so that the at the least things don't get worst. Even if they don't make things worse, at least by killing them off you will have avenged the whole of humanity.

            Mummies are also symbols of things getting fucked up, but conversations with them don't lead to a changing of the fundamental nature of the world. They may have screwed with civilizations, but they aren't responsible for the divide that Mages and Werewolves seem to be. So while mummies are annoying for eating the magic of the world and denying the AKD even their broken glass pieces of a higher world, at the very least they aren't walking testaments to a fallen world. The Guardian's issue with them is more business-like than it is with most- you hate them for the struggle of resources, but not on an ideological level.

            And by the way: Yes, this isn't humor, but funnily enough, horror and comedy play on the same principles a lot. And Hell, good storytelling is a mix between the inevitable and the twists. A straight "Oh, they both go after relics,revise the oath and themes to fit that" is boring. It is so boring. What they've done with AKD is far more interesting, more dynamic, and stronger on a thematic level that makes the organization vivid and alive rather than just an item on a checklist.

            Mummies as a frequent opponent? Sure. Mummies as part of the Oath? Fuck no.

            Kelly R.S. Steele, Freelance Writer(Feel free to call me Kelly, Arcane, or Arc)

                        Arc, I have to disagree, not with the core concept you have here, but with the underlying evidence you're using to support your point. Basically, you're saying "Werewolves because paradise lost." I think that Mummies have a far bigger paradise lost theme than Werewolves.

                        Yes the First Pack's sin, if taken as truth, did far more damage to the world than anything the Iremites did. But in contrast Irem is far more important to Mummies than Father Wolf's death is to the Forsaken. The core of a standard Werewolf game is taking some territory and guarding it. The core of a Mummy game is remembering your past, your Iremite past. In addition Mummies spend tons of time finding ways to honor Irem in the modern world or make the modern world more like Irem.

                        I think that what the survivors are doing today matters far more for a "paradise lost" theme than the scale of the cataclysm.


                        Next I point out that I actually didn't like the way the "paradise lost" theme was handled in the current AKD. 90% of it's actual effects can be boiled down to "kill Witches and Werewolves" It felt small minded and petty, in a Conspiracy that should be about the grand sweap of history. I don't want them to be focused on revenge for an ancient grudge, I want them to have big proactive plans that tie back to their lost paradise. Plans to rebuild it. Plans to rediscover lost historys. Legends of specific relics from the old world that they want to recover. Heck, you could say that they only care about monsters because they're in their way. Their theme of paradise lost has potential, turning it into yet another example of "we hate those monsters" wastes that potential.

                        And, well, the Mage setting and the Mummy setting are full of opportunities for grand Archeology/Relic themed plans to change the world. The Werewolf setting is not. Pick Mage, pick Mummy, pick neither. But just make whatever choice leads to big plans - that choice is not Werewolf.



                        In summery. I believe that Mummy is a better fit for the Paradise Lost theme, a better fit for the archeology theme, and most importantly it's setting provides more opportunities for the AKD to be proactive with their goals. In contrast Werewolf has a Paradise Lost as backstory but not a theme, and little opportunity for archeology or the AKD to be proactive in anything other than killing.

                                    On a more meta-level; the AKD are supernatural archaeologists. They collect magical stuff from around the globe. Mages have lots of artifacts and Mummies* have lots of artifacts, so they fit well as primary antagonists. *Mummies don't actually just have artifacts, they actively hunt for them and hoard them. It's nonsensical to not have them cross paths with the AKD more than any other supernatural group.

                                    TRYING TO PUT THIS DEBATE ASIDE FOR A MOMENT, I think Hunter needs more unique antagonists. The novelty of vampires, werewolves, and mages can quickly wear off for folks who have been romping around in the supernatural sandbox of the World of Darkness for a number of years. Throwing in some fresh new monsters that don't have easy analogues from other gamelines means that players can actually be as clueless as their characters for once, and have a fun time actually looking for weaknesses rather than trying to find fire/silver/cold iron/etc.

                                    I'd also love to divorce Tier from Compact/Conspiracy. A VALKYRIE cell should just as easily be a single fireteam that hears from their higher-ups every once in a blue moon for a Night at the Opera (Tier 1) as they are a jet-setting group of international military troubleshooters (Tier 3). Tier is essential to Hunter play and I'd like as many of the groups as possible to function at all levels.

                                    Remi. she/her. game designer.

One of my disappointments with Mortal Remains was that this aspect of things tended to get lost: for instance, the chapter on Prometheans didn’t read like wild speculation on the many different kinds of artificial beings that might show up in the WoD; rather, it read more like a beta test of the rules for the Promethean Chronicle book. I’d like to see the Hunter Chronicle book rectify that by presenting at least starting points for alternate takes on each of the standard monster types: rather than focusing in on how to more closely mimic the inspiring gameline’s protagonists, “blur the lens” to encourage the Storyteller to try different takes on them.
