    Originally posted by ReshyShira View Post

    Until the True Fae just make another one to send at you. Speaking of which, what prevents a True Fae from sending a dozen at you other than being rude to the spirits?


    I think, though, that a fixed 2 week period is too long. Maybe something less clearly defined? If you put in two weeks then it becomes predictable and that destroys the paranoia element. Also I think two-weeks is too short for the king of threat huntsmen are supposed to be. If the dude is someone you slay on a monthly basis, then it kind of loses it's luster I'd think? After all, it's the same huntsmen, so they're going to fall into a routine and become predictable.


    Also I wonder, what happens if a Changeling captures and restrains one rather than kill it and let it poof away to fairy land again?
    1) The summoning ritual requires an essential piece tied to you. Most True Fae have one, but damn if they lose it. No, killing a Huntsmen is a pretty good deterrent for the Huntsmen ever coming back again.

    2) You assume a Huntsmen just comes right back and splats themselves against the same problems. Huntsmen are more likely to take their time and come up with clever ideas with each failure than charge on in-they may want to go back home, but they're viciously scheming creatures out to figure out a win rather than raging bulls trying to knock down a gate. The time gaps increase as more provisions are put into play, and each time a Huntsman turns up, the stakes are raised.

    Unless you have a shit storyteller, but again, you shouldn't play with shit people.

    3) Good question. One to be answered at the table, I think.

    I mean, they don't just magically wiff away(unless I'm forgetting something, but I don't think I am), so you and your group has some options on what to do with a captured Huntsmen.

    They're about as slippery as changelings, though, so I wouldn't try holding them for too long.

                                        1) No, it's not a Title thing, it's a changeling thing. Give me a second....

                                        Here we are. Basically, the summoning ritual requires something, an item, that represents the changeling from their time in Arcadia. The Huntsman carries this item with it, it serves a major function for them, and when they are finally killed, that item is left for the changeling to reclaim.

                                        Unless it's worked into the background, most changelings don't get a lot of emblems(I'm calling them that until a proper name is introduced)-it's a safe assumption that there's only one or two as a convergence of sensibility and narrative.

                                        2) Physical existence is irritating. In some ways, it's frightening. But it's manageable.

                                        Underneath mortal refuse and Arcadian sinew, though, there is a living story of the hunt, and Huntsmen, for all their dislike of the human world and flesh, are creatures who revel in the Hunt. These aren't Uratha, where the hunt is a primal, adrenaline filled action. It's lyricism, an artistry, a thrill of intellect and personality and precision. Even the brutal Wolf, who does kick down doors and ravage his targets before dragging them in a bleeding stupor back to Arcadia, prefers a certain slyness to his approach, a little convincing of pig and ingenue to let him and his big teeth in. This is not just a rush, this is not just ritual-this is art, this is the invisible being found by feeling it out. Even the most brutally done paintings have some measure of thought or feeling to it, if it's worth anything. For a Huntsmen, there is a satisfaction in doing the hunt right, and that often weighs as heavily on them as the desire to go home.

                                        3) Like I said, I don't know. It's a question I would love to tackle in supplements, if the opportunity comes. But for now, it's best to say that's a question for the table. Cooler and stranger answers will come from there than us answering.

                                        But yeah, iron, cold iron, is master of all. The Huntsmen are reasonably chained under them.

                                        EDIT: Oh, there's a time limit? Well, that changes things.

                                        Anything I've said that can reasonably be assumed to be based on the Huntsmen having unlimited time? Scratch that.
