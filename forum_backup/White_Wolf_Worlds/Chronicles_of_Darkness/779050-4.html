                Activating the power is reflexive. Using it to attack people (instead of it's other functions, or just looking scary because "Holy crap their mouth is full of fire!") is an instant action.

                Reflexive single-shot attacks are part of a larger group of actions 1e allowed that multiple writers, and developers, have repeatedly said are things not intended to be part of 2e. Unless the text is 100% explicit that something would allow you to attack more than once in a turn, it should always be assumed that 2e authorial intent is for it to not allow you to do that.

                                                            The alternative that the rules written are, in fact, the rules written, however ill-conceived? Looking over the stuff Beasts can do in other atavisms - reflexively become invisible and immaterial at the end of their action, making them immune to attackers not specifically delaying until they pop out for a second, chunk heal their entire Health reflexively while also gaining immunity to wound penalties and unconsciousness - I feel like it's way more likely that someone said, hey, wouldn't it be cool if they could breathe fire reflexively while doing other stuff than that they said, hey, let's say that Dragonfire is a reflexive activation that counts as a ranged attack, and trust players to know that we're lying, it actually DOESN'T count as a ranged attack but allows you to spend an instant action to MAKE one, and the reflexive activation refers to the persistent power of fire resistance despite having neither duration nor indication that if it is indeed indefinite you can even turn it off.

I can understand a mistake like that. The one you think is "reasonable" to assume beggars my imagination.
