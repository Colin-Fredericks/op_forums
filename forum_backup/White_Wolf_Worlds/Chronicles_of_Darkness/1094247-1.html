    Maybe a variation of this Condition could help:

    Damaged (Persistent)

    You got a bad knee or shoulder from your sports days or the time that werewolf chewed you. After three combat turns, it's obvious that something's wrong with it. This Condition may reflect multiple body parts. Decrease the damage for inflicting a Tilt related to this parts by one. (Option 2: Receiving a successful specified target attack on a related body part causes an additional point of damage)

    Possible Sources: Getting a Tilt involving the body part, be on the receiving end of an exceptional success attack roll.

    Resolution: Gain a dot of Athletics, get it healed with physical therapy, surgery, magic ritual or other Storyteller approved mean.

    Beat: End an altercation without getting a Tilt related with this Condition.

        Originally posted by PenDragon View Post
        Two words: Lasting Repercussions.

        Players will be much more reticent to leap into combat if a mistake stays with them longer than a few hours. Player gets shot, turn that into arm wrack and deny them an arm for a month. Player kills someone, slap them with a PTSD condition that rears its head whenever they come into later combats.

        Be wary about killing characters though. That sounds like a good option, but frequently has the opposite effect. If players are afraid of losing their character in combat they'll deliberately spec to be better at combat. Which, in turn, forces you to turn up combat lethality. This spirals upward until the system splinters under combat optimization.

        Consider also evaluating the goals of combat. Who are the players fighting? Why are they fighting? What will they gain from combat? A random group of mooks is just something to beat up for fun, but the beloved town sheriff mesmerized by vampiric magic is a tragic figure to save. Basically, the more deeply tied the action is to the characters and the plot the more likely they'll have reasons to avoid violence.
        I like the idea of lasting repercussions. I would think that mimics reality quite well. And though it may not seem obvious, my intention is not to kill characters easier. Whether or not they get into gun fights, my aim is to make the damage of bullets more respectable. That said, the gist of what I'm trying to accomplish is to make things a bit more "realistic" within the confines of a game.

        When I consider gun combat, I think about two true statements:
        1. People can take multiple gunshots and survive.
        2. People can die from one gunshot wound.

        Of these two statements, #1 is represented in the rules, quite well actually (at least in my opinion). #2 is not. I'm trying to address #2 without the validity of #1...much.

                                        It sounds like you want damage to be a bit more random and weighted toward higher numbers (fatal or near fatal damage) being easier to achieve. I think probably the best way to do that without breaking the system is to give more weapons 9 or even 8 again. That increases the average number of successes 10-20%, and makes a successful roll scarier because of the increased chance of cascading successes, without making total whiffs any less likely or removing the possibility of flesh wounds.

                                        You could also pair it with another hack I've considered, which is having weapon modifiers match successes, up to their maximum, instead of always granting the full modifier to any successful hit.

If I make four attempts to kill someone (four separate attack rolls of average competency) with a gun, will one of those result in a kill (assuming 7-8 health) Do I need to apply conditions such as "assault rifle" or "a character heavily invested into shooting people" to achieve that result?

                                            Not everyone dies from a well-trained shooter wielding an assault rifle. And I don't think they should in CoD, either.
                                            Well, that 18 to 30 percent is mortality for people who actually get hit by the bullet, which isn't a guarantee with an attack roll. So what you should be asking is if an average of one in four successful hits result in death for a character with 7 undamaged health boxes. Now, the answer is going to vary depending on the dice pool of the shooter and the gun they are shooting. Since you're asking about an average dice pool, let's go ahead and assume a base dice pool of 3: 2 from having average Dexterity and 1 from amature-level Skill with Firearms. With only 3 dice, the shooter is most likely only going to get 1 success. However, if they spend Willpower, their dice pool jumps up to 6, and if they fire a long burst or aim for 3 turns, it jumps up to a 9. With a light pistol, the shooter has a 2.1% chance of doing 7 damage on that dice pool before 10-again. A heavy pistol has roughly a 7.35% chance before 10-again. A shotgun has a 17.5% chance before 9-again. Most rifles have a 26.8% chance before 10-again.

                                            Of course, all of this is assuming we're trying to do all of the damage to kill the target with the gunshot alone. Which is kind of silly. Instant death from a gunshot is extremely rare, and it's usually the blood loss that kills people who get shot. The bleeding Tilt kicks in on any successful hit with any firearm on a target with 2 Stamina and no armor. Which will kill the victim if they don't get medical attention.

                                            Personally, I would argue that this is a reasonably accurate simulation of gun lethality - someone who's well-trained and/or really trying to kill you has a pretty decent chance of doing it in one shot if they use a big enough gun, and can very easily do it if they shoot you more than once. A single gunshot from an untrained shooter who's not making the maximum effort to be as lethal as possible probably won't kill you right away short of a stroke of exceptional luck, but will kill you eventually if you don't get medical attention in time. Sounds exactly like the way it should work to me.
