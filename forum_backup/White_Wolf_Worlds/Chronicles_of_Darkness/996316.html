    Clash Of Wills

    10-31-2016, 07:20 PM
    The mechanics of a Clash of Wills is pretty straight forward. However there is one part of the rules that I'd really like to make sure I understand correctly:

    Originally posted by MtAw2 pg 118
    Mages may spend Willpower to bolster the contested roll, and are always aware when their spells are clashing.
    So by way of example, if a Moros has cast corpse mask on a dead body to change the cause of death, and another Mage comes along and inspects the body with Forensic Gaze, they know immediately that something isn't right, regardless of whether or not they are the successful party in the Clash, because they still know that their spell is clashing with something. Is that right?

    I find myself deeply ambivalent about this rule if that's the case. On the one hand, from a meta perspective I can see the appeal of not ever having an avenue of investigation completely closed off by a potent veiling effect that would otherwise throw a potentially insurmountable roadblock.

    On the other hand, it seems to me that a lot of veiling effects would be ruined by being able to immediately discern that something is amiss, even if they party attempting to perceive fails the Clash. Moreover, it would seem that the narrower the parameters of the detection effect, the more likely you are to get the truth of something regardless of the outcome of the Clash.

    I'm tempted to rule that the party attempting to perceive does not get to know their spell is clashing if the effect in question is from the practice of veiling and their attempt fails. Thoughts?

              I'm cautious about this rule for the same reason you are, Volition, but something worth remembering is that awareness of your spell clashing isn't always sufficient to tell you what you want to know. For example, it doesn't tell you what your spell is clashing with or why, and a failure on a clash means you don't gain access to that information.

              Likewise, this rule only applies to spells, which means Mage Sight doesn't get this benefit of being auto-aware of a Clash.

              In other words, a Veiling spell to make you invisible and an Unveiling spell to detect invisible things will Clash, but if the Unveiling spell loses, all they know is that there's someone invisible nearby, not where they are. Likewise, an Obrimos activating their Forces Sight at the Active level gets an auto-Clash but don't learn anything on a failure.

              So while a Moros casting Forensic Gaze and failing a Clash may instantly know something is amiss, they don't know what that something is. They just know this corpse is touched by spellery. They might suspect a casting of Corpse Mask, but what if it's Suppress Life and this is merely a sleeping person? Or even simply a vampire having a snooze with a spell cast on them by a friend to keep anyone from thinking them anything but a properly dead person?

              All this rule does is tell the Mage that there's something amiss. From there, it's up to Focused Mage Sight - which will almost certainly win in a Clash for a modestly experienced Mage - if they think it's worth burning willpower to find out what's beneath.

              In the end, it feels like a good rule to use, to my mind.

                                      I've asked about the spirit Clash of Wills dicepool before, I'm surprised it isn't mentioned in the book actually. I treat it as Rank+Influence (only if applicable), remember that ephemeral entities can spend Willpower, and just assume that moderately experienced mages usually winning a Clash against ephemerals is a feature not a bug. The higher pool for Annihilate Spirit might be because it's a straight-up save or die effect, I think it's generally too high unless you want ephemerals to be incredibly potent (which you might).

                                      In the interest of moving things along in a real gameplay situation, I would take a page from Withstand ratings and say that multiple Clash effects means you take the highest applicable dicepool and add +1 for each additional one, and just have that count for all of them. Or if you wanted to get fancy, maybe take the highest applicable dicepool and give it 9-again if there are 2 effects, and 8-again if there are 3.
