    CtL2E criticisms from my group

    08-06-2016, 11:08 AM
    First of all, I'm in love with the Chronicles/2E rules for the most part. Mortal CoD, Werewolf 2E, and the brand-new Promethean 2E are all freaking amazing. That said, switching over to the partially-revealed 2E CtL rules really hurt my 18-month chronicle. I've been meaning to write up my group's criticisms since the game ended in June.

    Things both my players and I disliked about the new rules:
    • The ability for changelings to escape any situation is questionable. One major plot in our game surrounded a civil war in the freehold, and the inability to question or provide due-process to defeated enemies limited the game significantly. There's suddenly no point in inter-Court conflict, as the only options for enforcement are now genocide and shutting the hell up. (I know you couldn't imprison changelings before, but you could at least hold them long enough for a quick trial.) There's no way to provide fae justice, whether in your chronicle you conceive that as an honorable pursuit or a mere mockery of law and order.
    • The changes to Pledges reduce the meta-drive to roleplay interactions with humans. Whereas in 1E my players would actively try to learn more about NPCs so that they might form Pledges with them, in 2E they rarely cared to roleplay interactions with mortals.
    Things I disliked as Storyteller:
    • I personally dislike the shift in theme towards "Wyrd as legend made manifest" and away from "Wyrd as the power of promises." I'm not a fan of fairies in general, but CtL 1E really captured me with the idea of a bunch of paranoid Rumpelstiltskins trading promises more valuable than all the gold in the world. The authors moving away from that idea tells me that I'm not the target demographic of 2E. Which is fine and all, but I obviously don't like it
    • Dual Kith is way too good NOT to take from a meta perspective, yet it dilutes the "feel" of a character. A Brewer/Woodwalker Elemental--a goblinfruit alchemy specialist--doesn't have the same flair as either a Brewer Elemental or a Woodwalker Elemental: it gets a little muddled when you combine the kiths. I don't mind OP abilities, but I want OP abilities to ADD to the game's panache, not dilute it. (Good examples of OP merits include "Professional Training" and WtF2E's "Embodiment of the Firstborn". They enhance a character concept rather than muddle it.)
    Things we actually liked a lot:
    • Huntsmen. Very cool. We couldn't wait to find out what Arcadian Weapons were, so we made up a system rather like Storied Weapons from one of the Armory books, but included that they did strange damage in order to "preserve" a Changeling. For example, they might reap Clarity instead of Health or Glamour. I introduced the Nemean Lion as a huntsman, complete with his pelt as his Arcadian Weapon.
    • The new Contracts. They're both more powerful and Experiences-efficient, although minor balance tweaks should be made.
    • The new Clarity system, including Seeming blessings and curses. We loved that it could fluctuate wildly, and I found that I had more players volunteering breaking points in exchange for beats and the Acuity condition. (The "volunteer your own breaking points" method is my personal preference as ST and as a player, and GMC/2E has really improved our group's roleplay thanks to its breaking-points-for-experience mechanic.)
    • The suggestion that unique Courts be designed for each chronicle. While I don't love everything about the new Court system, I do like being told to make s*** up instead of follow exact formulae.
    In summary: for me, the worst part was the shift in themes. Pledges (and by extension, the metaphysical nature of Contracts) taking the backseat really killed the fun for me as ST, and I burned out very quickly. For my players, the inability to interrogate or try any offending changeling ruined any subplots involving changeling nemeses, and since there aren't any "equal footing" fae nemeses, that was a majority of the conflict. Did we still have fun doing random fairy stuff, like throwing parties for Sin-Eaters and therapeutically helping people rediscover their faith? Sure, of course. But the driving soul of the game felt lost after the change to 2E.

      I think one thing worth keeping in mind is that only the smallest fraction of the book's fluff is available via the spoilers, so your misgivings may be based on misunderstandings or content which was overhauled between the drafts we saw and the final product, so I wouldn't write off the writing of 2e as a thematic shift away from what you like just yet. It's very possible the wording and language of it will be clarified before the final product drops and you'll find it more to your taste.

Little stuff like that might help make it a more attractive option I your game.

          To be clear, my problem with Dual Kith isn't its "OPness", but rather the fact that it's so attractive from a meta perspective while simultaneously being unattractive from a world-building perspective.

          I embrace OPness. If I didn't, there's no way I'd love WtF2E, haha. I just think something that EVERY PLAYER WILL PICK should have some soul to it; as it stands, we'll have Sandwalker/Manikin combos that make no sense (I use these two randomly, of course) just to get both mechanical bonuses. Unlike additional Tells for Wolf-Blooded, combining two Kiths is often just weird from a thematic perspective. All Tells are "werewolf-like", but most Kiths are very unique and, to an extent, mutually exclusive.
          --------------------
          With regards to the "don't judge till it's done" argument, you're right, of course. I feel like the entire version is taking a thematic shift because: 1) Pledges (oaths) are now easy to escape in most cases, 2) Goblin Vows replaced Pledges from a mechanical standpoint, and they're greatly diminished in scope, and 3) the Courts and Huntsmen, two HUGE components of the universe, are both highly entrenched in the "Story" rather than true names, pledges, and fae law.

          Edit to clarify: The Courts are now of more ritual than political/"legal" importance, which is meh to me.

                      In my experience, fae are as varied as you can imagine, and probably beyond (but since this is a tabletop game let's just settle with what we can imagine). I adhere to the idea that any two kiths could be combined into any Dual Kith you can think of - your noted one here, Sandwalker/Manikin, could have been a statue made of scarabs, able to recombine and be reshaped at their maker's will, or perhaps they were a snowman, or, to be more literal, they could have been a sand soldier who stood guard and one day decided to venture to a new post. While I agree there's not a lot of reason for players not to take Dual Kith mechanically, and that the aforementioned Changeling concepts could just as easily be one kith if not the other, it is ultimately up to the player to take it or not and to be creative with the resulting Changeling.

                      Even if you were to go with something like, say, Nix/Ifrit, where one manipulates water and another fire, or even a Nix/Fireheart where they control water yet can spark fire with their fingertips, there's a fairly easy way to go about that: make them into a being who boiled water. They could have been a human bunsen burner, a tea kettle, a bath tender or even have had the role of making geisers work in their gentry's realm. They'd need to generate heat to do so, so naturally above water they'd cause fire with such heat, and they'd need to manipulate the water to make sure it all stayed in the same place so it could be boiled.

                      In my eyes, no kith is mutually exclusive. Yes, the pairings can be odd, but they have an amount of sense to them from the context of the world they escaped. If you can't make sense of them, modify them to be more thematically linked as the notes suggest. If you have a Snowskin/Ifrit but you want them to control snow instead of fire so they'd be a proper snow queen, change the Pyrokinesis effect to Cryokinesis. If you have a space themed Changeling, Airtouched could be renamed Gravity Born or somesuch.

                      Of course, if you don't like it, well, it's not my place to tell you that you should. I personally love the freedom this gives, but I can appreciate that not everyone will.

                                                          I think this is overly focused on jailing changelings. In my mind, a freehold's justice system would be a lot more informal and rely more on social pressures than locks and chains. Unless your suspect is a wandering murderhobo, the way you keep them in town is to tell them the whole freehold is watching them. A sort of house arrest would work, too. "Swear to me that you won't leave your apartment before all this is cleared up, or there will be trouble." The sealing or oath would double as an alarm if they try and run. This all feels a thousand times more likely than maiming them or holding them at gunpoint for days. Some of you are suggesting, like, super-unnecessary Bond villain contraptions.


                                                          As to the subject of imprisonment as punishment, I expect that to also be very, very rare. Changelings would be more likely to exile someone from the community or put them under a punishing oath, I think.

                                                          For your freehold civil war, it may help to look at Toronto writeup. A traitorous court tried to stage a coup. The perpetrators are not put on trial. The leaders are executed and the rest are told "Swear loyalty to one of the other Courts or die." That's what your civil warring Courts would likely be doing. "Swear loyalty to us (or at least to stop fighting) or be executed/exiled/hit with a very large stick."
