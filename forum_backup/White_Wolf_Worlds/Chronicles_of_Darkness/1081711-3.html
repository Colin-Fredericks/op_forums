    Originally posted by Jeremysbrain View Post
    No. With Corridors of Time your present body stays where it is as you inhabit the body of your past self, your physical body does not travel with you into the past.
    I really don't care whether we're talking about Corridors of Time or Shifting Sands, because the only things the book expressly has to say on the matter are that the present doesn't move forward from your perspective while you're in the past (i.e. that you can catch up to the present rather than having to wait out the spell's Duration) and that you're insulated from the potential complications of deliberately or accidentally undoing major parts of your life up to and including your birth (i.e. temporal paradoxes are no issue).

    Edit: To clarify, your present self never leaves the location of where the spell is cast.
    Again, this turns the regular casting of time-travel spells into limited teleportation despite the Space requirement for projecting things backwards or forwards to somewhere other than their corresponding location.

    Edit2: You are right if a spell ends because you caught up with the present. If the spell runs out while you are still in the past then your present self would still be where you cast the spell.
    If I travel back in time three turns for a duration of two turns and run away from my present position at a maximum speed of ten yards per turn, I do not blink back to my starting position on the last turn at twice the speed I could move unassisted.

    If I travel two scenes/hours back in time with a spell that lasts for one scene/hour before I spend one scene/hour trekking down into a secret temple at the bottom of the Grand Canyon, I do not shortcut back to the top of the Canyon just because my spell ran out, nor does that allow me to circumvent the cursed road that makes going up take twice as long as going down without using other magic.

    If I travel back a year and a day with a spell that lasts a year and walk from one end of the continental United States to the other, I do not loop back to Maine three days from Vegas just because I didn't technically set foot in the present in that span of time, nor can I use regular spell control to dynamically downshift the Duration to get me back to the comfort of the starting point if/when I get ambushed by something that nearly kills me.

    The narrative space set up by time travel magic in other media encourages subtle or easily-recontextualized activity in the course of altering history, and treating the temporal return course as a spatial snapback turns time travel into an unassailable method of accomplishing a wide variety of tasks "from" the relative safety of your Sanctum. That's silly.

                    If you travel across the country from A to B during the Duration of Corridors of time, you have rewritten history so that you travelled across the country from A to B during that time. Depending on how you would have responded to being in B and what other circumstances were in play in the intervening time between the end of the spell's Duration and the present, it could well be that you managed to get back to wherever you were when you cast the spell, but it's similarly likely that you'll return to your present consciousness somewhere else entirely, possibly short several important resources.

                    Drastic time travel is regularly a headache to deal with. There's a reason the folks who get the most facility with it also have a natural talent for manipulating luck and chance.

                                So here is a scenario. I'm a mage in LA and I want to travel back a week in time with a Corridors of Time spell I set the duration at an hour. A week ago I was in New York.

                                My scenario: I cast the spell and take over the body of my past self which was in New York a week ago. I have an hour to do whatever I want, in New York. After that hour is up the spell ends and I return to the present, which is in LA. There is no teleportation involved in this scenario. I end up right back where I started.

                                Your Scenario (if I am understanding you correctly, which assumes you stay where you are when the spell ends): I cast the spell and take over the body of my past self which was in New York a week ago. I have an hour to do whatever I want, in New York. After that hour is up the spell ends and I return to the present, but since I stay where I am when the spell ends I am now in New York in the present. In your scenario I just teleported from LA to New York using a Time spell.

                                A third scenario: You don't actually travel back along your timeline you stay put where you are in the present. I cast the spell and take over the body of my past self which was in New York a week ago, but I stay in LA. Because the spell explicitly says you inhabit your past body, that means I just teleported my past self to LA.

                                So, which of these scenarios is the correct one when using Corridors of Time?
