                                    Originally posted by nalak42 View Post
                                    Beasts are not meant to represent the outcast or the underdog or that sort of thing, so this isn't a case of "It's wrong to persecute this person just because they're different. Even if he's a psycho who likes eat people's hearts out of their chests while the person is still using it."
                                    To grab a couple of quotes...

                                    Talking about Beasts in general:
                                    Beasts are born, not made, and reared in a world that instinctively fears them. It does not matter if she is rich or poor, hideous or lovely, gay or straight or somewhere in between. People look askance at what they know is not human even though, objectively speaking, they have no reason to fear. Not at first.

                                    Even as children, Beasts feel like outsiders. A Makara dreams of the deep ocean where she sleeps in solitude, far from the demands of her needling peers. An Ugallu’s eyes stray to a sky forever out of reach. At night, an Eshmaki runs on all fours through a darkened forest, only to wake up once again in a stifling human world.

                                    Even a Beast’s body betrays her. She knows this lump of flesh is not her. She is a prisoner of her own skin and blood.
                                    Talking about storytelling for Beasts:
                                    Instead, look to stories about outsiders, about disenfranchised people, and about communities that exist on the outskirts of the mainstream.
                                    Talking about Beasts' role in relation to the Anima Mundi:
                                    For millennia, lepers were reviled as harbingers of plague and corruption, both moral as well as physical. Homosexuals became the lepers of the AIDS generation and were victimized by the same moralist hysteria. Immigrants, Muslims, transgender, and impoverished people have all been vilified in the modern era, turned into scapegoats by bigoted “crusaders” with narrow minds and selfish agendas. The next pandemic or culture shift will produce its own outcasts and they, too, will be blamed for their plight.
                                    Talking about Heroes:
                                    Secondly, the Beast needs a Hero. In the usual story, a Hero believes himself the central character. The Beast is a simple creature, with easily discernable goals and desires. It is only there for the Hero to kill. Obviously, the tale is about the “good guy,” the upstanding, handsome, proper protagonist. The creature, as the outcast, the pariah, serves only to validate the Hero’s existence.

                                    Originally posted by Elfive View Post
                                    You know what I really find most questionable about this "subtext" people keep banging on about? Its existence.

                                    You are not a persecuted minority. You are a fucking dragon.
                                    I have to say, I would like Beast way more if it didn't try to gussy it up with bad comic antihero justifications about how Beasts are totally justified in what they do and how Heroes just deserve what they get for daring to go after one.

                                    I'd also like it a lot more if it wasn't so focused on hurting people, specifically, and instead used more general god-monster themes - hoarding, territorialism and control of property, fame and rumor, etc - with actual harm to people being a side effect. This way you can get some genuine moral ambivalence in there, in the "player characters are always tempted to slip a little further down the slope in search of power", instead of "good" Beasts just being petty assholes.
