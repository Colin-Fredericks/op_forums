        So, finally got off call and could read the new intro...

        I am generally sticking with my initial interpretation for if I run a game of Beast, since it still fits the general theme: Beasts reflect the Mythic Arcs recast into the Modern Day, where the legends have been lost to humanity and the Beasts act as a Legend trying to be "remembered"..

        My take (both pre and post revision) is: Each Beast reflects a story given form, a mythic tale being spun around it, with it as the source of the confirm that drives the tale as well as providing the thematic setting via the Lair. Those who become beasts were someone with a tie to the Primordial Dream, and through an event come to accept their role in the story and become the means of perpetuating the Myth in the World. Heroes are those with a sensitivity for the Dream but inability to process it, whose weakened identity results in them being shaped by the Myth into the role of the Heroes.

        The only differences are that Beasts now accept their role as the central element more fully, instead of being forced into it. I think the focus on them as teachers from a school of hard knocks isn't to hard to switch to "Myths manifest that are trying to be remembered": both have the goal of influencing nightmares, to make mortals aware of the forgotten. It is just one focuses on reminding humanity what it once feared and the lessons tied to those fears, while the Myth is more open (though the purpose of myths is to teach or explain things, the story is equally important). The new Devouring works as an acceptance as a part of the story, choosing to become the role you were cast for and joining the wider family of myths.

        The Hunger, Satiety, and its impact on the world is playing the role needed for any story: conflict. Withough conflict, there is no plot, and thus the beasts Myth cannot be remembered, as there is no Myth. The Beast can choose how it introduces this conflict, such as becoming the masked vigilante who strikes fear into criminals while being hated by that one detective who knows said vigilante is worse than any criminal, or playing the role of the Urban monster who stalks children unseen except on film and causes fear beyond just in those children he takes via the social media. Regardless of the how, the Beast wants his Myth remembered.

        Beasts are driven to one day join the likes of the Hydra or Grendel, becoming well known in the conscious collective, their Myths are cornerstone of human culture, or at the very least leave a mark among the humans they live among. This is my idea for their greater purpose: construction of a Myth. Beyond their Hunger and Archetype, I could see elements of their society derived from the type of mythic arc they seek, from seeking remembered certain through benevolence (or anti-heroism) to infamy for their malevolence, to making a land forever marked by their presence (but their role forgotten) or an urban legend that knows recognizes no borders. Some of those groups seek to weave other supernaturals into their stories, adding different themes into their Myth, while others choose to remain among the monsters.

        As an Aside, the idea of other Beasts facilitating a metamorphosis doesn't bother me too much, versus the original Homecoming. Sine the only connection before was the Primordial Dream, I view that the focus has just shifted on who the family is. The Horrors are all family, and share a kinship within the Primordial Dream. With the unity with the Horror, the Beast is essentially made a part of this network of conceptual terrors given form. Instead of a random human somehow born with a monster inside (remember, monsters didn't follow bloodlines), the focus is on an ancient horror leaving it's place amongst the purely conceptual to manifest in the world among it's kin. The fact that all humans have the ability but only a few forge the needed connection and then a mentor in many cases to transition seems almost a cross between mage and vampire, but I am okay with that.

        As an aside aside... I can't shake the feeling I have seen elements of this all in Mage. Awakening in general, the idea of Astral Journey's leading to a dramatic change due to acceptance of a lesson and modification of the soul? And that Banishers parallel Heroes: those who experienced the trip but failed to accept it, who now seek those who did and destroy these reminders of the Truths they couldn't accept. And the idea of alternations of the soul from Astral trips or connections to gain new powers isn't new; the Morphean Continuity, Tremere (Revised) and Unforgotten Scion legacies, to name a few. Now, not saying these parallels are bad or discredit either one. Just an observation I found interesting, especially with the new intro.

                                                    And mage... don't forget mage. Both we and 2e are predicated on joining Mystery Cults. 2e doesn't even hide that and expressly calls them out as such. The entire thing reads as a crossover of Plato and Gnostic theory, with a smattering of Kaballah, Alchemy and Hermeticism.

                                                    It doesn't make it bad, and I don't know why it bothers people to point it out at times (maybe due to the negative connotations associated with cults). It makes sense that Supernaturals will have their own cultures. And due to their exclusive and isolated natures, combined with the nature of their abilities along with questions of origins and history, these organizations are going to take on relgious/cult-like manifestations.

                                                    That said, it also makes sense for them not to have such organizations. Cults, religions and networks imply that you both have the numbers and enough unity of ideas to support an organization, amd not every splat does. Promethean offers a good example, as while the transmutation and origins can define them, their quest is too personal for more then generalities of organized beliefs. Depending on how Beast goes with their social structures, such as a low population, and I could get lower level of social complexity.

                                                    As for Family versus Cult for the Beasts creation, it depends on which side you look at it: Human vs Horror. For Humans, it definitely is cultish, as it is having unusual experiences that results in an existing member facilitating your entrance. But from the Horror side, the unity of the Primordial Dream and their origin as children of the Dark Mother makes me think family: they trace a common lineage and origin, and as part of the realm of concepts, their physical appearance is less important than what they represent.
