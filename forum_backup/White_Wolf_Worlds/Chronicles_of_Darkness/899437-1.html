                    Isn't the Sage concept of the Silver Ladder basically an Übermensch? One chapter of their Order book even starts with a Nietzsche's quote about man being a bridge between an animal and the Overman.

                    Mages have Virtues and Vices like typical humans. Wisdom is about knowing more things than the average human and acting accordingly, not an inherently alien way of thinking. Unlike vampires with their Frenzy, or the Blood Rage of werewolves, both of which are largely independent on their bearers' respective wills, mages don't have alien desires, and they have to consciously decide to and work on maintaining their wisdom and perspective, often despite their inner all too human passions (when you remove your vice with the Amorality spell, you can't even cause acts of Hubris). Mages might be monsters, but they are certainly human.

                    Their inner drive to know more has literally created their first Obsession, having obsessions is simply who they are and who they were as a mortal, they wouldn't have Awakened otherwise. And while many of them fall, the ideal of being a mage is to *become* an idea so powerful that it remakes them into a symbol of certain values, values they have because of what they are, not because of what they were told to be. To be given orders, and as a result of obeying them eventually Ascending is what the Seers are about. The Pentacle as a whole, especially Silver Ladder, is practically the Übermensch personified, individual cases notwithstanding.
