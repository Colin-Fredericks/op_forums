        Originally posted by nofather View Post
        I have to admit to being incredibly jealous of people playing 2e games already.
        To be honest, it's nice to play Werewolf again in a game where we aren't constantly having to check everything we do, running into errors and broken bits of the system, and can just play again. Playtesting really is work; now I get to actually have fun with all the stuff I helped write.

        There will be various bits of stuff that I'll have to wait til the corebook release before I can go into them here; once Werewolf 2e's out, for example, I'll likely do write-ups for the High Cromlech and the Data Angel.

        Currently, my next challenge is adding detail to the two Protectorates. One is based around the Zero Point at the city's heart, a coalition of packs trying to address the problem of the massive spiritual surge they can feel building up in Paris and seething across the Gauntlet. The Zero Point itself is the focus of this, and through extensive rituals, experiments and desperate methods the Zero Point Protectorate is bleeding off power and stopping what they expect will be the equivalent of a Shadow eruption. This Protectorate has strong links to lots of other Forsaken across France, through Tribal and Lodge networks, because the symbolism of Paris as the capital of France has an impact across the country; no French pack wants Paris to go horribly, spiritually wrong or even just physically explode. The second Protectorate, I think, will be the personal grudge match of two Forsaken elders who hate each other but are working together against the Pure, gathering the non-Zero Point packs (both Forsaken and Ghost Wolf) under their control through force of personality and strength of arms; while Zero Point hurl vast resources into stopping the eruption of whatever it is (it ultimately being, of course, the waking murmurs of the High Cromlech), the second Protectorate does a kinda taking-in-the-huddled-masses thing, gathering power and influence with the disaffected in order to war with the Pure and to erode the Zero Point Protectorate's power and influence.

        Then we have the Lyceum, actual worshippers of the Greek god Apollo in his aspect as a wolf. They see themselves as philosopher-kings, aesthetes, gourmets, enlightened types. Everyone else thinks they're pagan nutjobs. The Lyceum are a sorta hedonistic, Epicurean cult of pleasure and the hunt, believing themselves chosen to guard the treasures of a holy place of Apollo. They're Ghost Wolves technically, with the Lyceum being the Lodge that ties them together, but they are probably originally some sort of weird blend of a Hunter in Darkness sect with the influence of something else, possibly a Pure sect of some kind. They're no-where near as big as either Protectorate, but they have some serious and weird rite-magic at their fingertips and the aid of some very weird entities.

        I'll probably run with some other werewolf heretics as well, in the form of a ghoulish underground cult haunting the catacombs and sewers under Paris. They're basically just going to be one big pack of Ghost Wolves, all tied together into one weird Lodge that may well originally have been Bone Shadow. I'm tempted to have them as worshippers or attendants to some sort of shoggothian Lou Carcolh; I could just make them troglodytes, the equivalent of werewolf morlocks, but I want them to be less primitive and crude, more weird and devoted.

        Most of the other, 'regular' Ghost Wolves are getting hoovered up by the second Protectorate, but there's a good few small packs of them out in the banlieus still. These kinds of packs can literally have their own cult-like ideologies and believes that vary from one pack to the next, but the most common lore centres around Ys, the sunken city off France that was damned by Dahud and given to the Devil as a prize; the people of the city were cursed to become werewolves, so the Ghost Wolf cults claim. The Forsaken believe differently; they think Ys probably was real but may have been an actual community ruled by werewolves, attacked and destroyed by a powerful spirit that feared their power. I'll need to figure out the story of the place as, at some point, I think I might have Princess Dahud actually turn up.

        Finally on the 'non-Pure' roster, we have the Bale Hounds. The line between the Ghost Wolves and Bale Hounds is blurred; there are lots of Ghost Wolves that think they are damned by God and that the Devil is their master, and some of them have cults that grow up around them. The Bale Hounds are far more purposeful in how they foster cults and covens, though, actively gathering groups of humans and Wolf-Blooded to whom they can teach rites and occult secrets to further their plans. I used Pseulak and Baalphegor worshippers extensively before, so I'll probably go with Lamashtu this time, although I'm not yet sure how much importance the Bale Hounds will play in this game or whether anyone else will really make any differentiation between any of the 'mad werewolves gathering witch-cults' anyway.

        - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer

                                                    It's the spirit of an old bronze foundry that once stood in the banlieu. The foundry has long since collapsed into ruin and decay, and the spirit only survived due to a locus of Metal on the site. Once a glorious monstrosity of white-hot liquid metal, fire and ash, it is now a lurching horror of smouldering embers and rust.

                                                    The pack have taken it on as their totem because they want to see themselves as a renewing force in the banlieu that will help bring it back some of its former glory. I haven't yet settled on its personality etc or name, but I'm working on that.

                                                    It also comes with a really quite harsh ban - they can't destroy objects of crafted metal. This will definitely cause them some issues... but if they're successful at sticking to it, that might help rekindle the totem's old flame more swiftly.
