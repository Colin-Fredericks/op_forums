    Here, we can share our homebrewed Places-That-Aren’t, the area’s of the Shadow that correspond to no geographical locale!

    Mine:

    1)Granted, the game only lasted three sessions before dying, and the PCs never managed to get there, but I had the pack investigating a series of ritualistic murders in various old, rundown and abandoned churches by a cult loyal to a covey of murder-spirits. The spirits were using the same churches again and again and again, and, in the Shadow, these churches became one place, an upside down cathedral where spirits walk on the floors high above and hunt lesser spirits by using extremely alien equivalents of fishing poles. Where the altar would be was the man this covey was trying to Hive-Claim: the ‘god’ the cult believed would soon arise.

      2)The hospital’s cancer ward had a history of patients dying. It soon became a thing of urban legend, and eventually the ward caught fire. The hospital soon shut down...but the cancer ward kept going, on into infinity.

      If one enters the Hisil and goes in said hospital, they find it full of forlorn, bitter, angry spirits bewildered by their food-source going away. But if one enters the cancer ward from within the Shadow, one enters another place altogether.

      The cancer ward is a Place-That-Isn’t, an endless series of hallways with flickering fluorescent lights, low levels of baking radiation, and screams coming at the most inopportune moments from behind doors that can’t be opened by mundane strength, alone. Walking lumps of tumor, pus and human hair can be found nesting in hallways, and insects swarm in ventilation-ducts.

      If one could find the cancer ward’s center (which would require supernatural methods) one would find oneself in the ward’s head doctor’s office. Here squats a rail-thin spirit, flesh melted off by radioactivity, fingers lost yet still trying to write reports. It’s glad you’re here. Just in time for treatment...

      3)Gobekli Tepe (a very real place, by the way) hosts a Place-That-Isn’t of mammoth proportions. It is also a wildly powerful locus, with Uratha words of binding everywhere, albeit supernaturally hidden from human eyesight.

      The oldest known temple in the world, since its rediscovery Gobekli Tepe has become of deep interest to virtually every splat, as the site holds Mysteries to almost all of them. It is the Uratha-specific mysteries we explore here, today.

      Sidestepping in Gobekli Tepe immediately shunts a werewolf into the Place-That-Isn’t a primeval forest with trees so tall no matter how hard one tries one can never breach the canopy. Ancient spirits of extinct megafauna roam here, their hides cracked and sickly green light shining out from within. Their eyes are milky cataracts and they do not speak, even in the First Tongue.

      The forest is of immense size, though only the outermost edges are accessible to any but the mightiest Or cleverest of packs. Guarding the forest’s primordial interior are giant stone sentinels, spirits bound by strictures both Infernal and divine to prevent any from going further in.

      What lies within? No one knows for sure, but, under the light of the full moon, strange, hulking shapes can be barely discerned in the distance, performing what appears to be a strange dance, with piping flutes and beating drums breaking the forest’s normal aura of silence.

      4)Even Wounds can have Places-That-Aren’t, terrifying holes in reality where the very worst aspects of reality fester and boil. The Deeremonte Estate is just such a place.

      Home to the Deeremonte family, known for generations as inbred cannibals, this house has been abandoned since WWI. Horrible things happened to travels in there, atrocities of staggering inhumanity. And these horrifying things kept happening for generations, til, at WWI, the local police got up the gumption to get rid of the family.

      The Deeremonte Estate is a creepy place, to be sure, an abandoned, crumbling edifice. But, if someone enters it from the Shadow Realm, they might never leave.

      The Place-That-Isn’t inside the Estate is the house at its gory height, with a never-ending psychodrama of the site’s most awful events on perpetual replay. Wounded spirits enact the roles of the Deeremonte family, while a Maeltinet takes on the role as “Papa”.

      At the very bottommost part of the Place-That-Isn’t, corresponding roughly with the Estate’s basement, is a crack in the Earth, going...somewhere. The Maeltinet comes here, sometimes, to listen alien whispers on it can hear.

              For the curious, the above-mentioned Place-That-Isn’t is:

              The Forge-City

              There are many Places-That-Aren't, some little more than closets that don't exist in the real world, or are small spaces that are a bit larger on the inside than out. Others are quite large, one being an entire floor that's not there, or even entire realms of their own within the Shadow itself. The Forge City is one such realm, one of fire and smoke and steel. Connected to the administrative office of an abandoned automotive factory in the Shadow, opening the door to the office leads to a vast and incredible realm that defies mortal logic. The Forge City is a massive collection of foundries filled with white hot materials processing tirelessly, smokestacks billowing black smoke into the midnight black sky, pits of molten Essence dotting the landscape, factories filled with machinery endlessly operating nonstop, labyrinthine streets of basalt snake in between buildings, broken only by Essence Pits or vents that which erupt into pillars of burning energy. As stated before, this realm is vast, extending for miles around in all directions, though the very city limits seem to be a circular rock wall that extends vertically in the air eternally. Another feature of this realm is that the only light to be seen comes from the city itself and it's assorted structures, as the there is no day/night cycle in this realm, only the smoke filled obsidian sky.

              Needless to say, this Place-That-Isn't is quite antagonistic to newcomers that haven't quite adjusted to such a harsh environment. For Uratha it quickly comes apparent just how inhospitable this place is, the air is burning hot and choked with smoke, making breathing a challenge at best and seems to burn the very essence in their bodies, inflicting a point of lethal damage every hour as well as the loss of a point of Essence. Fortunately Essence is immensely plentiful in the Forge City, the trick is to be willing to endure high levels of pain in order to harvest it. Essentially the entire realm is one giant locus, it's bounty in the form of it's rivers of molten slag and geysers of intense energy, one merely needs to reach out and reap the rewards... at the cost of their flesh that is. for every lethal damage sustained from these sources the werewolf ( or non native spirit) gains one essence. Once the werewolf has remained in the City for 24 hours they will have adapted to the realm and find that the air becomes much more breathable and the temperature, while uncomfortable, is more bearable and no longer scorches the werewolf. They still take damage from harvesting Essence from the vents and pools, but they gain 2 Essence per point of lethal, same with non native spirits who have adapted.

              While the Forge City itself is ripe with an abundance of Essence, the Shadow locale it is connected to is not. the Abandoned factory is essentially a Barrens from so many years of inactivity, the flow of Essence long having dried up and dissipated. How the Forge City has managed to thrive is both as interesting as it is simple. during the late 20's all the way through the 60's that particular factory had gained quite a bit of fame as it manufactured everything from automobile parts to weapons and ammunition to fighter jet components to even lesser items such as baby bottles and children toys. For so many decades the factory had become one of the symbols of what the American people could engineer, and the emotional and conceptual energies tied to the factory bloomed, forming a locus tied to the resonance of industry and production inside the head executive's office within the administrative wing of the building, a locus that rapidly gained strength and grew to a font of Essence of legendary proportions.

              During the early days life was good for the spirits of the factory, and Essence was immensely plentiful, with more than enough to go around. Even without the pesky Uratha to police it, the factory was quite harmonious and there were few disruptions, the spirit of the factory itself made sure of that. Christening itself the Foreman the Factory-spirit, now a powerful Incarnae of Industry, oversaw that it's fellow spirits performed their natural tasks quickly and efficiently, for doing well within the Shadow ensured rich new Essence to flow from the world of Light to feed them all even more. During this time the Foreman also worked to create a place ideal for spirits such as itself and it's brood, a space where they could dwell and never worry about the meddlesome Uratha who would surely one day come calling, or the avarious spirits of greed and exploitation that began worming their way into it's Court. By the mid 60's it finally finished it's grand task of creating it's own realm within the Shadow, transforming the office containing the locus into it's own self sustained, self sufficient space, a realm of metal and fire and industrial fantasy.

              This event couldn't of happened a moment too soon as not long after the factory's shadow began to weaken. What had happened as that over the years the factory's importance began to lessen and the demand for it's products became less and less as other, more attractive forms of business began appearing. The Foreman saw this and desperately tried to aid the factory by creating more products with it's Influence, and for a time this did indeed help give the factory a great edge over it's competition, but in doing so it caught the attentions of a local pack of Iron Masters and for the first time in over four decades the denizens of the factory had to defend themselves against the accursed children of Father Wolf. While the spirits and the Foreman fought fiercely, the Uratha were no careless spirits starving for Essence, and they were able to push the spirits to the brink of eradication within a few short days. Terrified of what was happening and enraged at the Uratha who dared to interfere with it's agenda, the Foreman quickly saw no other choice for survival and ushered the surviving members of it's Court into the infant Place-That-Wasn't. After the last spirit fled inside, the Incarnae too retreated within and sealed the entrance behind it, placing itself and it's court out of the werewolve's reach.

              Since then the factory has long fallen into disrepair. As the Foreman couldn't aid it's dying factory with it's Influence it quickly went bankrupt and was then shut down. Soon after the Shadow of and around the factory whither and slowly began to die, becoming a moderately large Barren. As for the Forge City itself it has been thriving vastly since it's isolation, becoming a sort of spiritual Galapagos as it's spirits have evolved largely without human influence (save for a few rare occasions) , and in fact actually has been growing as it's pocket space seems to be expanding. the reason for this continued prosperity is due to the City partially being kept alive due to the occasional influx of new spirits that are drawn to the factory when the door to the Forge City is opened, acting as a Siren's call. These new spirits help refresh the pools of Essence within the the City and helps inform it's "citizens" of the changes that have happened in the outside world, and eventually these newcomers are added to the fold and gamut of the Forge City's population, furthering the growth of this strange "paradise".

                  6)Not every Place-That-Isn’t has meaning. Some were abandoned, while others formed for seemingly no reason at all. The Detroit Temple is one such Place-That-Isn’t.

                  Perpetually on the northern outskirts of Detroit’s Hisil, reaching the Detroit Temple is a somewhat difficult matter, as one must be in exactly the right frame of mind and be pondering automobiles and the automotive industry while in that frame of mind. And that frame of mind is Apathy. To reach the Detroit Temple, one must not care if one reaches it. Otherwise, it’s forever out of reach.

                  The Detroit Temple looks like a cross between a car museum and a Catholic cathedral, adorned on the outside with automotive symbolism, of all kinds. The cathedral’s doors stand wide open, and inside is....nothing. Just an empty space. Spirits refuse to go in willingly, but can’t explain why. Uratha have explored the Detroit Temple a great many times, and find no use for it.

                  However, if a mage used Mage Sight within the Detroit Temple such a mage would swiftly develop a very different opinion.

                  7)Mars. The red planet. If it has a Shadow of it’s own, no one knows. But there is a Place-That-Isn’t reflective of Mars, and it can only be found by aimlessly wandering NASA HQ after sundown.

                  This Place-That-Isn’t is a strange, dead landscape, embodying scientific fact, rather than pulp or science fiction. No John Carter, here. Furthermore, it’s not even on Mars, being within Earth’s Shadow.

                  Dust elemental spirits rule the skies, and the two moons glare down like dead, unseeing eyes. Great earth elementals slowly creep across the land. And, every so often, an undead being glowing with power appears but briefly, and Uratha unlucky enough to witness these beings often go mad with induced visions, especially visions of Martian planetary angels.

                  8)The tribal totems inhabit hidden realms in the Shadow Realm, and Rabid Wolf is no exception.

                  A terrifying eternal forest fire sends up vast clouds of soot that shut out the sky, leaving only fire and the light of painful doom to guide your way. Almost like a labyrinth, the fires seem to corral you ever deeper, drawing one to the apocalyptic center of this incendiary madness.

                  Looming shapes, so burnt they can no longer be recognized, stumble through the cacophony, while stags coated in lesions urge interlopers ever forward in mangled human voices.

                  At the very center squats a mound of bodies piled high, the bodies both gangrenous and aflame, the stench enough to floor even the hardiest werewolf. Atop this burning pile is the sizzling meat-puppet, a parody of a wolf carcass, animated by Rabid Wolf’s indomitable will. It leers blindly at you, this perpetually dying thing and it shrieks your sins with demented glee.

                  Chained all around it are werewolves who thought to spurn Rabid Wolf’s truth, forever burning. They scream litanies of belief, their wills long ago broken.

                  9)The Cave is THE cave, a Shadow representation of caves everywhere, and the notions that monsters lurk below, scared away by the sun’s harsh rays and the cleansing kiss of moonlit nights.

                  One must be in a cavern system of considerable size, though one system is more desirable than another. One will eventually find a crack in the cavern walls, just big enough to allow a single person through. One crawls into the fissure and spends a cramped half hour of minimal movement, before popping through into the Cave.

                  The Cave is a vast maze of tubes, chambers and waterways, that twists in on itself into a physically impossible manner.

                  Here, in the dark, lurk fossil spirits of things dead for millions of years, a whole society of mute, forgotten, frozen spirits. And ruling above them all is a fragment of Bear, the bear-spirit, torn away in a fight with Father Wolf and crawling into this Cave to heal. It is angry and hungry and eager to escape, but it cannot. Bear herself would simply eat it or kill it: no returned apotheosis awaits this vengeful, bitter godling.

                          11)The House of Fear is located...nowhere. At least, nowhere in cosmology as werewolves understand it. It exists outside of the mortal world and the Shadow Realm, in a nebulous place Uratha can’t easily quantify. Spirits cannot enter it, and know nothing of it. Physically finding it is impossible, as no amount of searching will uncover it.

                          But it can be reached through a unique rite known only to very few packs. It shifts into what is somehow a ‘different’ Twilight, and following the wooded trail beyond to a gigantic, festering human corpse with an oak door carved into its stomach. Enter that door, and you enter the House of Fear.

                          It a labyrinth, not a house, but the name persists. Spirits unlike anything werewolves are familiar with lurk here: minotaurs with cleaving axes, echoes of the future of the very packmates themselves, only antagonistic, even men made of curiously dull stone claiming to be of the fabled City of Atlantis that try to bar your progress.

                          Solving the maze is hellishly difficult, but possible. At the far end is an archway, beyond that archway light so blinding and so alien to the Uratha they must either avert their gazes or go blind. Winged angels with bows of fire and swords of ice prevent further passage.

                              Have you ever heard of Null island? Funny real life story: the word 'null' is sometimes used to mean 'no information' rather than 'zero'. Sometimes machines don't understand that or how to accept that information is missing. This means that something located at 'null' is taken to mean the coordinates (0,0). This creates the illusion that things people, places, and things of all variety can be found in some obscure spot in the ocean.

                              When I learned of that I thought back to werewolf: the forsaken. These are pictures of places that exist, just made to seem localized due to machine error. If perception can twist the Hisil out of proportion with the world, then what we have is a nexus of extradimensional pathways all across the world. If a Wound were to open there or a spirit noble open a travel business there, the story implications would be terrible. A spot of such opportunity and vulnerability.

                              Of course, should a pack take that place then territory disputes are going to be interesting.

                                  12)The Place-That-Isn’t for Death Wolf can only be reached through the largest of human graveyards, where the Resonance of death is at its strongest, yet the way cannot open if an Avernian Gate is open, strangely enough.

                                  Her realm is a somber forest in winter. Not the apocalyptic snow of some other Firstborn, but a gentle, pervading silence. Owl-spirits Nest here in huge numbers, and will occasionally allow strange “cousins from Dis” to nest with them.

                                  Stone resembling ancient shamans are really immobile spirits working dark rites to keep all ghosts from entering. It is as if Death Wolf is very concerned something from the Underworld might invade.

                                  Caves here are places to meditate and pray, though the religion in question can be whatever it happens to be. (One cave, in particular, is strewn with icons of Eastern Orthodox saints.)

                                  In the center of the Place-That-Isn’t is a vast stone pillar hundreds of meters tall, upon the top of which rests a wolf of enormous proportions that, despite being alive, looks freshly dead. Death-spirits attend her, and murder-spirits protect her.

                                              15)In an industrial park in Russia there stands a great corroded stone doorframe, utterly unlike anything the Uratha have seen before. Here, it seems, despite not being a locus, one can walk through this doorway and enter a Place-That-Isn’t out some nightmare.

                                              It is a maze of building, factories, water purification systems, dim alleys and winding streets, as if designed to get you lost. Eventually, you reach a choice: leave the city for the heavily forested wilderness beyond, or go deeper into the city.

                                              Going out of the city leads to a maze of thickly forested trees, tangled undergrowth and rivers suffering from industrial pollution. One’s inner fury begins to build, and the pack may tear itself apart at any moment. They can keep going, after a time they enter a clearing with a vast stone wall separating the pack from whatever lay beyond. Climbing the wall is certainly possible, but...reaching the top might not be such a good idea. Those who cross over are never seen again.

                                              Going deeper into the city is another matter altogether, as you eventually reach a bizarre commercial center, filled with dwarves from folklore selling things that can’t be sold in the material realm. The prices? Memories. A droplet of one’s blood, a rhyme. Russian Uratha have purchased very strange fetishes here.

                                                      17)Sometimes, Uratha swimming out at sea find themselves pulled down by unseen currents, down and across the Gauntlet, then deeper still, to a crimson shore that is neither Shadow nor material. A tiny red sun, a dying ember, casts all under its crimson glare, bathing everything the color of blood. The beach these werewolves wash up on are eroded by sanguine waters, and, sometimes, worm-like things come up from the deep to feast on whatever they find ion the sore.

                                                      Going inland only makes things worse, as it reveals an alien landscape of red plants with purple ichor, vast skeletons of upright-sitting creatures of unknown taxonomy, and rivers that stink of human feces. Eventually, one will find a great gore-covered toadstool, and, if a werewolf breathes in its spores, he then can return to the material universe—-but carrying a deformed lamprey egg inside himself.

                                                      18)The Leaf Cathedral is found in the Shadow near the world’s oldest Joshua tree, a 5,000-year-old vestige of an earlier time, before writing, before civilization. By stepping into the Shadow, this Joshua tree is beyond huge, with a labyrinth of ladders and steps carved into its bark. Solve this puzzle, and entrance to the Leaf Cathedral opens.

                                                      A vast structure of odd angles, sharp turns and splinters waiting to strike, the Leaf Cathedral shifts due to the seasons.

                                                      In spring, tiny flower-spirits take over, and make beer and dance and frolic.

                                                      In summer, Helions descend and burn hot as the sun, but the Leaf Cathedral does not char. This infuriates the Helions, yet it has always been so.

                                                      In autumn, a single harvest-spirit, Old Farmhand at the Edge of Town, moves in, driving the Helions out. He sleeps throughout his occupancy.

                                                      And in winter, a door to inky oblivion opens, and, while nothing comes through, spirits abandon the Leaf Cathedral en mass. Whispers invite travelers to step inside. No one who obeys has ever seen again.

                                                          19)There is a reason, Egyptian werewolves say, to avoid the ancient temple-city ruins like Crocodopolis. These places are inhabited in their Shadow opposite by sand-fleshed jackal-bodied spirits of Garm, bloody taloned sphinx priestesses of Ma’at and animate hieroglyphs that screech mad meaning into the ears of passers by.

                                                          But Crocodolis is the worst of them all, for something great and evil lurks here and considers wolfflesh a great delicacy. In the Shadow of Crocodopolis, crocodile-spirits of enormous size bedecked in gold and muddy saffron play subtle games of hunter and witchcraft with blind ibis-headed ‘mummies’, the winner devouring the loser. The statues of the gods are alive, sand pose riddles to passers by. Those who fail to answer properly get swept up....into a Place-That-Isn’t.

                                                          Serapis is an artificial god, created by the orders of the Greeks who conquered Egypt, yet Serapis has a blood existence, a spiritual chimera of different body parts all stitched together as one. In his temple, which cannot be escaped save by slaying the god, howling wind can be heard outside, with the mad echoing laughter of hyenas and the desperate cries for help from voices long silenced. Here, inside the Place-That-Isn’t lay half-devoured fetishes that obey no laws of their ilk, as Serapis slowly eats them to fuel his own alien Essence. Where do these fetishes come from? No one knows.

                                                          Serapis itself is a great god-beast, a spirit stitched together from mortal animal-parts and cast into Twilight. Egyptian werewolves have encountered such horrors before, but never on this scale.

                                                          Serapis angrily laments his disobedience to Anubis, and curses his fate for being locked away, here. Werewolves who find themselves in Serapis’ clutches must fight for their lives, as the bull-god command ushabti spirit warriors to attack intruders. Those who kill Serapis are cast by unseen hands back to the normal Shadow of Crocodopolis. Those who lose...are never seen from again.

                                                          20)Humans have uncovered a treasure, and both Forsaken and Pure viciously fight to control what mortalkind has unearthed.

                                                          In South America, archaeologists recently dug up a complex mandala of stone, broken skulls, teeth made of obsidian and spirals upon spirals. It is the physically largest fetish on Earth, weighing thousands of tons.

                                                          ...and there is a Place-That-Isn’t inside the thing. A place werewolves are eager to explore, for the fetish has somehow bound a Royal Avatar.

                                                          Should an Uratha pack subdue the fetish and gain access to its inner world, they find themselves in a shining white labyrinth that blindingly reflects the light, making it nearly impossible to see. Vast Helions shaped like humanoid llamas wander to and fro carrying massive obsidian mallets; those they encounter, the Helions smash to pulp.

                                                          The Royal Avatar, a goat-like humanoid of shining beauty and irradiating purity lies impaled at the heart of the labyrinth, bound by powerful rites. Free it, and the Place-That-Isn’t vanishes. The giant stone fetish that once contained the Royal Avatar can now freely be used.

                                                          21)In the foothills of America’s Appalachian Mountains, witches cavort with wolves, brewing strange concoctions made from moonshine, human fat and the blood of goats. Wolf-Blooded cultists and their Uratha kin worship at an ancient still from the 1920s, long since abandoned.

                                                          At first, they rebuilt the still in hopes in hopes of making easy cash. But they had dreams out in the pristine forest and amidst the kudzu. These visions led them down esoteric alchemical paths, til they learnt to make witches’ brew.

                                                          This brew allows Wolf-Blooded to sidestep into the Shadow at loci, and allows werewolves to sidestep into the Shadow without the need for a locus. A powerful boon, indeed!

                                                          But something lurks at the still, something intangible even by the standards of Twilight, and the witch-wolves soon found their way into a house somehow hidden within the kudzu, where spiders—-but not Azlu!—-made of dandelions and sawdust speak in eldritch riddles. The witch-wolves now bow low to these strangely fleshy things, in a Place-That-Isn’t.

                                                          And the brew demands a price of its own: a horrifying addiction that, given time, cause the Wolf-Blooded cultists to split open and birth more not-Azlu, while real Azlu find themselves strangely attracted to the cult’s werewolves.
