    I'm looking for a way for a character to be able to permanently change their appearance without the character being part of one of the major game lines. What I am not looking for:

    - Something that copies an existing person (ie New Face from Hunter: The Vigil) or alters just the face.
    - Something that is derived from one of the major game lines (Ghouls, Offspring, Oathtakers from Changeling etc).
    - Something that is possessed or in some other way has to struggle with something from their free will.
    - Something that is immortal as part of the package when being able to obtain this ability.

    Things that I am okay with:

    - A "minor" splat like a low magic user, psychic, witch (Witch Finders) and just about anything from the core books.
    - Supernatural merits.
    - Dread Powers or Numen.

    It doesn't matter if this allows for the moving of physical attributes or boosts them: I am looking for just the ability to permanently alter your appearance. CofD sources preferred, but if those don't exist all nWoD books are good. So the question is: does what I'm looking for exist somewhere?

                                        The text is correct. The summaries are just that, summaries, not necessarily comprehensive and complete.

                                        Originally posted by Ser Duncan Pennytree View Post
                                        Dodge (Page 88): Could someone explain to me (maybe using an example) how Dodge exactly works? Can I dodge multiple times per turn, as the description of Defense under Stage Three: Attack in the Combat Summary Chart could implicate ("each attack"), but which is not explicitly stated in the text? How exactly is the Dodge Pool modified by muliple attacks during the same turn against the "dodger"? Like "normal" Defense (-1 for each successive attack) or not at all? And what does the last paragraph exactly mean? How am I supposed to know in advance how many opponents are going to attack the "dodger" during the turn?
                                        Dodging is an instant action the character takes, which unlike other instant actions, can take effect before the character's Initiative. (So if Alice is faster than Bob's Initiative and attacks Bob, Bob may declare he is dodging this turn.) A single Dodge action applies for the full turn against all dodgable attacks that occur that turn. Multiple attacks in the same turn apply their normal penalty. This penalty is applied to the character's Defense before it is doubled to determine the number of dice rolled to dodge the attack, which is the meaning of the paragraph which confused you. Each attack is rolled in sequence of Initiative, so for example, after Alice attacks Bob and he dodges, if Lisa also attacks Bob that turn, Bob's dice pool to dodge Lisa's attack is (Defense - 1) doubled. If Frank then also attacks Bob the same turn, his dice pool to dodge Frank's attack is (Defense - 2) doubled.

                                        Originally posted by Ser Duncan Pennytree View Post
                                        Range (Page 90 & 91): Which range penalties are correct? The ones in the desciption text (-1 for medium & -2 for long range), or the ones in the Combat Summary Chart (-1 for medium & -2 for long range), which are printed in the GM Screen also?
                                        I believe the description text values (-1 for medium, -2 for long) are correct, and the -2/-4 values are artifacts from the previous rules edition which were not caught and corrected.

                                        Originally posted by Ser Duncan Pennytree View Post
                                        Autofire into Close Combat(Shooting into Close Combat – Page 92): Where exactly can I find the rules which apply in this situation? What exactly happens when I autofire into Close Combat?
                                        Second edition core rules don't appear to specify. First edition specified that autofire fired at someone engaged in close combat automatically targeted everyone that person was engaged with (complete with targeting penalties), even short bursts which normally cannot spread to multiple targets. So if your friend Bob was fending Alice and Frank off with a crowbar, and you wanted to burst-fire at Alice or Frank, you would also target Bob. The normal penalty for shooting into close combat is not applied in this situation.

                                        Originally posted by Ser Duncan Pennytree View Post
                                        Structure (Vehicle Traits – Page 99): Shouldn't it say "A vehicle suffering half its Structure ...", not "An object ..."?
                                        A vehicle is an object, isn't it? (This is likely a result of stuff being moved around in editing. The Durability and Structure rules apply to non-vehicle objects too.)

                                                            Fortunately none of that is actually relevant. While the rules state you can combine several tools, what you end up with is a set of tools (which is one "equipment"). Just look at existing equipment such as Automotive Tools, Climbing Gear, Disguise, First Aid Kit, Lockpicking Kit, and Survival Gear. They all consist of various amounts of tools, and grants varying bonuses based on how many tools you have in them. If you use several different singular tools you should just combine them into one kit like the ones already in the book.

                                                            Edit: The vehicle rules also has a unified modification trait for driving rolls which is capped at +5.
