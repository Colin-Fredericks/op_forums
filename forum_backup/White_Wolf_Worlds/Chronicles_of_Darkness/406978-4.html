            So, I decided to put on my quasi-munchkin hat and do some theorycrafting. I do have a newfound respect for the Coil of Zimitra. Opening up Supernatural Merits to Kindred allows for a different set of advantages that have just enough of a niche to be useful without overwhelming actual Kindred powers. The cost break sets them to being just cheap enough to be useful, and allows for easy access to a "reasonable" amount of competence in a large number areas. Essentially, a vampire specializing in the Coil of Zimitra is looking to be a jack of all trades.

            Auspex is better then the supernatural options available at investigating specific questions, people, or places. Supernatural merits are better when you have very little information to go on, or need a broad overview. Clairvoyance and Omen Sensitivity are arguably worth the coil all by themselves.

            In terms of combat, if Psychokinesis remains Lethal against Kindred then it offers a viable if extremely costly alternative to the normal Kindred options. It's a crass "throw a bucket of dice at the problem" option, but it is a decent one. Telekinesis is terrible in Kindred vs Kindred conflict because Physical Intensity trumps it.

            Having poked and proded these builds a bit, I noticed that Kindred on Kindred conflict is costly in terms of blood, with both sides trying to end the conflict as quickly as possible using one-shot kills and ambushes. Notably this means that while any of the following builds are terrifying one on one, a group of opponents can bring most of them down. Even one on one, going through a fight is likely to leave you vulnerable to the next one if you aren't careful. Feeding from your opponent helps with this, but that brings its own trials.

            Another observation is that the key to kindred physical conflict is high blood potency. Note that all of these builds are overly twinked as I'm only counting traits that move towards the test goal. Realistically only about 50-75% of a dedicated character's traits are likely to be dedicated so singularly to combat at the actual table, meaning these builds are more delayed than they first appear.

            Gangral
            Attributes: Intelligence 1, Wits 3, Resolve 3, Strength 2, Dexterity 3, Stamina 3, Presence 2, Manipulation 1, Composure 3
            Skills: Athletics 2, Brawl 5 [Grappling], Stealth 2 [Ambushing], Weaponry 2, Streetwise 2, Subterfuge 2
            Merits: Swarm Form 3, Acute Senses 1, Indomitable, Trained Observer 3, Defensive Combat [Brawl] 1,
            Disciplines: Protean 3
            10 EXP: Blood Potency 2, Celerity 1
            20 EXP: Blood Potency 3, Protean 4-5
            30 EXP: Blood Potency 4, Professional Training 5
            40 EXP: Close Quarters Combat 4, Resilience 1-2
            50 EXP: Resilience 3-5

            Highlights Nearly unkillable. Either you catch this vampire by surprise and kill / render unable to defend themselves in one hit (vs Resilience 5) or the best you can hope for is a tie. Lots of fire or lateral thinking are your only other alternatives. Don't discount Swarm Form, even at later EXP values.

            Nosferatu
            Attributes: Intelligence 1, Wits 3, Resolve 3, Strength 2, Dexterity 3, Stamina 3, Presence 2, Manipulation 1, Composure 3
            Skills: Athletics 2, Brawl 2, Stealth 2 [Ambushing], Weaponry 5 [Improvised], Streetwise 2, Subterfuge 2
            Merits: Improvised Weapons 3, Light Weapons 2, Professional Training 3, Defensive Combat [Weaponry] 1
            Disciplines: Obfuscate 2, Celerity 1
            10 EXP: Obfuscate 3, Professional Training 4-5, Blood Potency 2
            20 EXP: Vigor 1-3
            30 EXP: Vigor 4-5, Blood Potency 3
            40 EXP: Blood Potency 4-5
            50 EXP: Trained Observer 3, Indomitable, Acute Senses, Close Quarters Combat 4

            Highlights: Has a nice stealth add-on. If there's anything wood near this person, they can improvise it into a weapon and then have a very good chance of staking an opponent. Dicepool of 24 (29 during a Frenzy) with Rote and 9-Again before defense on an improvised stake that deal 8L base damage means most things are pretty screwed. You generally want to get the drop on people if at all possible. The primary defense tactic is to Celerity then Obfuscate away until the situation is more in the character's favor.

            Zimitra Vampire
            Attributes: Intelligence 2, Wits 2, Resolve 4, Strength 2, Dexterity 2, Stamina 2, Presence 2, Manipulation 2, Composure 3
            Skills: Occult 5 [Psychokinesis], Empathy 4, Streetwise 3
            Merits: Status [Ordo Dracule] 1, Safe Place 1, Nest Guardian 1, Crucible 3, Psychokinesis 5*
            Disciplines: Coil of Zimitra 1*, Celerity 1, ??? 1
            10 EXP: Coil of Zimitra 2-5*, Area of Expertise [Psychokinesis] 1, Professional Training 2
            20 EXP: Blood Potency 2-3
            30 EXP: Blood Potency 4-5
            40 EXP: Professional Training 3-5, Acute Senses 1, Indomitable, Trained Observer 3, Clear-Sighted 2*
            50 EXP: Telepathy 3*, City Walker 3*, Clairvoyance 3*, Omen Sensitivity 3*, Danger Sense 2
            *: Note Discounted EXP cost

            Highlights: You're looking at an utterly obscene 26 (Rote, 9-Again) dice pool for Psychokinesis (before factoring in Resolve) when going all out. That's enough to destroy almost anything. This costs a lot less EXP to "top out" and has easier access to utility. I *think* Psychokinesis still deals lethal damage to Vampires. This guy is an absolutely *terrifying* Sheriff thanks to Clairvoyance, Omen Sensitivity, Telepathy and City Walker.

            I am not sure Zimitra actually allows for Clear-Sighted. If not it's easily replaced with something else.
