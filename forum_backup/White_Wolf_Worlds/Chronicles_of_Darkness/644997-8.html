    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
    That’s a rather simplistic way of putting it. For instance, you're giving the impression that those who aren't in your camp are trying to get a free lunch by disregarding the fact that every proposed mitigating factor has some sort of cost of its own. Similarly, nobody has proposed full immunity to said supernatural afflictions outside of the likes of the Sleepwalker Merit; all alternatives to it that have been suggested in this thread have assumed that those afflictions do continue to affect “all normal humans”, including those hunters who are better able to cope with them. Even the suggestion to apply the Code has been less about eliminating the Sleeping Curse as it’s been about preventing a “death spiral” by replacing the Breaking Point with a Trigger Point, and leaving the rest of the Curse untouched: improving one's ability to cope with the Curse rather than getting rid of it.
    First, I noted both resistance as well as immunity to supernatural afflictions, and do indeed disagree with your perspective on the setting and rules, although implied no personal insult. Such disagreements are hardly unusual or personal.

    My main issue with some of the suggestions is that they seek to provide such resistance while simultaneously keeping all the benefits of being a normal human. In that sense, and to adopt the food analogy, while not a "free lunch," the Hunters are certainly "having their cake and eating it too." At least to me, it generally feels like you and others might be trying to give the advantages of Tier 3 Hunters to the lower levels without calling it as much.

    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
    That said, one suggestion that Charlaquin made deserves special note: the idea that it might be possible for a hunter to “push through” the Sleeping Curse on his own, without deliberate supernatural assistance. There's a rough parallel to this in Werewolf, in that botching a Lunacy-induced Breaking Point makes you Wolf-Blooded and thus free of Lunacy. That said, I suspect that of all the suggestions that have been made here, this is the single most likely one to draw the ire of “Dave's camp”, as it's a direct challenge to the notion that all Sleepers are victims who are utterly incapable of getting out from under Quiescence without outside aid. Never mind that the method proposed isn’t something that would be available to the vast majority of mortals, and that it would be a rough and painful path to freedom even for those who find it.
    It's more than simple agreement with Dave's perspective on comparative template power levels. Dave is the Mage developer and has explicitly ruled that Quiescence unique and not like Lunacy in all matters relevant to this discussion. Of course, you are free to disagree and disregard the canon rule, but absent a clearly conflicting ruling by the Hunter developer, it is the default state of affairs.

    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
    Meanwhile, I disagree with the characterization of everything that might soften the likes of the Sleeping Curse as “effectively amounting to a supernatural advantage”. Is Moral Support effectively a supernatural advantage because it lets a cell of hunters cope more effectively with Lunacy?
    Moral support does not lift the shard of Abyss that curses a sleeper soul, at least according to Mage cosmology. If you believe it should, I disagree but respect your choice.

    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
    Ultimately, the premise of Hunter is that mortals aren't as helpless as the supernaturals think. When looking at things like the Sleeping Curse from Hunter's perspective, that point should be emphasized.
    No one is suggesting that mortals are always totally helpless against the supernatural in the nWOD. However, that does not mean certain supernaturals don't possess very distinct and powerful advantages that are extremely difficult to overcome, particularly by small groups of normal humans. For instance, Dave did not suggest that Hunters never fight Awakened mages, only that it is often suicidal without the support, powers and other advantages provided at the Tier 3 level.

                                        Now, the way I frame the Awakened's relationship to Sleepers for writers is to describe it as the hyper-rich. Mages are like the 1% - they have achieved a position of immense privilege through just enough hard work and personal trial to make some of them ignore the fact that incredible, stupid, luck had an equal or larger part to play.The Lie means there's always a gulf of understanding - even the capacity for understanding - between them and everyone else. Sleepwalkers have the unenviable position of not being scarred for life by being around mages, but being nowhere near as capable - life for a Sleepwalker who's in an Order is like being Bill Gate's assistant.

                                        The Awakened vary massively in their reactions to everyone else - all positions between a committed desire to help the less fortunate to outright victim-blaming (and all Sleepers, as all mages know, are victims, whether they know it or not). But they all do it from that isolated remove. A Silver Ladder mage trying to make everyone's life a little better is like a wealthy philanthropist. They'll always be "poorer" than him, gnostically speaking.

                                        For Hunter... Well, mages do a lot of damage Hunters can take exception to; some mages steal souls perform human sacrifice because it's convenient, rewrite people's lives to suit themselves and treat Sleepers as annoyances or disposable resources.

                                        The problem is that being a Sleeper means you can't remember magic when it's cast in front of you, and seeing it rips chunks out of your soul. It's just a thing you have to cope with when hunting the Awakened, like "vampire blood is addictive".

                                        I'd love to expand on what the Awakened look like to Sleepers and Sleepwalkers - the Orders are semi-visible Mystery Cults in the World of Darkness. The Veil is for the protection of Sleepers' Integrity and magic from Dissonance, not because mages have a desire for secrecy or fear the mob like the Kindred's Masquerade.

                                        I wouldn't do it in a Hunter book, though.

                                        Dave Brookshaw
