                                Well... because the Holocaust is a very delicate topic and very close to me (I have family members who are survivors), I also decided to read the compact- and here is my take on it :P

                                Most of it isn't bad. While the Holocaust is something one should approach with care, it doesn't mean that using it as a material for a fictional organization is wrong- just that one must make sure to not make any mistakes. Like I said, this writeup is not bad in general. The description doesn't give me any feeling that the Holocaust was the creation of supernatural forces. It does contain a little too much use of terms of a "supernatural lexicon", which could create an impression that the Holocaust was full of monsters. The reason that the "occult hunting" paragraph feels a little artificial- like, it takes it for obvious that the Nazis had open connections with witches, vampires and other weird things. There is no "revelation" or "discovery" of the supernatural. It is just like "look, the Nazis worked with monsters, so we hunt monsters. Any questions?" (at least, that is my feeling from it).

                                What I feel that lacks is.. I don't know, personality? Like, the organization kinda lacks it right now. It is just "We are the good guys, and we try to save our country". There should be something more... a theme. That's the main thing which I feel that it lacks. A solid theme. If it had a more solid theme, I feel that the writeup could drop few "occult words" and in that way prevent the feeling that there were ALLOT of supernaturals among the Nazis. I mean, if they are simply a "governmental organization which protect the country from the supernatural because they helped the Nazis in WWII", it sounds a little childish and could potentially make someone to think that the Nazis were backed up by something supernatural. If they had a more specific ideology it could help the reader to feel better that there is a separation between the Nazis and the occult subjects they researched. Still, it could be felt that the writer doesn't try to hint that there was a supernatural "power behind the throne" of the Third Reich, and that humanity is the one which is responsible to the Holocaust (although it could be elaborated a little more, if the writer feels that it would be right)

                                TL,DR- The concept is ok, the writer doesn't say that the Nazis were controlled by some supernatural race, it could use having a more solid theme and the writer should tone down the connection between the Nazis and Occult. Those are my two cents.

                                                        Originally posted by Flyboy254 View Post
                                                        and "They're a touch bland"
                                                        To fix this my suggestion would be to put more focus the central theme. This is "Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution", you could play that up. The Bund isn't trying to protect Fritz, the average German citizen, from being eaten by a Werewolf. They are trying to prevent Fritz from being indoctrinated by a fascist/evil government or movement.

                                                        Here is some possible ways to reinforce that theme:

                                                        You could make the JAGHUNDs that directly deal with preventing monsters (human or supernatural) from infiltrating or influencing government the top dogs who get the funding, support and win their interdepartmental beurocatic conflicts. Anti-Vampire, anti-cult/neo-nazi/rogue-hunters, and internal affairs are top dog. Witches try to influence governments but not as much as Vampires so they'd be in the middle, while groups like Slashers and Werewolves are the runt of the litter. You might go as far as to say that they only have a silver hound because both vampires and werewolves can turn into wolves.

                                                        You could put more focus on internal conflicts. For example the anti-cult guys might be trying to make it harder for cults to get their message out (Germany does censor swastickers), while another group might be strongly in favour of the free press in the belief that an informed public resists indoctrination. They could be conflicted about using surveillance ("We're not the Stasi!" vs "it saves lives!"). Germany's recent spat with the USA (do they also blame the UK? They probably should do) over the NSA spying might serve as inspiration for another one. Who gives us the right to decide when a democratically elected candidate's politics are unacceptable? Basically, show their determination to not become "The Problem" as an active thing with debates, political fights and conflicts of opinion that PCs can take part in.

                                                            I think this is GENIUS!

                                                            First, the organization itself. The way it splits into different specialties, and demonstrates with brutal clarity why that just doesn't work very well in nWoD. The Silver Hounds take out shapeshifters, but what if the shapeshifter is a vampire or a mage with shapeshifting abilities? Black takes out Spirits, but what of Mages with Spirit Arcana or Werewolves? I love how you showed how the crossover potential of nWoD makes this kind of subdivided specialization really more trouble than its worth, yet have a pretty good rationale for why the group doesn't just come together into one uber monster hunting machine.

                                                            Second, the Nazi connection. Nazis are, perhaps, overutilized in fiction as pretty much the ultimate disposable bad guy. As Yahztee of Zero Punctuation said, "Nazis are like Skittles in that you can rid the world of as many as you like and never get a bad taste in your mouth." Something I loved about the Followers of Thule was how they sort of moved on from that concept, and dealt with the complex feelings of guilt and remorse for what was one of the darkest chapters in human history. I love how this writeup expands that concept, focusing not on "Nazi Germany," but on "Post-Nazi Germany." How do people come back from that, and how do they ensure something like it never happens again? And here, I think, is where some people are having a disconnect about respect and taste.

                                                            Sidetrack: See, in the real world, there are all kinds of organizations and laws and such in place in Germany as direct results of that dark time, to ensure those things never happen again. But this write-up is about a group that only exists in the nWoD, and thus only covers the changes from our world to that world. In our world, yes, Hitler and some of his followers were obsessed with the occult, but depending on what you believe about the state of paranormal, they were anything from slightly misguided to completely delusional. They certainly weren't wielding any kind of actual, mystical power of the type available to be found in the nWoD. Now, peering through the nWoD lens, we find that these forays into the occult could actually have yielded results, and like everything else the Nazis did, they left things a gods-awful mess when they fell. Someone has to step in and clean it up. As the same groups we have in the real world go about cleaning up the same messes that exist in the nWoD, this group peculiar to the nWoD appears to clean up the mess peculiar to the nWoD. So, yeah, the write-up focuses on things that are peculiar to the nWoD as well, because that's the point. There's no need to keep belaboring the point that mundane organizations exist to do the same job they do in our mundane world. The write-up never once lays the blame for anything that happened in Nazi Germany squarely at the feet of the supernatural. In fact, most of what ties back to WWII proceeds organically from what we already know about the nWoD. People who die, especially traumatically, can leave ghosts, and the camps killed hundreds of thousands of people very traumatically, so that leaves a lot of ghosts. Spirits are attracted to places that Resonate with their concepts, so spirits of death, fear, despair, torment, anguish, and so on will gather around the camps as well. That's simply a fact of the nWoD. How does that cheapen what happened? In simple literary metaphor, all it does is demonstrate that the horrible things that happened still echo today, that the atrocities can never be undone. All we can do now is live with them. This, I think, is what Flyboy is getting at. He's not saying that Hitler was possessed by a spirit of Anti-Semitism or some bullshit like that, he's just expanding the logic of the nWoD to include the aftermath of World War II and the Holocaust. Moreover, I noticed that he does reference other groups targeted for elimination by the Nazis. Yes, the Jewish people were the first and most prominent victims, but the Nazis sought to "cleanse" the world of so many, many more groups of people. Forgetting just how broad the death toll was cheapens the Holocaust more, in my mind, than noting that it happened and here are the consequences in a fictional world.

                                                            Third, the specialties. I don't personally see them as "know it all awesomes," but I can see how that interpretation would arise. I think it's mostly from the Status benefits, where higher Status confers more bonuses in regards your specialty monster, and the flavor descriptions of the knowledge that level of Status brings. Tone that down or re-write it, so that even Status 5 characters know just slightly above jack-shit, and that should address the uber-Hunter complaint.

                                                            Fourth, the innovative approach. I love that these guys are invested in not just lumping all monsters together as monsters. It fits with the roots in immediate post-Holocaust Germany, the vow to never again consider anyone "subhuman" just because they are different. I love that they pretty much view every monster on a case-by-case basis, and are perfectly willing to let werewolves and such go about their daily and nightly lives provided they don't hurt anyone. The only places I noticed this break down is as regards vampires and Prometheans. I can understand both to an extent, but would like to raise a few points. Prometheans, given the effects of Disquiet and Wasteland, may very well be viewed as constant threats if people don't figure out that these are things truly beyond their control, and learn a great deal about the core of the Promethean condition, which is difficult-to-impossible due to the nature of the Promethean condition. It's part and parcel of a Promethean's lot in life to be hated and feared everywhere he goes, and this is just another facet of that experience. However, should a Promethean start making inroads with Yellow, and show the group what kind of piteous creatures they really are. . . oh, story potential abounds. With Vampires, I wonder why Red views the only good bloodsucker as a dead bloodsucker. What about the ones just trying to survive from one night to the next? Is Red intent on dismantling the manipulative support structure Kindred invariably build for themselves (which makes sense, given their desire to never see Germany manipulated again), but at the same time, this sort of structure is the only thing that allows Kindred to survive in the mortal world. Is Red fully prepared to sentence every Kindred in Germany to death should they succeed in destroying the power structure that allows Kindred to feed and interact with minimal risk? Do they understand that the nature of the Beast means that dismantling Kindred society will result in things getting way, WAY worse before they get better? Is there any attempt to reach a kind of treaty or truce with the vampires as a whole, or with individual Covenants? Also, I love how Red Hunters are less classic "stake-and-bake" vampire hunters, and more about infiltrating the Kindred and beating them at their own game. I don't think I've ever seen an organization try and take on vampires in their own arena of politics, manipulation, and deceit, and for that alone this idea gets a gold star from me.

                                                            All in all, I think this is a terrific idea, and would totally run a Chronicle based solely on this writeup alone.
