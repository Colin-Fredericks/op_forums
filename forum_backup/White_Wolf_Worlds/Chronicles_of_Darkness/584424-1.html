    I guess that if the creators of Changeling ever had to weigh in on the issue in an actual book, what we would probably get is a side bar or something similar that makes it clear this is a game about Loss. It's right there in the name. It's not a Loss if it doesn't change something about you. Representing the changeling condition/viewpoint as something that can be 100% fixed could be misconstrued as being insensitive toward real life trauma. On the other end, implying or even saying that you are Forever Lost is a foolish move as well, because it can make real people who are trying to deal with their traumas think it's pointless, even if this thought is relatively brief. That can be a very dangerous perspective for someone who is already very hurt, no matter how brief. They'd compromise and suggest something along the lines of what MCN said. "Earn Your Happy Ending", as TV Tropes would put it. The key word here is "suggest", as is normal for the NWOD.

    Some people like to play the role of the one who tries to deal with tragedy.

    Others like to play the role of the one who gives the middle finger to the Gentry, proceeding to kick ass on the way to building their own Hedge Domain or something similarly defiant.

    Both can be cathartic and reinvigorating in their own ways. Neither are necessarily and inherently wrong.

    So, being game developers, they need to make their games as fun as possible for many people. That requires them to admit the validity of multiple gaming styles. It's the sensible approach to their business. Nobody ever seriously claimed that you can't have a Rogue Trader-like game where you play as a motley of Loyalists with claws and tendrils on lots of shadier-than-average Goblin Markets. You would be playing a role that is abhorrent in the eyes of the standard protagonists, which some would call "missing the point of the game". But the OPTION to do that should be in there. More stuff to do is very unlikely to be received with nothing but boos and ah-hell-naws.

    Just my two cents. Correct me if you wish to, I'm all ears. Have at thee! :P

                    Originally posted by Jacob View Post

                    Realistically this would be the case. You can forgive and overcome but not really forget or turn back into what you were before. On the other hand this is a storytelling game so a better question to ask is not how it can be accommodated using the system but rather, if that's the kind of story a troupe wants to tell, what's the best way to go about it?

                    Note that I agree that it isn't something that should be possible, as MCN notes further below (or above this particular post) you can overcome trauma but you still bear the scars. Nevertheless, a lot of stories gloss over this (just look at the recovery / "return to normalcy" that Fitz went through during the course of AoS season 2 -- people with brain injuries (or a lot of other kinds of injuries) don't return to "normal"). Even if it's against the preferences of some of us, I don't see a problem with speculating as to how the game can be used to accommodate such a story. As has been pointed out, it'd probably look like Vampire's Golconda. IIRC, oWoD Changeling actually had a bunch of rules and text devoted to answering this question. You (the original poster) might want to have a look-see at that material for ideas how it might be adapted to the Lost. Most likely it'll be combination of factors (recovering bits of soul from the Hedge, reducing Wyrd to 1, and increasing Clarity to 8+).
                    Changeling: the Dreaming changelings were never pure human, but hybrids and body-swappers. There was a state similar to Golconda called Siochain(sp) where a Changeling could balance his fae and human sides, and escape Banality.

                        There are a couple of ways for a changeling to become a human in the world of darkness that I can think of offhand.

                        1) Go to the underworld, dive into the ocean of fragments until you lose your identity as a changeling. Of course one risks coming out a human of a different age, gender, political idealogy, handedness, ethnicity, nationality, sexuality, height, body type, and every other identifier, so one runs the risk of it being a really creative form of suicide.

                        2) Die and be resurrected by a spirit with that resurrection numen, the one that downgrades supernaturals to their minor template form, so the changeling would probably become fae touched.

                        3) Get an archmaster to remove the template, but that would require, thanks to the pax arcana, the permission of a true fae to do so.

                        4) Get the help of some hunters, in hunter curing monsters is something that, with the ST's permission, can work. Magical ritual, science, miracles?

                        5) Use the couch of dream time travel from GMC to prevent oneself from being taken by the Fae in the first place, although that runs the risk of the character fading away from temporal paradox.

                                Frankly, I dont see the problem.
                                I think Vampire and Changeling has a certain degree of common ground in this regard, in the sense that both types has had something tangible done to them, that cant easily be reserved however much they might wish it so. I think it'de be interesting to have it as a possibillity. The Gentry took something from you, to make you a Changeling. Take it back!

                                In the context of the metaphor or 'message', I think we could all benefit from remembering that a game like this is a merger between what one wishes to say metaphorically and whats interesting to play out, while also keeping in mind several interpritations of those things. Some people might subscribe to the idea that trauma cannot be overcome and put behind oneself in this fashion and other might people might take an opposite view. I think the games possibillity space could include both of those interpritations and the gamestyles that spring from them. Thats how one creates an enjoyable experience centered around a common theme. To allow the game to express all possible interpritations of those themes and not just one particular one, which oneself subscribes to. You should never try to force people to play something, specific. Yes, Changeling is a game about loss and trauma, but loss and trauma means something different to different people.
                                I dont think we will be hurting anybody by allowing that multiplicity, and if we somehow end up offending some delicate snowflake, then thats no fault of the writers or any of us.
