            Originally posted by lnodiv View Post
            The problem I have with combining them into a single skill is that it makes combat characters much, much more versatile, and more versatility equals more power (because the number of situations in which you can utilize your power grows with versatility, granting you power in situations you would not have had it otherwise).
            The problem is that just saying, "much" a lot doesn't equate to demonstrating this to be the case.

            What's the versatility achieved?

            Some Merits now have effectively lower prerequisites sure, but close combat is still the most Merit intensive part of the game. Access to more Fighting Merits is greater versatility, but each of those Merits still costs you investment elsewhere.

            Unarmed focused characters have a greater versatility of equipment. This is ultimately part of the whole point. Brawl's biggest issue is its lack of equipment options that allow it to be competitive with Weaponry, forcing Brawl characters to rely on Merits and/or supernatural powers to have the weight in combat a Weaponry character has naturally. This increase in power is meant to close this gap.

            Armed focused characters have access to grappling/non-lethal disabling without special equipment (since that exists) and unarmed attacks. How potent is this really? The increased versatility with attacks that deal lower and worse quality damage, and likely lower dice-pools... isn't really even much of an increase outside of a white-room where there's almost always something you can pickup and attack people with.

            So, where does this increase in versatility really equate to such a significant overall change in power for a combat focused character?

Firearms, Athletics, Brawl and Weaponry, the four main "combat skills," share in common the fact that they're all used as a major part of the combat subsystem in order to deal damage.
