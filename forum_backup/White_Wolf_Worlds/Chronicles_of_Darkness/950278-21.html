    Possibly semi more back on topic - Maybe a means of horror theme can be expressed through tragedy? Playing off the Inspiration angle, maybe Genii are cursed with the inspiration they find in the world around them that allows them to make so much out of seemingly so little, but the tragedy is finding out that the rest of humanity is blind to the inspiration that surrounds them (and every attempt to force them into becoming aware of it doesn't actually open their eyes, it just blinds them with the Genius' own personal Inspiration and turns them into Igors).

    Maybe a newly catalyzed Genius made something inconsequentially charming for his teenage children because he loves them and wants to share the fruit of his inspiration with them in the hopes that they too will find the same joy he does and they can connect like they used to before his wife left him (because none of his inventions ever amounted to anything before and after ten years of arguing about all his junk she couldn't sit by and watch him fail any more). And at first it seems like it works, they love whatever it is and they want to spend more time with dad in his workshop and they want to tinker with some of his leftover ideas. Only it soon becomes clear that they aren't Inspired the way dad is, they just want to see what he makes next. It's all they can think about. They start skipping class to spend more time in dad's workshop. To a Genius, Inspiration is a lantern that is invisible to everyone but themselves. It guides the way for them, but everyone else is doomed to wander in the dark forever, unless they blindly follow the Genius.

    When one is accustomed to privilege, equality seems like oppression.

        I might be misreading, but the sense I get is that that part of the setting is, "Geniuses will change the future, but they haven't caused many visible changes up to now."
        Not really.

        Genius has a current day setting, but the Inspired have at least partial responsibility for that present day setting. To give one example, in the real world one of the most famous conspiracy theories is David Ike's theories about secret lizardmen overlords.

        The leaders of Lemuria actually were lizardmen; so chances are in Genius: the Transgression David Ike's books were written about Lemuria. So that's one small example of Geniuses causing visible changes. There's no list or anything in the book, but it should be easy for a creative storyteller to fill the setting with more examples.

                                                Okay so here's some rambling thoughts that I've tried to trim down into a narrative that makes sense about a way to do horror in genius.

                                                Divorce Inspiration from mental illness while still maintaining a thematic connection. Inspiration strikes without warning, without regard to any identifying features at all (race, age, sex, etc), but it doesn't make you crazy. What it does is make you dysfunctional. Just because you are Inspired, it doesn't make you a Genius - that's status only comes once you've made a Wonder. Instead, you're constantly distracted by the potential in the world that you can't articulate to anyone else. Inspiration's first great tragedy and horror is that it isolates it's heralds. The second great tragedy and horror is that Society sees these people and it throws them away because they get labeled "defective", children inaccurately get labled with hyperactivity or attention deficit disorders that they don't have (or simply as problem children). Adults are likewise labled with mental illnesses they don't have (or simple laziness), while the elderly get diagnosed with dementia.

                                                Society punishes them for circumstances beyond their control. The Transgression isn't Inspiration driving you insane, it's society gaslighting you because of it. In ideal world, being forced to carry the burden of an inescapable and inarticulatable creative urge wouldn't be a social death sentence. Ideally, society would recognise that there's some sort of untapped talent and the Inspired would gravitate to positions where creativity is celebrated - many would become artists or musicians or similar things. Instead, you're told that you are broken and defective* and you're left to fend for yourself in a dark world. That kind of thing builds up a great deal of emotion. Mixing that emotion and your Inspiration and sometimes that catalyzes into your first Wonder. Congratulations! You are now a Genius, capable of articulating your Inspiration to others.

                                                Only, you can't share this with Society any more than you could have before! Your theories inspired by your Inspiration make no sense to the un-Inspired, and they mock you for them. Your devices short out and break when you try to reveal them to the teeming masses. Maybe there's some heretofore inexpiable force that breaks your Wonders in the face of un-inspired scrutiny in a manner that makes the wreckage look like mundane tech with some special effects mixed in (your broken ray gun looks like a pistol rigged up with lights now), some Genii struggle against this process by trying to raise the general technological level to the point where what they do falls into what the un-inspired can accept (before guns were invented, a broken Wonder that worked like a gun would look like a fancy crossbow or something under un-inspired scrutiny). The third great tragedy and horror of Genius is now the nagging worry that Society is somehow correct, that you and all your peers are somehow delusional. The Transgression Society commits against you now forces you to deal with the urge to gaslight yourself.

                                                *depending on how deep you want to take it, you can also dig into racism and classism when dealing with this. A six year old Inspired from a rich white new england family that can hire all the best tutors gets more support for their creative and bright child, while the poor dark skinned six year old with a single mother taking care of them gets labeled with an attitude problem and failed by the system. Digging into that further than what I just did is beyond the scope of this post (and I don't want to derail the thread through a discussion of the injusties of society)

                                                When one is accustomed to privilege, equality seems like oppression.
