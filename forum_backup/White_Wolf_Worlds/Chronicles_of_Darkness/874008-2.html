                        If our argument is that sunlight is "light with the quality 'coming from the sun'" and then propose that our common sense understanding is as follows:
                        "Light with the quality of 'coming from the sun' harms vampires"
                        "Light with the quality of 'reflected by the moon' does not harm vampires"
                        then we inevitable end up with some inconsistent triads and defeat by simple logic occurs.

                        Consider the following triads the represent the assumptions of the above two statements:
                        A: "Light that has the quality 'coming from the sun' harms vampires"
                        B: "Light that has the quality 'being reflected by the moon' does not harm vampires"
                        therefore, C: "Light that has the quality 'coming from the sun' is not the same as light that has the quality 'reflected by the moon'"

                        D: "Some light that has the quality 'coming from the sun' strikes a mirror"
                        E: "That light loses the quality 'coming from the sun' and gains the quality 'being reflected by the mirror'"
                        F: "Light that has the quality 'being reflected by the mirror' harms vampires"

                        This second triad is what we call an inconsistent triad. The light that is harming the vampire doesn't have the quality of "coming from the sun" but our common sense tells us that it will still harm vampires. Ergo, the quality of 'coming from the sun' is not a sufficient common sense explanation for why the light harms vampires.

                        There are a few ways out of this. You could reject D but that makes the whole thing moot so I wouldn't. You could reject E and claim that the light can have both the qualities of 'coming from the sun' and 'reflected by the mirror' but, if we do this then we're locked into either rejecting C or constructing a pair of infinite lists of things that have reflection qualities similar to the moon and things that have reflection qualities similar to the mirror. This can be broken again by doubling down and having "light with the quality 'reflected by the moon' lose that quality and gain the quality of 'reflected by the mirror' which can be escaped from by rejecting C. However, if at anytime we reject C then we must also reject either A or B. Since A is our primary assumption that we all agree that it's necessarily true and it matches our common sense then we'd have to reject B and suddenly we find ourselves with your "shitty game".

                        But moreover, my Ordo Dracul Mekhet philosopher is forever unable to make any progress on answering her "what is sunlight anyway?" question.

                        In the main, radiation and vampires is an edge case and it can be handled a number of ways (poison conditions and tilts, extreme environment tilts, twists to existing rules, etc.). The main question should never really be how does X affect vampires but if X affects vampires in way Y, what are the implications for my story? Our goal shouldn't be to crunch out some ivory tower canonical understanding, so as much as it should be to help one another tell engaging stories.
