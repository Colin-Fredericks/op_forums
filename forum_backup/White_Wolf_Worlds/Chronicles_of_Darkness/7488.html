    'Threats Unknown' and Other Such Strangeness

    11-10-2013, 01:50 PM
    There's some talk about a Hunter book being pitched somewhere down the line, after The Something About Monsters Comes Out, that covers antagonists and phenomena that don't fit any of the main gamelines (Vampires, Werewolves, Mages, the Reanimated, Changelings, Ghosts/Sin-Eaters, Mummies, and God-Machine Demons). While such a thing is at this point an idea of an idea, I'd like to open up this thread to gauge interest and maybe inspire the Onyx Path writers a bit.

    What would you want to see in an 'assorted weird things' book?

    I'm hoping for more on Intersitial Terrain, as described in the Horror Recognition Guide Story "Ten Photographs." The idea of overlapping realities that aren't explicitly the Hedge or anything else familiar (like the weird meat-machine Philadelphia) sounds fascinating, and allows for all sorts of fun stuff; everything from slightly different realities to wherever the USS Eldridge went on that fateful October day.

    I'd also like to see some info on aliens and vague government mystery (I think the Horror Recognition Guide had a story on this as well?), if only to feed my infinite hunger for 90s-style conspiracy fun.

    Remi. she/her. game designer.

                          You don't need a book for that. In fact one word would be enough: "Avoid".

                          Ok, I'm significantly exadurating for effect. However each compact / conspiracy is basically a different game. You can definitely do it, however it is basically crossover in my opinion and crossover always dilutes the themes of the individual gamelines, and usually requires some furious hand-waving. Why isn't the VASCU guy reporting the Ascending One for connections to the drug trade? Why doesn't the Ageis Kai Doru agent kill the MM priest for Witchcraft? It's hard to get the Union's feeling of ignorant hunters who get by on grit and community spirit if your cellmate is a Loyalist with ye olde tomes of accurate knowledge

                          So IMO, any crossover section would have to talk more how you create new themes to replace the diluted themes of individual groups than just being a big list of excuses to tell the brass. That said, I think that what's much more important than a crossover section is really solid rules going in depth in one group. Everything form the basics - how to ask for backup - to entire games spent dealing with internal matters. (I'm working on a fanbook called Cults where I hope to address some of those issues)

                                  I can see two possible formats for this book.*

                                  One is based on the Night Horrors format, with relatively short sections on each beasty. But like Wicked Dead, it could have a longer section for a more elaborate take on a creature. The first and second sections could be an assortment of monsters, the third a longer treatments of a specific one- Ghouls from the corebook's fiction being the most obvious choice.

                                  The other format is like Monstrous Basterds; i.e., more in-depth treatments but a smaller number overall, modeled on Witch Finders/Night Stalkers/Spirit Slayers. I can see expanding on Interstitial Terrain ("threats from the universe next door"), Ghouls and the Djinn (from Compacts & Conspiracies).

                                  *Or two books! Or three. A Hunter Night Horrors book, a core mortals Night Horrors book, and then a Hunter Threats Unknown book.

                                  The mortals/demons-oriented Night Horrors book could be themed around the God-Machine's "rivals." The God-Machines of neighboring universes, viral deities infecting the operating system, other malfunctions, the prototype God-Machines from the universe before this one and what they've gotten up to lately, etc.

                                  The hunter-oriented Night Horrors book could be themed around stuff that resembles, but isn't, the other game lines. One-off vampires, shapeshifters, zombies, ghouls, etc. Stuff with specific folkloric backing and weaknesses to support a monster-of-the-week Hunter game.

                                  Then Threats Unknown for Hunter material intended for "deeper" effects on the wider world of darkness. You consult Night Horrors: Monster Safari when you're killing the one and only specimen of its type in the world. You consult Threats Unknown when you want to do a campaign opposing an invasion by body-snatching aliens from a dark future, or a cannibal ghoul people-smuggling/eating ring out of Russia.

                                                          Professor Phobos: The other format is like Monstrous Basterds; i.e., more in-depth treatments but a smaller number overall, modeled on Witch Finders/Night Stalkers/Spirit Slayers. I can see expanding on Interstitial Terrain ("threats from the universe next door"), Ghouls and the Djinn (from Compacts & Conspiracies).

                                                          Another that comes to mind is one of the most oft-fanmade/called-for additions, the Reckoning Imbued. It might seem strange to count them as monsters, but they are - unlike tier 3 hunters - unabiguously powered and guided by external supernatural forces (and "they're still human" is already something that can be applied to witches, so it's not really a get-out here).

                                                          They're supernaturally-powered individuals who seem driven to destroy other things like them. Are they allies? Another third tier group in effect? Or monsters who are just taking on the competition? (And are the first and third necessarily exclusive)?

                                                          Additionally, one of the themes of Reckoning was that the Imbued were a new phenomenon. Keeping that aspect adds an element to hunters' responses to them that's not present in the existing monsters, or in other ideas for TU.
