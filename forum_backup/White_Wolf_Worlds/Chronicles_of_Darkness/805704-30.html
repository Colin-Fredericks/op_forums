    Originally posted by ReshyShira View Post
    I mean you can kind of justify it. But if they're going to get themselves killed anyway, why let them pull you down with them?
    If you know you would fare worse than them. Or, again, what if you've seen that they're worth trusting? What if they've proved time and time again that they can get the job done when given the chance? Buying them the time they need at a personal cost to yourself is a decision founded on pragmatism, not on blind faith.

    Besides that, sometimes making the tough call does mean trusting someone. In those situations, the Darkling gains Clarity and rolls a Breaking Point. Worst case scenario, it's a wash. Best case, you succeed (or maybe even exceptionally succeed) the Breaking Point roll and don't lose the Clarity you just gained.

    Originally posted by ReshyShira View Post
    Seems kinda metagaming, but I'm not the designer. I do know that in my groups if you take a dramatic failure the STs usually punish you for it egregiously.
    If that's the case, your groups don't sound like an environment I would enjoy playing in. But you do you. More importantly, this kind of "metagaming" is central to the design of CofD 2e. The system is meant to give the player out-of-character incentives to take actions that are not strictly beneficial to the character for the sake of drama. That's literally how characters grow and progress in this system.

                                                            I totally get where ReshyShira's coming from.

                                                            I regularly play with two disparate groups of various NWoD/CofD games and the difficulty to transitioning over to the metagame currency style of CofD was literally too much for one of those groups so they're basically sticking with 1E from now on. The other group took to it like a fish to water, even in spite of some of the conditions being too heinous to resolve, the aspirations/beats/conditions cycles flowed pretty naturally and it worked really well.

                                                            If you're having a hard time grasping that style of meta-roleplay with incentives and it doesn't come naturally to you, the rules can read like you've been given the ability to play six different flavours of fishmalk with hard stereotypes that are likely to destroy any cohesion your group has. There can be a huge amount of dissonance between the expectations of 1E and CofD, so I'm not surprised that this sort of issue keeps coming up during the preview when the mechanics get into the heavily meta area (especially in some areas I have myself gone "wait what, how is that ever going to work?" which I will willingly own starting arguments on).

                                                            If you've got a group that gets it intuitively? It works really well. People enjoy the complications and interesting things. You get interesting stories and have a lot of fun.
