        I see pointing a gun to your wife as an action that could or could not trigger a breaking point and shoot to your wife as a totally different action. Both provoked by two different command: "point the gun to your wife" (extended action) and "shoot to your wife" (ISTANT ACTION). In the second case you shoot to your wife as an istant action in just one turn, incurring in the breaking point after having performed that command.                                     Mesmerize is the first dot of Dominate. It costs no Vitae to use and will more likely than not, succeed vs most Mortals. There are a wide range of benefits that can result from the issuing of simple four word commands (Let me in, give me your wallet, drop your gun, scream if people arrive). The Mesmerized condition lasts for a single scene and is resolved when one takes damage or experiences a breaking point as part of the Vampire's command. This is intentional design so that he first dot of Dominate is not all-powerful. It is intended to nudge a situation in a Vampire's favor but does not give them complete control over it. The Vampire's target will only carry out the command up to the point where it causes them to experience a breaking point.
                                    So for the example of "Shoot your wife", the Vampire rolls and succeeds at using Mesmerize to inflict the condition on his target. Now let's set up the scene, the Target's wife is present and he was already holding a gun (probably to defend he and his wife). The Mesmerize condition forces him to follow the command and point the gun at his wife. At this point, the Target experiences a breaking point (Pointing an armed weapon at his wife with the intent to kill, probably a breaking point for the vast majority of people). Mesmerize resolves and he is unable to follow through with shooting his wife as he regains control and realizes what he's just done.
                                    Now let's continue the example but assume the Target also has the Dominated condition as a result of the Vampire using Iron Edict. In this example, Mesmerized resolves during the first breaking point (pointing the gun at his wife) but Dominated continues and forces the Target to experience a breaking point(Mesmerized resolves but Dominated persists in this instance). Now the Target makes a resolve + composure check resulting from experiencing a breaking point. If the Target fails this resolve+ composure check, he shoots his wife. He now experiences a final breaking point from murdering his wife and has to make an Integrity check. Otherwise, Dominated resolves and he snaps back from the Vampire's control, having nearly shot his wife.
                                    The first breaking point needs to be acknowledged because in all likelihood, this is where the command 'Shoot your wife' is the most likely to be the point where the Vampire's control over the Target ends as after this, the Target will gain a +3 bonus to rolls to resist being further Mesmerized (which one would need to inflict Dominated). These rolls are important because they dictate very dramatic and scene-changing events. Breaking points in cases where the player or character has total agency over their actions should probably be combined and rolled afterward. But in this use of them, it's extremely important to the outcome of a scene to make those Breaking Point rolls.
