    Bloodline: Amrita Bhaga

    09-09-2016, 01:24 PM
    Player of mine has an aspiration to be adopted into and explore a bloodline, and chose one of the the mentioned, but not fleshed out, bloodlines from Ancient Mysteries/Bloodlines.

    Their blurb is: "Amrita Bhaga (Daeva): Sensuous and elegant, this almost entirely female bloodline serves the gods of love and pleasure, often choosing to present themselves as avatars of the beneficent goddesses of art and music."

    Thought Process
    The Sanskrit translation comes across along the lines of:
    • Amrita: Immortality / Nectar of Immortality / Ambrosia
    • Bhaga: Lord / Patron / Prosperity / Distributor of Wealth & Prosperity

    First - I'm ditching the gender bit. One of the themes in my setting is that while a character's Mask is affected by any discrimination in their mortal culture, those don't apply in the Dirge. Vampires are vampires. In the All-Night Society, it doesn't matter what gender you are or present as (if any), what race you were born as, etc. Those mortal considerations are replaced by Clan racism/stereotypes and Covenant biases when I want to explore those kind of things on this side of the Masquerade. So, the bloodline will be open to all genders/sexes generally.

    Second - There's potential for a lot of overlap with the Greek Muses and the Invictus Cherubim, so I want to break things more Indian in theme. Rather than love/pleasure/art/music, I like the idea of the bloodline being focused more on the concept of the Hindu divine feminine. Gives a little more wiggle room, and wraps back in that female theme I watered down from above. Devi/Shakti makes a good conduit for this.

    Looking more deeply into the concept of Amrita, the mythology of it points to 'The Churning of the Seas' - where the Devas (Gods) were briefly cursed and began to lose their eternal life. They formed an alliance with the Asuras (evil spirits, demons and opponent of the gods) to churn the Ocean of Milk to regain the gift of immortality.

    This gift was the Amrita, one of fourteen treasures produced by the churnings of the alliance. The Asuras had already been less than faithful in the alliance, so the Devas attempted to keep the Amrita for themselves and give the Asuras divine alcohol in its place. A scuffle ensued, some of the Amrita spilled on the ground, forming temples and gemstone mines where they fell.

    So, there's a potential new theme. Amrita as a concept of treasure/gemstones, fueled by the nectar of immortality. Combine that with the latter translation of Bhaga - the patron who distributes wealth - and the Bloodline will be focused on granting the wisdom of the divine feminine to worthy individuals.

    I was curious if there were any gemstone-related myths/folklore and sure enough, I found out about the concept of the Navaratna (Nine Gems) - an arrangement of nine particular gems that represent the Vedic planetary aspects and are considered auspicious / lucky / magical. So I think that will fit in well for the Bloodline advantage itself, using these physical gems as conduits between the Amrita Bhaga and whoever they are acting as a patron toward.


    Amrita Bhaga

    Is there any greater pleasure than to know god? So many wander in search of the divine, hoping it will spill down upon our heads, a touch of fate that presses upon us like a parent's kiss. The wise know there is nothing separate from god, the entire universe is suspended from its form as a necklace of jewels. Our people do not wait for god from the unconsciousness of the heavens. We find stones of divinity, instead, within our companions. We mine them from their hiding places, we carve away their flaws, we set them among gold and silver, we polish them to a fine sheen. All until they reflect our light back to those divine adornments - shining for all to see: we wear you, great one, though we are but nothing.

    Clan: Daeva

    Nickname: Sanrakshak, Gemcutters (anglicized), Abhushan or Pendants (derogatory)


    Background:
    Legends tell of the gods and their alliances, their great churning of the waters in an effort to find the gift of immortality. The alliance worked, but in all things, when time to divide that success, there was fighting. The Devas of lore maintained control of this eternal life-giving Amrita, but its secrets, and even a few drops, spilled to the earth. Where they landed formed both holy temples and precious gemstone mines.

    Shiva and his consort Shakti, in-separate and coexisting forces of the divine. He is the unconscious, the impersonal, the omnipotent. She is the personal, the active, the creative. They are one and the same. They are everything the opposite is not. They are nothing without the other. Being one is the only path to becoming the second. This is the truth that Amrita Bhaga aspire to personify.

    The path to understanding god lies through understanding the little gods surrounding them. Every vampire has a touch of the divine, a river of the immortal liquid, the Amrita, flowing through them. Often mistaken for simple guards, consorts or patrons - when an Amrita Bhaga dedicates themselves to a charge (a Rakṣita) - it is a bond much more intimate.

    Every flash of the divine is overshadowed by the fears, flaws and cracks of the Kindred it is housed in. The Amrita Bhaga dedicate themselves to eliminating those shadows within their Rakṣita, so the divine can shine truly and bright. When the Amrita Bhaga allow themselves to be called Gemcutters, it is because they understand their gems are other Kindred.


    The Becoming:
    As most Daeva, Gemcutters usually possess the social and physical skills the Clan is known for. How Daeva treat their mortal herds is the first inkling on if they'd be good for the bloodline or not. Serial mono-feeders, who drink from a vessel once, then cast them away - don't show the ability to truly bond. Daeva who fuel their obsession to the extent that the vessel is used up or destroyed, don't show the nuance needed to value a sacred treasure. But Daeva who navigate the obsession that develops, who keep their herds alive and functioning - that's the start of the skills needed to bond with a Rakṣita.

    When being inducted into the bloodline itself, Gemcutters dedicate their starting path to one of the Navadurga, the nine goddesses. Each represents a different aspects of the divine feminine forces: Creation, Sustenance, and Destruction. Creative Gemcutters focus on adding new aspects to their Rakṣita, opening up new paths for them to explore. Sustaining Gemcutters fulfill their Rakṣita's needs and desires, freeing them to better themselves in other ways. Destructive Gemcutters strike down opponents and topple obstacles, even internal once, blocking the Rakṣita from progressing. Ultimately, a Gemcutter will utilize all of these aspects, but much like the additive, transformative and subtractive methods of sculpting, early specialization or concentration helps a Gemcutter find their unique process.


    Interactions with the Danse:
    Most Gemcutters remain in their homeland of India, some having spread early to the Javan seas and Southeast Asia.

    When the Invictus arrived, conquering and gaining control of many Domains in the subcontinent, the Amrita Bhaga were a soft form of interaction. Attempting to understand these new Kindred forces allowed them to act as translators and diplomats between the two groups when peaceful interactions were possible. A Knightly faction - The Order of Parajit (The Jasmine Order) - developed naturally within the Unconquered to accommodate them.

    Many Invictus were instantly attracted to the concept of beautiful attendants focused on their internal divinity. Conflict with Sanctified was smoothed by the commonalities between Christ and Longinus in the West and Krishna and the Asuras in the East. And this adoption process had already been test run before with the absorption of the Japanese Sotoha.

    The Jasmine Order in the modern nights still represents the biggest faction of Gemcutters outside of India, most of them contracted within the Invictus. The Circle is next, the non-Abrahamic beliefs easy to fold into. The others rarely see a Gemcutter in their ranks. The Ordo are too focused on self-evolution, the Carthians less apt to appreciate a dedication to a single Rakṣita over a culture or community. And the Sanctified may tolerate a fusion of divine ideas in the First Estate, but rarely in the Second.


    Bloodline Bane: The Banded Curse: Gemcutters improve themselves by improving those around them, and then rising to their level. Learning anything on their own is not the path they take. When improving a trait, the Gemcutter must receive guidance from the Mentor merit, or it must be a trait already possessed by their current Rakṣita. Otherwise, double the time and resources cost of the training. If money or favors are required, twice as many dots must be expended. If you use a downtime system for improving, Gemcutters must spend twice the usual downtimes. When learning something with a supernatural requirement, for example needing to drink the teacher's blood or make a Humanity check - the Gemcutter must drink blood on two occassions, or make two Humanity checks. XP costs are not changed.

    Bloodline Disciplines: Celerity, Majesty, Resilience, Vigor

    Bloodline Gift: Adorning the Worthy

    Jauharee [ ja-hVEE ] (New Merit: ● to ●●●●●)
    Requirement: Amrita Bhaga Bloodline

    This Merit allows the Gemcutter to begin crafting the protective jewelry that maintains the connection between them and their Rakṣita. Add the Merit's rating to any Crafts rolls to create, repair or appraise any jewelry, precious metals and gems.

    The Gemcutter is now able to create the facets of the Navaratnas, supernatural gems that each supply a different effect. For each dot, choose two of the following facets (see below) that your Gemcutter knows how to work. Crafting any of the Navaratnas requires a small studio with the appropriate jeweler / gemcutting tools and the appropriate gemstone(s) to be worked.
    • Action: Extended
    • Duration: One roll per Hour
    • Target: Ten Successes per Gem
    • Pool: Dexterity + Crafts (+Jauharee) + Mods
    • Cost: Varies per facet (see below)
    The cost is always at least (1) Vitae and (1) Willpower when crafting one of the facets into jewelry. More powerful effects require more Vitae to be expended. On an exceptional success, add an extra use for that particular facet.

    The effects of each Gem are only available when the Gemcutter is within the same proximity / room of their Rakṣita. They don't need to see each other directly, but must be within range of the Gemcutter's Kindred Senses.

    The jewelry itself need not be visible, but must be worn by either the Gemcutter or the Rakṣita to be in effect. Multiple gems of the same type do not stack, and in fact, once one is completely expended - no gems of that particular facet may be utilized again for the rest of the scene. Using a facet is a reflexive action.

    Jewelry may be targeted with called shots in combat, or the disarm maneuver during grapple. Knowing this, many Gemcutters choose to adorn themselves in many kinds of jewelry, to better hide the supernatural pieces among them. Facets that have lost all uses may be re-used as materials for creating another piece of the same facet.

    Mods
    • Using materials of Resources 1 Value: -2
    • Using materials of Resources 2 Value: -1
    • Using materials of Resources 3 Value: 0
    • Using materials of Resources 4 Value: +1
    • Using materials of Resources 5 Value: +2
    • The Gemcutter has Blood Sympathy toward the Rakṣita: +1 to +3
    • The Gemcutter is under the effects of any sort of Condition representing a lack of clarity or empathy: -3

    Gem Types (Facets):
    • Manak (Ruby)

    Uses: Resolve + Composure <Spends>
    Cost: (1) Vitae, (1) Willpower

    The Gemcutter may spend Willpower on behalf of the Rakṣita to augment their rolls, granting (+3/-2) per normal. The Rakṣita may not double dip by also spending Willpower on the same roll.
    • Moti (Pearl)

    For the purposes of pulling the Rakṣita out of torpor, add the Gemcutter's Jauharee rating to their Blood Potency.

    Uses: One
    Cost: (5) Vitae, (1) Willpower
    • Moonga (Coral)

    The Gemcutter may actively use Resilience on behalf of the Rakṣita. The Gemcutter may still only use each active affect of Resilience once per turn, regardles on if it's for themselves or their charge.

    Uses: Stamina + Resilience <Rolls>
    Cost: (1) Vitae, (1) Willpower
    • Panna (Emerald)

    The Gemcutter may remove (do not gain a beat) the following conditions from the Rakṣita: (Mesmerized, Dominated, Amnesia, or False Memories) by engaging with a Clash of Wills using Presence + Empathy + Majesty against the Dominate user.

    Uses: Empathy <Attempts>
    Cost: (1) Vitae, (1) Willpower
    • Pukhraj (Yellow Sapphire)

    The Gemcutter may personally instruct the Rakṣita in attributes, skills, styles or disciplines. The Rakṣita gains a beat when purchasing dots in the chosen traits. If there is a time or resource component to the learning process, halve it (round up).

    Uses: Intelligence <Purchases>
    Cost: (3) Vitae, (1) Willpower
    • Heera (Diamond)

    The Gemcutter may add their Blood Potency to any Clash of Wills or perception checks against obfuscated targets who target the Rakṣita with any attack or discipline.

    Uses: Wits + Composure <Clashes>
    Cost: (1) Vitae, (1) Willpower
    • Neelam (Blue Sapphire)

    The Gemcutter's melee attacks do lethal damage to any targets who have inflicted lethal damage against the Rakṣita during the scene. If the Rakṣita falls to torpor or is destroyed, the Gemcutter's melee damage upgrades to aggravated against whoever landed the final blow.

    Uses: One <Scene>
    Cost: (5) Vitae, (1) Willpower
    • Gomedh (Hessonite)

    The Gemcutter may mitigate the effects of frenzy against the Rakṣita. Each of the following effects takes up one use of the gem.
    1. Eliminate (do not gain a beat) the Bestial, Competitive, Tempted or Wanton condition from the Rakṣita. This is done through an interaction between the Gemcutter and Rakṣita using a theme of the condition being removed. Sparring, a competition, a sexual encounter, a mutual hunt - something which placates the beast, but not to the extent of the normal Condition resolution.
    2. The Gemcutter may suffer a frenzy trigger on behalf of the Rakṣita. The Gemcutter suffers the worse of any modifiers which would apply (if either of them are starving, for example, apply the -4). The Gemcutter may utilize any mitigating abilities, such as Riding the Wave style or Coil of the Wyrm normally.
    3. If ever their beast's desire during a frenzy ends up focusing on the Rakṣita, the Gemcutter may reflexively choose a new goal/target as if riding the wave.

    Uses: Willpower <Effects>
    Cost: (3) Vitae, (1) Willpower
    • Lehsunia (Cat's Eye)

    The Gemcutter may assist the Rakṣita when facing breaking points. Delay the breaking point roll. First, let the Gemcutter roll against the breaking point as if they had suffered it, using any modifiers they normally would. If the Gemcutter would not be eligible for (or not need) a roll - if they were below the humanity rating or already had a bane for that sin - they cannot help. Apply any successes gained on the Gemcutter's roll to the Rakṣita's as per teamwork rules.

    This should be done over the course of a conversation or scene where the Gemcutter and Rakṣita speak about and address the sin, relating it to each other's lives and relationship.

    Uses: Empathy <Rolls>
    Cost: (3) Vitae, (1) Willpower
    • Navaratnas (Nine Gems)

    This piece of jewelry may only be crafted when all nine of the previous facets have been cut. The new piece incorporates all nine facets into a grand display. The previous abilities may no longer be used, instead, the Gemcutter may now engage in the Avatar of Ekata (see below) devotion with the Rakṣita. These pieces are typically larger, either fixed - such as a pendant or broach, cascading chain pieces, or as a series of rings on specific fingers or toes. As an exception to the normal process, when the Navaratnas have been expended, the piece crumbles to dust.

    Uses: Nine <Scenes>
    Cost: (1) Vitae, (1) Willpower



    Bloodline Devotions:
    • Adorning the Necklace (aka Knight Commander by David Hill)

    (Majesty ●●, Vigor ●●)

    This Devotion allows the Invictus to build unwavering commando units, centered on a Daeva focus, the way majestic planetary systems orbit a star. This inspires those the Daeva has charmed to utter greatness.
    • Cost: (1) Vitae
    • Dice Pool: Presence + (Brawl or Weaponry) + Majesty
    • Action: Instant

    This Devotion has a number of effects, which last for one turn per success on activation. You may pick and choose to which characters it applies:
    • If a character has Vinculum toward your character, for every stage he's subject to, you may give him +1 die on all combat actions.
    • If a character has the Charmed Condition, you may allow him to ignore the first point of bashing or lethal damage from any successful attack.
    • If a character has the Enthralled Condition, you may allow him to spend one Willpower point per turn without actually losing Willpower.

    Additionally, if your character achieves an exceptional success on an attack roll during this time, any witnesses affected by this Devotion also gain the Inspired Condition.
    • Chatoyance

    (Majesty ●●, Celerity ●●)

    This devotion allows the Gemcutter to keep outside attention on themselves over their Rakṣita. The Gemcutter becomes the center and focus of that attention, outshining their charge - allowing them to escape notice or even escape physically, depending on the circumstances.
    • Cost: (1) Vitae
    • Dice Pool: Presence + Persuasion + Majesty
    • Action: Instant
    • Duration: Scene

    Apply rolled successes (capped by the the user's Celerity rating) as a penalty to any incoming attempts to target or notice the Gemcutter's Rakṣita in any capacity. The benefit is not invisibility or obfuscation of the protected, but rather the Gemcutter drawing all eyes to themselves.

    This bonus is on top of any natural resistance penalties the target would normally apply, such as Defense. The Chatoyance bonus applies even when the Rakṣita would be denied a resistance, such as due to surprise or the use of firearms.

    On an exceptional success, enemies may not perform a Killing Blow on the Rakṣita while in the Gemcutter's presence.
    • Soṇita Mehendi
    (Resilience ●●●)


    For three rounds, the Gemcutter expends vitae into a mixture of ink-paste henna. This expenditure is subject to normal per/turn limits of blood potency. After the mixture is complete, the devotion itself costs one vitae per five used in the mixture, rounded up. This, too, is limited by the Gemcutter's per/turn limits.

    The red-brown henna is then applied to the skin of the Gemcutter in intricate and detailed patterns representing different blood-based abilities. Use the total pool of blood expended for the mixture (not counting the activation cost afterwards) and assign them to any of the following uses:
    • [1-6] Any active use of Celerity, Resilience, or Vigor
    • [1-3] A (+2) Physical Intensity bonus for Strength, Dexterity or Stamina
    • [1] Healing Two Levels of Bashing Damage
    • [1] Healing One Level of Lethal Damage
    • [5] Healing One Level of Aggravated Damage

    Each use must be specifically determined and defined while the Mehendi is being applied. Using the heavy lifting ability of Vigor is a distinct pattern from the enhanced weapon damage ability. The largest mixture possible - assuming activating all six active discipline abilities, physical intensity for all three attributes, and healing each type of damage - would require a concoction of (16) Vitae, which would cover them from head to toe. A concoction of one vitae might only encompass the back of one hand.

    Applying the Mehendi itself takes one round per vitae in the concoction (1-16). Use meditation rules for coming up with distraction modifiers if attempting this during a commotion or in an antagonistic environment by rolling Dexterity + (Crafts or Expression) as a base pool.

    Once the Mehendi is applied, thereafter the Gemcutter may utilize it at the top of any round, before any rolls are made. The blood and abilities crafted into the Mehendi are used that turn in place of the Gemcutter spending any of their own blood naturally. They may still take their normal action, benefiting from any discipline or intensity effects that are applicable to it. Other powers and abilities that don't require vitae may still be utilized.

    The Mehendi will also automatically trigger if the Gemcutter's last health box is filled with Lethal or Aggravated Damage, however they receive no normal action that turn to pair the effects with.
    • Cost: (1) Vitae per (5) Vitae spent in the Mixture
    • Dice Pool: Creation - Dexterity + Crafts + Resilience, Use - None
    • Action: Creation - Instant + 1-16 Turns, Activation - Reflexive
    The Mehendi fades if not used by sunrise.
    • Avatar of Ekata

    (Majesty ●●●●, Resilience ●●●●)

    The ultimate act of connection between a Gemcutter and Rakṣita, this powerful devotion allows a physical union in every sense. Both Kindred bodies merge into one single entity, sharing a host of benefits and new possibilities.
    • Cost: (5) Vitae, (2) Willpower - Each partner must spend at least (1) Vitae and (1) Willpower during the initial process, but the other (3) Vitae may be expended by either partner.
    • Dice Pool: Stamina (+ Resilience) + Empathy - Humanity Difference
    • Action: Activation - Instant; Maintaining - Reflexive

    While Merged, apply the following effects to the combined Avatar:
    • Increase all Attributes, Blood Potency and Size by (1) based on the character with the higher amount of each.
    • When making a skill roll, use the character with the higher skill as the base. As a Reflexive action, the character with a lower pool may support the roll with their unmodified skill dots as per teamwork mechanics.
    • Add the dots of each character's Celerity, Resilience, and Vigor together (subject to blood potency limits).
    • Apply all physical and style merits as normal. If not already possessed, gain Striking Looks ••.
    • The Avatar may attempt to utilize any Social/Mortal merits (Allies, Touchstones, Status, etc) of either character with a successful Presence + Persuasion -or- Manipulation + Subterfuge check. This counts as the owning character having used the Merit in question, in case of delayed or persistent consequences (such as expending Resources or burning Status with an organization).
    • Ordo Mysteries, Theban Sorcery, Cruac and Bloodline disciplines may not be utilized by the Avatar unless both characters possess the Coil, Scale, Ritual, or Discipline in question. The other seven base disciplines and common devotions can be used at the rating highest to either character.
    • The characters making up the Avatar are considered to have a Stage Three Vinculum toward each other. This removes any other Vinculum on each character for the duration of the power.
    • Both characters check for any Breaking Points encountered during the merge as long as either would need to check. If either character fails a check, the devotion immediately ends.
    • The Avatar suffers the banes of both characters.
    • Combine the Willpower and Vitae points in both characters to make the new pools of the Avatar based on their new limits. Excess points are returned. When the effect ends, redistribute any remaining Willpower and Vitae points in the Avatar between the two characters, rounding in favor of the Rakṣita.
    • Conditions and Tilts affecting either character are not transferred to the Avatar. However, any Conditions or Tilts remaining on the Avatar when the duration ends are applied to both characters.
    • Conditions and Tilts inflicted BY the Avatar remain on the targets as normal, however, persistent bonus effects (such as a bonus to tempt a target with the WANTON condition) only apply half the usual bonus if exploited by either character while not in Avatar form.
    • The two characters should agree on actions taken by the Avatar. If there is ever a dispute, make a reflexive and contested Blood Potency roll, winner takes the action desired. On a tie, the Avatar takes the Stunned tilt instead of acting at all.
    • Both characters can agree to prematurely end the effect. One character can attempt to prematurely break the effect without an agreement by engaging in a contested Humanity roll as a reflexive action.

    The Avatar remains active for one scene. They may maintain the form for further scenes by spending the required Vitae and Willpower for each extended scene from the Avatar's pools. On an exceptional activation success, the Avatar may stay formed an additional scene without cost.

    The (Humanity Difference) penalty to activate is never positive. Subtract the higher character's humanity from the lower's to get the negative variance.

      I'll end up fleshing out the Jasmine order more fully, maybe make it a Mystery Cult merit, converting some of the real life Navadurga rituals into Kindred equivalents. Expand upon the different forces the bloodline uses (Creative, Sustaining, Destructive). I want the flexibility of seeing one Gemcutter dedicated to Kali as the overbearing attendant battering their charge into shape, right alongside one dedicated to the mother figure of Devi encouraging their charge to be the best they can be.
      Specific feedback requested:

      • Bloodline Bane - Does this seem too light? I didn't just want to impose an XP tax (though that kind of gets covered by having the Mentor out). I could also consider adding some kind of Condition for when the Gemcutter doesn't have an active charge (similar to when Kindred are without Touchstones) or adding penalties to breaking points involving the Rakṣita.
      • The Gem Facets - do any of them seem too wimpy or overpowered compared to their Vitae costs?
      • Avatar - Any weird edge cases you can think of that I haven't thought about?
      • Research - I'm white-bread American just doing internet research on these topics/terms, so if anyone happens to see where I'm referencing anything completely wrong, or in any way that's insulting or appropriation, please let me know. PM is fine, too.
