                                    To share my view of the Gentry, which I probably should've stated a bit back, I view them as psychopathic children who never grew up. They're selfish, incapable of empathizing with those they hurt, or worse choose not to, and do everything for fun.

                                    Obviously, this is just one interpretation, but, its the lens by which I view the Gentry. Less Eldritch Abomination, more the child tearing off a spider's legs for the fun of it. The spoiled child that never learned to respect other people as more than objects to gain pleasure from. Them lacking creativity fits into this, which I note is emphasized, because they need other persons to give them entertainment.

                                    Now, again, just one interpretation, but it does make me view how Seemings and Kiths work somewhat differently, whether 1e or 2e. Its why I view them always having a purpose, albeit an extremely shallow one, for kidnapping people, as an example.

                                    On another note, I see Seemings as what you were willing to give up to escape, rather than as an expression of agency. While the latter is there... its more as a symptom of personal growth. Hence, why I liked the Darklings as is, because they fit that narrative pretty well. If viewed primarily as an expression of agency, yes, the write-up isn't as good, but, I would argue that isn't the only, or even necessarily dominant, part of being a Seeming.
