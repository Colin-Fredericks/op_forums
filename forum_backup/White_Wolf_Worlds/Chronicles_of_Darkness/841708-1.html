    Building off the previous ideas, a few proto-campaign Mage ideas that are based off what little I as a non-backer know of Sundered World.

    •Modern-To-Vinca: The pcs are in their Hollow-Sanctum, hanging out or doing business or just sleeping, when there's a sudden lurch, and the next thing they know is that the world outside their door is gone. Instead, the place they were in is now in one of the larger Vinca settlements, surrounded by both Sleepers (who are wondering why this structure is now in the middle of their home) and the local Wise (who are wondering why this structure is now in the middle of their home AND are curious as to how it developed a Hallow overnight). Now our heroes have to learn to survive in a world untamed, bargaining with the locals, discussing the hows and shoulds and cans of altering history, and trying to figure out how they got here and if there are any ways to go back.

    •Vinca-To-Modern: The pcs are Wise who, after some scenes in their village to remind them of what the characters are about to lose, end up taking the wrong turn in a Time-Before ruin and end up wandering into a Hallow in a modern Consilium. The beginning of the chronicle would cover the adjustment period, as the pcs figure out the obvious aspects of what happened, make first contact with the locals, and learn the basics of fitting in in this brave new world (including a lingua franca that Sleepers can understand), preferably becoming embroiled in local shadow politics in the process. Then the story would move onto the living. Do the characters wish to rush to return home, or do they feel they and their people might benefit if they take their time? Can they even go home?

    •Vinca-and-Modern: A somewhat complex and convoluted idea on my part, I'll admit. It starts with the dual-chronicle idea, with players having one character in the Dark Era and another in the modern day. The twist being that the two groups are aware of each other somehow. Maybe they have an Artifact that allows them to send written thought-records at each other. Alternatively, maybe pair each character with another from a different era and player. When one set of characters sleep, they do not dream, but instead find themselves a disembodied voice in another Mage's head (still played by the same player). Sort of like what I've heard about how the Shadow is played in Wraith: the Oblivion, but without the mandate to make the other player's life harder. The first option seems like it'd be easier and require less bookkeeping, however.

                    A Campaign hack I've been thinking of....

                    Everyone wants Heroes. With the rise of global Fear and cries for something to change, the Mayan calendar slowly ticked on, in 2012 something Broke. The Flood of Fear and Hope reached a peak and summoned something, something that unleashed energies into the world that had previously only appeared in legends, Magic. Inspiration. Infernal and Divine. Feral and Industrial. These powers found and bonded to individuals and empowered them in ways that were Miraculous but Dire, with each of them came a Hunger, a Need. Those that used their new powers found themselves becoming something different then human.

                    The powers themselves are already in the World of Darkness... It's just a Way of having different Archetypes linked to them.
                    Divine Fire: Linked to Inventors and Scientists, allows them to make most sciences work even Theoretical ones. The Hunger Linked to them is the need for Knowledge and the Need to use their Science more and More... Consuming their own hopes and dreams.. Burning them out into a Husk of nothing but Madness and Knowledge. The only thing that keeps them Stable is Reason.
                    Spiritual Communion: Linked to Naturalists and the Deeply Religious. Allows for the calling of Miracles and communion with Nature. The Hunger for them is "Restoring the Natural Order" by placing humans as Prey or forcing them into Worship of the Spirits. The only thing keeping them Stable is Faith.
                    Deathless Harrowing: Linked with anyone who Works with the Dead or Dying, allows them to Communicate with ghosts and bind flesh. Their Hunger is slow, they feel the need to become closer to the dead and feed upon the living to let them learn what the Dead feel. The only thing keeping them Stable is Hope.

                    Their is more... But you get the Idea. Mixing Superheroics with Legends and Ancient myth while dealing with the horror of being something other then Human from now on.. and Not having a Culture to help you adapt to the new needs.

                    Light and Dark are two Sides of a Coin... Humanity is What Happens when it Lands on it's Edge.

                        Boston, present day. The world is mourning Great Britain, having mostly burned in a horrifying anti-establishment revolt years before. The City on the Hill has gotten quieter, more still, more safe. The cost of all that change is fear. There are things going bump in the night, and people are starting to catch on. The lot of you are summoned together by the Handler, an enigmatic man with the ashes of London smoldering in his mournful eyes. He seems to learn new things about you just by looking you over, and knows a lot about you on top of that. For instance, you have no allegiances, hardly any ties to your respective communities, and most importantly none of you seem to care about the disparate supernatural natures each other displays. He needs all that, and he has offers seemingly tailor-made for you, which you take. His eerie assistant who you swear has kuzdu vines laced through her hair when you see her out of the corner of your eye smiles mirthlessly at you as she demands your compliance with a rapier to your back gives you your marching orders, and sends you adventuring to your well-deserved deaths, and you will never deserve to take orders from this glorious man no matter how much he wants to trust you into the night. He will always trust me more than he will trust all of you. Do not forget that.

                        The changeling freehold in Waltham is scrambling for sanity. In some part, because the Master of the Spring Court has been murdered. Some, like the Master of the Summer Court, just think he was an idiot who is dumb enough to be dead. Others, like the Mistress of the Autumn Court, fear that the Winter King had him assassinated in efforts to delay the end of Winter and bring Boston to a grinding halt under a quilt of snow. All three opposing courts are preparing to engage in full-scale war to prevent the Unrelenting Blizzard that would come with Winter's absolute rule. However, the bigger part is that all of the Fae lords and ladies on the other side of Boston's region of the Hedge are very, messily dead. No one knows who or what did it, but everyone wonders what happens when something that obscenely potent wants to enter Boston from Arcadia....

                        Meanwhile, the vampire court erupting out of Boston's Financial District is on the look-out for a very specific vampire. He's 5'10", chalk white skin, wiry build, kind of a douche, smiles all the time, doesn't give a damn about the Masquerade, not in an eating-people-in-front-of-people kind of way but rather a "yo you heard of Mos Def I'm his cousin Mos Feratu haha holla" kind of way. The Prince (Nosferatu, grouchy, loves to read, irritated by anything that gets in the way of her reading, is also a secretly-terrifying power player who is rumored to have disappeared the last Prince and most of her political rivals in the course of a week) is superbly irritated by this. The problem is, whenever this guy's brought in front of the Court for a tribunal, he puts a spring in everyone's step. He's got people chuckling under their breath and whistling showtunes with him. What's more, the more ancient a vampire is, the more they're under his... thrall? It's not like he's actively whammying other Kindred, it's just that they seem to gain the capacity for happiness and joy when he's around. He makes vampire friends, and that is WEIRD. The Danse Macabre ceases to exist in his presence, and no one knows what to make of it. At least, until they're IN his presence, when it actually seems like a great idea all of the sudden! Soon, everyone is falling over themselves to get him for their own reasons. The Carthians want him to break Progeny hardcore and usher in a new vampiric golden age. The Ordo Dracul believe he has achieved Golconda and want to study him. And what's left of the local Invictus just want him stone-dead. Of course, he has other (really misguided) ideas, and he'd like your help.

                        Werewolves and sin-eaters have found reasons to work together in the middle of this madness. Packs and krewes get together and have long heartfelt talks about their hopes, dreams, and aspirations. They get some real bonding done! It's pretty great until all the Loci and Avernian Gates start snapping shut. Have the Pure come down from the White Mountains to ruin everyone's day? Are the local mages making some kind a power play? Or does it have something to do with the throng of stiffly-moving empty-eyed little ballet dancers, smelling strangely of crematory ash mixed with porcelain, who don't seem to have homes or souls?

                        The mages? Usually the novices all dress up like Gandalf the Gray at least a few times a month, crash at least one vampire club, and ironically dance like morons. The local Prince after all has banned directly interacting with mages down to making smirking eye contact, so it's a fun way for them to piss everyone in attendance off. But now, nothing. They've been keeping to themselves. That is probably bad. Your Handler recommends looking into it.

                        Other mysteries abound. What's with the shady pharmaceutical conglomerate buying up cursed condemned buildings, leveling them, and building nothing on top? Have all the mundane human crime organizations banded together to fight back against the things that hide in the shadows and screw with their money (hint: you will probably find out the hard way)? Who is the Gestalt, and why are there so many of him all over the city at once? What is the Screaming Tunnel? Does the terrifying Blood Sister of the Blue Hills Pack really think that macaroni art is the key to overcoming kuruth? Why hasn't the Mayor been seen outside of City Hall for the past two months?

                        All this and MORE, when I can finally get a group of four-to-five people I want to play with to sit down in a room at the same time every-other-week. :\

                                    I was considering something similar to this for Vampire; a coterie of disenfranchised down-and-out neonate outsiders reluctantly volunteers for an Ordo experiment in gleaning genealogical and personal insights from the characters' bloodlines (not Bloodlines) from close study of blood sympathy.

                                    It failed wildly at its intended goal. However, it has Butterfly Effect-ed their consciousnesses into their distant proto-sires Torpid bodies. When the characters' blood sympathy leads them back to one another, they pop home. From then on, whenever any one of them goes into Torpor, they all do, and find themselves back in their proto-sires' lives.

                                    The whole thing would revolutionize kindred culture and occultism if only the damn researcher could be convinced it was more than a shared past-life fantasy communicated to one another by accident.

                                    Also, the Fog of Ages makes a triumphant return as a literal fog that scents and hunts time travelers a la the Hounds of Tindalos. It gets persistently more aggressive if the characters start cross-contaminating timelines.

                                                I tend to be more of a 20 minutes into the future kind of guy as opposed to historical, but lately, I've got it in my head to try a rather interesting setting...


                                                [Any]
                                                Imagine an underwater city, somewhere in the heart of the Atlantic at the bottom of the depths. Founded via a coalition of government and businesses, this city was founded to ensure humanity's future would be a prosperous one. It is self sufficient and derives power from natural sources, growing a promising city-state beneath the waves within a decade.

                                                In here, people live about these massive spires of arcologies, each practically a city district in both size and function. Yet where there is people, the monsters aren't far behind. People think they're safe, unable to be harmed and seperate from the worries in dangers in top side. This model city of the future features advanced industries suited for underwater work and public transportation services, Artificial intelligence have been developed and are considered "free citizens" within its borders, and it pioneers in advanced robotics for industrial and security use as traditional vehicles were too limited in their use.
                                                Wi-Fi signals are broadcasted everywhere via hotspots, though independant and secure broadcasts, especially from the outside are unheard of. Space is surprisingly roomy in most public areas, but there's enough walls and service shafts to get lost anywhere and into the jaws of some creature.

                                                It seemed like a veritable city of tommorrow, but the crime rates are still so high and poverty just seems like it is everywhere. People blame smuggling. The truth is, they built a cage... and locked themselves inside.
                                                Vampires still draw blood, Werewolves still claim territory, Mages still seek mysteries, and other stranger things lurk in the walls and ventilation. There might be robotic drones and security cameras keeping watch, but not enough of them everywhere and anyone with the right knowhow can basically remove them from the equation.
                                                Even worse, there's rumors around the city that its inception was not some noble goal but the hinting of something far darker and more sinister....
