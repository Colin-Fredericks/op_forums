                    Professor Phobos I see where you are coming from, really, and I wouldn't mind seeing a hack like that making Mage a different Horror game, but I still disagree with the notion that Mage is not a Horror game, or that Magic is not dangerous/scary.
                    We've covered the former extensively, so here's why for the latter: the magic itself needn't be the source of the horror in the first place, despite Ken Hite's opinion. The usage of it is plenty source for horror, already.
                    Which is why I don't think it fits in a horror setting, because it's just not black enough magic to be That Old Black Magic.
                    I disagree: occasionally fraying reality, calling down monstrosities from beyond, potentially even contributing to the Abyss's growth in power (The Guardians had this belief that Vulgar magic strenghtens the Lie, right?)... those things make it very fitting for a Horror setting. But most of all, the Horror of the magic comes from the ease of its use! Being able to rewrite minds makes it so easy to give in and make people think the way I want them to. After all, there is little incentive in stopping me, right`? It encourages the theme of Hubris, sure, but also makes using Magic for horrific means much easier. "I need to sacrifice a goat and a small child's right thumb for this spell... you know what, on second thought, I'll just go shoot him!" or "This might make me crazy, is it really worth it?" vs. "What an annoying twat! I'll make him forget his college years. Let's see how you'll do without that PhD, prick!" or even "Hey, hey! See that skater over there? Watch this! *imitates Force Push, guy falls down* Ahahahaha! Aw man, the look on his face!" Both are valid Horror scenarios, albeit very, very different.

                    Being able to level cities with little trouble, burn down buildings on accident and guarantee I never have to go to work via winning small lottery tickets/finding wallets etc., covering my daily expenses... those things remove me from humanity, sure, but they are so damn easy. Temptively easy, really. Why bother doing things the old-fashioned way?
                    After all, spells are reliable- most of the time. But if something works most of the time, and only in 0,1% of the time goes really bad, and you fling this stuff around ten times a day, how long does it take for something to go really bad? On average, about 4 months. And this is generous: most mages are likely to use magic more than ten times a day; it is, after all, so very reliable. Especially those actively fighting, like Arrows. Thing is, you won't see it coming or expect it, BECAUSE it has been so reliable. It will most likely fail in a very unfortunate moment, too. Ever went into a situation with a tool, confident you can handle it thanks to that tool, then have the tool fail you? Now imagine this could be life-threatening, soul-shattering, reality-ripping or destiny-fraying.
                    Imagine you relize you don't really know how to deal with situations outside of using your magic anymore.
                    And even if it works as intended: there are so many, many horrible things done through magic, even on accident or through a misunderstanding.
                    Is this not your brand of Horror? That is fine. But it is horror.

                    Regarding black magic that would make you scared to use it even when you're about to die: It helps with a certain atmosphere, but I wouldn't want to play it. There would be little to no incentive to invest in something like that, imo. Unless you make it so that it's the only way to face certain threats, and then we're simply in Cthulhoid territory. Works, sure. Horrifying? Yes.
                    But anyone who says a Merlin having to face a Morrigan is not horrifying sees the world through a different lense than I do. Their magic is beautiful, and not scary in and of itself, but in what it can do and how it can be used. If two basically Demi-gods slug it out, I do not want to be anywhere near them. Preferably, not even in that plane of existence. As was already mentioned: Power difference like that is freaking scary. People flinging around high power like that, without any real merit as to why they got it is like arming every other jack on the streets with military-grade weaponry.

                    This is mostly incoherent and lacking a thread since it is late, I apologize for that. G'night.

                                        I somehow feel compelled to reply to this thread because I had so many crossovers with my players over the years that these kinds of pondering and evaluating various splats is my bread and butter. I speak not only from the theory but from the practice too and actual play and game sessions.

                                        Before I start giving some advice (hoping they could be useful for u) some considerations :

                                        1) 1vs 1, white room scenarios, pvp etc... ARE NOT BALANCED scenarios. What does this means? It simply means that some characters and some supernatural creatures are weaker or stronger than others in a straight up challenge on the street. Period. No beating around the bushes, a mastigos can insta kill any enemy with mind domination, and an acanthus can spoil and ruin entire game sessions with deus ex machina shifting sands.

                                        2) GLOBAL scenarios and scenarios where strategy, time and resources are involved, allies influence etc.. CAN be balanced with planning.
                                        Notice the emphasis on the can factor and I didn't use must.

                                        Simply put, where others might shine in sheer potency and might, maybe others shine in.. Deceiving and numbers. Keep in mind that the only games I really know are the main three, vampire mage and werewolf. I tend to focus on these because in my setting usually other splats are not that common or are politically irrelevant to the crossover (I know I might be missing out and other games are fantastic but it was a choice we made as a group, pronetheans and beasts Geist etc... are weird and rare creatures that are very reclusive dangerous and misterious to find)


                                        That having been said, I will try to give my best two cents. I had to master a battle between a local coterie and a small cabal of mages and I had to think about ways to interfere with mages affair and how that war would play out. Here are some thoughts on vampires and wolves specifically that can help u balance settings:

                                        -in my games, vampires have the political, mortal strings of anything in the city, and they can and will use it to their advantage_even when we are talking about ordo Dracul weirdos, Lance a sanctum knights etc.. I tend to portray the undead society with that feeling of omnipotence and manipulation over entire cities political entities and authorities. Police, newspapers, politicians, mafia mobs... Anything can be bought used and corrupted to attack the group. Only elder mages in the silver ladder or seers can hope to compete with the kind of influence a political coterie can pull with small effort. If u go at war with vampires and they know about it, u will face tens of ghouls armed, police and corrupted investigators, hunters and the entire human society in the city. They will try to ruin ur reputation or make up accuses to make u a terrorist or a wanted criminal. They will hunt and arrest kill or kidnap parents friends and lovers.. Or maybe turn them into ghoul.

                                        -vampires have the numbers. In my setting mages usually are rare and cabals are smaller than coteries or covenant. Even when they are at peace, vampires have higher rates of recruitment than mages. The city is full of ghouls and servants, elite characters who are ready to be embraced if necessary and are not simply cannon fodder they start as good vampire characters like players would do. If they have to resort to it even the Prince will start embrace new candidates to fight the wizards menace. Usually the vampires outnumbers the mages and will outnumber them if they have to fight a long war with small losses. Usually I have vampires outnumber mages 3 to 1.

                                        -"the enemy of my enemy...."_ let's be honest, do u think that if out there people crazy powerful like mages walked the night , social predators like vampires wouldn't know how to deal with them during the centuries? Vampires are not stupid and will have contacts, strings and allies in the awakened society. If they have to face a cabal, they will know someone who know someone .... And will find another cabal, order or crazy sclelestus abyssal mage that can be useful to them. Also they might as well have some mage dominated enslaved or straight up open allied with them. Vampires can be formidable manipulators and can grant u many many favors. After all, even the most hardened adamantine arrow can fall in love with a daeva innocent young girl....
                                        In practical terms, vampires can access to allies, be it wolves, mages or entities that can counter the mages power. Usually in my city they are allied with the seers and will call them in case of assistance.

                                        -if vampires have to face mages, I give a small buff and emphasis on blood magic, occult and lore. What does it mean? Occult is very powerful, and the most alien and Witchery covenants and coteries will have knowledge on how to counter magic and other powers. I also allow for scales, rituals, sorcery and devotions that can imitate or counter specific awakened magic. Like a theban sorcery or devotion combo that can allow for a clash of wills against an acanthus trying to predict the future of the coterie, or open a scrying window... Thing is, I don't downplay occult lore, I enhance it and use it as a powerful vampire tool.
                                        Those weird undead might possess primitive blood magic, but it can be used against awakened magic! (shock on mages)

                                        -obfuscate can overpower even mage sight, remember it folks. Spies and assassins can cut mages throat on the street without even them realizing. And vampires will never ever play it fair.

                                        Small appendix on our fur friends called werewolves :

                                        -honestly werewolves are pretty strong in a fight, and when they fall into rage they can even resist mind control and activate devastating power at will without essence. Ina straight up fight without preparation they are very scary.

                                        -I really try to put the emphasis on the fact that wolves aren't simply brutes ignorant who go around biting enemies, they are strong cohesive apex predator who work as a Navy seal team and a family or tribe. They will use smart tactics, spirits as fodder cannons and careful study of the situation and the prey before acting. Don't just throw them around like wild animals.


                                        -small factor that I usually showcase_ "dear thyrsus, u might have the theory and the numbers but ithaeurs and bone shadows have the blood" what does it mean?
                                        Wolves will enjoy contacts, allies and spirit influences that other mages can only dream of or reach only if they are very powerful. They enjoy spiritual connection and affinity for the umbra that few thyrsus can match. Totem wise, a pack is very powerful.

                                        -don't ever underestimate spirits. Influence can be literally anything. A spirit with the concept of time or fate can counter the abilities of an acanthus , and a psychiatry or mental sickness spirit can protect and counter even the worst nightmare of a mastigos power. If the wolves understand the basic of the powers they are facing, they will seek for a spirit ally that will counter those powers and challenge them in their influence.



                                        I know that these advices and little changes won't change the overall power difference between single individuals, but they can change the overall tone of the setting, and maybe can help u reevaluate other splats capability and resources, and feel less the awakened menace.
