    Investigating to find a Breakthrough

    You don't need a lot of Clues to achieve a Breakthrough. What you do need is for every Clue to be Tagged as “Mind-Blowing”, i.e. a Breaking Point trigger. “The character witnesses something traumatic, terrifying, or that rattles his understanding of the world.”* (emphasis mine). A discovery that doesn't threaten the mad scientist's grasp on reality isn't enough to provide the paradigm shift needed for a Breakthrough; and Breaking Points as an integral part of the discovery process is part of why the premise is horrific.

    More thoughts in a bit.

    * Chronicles of Darkness, p.29

    My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                Here's my thoughts in a nutshell - you're just remaking another Mage variation by focusing entirely on the scientists. And you're going to run into the same problem as Genius, constantly butting its head against the same themes as Mage, just with a different power set. Just... why? Why focus on just some variation of the obsessive doctor Frankenstein? Why limit yourself in that fasion?

                                At minimum, I honestly think that a game of occult science* should be more broad that that. Simply by tapping into the whole archetype from just a single season of Buffy the Vampire Slayer, we have sceintists (who experiment on themselvs!), but you also have your Creations, you have enhanced humans, you have genetically bred hybrids, you have crazy machines that do impossible things, plus we should always have some talking heads / brains in jars. Currently, in the CofD, we have five different games tapping into the idea of occult science - Mage, Promethean, Hunter (a few of the conspiracies), Demon and the new Deviant. These are all scattered around and focusing on one aspect of the whole supernatural science theme.

                                If you are really keen on making a game of occult science, I fully believe that the game should combine all these things into one. Really dig into the entire breadth of the thing. You can play as both the crazy guy who made the advanced robot, the robot itself, and the enhanced human assistant / Igor archetype.

                                * I think occult sciences sounds a lot better than "mad." Its been in a few threads that people are uncomfortable with the ableism that the term egenders, so I'm sticking with this instead.

                                    Uh, I kind of disagree? For one thing, I'm getting the idea that this is more of minor template, low-powered thing. So that right there differentiates them from Mages or Geniuses.

And with this idea, it doesn't make sense for the opposite reason: because it would disrupt the focus of this project.

                                        Honestly, my aim is for “minor template” to be a bit of a stretch; these mad scientists/geniuses/whateveryou want to call them are a “minor template” in the same sense that hunters are a minor template. There's a reason why I chose to title this as “Mad Science” instead of “Mad Scientist”, and why I described the difference between mortal, hunter, and mad scientist solely in terms of how they react to encountering the supernatural. (Also, I'm not going to back off of “Mad”; “Mad Science” is a horror staple, and I'm not going to shy away from that terminology just because it's not politically correct these days.)

                                        Likewise, I have no objection to dealing with modified people and creations as well as the guys who do the science and engineering. The modified people would be more like tier 3 hunters d their Endowments than like deviants by default, and the creations would be more like the Imbued from World of Darkness: Antagonists than Prometheans. It's all Mad Science, after all. It's just that I haven't gotten that far yet.

                                        I was already thinking that Innovations might take three forms: Mods, Feats, and Devices. In a nutshell, Mods alter what you are (“To Be”); Feats alter what you do (“To Do”), and Devices change what you possess (“To Have”). Everything I've talked about regarding Innovations prior to this post have actually been about Feats.

                                        Mods involve a subject (human or animal) who gets altered by an Innovation, who picks up the Mod as a Supernatural Merit; Feats are what the scientists and engineers develop, and what I've been describing as Specialty-like traits that attach to and expand the character's skills; and Devices are Equipment that incorporates Breakthrough technology. There's likely to be a fair amount of overlap between Mods and Devices, with AI and automation Breakthroughs allowing for the possibility of Devices that can think and act on their own to some extent. Feats are at the core of this, as creating a Device or installing a Mod requires the use of a Feat.

                                        I see Mods and Devices working a bit like Hunter Endowment R&D and Reliquary Relics, but with powers and curses you'd expect from weird science rather than mysticism; and I see Feats working a lot like the Skill Tricks from World of Darkness: Mirrors. Unobtainium is tied to the use of Feats, not Mods or Devices, which means that you need Unobtainium to create Devices or install Mods, but (usually) not to use the Devices and Mods — which is not to say that Devices and Mods don't need some sort of fuel to work. Mods are usually fueled by Willpower, while Devices are usually fuelled by something like Source (from the Gutter Magic system in Witch Finders).

                                        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                Before I do that, let me remind everybody that since this is Mad Science, the Mods and Devices should always have some sort of drawback serving as a counterpoint to their benefits; something like a Mod that takes a toll on the host's health, or a Device that pollutes the environment. Without that, this could very easily get into territory that's more suited to the Trinity Continuum, flavor-wise.

                                                Basically, every Innovation that you introduce via Mad Science should require that most dangerous of questions to be asked and answered: “What could go wrong?” This is on the meta-level: the inventor or engineer doesn't have to ask the question*; but the player and/or the Storyteller should. And the Innovation's drawbacks should be chosen in accordance with that answer.

                                                * though he can, and can try to take steps to safeguard against the answer, which in turn triggers a new round of “what can go wrong?”, this time with the safeguard…

                                                        On the earlier “what can go wrong?” point, I'd like to set it up to encourage players to come up with “double-edged sword” inventions, where the drawbacks are built around the same statement that the effects are built around, differing only in terms of whether you're looking at the core statement as an optimist or a pessimist.

                                                        For instance, a treatment that isolates the subject's emotions. The Benefit: your emotions are isolated, so attempts to influence you take serious penalties, in dice pool reductions and/or additional Doors. The Drawback: your emotions are isolated, so you have trouble empathizing with others and you take penalties when attempting to influence them. The treatment itself would be a Feat (probably Medicine-based) which installs a Mod in the subject that alters his psychological makeup; and the Unobtainium for the Feat might be some sort of exotic mind-altering chemicals.

                                                        Or instead of “isolates”, try “controls”: the benefits and drawbacks are the same, the former because it's harder to influence someone who's in complete control of his emotions and the latter because complete control means no spontaneity and a lack of a sense of genuineness in your social interactions that tends to be off-putting.
