    When Worlds of Darkness Collide!

    09-29-2016, 04:25 PM
    (Notice the lack of "vs" in the title. This is NOT meant to be a "fight thread", although if you all really WANT to debate such things, I'm game!)

    Opening Premise: Some sort of Wyld storm occurs, making the impossible happen. The two separate worlds of darkness mix and merge. As improbable as it sounds, this is a stable union, that somehow preserves cosmologies that would normally be considered incompatible (IOW, this is pure Handwavium, meant to preempt arguments that only one set of Mages' powers should work, etc, which would not be in the spirit of the thread's intent).

    How do you think the various Supernaturals would react on meeting their counterparts? Would Requiem Kindred and Cainites easily find common cause? Would Garou and Uratha recognize each other as werewolves? Could the Traditions fit the Pentacle into their Paradigm, and is the world big enough for the Seers and the Technocracy?

    From Mummies to Demons to Changelings, who has the most impact on the others' culture and how might they grow from here?

Ability to deal with normal conscience not included.

                              I assume that the Cainte would look at the Kindred like how the Kindred look at the Wicked Dead- something strange and terrifying which doesn't play by the rules. The Strixs would be, undoubtedly, be connected to the Dark Mother- and as such they may even claim that the Kindred are abominations made by Lilith as adversary to Cain's children (depends on how religious they are). If anything, I would imagine that the Cainte would try to close themselves from the All Night Society, seeing their practices and abilities as both familiar and strange. The Kindred, on the other hand, would have a lot less problem to accept the new vampires- they are more human than most of the Wicked Dead, after all, although they would not want to enter their mad political games and the Jihad their Blood Gods are playing.

                              The Uratha would see the Gauro and the rest of the Fera with suspicion, as they see every other weird shapeshifter. They would think that Fomori, Drones, Gorgons and Kami are either forms of Hosts or Claimed, and perhaps even some of the Fera (like the werecrows). The Gauro may have a lot harsher view of the Uratha- while they are not tainted by either of the Triad, they are not the children of Gaia but bastards of the Moon Goddess and Wolf. Even further, they have destroyed their own Totem, which must have weakened the Emerald Mother, creating some grudge between them. The Pure may be more accepted among the Gauro, especially Lupus. We are talking about a group who once butchered their own kind- they'll have little qualms attacking the Forsaken, not to mention other shifters (the Unclean would probably be seen as Wyrm spawn). How do you mix the two spirit worlds together I don't know.

                              Awakening and Ascension could work quit well together, since their cosmologies are quit different- Awakening Mages already accept that there are weird forms of magic in the world, while Ascension Mages would just claim the former to not practice True Magick but some variant using pacts and soul bindings with primordial beings. Dreaming and Lost Changelings would also work pretty well together as two separate cosmologies, although just try to explain a Lost that you are a faerie in human body without her trying to impale you on cold iron. The Dreaming may be tied to the Skein or the Primordial Dream, with the Kithian being "neighbors" of the Begotten. Reckoning and Vigil Hunters share nothing in common, with the former may treat the latter as "supporting cast" or potential threat (Cheiron would, undoubtedly, rip a Reckoning Hunter to shreds in order to make new taumatech). Geist, Orpheus and Wraith may work well together as each taking a different niche (ghosts, projectors and mediums), but like Werewolf, you'll have to solve the "two Underworlds" problem. Perhaps using the Lower Depths or some other layer of reality. The Arisen would undoubtedly claim the Amenti to be a form of Lifeless- broken souls of ancients who tried to mimic the Rite of Return, although they are not tainted by the Devourer. The Amenti, on the other hand, would see the former as ancient and terrible beings which carry the shadow of Apep, or perhaps reborn gods of the old. Anyway, they would recognize them as beings older than they are and, like anyone else in the setting, would do their best to stay way from them. The Fallen and the Unchained would also share little in common- the Fallen may think that the God Machine is a creation of Lucifer or one of his generals (or even an Earthbound by itself) which is used to rebel against God and cut away His/Her power. They would look down at the Unchained as "mockery" of both angels and demons. The Unchained, however, may have great interest in the Fallen as a tool to use against the Machine or some path to Hell. Of course, the Hell they come from may not be the Hell the other wish to live in. The Kui Jin would be another form of "Wicked Dead" for both the Cainte and the Kindred.

                              And sad little Promethean is all alone, with no one to play with- everyone would hate them as usually, and the Fallen may be even more terrified from their qasmalim than anything else in the setting. The Fallen would also look down ion the demons of Inferno (taints of human sin and passion are nothing compared the true fallen angels, in their opinion). Gypsy and Changeing Breeds would become true soul mates, as no one else would like to play with them :P

                              Hope anything of that has helped!

                              (I'm a bit tired, so I may expand things a bit after some sleep)

                                  Mage and Changeling cosmologies are surprisingly complementary along the WoD/ChroD divide:

                                  Awakening's mages would eventually end up referring to the two types as Supernal Mages (themselves) and Phenomenal Mages (their Ascension counterparts, who manipulate the FallenPhenomenal world directly, without recourse to Supernal symbols). For their part, the Ascension mages would view their Awakening counterparts as a more splintered variant of the Order of Hermes. The Technocracy and the Seers would likely despise each other: to the Technocracy, the Seers are everything they fear and loathe about Superstitions; and to the Seers, the Union is a rival to their own Pantechnicon that shows no fealty to the Exarchs.

                                  You could merge the two versions of Changeling by treating them as flip sides of the same coin: where ChroD's changelings are humans who were abducted to Arcadia and eventually escaped after being changed by the ordeal, WoD's changelings are Fae who were exiled from Arcadia and forced to adapt to a hostile world. From the Lost perspective, that sounds an awful lot like a Charlatan…

                                  That said, I'll reserve final judgment until CtL2e and CtD20 are both out: both promise significant changes from their respective prior editions.
