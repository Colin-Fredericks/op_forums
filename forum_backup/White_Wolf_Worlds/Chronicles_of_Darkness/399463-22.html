                Diggs The Threshold/Archetype situation always bothered me. Thresholds did nothing for you besides give your starting Keys. After that, there was no benefit. No curse, no blessing, no cost reductions. At best if you extrapolate you could regain Plasm by invoking your Threshold, but there were no rules for it [to bring up my homebrew, skirting Death allowed you to roll to regain Plasm; doing it related to your Threshold added Psyche to the roll]. Archetypes were worse. Ostensibly they let you regain plasm and in very narrow situations they let you use a Manifestation for free, but in practice they're nothing but fluff and have no actual purpose [Again with my homebrew: I replaced them with Wake and Epitaph, Anchors based on how you relate to the dead and how you use your second chance at life, which were mechanically the same as Mask/Dirge].

                                                Originally posted by Robert D View Post
                                                On a different note, I recall lots of nicknames given to Sin-Eaters in various other lines' books (Hunter, Dark Eras, Changeling Tokyo preview in the Changeling Lost forum): Fogmen, Risen Dead, etc.) Do Sin-Eaters refer to themselves by any other names besides the Bound or Sin-Eaters?                                                 "Bound" is the general term for "people who accepted the Bargain and returned from death mystically bonded to a geist."

                                                "Sin-Eater" is a specific term for those Bound who fall into the player character-suitable splats.

                                                To continue using the Werewolf analogy, it's like how "Uratha" means all werewolves and "Forsaken" means "this specific group the game is built around playing."

                                                Those are the only names in general use in the core book--though, as you say, sometimes in particular places or Dark Eras the locals might have different terminology. "Krewe," for example, only became associated with the Bound and their religion in the 1920s, during a major resurgence of Sin-Eater culture around New Orleans, and it wasn't particularly widely used outside the Americas until decades later.
