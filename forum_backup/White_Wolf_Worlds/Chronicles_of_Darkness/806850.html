                                  Cool. Well, to make it look a bit more like a merit:

                                  Abjurism (• to •••••)
                                  Prerequisite: Septemi
                                  Your character is trained in the ways of Abjurism, the techniques developed by the bloodline's founder for focusing the supernatural potency of the Blood. With it, you can not only better protect yourself against supernatural powers inflicted upon you by others, but extend your own will outward, suppressing or even dissipating existing magic.
                                  This merit provides two benefits, one passive and one active. The passive effect, often referred to as Buttressing the Soul, provides a bonus equal to the merit's rating to any dice pool your Septemi uses to contest a supernatural power targeting them. If there is no contested dice pool, it instead causes a penalty equal to the rating to the originator's activation pool, even if there's normally no resistance allowed. The benefit works against any type of power, from the Disciplines, Devotions, and Blood Magic of other Kindred, to the Arcana of Mages, the Numina of ephemeral entities, and anything else of a supernatural origin.
                                  The active benefit, sometimes referred to as Breaking the Weave, allows the Septemi to suppress supernatural powers (again, of any kind) that he's aware of for a length of time equal to that shown in the chart below, according to your rating in Abjurism. This requires that a willpower be spent, an instant action be taken, and a Clash of Wills made against the user or originator of the targeted effect, with Abjurism replacing the usual Discipline rating, and the maximum suppressed duration providing a bonus as if it were the effect's duration. If the Septemi wins the Clash of Wills, the effect is suppressed for the duration listed on the following chart, effectively disappearing for that period of time, its effects no longer working. If the effect's duration is equal to or lower than the maximum listed below, the effect is instead dissipated entirely, as if its duration suddenly ended.
                                  Merit Dots Suppression Duration
                                  • One turn per dot of Blood Potency
                                  •• One scene
                                  ••• One chapter (or 1 day per dot of Blood Potency)
                                  •••• One story (or 1 month)
                                  ••••• Permanent
                                  With two dots, the Possess manifestation can be ended using the same system. Three dots allows the Septemi to attempt to end a Fetter on a living being, ignoring the normal duration. Five dots can allow the Septemi to attempt to end the Claim condition on a living being, although this is treated as if the entity purposely detached itself, with the normal trouble for the host it causes. Similarly, five dots can potentially end a Stryx's Possess Living/Kindred/Revenant embodiments, although it won't affect Synthesis. All other manifestations and embodiments, including the listed ones against inanimate objects, use the normal systems for suppressing effects, although the Twilight Form manifestation and the Shadow Form embodiment cannot be suppressed, nor can similar 'default' capabilities.
                                  Abjurism cannot be used to disenchant supernatural objects, but it can target any effect generated by one. In the case of a singular "always active" effect generated by an object getting suppressed, the effect can be 'restarted' by the user by paying a point of willpower and using an instant action to do so.


                                  What do you think?

                                  EDIT: And to note, I do like the idea of them getting Auspex as their fourth discipline; it'd help them discover the effects they're working against.
