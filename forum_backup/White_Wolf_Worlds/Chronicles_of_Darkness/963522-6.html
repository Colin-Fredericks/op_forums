                        Originally posted by Michael Kenner View Post

                        I'm a big believer in informed consent for all medical or psychological practice. The only exception, which should be used very rarely and even then only justifying the minimal intervention necessary, is if it can be established that the person is incapable of making decisions themselves for whatever reason.                         All Sleepers are by definition incapable of making informed choices about magic. That's where they fall down - and recognise that they fall down.

                        The Ladder's attitude to people they think act against their scheme is interesting and illuminating - if a Sleeper is ruining the lives of those they have misplaced authority over, the Ladder prefers to show them the error of their ways by arranging positive reinforcement. The bad boss gets hooked by a taste of whatever they're lacking (say, membership in a club, or something in their life fixed) and then some advice from an Illuminate (a NonAwakened member of one of the Order's mystery cults) - if they follow the advice, they get more good things. Eventually, the situation is fixed in a virtuous circle without the illuminate - or the mage behind her - needing to play an active role any more, and the now-good boss becomes an illuminate. And the Ladder looks for the next dysfunctional system to repair.

                        This only applies to Sleepers, because Sleepers don't know any better and can't be expected to without guidance (though those that do are praiseworthy). Mages, though... A tyrannical Hierarch will enjoy the Ladder trying to advise them into better systems for a while, but eventually the Order will take Steps. Even if they're in the Ladder. Especially if they're in the Ladder.
