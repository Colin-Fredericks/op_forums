      After a bit of research, it can be thought that perhaps these five horsemen are using the name of the Apocalyptic figures and are not so the original Horsemen as sealed in the first of the Seven Seals that seal 'The Book', as spoken of in the Book of Revelation. I say this mainly as we know that when the fifth seal was broken, it caused the martyrs to cry for the word of God rather than unleash a fifth Horseman.
      It is likely the Five Horsemen of Geist (as in the line, not saying they are specifically Geister) took their names from this quintessentially apocalyptic heralding group. Wikipedia has quite an extensive page on the Seven seals. Them getting mentioned in the Testament of Longinus is interesting, as whilst taken as Kindred mythos, it goes and mentions something linking only to Geist, which would err me to think not only did the Horsemen at least exist at one point, but it was around the time of Christ's death. The Horsemen therefore are at least some powerful Yellowbones somewhere quite deep in the Underworld past Rivers enough that likely a living Sin-Eater could never reach them and/or likely either Geist or Kerberoi to exist in the modern age.

          Having them be geists, I think, actually works rather well, as geists are tied to a type of death. Nitpicky, but they're more likely to be rank 7, though. Imperial Mysteries theorizes that the Deathlords are rank 7 or 8, each ruling over a type of death, and are possible exiled gods of Stygia. According to some of Dave B's posts on the subject, rank 7 are "spirits of a species or tribal totems", rank 8 are spirits which "rule a world, like Father Wolf, Luna, and Helios." So I'd guess the Horsemen to be the rank 7, and Death itself (if there is such an entity) to be rank 8.

          As I said, them being geists makes a lot of sense. But. Geists were at some point human ghosts, according to the popular theory, and the Thresholds are much older than humanity [with the possible exception of War]. Horseman might be human-like avatars of the thresholds (geists), but the archetypes of the thresholds themselves I'd characterize as much older and much more alien. Perhaps "geists", but born from creatures that far predate humanity; perhaps spirits which became warped into Underworld-ly beings; perhaps Stygian symbols [although I shy away from using mage-centric explanations, especially in other gamelines].

          On a similar adjacent note, because I am a history geek, I would propose that the Horsemen have made bids in the past to become the True Death several times in the past: Chance with the KT extinction, and Famine with the Permian extinction, and perhaps War with some of the conflicts this century [or perhaps War's attempt is soon to come . . . ] Gambles pitted against whatever other god-like archetypes guide evolution. [oooh, pretty please tease an idea like that in Beast?]

          Then again, I'm pretty influenced by Cosmos. World of Darkness is very closely tied to human experience. Things from before the Gauntlet or the Fall are increasingly vague and non-consequential; what Things Were Like, from a supernatural perspective, even a few million years ago are too distant to have much impact (with a few rare exceptions, like nods to dinosaur-predator magath).

                  Could it be possible that these Horsemen be the same as the much rumoured Deathlords of the Underworld? Whilst the occasional Sin-Eater may have a vision of the Horseman of their Threshold, nobody has ever seen a Deathlord though their existance is passed on as a kind of myth through the Underworld... Much like the Horsemen get treated by the Bound... Both by their general positions would likely be Rank 7 ghosts/geister. They could also be Cthonians if we're talking about creatures that are older than humanity and personify a Threshold of Death. I've heard some talk that people think the Judges of Duat and the Deathlords may be one and the same however...

                  The Geist corebook does mention that spirits can become Geist if they merge with a ghost, same as a spirit could eat a human ghost and form a geist that way (and possibly vice versa if a ghost felt the need to eat a spirit...). I'd really have to go back to the books and re-check them over for more thoughts, especially if they could have been Geist and who they'd have been Bound with... The other thoughts to think of are if they never struck a Bargain; why not? Also if they can Bind themselves to make a Sin-Eater, then that means it's possible for a Sin-Eater to be about in modern time holding one of the Horsemen in them, if not having the Horsemen themselves form their own krewe... Is this an example of one of the lost Tier 3 Conspiracy krewes?

                  I feel perhaps the Horsemen are something more than mere human ghost; They personify the powers each type of Threshold have and could even be the original source of such powers... You could even say they were the first Geister... Their own keystones holding the two keys that each Threshold holds as their affinities. I'd say if they had keystones, they would either be the symbols of their Thresholds or a ghostly-steed that is their colour. I'd like to say through their initial teachings the first geists came from them. Though I see a ghost/spirit forming into a geist as more accidental and based on circumstance and a rarity than these beings going about the Underworld and showing ghosts how to do it... There's something more primal about the formation of geists being a side-effect of spirits and ghosts being able to slither from the Underworld and Shadow, along with the general swapping Anchors for Influences methods mentioned in the Geist core...
