    Naughty PC's and the Guardians: When do the GOTV step in, and what do they do?

    12-03-2016, 09:10 AM
    Of all the Orders, how the Guardians of the Veil operate is one of the fuzzier concepts I have, and were I to ST, I would like to know how the operate especially as it pertains to the PC's.

    First of all, what kinds of activities bring the attention of the Guardians?
    Paradox? Does it matter whether the paradox is contained released?
    How often would someone have to tempt paradox before the Guardians took interest?
    What about the Lex Magica? The Guardians seem to be more concerned about Wisdom than the Lex, but it seems like any action they might take would preferably be within the Lex... It's kind of hard to justify taking action against a mage, if by the Lex they've done no wrong.

    Then, how do the Guardians find out? Obviously they aren't omniscient, so it's not as though they are going to swoop down like Batman out of nowhere the moment the PC's get out of line. But at the same time, they ARE mages, and MIGHT be scrying on the PC's, or they might not. How do you determine whether the PC's are being watched by the Guardians?

    What if one of the PC's is a Guardian, but is lying down on the job?

    And when the Guardians do come down on the PC's, how will they do it? Will they kindly ask them to stop? Bring the Lex down on them? Try to teach the PC a lesson? Take the PC on a long walk down a short pier in concrete shoes?

    And as a Storyteller, how do you balance the conflicting philosophies of: "The players' actions should have consequences" versus "Don't try to punish your players" while still being true to the setting and the presence of the Guardians of the Veil?

      The Guardians of the Veil are the secret police of Atlantis, so the legend goes, and that's about the role they try and fulfill. In their ideal world, the Guardians are omniscient; watching their fellow Awakened (including each other) for signs of moral failure and correcting it, with Wise advice if possible and long knives if not. Their fellow Awakened hate them out of ritual necessity, for none can be allowed to love their duty, but accept them and thank them for their grim service.

      In practice, pretty much none of these things are true.

      The expectation a neophyte might have of the Guardians is that they're sitting in their hideouts all day, scrying other Awakened, tracking them, building webs of information. The solution to a nail in the mind of all unexperienced Awakened is the magical hammer. In practice, though, this is a terrible way to collect information on fellow Awakened because they can all sense when magic is being cast upon them. Not to mention that you need sympathetic connection to them which is harder than it sounds to get. And on top of that, odds are good that your scrying window isn't going to catch anything heinous because you can't really maintain it 24/7; you've got your own shit to do if nothing else.

      Instead, Guardians have to rely on tools other than their magic (at least in a direct manner) to get information on their fellow Awakened. This means relying on contacts and omens as signs of danger.

      Your average Guardian has a roster of Awakened to spy on provided by their Caucus, on whom they maintain dossiers (usually mentally; writing things down is a dangerous risk). To build those dossiers, they need to rely on the aforementioned sources.

      Contacts are people - Awakened or not; sometimes even stretching the definition of "person" - whom the Guardian can rely on for information on the target for one reason or another. No Guardian relies on a single contact unless circumstances prevent otherwise, because every contact is in some way compromised: The closer they are to the target, the less likely they are to be unbiased, and vice versa. At the top level of unreliable but close, they might be other Mages from the target's Caucus or cabal. More distant, they might be members of the Mage's cult (if they have one), or spirits they deal with regularly. At the most distant level, you might have people who hang out in the same places as the Mage or who live near them or even just the squirrels that live in the trees outside their sanctum.

      Contacts can also be spies. Sleepwalkers or young apprentices chosen to spy on a target on certain nights, or spirits bound by spells into following and observing. The latter are great when dealing with Moros because the odds of them being detected is pretty much nil.

      Omens, meanwhile, are signs and portents that aren't obviously connected to the unAwakened but which to the mind of a Mage bear ill tidings. For example, if the trees around the Thyrsus's sanctum start getting sick, or a lot of not-so-nice spirits are seen skulking around the neighbourhood, the Guardian knows something is up and can begin a more thorough investigation. Omens are the characteristic signals in the world left by dark deeds; the ripples made by evil. They might also just be changes in the target's behaviour. A strongly religious Mage suddenly not going to temple anymore, for example.

      No omen is proof of malefica but they're good warning signs to start paying closer attention.

      Once a Guardian's radar is pinged by a contact reporting something unusual or an ill omen marking their radar, then they start to look closer. That's when they might start to break the Lex for the sake of their curiosity; stalking the target in the target's own territory; breaking into a sanctum to rifle through their belongings; using spells to scry the target or sneak into their Oneiros, etc.

      This is the important point because Guardians aren't just watching you constantly with their scrying windows; they're looking for unusual behaviour, deviations from the norm, etc. It's once those behaviours get strange enough or are tied to odd enough coincidences (the Counselor started to go to a new basement bar two weeks before the attempt on the Hierarch's life; coincidence?) that the Guardian decides to act.

      A Guardian is not there when a Mage causes a Paradox, typically, or commits any unWise magical act. Instead, they look for the telltale signs; the odd behaviours caused by a Bedlam; the dire omens caused by an Anomaly; the raw destruction caused by a Havoc. Then they track the strangeness back to its source.

      Once that happens, it's time to give some advice.

          What all of my previous post means in actual gameplay is that your PCs can be targeted by the Guardians whenever you feel it is justified, as strange omens and informants on their own behaviours draw a Guardian or two to their doorstep. They might not even have actually done anything unWise to warrant it; the Guardians might simply notice other behaviours.

          The Guardians can't logically punish every Paradox. Minor ones are common enough and the consequences thereof are usually not related to the Abyss (a critical spell coming unwound in a time of crisis is usually about as dire as many Havocs get). Unless someone accidentally levels a building with a miscalculated fireball, the Guardians probably never hear of most.

          If a Mage does cause a Paradox, and manages to contain it without suffering Bedlam or some similar abnormality, the Guardians probably don't notice then, either. The Guardians tend to view those who contain a Paradox successfully as more responsible than those that don't, and the results of containing a Paradox successfully are usually not that severe.

          When a Mage does cause a big Paradox, the response of the Guardians is usually not so nice. If the Consilium has causing so severe a Paradox as a punishable offense, as many do, they'll demand the offender offer themselves up for punishment (if the person has otherwise good standing) or drag them before the Hierarch themselves (if not). If the Consilium doesn't have punishments for such offenders, or the Guardians simply don't trust the person enough, what follows is Guardian-style rough justice. For offenders with extenuating circumstances (and there are not many that would meet the Guardians' standards), they might simply levy a heavy penance for the deed. The rest end up sent to whatever next life awaits them.

          If a Consilium has one, this is a job for the Interfector, who can deal out such punishments without violating the Lex.

          These sorts of instances are rare, though. Most Mages, if they don't contain their Paradoxes, receive a stern warning from their local Guardian and change their ways. If they keep violating if flagrantly to little consequence, they might be challenged to a Duel Arcane. If they cause something nasty to happen, the Guardians won't bother with a trial.

          ---

          But Paradoxes aren't the only negative behaviours that draw Guardian attention. While the Guardians theoretically target any violation of Wisdom, there are three acts besides Paradox that draw their attention:

          1. Left-Handed practices.
          2. Exposing the Mysteries to Sleepers.
          3. Self-aggrandizement.

          The Guardians are always on the look-out for any of these. The investigation tactics aren't that different for any of them, except the latter (you don't really need to look too hard to spot a ladder-climber; they want to be seen as often as not). Any Mage suspected of any of these behaviours will be investigated and the results handed over to the Consilium once the case is air-tight.

          If the Guardians can't prove beyond a shadow of a doubt that someone is performing Left-Handed magic or exposing the Mysteries, but they're certain that they are, well... Sometimes the Lex needs to be shunned for the sake of the greater good. Sins for a just end and all.

          ---

          I'm not sure how many questions that answered, but... That's all I have in me for now.

              Guardians are basically as able as you want. When designing your setting, "how impotent or potent are Guardians?" is one of major questions you need to ask yourself. In some places, they can't do much using official channels, and are like annoying preachers that just won't shut up about how you are damaging your soul and will try to convert you to proper behavior by evangelization. In other, they might be scary, decisive and murderous.
              I personally feel that Paradox is most interesting when it *does* happen, so I tend to drift towards settings where Consilium doesn't care about your Paradox as long as you don't do something major, like endangering another Mage or ruining some important project. Sleepers? Please, who cares about Sleepers.
              Then again, I feel that most fun setting for what we run is a Consilium that is passively dickish (won't tell you to kick dogs, but also won't care if you are putting dogs on fire unless you are doing that to pet of another member).
