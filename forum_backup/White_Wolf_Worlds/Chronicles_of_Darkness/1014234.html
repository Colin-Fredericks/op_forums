    Covenants and Second Edition

    12-08-2016, 06:52 AM
    Now that Secrets of the Covenants are out we have a better understanding regarding the developers view their role and are in this Edition

    The way i see it Covenants are very broad in their scope you can have pretty extreme differences between views with their members.

    Meaning

    You can have a new age minimum blood spilling Crone Priestess and a baby-sacrificing follower of the Goddess

    A Gnostic Lancea interpreting the words of Longinus metaphorically and hard-liners of the Faith that routinely torture virtuous people to save their soul

    A Fire Bombing Anarchist Carthian that wants to burn the establishment and a political savvy one that abhors violence and fights to pass a vampire Constitution

    An Invictus Manager that tries his best to minimize civilian casualties in defense of the Masquerade and a Thug that wants to rise the pyramid of power as fast as he can no matter the cost

    An Ordo DNA researcher feeding in blood bags trying to unlock Ascendancy and a crazed Scientist on the Hill House trope killing mortals slowly trying to measure how much a soul weights


    In the Second Edition Vampire book there is no mention in the first Editions Covenant Books and the Clan ones are cited for inspiration only not mechanics. So in the Second Edition it is safe to say that each covenants power structure and reach is up to the individual Storyteller There is no Canonised way that a covenant should work and what is the "right way " to play a typical member of it so its up to the St and players to make decisions on what aspect of a Covenant is dominant at their Chronicle.

    How do you treat the Covenants in your tables ? Do you use fluff from the first Edition? You make up your own according to what fits?Do you take First Edition as Cannon?

    We are more interested in the larp setting where you have the numbers to make covenants in believable numbers and be mainly player characters.

    A developer input would be very nice so we have understanding on how they see it in their way

    Thanks very much for your time any input would be most welcome.

    Member of the Requiem For Greece Storyteller Team

    City of Athens Storyteller

      I make a few changes to the covenants as a whole and pretty much completely ignore all first edition material on them.

      The Circle I will use a different kind of pagan religion depending on my needs for the game, for one I'm playing in I've designed a sect of spirit worshippers that use Blood Tenebrous. But if I don't intend to focus heavily on them the Circle is just a dark vampiric form of Wicca. The only national or international presence they have is if a cult is big enough on its own to have one, very few of the cults directly work together except to exchange information and even then a lot of them are "kill the heretics!" and will either refuse to share or share flat out dangerously wrong information.

      The Invictus I build in a more national and international presence where domain leaders have an organized meetup that is overseen by a regional leader and regional leaders have an international meetup overseen by a global leader. The regional and global leaders don't hold too much political power though, they're mostly there as a mediator in inter-city or inter-region disputes and as a way to facilitate things like vampire exchanges to build relationships between areas. A city level leader could pretty much tell their regional leader to fuck off without any repercussions except maybe being given less favour by those that are on the regional leader's side.

      The Lancea I give a greater international presence as well, with Priests in charge of cities and directly reporting to a regional Bishop who commands several Priests and then a vampire Pope known as the Patriarch or Matriarch who is elected by a council of all bishops (typically held with a video chat in modern days) who serves a similar role to the Lance as the Pope does for the Catholics (being the direct voice of God) though the Patriarch or Matriarch doesn't serve until death. The majority of power here is held by the Bishops who gain a lot more loyalty than the regional leaders of the invictus and religious fervor keeping subordinates in line.

      The Carthians are different all over the place, usually being a commentary on modern Democracy or Anarchic movements. Again no real presence beyond the city level. I have a game in the planning stages where the Carthians have turned the domain into a total democracy where every recognized vampire citizen has a vote and all domain matters are voted upon (from the assignment of feeding grounds to the assignment of the right to embrace) with a President leading the domain (always a Carthian because they've stacked the domain and are 2/3 of its voting members) whose role is to act as the judge of the domain and tells the domain members who have an official office what to do.

      The Ordo Dracul are like a bunch of universities with each domain being its own school. Different domains will exchange information and have members visit each other and the leaders of domains in an area will meet every so often in order to discuss important positions such as Twilight Judges. The majority of focus is on the coils and scales though, where the usual practice is to document and publish information on coils in a similar manner to mortal academia, with peer-review and everything, though less of a care given to plagiarism.

      VII and the Brood I pretty much use as antagonists exclusively and never really explore. If I want a bunch of vampire hating vampires for players I'd use a Lancea sect and if I wanted a bunch of demon worshippers I'd use the Circle, saves me having to do any mechanical hacking given we've yet to see proper writeups of either VII or the Brood for 2e.

              I, too, make some changes to the Covenants to globalize them a bit. I actually take a fair bit of inspiration from the 1e Covenant books, as well as Masquerade.

              The Invictus and the Lancea et Sanctum form the First and Second Estates of an antidisestablishmentarian governing body known as the New Camarilla. Both are top-down organizations.

              At the top of the Lancea et Sanctum are the Curia Cruentis, an international council of Cardinals who interpret the word of Longinus, and generally serve a similar role as the Inner Circle in Masquerade's Camarilla. They also elect the Pontifex Maximus (more commonly known as simply the Pontiff), who acts as Bishop of Graubünden, Switzerland, and coordinates the annual meeting of the Curia in Davos, which coincides with the World Economic Forum. Below the Cardinals are the Bishops, who act as the religious authority within the Domains they oversee, and as Regents of all Lancea et Sanctum territory within their domains. The Bishop of the largest Domain within a region is called an Archbishop, and coordinates between the other Bishops within their region. Below the Bishops are Pastors, who act as sub-regents of smaller parcels of territory within a Bishop's Diocese, and Priests, who are simply Sanctified who have been ordained by a Bishop and are permitted to learn and administer Theban Sorcery Rites. Members of the Lancea et Sanctum who are not ordained and cannot perform Theban Sorcery are called the Laity. Some prominent Lay members may be named as Deacons, which is essentially a catch-all title for Lay members with special responsibilities to the Covenant. There are a number of other special positions, such as Inquisitors who seek out heresy and Crusaders who punish it, but that's the basic organizational structure.

              The Invictus is the executive branch of the New Camarilla and looks pretty much exactly like Masquerade's Camarilla. At the top are the Justicars, representatives of each Clan elected by the Curia Cruentis and the highest authority on matters of the Traditions, and the Archons, the hand-picked agents of the Justicars. Below them are Princes, who act as the highest Domain-level authority within New Camarilla Domains, and own all of the territory within a Domain, which they subunfudate to Regents, such as Bishops. Advising the Princes are the Primogen, a council of representatives from each Clan within a Domain. They are generally responsible for passing Domain-level laws abs amendments to the Traditions, though the Prince has veto power over their decisions. Then there's other specific positions like the Sheriff who enforces the Primogen's laws, the Scourge who executes Revenants and anyone marked for death by the Curia, Seneshal, Keeper of Elysium, all that jazz.

              The Ordo Dracul is a scholarly organization that is at least nominally allied with the New Camarilla. They obey Invictus Traditions and respect to Lancea et Sanctum dogma, and in return the Inquisitors don't look too closely into their experiments. They have their own internal hierarchy, which is kept secret from outsiders, and functions pretty much as described in the 1e Covenant book. Similar to Fumus' take, they function similarly to universities and are largely concerned with the pursuit of the Coils of the Dragon and development of Scales. In some Domains they simply live and let live with the New Camarilla, while in others they are the real power behind the throne, with membership being an unspoken prerequisite to political advancement within the Invictus.

              The Carthian Movement is an alliance of New Camarilla separatists. They host a practically unlimited number of disparate ideologies, but are united in their opposition to the union of the Invictus and the Lancea et Sanctum. The most moderate Carthian Cells respect and obey the Traditions, but are merely opposed to the union of church and state; such Cells often live in relative peace within New Camarilla Domains, and may even have representation on the Primogen Council. More radical Carthian factions create Domains of their own, which, as per the 1e Covenant book, tend to be experiments in different forms of Kindred government. Some are more successful than others. It's impossible to list positions within the Carthian Movement, because there are so many different approaches to organization that different Carthian cells take. No two Carthian Domains are exactly alike.

              The Circle of the Crone are a paganesque cult encompassing a variety of beliefs and practices, some of which are considered heretical by the Lancea et Sanctum, and accordingly outlawed in many New Camarilla Domains. They are completely ground-up in structure. At the bottom are the Choristers, non-Cruac performing Acolytes who organize themselves into Choruses. The most respected member of a Chorus is sometimes designated the Coryphaeus by their fellow Choristers, designating them as their leader. Within a Choir, there may also be up to three Cantors, Acolytes who have learned some degree of Cruac. Theoretically a Choir could have more than three Cantors, but three is generally agreed upon as the ideal number, being enough to share an altar, but few enough not to attract unwanted attention from Lancea et Sanctum Inquisitors. Sometimes a Choir's Cantors are referred to as the Protagonist, Deuterotagonist, and Tritagonist, as a way to indicate rank in keeping with the Greek theater naming conventions, but each Choir has its own beliefs and practices, and may have different titles and/or specific roles or duties for their Cantors. When a Choir begins to grow too large, some of their membership may split off to form a new Choir, or to join another that may be lacking in number. An especially gifted Cruac ritualist will sometimes be entrusted to serve as a Chorego, a spiritual authority trusted by a number of Choirs but belongs to none, who acts independently and coordinates between the different Choirs in a Domain. Finally, the Exarchon (or Rex Nemoransis) is an old title, no longer in use in the modern era, and its historicity is somewhat questionable - for example, it's not entirely clear if this was a position reserved for the most knowledgeable Acolyte in a large region, or a name that referred to a single individual. But it is thought that the Exarchon was entrusted with the Covenant's greatest secrets, and that the title was held for unlife - the only way to claim it was to kill the previous Exarchon; traditionally by Diablerie, lest his secret knowledge die with him. It's speculated that this may be precisely why the position no longer exists - the last Exarchon may have been killed without passing on his knowledge. When Acolytes form their own Domains, they tend to treat the Traditions as personal responsibilities at most and good advice at least, but don't tend to enforce them legally. However, Circle of the Crone Domains are often in open conflict with New Camarilla Domains, so many Acolytes consider it safer to ally themselves with the Carthian Movement, as Carthian Domains often allow them religious freedom, provided they abide by whatever governing system is in place. Some Acolytes do live in secret within New Camarilla Domains, though it can be quite risky. In such Domains, the three Cantors to a Choir rule is more strictly adhered to, for safety's sake, whereas in Carthian and Circle Domains, avoiding attention is a less pressing concern, so there's a lot more wiggle room.

              Belial's Brood isn't really a Covenant in the traditional sense of the word, but rather a catch-all for diabolist Kindred. Sanctified who take the Pledge to the Worthless One, Acolytes who count demon princes among their pantheons, Invictus who swear Blood Oaths to diabolic regents, Dragons who take their "spit in the eye of god" ideology to its logical extreme, etc. These are theoretically the heretics the Lancea et Sanctum seeks to root out, but as often as not, accuasations are thrown out baselessly to excuse what amounts to religious persecution and political assassination. The Circle of the Crone are the most frequent and egregious targets, but no Covenant is immune. Some young Kindred even suspect that Belial's Brood might be a conspiracy cooked up by Lancea et Sanctum elders specifically to mask their baseless crusade, but anyone who has lived through an attack by the Brood knows better.

              Likewise VII isn't really a proper Covenant; in fact, no one really knows quite what they are. Sometimes Kindred are just murdered, and the Roman numeral VII is found written in blood near their dusted remains. Again, maybe it's just ordinary Kindred trying to hide their murders behind the boogeyman of VII, or maybe there is something more to it. Maybe there's a bit of both going on. But no one has been able to prove one way or the other.

                  Originally posted by Balancer View Post
                  The way i see it Covenants are very broad in their scope you can have pretty extreme differences between views with their members.
                  Think this was a point made way back in first edition and repeated many times by writers and the developer. People bring a lot of baggage to the game and each book tries to wrench it away but it's a hard fight, apparently. I particularly enjoyed this exchange from Secrets of the Covenants:

                  'How you will handle attacks on your praxis and your court?'
                  'Well, I'll start out by not calling it a court, like we're Eurotrash wannabes.'

                  That said most of my games have been fairly local. I didn't do much international vampire, so when there is an internationally-spread covenant it's a special case. I incorporated Danse Macabre's Prima Invicta alongside the Invictus as a sort of far-flung idea of what the Invictus wants to be. The Circle of the Crone was meshed with the Mother's Army, and upon initiation a vampire would literally taste of the Crone's blood and be able to hear her inside them, similar to how I run the Beast. The Lancea et Sanctum was a wreck of a chantry when begun, the leader killed and two 'newcomers' came, the Plague Nun and a much more brash 'hellfire and brimstone' televangelist-like preacher who both wanted to basically make the faith great again and had drastically different ways of going about it. The Carthians in the city were more like expats, waiting on the outskirts and plotting and planning and wishing more than anything else. The players went upstate for a while and I had a domain run by Carthians based on the Premier from Damnation City, who had a vaguely democratic setup with five senators.

                  The VII were expanded to have all the versions of the VII inside them, from the brainwashing experiment gone awry and Dogs of War's Zero Company to the Akhud and Betrayed.

                  Belial's Brood was handled as more of an insidious threat. The factions don't really pit themselves against the covenants, so I had no problem slipping them inside. I believe in our last game there was one in each of the city's stronger covenants, all going entirely unnoticed because the eccentricities of the Brood were easy to overlook as an aspect of the covenant's practices. Mia would hunt down and commit diablerie on Slashers to bring more of the Adversary into herself, but to the Circle she emulated a Fury, punishing the wicked. Devlin of the Nameless was an anthropologist ostensibly bent on discovering how 'lost' vampires worked, while actually delving into the lineage of his Messiah, Belial. Even his pneuma rites were easy to look at as some form of his covenant's experimentation into coils and scales. And members of the Throne of Smokeless Fire practically lend themselves to the hierarchy of the Invictus, especially when they're at the top.

                      So far I've yet to make a Chronicle larger or smaller than the city level, so I've mostly just borrowed ideas and terminology from the Covenantbooks, Clanbooks, and of course Damnation City but always though the lens of 2nd Edition. And that's one of the things I love about Requiem and especially Second Edition (you could really see this too in the later supplements for the gameline.)

                      Requiem wants your vampire setting to be exactly how you want it. By boiling down the Covenants to their core essence (and styles of play) you could really set them up in a city any way you like. Just like you mentioned in the description, if you want your Invictus to be a modern day medieval court ala Masquerade style more power too ya. You want a no bullshit vampire crime family where lineage means nothing and all that matters is who owns the most herd, go wild (and give my regards to the Don.) You want an Invictus Theocracy that uses Christianity solely as a hierarchical system to manipulate and influence city policy, well the Sanctified might have something to say but forget them that's awesome! The sky's the limit to what you can cook up for your own Chronicle, and the best part is all the ground work has already been done for you.

                          The way I typically set up my covenants in cities takes a similar stance on compacts in Hunter: the Vigil. While the Invictus in one city and the Invictus in another would be nominally allied, it might not be so in actual interaction. The Circle is fractured in a way that there might be one cult in a small city, or multiple individual cults in some of the larger cities who are only bound together when a group (say the Sanctum) decides to purge the heretics. The Ordo is a scientific community with the different domains acting like colleges. The Carthians are generally divided city to city, but the Movement holds together in whatever city it is in typically, even with the differing view points. The Sanctum is similar to the Invictus in nominally being allied, but differences in Doctrine and how to go about their holy mission divides them.
