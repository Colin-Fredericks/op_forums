    I think an "Ice Age apocalypse" Promethean chronicle could easily use the Antarctica material as inspiration: humanity has been reduced to a few isolated pockets just trying to survive from day to day, praying that whatever source of food and fuel they have doesn't run out. It would certainly put the Pilgrimage in an interesting light, because why go through all that if your just going to face extinction sooner or later? As humanity's flame begins to sputter, do Prometheans keep searching, or does it turn into one big miserable Flux fest?

    Stemming off of that, "Wolf's Rain" provides some fun inspiration for a Werewolf Ice Age chronicle: the main characters are wolves with the ability to disguise themselves as humans, living in a world where humans have guaranteed the imminent extinction of the wolf. Too bad humanity's only hope of survival rests with the very animals they've been trying to destroy.

                    Which gameline?
                    Ah, Geist and Changeling. Here is my elevator pitch:

                    The first Mars colonies were established in the 2030s, and others soon followed. Mining operations on asteroids and Mars became big business and the corporate concerns that oversaw them bloated under their weight. Earth started to run out of clean water and food about ten years later. Decades of political squabbling over the truth of any potential problems left a world devoid of solutions to those problems, and riots began. The same story of ages past played out before – war burst out, colonies stopped imports and exports, and people stranded in the reaches of space began to die.

                    The Martians didn't.

                    They buckled down and weathered the vanishing support with fortitude and perseverance born out of their frontier ancestors and they lived – thrived, you might even say – in the cold, red wastes. Gangs became tribes, and tribes warred amongst each other over dwindling resources, renewable energy materiel, and found ways to integrate ever stranger things into their bodies, to better stave off the terrible climate. A century passed, and signals from Earth came again. The companies that put the ancestors on the red dust came back and claimed ownership over the Martians and their tools, and the Martian propensity for war turned up.

                    That didn't work out too well; the invaders held the ultimate high ground, and even with all their cybernetics and emancipated robots and reborn machinery, the Martians did not win the day. The companies came down and reprogrammed what they could, fixed what they couldn't, extracted hefty fees from the rebels, and locked down the colonies. Now mines and work camps are little more than prisons, and they make them more like that every day, shipping in new meat from Earth to grind up in the mines looking for gold and copper, ignoring the tribes living squalid existences in the dust.

                    The corporate concerns sit in orbit in their glittering homes with terraforming implements and gases all sitting and waiting for the button to be pressed to turn Mars into a new Earth, but they don't because the bottom line says that would lose them more money than it would save. They set lights out and exercise periods and uniform requirements for employees on the ground and implement their decisions through ruthless robotic stand-ins. It's 2150 and it's same-shit, different-day.

                    Maybe you died. You suffocated in the thin air, you got pulped by a mining machine, you were the last survivor of a Martian raid or a casualty of war. Whatever happened, you got back up, you just had something else in your brain that tells you to kill, to fight back, to let the Martian dead find their rest and their exaltation in war.

                    Maybe you went somewhere you shouldn't have. You found yourself in a winding canyon during exploratory EVA, your ship crashed someplace it couldn't have, your adulthood ritual among the tribe went wrong, and you found yourself in some other Mars, someplace with canals and angry red weeds and Martian cabbage and you took water with strange booming things in the depths of the caverns or hiding amongst the sharp-edged ruins and you know that Mars is still out there, and it wants to be let out.

                                I've got a few ideas for more human and human+ settings.

                                One is a faux fantasy world... that was formed by a Ring of Unchained suborning enough Infrastructure to split off part of the earth and set themselves up as gods. Gods are Unchained. Heroes (in the Greek sense) are Offspring and Fractals. Sorcerers are Stigmatics. "Magic" items are Gadgets. Monsters are Cryptids. Weird religious ceremonies and prophetic convergences are Occult Matrices. Anachronistic tech abounds. Targeted for a different, grittier (due to the CofD ruleset) take on High Fantasy.

                                Another is based on Leviathan, inspired by the Cinder Spires and Dishonored. It's an "After the End" world on the slow road to recovery. Technology used to be higher, but is now poorly understood and rediscovered. The Marduk Society is a full on society, largely keeping themselves as high up and away from the water as possible. Less developed people are reduced to Bronze Age near-barabarism and often worship the Deep Ones (Leviathans). The Leviathans themselves war amongst each other in the seas, using their mutant (Hybrid) cultists as proxies. Oh, and in the undefined frontier, a cabal of Atolls is enslaving Leviathans as their personal monsters. Targeted for Conan-esque adventure, lite eldritch horror, and ruthless pragmatism.

                                Another one I just thought up is a Judge Dredd-like survivor setting with Werewolf. Humanity and Spirits are crowded into some mega-city structure and forced to work together. Werewolves have to keep the peace and the balance between them. Basically an overt rehash of the "Spirit Cops" concept, but more Cop. And they are out in the open. But answerable to superiors (who may well be human). [This one isn't Human+, though]
