                                            I personally interpreted the mirror realm as a frequency of Twilight - one notably lacking in native beings. From the style of its distortions and the general feel of their magic, I would guess a "space" frequency. It almost works as a pocket dimension or use of Quarantine, except that they can clearly see parts of the physical world which don't interact with the mirror realm.

                                            While I was watching, I thought of the Dark Dimension as a Lower Depths which lacked Time - and consumed the physical world for change and new elements, which it was unable to create for itself. It does very well as an Abyssal realm also, though, and I did really like Lost Light's explanation that it perfectly demonstrates how to defeat abyssal entities by forcing them to confront ideas they can't comprehend.

                                            I loved this movie, and as a current mage storyteller, I will definitely be mining it for inspiration. It's probably the closest movie I've seen to Mage the Awakening, and will definitely recommend it to new players, as others here have also said. I particularly loved the minor details of enchanted items and artifacts which demonstrate really cool things you can (and probably would) do with magic, not for any real purpose, but because it's awesome, and why would you not want a window that literally opens a portal to beautiful landscapes? I think it will probably help me most with inspiration of the casual spells mages leave around their sanctums - it brings a lot of flavor to characters, and it's something that I'm really good at and enjoy as a player, but struggle with as a ST.
