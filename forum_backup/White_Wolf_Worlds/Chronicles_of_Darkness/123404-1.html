                So I didn't finish the demon chapter today. So here's the first bit with more to come soon.

                To Hell and Back

                Very first few paragraphs and I am immediately hit by a feeling of “this is about Unchained”! No problem, but it's worth mentioning. We'll see what happens when we get to the Lucifuge though

                Then we get to lesser demons. Bodyless, tied to Vice, using words from Inferno. But a mouth full of grinding gears? Is the intent to blur the lines between Inferno and Unchained, hinting at an upcomming expansion to Unchained or just that Infernal demons can be asthetically mechanical too? I'm just hoping there's a clear statement of what's what later.

                The lesser demons section continues this blurring, lots of Unchained imagery but creatures that would fit right at home in Inferno and look a bit out of place in Unchained. The greater demons, however, are clearly unchained. Also, Lucifer is a signal. Yay! Though I'm only dimly aware of that idea moving around the forums I did like the concept.

                Pacts are good. The concept that demonic pacts do take some of your soul is new to Unchained (as far as I know) and deserves more material. You can't just say souls are being trimmed and not mention it again, though saying it grows back would be enough. All in all, I liked the pact section, I dind't quite feel the mortal pactee's motivation but it's just an example.

                Purposes – it seemed ok, but I've already read the Agendas so I mostly skipped over this. Same with the power section just afterwords.

                Institutional Demons – once again I get the feeling that these guys are Inferno. The G-M and the Unchained cannot be so easily pegged as “always making the world worse”. In fact, I really hope this is inferno because I think it would be a big blow for Demon's themes if the G-M's actions were so concretely defined. Even the Fiendish Flaws feel a little more occult and less technical than usual. Notice that nothing mentions silicon or circuitry.

                Witnesses: Nice and simple, though these obviously Stigmatics come from all types of demons, further confusing the question: Unchained or Infernal.

                So, my thoughts. It was functional, but like most of the opening sections it's mostly for people who haven't read Demon. I have, well the kickstarter preview anyway. I think a small sidebar addressing what's Unchained or what's Inferno was really really needed. My final guess is, this is all Unchained but it's really non obvious and could have been easily cleared up. I kinda hope I'm wrong though, the portrayal of Lesser and Institutional demons, I don't think it works for Unchained.

                Having ready all the opening sections; I give the award to Jason Needham. I think his decision to focus less on mechanics and factual info, and more on presenting the themes worked great. In general I think mechanics are easy, themes are hard, so that's a better place to focus. Plus it's useful if you have or haven't read Geist, and heck it's even useful reading if you're playing Geist without Hunters.

                                The Response

                                Ashwood Abbey – The Ashwood Abbey is split into the three subgroups. The Competitors enjoy unravelling a demon's webs of lies to track it down. The Pursuit are fascinated by the theological mysteries. Libertienes like to get possessed for fun.

                                Libertines were exactly what I expected. The other two, not so much. I always saw good ol' Abbey as being about quick cheap thrills, while the Competitors seem to be playing the long game which I can certainly see being a niche hobby, anticipation has it's own sort of thrill. The Pursuit are more interested in awful taboo breaking secrets rather than knowledge itself. I don't see them as interested in the truths of the universe, so much as which celebrity sold her soul to who.

                                The Long Night – We come to our first religious hunters in the demons chapter. This section has about half a paragraph on the Long Night's views, tactics, lore, etc. The rest is from the demon's point of view, why might a demon target a church community? Considering this is repeating stuff that was mentioned a few pages ago, I didn't like this section.

                                Even if you talk exclusively about the Unchained – who have no religious connection to the Long Night – there's a whole wealth of interesting parallels. Does a person accept suffering and hardship on Earth in faith that he'll be rewarded in heaven, or take the easy way out and sign on the dotted line? That's the core of Christian theology, and it applies just as well to Unchained as Inferno. At least the Long Night will assume it does, because they'll assume an Unchained contract's are damming. There's lots of missed potential here.

                                The Loyalists of Thule – this one is very straight forward. Sometimes they collaborate with demons for information, when they don't they are very good at sniffing out the discrepancies in cover. I did like the idea that the Loyalists would intentionally turn their lives into a minefield if it looks like a demon wants to buy it. If you think you're doomed to hell, maybe oblivion would be better.

                                Network Zero – another straight forward one. They will get a demon transforming on camera one day and then what? Each group's opinion is presented.

                                Null Mysteriis – The Rationalists know about lessoer demons, but haven't had much luck getting them to hold still enough to study. Still, they accept their existence which is always good. Greater Demons seem to have hidden from them so far. Open Minds have laxer standards, and the cataclysmics suspect a war they know nothing about is coming. Sadly, nothing was done with the idea that Embeds are laws of physics, and interesting things could happen if the NM found them.

                                The Union – demons being particularly infrastructural makes the union think about three letter agencies, banks, and soon they're moving from Home Front to General Strike. It's a good angle.


                                Ageis Kai Doru – This entire section is one big plot hook. Behind the scenes I believe what happened was that some ancient Greeks tried to build an epic bit of Infrastructure, the AKD stopped them leaving the device sunk on the ocean floor. Now in the 20th century they've finally managed to start carefully gathering pieces and trying to figure out what it was.

                                There's virtually nothing on their opinions or strategies, but at least it's a good plot hook. The AKD being the second oldest Conspiracy (with more infrastructure to retain the benefits of age than the AO) is, IMO, one of their strongest themes so it's nice to see it played to. I didn't like the fact they never found any other Infrastructure in two thousand years.

                                Relic – long backstory aside, this is a compass that points to demons.

                                The Ascending Ones – As always they don't agree on much. The Southern Temple questions whether demons are the actual demons – in a way that strongly implies they're not the ones from Inferno. I did like the way they believed in the idea of king Solomon commanding demons. The Knife thinks that demons are bad news, no matter where they come from. The Crescent have on the ground knowledge, and for some reason that's not made clear they aren't sure if the other Ascending Ones are the people they can turn to.

                                The Cherion Group – is Deeva Corporation supposed to be bigher than Cherion? Or just equal so that they can laugh at Cherion thinking they're tiny? Either way, Cherion has a rival for hunting demons, which is unsuprising. I did especially like the way Cherion is behind The Prisoner – their basic goal is to learn how to make Demons into their apocalyptic forms so that they can be harvested.

                                Cheiron gets no Endowment.


                                Lucifuge – This one... it's not very good. Lets start with the preliminaries. The Lucifuge are descended from Inferno, not Unchained. Castigation is solid evidence for this. Pre GMC you got bonuses for low morality. The Familiar Castigation is damn near identical to the one in Inferno. Actually, it's explicitly said so. Les Infants Diabolic and Lucifuge are said to be targeting the same people for Recruitment, Inferno page 180.

                                So the starting point is that Lucifuge are tied to inferno. Now Inferno has a whole demonic theology, and a brief mention of an angelic counterpart (p 158). So you'd expect there to be some interesting theological debates; about how these two gods with their angels and their fallen angels fit together into a broader universe.

                                Well, nope. The whole section can be summeriesd as “If a Lucifuge is convinced that the Unchained view is the core cosmology, here's how they might react”. I would describe it as guidelines for how to make Lucifuge fit into the Unchained setting, but this is a Hunter book, the Lucifgue's own worldview and cosmology deserves more weight than this. There is room for a Lucifuge who comes to believe that we're all living in the God Machine's personal universe, and all the other angels and demons are creations of rival gods poking their heads in. But not at the expense of the Lucifuge's own world-view.

                                The Malleus Maleficarum - This one on the other hand is quite nice. It says the MM mostly ignore the questions of what's a demon, which Catholic theology would do. Them being influenced by papal policy, and using influence as well as guns. I like that quite a bit. The only missing piece was something on angels.

                                Task Force VALKARIE – this one was a bit general. It does seem to imply TF:V are ok with the G-M, but it's probably a bit closer to them not knowing about it and knowing about demons. Demons say it created the world and it's bad. TF:V says the world is pretty ok, so if it created it then it can't be that bad. They might change their mind if they met it.


                                Utopia Now – So another corporation trying to harvest the supernatural? This niche is getting quite crowded. I won't say it's over-filled yet, but it will be quite soon.

                                First page: They're techno-utopian captalistis. That is a weird mix. Really weird actually. Take this quote from the stereotypes “We want a future where everyone works for honest dollars” and now this quote from earlier “His utopia will be founded on principles of self-governance and self-sufficiency. The individual modules of his floating environment will be able to produce goods for
                                themselves with 3-D printing and other cutting-edge advances.”

                                If everyone is self sufficient, what exactly will people be doing for those honest dollars? There's only so many jobs to go around in resource extraction, and designing things to 3-d print will probably end up competing with an open-model community.

                                I don't actually think this is a bad concept, though I'm not sure. Utopian visionary with a major blind spot can lead to epic stories. But I would have liked a bit more on the ideological conflict between capitalistic techno-utopians and regular techno-utopians.

                                On the whole though, they're a regular hunter group. There's not much methodological distinctions when it comes down to what they do on the ground. They like humanity, dislike monsters, fight like any other hunter group, and specialise in infrastructural demons. A solid compact, but the real story possibilities is where they might go in the future.


                                Knights of Saint Adrian – What can I say about these guys that hasn't been said on these forums already?

                                I have a strong dislike of hunters who're unwittingly serving monsters. Let me clarify, I'm fine with hunters unwittingly serving monsters appearing as antagonists, but not as the default PCs. Hunter is thematically about playing humans fighting back after all. So these guys, well it's mostly speaking not unwitting.

                                They know who they're working for. They're doing it for a pay check. Some might be upset if they learned that “god” isn't the good guy; but I think at least 60% will just shrug and maybe raise their prices a bit.

                                The core concept here is a mixture of Dog the Bounty Hunter and 80s action heroes. It pushes the feel of hunter as a gritty dark game as far as it can go without actually breaking it, and behind the explosions and shiny motorcycles there is a strong element of back room deals and shady characters. All in all, as someone who said that playing a mixed Conspiracy group is almost as much of crossover as playing a Vampires and Changelings; I approve of this new thematic ground.

                                Their Endowments are solid, it could use one that boosts Stamina or provides armour. But why does the picture have a guy with no tats?



                                I'm not going to do a where I read on the entire list of new Mechanics. Except to say Elixirs don't cost exp. About damn time! The new rules for Monsters Don't Count, however, are perhaps the worst possible mechanical decision you could make. [edit - someone on RPG.net suggested that this might refer exclusively to the rule of the same name on page 322 of the Hunter core, if true that would nullify my complaints.]

                                There is definitely room in Hunter for paranoid wrecks, but there is also room for stone cold badasses who keep everything together during a long campaign of doing bad things to bad people – not least because most of the named Hunter NPCs come across as having high Integrity. Does Lady Lucifuge come across as someone who can't hold herself together? How many people has she had tortured or killed?
