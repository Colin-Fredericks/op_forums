                    Originally posted by RomulusGloriosus View Post
                    I am SOOOOOOOOOOO on-and-off about the damn God Machine. I think the Exarchs and Mage do Gnosticism much better than Demon and the GM do, and Dave Brookshaw has excised the GM and everything to do with it from the Mage core and has said on the forums that he personally doesn't even broach how the GM and Exarchs coexist.
                    I think you missed the point. So let's have a quick rewind. Note that this is from how I understand it and does not reflect on Brookshaw's actual opinions or choices.

                    Around the time of Mage development, this entire argument concerning the God-Machine was going on. At the time, with the introduction of the Second Edition rules alongside it, one of the big prevailing worries that the God-Machine had gone from a cool bit of niche lore to an all consuming cornerstone. It didn't help that the God-Machine got a mention in Blood and Smoke(Requiem Second now) under the VII section as a possible origin for the 'covenant'. It was a problem.

                    Mage, in the meanwhile, was designed to be a diver into the whole of the Chronicles world, that mages would explore every corner. That's a metric shit-ton of material to at least lightly touch on, and that's before you account that Mage itself has a wide amount of material to include and incorporate. As such, certain things needed to be left out. And since there was already plenty of attention/writing done on the G-M part of the world at that time, it was a pretty clear thing to ignore for the core book.

                    I don't think Brookshaw's intention was to say "The God-Machine is excised from Mage because problems"-hell, Brookshaw was one of the big writers for The God-Machine Chronicle, and has stated his enjoyment of it a couple of times. The problem was that Mage was a stuffed book. You'll notice there's no mention of Changeling, the Strangers, or Arcadia either, and it's by and large for similar reasons.

                    There probably also wasn't broaching it because the exact mechanical relationship between the Exarchs and the God-Machine are best defined at individual tables in the spirit of solving mysteries and becoming Mysteries than providing some kind of definitive answer. It works out, because I've seen Brookshaw's headcanon on that(it's here on the forums somewhere) and while it's cool, I wouldn't run it that way.

                    Mage will cover the God-Machine and Descent as surely as it will make a Mystery out of cArcadia and Lost eventually, but the core needed a better focus on the things that would matter most to Mage, that's were much more immediate.

What does the interaction of a splat whose powers are based on symbolism and beings who are living stories look like?

                                                        Admittedly this is only partially relevant, but to elaborate a little bit on what Leliel said before about Lovecraftian "sorcery as hyper-technology", there has been an effort in late years to reconcile the tech aspect of the Cthulhu Mythos with the horror one. Not as in, "sci-fi tech weird aliens use and that we can grab" but more like "I absolutely have no idea how this works and is so removed from my conception of the laws of reality it is magic for me".

                                                        It all relates to the fact 80 years of pop-cultural osmosis kinda deprived the Cthulhu Mythos of the sense of mind-melting revelations that are at the core of the Cosmic Horror genre.

                                                        Alongside that, there's this little heresy which I appreciate a lot that's all about what happens when Gnosticism meets Lovecraft. Curiously, the two currents coexists. Dark Gnosticims enable by science so advanced only an enlightened (read: broken but now wise) mind can comprehend. Advanced technology has a place in horror today more than ever. It fits the zeitgeist.

                                                        I know that many people here disagree (even a couple of notable ones, like some Demon writers and such) but the God-Machine is Cosmic Horror. Sure, it does not meets all the points of the "Lovecraft's checklist for Cosmic Horror" but, listen to this crazy idea, that's because it's done by people that know what they're doing. You can do Lovecraftian homages in thousands of way, and the examples of people that pick the superficial aspects of the Cthulhu Mythos for their work are countless. They can be good, they can be bad, nothing intrinsecally wrong with that. I do the same, sometimes.

                                                        But to create something new, fresh, and that works, you've got to pick certain aspects of the genre, discard others and, perhaps most importantly, burn some others down and scatter the ashes. You can do that by having a deep understanding both of the tropes of the genre and of narrative in general. God-Machine and Demon do that. Bloodborne does too (seriously, I can write pages explaining how that game is utterly devote and beneficial to Lovecraft while departing from many core elements of H.P.'s works)

                                                        It's not deconstruction nor reconstruction. It's an evolution and the existence of these is a good thing all around. You can improve the Cosmic Horror genre as a whole by looking at works like this.

                                                        Now, I do believe the authors were fully aware of what they were writing (they're a smart bunch of people) but, conscious or not, the elements that give the God-Machine and Demon their personality don't deprive them of their bite.

                                                        It would be a disservice to say that the techgnostic elements of Demon are mere aesthetic, but it's also true that to focus on them while not giving a chance to the whole picture under the mechanical surface is like looking at the finger instead of looking at the moon.

                                                        If you still don't like it afterwards, it's perfectly fine, but Demon is not about "Matrix robots fighting the system" just like Changeling is not "5 Stages of grief: The Game" and Beast is not "1001 ways to justify abuse". There will always be people that will describe the game as that, but that's objectively not true.

                                                        Or at least, there's more than that.
