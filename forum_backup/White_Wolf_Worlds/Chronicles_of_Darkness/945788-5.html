                    Hm... well, these are just a few thoughts, but:

                    1. Crusades. Vampire could be fertile ground to explore, since it had a chapter in Ancient Bloodlines/Mysteries focussing on the Middle Ages; the Lancea et Sanctum using the Crusades as cover to go after non-Sanctified vampires could also be an interesting angle. Mages could also be interesting, with the East-meets-West dynamic of Alexander's conquests in "To the Strongest" getting echoed, and some mages wondering if a new paradigm shift in Awakened society is coming. A hunter setting with Hunter-on-Hunter action due to the confusion of religion and politics, while the monsters go unfought, could be interesting as well.

                    2. Renaissance Italy. Vampire all the way. The debauchery, the intrigue, the juxtaposition of humanism and religion, republicanism and despotism. A Methuselah Embraced before the Western Empire fell feels genuine joy for the first time in centuries as he watches Italy return to her former glory... an elder who entered Torpor after the Camarilla fell awakens to find the Invictus has essentially replaced it.

                    3. Golden Age of Piracy. Mage could be interesting, perhaps with a proto-Free Council Nameless Order mirroring the Pirate Republic of Nassau, and European mages coming into contact with American Awakened and comparing notes. Werewolf could be cool, because "werewolf pirates" just sounds like an inherently awesome premise. Sin-Eaters could be fun too, and I think others have echoed similar sentiments.

                    4. Seven Year's War/American War of Independence. Maybe see where Hunters stand after "Doubting Souls," or explore how the interplay between European and Native werewolves turned out, plus the fact that a lot of battles were fought on the frontier sounds like werewolves could be in their element on either side of the fight.

                    5. French Revolution. Especially picking up where "Lily, Saber and Thorn" left off, and see how things have changed for the French Changelings. Beast could also be appropriate, because they didn't call it the "Terror" for nothing.

                    Haberdasher's Requiem Conversions and Homebrew

                                    The Taiping Rebellion (Vampire and Werewolf) - China is dominated by a calcified Confucian Covenant, deathless elders even more resistant to change than the decaying dynasty they help to prop up. Try as they might to stamp out the encroaching infestation of European Kindred they keep coming: the Invictus with their fingers in colonial interests, the Lancea following the spread of the missionaries, the Ordo seeking to plunder hidden knowledge. Vampires were not behind the Taiping revolt, but they did seek to exploit it. The Lancea in particular latched on to the Taipings' peculiar brand of Christianity and sought to make them their proxies. They failed, instead spawning an aggressive movement of Taiping Kindred that expand rapidly and gruesomely dispatch Confucian and European alike. The Confucians entrench themselves in palaces of shadow and gardens of smoke, obsessively playing out pomp and ritual that increasingly has no meaning, while the Europeans barricade themselves in colonial enclaves and gorge themselves on the excesses of empire. Outside blood runs aplenty and plague ravages those not cut down by war.

                                    To the Uratha one thing is obvious, the world is not in harmony. Things could hardly be more terribly unbalanced. Natural disasters strike with ominous frequency, the Yellow River floods so severely that its course is altered by hundreds of miles, famine and disease are rampant, law and order are absent or impotent. Contact with Europe has brought new ideas, new technology, and new problems, whether they want them or not. The natural, cultural, and spiritual worlds are in chaos. It all seems too much, too vast, and though the Uratha have to will to put it right they lack the unity. Some believe in expelling all intrusive influences and returning to a state of purity. Others contend that the only salvation is through adaptation, taming the storm by force and with new visions. Both are divided within themselves over whether the old leadership must be preserved at all costs, or removed by any means necessary.

                                                    I still want my Weimar Republic Werewolf/Changeling/Geist clusterfuck. This has not changed.
                                                    To clarify:

                                                    For Werewolf you have the freikorps and the (sometimes violent) politics -as one of my professors once put it, it was a democracy run by people who didn't want a democracy for people who didn't want a democracy. You have that whole aspect of Germany losing a war for the first time to play with. And I don't doubt that the Shadow would be... interesting.

                                                    For Changeling, you have the whole hyperliberalism of the Weimar period. Prostitution and homosexuality were legal, and women had the vote (almost 2 years before the US). There's a wild backlash in the art and nascent film movements that oppose the naturalism of their predecessors, and philosophy is all over the place. Juxtapose that with the atmosphere of poverty, shame, and anger that ultimately led to the Third Reich, and you've got an interesting backdrop for Changelings.
