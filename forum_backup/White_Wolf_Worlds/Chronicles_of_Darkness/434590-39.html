    It's not so much that they cling to their old identity or a question of less/more.

    Think of it as the difference between theory and practice. The young Leviathan might leap at the chance to reject humanity and be "special", but ask him what being a Leviathan means and he'll just say "Oh, you know, we're descended from Tiamat, we have a wake, we do rituals".

    But to the old Leviathan "descended from Tiamat" means the time he broke into a museum to read ancient Babylonian tablets. "We have a wake" means that time he hid in a basement in a remote town and coming out to find the town a bullet ridden no man's land between fortified compounds. "we do rituals" means the time the local top Leviathan knocked on his door and said he's rounding up an Opheon hunting posse before it does Tiamat-knows-what with all those ancient artefacts he stole; and how just for a little while slaughtering defenceless cultists felt not only good but also heroic.


    If God poked his head out of heaven and turned every Leviathan into humans, a high Sheol Leviathan may be outright ecstatic. But he'd still seek out with and hang out with (much more frequently now there's no territorial instincts) other Leviathans because of shared experiences. He'd still think of them as "my people".

                            Lets get some movement here again. Here's one of the sample Emperian Thurgists. The heart ripping Aztec.

                            The second Thurgist will probably be the exact opposite in every way except for also being hispanic. A modern, progressive, priestess of Santa Muerte with a congregation of working class and outcasts.


                            Enrique Puente

                            This is a different kind of company. We offer you flexible working, mentorship, benefits, and most importantly a chance to make our world a better place.

                            In return, we only ask that you give us your heart.

                            The Aztecs believed that the world was a fragile delicate thing. Only the constant labour of the gods kept the world from collapse, and only the sacrifice of man gave the gods the strength they need.

                            An discriminating observer may notice that it has been a long time since the Spanish ended the Aztec practices of human sacrifice, and the world hasn’t ended. Enrique sees it differently: Global warming, environmental collapse, without the Teotl at the helm the world is very much dying. The enormous lump of rock and water we live on will survive, just as it survived the previous four worlds ending in Aztec mythology, but if humans want to survive we’d better start taking care of the planet and offering up blood.

                            In his quest to save the Earth Enrique blends mundane and occult methods. He is the founder, chief scientist, and sole owner of Huitzilopochtli Photovoltaics, as well as a philanthropist and leading sponsor of archeology and Aztec heritage events. Few outside the upper echelons of Huitzilopochtli Photovoltaics realise know his interests go beyond the academic curiosity and establishing a sense of national pride; or know about the secret attic in his corporate tower where the sacrifices take place.

                            Appearance

                            Enrique looks far more South American than his mixed indigenous and Spanish heritage would suggest. In person he cuts an imposing figure, standing six foot two with a regal continence and flowing jet black locks. One might say he was made for the roll, especially if they see how different he looks in his childhood photographs.

                            Storytelling Hints

                            Unlike most Empyrean Theurgists who merely react to the Tribe’s presence, Enrique seeks out Leviathans to sacrifice their enormous blood filled hearts. His focus is on his photovoltaic research and attempting to subtly restore the worship of the Teotl but he has enough resources to pursue secondary goals.

                            As a corporate man and a philanthropist Enrique’s resources don’t provide him with much in the way of firepower, especially not the specialist forces one needs to take on a Leviathan, so unless his interests are directly threatened Enrique is unlikely to take the risks necessary to acquire mercenaries. Instead he will discreetly use his considerable resources to support any Hunters or Ahabs who looks competent.
                            Most of Enrique’s worship is just that, worship and sacrifice to honour the gods rather than a practical use of occult power. When he practices theurgy Enrique focuses on enhancing his company, providing Blessings that help him outcompete his rivals and create life-saving green energy to an increasingly polluted planet. Often without the knowledge of the recipient.

                            To fuel his miracles Enrique’s mostly uses small measures of blood donated by the congregation, and occasionally abducts somebody who won’t be missed when a more potent sacrifice is required. He’s no hypocrite, and if he believed would sacrifice his friends or himself in a heartbeat if he believed the gods wished it.

                            Sidebar: Seems Sacrilegious

                            Empyrean Theurgists cannot use Sacrilegious, but human sacrifice is a horrific crime. Does that not qualify?

                            In Enrique’s style of Theurgy the quality of the sacrifice is important. An exceptional individual provides a better sacrifice than an average joe, and a Leviathan is a uniquely potent sacrifice. Enrique gains Ardour for tracking down and acquiring the right victim, not for making his ritual especially heinous. This shows that his sacrifices fall under Portents, not Sacrileges.

                            Mental Attributes: Intelligence 5, Wits 2, Resolve 4
                            Physical Attributes: Strength 2, Dexterity 2, Stamina 2
                            Social Attributes: Presence 5, Manipulation 3, Composure 3
                            Mental Skills: Academics 3 (Aztec Theology), Computer 2, Investigation* 2, Occult 4 (Theurgy), Politics* 2, Science* 4 (Photovoltaics)
                            Physical Skills: Athletics 1, Drive 1, Firearms 1, Stealth 2
                            Social Skills: Empathy 2, Expression 3 (preaching), Intimidation 1, Persuasion* 4, Socialise 2 (fundraising), Subterfuge* 4
                            Merits: Good Time Management O, Indomitable OO, Library OOO (Photovoltaics), Library OO (Aztec Theology), Professional Training (Scientist) OOOO, Professional Training (Executive) OOO, Tolerance for Biology O, Giant OOO, Allies OO (Political), Allies OOOO (Scientific), Allies OOO (Archeology), Contacts OOOO (Politics, Corporate, Scientific, Archeology), Fame O, Inspiring OOO, Resources OOOO, Safe-Place OOOOO (a temple hidden in the penthouse of his corporate HQ), Striking Looks OO (Regal)
                            Virtues: Leadership 5, Piety 4, Dutiful 2
                            Domains: The Sun, Sacrifice, Death
                            Willpower: 7
                            Integrity: 6
                            Humble: Hopeful. Enrique’s methods might be suspect, but his motives are not. He his genuine in his faith, humble before the gods, and humble before the common good.
                            Vice: Deceitful. While Enrique is not untrustworthy by nature, as a devout practitioner of human sacrifice he is always hiding both his crimes, and his true beliefs.
                            Health: 7
                            Initiative: 5
                            Defense: 2
                            Speed: 9

                            Cult: Huitzilopochtli Photovoltaics. Fervour 1, Piety 4, Zeal 3. Legitimate Organization. Compartmentalized OOO, Home Turf OO (the corporate headquarters), Old Hands OO (Kidnapping).

                                            Upwellings and Undertows are done - the Tribe's virtues and vices.

                                            Upwellings and Undertows are the two paths that lead from the transitional state that is a Leviathan’s existence; Upwellings raise Leviathan up to the heights of godhood while Undertows drag them down into the thoughtless depths of beasthood. Together they threaten to pull a Leviathan in two.

                                            An Upwelling describes the fundamental identity of an individual Leviathan’s divine nature, while an Undertow does the same for her beastal nature. When she acts in coordination with her other natures the two facets of her mind ally, giving her a much needed respite from the Tempest. For a few moments she breaks the surface and may draw a much needed breath of air before the Tempest once again threatens to drown her.

                                            A simple act in accordance with a Leviathans Upwelling or Undertow restores a point of Willpower, while one which causes the Leviathan significant trouble and creates dramatic possibilities restores all Willpower. Phronesis & Eunoia chain the beastal and divine natures; and loosed from their chains the other natures can also hold a Leviathan’s Ichor hostage, demanding that a Leviathan fall or rise further.

                                            The beastal nature is primal and direct, so regaining Willpower from Undertows requires the Leviathan act personally. Always it’s opposite, the divine nature is calculating and works in mysterious ways and so the divine nature provides Willpower when a Leviathan works through proxies, usually but not always their Cult.

                                            Sample Undertows

                                            The Man-Eater

                                            The Man-Eater hungers for human flesh. He is the nightmare of civilised man, that the walls between us are thin stand only because the monsters outside fear the strength of a long gone generation when man remembered how to hunt and survive.

                                            The Man-Eater regains a point of Willpower when he solves a problem by eating a person (or uses some other suitably dramatic and personal method). He regains all Willpower when the act is witnessed by a significant number of mortals.
                                            The Hoarder

                                            The dragon atop his mountain of gold. The serpent with lidless eyes, forever watchful for thieves and intruders. The Hoarder lives to expand his collection and to guard it against all comers.

                                            The Horder regains a point of Willpower when he prevents a loss to his collection (this can include preventing escapees from his collection of princesses) and regains all his Willpower when he causes a significant problem in order to add add to his collection.
                                            The Fury

                                            The Fury is the sea and the storm made flesh. She has no desire to destroy, and she has no understanding of any way but destruction. Cliffs are shattered and cities sink in her wake.

                                            The Fury regains a point of Willpower when she causes a significant amount of destruction. If the Fury chooses the path of destruction when there are clear alternatives she regains all spent Willpower.
                                            The Nest-Maker

                                            A shark on dry land is pitiful, not threatening. The Nest Maker knows well that a beast is only fearsome in its own lair. He stakes his claim, and permits no trespass.

                                            The Nest-Maker regains a point of Willpower when it drives intruders from it’s lair. When he claims a new lair by driving out its current inhabitants he regains all his spent Willpower.
                                            The Terror

                                            She lurks beyond the village, a story mothers use to make their children behave. When she emerges from the depths she need not fight, she need not ask, she gives one look and the tribute flows freely.

                                            The Terror regains a point of Willpower whenever she uses fear and intimidation to get her way, she may not regain Willpower by intimidating her own Beloved. If she successfully uses fear and intimidation against a superior foe she regains all spent Willpower.

                                            Sample Upwellings

                                            The Conqueror

                                            The Conqueror sets forth with a sword and an army to bring foreign nations under his divine rule. Victory is everything, and he weeps when there are no more lands left to conqueror.

                                            The Conqueror gains a point of Willpower when his followers win a marital conflict in which they are the aggressor. If they take ownership of land through force of arms the Conqueror regains all his spent Willpower.

                                            The Devil

                                            Even the gods must acknowledge others, even if only other gods. The Devil defines herself by opposition, another church, another nation is her enemy. But she has no wish to destroy it for where would that leave her? The Devil instead spreads her corruption, turning her enemy against it's sacred values. Leaving it alive in name only as a testament to her victory.

                                            The Devil regains a point of Willpower whenever she makes one of her enemies betray their principles or ideals. If she corrupts her own allies or some other group whose moral behavior is of benefit to herself then she regains all Willpower.

                                            The Judge

                                            The Judge does not wish to create a world without sin, no his motive is far more brutal. The Judge wishes to create a world where all sin is punished and all sinners face their judgement. The Judge would be satisfied if the whole world turned against the gods, so long as the whole world was thus drowned for their sins.

                                            The Judge gains a point of Willpower when her followers exact a harsh punishment on a Sinner. If she sacrifices an asset or an opportunity on the altar of judgement she regains all spent Willpower.

                                            The Lawgiver

                                            In many ways the Judge's twin, the Lawgiver emerges from the depths with bronze tablets to create a world where all follow the laws within. He punishes harshly and rewards sparingly but when faced with the choice the Lawgiver would ignore one sinner to instil his commandments within two.

                                            The Lawgiver regains a point of Willpower when he witnesses his followers obey one of his laws despite a personal cost. He regains all his Willpower when his followers act against his own best interests because his laws demand they do so.

                                            The Mother

                                            The Mother exists for her children, she has given birth to heroes and monsters and will give birth to many more until the day she is dragged up from the depths. Her only wish is to see her children triumph. Her children’s own wishes are meaningless to her.

                                            The Mother regains a point of Willpower when his children use his gifts or advice to overcome a challenge. If his children overcome a significant challenge (as a rule of thumb, if you could imagine a Greek myth about it, it qualifies) then the Mother regains all his spent Willpower.

                                            The Patriarch

                                            Parisian without limits, The Patriarch will see his people thrive. He bestows his favor freely but never spares the teacher's rod when his people stray from their own best interests. To those who threaten the chosen people or just stand in their way The Patriarch sends his storms and his plagues.

                                            The Patriarch gains a point of Willpower when her followers expand their numbers or influence. She regains all her Willpower if they are able to assimilate another group into themselves.

                                            The Weaver

                                            It is said Fate binds even the gods, but the Weaver believes she can play Fate at it's own games and master the future. But no Weaver plays for the love of the game, they have goals and plans which they enact through mortal pawns.

                                            The Weaver gains a point of Willpower when she fulfils an Ambition or makes significant progress on a long term Ambition without anyone other than her Cohort being aware of her involvement - that usually means they mustn’t notice her Cult’s involvement either. If the Weavers enemies contribute to her success of their own free will and without coercion then she regains all her spent Willpower.


                                            I still need a couple more sample Undertowes to make 7.

                                            In other news, after wavering back and forth for a while - Judges are going to be officially included in Leviathan. For those who don't know Judges were originally written a blue-book minor template that I wrote in collaboration with LostLight. Based heavily on the Biblical book of judges, they're basically God's (or Zeus' etc) personal Jack Bauer. I made a few small tweeks to the mechanics to fit better in Leviathan, most notably they now get Supernatural Tolerance so that they're immune to becoming Beloved without needing to be a special case. The updated version is in the Leviathan folder.

                                                        And now I've ported the 1E Merits. Not much to do, but a few of them have become obsolete in 2E. Suggestions for replacements will be welcome (note that I'm quite strict about Merits that just modify dicepools. I'd allow some, but they'd have to really impress me)

                                                        Dehumanising Ethos (OO)

                                                        The Leviathan subscribes to an code of ethics accepted by mortal culture that dehumanizes some rival group, allowing them to focus their wickedness upon them with less guilt and gaining a +2 bonus to Erosion dicepools when the victims are in the target group.

                                                        The drawbacks of this strategy should be extremely obvious. When regular humans come up with a dehumanising ethos it tends to have horrible results. When Leviathans do so it tends to be even worse. In addition, a Leviathan who subscribes to a Dehumanizing Ethos act in ways that cause more frequent and worse Erosion.
                                                        Divine Prerogative (OO)

                                                        The divine bloodline inside your character is eager to express itself in the defeat of mortals. You may select a Skill. When engaging in a contested roll using that Skill against a human (one not subject to a major supernatural template), you may chose to risk Willpower instead of spending it. You receive the normal bonus dice as though Willpower were spent, but, if you are successful in overcoming the victim's resistance, you do not lose the Willpower risked, instead retaining that point and in fact regaining an additional point beyond that. You cannot risk Willpower to boost your resistance - you must be acting aggressively to benefit from this Merit.
                                                        Drawback: Should you fail, the risked Willpower is spent as usual and your failure is upgraded to a Dramatic failure. This often triggers an Outburst check.
                                                        Gatewater (O)

                                                        Your character has control of their own Gatewater. See page xx for rules on Gatewaters.
                                                        Giant (O)

                                                        Leviathans may buy the Giant Merit at a discount.
                                                        Heirloom (O - OOOOO)

                                                        Your character has some physical artifact that was once a possession of a long dead Leviathan and has soaked in some of their essence. It might be an art object or everyday tool, but it is generally unsuitable for its original task - too brittle or too precious to make use of. Its primary role, instead, is to be a vessel for the Tribe’s cultural memories.

                                                        Should a Leviathan take a Heirloom into the Rift it will manifest as a Rift Creature, as an inanimate possession it is infallibly loyal to it’s owner and it can unerringly seek out its own presence in other memories. A pen that sat on a Leviathan’s desk could take it’s owner to every memory of events within that room.
                                                        Searching through the Rift takes time, but there is no need to wait until information is vitally important before taking a study session. A Leviathan who spends a significant amount of time investigating a Heirloom stores it’s dots in points that may be redeemed for:
                                                        • An answer to a short question about a Leviathan who owned the heirloom.
                                                        • A yes/no answer about something a Leviathan who owned the heirloom encountered.
                                                        • A Clue for an ongoing investigation related to one of the Heirloom’s previous owners.

                                                        Alternatively if the Leviathan is able to personally relate to Heirloom’s original owner they may trade in dots in a Heirloom to acquire and experience point to be put towards Sheol. Dots spent this way do not diminish the Heirloom’s potency. Rather, like a book that’s been read again and again, a there is nothing more to be learned.
                                                        Drawback: Nothing says the former owners of a Heirloom have to be dead. Any of of them might object to another Leviathan having a map to their memories, and Heirloom’s make great foci for rituals.
                                                        Sidebar: That Belongs in a Museum

                                                        Leviathans aren’t the only ones with interest in Heirlooms. All Heirlooms are receptacles of mystical energies and while most simply contain those energies some develop other, rarer, properties. Mystical orders such as the Aegis Kai Doru are frequently tempted either by what they could learn about the past or in using their secret knowledge to unlock an Heirloom’s practical applications as a mystical talisman. Private collectors, archaeologists and museums also have a stake in owning an Heirloom. Iremite Mummies will go to great length to acquire Heirlooms, or as they call them: Vestiges.
                                                        Despite this Heirlooms tend to cause little stir among those not in the know: vanishingly few Heirlooms predate known civilisation, most come much more recent exploits of the Tribe and aren’t markedly distinct from entirely human artefacts of the period. It’s unsure what exactly would happen if regular academics did get hold of a Heirloom that predated Tiamat’s death, such a discovery could rewrite known history but only if it wasn’t snatched by Leviathans or some other party. It would certainly make an interesting story.
                                                        Mandate of Babel (O)
