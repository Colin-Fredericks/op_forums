                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                While some of this might be subject to change with a 2e Geist book:

                I think the difference is most pronounced looking at a simple comparison. Synergy vs. Satiety.

                Synergy is about two separate beings working together. A high Synergy Sin-Eater represents a Bound and Geist closely aligned in actions. Dropping to being one of the Wretched means the two have become inseparable but completely disconnected as they battle over control of a single body with no cohesion between different states. Maintaining a relatively high Synergy is purely a good thing for the dual-entity to exist.

                Satiety is simply about keeping a dark hunger within a Beast fed. The connection between Beast and Horror doesn't change as it goes up and down. How well fed the Horror is simple dictates how a Beast interacts with their Hunger. There is no "good" level of Satiety. Do you want to be hungry, but sharp and full of raw power from the drive to hunt? Do you want to try to balance the Hunger to keep it easy to feed but the Horror sleepy and weak? Do you want to sate the Horror for a more subtle form of power in freedom and selective hunting, but risk putting the Horror to sleep and being weakened for it?

                For all the other differences, I think these traits are highly representative of the core reasons why these end up as very different games.
                Expanding upon that: your Geist, even if rather inhuman most of the time and eerily human sometimes, was indeed human long ago. Besides maintaining a high Synergy, exploring the past of your Geist is suggested as a plot hook in the Geist core. They could have been a warrior-saint, one of the darkest serial killers in history, or just someone who had a really bad day and coped as best as they could. The proclivity to be a part of death-tainted events and phenomena is a given, but Geister can come from all walks of life.

                Horrors, on the other hand, don't have the same versatility in backstory terms. They might have been an Incarnate that inspired a whole mythology and was served by other monsters who were nightmarish in their own right. With the exception of the Mimir's Wisdom Atavism, a Horror can't teach you anything new, because the Horror isn't someone else. A Geist, however, might have whole swathes of forgotten and rich history stored in its immaterial mind. There's a 3 dot Ceremony from page 161 of the G:tS core that allows you to extract information from your Geist, who is explicitly referred to as a separate entity.

                Sin-Eaters have to worry about the hopes, fears and other human-like traits of their Geister (such as they are), but they can count on the Geist to help in a pinch if they work as a team.

                Beasts are hosts to entities with no such frivolities, but cannot expect their Horror to not invade the dreamscape when Ravenous.

                The question of who has the better deal is worth exploring in-character, I believe.

                        Originally posted by Vithrir View Post
                        Even if you accept Devouring over Homecoming, the resulting entity left after the process of Devouring is complete and the human soul is nommed up by the hungry Horror is you. You aren't a human being spirit-ridden by a Horror. You're one of the Begotten. The Horror is now just as much you as the human fleshy bits are. If you were to separate your body from your Horror, your human consciousness would end. You would be the Horror, wandering in the Primordial Dream; you would probably lose most of your ability to behave like a human being, and you would probably no longer a playable character.
                        Well sort of. I agree it's never the case that your Horror takes you out for a ride around the town. However, your Horror is pretty much stuck in the Primordial Dream and you merging with it is pretty much you taking it out for a ride in its spirit skin. So the effect is rather 180 to the Geist approach where two entities share a body. Instead you're split across two bodies. The split is drastic that there's an entire section of a chapter devoted to what happens if you die, if your Horror dies, or if the two of you truly merge. Now you can can say that the Horror is YOU and on some metaphysical level I suppose that's true but the game's mechanics don't actually back it up very well.

                        The intention that you are one being split across two bodies is pretty clear but you can only really play the human body and the Horror pretty much autopilots through life (possibly because its essential nature is as alien to us as angels, ghosts, spirits, and other ephemera) except for those few times you enter your/its lair and pseudo-merge with it. Your human body is separate from your Horror and resolving that issue is one of the features of the game. But we've rather sharply digressed from the OP's notion that Beast and Geist are similar games with similar themes, which I believe has been shown not to be the case in so far as any of the gamelines are similar/dissimilar to one another.

                                                The book supports Horrors existing as separate entities, Horrors being passed down between Siblings, all Horrors being faces of the Dark Mother, and allows for none of those to be true.
                                                I'm not a particular fan of the final option unless I'm comfortable enough with the system for intensive homebrew, so my preference is that the preceding three each be as true as possible.

                                                This preference leads me to the Dark Mother having created the equivalent of avatars, her true children being these aptly named Horrors. When a Beast has their soul Devoured, the Sibling serves as a conduit (or the Horror itself acts) for a fragment of the Horror's power to ascend from the Primordial Dream to subsume the soul, taking on characteristics which we see mechanically as the individual Horror which informs our character creation. Since this is fully supported by the flavour of the book unlike the Horror being nothing more than a perverted soul, and not refuted by the mechanics, this is what I'll go with.
