                    I just have head-canon that both Arcadias are from the same instance of Arcadia, but were split into alternate versions of one another. M!Arcadia became an integrated part of the supernal realms, while C!Arcadia developed into the Hedge and, in stealing people, integrated itself as part of the cosmology at large. Both realms embody fate and time, and both embed parts of themselves into mortals (Mages and Changelings) but their methodologies are...decidedly different.

                    Or, to put it in a metaphor, the two Arcadias are twins - they have many aspects that are superficially similar and are part of the same universe, but they are two entities with their own identities, methods and, ultimately, influences upon the mortal realm.

                    Of course, I don't know a lot about Mage, much less its version of Arcadia, so I could be kinda off here. :-p

                                    Originally posted by gadren View Post
                                    I was curious if any of the official books ever said anything on this topic, and if not what do you guys think about it: how do Changelings and Mages feel about each other? Under what circumstances do you think it would be most likely for them to be friends or enemies? Do Mages connected to the watchtower in Arcadia (Acanthus) tend to have a different relationship with Changelings than the other four types of mages? Also, I assume Changelings are not considered "Sleepers", even though they are not Awakened.
                                    My general answer about this is that Changelings and Mages don't like each other on first blush but they are, when actually engaging one another, more likely to be friends than enemies; they simply have too many ways to be mutually beneficial for anything but friendly interaction to constitute the majority of proper interaction, even if only to a slim degree.

                                    Mages crave the things Changelings have. Changelings give them access to whole new areas of magical study otherwise unavailable to them. Changelings can produce odd magical trinkets, curious and impossible baubles, and the ever-unusual goblin fruit for a Mage to study, all with (relatively) minimal effort.

                                    Changelings, meanwhile, can always use the staggering firepower a Mage represents. Even a novice Mage has a degree of acuity and diversity with their magic that dwarfs most other forms of supernatural ally, and all they ask in exchange is a chance to stare at you oddly for a few minutes. For most supernaturals, the odd demands of a Mage to get to analyze someone or for a bit of their hair or what have you might seem strange, but for Changelings, it's literally just another trip to the Market.

                                    None of this means that any individual Mage is trustworthy, though, and once a Mage finds out the Lost exist, the Lost get no sleep until their curiosity is satisfied. No amount of threats or bribery will allay their rampant thirst for knowledge. Likewise, sorcerers aren't usually keen to approach anything at a disadvantage; even the most freedom-loving libertine or thearch prefers in their heart to have a position of extreme strength when approaching anything. As such, the first meetings are usually exceptionally frosty for the Awakened and exceptionally intrusive and suspicious for the Changeling.

                                    Acanthus aren't any more connected to Changelings than any other Mage, but their first meetings probably go far more smoothly than those of other Paths. An Acanthus knows a Changeling's perspective somewhat; unhinged from Time and beholding the vast weave of Fate. They know what it's like to be bound to odd laws and strange rules. That makes it easier to understand their perspectives. The downside is that the similarities turn uncanny when an Acanthus finds out that the bargains empowering a Changeling aren't with with any entity as the Acanthus would understand it, thus creating a problem of comprehension that is not easily solved.

                                    Thyrsus also kinda get along for that reason; they're in the "We know odd rules and have strange urges" support group too.
