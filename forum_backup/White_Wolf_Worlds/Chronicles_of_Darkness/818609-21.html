            Ultimately it seems like Illuminated otherness and inhumanity is left to be portrayed by each Storyteller, but I fee like guidelines that might help capturing their Illuminated-ness:

            -No Endgame. Illuminated's grand plan is all they have. There is no next step. Their achievement is as far as they have planned (usually because there is nothing left to plan after you extinguish the sun or engineer a plague that will kill all life on Earth.)

            -Utterly driven and obsessive to the point beyond human limits. Illuminated never stop to wonder if what's they are doing is right or good. It doesn't matter. They have put their minds to it, and there is no turning back.

            -Inconvenient fragile shell. It's quite clear that Illuminated are not human despite having a human body and human memories, but despite of this very few of them seems to be actually familiar with its need and basic functions which results with them often straining themselves too hard or are taken completely by surprise when they feel pain.

            -Murder is best option. First and foremost solution Illuminated seem to find when dealing with any inconvenient form of life is to end it. Each Illuminated believes in a way that they are the only thinking sentient being in the universe and that eventually anything that might kill them will try to kill them, so each Illuminated saves themselves a lot of trouble by killing anything that comes across their way at slightest opportunity unless they have a better use for mostly live thing.

            -Distractions. Illuminated no longer feel need to even bother with their virtues and vices (honestly they'd need less human willpower regaining mechanic upgrade). To them there is only They, The Goal, and Distractions. Some distractions are particularly pleasant to Illuminated and require additional effort to hold back (resolve + compulsion), such distractions are often their past people of interest (extremely bad news to those people since Illuminated usually seek to eliminate them to stop worrying about them) or addictions or phobias.
            For example Glutonious Genius will still need to resist gorging themselves after they lost interest for food in general in favor of building a blackhole in their basement, and Genius who used to be particularly afraid of germs ends up upon becoming Illuminated being unable to work in enviroment that isn't perfectly sanitary.

                                    That would break the fundamental themes of the Peerage and either destroy the player's flexibility in choosing how their Wonders work or destroy any hope of a coherent setting.

                                    In other words: Either

                                    1) Every cool-but-wrong scientific theory in history is actually true all at once. Which leads to an incoherent setting.
                                    2) Geniuses can only use the theories that are actually true. Which removes player flexibility.
                                    3) Geniuses can use theories which are false. Which is what we have now.


                                    Anyway. There's nothing wrong with including superhero tropes in Genius. Even Girl Genius uses the runs-on-nonsense trope (it's mentioned in the novelizations) and we'll never stop players from building powered armour and fighting crime if we wanted to (which I don't, the Irridum Sentinels are cool). It's far better to accept the superheroic tropes and tie them into Transgression and Bitter Disapointment.

                                                        I think we should take another look at how the book defines Obligation so we can identify how it is tied to Morality, and then identify how it could be more like the Integrity traits of 2ED.

                                                        The Inspired are extreme creatures, capable of great and terrible transgressions. To fight the corrosive influence of those crimes against the natural order, most geniuses focus on the one thing that still ties them to mundane humanity: their Obligation to it. Their Obligation may be to improve, to protect, or even to punish, but it ultimately ties them to their unInspired brethren. Obligation monitors how close a genius is to losing all ties with humanity and becoming a true mad scientist, one of the Illuminated who has lost all concern for the well-being and dignity of others, and who sees humans as we might see laboratory mice. As Obligation falls, the genius becomes less and less able to interact with normal humans, and less and less interested in doing so for any decent reason.

                                                        Geniuses quickly learn that Obligation is more than Morality: it is tied to the standards of sane and normal behavior that humans apply to their fellows. Many acts that a genius can rationalize as for the greater good―or even demonstrate to be beneficial and useful―are transgressions, since they violate the standards of regular human behavior and distance a genius from the mortals in his charge.
                                                        Emphasis mine.

                                                        So what can we gather from this?

                                                        1) Obligation is about making an effort to think like a regular mortal, in other words, distance yourself somehow from your Inspired worldview.
                                                        2) Obligation can mean different things between one Genius and another. This is similar to the questions of what constitute Breaking Points for Integrity. That is to say, Obligation is not objective.
                                                        3) Obligation cannot be used to rationalize acts which would provoke a Breaking Point in the vast majority of human beings.

                                                        So, here's what I'm thinking. We can stick to the 1 to 10 scale, but make it easier to go from one extreme to the other, to reflect the instability of Inspiration. One day, the Genius might have a firm grasp on how mortals think through effort, but care less the next day.

                                                        We could associate a Condition to each of the extremes.

                                                        Near 10, you would have a Condition that represents your Paragon status, which would allow you to negate some of the penalties of Jabir. However, keeping up this level of Obligation should be taxing and tempt Geniuses to move away from mortal ways of thinking. Perhaps Social rolls to interact with mortals could deplete your Mania reserves, or your Obligation naturally depletes over time.

                                                        Near 1, your Condition of Rogue marks you as dangerous and increases Jabir, but since you are closer to the Inspired worldview, you should gain a bonus to interacting with other Geniuses, Manes, Wonders and other supernaturals relevant to your worldview.

                                                        What I don't know is what Conditions could be associated to the middle of the scale. A Genius with average Obligation should be caught between a rock and a hard place, similar to Beasts at mid Satiety.

                                                        What I would like to is an Obligation that is not bought with Experiences, but handled through scenes that either bring you closer to or further away from mortals. We could something like the Satiety Potential chart, with certain actions granting dice that you roll to increase or decrease your Obligation.

                                                        How does that sound?

                                                            I like it, in a general sense. I'm more for not requiring XP for something that almost seems to be intended to be lost.

                                                            EDIT: I think it should strongly interact with Touchstones. There are many tales of a mad scientist who has reconsidered when something/someone improtant to them was affected. Or damned themselves completely when they sacrificed even that. I think Touchstones (like Vampire has), would be a good idea to reflect all the ways a Genius has to stay/become "Restrained".

                                                            I think we all generally agree that High Obligation Genii are: Empathic (even if only in an understanding/abstract sense) of unInspired, Restrained in their pursuit and application of Science.
                                                            Low Obligation: Could care less about (most) Mere Mortals, Unrestrained in their pursuit and application of SCIENCE!

                                                            Middle Obligation then seems like they are the Genii that are seen as risky or dangerous, but still mostly mean well. They have lines they don't cross, even if those lines may be odd or still beyond some people's.

                                                            Low Obligation Genii are still distinct from Illuminated in that they still have a few lines left, or they at least know what lines are. (A low-Obligation Genius might know chopping up orphans for parts is wrong, but do so anyways, while an Illuminated wonders why someone left some free material unguarded)

                                                            Elaboration upon Touchstones

                                                            You could even have one (at least) be Catalyst inspired/based/dependent:
                                                            • Klagen: The source of their sorrow. It reminds them of why they bother, and repeating it themselves can be lead them to cast off all care.
                                                            • Hoffnung: Their vision of a new/better world (for all, or just someone). Seeing their actions threaten it might shock them back into Obligation, or efforts to "force it" might decay their ethics further.
                                                            • Grimm: Their target of anger. It might drive them to be better, or right wrongs, or drive them to commit the very same wrongs in pursuit of revenge.
                                                            • Neid: Their need for acknowledgement. They might keep on the "straight and narrow" for continued high regard, or descend into the depth in search of it.
                                                            • Staunen: Their sense of wonder. They might ensure they can share their insights and vistas with others, or they might sacrifice other to delve into things only they can see.


                                                            The idea being, the Touchstone(s) are the reason why a Genius bothers... and their loss leads them to question why they should bother.

                                                            Idea: Touchstone "evolution" based on Obligation? Low Obligation has very specific focus, while High Obligation has broader focus? Rather than simply becoming "detached" like in Vampire, that is.

                                                            EDIT: May even be able to link Larvae to Touchstones instead of Transgressions. Or more specifically, a Transgression that costs one a Touchstone. Like the brains of the ethics board that mocked the Neid, or the heart of the Hoffnung's beloved daughter.

                                                            Will elaborate further if someone has questions.
