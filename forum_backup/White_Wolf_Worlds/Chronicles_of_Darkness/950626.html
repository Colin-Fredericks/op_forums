                                          A 1 doesn't even mean, "bad" it just means, "below average."

                                          I feel like there's a tendency to overstate the in-character extent of how bad being "below average," is, because Attributes are described on a 1-5 scale, with 3-5 all being "above average," leaving the impression that a 1 has to carry everything from "C- student," to, "bumbling idiot that can't figure out how to tie their own shoe laces," and disregard WHW making the proper case that anything below the "C- student," level of bad at Intelligence should be handled via the Conditions system.

                                          Someone with no Conditions and Intelligence 1/Wits 4, would most likely be someone others see as, "book dumb, street smart." Outside of academic and scientific arenas, most people would see them as competent and conversant in practical matters; even if things like theory and trivia are probably things they don't know well.

                                          Adding in the Firearms specialty of "theory" is a bit odd (what does that even do?), but actually makes me think of Archer. Sterling is pretty stereotypical (if comedic because of the nature of the show) of what low Int/high Wits can look like (even if his Wits isn't has high as he likes to think it is) with high Firearms and some strange specialties. A good example is his ability to identify the guns other people are firing, and track bullet counts in the middle of highly chaotic situations.

                                                  Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                                  A 1 doesn't even mean, "bad" it just means, "below average."

                                                  I feel like there's a tendency to overstate the in-character extent of how bad being "below average," is, because Attributes are described on a 1-5 scale, with 3-5 all being "above average," leaving the impression that a 1 has to carry everything from "C- student," to, "bumbling idiot that can't figure out how to tie their own shoe laces," and disregard WHW making the proper case that anything below the "C- student," level of bad at Intelligence should be handled via the Conditions system.

                                                  Someone with no Conditions and Intelligence 1/Wits 4, would most likely be someone others see as, "book dumb, street smart." Outside of academic and scientific arenas, most people would see them as competent and conversant in practical matters; even if things like theory and trivia are probably things they don't know well.

                                                  Adding in the Firearms specialty of "theory" is a bit odd (what does that even do?), but actually makes me think of Archer. Sterling is pretty stereotypical (if comedic because of the nature of the show) of what low Int/high Wits can look like (even if his Wits isn't has high as he likes to think it is) with high Firearms and some strange specialties. A good example is his ability to identify the guns other people are firing, and track bullet counts in the middle of highly chaotic situations.
                                                  I second this view. You're 'below average'; if you're playing a truly terrible character, that's better represented through Conditions.

                                                  For example, grabbing this Condition from a brilliant GM in a game I am playing (Requiem-Masquerade):

                                                  Unskilled

                                                  Your character is naturally bad at something. Choose a single Skill or Skill Specialty, such as Expression (Singing) or Subterfuge (Lying). Your character always rolls a chance die on rolls with this Skill or Skill Specialty, no matter how high a dice pool his Attributes would normally entitle him to. This Condition can be paired with the Delusional Condition to make a character wildly overestimate his abilities and believe he is an expert at something he’s terrible at.

                                                  Resolution: Your character works to overcome his deficiency and purchases a Skill dot or Specialty for the relevant Skill.

                                                  Beat: You fail a roll for the Skill or Specialty you are deficient at.

                                                  GM’s Notes: This Condition is intended to model characters who are bad at something but have high enough Attributes to compensate for it. In one Vampire: The Masquerade setting book, there was an elder Toreador who was a poseur and a catastrophically terrible artist. His superhuman Presence and Dexterity dot ratings meant he was still better at the arts than most mortals, even factoring in the -1 untrained penalty for no dots in Expression.
                                                  You could possibly tailor the above to Attributes instead, for example making any call for a Strength roll would automatically be a Chance Die (signifying an elderly, decrepit character that is infirm - this in comparison to just having one-dot in the Attribute that simply means the character has a weak handshake or can't open a jar if it's too tight).

                                                  As for my broader thoughts on what a one represents in the game-space:

                                                  1 Intelligence = unintelligent, forgetful, poor at academia
                                                  1 Wits = it makes the character a meat-head / bimbo, not very perceptive
                                                  1 Resolve = lazy, unmotivated, easily distracted or mentally taxed

                                                  1 Strength = weakling (seen a character take 1 Strength and the GM represent this as him having a limp handshake)
                                                  1 Dexterity = clumsy, uncoordinated
                                                  1 Stamina = fat, thin, unfit, soft like a marshmallow

                                                  1 Presence = unremarkable, plain, boring
                                                  1 Manipulation = ineloquent, inarticulate, uncogent
                                                  1 Composure = anxious, easily perturbed, shy, again not very perceptive

                                                  If you want to tailor the above Condition to each of the above attributes to be automatic Chance Die, I'd title it as:

                                                  Intelligence = Amnesic
                                                  Wits = Witless
                                                  Resolve = Listless

                                                  Strength = Infirm
                                                  Dexterity = Ungainly
                                                  Stamina = Frail

                                                  Presence = Unlikable
                                                  Manipulation = Incoherent
                                                  Composure = Meek

                                                  edit: The way I think about it, the average person / mortal is deficient in at least one attribute (worthy of one-dot). Something to consider when populating your world with NPCs is that they're more likely to have mostly 2s and a 1 in their attributes as opposed to having a 3+ in any of their attributes. A three to me is somewhat remarkable, like a person with Intelligence 3 is notably book-smart and Presence 3 is notably charismatic (in the context of people you meet in your day-to-day life). Four is notable enough to garner celebrity. Five is freakish.

                                                  3 = one of the best in your city
                                                  4 = one of the best in your country
                                                  5 = one of the best in the world
