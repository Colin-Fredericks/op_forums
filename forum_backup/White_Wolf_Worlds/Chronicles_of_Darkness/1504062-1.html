                                            Again, I'm at work and just don't have the energy to deconstruct how the emphasis on evil is indicative of not engaging with Hunter's themes, or Chronicles's themes on the whole, for whatever reason, but I do want at least point more in the direction of what my issue is than an admittedly glib response will do, so to that end, I'm gonn post a bit from one scrapped response that I feel still really worked well.
                                            I mean, Cheiron is not made up of victims. Taskforce VALKYRIE is not made up of victims.
                                            You're starting from a top down perspective that is not helping you to understand the picture Hunter is painting.

                                            The books demonstrate time and again that engagement with the supernatural is not a thing that people casually do for almost any given reason, given that the supernatural in all of it's forms its actively psychologically and spiritually stressful and damaging-most people's responses are to try and keep themselves as distant from the supernatural as they can after one or two exceptional intrustions. Mortal investigators from the Chronicles corebook are often individuals who are in the middle of one of those exceptional intrusions and are written with a general understanding that most of them will go back to not looking too closely at the shadows once a chronicle is over, with a rare few exceptions diving deeper to understand it better to better interact with it (or, more realistically, be even better at not engaging with it, or at least not without protections).

                                            The sort of person who becomes a hunter, a person who not only interacts and investigates the supernatural but actively work to harm in it a variety of ways in the name of a variety of causes and reasons, is an expectation above and beyond that, and the general root of that is a resolution against the harm they dealt with in their one or two exceptional experiences with the supernatural. Hunter: the Vigil First Edition's corebook is pretty upfront and explicit about that, pretty well opening it's world building chapter at the start of the book with "Yeah, pretty much every hunter is a victim of supernatural violence of some sort."

                                            So if you wanted to provide a fuller line, a non-reductive line with this, it might go something like "The Field Projects Division of the Cheiron Group and Task Force: Valkyrie are not, institutionally, victims in any meaningful sense, but there are things about them that are attractive to victims of supernatural violence that lead to making up a lot of those organizations with those victims."​
                                            And as addendum to that, it's in that attraction that more of the overall point is found.
