        Originally posted by The Boz View Post
        The wording of it is confusing. So... Crafts (knitting) comfort blanket is the intended use?
        Dramatic comfort blankets. Its supposed to be the religious use of Fetishism, like non-supernatural versions of what Werewolves use. Rather than "These are my lucky socks. I never miss when I snipe things while wearing them!" its supposed to be "This is the silver cross I found in the dirt along the roadside during my first hunt. It represents my covenant to God, and protects me from evil influence. So long as it is with me, I will never fail!".

        Again, it has two parts:
        1a: Free WP points that can be used on anything.
        1b: But you can't gain any WP if you lose it.
        2a: If you use WP on one Specialty, you get an Exceptional Success on 3 instead of 5
        2b: But if you fail you get a Dramatic Failure instead.

        That is what it does.

                        YMMV but this sounds like an engagement problem:
                        The per-scene WP from anchors should not be hard to get at all, and there really don't need to be story allowances for it. Your short-tempered character lashes out once. Your indecent character makes an off-color comment. Your egotistical character pointedly sneers at someone they perceive to be beneath them.

                                            Originally posted by The Boz View Post
                                            How often do players at your tables regain Willpower through those things?
                                            That really depends on the player and character. Character with Vice-Ridden (or Demons with Living the Lie) can usually manage 1WP every other scene, sometimes doubling up and regaining Willpower in back-to-back scenes. Those without anything enhancing Vice (or equivalents) that also make no exceptional effort to recover Willpower, maybe every 4 scenes or so. We have really long sessions and pretty tight scene pacing, though, so one of our sessions will usually cover many scenes.

                                            Aside from the WP aspect, treating Vice this way means it's a consistently reoccuring trait that really defines the character, not a vague thing they engage in once a session or so.

                                            All of that said, the Merit is still incredibly powerful, because even assuming 1WP per scene (the max recovery Vice-alikes), that's still 5 scenes worth of Willpower instantly at the start of every session.

                                                Originally posted by Tessie View Post
                                                Look at all the examples for regaining a single WP. What's eliminated is that you need to set yourself at risk. Fulfilling the Anchor in a dramatically relevant fashion is still very much a thing.
                                                Why can't you make any obvious display of a character's truest self 'dramatic' though?
                                                The display is the drama. You don't need to wait until xyz story conditions are 'just right'.

                                                You lashed out at someone, and now you've set back your negotiation, or temporarily damaged a relationship.
                                                You made an inappropriate remark, and now your new contact has a first impression of you that's going to be hard to change.

                                                I'm just having trouble imagining a circumstance where you could fulfill any given Vice that would not also be dramatic by definition. Any scene with something interesting happening can be made more dramatic by introducing these behaviors.

                                                If nothing interesting is happening in your scene, it shouldn't be a scene, it should be downtime.

                                                Specific examples (from GMC):
                                                Pessimistic: Regain a Willpower point when he refuses to act in a scene because he believes anything he does will be fruitless.
                                                Hateful: Regain a Willpower point when he spends time in a scene persecuting the object of his hate.
                                                Deceitful: Regain a Willpower point when she successfully maintains a lie despite others having the opportunity to see through it.
                                                Arrogant: Regain a Willpower point when he takes an opportunity to express his greater worth relative to someone else.
                                                Untrustworthy: Regain a Willpower point when your character breaks a promise for her own sake.
                                                Treacherous: Regain a Willpower point when your character betrays a person or group.
                                                Ambitious: Regain a Willpower point when your character attempts to increase her social standing instead of pursuing more useful activity.
                                                Cruel: Regain a Willpower point when your character needlessly victimizes someone in his power.
                                                Violent: Regain a Willpower point when your character resolves a scene by using needless violence.
                                                Greedy: Regain a Willpower point when you deny another character an advantage by taking it yourself.
                                                Corrupt: Regain a Willpower point when your character misuses status or influence over a group on behalf of herself or another interested party.
                                                Cowardly: Regain a point of Willpower when your character persuades or tricks another character into a risky situation instead of doing it himself.
                                                Hasty: Regain a point of Willpower when your character forces a scene to start by taking decisive action.

                                                None of these are 'dramatically relevant' in and of themselves. It's the actions themselves that cause the drama, they don't require pre-existing drama or ideal circumstances.
