    And now, Transcendents/Avatars!

    Avatar

    "Oh God, save me?" Sorry, not in the mood.

    The Avatar remembers her past, and thinks of a discarded husk. They are still recognizable as having once been mortal, and can still walk through the herds of two-legged sheep that surround them without notice, but they are divine wolves. Maybe they reached too far into dark magic and saw no reason to stop, maybe they were so warped by a wicked influence they don't remember what they lost, maybe they were just born a little more powerful than average and learned to revel in it in murderous ways. Whatever the case, the Avatar is both the least and most human of all slashers, for they are small gods of murder, mystical powerhouses in ways comparable to true monsters, all the might of which is attached to an intellect that knows how much of it is only in comparison to humans. At their core, Avatars are ultimately just as insecure and empty as Seekers, and are driven to murder because of it - to confirm their identity rather than form it, but the Avatar ultimately still needs to kill to constantly remind themselves that they are strong. Avatars are the ultimate supernatural bullies, and the teasing only ends when their victims are dead.

    Avatars tend to fall into one of two camps. Thankfully for everyone nearby, the majority are loners; these scourges are glory hogs, and other slashers, especially other Avatars, leave less room for the Avatar to flex their powers. This isn't to say that they scorn company, not hardly. Rather, loner Avatars embark on sprees of gore and terror with the express purpose of sowing fear and, to the Avatar's mind, respect in the mortals around them. If these slashers happen to have the same hunting territory as other slashers, they often are driven to compete with said other killers for space in the tabloids; even if the competition exists only within the Avatar's own mind, having "rivals" that detract from the Avatar's own exploits drives them to ever more spectacular (and lurid) deeds to get the media to focus on them and not their contemporaries. They're generally not stupid about it (few things end the murderous career of a slasher in a more embarrassing way than being arrested), but loner Avatars tend to burn like homicidal candles; twice as brightly and half as long. Unfortunately, Avatars who were once part of a cabal of Seekers, increasingly common in the age of both the internet and the slasher epidemic, tend to be of the opinion that the very fact they have powers means they're secure enough to feel like others don't detract from their ego, and the same need for validation that drives them to murder leads to these Avatars forming regular pantheons of murder. Most tend to be lead by the Avatars among the slashers or are composed entirely of them, but even the vanishingly rare ones where the Avatar takes a back seat find themselves with a few minions. Avatars are still the scourge version of Seekers and desperately seek emotional validation, and few creatures do that better than cultists, and so social Avatars are cult leaders. Most set themselves up as the dark gods (or incarnations of the devil) they see themselves as, but some instead cast themselves as the natural evolution of man, or an ideal to aspire to rather than an inherently better creature. Whatever the case, a frightening number seek to raise favored cultists up to their level - like most narcissists, Avatars feel anyone worthy of being called their friend is deserving of the world they are making, and anyone who hurts an Avatar's priest will find a very lethal guardian angel on their heels. Not infrequently, these include monsters; Seekers were already rather learned for slashers, and Avatars seek lore to better carry themselves in the shadowy world and to anticipate potential threats. A few sacred beasts or even more inhuman gods in their pantheon is an excellent way to cement their personal mythos.

    If Seekers are easily confused for hunters, Avatars are the kings of being false positives for other monsters. By certain metrics, they aren't quite slashers either, but a cell bringing garlic for a vampire is going to be surprised when the self-proclaimed Fang of the Night attacks them in broad daylight while in the middle of a church service. In comparison to other monsters, these slashers want attention and notoriety to a degree, and quickly realize that these new blasphemers are trying to keep their existence a secret and under the radar. All the better to force them into situations where they have to chose between secrecy and the scourge getting away with another atrocity. Social Avatars are even worse, because unlike most mastermind-type monsters, Avatars relish in being able to lead from the front, so that both their enemies and followers may tremble and gape at their power. A few more confident Avatars may even blackmail hunters into getting rid of the supernatural competition or tempt them into satisfying their voracious appetite for lore in return for their cult's own expertise. All in all, an Avatar facing a hunter doesn't just want the hunter to fail and die, but to fail and die in such a way that they realize they never should have tried. In most cases. In the worst, the Avatar decides that such bravery to oppose a god deserves respect - and recruitment to their cult. Few things are more creatively sadistic than a god trying to get a hunter to pray for mercy.

    Tells: Avatars are showoffs, either to get the media to gaze upon their deeds and tremble, or to prove to their cultists and peers that they are worthy of their title. Their Tells are thus bombastic and brutal, showing their supernatural powers in such a way that investigators are either completely baffled or intimidated by the realization their quarry had to be more than human. Beyond that, it depends on the exact personal beliefs of the slasher about their nature and powers, but outright apocalyptic imagery, self-written prophecies of doom, or taunts aimed at the concept of humanity itself are common. The cults of Avatars are even more lurid and brutal in their rites to appease their master, though a bit more out of the way, as the very fact a cult is making rituals to appease and honor them is good enough for the ego of most Avatars until they feel it's time for a godly hunt. Of course, the thing about being unsubtle is precisely that, and figuring out exactly what the Avatar's self-image is becomes a break in the case to predicting them.

    Examples: The reservation bars its doors at night now, its inhabitants afraid of more than prejudice. Wendigo Bill stalks their homes, once a white man deformed by a blessing the shaman he brutalized was too terrified to perform properly; he has come to revel in the feel of Native flesh dribbling down his chin, but the relatives of his first meal need to be reminded of their weakness. Okiku Ikiryo holds her entire town in thrall, people regularly praying to the bone-covered shrine she once oversaw so that her disembodied soul doesn't kill them with their own bodies, even butchering the occasional outsider to change out some of the construction by themselves.The scholars bring the corpses of monsters to Esther, not knowing the reason she pays them in money and security against prying cops is that she takes out the best parts to augment her prosthetic arm, every design of which must be tested on a live subject for her to be content with it. The Cobalt Rebis emerges from their sanctum to inspect the intoxicating potions their alchemical circle made for them to enjoy. The best out of every batch is tutored by the master, the worst must supply blood for the lesson, by themselves or otherwise; they are rather forgiving to those who bring them along to harvest the tithe, naturally.

    Talent: Personal Myth --- Avatars are exceptions to the rules of slashers being relatively weak, supernaturally speaking. While they aren't as strong as some monsters, an Avatar believes so completely in their myth that whatever force made them scourges has made them conduits for their own belief. Like Seekers, they do not have Addicted (Murder), but Addicted (Grandeur), and may risk Willpower in pursuit of actions that support their own personal sense of power. Moreover, each Avatar has a personal supernatural identity; the Cobalt Rebis views themself as the alchemical embodiment of the worth of a human life, while Okiku Ikiryo styles herself as both a living woman and a mighty onyro. As a result, they have the ability to manifest new abilities as their own sense of invulnerability demands; in addition to what Dread Powers they normally have, all Avatars have two "free slots" that may be filled with Dread Powers of their own choosing after an hour-long ritual that incorporates their Tell and leaves evidence of it behind (for a lone Avatar, this generally involves menacing their community in a ritual way, for social Avatars it likely involves their cult in some fashion). These Dread Powers must fit the myth somehow, but given how the Avatar is literally living a power fantasy, they often manifest traits that are useful before being directly thematic (Okiku, for instance, might manifest Hex one day to curse someone who she regards as not giving her shrine enough respect, and Infernal the next day despite not being a demon as her "ghost flames"). These Dread Powers last for a day or until the Avatar's stimulus is invoked (see Frailty), and cannot be changed until they have been reverted. In addition to their free slots, Avatars manifest up to three Dread Powers as part of their fundamental myth and general theatrical nature, like Madness and Terror, Predator's Sense, and Reborn (though of all the scourge Undertakings, Avatars are the most varied).

    Frailty: Man in a Mask --- Avatars' grand gestures are driven by their inadequacy. The same penalties for Willpower recovery Seekers suffer apply to Avatars, but their penalties begin two days after their last murder (or Addiction satisfaction for Avatars with Integrity), and increase by one every two days thereafter (the same taste of power ironically makes them far less patient and more easily frustrated). In addition, Avatars have a personal stimulus that effectively breaks their personal myths by reminding them of the human underneath them. This is different for each Avatar (for Wendigo Bill, it's the seeing the name of the shaman who accidentally cursed him due to Bill's own greed and impatience, for Esther it's being wounded by industrial machinery), but whatever the case, this reverts their Personal Myth Dread Power slots to their unclaimed state, ends all ongoing Dread Powers, and the Avatar must spent a Willpower point to avoid fleeing the scene or immediately attacking the stimulus, suffering a -2 penalty on all dice pools so long as it remains present.

                    mythic: Hunters often use the word “mythic” to identify legendary monsters, locations, or relics that either don’t exist (and therefore can’t be hunted) or are a once-in-a-lifetime find. Examples include: the phoenix, the Lost Continent of Mu, and Poseidon’s Trident.
