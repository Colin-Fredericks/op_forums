            Originally posted by Acrozatarim View Post

            Things about Primal Urge that I've already mentioned, condensed here:
            - It's far better value than in 1e.
            - Increasing your Primal Urge means you can spend more Essence and regenerate faster. Expect a superior rate of increased Essence per turn compared to 1e; look more to the later games and B&S for the sort of rates involved.
            - The old Primal Urge drawbacks basically encouraged passivity - at high levels, you ended up unable to interact effectively with humans and with a strong incentive to sleep on a Locus and not leave it much. The new drawbacks encourage the opposite - activity.
            - The story implication of this is that high Primal Urge Uratha are no longer hoary old veterans that you seek out because they might know something you need or because you need their help with something specific - instead, they're story drivers. A high Primal Urge Uratha is an instigator, a paragon of the hunt and thus a creature that does hunt rather than sleeping in a magic cave somewhere.

            This has all already been mentioned here and elsewhere

            We can't really give you more than this, as it involves getting into the details of the mechanics to explain things.

                        Relevant to this thread, posted elsewhere:

                        Originally posted by Dave Brookshaw View Post
                        As for the Hisil in second edition...

                        * I describe the inside of the Gauntlet. In 1e you stood in one world for the time it took to cross, then crossed. Now, you vanish from one world, hang inside the Gauntlet for a few Turns and then appear on the other side. Sometimes, you see things in there.

                        * We've given Helios a purpose and motivation other than just "Luna's twin brother," which doesn't alter how Helions act toward werewolves but reveals much about the Hisil's "physics"

                        * A lot of attention is placed on describing what the Shadow looks, feels, and tastes like - it's a sensory experience, especially for an Uratha.

                        * Werewolve's Essence-senses are explicitly perceived as their sense of smell/taste. You don't *see* dormant spirits, you taste them in the not-air.

                        * One of the example spirits is a big-box retailer; they act like corpulent slum-lords of the Shadow, enslaving the lesser spirits linked to the goods and consumers.
                        I VERY MUCH LIKE.
