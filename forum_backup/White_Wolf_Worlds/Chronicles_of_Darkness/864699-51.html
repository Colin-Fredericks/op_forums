            It does this for the sake of "an ephemeral entity can harm a subject with a power represented by the innate ability to wound subjects within a certain range, dealing lethal damage based on the rolled successes as though it were an attack with a weapon, even if that subject is in a state where it appears pale and semi-transparent and the sounds that they make are distorted and the world otherwise operates at a remove from the entity in question," a reading that seems to serve no purpose other than to take away any need for an entity to make its presence known in order to directly wound a physical being in contravention of the entire process represented by the Manifestation system introduced in this edition of the game.

            What is the actual concrete benefit of this reading to players of the Revised Storytelling System, given this?

            It is described as functioning like a weapon, but not as actually being a weapon, and the word weapon is put in quotation marks every time it is used. This isn’t just a case of the text failing to call Blast an attack explicitly, this is a case of the text going out of its way to avoid directly correlating Blast with an attack at every opportunity.
            This is untrue. The word "weapon" is used three times, only once in quotation marks — and here I must remind you that this is a Numen that represents the ability of an ephemeral entity to wound at a distance in ways that do not necessarily bear any resemblance to the usual things the ChroD system provides additional damaging successes to rolls for — once with the words "wounds as a" in front of it — for which see the first point — and once with no qualifying grammar whatsoever. Leaning hard into semantics is the only way in which this is not clearly intended as "this is mechanically a weapon, even if in the fiction it is not represented as a weapon."

            None of which, you may notice, involve an activation roll. If the intent was for Blast to be a similarly passive, costless Numen, it could just say “the entity can make attacks at a distance of up to 10 yards per dot of power. These attacks ignore the target’s defense, do lethal damage, and the entity can spend 2 essence to increase the damage of a successful attack by 1” or something similar.
            This in turn brings up another point: If Blast is not supposed to be an attack, then one of the entire points of ephemeral beings applying their Defense against every attack (including ranged attacks which normally ignore that Advantage) — that they have an overwhelming advantage against characters who do not possess their bane — is obviated by a basic Numen for a class of entity that by default typically represents being armored through a Defense value derived from a single Attribute. I'm reasonably certain the ability to send a stream of fire at another spirit is not supposed to bypass the other spirit having four-inch-thick steel plates for skin.

            Also, if it's not a ranged attack, then the cover mechanics need not apply either, since the entity doesn't suffer range penalties and the description doesn't say anything about it needing to be able to perceive the target. (I hope it should be clear why the default generic "wound at a distance" power should not have this as a universal quality.)

            The R tag is a pretty obvious use for those Numena under my reading of Blast, since Blast lacks it.
            The rationale for it not being able to be used from across the Gauntlet is roughly identical to the rationale for it not being usable from Twilight: the interface between the physical world and an unManifested state is not without interference, as 2e's material on Twilight consistently describes.

            As well, Blast doesn’t provide an entity that can influence Fire a source of it, or allow any entities to move objects. In fact, dealing damage to material creatures really seems like a very narrow application of both Telekinesis and Firestarter, and would certainly not be the primary reason I would give either to anything but a spirit.
            That was, in point of fact, the reason I said "you could just say that a fiery Blast can set things on fire or a forceful blast can move things around."

            In fact, turn this comparison around. If Blast can’t be used to affect material beings while the user is in Twilight, how is it not inferior to both of these Numina?
            Because it costs nothing to use unless the entity is actively aiming to deal more damage in a single strike, which there is no need to do if the entity can hit its target turn after turn from an invisible state with no up-front investment of Essence.

            I don’t agree. What else is its niche? Under your interpretation, what does Blast offer that an ephemeral gun doesn’t? As a storyteller, why would I want use up one of an entity’s limited Numina on Blast rather than giving it a ranged weapon?
            Because ephemeral objects are uncommon and relatively mundane, because an ephemeral gun does not have an intrinsic thematic tie to the entity that uses it, and because there is nothing to suggest that a gun has infinite ammo just because it exists in Twilight.

            Also because your goal as a Storyteller is expressly not defined by the system as "optimize the antagonists into hard gates on protagonist progress based on their ability to hit ghosts."

            I know you don’t agree with that supposition, I was merely expressing that I find the contingency of using Image first to be inconsistent with that supposition. And while you may be content to interpret the rules through the lens of what exists in CofD inspirational media, I prefer to interpret them based on the words that are written in the books.
            The fiction and use-advice of the setting is, in fact, the words that are written in the books, and generally has a better track record for not containing unintended side-effects of literalistic reads on the mechanics.

            Nobody writing on the books has seen fit to include anything to the effect of "a mortal stepping into the yard of a haunted house may suffer grievous injury as the wrathful shade freezes the blood in their bones," but there's plenty of examples on the books of "this power only works if the narrative has proceeded in a way that allows it to work" — Aggressive Meme requires the entity to be in any position to talk to somebody to use it, Awe works specifically on characters who are looking at the entity, Left-Handed Spanner needs the entity to "touch" its target to work, and so on.
