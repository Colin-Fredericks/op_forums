    Can a mage evenly match a werewolf in melee or hand-to-hand combat?

    02-03-2017, 05:59 PM
    Incoming stupid question ...

    To make things easier, let's say they're both of equal "level," whatever that translates into.

    I figure if a mage (Adamantine Arrow) wanted they could specialize in melee combat and use all of their abilities to become Neo (the Matrix: Revolutions).

    However, I'm having trouble seeing how a mage could physically keep up with a werewolf if the goal is to match them in: strength, speed, life regen, etc. I'm not very knowledgeable in werewolf stuff, though.

    It seems as if a melee mage would just opt to incorporate melee-flavor into their spell-casting and just use spells that more or less mimic or approximate the things that a werewolf could do. Instead of throwing a car with raw strength, he might tap it and Force Push the vehicle (I know he doesn't need to.) Or instead of raw speed, he'll just use his brand of Celerity. Instead of life regen (or in addition to), use tons of damage mitigation that make him seem like he can go a few rounds in a boxing match with a werewolf.

    Anyone have an experience with this before? Any players that wanted to pummel a werewolf into paste on the wolf's terms? Anecdotal advice says a mage should plot from afar and engage only when certain of the outcome. Does this mean a mage and a werewolf could not go toe-to-toe gladiatorial arena style?

    Is this really difficult to do without committing a crapton of resources?

    I assume the main reason for a bunch of mechanical restraints is to keep the game lines sufficiently separate in playstyles. So if you want to be the strongest tank that ever was, you play Werewolf.


    (Sidenote: When I think about it, Neo isn't really stronger. The physics of the matrix just succumb to his whim in a way that makes him appear absurdly powerful.)

                      it's hard. Really hard. not impossible, but really really hard and pretty vulgar and reach demanding too.
                      U probably need turn momentum (forces) with extra reaches to become immune to any physical attack from the giant wolf size enemy u are facing , set it to dodge as a reflexive action each turn. That should get u covered against fangs and brute strenght. Each time the werewolf tries to bite your throat, u simply dodge automatically.
                      Then u probably need a forces effect (don't remember the exact rote but i think its forces 2 or 3) to power up your fists,, giving them lethal damage and set the potency high so they can deal 4-5 or more potency in damage each attack, then spend extra reaches so your attacks can produce the knocked down and stunned tilt BOTH.
                      Now u have only with Forces a physically untouchable mage that can deal impressive damage with his bare hands.
                      Only forces 3 required. Of course u are only going to "almost" match the werewolf and not actually gonna kill him, since he simply keeps regenerating all damage each turn. Also he can fuck u up with spirits and gifts.

                      Now if u really want to build a werewolf hunter killing machine who can face a werewolf in a fight and probably win u need a thyrsus with forces or an obrimos with life and spirit. U need a high level mage, capable of dealing agg damage. 4 or 5 in the arcanas and high gnosis and mana...

                      use turn momentum to dodge all physical attacks, t
                      hen use spirit to shield yourself from numina, influence and gifts ( clash of wills)
                      , use spirit to get yourself invisible to spirits and spirits perception powers,
                      use Life to buff ur strenght and dexterity and stamina, and
                      use forces to deal 4 to 5 or more agg damage with bare hands with both stunned and knocked down conditions on ur enemies. Thats a pretty bad enemy to face for a werewolf.

                      Don't know about regenerating damage but u need at least life 4 or 5 to match werewolf regeneration imho.
                      also time and fate are not bad at all.

                      Use time acceleration to win the initiative or go for a clash of wills against the werewolf ability to interrupt other actions (wolf form, shapeshifting reflexive) , and use fate to gain 12+ dices on all your unarmed attacks, clash of wills or whatever.
                      U can even give yourself a huge amount of bonus dices on your attacks and the 8 again too. Imagine a mage with 6+ strength, 5+ automatic aggr damage on his fists+ stunned and knocked down contidions each attack, and 10+ extra dices on all his attacks with reroll 8 on brawl.

                      yep. That's pretty scary.

                      But wolves have other ways to fight back too, gifts spirits allies fetishes...
                      so i don't know -white rooms don't make too much sense as always.

                      anyway i think i answered your question .)

                                                      Originally posted by kryptonitekrusher View Post
                                                      Is there a freaking exercise pamphlet or something? I'd love to be able to think of a thing I want to do and know instantly what Arcana I need to do that.
                                                      Read the Practices and the Purviews in the rulebook.
                                                      Combine one with the other, and look to the Creative Thaumaturgy section for advice.

                                                      So say for instance you wanted to keep some sunlight in your pocket for a rainy day, or an aggressive Vampire.
                                                      You could either use Force 4 to Pattern Sunlight into behaving like a tangible object for as long as you want, allowing you to literally bundle it up and keep it in your pocket.
                                                      Or Matter 4 to change the properties of, say for instance, a Glass sphere to contain light until you need it based entire on refraction.

                                                      Or say you wanted to transform say... A Vampire into a Chair. That would require Patterning to completely change something under the spell's purview, and since you're modifying a Corpse into an inanimate object, you'd need that much in Death and Matter. So Death 4, Matter 4.

                                                          Its actually pretty easy. Kinetic Blow with enough Potency and a good weapon can deal enough damage to kill someone in Gauru in a single hit. Armor, defense boosts and mage armor can keep the magus alive. Superlative Luck or Destiny can ensure a hit.

                                                          The key is that the mage would need to have these spells prepared, and the appropriate mage armor up. That generally requires having time outside of combat to prep the Yantra and possibly use an extended cast ritual.

                                                          This comes down to the same refrain, really. A mage who has the time to prepare will generally succeed in any encounter. A mage who's caught off guard or blind sided is generally in trouble. Chances are, any given Arrow is not going to have these spells and the appropriate defenses prepped to take on a Rahu werewolf. Even if they had all the spells ready, the sudden appearance of something unexpected (lets just say a promethean for the hell of it), means that the mage is basically easy bait for that promethean.
