    Maybe not?

    Look, let me level here with something personal. The reason I went to Werewolf first(back in my second year of high school), before any other WoD game, and why I like werewolves has always had to do with two factors.One was wish fulfillment, becuase yay I love wolfies and I'm not a strong person in life so it'd be awesome to just have this amazing strength come out of nowhere.

    The other was how profoundly alone I was in my youth.

    There's a lot of factors that tie into it, but if I had to summarize, it would be a)I had anger issues as a kid, as well as generally sharp emotions, and b) I often looked at the world very differently from my peers. on the emotional front, I just had very little sense of restraint. If i felt like I loved, I tormented myself over it, if I felt cruel, I found no limits, if I felt righteous, I acted like a tsunami, and if I felt angry, I fought. It didn't matter how little a thing was, if it made me feel something, I felt it improportionally large and acted in accordance. I fought a lot of people. None of that did me any favors. On the perspective point, I was an intellectual, I understood things a lot of my peers didn't, and everything was colored in very different ways for me. I was aware in ways they never could be, not for years to come.

    Sound like Werewolf? It should.

    Werewolves have ways to connect, and when it works it works. But there's a reason I really want to see Kuruth. For as much as it seems like the Uratha are slowly being accepted, Death Rage still remains. Those large, sharp emotions that people respond with to small things will always be one big reason they'll always be Forsaken.

    When it easier for people to get closer, it's also easier to hurt them. Even if you don't want to.

    PS: Oh, and just because I'm a jerk-Hey,Dessembrae?

        Originally posted by Ephsy View Post
        The term Forsaken was coined by two constants: the innate hatred of the spirit world for taking up your duties against it's balance breaking freedom; and the instant divide with human society born from your spiritual side, the fact that after little stimuli you can catch yourself eyeing a human like a talking burger (or the fact that a werewolf's pragmatism with regards to human life makes a fanatic vigilante seem like a saint by comparison).

        Your place isn't in either world, you have to carve it tooth and claw along with the only ones who'll ever truly, spiritually understand you: your pack. Of whom, regardless of the bond, you couldn't find the required special someone there, because A) they were your spiritual siblings and that union carried with severe, severe consequences and B) to regard a single packmate, or human mate above the well-being of your pack as a whole was a step away from betrayal.

        The above conflicts are effectively nullified.

        I'll admit that, indeed, Promethean overkills you with it's isolation theme, but Forsaken's was cool, because it was something you could taste and savor, it didn't bashed you up the head with a pipe like the Created does.

        I just kinda wanna see if it's still gonna be enforced in some way, or we should ditch the subtitle.
        I must gently disagree. Not to say that you're wrong, certainly there are elements of isolation that appear to be leaving the system (Physical passion and abusive parenthood). However, while those elements would certainly be sources of isolation in real life, they did not reflect that in play. I mean, how often does a characters inability to have carnal relations with another Werewolf affect play? And when was the last time you had a scene in which a PC Werewolf lost his cool and beat his child?

        Idigam, from what I can tell, retains the most relevant source of isolation, the chance that you could transform into a massive killer wolf-monster and maul all those around you. And adds a few new ones via Harmony: Getting too close to the man, and being unable to effectively run with your pack. Or getting too close to the spirit and being literally locked out of the material. Those are forms of isolation the player will feel.

            I think in Idigam Chronicles, they are focusing much more on just what it means to be a feral creature, and how being close to one can affect you. Think about it, just by being near you, your perfectly human sister or mother can become a Dalu taking monster. A chance encounter with one can have the same effect. Just by existing, you can ruin lives and put people in danger without intending to.

            But then again, you are also a wolf, and wolves have packs. Your wolf-blooded and your fellow uratha are the closest to you. You may want to avoid hurting anyone by breeding with other werewolves, or you might risk it by looking for a human mate. It is no longer about anger, it is about making them into monsters like you.

            But you cannot be solely a werewolf, either, because doing that will make you too spirit. But succesfully hiding among humans means you might one day explode and kill who you love, and leave wolf-blooded going after you blood.

            So it is a tightrope walk, constantly being pushed to be more spirit or more human. And you belong to neither world fully.

                                    No way I'm going to ditch the Unihar.

                                    I always liked to present the Unihar and their creation as a little similar to Rose's thoughts on the embrace - they're a very little understood fact of werewolf nature, and you're tempting fate by having hot wolf-on-wolf action. She might or might not get pregnant, and the child might or might not be wolf-blooded. The lower your Harmony, the more prone you are to death rage, the higher the probability that your own kid will be a creature that exists only to kill wolves.

                                    I also enjoyed the implications of the result of two werewolves mating being a true monster. Shouldn't the child of two werewolves be a creature that's, like, more werewolf-y than werewolves? Makes you think about the nature of werewolves.

                                    I like the new wolf-blooded!
