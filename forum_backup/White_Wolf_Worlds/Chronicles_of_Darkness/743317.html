    Werewolf: the Apocasaken - Mixing, mashing, and seasoning to taste.

    10-30-2015, 08:46 PM
    As may be obvious I really enjoy combing elements of the cWoD and the NWoD, (I know, I'm a heretic.) because there are elements of one I like more or less than the other. I really, really enjoy Forsaken, but I enjoy pieces of Apocalypse. I've started with the creation myth, and the purpose of the Uratha ('Uratha' sounds much better than Garou)

    The Birth of the People
    This story is true.

    The dawn-time of the world was magical. All was mutable. Spirit and matter naturally flowed in one another during the cycles of youth, maturity and decline. Gaea looked upon the multitudes and was pleased, she birthed Wolf. He was to be her protector and warrior, he would keep creation in line and make sure nothing threatened it. Humanity grew and learned to speak and tell tales. These primitive people, higher animals as they were, beseeched the spirits for protection and blessing. Many came to worship these primordial spirits, nourishing and strengthening them. This was the time the Greeks call ‘the Golden Age’ man was great, close to the gods.

    The Uratha came from the wolf-people, we were blessed by Wolf to change shape. Our tribe was the strongest and of the three greatest spirits: Gaea, Luna and Helios. We gained the special favor of two, Gaea and Luna. Helios was, and is, too jealous to let his worshipers honor anyone but himself.

    Luna gave us the ability to assume new forms between human and wolf, matching her auspices. Gaea extended our abilities to track and hunt, and gave us the basic foundation of our society — pack and territory. Both taught us the ways of the spirit world. The Lunes and elementals guided our path and rewarded our victories. Harsh Luna exacted a ban upon us for her gifts — the touch of her metal, silver, would burn us. Gaea, the earth mother, bid us to keep her world and its Shadow in balance, we became her wardens and warriors. To do this, Gaea gave us the right to prey upon human and spirit to keep the worlds in balance.

    We became powerful shamans who knew how to walk with one foot in both worlds — one in light, the other in Shadow. We were kings of the primordial wood. None could out-fight, out-think or out-magic us. We kept the wolves in check, and we kept the villages of humans small and isolated.

    Then our Father grew weak, Wolf was tired and slow from fighting the enemies of Gaea. The Uratha picked up his work, working hard to fight the corruption that constantly plagued the mother Gaea. The nations of the wolf gathered and spoke amongst themselves, and it was decided that the Uratha should take the job of the Primal God upon themselves. The nations gathered, and ended the hunt of the great wolf, and took his job for himself.
    Our leaders, drunk on their own power, waged war upon the humans and the other shifting people. They decried them as enemies of Gaea, that they failed her when they let Father Wolf grow weak with his great burden, for they did not help. This was the war of rage. Our territories became nations. We began ruling each other, and both humans and wolves were kept as slaves. Humans were herded like cattle in some of the darkest lands; wolves were pressed into service as our scouts and warriors. We neglected the promises we had made to the goddesses, or worse yet, twisted them into the justifications for our deeds.

    But while we waged wars of conquest and slaughter, some of the least of our charges, the humans, escaped from our sight and built mighty cities far away from us. Many of these humans naturally rejected all of the wisdom we taught humankind and sought their own power. Others remembered some of what we taught, and they called to the old gods of Sun, Moon and Earth.

    Luna, the ever vengeful, told some of these humans of our weakness, and when our armies finally clashed with them, we were the ones slaughtered. We called out to Luna, and we heard mad laughter. When we called out to mother Gaea, there was only silence.
    Gaea never again answered our prayers, and the spirit courts that venerated her turned against us — especially the Natural and Elemental Courts. Without Gaea’s favor, we no longer breed true. Some fear that one day she will wake up and punish us. The wisest of us decided that we must take up our duties to Gaea in hopes that she will one day forgive us, or at least to forestall or ameliorate her impending revenge. Then there were those who called themselves the purest children of Gaea, they forsook Luna and blamed her gift of Rage for the atrocities we had committed against Gaea, and some even blamed the moon goddess for the weakening of Father Wolf.
    I feel this keeps the Forsaken, well, forsaken, while adding protection of the Earth to their duties. The other Primal Gods are also left open to have children or be corrupted (my working origin for the Shartha are animal gods who got wyrm-corrupted) I'm completely not porting the Triad over, as I feel the WtF's spirit ecology is better suited for games, and the Weave/Wyld/Wyrm mess things up. However, the Wyrm is the one I am keeping ,as noted, not as a Triatic force, but as a general spiritual concept or perhaps Idigam. Tying the Maeljin and Idigam and all into the general thought of 'Corruption' and unbalance.

    A normal spirit won't become magath, it is abomination, but a spirit under the sway of the Wyrm will not care what it eats, and will unbalance everything. In this, magath become tainted spirits who's gorging of unnatural Essence either comes from Wyrm-taint, or the ending, mad spirit is Wyrm tainted. This is, all in all, an academic point as the end result is still a crazed, ravenous spirit who will disrupt the spiritual and Flesh-side ecology to it's own ends, or the end's of the Wyrm.

    With the pick up as 'Wardens' and 'Warriors' the Uratha get to stay in Garou form indefinitely. The Pure are in, and I've retooled them to those who blame Luna for all that's happened, going to greater acts of depravity and atrocity to appease Gaea. They usually serve, as in canon, spirit patrons. I'd gather they serve mainly Elemental or Natural spirits. I'm not a big fan of 'tech spirits' being unnatural, so they are just spirits, like anything else.

                  Now for some rules! While some people may disagree vehemently (to the point of moralizing) about the Breeds from the Garou, I've chosen to include them. Primarily because one of my players likes the idea of a wolfborn. So below are my rules for the Breeds. In this, uh, hack the Metis don't exist. Partly because of their name, and partly because I'm not much for punishing couples. I like that the Uratha can now have Uratha mates. Just like Forsaken some readings of the Oath (or Litany if you prefer that name for it) strongly hint at Uratha-Uratha pairs as a no-no, but it's not an inherent part of their biology.
                  A good bit of the rules are based, of course, on the translation guide, but updated to 2e.

                  BREEDS
                  Werewolves are wolf, human, and spirit melded into one. But they have to come from somewhere. A werewolf’s breed is a function of immediate parentage, never perfectly balanced, always slightly askew. Each Uratha’s breed is determined by his or her mother’s natural form. Hishu are born to human or hishu Uratha mothers; urhanu are born to wolves or urhanu Uratha. A female lupus Garou who takes on human form and mates with a human man will bear wolf cubs or, perhaps, a urhanu cub or two. Female werewolves who bear offspring always wear their breed form when giving birth. Werewolves can be born without a werewolf parent as well. Some come from Wolf-Blooded or wolf-kin lines with no immediate Uratha parentage. Every so often, a werewolf child is born to an ordinary human or wolf mother who may be many generations removed from werewolf and Kin. Each breed has its own strengths and weaknesses, and each group has a slightly different connection to Gaea. Urhanu have a stronger connection to the wild. Hishu have the experience and talent to move in human society. Breed should inform your character both with interesting mechanics and inspiring possibilities for a backstory.

                  Urhanu, the Wolfborn Uratha
                  Deeply connected to the soul of the world, the wolf-born are born part of a litter of cubs. The young werewolf soon develops heightened mental capacities—able to reason faster and perform cognitive leaps beyond the abilities of his peers. These new thoughts and sensations slowly drive the werewolf mad, as he wonders why the rest of his pack cannot understand what he does. Some challenge for leadership, believing that they would make a better alpha and subconsciously re-enacting the murder of Father Wolf. Others remove themselves to the fringes of the pack, needing to be with others but unable to bear it for long.

                  The challenges of life as a wolf make an Urhanu’s First Change almost a given—whether it’s a period of starvation when the pack cannot bring down any prey or a helicopter bearing humans with automatic weapons on what the monkeys laughably call a “hunt,” the life of a wolf is dangerous. A few Urhanu are born in captivity, but even that’s no guarantee that the young werewolf won’t Change. The inability to roam free builds the Rage in her breast; she changes form and earns her freedom in blood.

                  Though the rules of human socialization seem foreign to the wolf-born, they have an inherent ability to read body language and notice subtle smells that makes up for any social deficiency. They do rely on Hishu werewolves to bring them up to speed, and though they have little trouble picking up skills involving co-ordination or simple understanding of social order, Urhanu have a hard time focusing on more theoretical pursuits. Their birth in nature, where the Gauntlet hasn’t felt the strong touch of humanity, leaves them with a strong connection to the spirit world.
                  • Werewolves and wolves are infertile together, unless the werewolf is already a Lupus or until a werewolf has reached a minimum Primal Urge of 4.
                  • The "species" of the children of such a pairing depends on the mother; wolf pups are born if the mother is a wolf, or a werewolf that remains in wolf or near-wolf form through the pregnancy, whilst a female werewolf who returns to human or near-human form for the pregnancy gives birth to human children.
                  • Humans born of a wolf/werewolf pregnancy are always wolf-blooded, and much more prone to manifesting shapeshifting-based Tells.
                  • All Lupus require a direct werewolf/wolf pairing to occur; "wolf-blooded wolves" don't exist. There are rumors that non-Lupus werewolf pups and their descendants are touched by the spirits in some way, such as requiring a lower Primal Urge for a werewolf to breed with them.
                  • Lupus exist as ordinary wolves until they make their First Change.
                  • Lupus are vastly more common amongst the Pure than they are amongst the Forsaken.
                  Systems: An Urhanu cannot choose Mental Skills to be primary at character creation. Worse, any time an Urhanu character attempts an action that relies on a Mental Skill in which she has no dots, she receives a −4 penalty instead of −3. Gift activation rolls are exempt from this rule. On the positive side, Urhanu start with Harmony 6. They’re also able to focus on breaching the Gauntlet to the exclusion of pretty much anything else, treating the roll to step sideways as a rote action.

                  Hishu, the Humanborn Uratha
                  Hishu werewolves are all those born of human mothers and form the major proportion of the Uratha population. Born and raised in a human family, they have a mindset and culture rooted in the human world. Without that, werewolves would have no context for much of the modern world, from telecommunications to firearms.
                  Being born among the hairless monkeys does have its disadvantages—human culture breeds a weaker connection with the spirit world as the young werewolf doesn’t have any context for what it feels like when the Hisil touches her soul. Urhanu werewolves resent Hishu for their impressive numbers, fearing that at any point the two-legs could simply leave the others behind.
                  Systems: A Hishu werewolf starts with Harmony 7.

                          Well I've hacked Rage back into WtF based upon the rules hack in the Storyteller's option for expending 'Primal Urge' to exchange your Dice Pool for some extra dice if your pool gets too low. Below is the total of the system, it's baked into Primal Urge and is, definitely, one more reason to buy Primal Urge.


                          RAGE
                          Every Uratha is a crucible of primal anger, a gift from Luna (or Gaea) to combat the Wyrm and the Uratha’s many enemies. This Rage is a force of mindless violence, a constant slow-motion path of unthinking destruction and violence that each and every Uratha must keep control of lest it consume her. Rage mixes instinctive cunning and hunting ability with savage bloodlust and unpredictable horror. To the Uratha, Rage is both a blessing and a curse. Many think that Luna gave Rage to the Uratha through her influences as the strength of Rage is highest just as she is at her fullest. Others contend that Rage is a spark of the Wyrm within each Uratha, the force of primal destruction corrupting the children of the creator. The most vocal say that it is Gaea herself who would have her children use her Rage as their greatest weapon.

                          Every Forsaken has a number of Rage points equal to their Primal Urge. Each points are recorded under Primal Urge. As the Uratha grows closer to their primal self, so does the Rage of their existence grow stronger. Any time a player uses a Rage point for one of these options, they must roll to resist Kuruth. Roll Resolve + Composure, on a failure the Werewolf enters Wasu-Im. When a Uratha runs out of Rage points they immediately enter Basu-Im, at the end of a Death Rage you automatically gain one Rage point. If a Rage point is spent in Wasu-Im, a failure immediately sends the Werewolf into Basu-Im.

                          USES FOR RAGE
                          • Frenzied Attack: Spend a Rage point and add your Primal Urge to one attack roll for the turn. This can be spent in addition to Willpower.
                          • Remaining Active: If a character falls unconscious from damage in their right-most Health Level they can spend a Willpower and roll Resolve + Primal Urge, each success heals a health level of damage, regardless of type. A player may attempt this roll only once per scene. If this roll fails, the character doesn’t recover.
                          • Resisting Stuns: When an Uratha gains the Stunned tilt they may spend a Rage point to automatically cancel or resist the tilt as a Reflexive action.
                          • ​Last Ditch Effort: Swap your dice pool after modifiers for a pool of 3 + Primal Urge dots. This is a last ditch effort to hit a high Defense pool, or when other modifiers bring the Uratha’s dice pool below this threshold.
                          GAINING RAGE
                          • The Moon: The first time a werewolf sees the moon at night, the Beast inside stirs, and Rage floods back into her. The Uratha immediately regains one Rage point. If the moon phase corresponds with the character’s auspice, she regains all of her Rage. This phenomenon only occurs when the character first sees the moon each night.
                          • Dramatic Failure: If the Storyteller approves, a werewolf might receive a Rage point after a Dramatic Failure. Rage comes from stressful situations, and seeing the action you were attempting blow up in your face, sometimes literally, can be a very stressful situation.
                          • Death Rage: At the end of a Death Rage, the Uratha regains one spent Rage point.
                          • Humiliation: Rage will also come rushing back if anything a Uratha does proves particularly humiliating. The Storyteller decides whether a situation is embarrassing enough to warrant a Rage point.
                          • Confrontation: Again at the Storyteller’s approval, a character could receive a Rage point at the beginning of a tense situation, in the moments right before combat starts. This gain accounts for the anticipation and hackle-raising that happens just as tempers start to flare.
                          • New Stories: When a new story begins, each player regains their full Rage pool.
