                                        Here's my attempt at mechanics for Priests. Feel free to comment, review, flame etc.


                                        Priests
                                        For all their power, demigods are not omnipotent and one of the greatest checks on their power is the fact that in order to exercise it, both in Numinous Form and in Miracles, requires Potentia, the divine energy of the Drasil. Potentia can be harvested from cults, Temples and the Drasil itself but all of these come with drawbacks and risks. Cults can be destroyed or can lose their faith, Temples are inherently fragile structures and are never far away from the possibility of destruction and access to the Drasil requires a Temple.
                                        Therefore, many demigods seek to create more reliable sources of Potentia and to that end, many create 'Priests', mortals endowed with the ability to use and, more importantly, collect Potentia independently of the demigod as well as acting as a kind of repository that the demigod can draw upon, should the need arise.

                                        To Be Ordained
                                        In game terms, creating a Priest is done by simply spending Experience on the Priest Merit and applying the Priest template to a suitable NPC. In character, however, the process is somewhat more involved. In order to become a Priest, a mortal (hereafter referred to as the Acolyte) must be under the effects of the Symbol or God Thresholds of Halysis. In addition, they must know that the demigod is supernatural in some way – although their belief need not be accurate – and the demigod must offer them Priesthood verbally and in person. They need not explain all the mechanics of the position but they must make it clear that the position grants the Acolyte power at the price of service to the demigod. The Acolyte must then accept the position, willingly and under no compulsion, supernatural or otherwise, excepting that imposed by Halysis. The demigod cannot word this as a command, it must be an offer which the Acolyte is free to decline (not that he is likely to do so, given his adoration of the demigod, but it is possible.
                                        Having done this, the demigod spends a point of Potentia and presses a hand to the Acolyte's skin, in some location. Where the hand touches, a mark not unlike a brand or tattoo is burned onto the Acolyte's skin. This mark is unique to each demigod and is usually representative of that demigod's Domains, Aspect or Following. A demigod may not attempt to imitate the Mark of another. This Mark cannot be removed by mundane means, although the Priest may spend a point of Willpower to cause it to disappear for a scene.

                                        The Fall From Grace
                                        There are four ways in which a Priest may lose his power and position.
                                        • If the demigod which Ordained him dies but still endures (by incarnating in his Icon, for example), the Priest temporarily loses the template and his Mark fades to something not unlike a scar or a birthmark. The template is re-applied upon the demigod's resurrection.
                                        • If the demigod which Ordained him dies permanently, the Priest loses the template and the Mark burns itself off. If the demigod is later resurrected by some arcane means, the template does not return.
                                        • The demigod who Ordained a Priest can strip them of their position with a touch and another point of Potentia. If this is done, the Priest immediately loses all Willpower and Potentia (see below) and has the Stunned Tilt for the rest of the scene, as well as the Soul-Shocked Condition for the next week. He also immediately falls a THreshold of Halysis, as his faith is shaken by the demigod’s dismissal of his service.
                                        • If the Priest ever falls below the Symbol Halysis Threshold, he loses the Priest template until that Threshold is regained. He also gains the Shaken Condition, which is only resolved when the former Priest either falls another Threshold of Halysis or returns to the Symbol Threshold.
                                        The Gifts of the Divine
                                        The Priest template grants the following abilities:
                                        • Priests are immune to the Rapture, although not to the effects of Halysis. They are also immune to Breaking Points due to witnessing the supernatural.
                                        • A Priest has an extra Virtue, entitled 'Serving My God', which is fulfilled whenever the Priest significantly inconveniences himself to do something for the demigod. Whether or not this is what the demigod actually wants is a moot point.
                                        • Whenever the Priest would regain Willpower, he may instead elect to roll (Resolve + Composure) and gain Potentia equal to successes rolled. This Potentia can be stored in place of Willpower. For each point gathered, mark a horizontal line through a Willpower box (which may be used or not). That point of Willpower may not be spent while the Potentia is there. If this roll is made, the Priest gains no Willpower, no matter how many (or few) successes are rolled.
                                        • A Priest may spend Potentia in place of Willpower, gaining +3 dice to a single roll. However, he may also choose to spend Potentia in order to attempt to work a miracle (although not a capital ‘M’ Miracle), using only the dice gained from the Potentia spent. This allows Priests to channel some of the power of their patron into superhuman and magical feats. A Priest may attempt to use these minor miracles to affect anything which relates to one of their demigod's Domains, although they can never create any effect beyond that of a 1-dot Miracle, can only affect the Priest and those he is touching and cannot last any longer than 1 round per success.Willpower cannot be spent to augment this roll.
                                        Example: The Priest Martin Goldman, serving a demigod with the Domains of Fire, Conflict and Earth, is trapped in a burning building. Conflict is little help here, and the power of a miracle of Earth necessary to allow him to sink through the floor is beyond the skill of a Priest, but he can use a miracle of protection to keep himself safefrom the heat of the flames. He spends 2 of his 4 Potentia, giving him a dice pool of 6. He rolls, and comes up with 3, 4, 9, 3, 10 and 6. He rolls another dice for the 10 and comes up with a 9. He has atotal of 3 successes, enough for approximately 12 seconds of fire protection. He'd better hurry!
                                        Later on, the same Priest is called by his demigod to command a party of cultists in defending their Temple from a rival Cult. In the fight, Martin's group is affected by a fear-causing Miracle from an Icon wielded by an enemy Cultist which inflicts the Beaten Down Tilt on a number of Martin's war party. Seeing this, he spends another Potentia, this time using the Domain of Conflict to fuel a Miracle to inspire his followers. He rolls one success on his three dice and roars in defiance at his enemy, letting out a fierce battle cry, rallying his comrades and removing the Tilt. Because the enemy Miracle is an Instant effect which simply imposes the Tilt, not an ongoing one which keeps it there, the Priest's miracle need only act for oneturn to remove it. It does nothing to protect the cultists from the Icon's Miracle being used again.
                                        • A Priest may spend their stored Potentia in order to make use of Miracles imbued into Icons, spending 1 Potentia for 1- and 2-dot Miracles, 2 for 3- and 4-dot Miracles and 3 for 5-dot Miracles. He may still elect to spend Willpower as if he were a normal mortal if he wishes to do so.
                                        • A Priest's demigod patron can, in any scene that he can directly see (not through cameras, although lenses or binoculars work fine) or touch the Priest, roll Resolve + Composure + Luminance and drain a number of points of Potentia from the Priest equal to successes rolled.

                                                            If I may suggest a small idea, what if Followings covered mainly the five aspects most WoD games follow? Leadership/Social, Warriors/Fighters, Lore and Secret Keepers, Tricksters and Spies, and finally those who follow human development and the like? I know it may be a little cliche, but most of the other gamelines seem to have the pattern for either X or Y splats. I can also admit Siren sort of does the same for the "Good" currents.

                                                            Ideas on this
                                                            The Leader/Social Following could be the somewhat blatant religion that you know from the old world. They take the forms of lost gods from dead religions, adopting the forms that people know of and can look up to. For example, in Scandanavia, most Leader Following Demigods could take the form of the Aesir or Vanir in Viking Mythology. I can see this being a mix of the Followings of Heirs and Sparks.

                                                            The Warriors and Fighters are both the gods of conflict and war as much as harmony and peace. Their Following is perhaps the most involved in the human state of matters, as they're the ones who are most likely to try and keep the state of affairs of their cults to be peaceful and smiting down enemies to the cult with great prejudice. This could be a mix of the Following of Banners and Descendants.

                                                            The Lore and Secret Keepers are the Gods you imagine when it comes to hierarchal societies. The Gods are always up top, and the members usually seek enlightenment through the god. Those with greater amounts of enlightenment tend to be amongst the Priests or greater levels of Halysis. However, their thirst for knowledge and enlightenment aren't quenchable and it always goes back up to the god up top too. In a sense, think of them like a less malignant Hermaus Mora from the Elder Scrolls. This could be a mix of the Followings of Vassals and Eidolon.

                                                            The Trickster and spies are the Gods that you automatically think of when it comes to secret societies or cults. They're the gods who hold large amounts of influence and exert it on others.Another idea I had was that these kinds of gods do decieve and lie to their own followers, but those who manage to work their way past the lies and tricks the god puts up, they tend to be able to move up to greater levels of importance. They could be a mix of the Followings of the Morning-Star and Ascendants.

                                                            And finally, the Human Development Gods. These gods tend to be less "God" and more like forces of nature, lines of psychology, they embody the way the human world tries to grasp on natural and unnatural phenomena, and trying to change themselves in turn to human development and belief of the phenomena. Heck, maybe even the Following tries to lead the development. Afterall, a man of science reads hundreds of books and believe that there's more to learn, which in turn can give more potentia to a Demigod. The ideas I have atm is that this could be a mix of the Followings of Annunaki and Embodiments.


                                                            Of course, these are just ideas, feel free to disregard them. I'm just stating some suggestions on the number of followings. If you do take these ideas into account though, perhaps some of the ideas for Followings could still be used for Z-Splats?
