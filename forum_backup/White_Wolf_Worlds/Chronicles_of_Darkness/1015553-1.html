                        SNESferatu, I didn't know about it, though it makes sense. Let me ask: how much people actually learn it for more than just passing tests? I'm actually curious. I remember your thread about modern Rome, it was the first one where I posted in the forum, and it got me rethinking this setting since. Your advice is welcome.

                        By the way, I also expect material for modern Rome. I find it far more interesting to deliver modern with just a hint to the past, than to make so much material about the past outright. Not that I'm against games set in the past, I just find it more interesting as a development policy for the setting. It makes the past mysterious even giving material to work, make it simpler to use the modern setting and let the ST adapt the hints better to a personal take in ancient history.

                        The Gallic Wars are great, and full of mysteries by themselves, since we only have good descriptions of the Gauls coming from greatly unreliable sources. The druids can be a lot of interesting things, and given the tribal nature of the Gauls, they can be more than one thing at once.

                        Even then, it would be interesting to have the Gallic Wars and the Gaul society tackled from a modern perspective in the books. Something about the effects of both the past and its legends in the current supernatural societies of the region, their theories and, maybe, some ruins and relics of this past.

                        nikink, the take on Rome is a relic from WoD, in two ways. First, Vampire was the game line that most dealt with ancient history in WoD, so Rome was "their turf". Werewolf dealt more with pre-history, and them with very recent events. Mage is the main source in the Middle Ages (curiously even more than Vampire Dark Ages, setting-wise). It IS a simplification, of course, but this focus carried over. Then they decided to link the Camarilla with Rome in the core book of Requiem 1st edition, and that was it. Rome become iconic in Requiem, while other game lines kind of went through other waters. It is a shame, of course.

                            Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

                            It depends on what kind of high school you were in, we have lots. The most popular are the Classico and the Scientifico. The Classico is the one with the most "prestige", but times are changing and nobody cares about humanities anymore, so it's losing some of its "status". Classico high schoolers learn Latin and Ancient Greek, and are the most versed in them. Still, probably not enough.
                            I studied in a Scientifico. As the name says, it's mainly focused on maths and sciences, but Latin was super duper important there, too. Of five years of high school, two are spent learning grammar (and translating random stuff), and three studying, reading and translating classical works, mainly Cicero's and Caesar's.
                            Most other schools have abandoned the study of the language, but the vast majority of people have some passing knowledge of it. And they have probably hated every single minute of that.

                            Problem is, the way Latin is taught is much more akin to a puzzle than a language, and that's a shame. I mean, we suck at English too, but that's on a whole different level. You cannot translate anything without a dictionary. The classic Latin test is the versione, aka translating 12 or so lines from Latin to Italian, in two hours. It's a pain, and most people are rubbish at it. I was the best at it in my class, but my Latin is franky non-existent.

                            So, your werewolves will probably know how the language works, but not how to actually use it. They cannot read it, but they have no issue with spellingand pronunciation.
                            PS: Ancient Romans and Latin have a very strong link with the Fascist Regime, for obvious reasons. I expect lots of confusion between your packs and the various far-right groups of Rome...

                                SNESferatu, I was already thinking of strong links between most of the fiercest Patricians and far-right groups. In the end, the Patricians are mostly your average nationalists, and most packs are indeed grounded on actual roman politics.

                                What I'm not sure is how to tackle it in the mundane side. Of course, being a Brazilian without any link to actual Italy, my knowledge of the details of current politics in your city is limited, to say the least. But on the other side, fiction demands fiction, and maybe my ignorance can help by actually forcing me to create parties and such instead of leaning too much on personal readings on actual ones.

                                But what would you recommend? And there is something you can talk about this subject that you think would help?

                                Oh, and probably most of them will rely on Latim learned from school, anyway, maybe with more pride than it deserves. And I can picture a certain contention between scholars that defend the current take on pronunciation, and revisionists based on recent theories of how ancient high-class Romans actually talked.

                                LostLight, it will take some time to find the material, but yeah, I'll share when I find. What I can present for now, though...

                                First, it should be clear that the Patricians aren't necessarily an ancient phenomena. Maybe their origins are in ancient Rome indeed, but their current form is quite a modern phenomena. They aren't from classical times, they are just modern classicists.

                                I thought of the Patricians liking very much the notion of Lares, ancient spirit protectors of Roman streets, buildings and landmarks, and linking their Totems to that notion, instead. The most powerful "Lares" are the patrons of the Patrician "Tribes". I had found a proper terminology for Tribe, but forgot it by now. The use (not necessarily correct) of Latim instead of shamanistic or First Tongue terms will be an obvious development.

                                The Tribes divided pretty much on general views of the Roman society. One is founded over the notion of the Legionnaire as a glorified servitor of the city. Under 2nd edition they would probably declare Sacred Hunt on the enemies of the city of Rome (as they perceive it, of course). Another is about the Caesars, about rulership of the city and of "lesser citizens", probably being receptive to "barbarians" in their own way. Sacred Hunt could be aimed to keep the power structure, though I accept suggestions (for everything, actually). There is one about Roman scholars, devoted to learn and keep the civil traditions of Rome, and two religious tribes at each other's throats, a Cristian one and a Pagan one, dedicated each not only to their religions, but to what they represent to the Eternal City.

                                As you can see, the Patricians are incredibly devoted and structured around the city itself. Some still dream of expansion, delivering the Light of Rome to the bleak barbarian world. Others defend that it was the ultimate downfall of their empire into crisis, and that Rome should focus on itself. They are obsessed with purity of the Roman blood, which also means that some are Patricians only because they are from Patrician families, or otherwise of "pure" Roman blood and decided to keep with kin, while others are outright fascists and xenophobes. Their territories, for now, both comprise the city and metropolitan area itself as wild and farm areas around it.

                                Patricians aren't the only Uratha in Rome, either. There are both Forsaken and Pure in the area, making for quite complex interactions. Since Patricians only accept "true" Romans among them, werewolves that don't get acceptance have to seek the common tribes, that aren't as present because of the Patrician Tribes. The requisites to be considered a True Roman aren't that well defined, either, which lends more room for conflict when a given werewolf is accepted as a member of a given Tribe, but other werewolf don't consider this a valid decision.

                                Conflicts in Rome, so, are characterized by lineage and beliefs taking precedence. People fight over romanticized perceptions of things, be it past, blood, religion. They don't fight so much over things, but mostly over how each interpret those things. A lot of Patricians will sense more kinship with cursed undead, otherworldly fairies, and other, stranger, things, if they have personal reason to think those as "true Romans", than with a fellow Uratha that came from Sicily.

                                Just to finish for now, the Patricians do have a common meeting ground, their greatest institution. The Forum. They deem the Shadow of the ancient Forum Romanum as neutral and sacred meeting ground, a place where every Patrician can have a voice, share news and opinions, where physical and mystical fights are forbidden, but vicious political fights are deadly. By force of law this place is protected by all Packs when needed, and at least one Pack is tasked to stay and guard at all times. It is maintained by alliance with local spirits that reflect the Patrician view of the city, and other spirits, along with any "barbarian" that somehow manages to approach it by the Shadow, are expeled with extreme violence after only one warning if that much.

                                                Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post
                                                I have a half-finished Vigil for Rome project I should reopen.

                                                I replaced the official version of the Aves Minerva because I found them too much "generic Roman pagans" which works when they're the only Hunter group in the setting, but if they're sharing it with other Roman Hunters they need to be more distinct. Quick run down of the groups I decided to focus on...

                                                Compacts
                                                - Aves Minerva: Plebs and Freemen defenders of the streets of the city. More like the Union than any other modern Compact. They've always been patriotically dedicated to the gods of the city, but debate rages over the admittance of Christians.
                                                - Mos Maiorum: Patricians protecting the honor of their families from the blight of the walking dead or other accursed stigmas
                                                - Vox Martyom: Heretical Christians of the Agonistici tradition, who believe martyrdom is the only path to salvation. As the state doesn't serve that purpose anymore post-Constantine, they turn their attention to the unholy forces in the shadows

                                                Conspiracies
                                                - Collegia Chironos: A mystery cult primarily made up of educated Greek slaves, seeking knowledge of the medical and mystical
                                                - Ieros Dromos: Nicean Christians answerable to Constantinople, breaking the power of the Gnostic witches and the cults of pagan horrors to save the souls of the masses
                                                - Lux Mithrae: a Conspiracy within the Legions wielding consecrated blood of the bull against supernatural foes of the Empire (this is where the Red Rites move)

                                                Other groups (like the Ascending Ones who are the premier Hunters in the Parthian Empire at this point) get some sidebar attention.
                                                I like your idea for the Aves Minerva, but I'd sooner see that concept tied into the vigiles while the Aves Minerva are free to be tied to her cult.
