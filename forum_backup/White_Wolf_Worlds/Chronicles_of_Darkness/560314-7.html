                                        I'm going to say what I've always said on the subject- having supernatural powers doesn't mean you are a Conspiracy, and not having supernatural powers doesn't make you into a Compact. TF:V, for example, don't have supernatural powers- they are a Conspiracy with a very high tech weapons and devices, and so are the AKD who are "just" and ancient Conspiracy who has tons of Relics.

                                        Endowments are, the way I see it, a way to mechanically represent the collective knowledge a certain group (the Conspiracy) has gathered and made available to all of its agents of certain level of recognition (Status) which gives them a special edge upon both supernatural, mundane and vigilant threats and/or competitors. If that knowledge can't be replicated in an efficient way and be taught to all of the agents of the Conspiracy, then that knowledge is not an Endowment, just a supernatural or mundane advantage certain individuals in a certain group has.

                                        That means that being a Mystery Cult which has access to certain Supernatural Merits, and there are big organizations which do not have any kind of supernatural or mundane edge. Why? Because they are not hunters. The Compacts and Conspiracies of the hunters are focused around the Vigil, and the knowledge they gathered through their hunt is what makes their Endowment. I think that the reason some people think Endowments and Conspiracies should be broken apart from each other is because of two groups- the Lucifuge and the Promethean Brotherhood, which have their Endowment as their "reason of existence". I don't think it's the right way to read them (that is, Castigations are not inborn abilities but rites which unleash the potential of the hunter and that the Rite of Hecate could be considered as a proper Endowment only when it is finalized), but I agree that they can cause some confusion. The Lucifuge also create that feeling because they have a limited number which sounds small- but Tiers are about the reach of the organization, and the Lucifuge has been detailed has having a 3rd tier reach through all the books.

                                        In short, the reason only Conspiracies have "real Endowments" (not including the C&C ones) is because it is an easy way to mechanically represent old and powerful occult organizations. For a Compact, meeting someone with and Endowment like power should be terrifying. For a Conspiracy, it's what makes them terrifying.

                                        At least, that's how I've always viewed the issue.

                                                Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post

                                                Yes they absolutely do. There is nothing "realistic bleeding edge science" about Etheric bullets. I'm not saying they didn't use the scienfic method to develop said tech, but it was the scientific method applied to supernatural phenomena with supernatural materials. A good portion of Advanced Armory is occult in nature.
                                                Ironically, by the strange rules of RPG definitions of magic and technology, Etheric Bullets are the most technology-like piece of equipment available.

                                                While the philosophy of fantasy/sci-fi has always been "Any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic, and any sufficiently analyzed magic is indistinguishable from technology," in RPGs, the difference has been one of transferability. Magic is a property of the wielder, while technology is the property of their equipment. Wizards rely upon inherent magic powers, while fighters can trade the magical powers of their equipment with any other fighter they run across. The space marine's plasma gun is usable by anyone with working opposable thumbs, but the psychic's powers are innate and cannot be transferred. Even magic artifacts are distinguishable from technological tools by their (lack of) mass reproduction. Magic is a special snowflake. Technology makes us all fungible. Tone aside, their results are the same.

                                                Etheric rounds are only purchasable/requisitionable by Valkyrie, but any random yahoo that gets a clip can jam it into a gun they bought at WalMart and cap some ghosts. Their bleeder and lightning guns, meanwhile, refuse to function for anyone but their specified masters, making them an innate, non-transferable part of the character who purchased them. Comparatively, Aegis Kai Doru relics are transferable (although story reasons would make one not want to) to the point that the only thing "special" about any given character is admittance to magic club, while alchemy from the Ascending Ones is only transferable to another Ascending One of equal or greater endowment dots, but the bleeder gun that a TF:V character has can't be used by any other TF:V character in a way that makes it equivalent to one of the prosthetics of Chieron.

                                                ---------

                                                Anyway, as for the distinction between compact and conspiracy, I think it may be better to take the path glamourweaver talked about, and take the implication of size away from a conspiracy. So far as game effects go, the difference of making one group have a superpower, and another group not having one is simple and clear enough a dividing line. Inversely, size is sketchy and always ill-defined for storyteller flexibility purposes, anyway (barring Lucifuge). It also may have little to really do with the themes of the game. (How impotant is it whether The Union has equal distribution around the world versus small pockets around the world with a few clusters of concentration in the Rustbelt, versus nowhere outside the Rustbelt if you're playing the whole game in Chicago?)

                                                A conspiracy, technically, is just more than one person, anyway, and it can mean any group that has some secret. Use the difference in tiers to mean something like "Some random guys who REALLY don't know what they're getting into" -> "A group organized enough to understand enough to be dangerous and not get wiped out so fast they can learn from their mistakes" -> "A group deep in enough that they're gaining monster powers, themselves, but who now have a much stronger reason to keep others out."

                                                    Originally posted by MachineIV View Post
                                                    I actually don't like the distinction between the two. I think it's way too arbitrary.
                                                    Absolutely. Which is why I'm hoping that 2E will clearly define what the distinction between a Compact and a Conspiracy is.

                                                    IMO a Compact should be that you have access to a network of like minded hunters who'll share information and resources; but you're still mostly a free agent. A Conspiracy means you're not a free agent, somebody gives you orders and you obey them* but you get all the benefits organisation can bring: More resources, more information, more backup; the simple fact that you can organise a raid on every branch of a vampire's network at the same time.

                                                    Sometimes a Compact has a powerful supernatural Endowment. Sometimes a Conspiracy has no Endowment.

                                                    *Unless you have enough Status. Then you're giving the orders.

                                                    Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post

Plenty of groups without Endowments are going to be world-spanning networks.
                                                    I think wheather or not you're getting orders from the chain of command would have more effect on the central "feel" than wheather or not you're Endowed.

                                                    But ultimately I don't care too much what's the "primary" division. I just want the tangle between Endowments, Tier, size of the organisation, and structure of the organisation to be untangled.

                                                    I'd like Compact and Conspiracy to be defined as above; because I think it's a logical division point and because tying the words to Endowments is linguistically weird and there's no need for a special keyword to say Endowments are present. You can just look at the Endowment section.

                                                    But so long as the tangle is untangled and significantly more attention is paid to how groups organise themselves I'd be happy.
