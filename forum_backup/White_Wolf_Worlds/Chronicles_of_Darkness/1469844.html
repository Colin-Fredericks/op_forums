      My short answer would be that Social Maneuvering attempts (even those to close Doors someone else opened) should be specific to a goal.
      For example, if you’re persuading someone to donate to a cause, they might go home and listen to their echo chamber about the economy being in a bad state or that greed is a virtue - but that isn’t providing specific points that contradict /this/ cause. Someone being in that echo chamber would certainly be worth a penalty, but I wouldn’t rule that it automatically closes the Doors you open.
      Based on how Social Maneuvering works, I’d also rule that a person can’t use it on themself. That is, if you make progress persuading someone, they won’t go looking for counterarguments - that should probably only occur from a failure or dramatic failure (justifying why that door stays shut, not to shut more Doors).


      …But if it’s about a prominent social issue, or the data mining is accurate enough, the eco chamber might just be specific enough to be actively closing Doors. And you just can’t change their minds about that subject - until you have a way to get them out of the echo chamber, or switch to a tangential argument that’s not specifically targeted, or make it personal and specific enough to not get shouted down.

          Friends, counselors, and advisors would be a similar factor to this, I do believe - unknown sources of opposition to your social maneuvering attempts. It’s very reasonable that a person gets a second opinion from someone close to them, especially while still making a decision about an exciting or nerve-wracking subject.

          One way around this is to choose your mark - find someone who doesn’t have confidants (or an echo chamber) working against you.
          Another way is to make your arguments in a way that doesn’t raise opposition. Like, if the target feels like it’s a Small Deal, they might not talk it over with others. This could be an optional benefit for an exceptional success, or using Subterfuge over another skill.
          Finally, if the target does have a confidant (or echo chamber) that closes a Door, lampshade that to the players. Have the target be more closed off at the next meeting, and literally say the counterpoints they heard - maybe even the source. Let the players know that there’s significant opposition to what they’re trying to do, and make them deal with that before making progress.

          I find it reasonable, and both true to life and true to the genre, that not everyone is ‘vulnerable’ to Social Maneuvering; their social networks are too strong to allow any leverage. Success becomes a matter of finding “the weakest link”, or cutting someone off from their network, or gaining a form of leverage that can’t be so easily contradicted. And even then, it’s dramatically appropriate for them to have a crisis of conscience or sudden back up just after following through with the demands, (in a “success with complications” fashion).

          The counterbalance to this is that persuasion can work on many different levels, from many different types of argument. Something that appeals to a person’s Virtue will get to them on a much deeper level than their social network can contradict; fanning their emotions works really well in the short term, but might lose all progress once they have a chance to cool down and think it over.
          This is something I think Exalted 3rd Ed did really well, and isn’t well reflected in CoD’s mechanics. Situational modifiers can get into this a bit, but…
          I have Thoughts, many of which are still half-baked, but my ‘long’ answer is creating a new hack. Or system.

                                                          A detail to bear in mind about Forcing Doors is how it "burn the door" for you. It's not just the "I want it now" route, but the All or Nothing nature of it. Observe that the text explicitly says that at failing the target becomes immune to Social Maneuvering from you. No time or condition, they're immune forever.

                                                          You encounter is certainly a great example. Convincing you to baptize and begin in their church wasn't something a simple attempt was likely to succeed at, so they tried a desperate tactic. Now I doubt you'll even consider listening to anything they say if you ever recognize them again in any other context, or at least you'll be very wary of them.

                                                          This is beyond simple Intimidation and Intimidation can be used in Social Maneuvering without such effect. In the US this is standard procedure for police interrogations, it is basically Social Maneuvering using Intimidation, among other things, but still working in the long run instead of rushing things up too much, even if they set up the situation to maximize time and success (there are cases of interrogations taking 18 hours).

                                                          Every time the character going for a Social Maneuvering decides it's time for a Now or Never tactic, this is Forcing Doors and failure means Never.

                                                          By the way, that's another thing to be said about the rule on Social Maneuvering. It doesn't have to be a positive interaction, as the target doesn't have to be convinced due to liking you. It can be a cult brainwashing, it can convince you little by little that you'l get in trouble if you don't do this or that, it can be anything.

                                                          I even argued in the past that Social Maneuvering is the best rule to model torture, because it is basically what torture does: it uses pain and abuse, physical and psychological, to convince you to do something. Some people say that torture doesn't work, but that isn't true. Torture doesn't work for getting the truth, which Social Maneuvering doesn't do either, it works to convince you to do something, even if this something is to confess (regardless of your confession being true or not).
