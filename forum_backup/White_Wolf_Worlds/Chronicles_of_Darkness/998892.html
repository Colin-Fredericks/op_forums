    Questions about emergency surgeries

    11-05-2016, 05:42 PM
    Hi,

    one of the player characters is a surgeon in an emergency room. Therefore I'm trying to figure out how everything works starting from being badly injured and bleeding to death until finally no more medical care is necessary.

    In reality someone who enters the emergency room has still a high chance of dying. Therefore
    in my opinion it should happen now and then that a patient does not survive the surgery based on the rolls.

    ---

    Obviously there are three phases where medical dice rolls are needed after some was badly injured and is now close to death:

    1) First aid / Ambulance
    Dexterity + Medicine + Modifiers
    One roll per minute.
    Extended action.
    Success equal to the health are needed.

    2) Emergency surgery.
    Dexterity + Medicine + Modifiers
    One roll per minute.
    Extended action.
    Success equal to the health are needed.

    Sources:
    "She can’t recover from that without urgent medical attention and emergency surgery."
    (CofD p. 95)

    Medical care is an extended action, requiring successes equal to the total number of points of damage suffered by the patient. In the field or ER, the dice pool is Dexterity + Medicine, and the interval is one minute.
    (CofD p. 95)

    3) Medical treatment in the hospital (until all aggravated damage is healed).
    Intelligence + Medicine + Modifiers
    One roll per hour.
    Extended action.
    Successes needed are depending on the damage type and amount.

    Source:
    In long-term hospital care, the pool is Intelligence + Medicine, and the interval is one hour. The number of successes required is five for a lethal wound and 10 for an aggravated one.
    (CofD p. 95)

    ---

    And now my problem:
    I can't find anything that makes a emergency surgery difficult that is mentioned in the rules. The characters is a surgeon working in an emergency room where he has also all the equipment that he needs. So there is no way he would ever be reduced to a chance die. So failures would only make the surgery take more time ...

    The 1E rule book also mentioned that a normal failure just means no progress:
    "Failure: Your character makes no progress in alleviating the patient’s injuries. If the subject is bleeding to death, a wound turns from lethal to aggravated over the course of the wasted minute."

    Since it is said that you need both, first aid and an emergency surgery, I kind of wonder why they look so similar and exchangeable. Furthermore the "difficult part" seem to be the first aid where someone has only minutes before he bleeds to death. For example a person with seven health dots can't survive for more than seven minutes. In my opinion this is not long enough to get someone to the emergency room. Even with an immediate call and the ambulance starts driving to the location right away. So stopping the bleeding has to happen before. And is therefore the goal of the first aid.
    After that the emergency surgery is next. But what could happen? The immediate danger where the character can bleed to death is over, otherwise he wouldn't have made it so far.

    Did I overlook something? I don't think it is very interesting nor realistic if an surgery can only become longer...

      First aid and ER are treated as the same action in this system. Either way you're rolling Dex + Medicine to stabilize someone who is bleeding out. The biggest difference is that in the ER the character probably has access to better equipment. The primary risk is just that you might not get enough successes before the patient dies from the bleeding. Although, since it's an extended action, failures don't just do nothing. You have to take a negative Condition to continue, which will most likely apply penalties to subsequent rolls on the extended action, meaning more likelihood of failed rolls. And you have a limited number of rolls you can make. So, it is entirely possible to fail the extended action before achieving the target number of successes, leaving the target to bleed out. And while a "natural" dramatic failure isn't super likely for a well-trained character, the player can always opt to turn a regular failure dramatic and take a Beat.

                  This game simplifies advanced and complicated actions all the time, but it feels like Mental skills suffer the most from this; combat rolls, pursuit rolls (drive and athletics), and social maneuvering are all fairly complex while the only mental skill with an in-depth system is investigation (unless I'm forgetting something).                   Diagnosing, treatment, surgery, etc, etc are very complicated things (same with other mental skills). How do you build a narrative around mental skills in a way that is as immersing and engaging gameplay as physical or social skills? My experience has often been 'roll attribute + skill' to find an abstracted solution to the problem, but that's not always super satisfying when trying to weave a story.

                          My understanding of the penalty and bonus system is that only the end result cannot exceed +5/-5. For example, a Demon rolling Cover after partially manifesting their demonic form rolls Wits + Manipulation as well as a +1 for every form power they have not manifested. By default Demons have seven form powers, and there are ways of having more. This usually means a flat 5 bonus to the roll. However, that roll can be penalized by other things - say that someone or a group sees them doing an act who is not aware they are a Demon. This means that the +6 a Demon normally gets for the roll can be reduced down to a -5 if enough people see them.

                          Basically, if you have enough external negative factors, you can hit someone with enough penalties to overcome any innate bonuses they get from equipment and specialties. There are also powers and conditions that artificially reduce the dice pool someone has to begin with, if not forcing certain actions into being chance die. If someone has a high enough attribute and skill pool, then it is unlikely they will have a chance die, but it still makes their rolls harder.

                                          I agree that I do focus on the ER. Without someone playing a surgeon working in the emergency room I don't think, there is a need to change anything.                                           Well, in this particular case you want to focus on the ER because it is where your action will happen. It is precisely where the game usually simplify things because most groups won't have a PC working at ER. I think I can come up with some simple rules for your entertainment.

                                          1st- Effects of Damage: A character gets Fast Dying Condition. The only default source of it is to get the Health Track full of Lethal. At the ST's discretion the character can get it from a source of Aggravated damage without filling the other boxes, and maybe other sources can cause it. With Fast Dying, the character takes 1 Bashing Damage each minute until the Condition is Resolved. Usually it means upgrading Lethal to Aggravated, given context. The Condition is Resolved (and the Beat taken) only with 1st Aid, but the Resolution also causes the Slow Dying Condition.

                                          2nd- Effects of First Resolution: Resolving the Fast Dying Condition downgrades it to the Slow Dying Condition, though the former doesn't need to be the only source of the latter. The thing is to make the Slow Dying Condition the actual target of procedures on ER. The Condition works similarly to Fast Dying, but the damage comes per hour, and it is Resolved with ER Procedure. Slow Dying, once Resolved, doesn't cause any further Condition by itself, but the Procedures can result in other Conditions.

                                          3rd- Effects of Equipment: There is where you'll fine-tune the entire thing. As a rule of thumb, be conservative with bonuses here. The default procedures, both for 1st Aid and ER, shouldn't have bonuses. They are Default, after all. The right tools here aren't making things easy, but possible. The effects of lesser tools can vary, from dice penalty to more time between rolls, or anything else you thing appropriate for a given set of tools. Only the most high-end tools or exceptional contexts should give any bonuses. Or, instead of bonuses, they could let some other characters to actually help your rolls. The default staff support shouldn't give any advantage, it is default. But situations or equipment that let professional characters to help in associated stuff, like diagnosis, with exceptional proficiency, could let the professional roll to provide bonuses. At the end, the exact effects have to be fine-tuned by you.

                                          4th- 1st Aid Roll: It is an extended Dex + Med roll, with also means it gets the character's base dice-pool as a limit for how much rolls she gets. It doesn't interrupt the effects of Fast Dying and it is 1 minute per roll, so the target's wounds are effectively worsening at each roll that don't Resolve the Condition, even a successful one. As a change, I suggest for your case to make the Target Number into 3+1 per Aggravated on the Dying character. Most of the times a patient gets to ER it will be already on Slow Dying Condition, but on a slightly worse situation than when taken on field or arrived at the hospital, because it isn't likely that the 1st Aid had all the needed successes at once. If it was more than an hour from field to ER, it will also have worsened. As per above suggestions, the tools used on field aren't proper for ER Procedures, and if the character is forced to make them this way, it should be at penalty.

                                          5th- ER Procedures Roll: Even though I know Dex is crucial for a surgeon, I would suggest to make it into a Composure + Medicine Roll instead. What makes it worse for the hands of the doctor here isn't low natural Dex, but the need to keep calm under pressure and ignore distractions. Either way, just keep it similar to 1st Aid, but with hours instead of minutes. Take into account all the possible complications until the patient got into ER to decide how much Agg the surgeon will have to deal with. Now you have the character actively racing against time, every roll meaning an actual change in the situation instead of just adding successes to an almost unavoidable complete recovery.

                                          6th- Complications, Consequences and Strategy: You'll probably find everything coming down to "moah dmg" becoming a little dull. Also, this system can be too harsh sometimes, taking out the fun of truly dramatic surgeries. For that, I suggest a relatively simple means of actually reducing the damage taken by the Patient. When there is a failure at the roll, the ST can choose to apply, instead of another point of damage, a Condition to the Patient. This Condition can either make it harder for the 1st Aid or ER rolls, or be a Persistent Condition. Also, if the character doing the roll desperately needs to buy more time, the player can spend 1 success to achieve the same effect, but with a non-Persistent Condition that won't make it harder for future rolls, instead. The actual Condition is at the ST's discretion, though it can be reasonable to ask and listen to suggestions from the player.

                                          It seems a lot, but boils down to the following:

                                          TL;DR: The character loses Health each minute until treated at 1st Aid. If successfully treated, takes a Beat and looses Health each hour until treated at ER. If this is achieved, takes a Beat and is alive and ready to recover. Treating at each stage is Extended with target 3+1/Agg, and the roll takes the time until next damage. Equipment shouldn't give bonus easily, because it is more of a requirement. And you can change damage for Conditions during procedure if you need to slow down the worsening.
