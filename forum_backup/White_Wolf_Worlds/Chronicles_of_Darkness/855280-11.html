    Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
    Also, if you were going to pick a chapter to introduce something that straddles and blurs the space between werewolf's neck of the woods and mage's cosmology...Well, never a better chapter.

    As I indicated in my comment here and elsewhere, as a fan of both Mage and Werewolf, I was overall very pleased with The Sundered World chapter and the rest of Dark Eras. Dave and Chris did a fantastic job.

    No doubt TSW would be the appropriate chapter to bridge Mage and Werewolf. However, given the historic and recent (and sometimes quite heated) complaints about Mage "supremacy" and cosmological intrusiveness into other gamelines (arguments I often disagree with quite strongly) and Dave's clear and unmistakable intent to limit the Supernal as the cause for much of the CofD's strangeness and horror both within Mage itself and setting crossovers (e.g., Arcadias, Signs of Sorcery spoilers, etc.), I was indeed surprised to see the Supernal so strongly implied to be part of the Uratha's actual origins. While superficially fun and definitely interesting, I believe it may ultimately prove distracting or contentious among a number of fans and renew, rather than settle, the setting arguments that the new editions sought to lay to rest.

    For instance, and certainly meaning absolutely no criticism or disrespect to Arcanist and others, the Werewolf Supernal origins are encouraging precisely the theorizing about connections between Supernal Arcadia and Changeling Arcadia and the Hedge that Dave wanted to end once and for all.

            Originally posted by Leetsepeak View Post
            I feel like it's such a massive leap to go from the Wise theorizing about what the Pangeans maybe are to them having a supernal origin. Like, just because they can manipulate stuff with Arcana implies connection, but not really any kind of meaningful origin?

            And what meaningful origin is there in this situation anyway? All it would say is that the Pangeans were maybe around in The Time Before. What does that actually do or suggest for people to take issue with?

            There are many fans who, rightly or wrongly, try to ascribe everything in the setting to Supernal origins or nature, and believe this somehow makes Mage the setting and mage characters "supreme" over other splats. This is often discussed on the OP forums and elsewhere. While I do not personally agree with this perspective or believe it particularly meaningful in running a chronicle even if true, many others certainly are not of the same mind on the topic. I also appreciate that many fans like the mythos of their preferred gamelines to be generally self-contained, no less when crossover is not a priority. In fact, Dave has intentionally tried to limit Supernal cosmological "spillover," going as far to explicitly and very conclusively dealing with old arguments in Mage 2e like the lack of connection between Supernal and Changeling Arcadia.

            My criticism is certainly not that Dave and Chris didn't handle the topic or TSW in a creative and fun manner. Quite the opposite. I was simply surprised that the Werewolf supernal connection was so explicit instead of very vaguely or tangentially implied. If I was the Grand Poobah of The CofD and all its gamelines, I would have likely made a minor some changes here and there concerning this matter to forestall some potential problems and fan discontent. I fear my relatively minor criticism might be blown well out of proportion.
