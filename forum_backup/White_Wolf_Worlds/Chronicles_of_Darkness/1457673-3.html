        Originally posted by Madhatter View Post
        True Fae are given material form by the folklore, legends, and myths they represent.
        Is this the position you're consciously taking to move the game into the idea space you're trying to move into, or is this the assumption you're starting from about the game that is influencing what idea space you see it moving into?

        If the latter, this is the point of disagreement. If the former, I still disagree with this move in terms of whether it plays well to Changeling's themes, but it's at least a conscious decision to change matters towards a specific end, and I respect that much.

        There's some room for interpretation, but this element of narrative design, in my opinion, best belongs on a higher layer up from the diegetic. In terms of you and I telling stories to each other about fictional changelings, yes, the True Fae take direct inspiration from folklore and myth about alien beings and places of both wonder and terror (both traditional and modern; remember that Changeling accepts UFO abduction stories as being of a kind with premodern tales about faerie lords spiriting people away). That's not literalized within the setting. The True Fae are not made of stories humans tell one another. Rather, humans sometimes tell one another stories (at one or two or three levels of remove from the moment of contact and inspiration) about the True Fae. They are alien gods. Aesthetically, yes, from a narrative design standpoint, they are also dragons and princes and devils and enmas. But they aren't paper tigers or creative hollows humanity unconsciously creates through their subconscious fears. They sometimes deign to wear the trappings of human cultures, but this is an affectation, almost a diversion for them.

        It's okay if you don't agree with me stating it this strongly; again, there's room for interpretation. But Changeling isn't a game about physical manifestations of culture concepts where changing the idea landscape changes the enemy. I think it would be a different game if it were that (and there are other games that are more like what that is). It would be less personal and more zoomed out and ideological. The fundamental focus is on the relationship between changeling and Keeper, not between culture and Faerie. Culture is relevant because it is important to human life and speaks to what ties the Lost to the people around them ironside. To the Strangers, human life is just another performance.

                More thoughts, there are two types of Asian American Changelings..

                Eastern Court Changelings: These are changelings of the Eastern Courts/ Asia who immigrated to the United States for some of the following reasons.
                1. They are following their mortal families.
                2. They are fleeing their Eastern Keeper.
                3. They want to expand the power and influence of their courts.
                The Eastern Court changelings have discovered that with the growing Asian communities/districts in the West(China Towns, Japan Towns, Little Saigon's) Asian changelings can use the Eastern Hedge to travel to the West. Right now, this is limited only to the Eastern districts, but with the growing influence of Asia in the West, Eastern Changelings are having an easier time traveling to Europe and North America.

                Western Netted Changelings: These are Burmese, Cambodians, Chinese, Japanese, Koreans, Laotians, and Vietnamese who were captured by Western loyalists/racketeers and sold to Western True Fae. This was especially common during the Imperial European Era, Post WW2 US occupation of Japan, and the Vietnam war. It still happens, but to a MUCH LESSER degree. They usually join the seasonal court, but with Asian rituals and themes.

                                    I feel we are kind of getting off track with the discussion on this thread, as I was hoping to share ideas for changeling chronicles that focused on Asia or Asian characters.

                                    Being a fan of Japanese folklore, especially that which focuses on the youkai, I wanted to introduce a concept that would allow the story teller to introduce and explore the many different youkai of legend. Also, influenced by Takashi Miike’s “The Great Youkai War” I also wanted to introduce the themes of the chosen one, and the forces of good confronting the great forces of evil. One manga I really enjoyed was Nurarihyon no Mago which is about a boy who is ¼ Youkai and the emperor/ruler of all the Yokai of Japan. Researching on Youkai wiki, I learned that the Nurarihyon IS an actual Youkai of folklore and the most powerful and ruler of youkai all over the world.

                                    The concept I came up with is the eldritch entitlement, Nurarihyon in which the character is essentially the emperor of ALL the Changeling courts of Japan. The privileges it provides him is that the character/player gains ALL the mantles from the courts of Japan AND can use their contacts as in court. The Nurarihyon also gains the powerful token/katana Nenekirimaru which acts like cold iron and deals +3 aggravated damage against faerie creatures. Against non-fae entities it does +2 lethal damage.
                                    In exchange, The Nurarihyon’s duties include;
                                    1. Protecting the people of Japan (changelings and mortals) against the forces of the True Fae.
                                    2. Settling disputes between the different courts of Japan.
                                    3. Committing heroic deeds for the Japanese people.
                                    4. Protecting Japanese changelings from hunters and others who would torment them.

                                                            Originally posted by Madhatter View Post
                                                            When Japan was building its alphabet, it drew heavily on Chinese characters and China was the source of Buddhism in Japan. So some of the terms could have from the, though I DO agree most of the changeling courts would have their own local dialects and terms.
                                                            A lot of written characters are shared, but the pronunciation is completely different. (This is even true for different Chinese dialects.) For example, 悪魔 is read in Japanese as "akuma," but while it is also a word in Chinese with a comparable meaning, the Mandarin Chinese word is something like (I am not a Chinese speaker) "èmó."

                                                            This, incidentally, is why even Kindred of the East's "native" term for its titular monsters is glaringly off. "Kuei-jin" is clearly based on the characters 鬼人, but a Japanese speaker would read these characters "kijin," and a Mandarin speaker would read them "guĭrén" ("kuei" and "gui" just being two transliterations of the same sound). You literally can't write "kuei-jin" with its own proper characters without outside notation.

                                                            All of this also is specifically in terms of borrowing from Chinese into other languages. India does not share this relationship of borrowing from Japanese linguistically; that relationship goes in the other direction, though China as an intermediary. Japan might use words derived from Sanskrit, but India does not use words derived from Japanese, except maybe among pop culture fans in the modern age of anime.

                                                            I still object to the idea that there would be an "emperor" of all the Japanese fae courts as ringing false, but I will assent that "youkai" is a good fit for a general Japanese term for the fae, especially inhuman fae like hobs and the Gentry.
