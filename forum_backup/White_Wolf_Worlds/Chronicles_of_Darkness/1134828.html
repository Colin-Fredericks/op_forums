                      Goosebumps has some pretty cool ideas for you for sure.

                      Have something weird happen to a friend of the siblings e.g. Sudden extreme fur growth Or An imagined Friend From Their Past Come To Live As An invisible troublemaker.
                      Make things disappear it by bit or even all at once. e.g. all mirrors or silver Ware, so they organize a stake out to find the culprit (maybe tiny fae?).
                      Have the local museum get a special delivery/exhibition that goes wonky as their exhibit A Starts running around (mummies and cavemen are all - time favorites for this). Maybe the school pushed harder on their tests, and Children who fail the New monthly special test are sent to the Principal, to be a) never seen again and even forgotten by the adults b) Become Model Children - obedient, always Clean And proper, diligent, unimaginative, lifeless.
                      Mirrors are now a pathway Network that can be used by Children a la Jonathan strange. What seemed perfectly safe and fun at first turns more and more into stranger things upsidedown, and Children start to go missing - even after no one uses the mirrorpathways anymore. Were they a trap? Was there something coming for them from the other side?

                      Any "adults dont get it/dont see it" e.g. due to magic makes their actions more impactful. Adults are useless is a common trope for a reason.

                      You could also have them stumble upon a magic book and Release an ancient evil/curse one of them. Or have someone from their class do it and them clean up the mess (e.g curse of monsters coming for that classmate Whenever They fall asleep - now they need to break the curse, and fast! There is only so much bonus the magic option known as coffee can provide to the Stay awake roll...)

                          To go a step further than Wormwood, "Kill the parents" is a foundational piece of child story writing. You don't need to literally kill them, but for child characters to have meaningful agency it's best to remove parents and other adult authority figures from the equation early on. The easiest thing to do would be to make this the crux of the story. Something kidnaps, kills, curses, or otherwise incapacitates the parents and the children have to figure out a way to stop it.

                              You can keep adults from getting involved by just falling back on the cliché "That's nice dear. He has such a vivid imagination."

                              ​Or use isolation, a horror story classic, by placing the story in a place where the kids don't have any adults they think they can trust, summer camp for example. I will say that if you want to keep it PG enough for a 7 year old I wouldn't go too dark.

                              ​Back to the OP; there is a new kid at the playground and they really like him but no one knows where he lives. They ask him but when they go to the address given they find a house that has been empty for years, perhaps due to a fire or maybe the people who lived there just disappeared.

                              ​They find a stray animal and because mom and dad won't let them have a pet they keep it hidden. It's a strange little creature though, not like any cat or dog they've ever seen.

                                  Okay, so I have an idea for an innocents chronicle that I have been playing around with. The core principle could easily be turned into a story for kids, though I only have experience roleplaying with kids around age 10-14, so Im by no means an expert:

                                  The parents are in a car crash. They will live, but they are hospitalized for a long time (Nevermind that they are hospitalized for months, thats the sort of thing thats pretty easy to buy into)... So the players are sent to live with their uncle for a while, while the parents make their full recovery.


                                  Now the thing about this uncle is that he is the classic enigmatic old coot, also, he is secretly a mage. He is by no means a people person, he is a grumpy solitare that takes no pleasure in taking care of relatives he havent seen in years. Whats more important, he happens to know just how deep the rabbits hole is around his home. In my concept, the small town he lives in is on top of a huge Iris into the hedge (Though it could be any supernatural force... A gate to Shadow is a good alternative by my book).. So the uncle is not fond of having children present in what is basicly a hotspot for supernatural dangers.

                                  Naturally the uncle aint around much, seeing as he is secretly out acting as a protector of the city, while doing everything in his power to keep the players from getting entangled in his "Grown up buisness"

                                  Then all you need to do is have the players stumble upon the supernatural.. Maybe they get in trouble over at school, and overhear that the principle conspireing to get the uncle killed or taking over the town, you know, because he is a true fae, or spirit ridden or whatever suits your tastes.



                                  I think the smart thing to do is not to get too dark at first, in the setup I have been chewing on, the kids are not in any immidiate danger. They havent lost anyone dear to them. And if they get in over their heads, they might get rescued by the uncle in disguise.

                                  That way you can adjust the plot and darkness according to how your players respond, if they dig into the plot full on, figuring its up to them, then you can amp up the tension a bit, when the uncle gets wounded or disappears. If they instead seem uneasy, and seek constant comfort by avoiding the plot or trying to get adult help. Then you can use the uncle as a safety net: he reveals that the monsters are real, but for one contrived reason or the other, he needs the kids help in the extend you deem appropiate. Could be anything from "Spy on the principal" to "Almost get kidnapped so he can lure the monster out."

                                  Some great inspiration if you want to share some time watching tv or reading stories with the kids, to get a feel for what they are interested in could be:
                                  Brothers Grimm stories.
                                  Over the Guarden wall
                                  Gravity falls

                                  So yeah, main thing is figuring what they want to play, and how dark it should get... No idea if this helps, but best of luck!

                                      Ooh, I like the Summer Camp and strange uncle ideas!

                                      Simplest way to take all adults out and give a plot hook would be to just make all adults disappear. Now the Children have to Find out what happened, deal with however you decide to get them back, take care of each other in slowly failing infrastructure and possibly have to deal with those Kids that oppose the adults' return efforts. Then, those that have their (age of your choosing, let's go with 12) th birthday Suddenly vanish at midnight, leaving just empty clothes behind ...

                                      Reasons can be anything from Spirits of Childhood, mage spell going wonky, a fae Whisking people away to create his own neverneverland, some sort of magic artifact (possible tie-in with the aforementioned Museum exhibition idea) etc etc. I am sure you are able to find something to use. Although I am partial to Ghosts/Spirits, Mages or Fae for kid stories, myself. Nice, rich, suitably Creepy symbolic value, without going overboard. Making vampire or werewolves fitting might be a tad bit harder But is Certainly possible.
