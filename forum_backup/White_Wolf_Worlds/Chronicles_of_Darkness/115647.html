    04-18-2014, 10:18 PM

I've been thinking about it for a while and it just seems like a better mechanical system for changelings power-wise. It fits the grab bag nature of changeling contracts without stifling it to a set in stone power system. It honestly feels more like a contract with the wyld for powers than the current system does.

                          A quick note on the permanent willpower ones. Those are generally Contracts of Sacrifice and their ilk. I wouldn't expect the average player to have them. I wouldn't even expect the average NPC to have them. They're generally powers that fit a particularly niche character concept (the witch sacrificing her own health to slay a foe, for example). The Goblin Contracts my group uses are more along the lines of Invisible Guardian, Delay Harm, and that one that lets you spot lies at the cost of having to speak the truth (can't recall the name).

                          And I don't believe the system in which Changelings gain powers is remotely personal. The durance is, absolutely. But the reason the Changelings gain powers from it is because their durance makes them part-fae. This is true for all Changelings, regardless of their experiences in the durance. And as a result of being part-fae, they are now "signatories of the second party" (the book uses words to that effect, from memory). That is to say, the Wyrd considers them Fae, and they are now privy to the Contracts and Clauses that were already signed and agreed upon by the True Fae in their dealings with reality.

                          This means that Changelings had no part in the initial creation of the powers they use. They are merely getting access to an agreement signed by other parties by virtue of their new fae nature. The Contracts and Clauses existed prior to the Changeling being able to use them, and the Changeling has no say in what these powers will do. They're all working within the framework of an already existing pact.

                          As for the True Fae, I think you're misreading something. The books mention that the True Fae are capable of almost free-form powers by renegotiating their powers with reality on the fly. That's something different from the way Contracts work for Changelings. The True Fae are the original signatories, after all. They have a right to go back and work out an extra deal on the side when it suits them.

                          Also, the True Fae are not really "different" from one another. I mean, yes, the defining feature of each Fae is that they've managed to define themselves against the Wyrd, as a unique individual. But they are still a part of the Wyrd, formed solely from the Wyrd and composed of none of the extra human bits that Changelings are lugging around. The books imply therefore that, on some level, Contracts are forged between the Wyrd and reality. The Fae get access to them, and the right to reshape them, because they are the Wyrd (and they don't get access to all of them because part of defining yourself as apart from the Wyrd is not being as all-powerful as you can be). Changelings have a bit of the Wyrd about them, so they can sneakily get access to some of the agreed upon powers, but as they are not the Wyrd themselves they cannot reshape powers like the Fae can. They can only work within the existing structure.

                                  Wow, my group seems to be the exact opposite to yours in terms of Contracts. Well, yeah, Dreamsteps and Cobblethought don't see much use (and I do agree that the Contracts of Dreams are the most poorly designed Contracts in the game), but I have one player who adores Hearth. A second level Clause that gives you or a friend +4 dice any time you need it for 1 Glamour? A guaranteed success whenever you need it? Those two Clauses are amazing. The guaranteed Exceptional Success even sees some use, although since our games rarely jump a year at any time it gets treated as a once-per-game-per-player super move. It's still pretty fantastic to have when you need an NPC to accomplish something.

                                  I think we will need to agree to disagree. You state that this system doesn't work for Changelings, but I honestly think it is far and away more appropriate for Changelings than it is for any other splat. Vampires are the ones I would expect to have a grab bag of largely unrelated powers. Changelings have formal Contracts with reality, a list of Clauses they can enact to bend the world in particular, pre-determined ways. Lists of powers you buy in order is very, very appropriate.

                                  Regarding the dragon, he is explicitly stated to be a special case due to the iron embedded next to his heart. Fae in the real world are capped at Wyrd 6 usually. They need some kind of real threat to define them. If they ever go all-powerful, they cease to exist. The dragon can only survive doing so because he is perpetually on the very precipice of death. He doesn't need to be weak enough for a Changeling or mortal to kill, because the iron provides him with all the threat he needs. That's not "whatever reason", that is "if the Fae is challenged by some facet of existence that extra power will not help them overcome". There is no going "ult" for Fae when faced by something they can't immediately overcome. If they were to do that, they would simply fade back into the Wyrd.

                                                          Not too many humans are going to put up a fight against a surprise attack by a being with magic powers and stats as high as 6. Not to mention that if the Fae come at someone unprepared (and since humans don't usually know of the Fae, they're all unprepared) then the odds are good that said human is just armed with their fists. Even a Strength 5, Brawl 5 character still does Bashing damage. Which is to say, they can't actually hurt the Fae. And while props are amazing, their existence doesn't (or rather, shouldn't) put a Fae's stats into the same realm as a dragon or make them impossible to defeat.

                                                          If your GM is running Fae in a different manner from the books though, then your examples aren't really of any use except in your particular case. The fact is that outside of your game, the Fae are never undefeatable because to be so would be to render their existence meaningless. That, in turn, destroys the Fae just as well as killing it would, for all extents and purposes. The core book has an extremely powerful example of a Fae, but none of its stats are higher than 6. That's the default for most of the game line, so I'm not sure what you're referring to when you say that "most of the game tends to ignore those stats". Equinox Road, as an end game supplement, has rules for making them stronger, but it is the book that explicitly states that a Fae that is too powerful just dissolves back into the Wyrd.

                                                          All of which ties back into my original point, that Contracts are not individually struck between different fae creatures (True Fae, Changelings, Hobgoblins, whatever) and reality, but rather between the Wyrd as a whole and reality. This creates the set Contracts, which all Fae potentially have access to (but don't, for the "can't be all-powerful reason discussed above"), and Changelings can sneakily access by virtue of being part-fae.

                                                          Anyway, like I said before, I can certainly sympathise with quibbles over individual powers. This topic is about the actual structure of the Changeling magic system though. While I can agree not all the Clauses are always perfect for their respective Contracts, and some aren't always that useful and/or interesting, I think the way Changeling magic works on a fundamental level is entirely appropriate to the background provided by the books.
