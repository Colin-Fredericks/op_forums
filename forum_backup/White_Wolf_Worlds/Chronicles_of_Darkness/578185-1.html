    I don't say that the effect of supernatural blood have to be detrimental. But that it have to be with a given chronicle needs it to be. If you have an official blessing, then you have an official road to powerplay. Not that powerplay is always a bad thing, mind you. Maybe what your chronicle needs is precisely a way to reasonably turn aside feeding issues, so the vampire PCs can concentrate on other things. Maybe the only effect of Changeling Blood in your game is that such blood can be used as Glamour, and you need that so the vampires can interact more freely with fae and open their own doors to the Hedge. It doesn't matter what supernaturals your group mixes, maybe what you need is precisely that your vampire players have considerable access to boons granted by the blood of their strange friends.

    But another chronicle will be impaired by such. A vampire with seemingly limitless access to blood due to a werewolf companion can become too much powerful for the game. That isn't good if the other players sense they are just fuel to the super-vampire. Also, the drama of your story can be precisely about the difficulties of hunting, and how the mix of characters is something to be handle with caution by the group, for they can be the undoing of one another if not.

    Also, crossover can be handle in a variety of ways, and seriously, I don't think that getting sick with strange blood will be detrimental to that. If you run a game where the players can be of any splat, it isn't the effect of blood that will convince a player to be a vampire, or to be a non-vampire, except maybe on very few cases (and again, with custom effets the ST can handle this in her favor). And if you run a one-splat game but want to add others as NPCs, I heavily doubt you will avoid that just because of detrimental effets of strange blood.

    Quite the opposite, the strange blood is a sure way to tell vampires that a given person isn't what it seems to be, and from that they are sure to look for more info. Just because as long as they don't know WTH was that, they don't know what kind of dangers or boons they can get. And you don't need boons from the blood, it can even be poisonous. The PCs will search for info even if just to make sure they never get this poison again by accident. By the way, those new guys can have something for them without the need of special blood. Werewolves can offer power over spirits, Chagelings have those juicy Goblin Fruits, and so on.

    Neither the thing about "potent blood" can be so straightforward. It is an element of vampire lore, but being inhuman is also an element of the lore of those creatures. A Demon is a rogue piece of the God-Machine. It makes sence for his blood to be the fluid of a machine, and not blood at all. The Promethean is a being of wild Pyros, the energy of life so badly contained that it turns anathema to life, and also artificial beings to begin with. Changelings are changed by a realm of madness that weren't meant to minds of this plane. Don't forget that the effect of such blood isn't just to highlight the themes of Vampire. More than that, it is to highlight the themes of the victim's template.

    Unnatural Affinity doesn't sound to me as being necessary to feed from such vessels. What that first sentence do is guarantee you will be able to digest such substances. After all, even if it doesn't give you the hability to grab ghosts, without it you shouldn't be able to suck Essence, even if you could grab a ghost. I second the oppinion that UA should eliminate or aleviate the drawbacks of such blood, but it's main purpose is to give it the capacity to nourish you as vampiric blood. You know, not just to broaden your feeding options at BP 6+, but so those other options don't risk a Vinculum.

    That said, I would mostly use a simple system of give the vampire a Condition based on each type of blood. Nothing very complicated, just a "do this to resolve the Condition" will do. You can give the players a drawback that is also a boon with this, in a simple way. The beats from those will be enough to make most supernatural blood addicting in its own way, and those beats can be used precisely to explain the power of this strange blood, if you interpret the traits bought with such experience as being spontaneous manifestations born from inhuman Vitae.

                    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                    I don't think it necessarily needs to be bad for the vampire. The 1e effects of Werewolf blood, for example, is a mixed blessing any way you look at it. I just like the idea of supernatural creatures' blood having special effects, but those effects dont all need to be bad.
                    Generally speaking, most people asssume it is. While werewolf can be considered a mixed blessing, the bad seems to outweigh the good by a fair amount - its playing with fire. Meanwhile, mage blood causes hallucinations, no benefit. Changeling blood was addictive drug-like messing with perception, no benefit, promethean was just nasty. And so on. There's definitely a theme of "non-human blood is bad!" as the game goes on.

                    You might protest that it might not be bad, but looking through the thread? All that anyone suggested (beyond the Unnatural Affinity feeding thing) is negative effects​. "Supernatural blood is bad!" is absolutely the message I'm taking away from the discussion here.

                    And I still argue that its generally detrimental to games. While there are plots and stories where having it be bad is definitely worthwhile, I find that having more benefit than detriment serves as a kind of plot hook that encourages more stories, seeking out rare and exotic blood. Blood is everything to the Kindred. Side effects are one thing, they could even be a good thing. But the overwhelming number of so-called side effects are overwhelmingly negative.

                    Lets put it this way. What storyhooks does having all these negative effects bring? How does it encourage stories? Having vampires that are interested in other supers for their blood can lead to tension and conflict as others supers look askance at the idea of being fed from. How does having negative effects encourage stories? Because that's the bottom line - we're here to tell stories, so what stories come from this?

                    Also, if we're going to do this, we should do it in the form of Conditions.

                    Originally posted by Maina View Post
                    You need Unnatural Affinity for this. Interestingly, by RAW it looks like you need a merit to get nourishment at all from other supernaturals in 2e. The first sentence of Unnatural Affinity seems to say such, at least.
                    The Blood Potency section that discusses the merit indicates that we're talking about BP+ elders, so taken in context, I'd say its regular feeding time without the merit. As if human, that is.

                                                I'd expect that without "Unnatural Affinity" a vampire wouldn't be able to take sustenance from any other supernatural if their blood potency was above 2. When it comes to creating vitae vampires are like Spirits which can only derive essence from a matching resonance. As their own resonance (blood potency) grows further from the human norm, it becomes harder to find a match. At first any living blood will do, but over time they need human, and finally vampire blood, to gain nourishment. As other supernaturals are differentiated from humans, in ways far different from vampires, I'd treat their blood like animal blood. It's only close enough to feed from when the vampire is closest to human.

                                                I do like the idea of supernatural blood bringing some benefits though. When a vampire drinks from another vampire it helps them learn that vampire's disciplines, so I'd treat other supernaturals the same way. A vampire who feeds from a werewolf might gain insight needed to learn Protean, or a Mind mage might awaken the powers of Animalism, Dominate, or Majesty in vampire by feeding him and trying to teach him magic. Perhaps a local branch of the Ordo Dracul might consider a diet of werewolf blood as essential to developing the coils of the Mystery of the Wyrm. Also, new Mysteries of the Dragon, Cruac rites, Devotions, or Disciplines, might result from feeding on other supernaturals.
