        Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
        Ignore the technology side of demons. Demons are spies, not tech, and the Demon-Leviathan relationship should be built on that.

        Sadly most of the cool spy themes would hinge on Demons working for a higher power that might have an interest in a Leviathan and their cult. Demons don't, so I'm not sure what to do.
        I don't know if this is or isn't a wasted opportunity - when I look at Demon/Leviathan interactions, I see the possibility for something glorious, something wonderful, something terrifying.

        If Leviathans represent the bloody past that progress has pulled us out of, then Demons represent the potential evils of progress unchecked. They are literally the principle of the soulless, coldly logical destructive machine made manifest in flesh and steel and data and bone. And, through this twisted yin-yang resemblance, they have uses for each other.

        Inquisitors have the hope that Tribe has secrets from before the God-Machine was born hidden away in their racial lore, in their cult's practices and their very blood. Such secrets could be useful to finding Hell.

        Temptors want something simple from humanity: it's most basic, simple of pleasures. They crave the pleasures of the flesh, the satisfaction of the lizard-brain in all its many forms. A Leviathan's cult could easily be a Temptor's private Hell.

        Saboteurs are the simplest and most direct. In a Saboteur's eyes, a Leviathan represents a perfect ally - or at least a perfect weapon - against the God Machine. With the combination of the Demon's Brain and the Leviathan's Brawn, all things can be rent asunder.

        Leviathans, meanwhile, have something more simple to gain from Demonic alliances: Hope. Demons, with their flat immunity to all psychic effects, offer the possibility for real companionship. Demons offer comfort, the ability to better reassure Leviathans that they aren't wrong to survive and that they have their place. They offer a target for Leviathan frustration and hate with the world - the God-Machine is so easy to demonize as being worse for this world than the Tribe ever was, and it's so feasible that if they could smash it all, then all this awful, awkward civilization would just GO AWAY. Moreover, Demons are strong in ways that Leviathans are weak. The "soft combat" that the Marduk Society likes to use to soften up the Tribe can be negated and turned against them by entities who can literally hack society in their sleep.

        Demon/Leviathan convergence is a Match made in Hell. So what if it often ends with the Demon secretly manipulating or controlling the Leviathan, turning them to its own ends? That's just the way it is. Some Leviathans may know that they are the subordinates in the relationship... and be perfectly happy with it. A place as a well-treated pet is better than no place at all, after all.

        Picture it.

        A Marduk cell begins its standard operating procedure against a Leviathan... but everything goes wrong. Their credit manipulation fails and gets them traced as hackers. Attempts to manipulate the system turn against them. And then, one night, the door to their hideout is torn open as a half-man half-monster backed by red-eyed cultists swarms in, catching them off-guard. And their guns stop working right, misfiring, aiming wrong. They are torn apart. And the last thing they see is the amused smile of a man whose empty eyes are filled with streams of whirling, neon-green 0s and 1s.

        A Hunter-Angel tears through the doors guarding its quarry, intent on slaying another Demon that would defy the God-Machine. And then the curvaceous whore at its target side roars and tears apart, a squamous, piscine horror of muscle and maw erupting into being. Teeth of razored bone close down on the Angel, devouring it effortlessly as an old goddess protects her new consort.

        It's very easy for Demons to manipulate Leviathans, simply by doing what they do best: covert infiltration. Assume a mortal form, work its way into a Cult, stand out by knowing how to tell a Leviathan what it truly wants to hear, win its grace as a Beloved who isn't a brainwashed (censored) idiot, and then play the Leviathan for the Demon's own ends. Perhaps even a mutual respect can be earned.

        Sorry, but I see a lot of possibility for Demon/Leviathan crossover. They're just too much yin-yang when I look at them.


        Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
        Any thought put into the Marduk Society's relation to other splats would be just for fun. But it is fun, so think away
        Offer accepted. For this exercise, I will be going under the presumption that Marduk Society may be more virtuous than the norm for the WoD, but that does not make them more powerful. You cannot be all about "the way reason makes us better human beings" and still be the ultimate fighters. A truly "good man" will not use weapons or tactics that bad men will. Marduk Society cares about collateral damage, cares about civil rights, cares about law and justice in a way even other Hunters don't (or reluctantly accept that they can't, not and be effective). In a cynical, grimdark setting like The World of Darkness, being The White Hat WILL hurt. If it was easy, it wouldn't BE so rare to be a White Hat in this game.

        Vampires tend to roll their eyes at the Marduk Society. Their hopelessly optimistic outlook and idealism scream "weakness" in a vampire's eyes anyway, but when they bring that into the socio-political arena, where vampires are masters? ...The phrase "lambs to the slaughter" comes to mind. Vampires do have a tendency to stagnate with age, but fresh embraces and Torpor allow them to regularly "shake things up" and keep them up to the curve, so the tactics that work so well against Leviathans are easily countered and deflected. In actual combat, Marduk tends to be at a disadvantage; they have flamethrower tech, but it's rarely deployed in the field since, y'know, Leviathans are mostly indifferent to fire and flame weaponry is considered a War Crime by civilised people and so Marduk is squeamish about using it. Which is bad news for the agent trying to fight a super-fast, super-strong bloodsucking monster who shrugs off bullets and lightning bolts alike as if they were spitballs. Fortunately for both sides, vampires rarely make the kind of waves that the Marduk Society is trained to look for, meaning they are usually ignored and so are content to ignore Marduk right back. No sense in picking a fight unnecessarily.

        ​Werewolves give the Marduk Society a feeling of unease. There's so many similarities there to Leviathans... but at the same time, the differences are critical, and have led to disaster for more than one hunting group. The idea that werewolves might be a branch of the Tribe that has managed to shake off some of its stagnation and evolve - symbolically represented by their land-animal nature rather than their sea-animal nature - is a nightmarish one. They rage like Leviathans, but they can strike through the same angles that the Marduk Society normally uses to prosecute them. And whereas Leviathans tend to be solitary, werewolves are always found together. The Iron Masters tribe, in particular, has a love-hate relationship with the Marduk Society; they envy the progress of Marduk's technology and strive to be more like them, but at the same time, Marduk keeps trying to kill them. Standard operating procedure for Marduk is to pull out and back away if the "Leviathans" they are after display the ability to effectively counter their social and technological warfare. This keeps them from being the subject of too many Iron Master-led hunts... because those often don't end well for the Society. It's not fair when the enemy can make your computers, your vehicles, every city utility and even the terrain itself want to kill you... or when they can get around any barrier you can create by sliding into some other dimension and out again....

        ​Mages puzzle the Marduk Society greatly. On the one hand, their powers are demonstrably real - there's a difference between skepticism and stupidity, and insisting that the man who just caught your lightning gun's shot in one hand and threw it back at you did it with smoke and mirrors leans towards the latter. On the other hand, their ideology sounds like primitive, backwards superstitions, running completely contrary to the Society's philosophy of reason and logic. Fortunately, they rarely cross paths, as mages tend to avoid the wide-scale and obvious disruptions of the Leviathans, and the Society has nothing mages generally want. That said, the Free League adores the Society, who seem to be living proof of their own ideology about humanity's inner nature being inherently magical, and it's not unheard of for them to secretly protect or support Marduk cells.

        ​Prometheans elicit a potent cocktail of guilt, shame, and fear amongst the Marduk Society. Like Leviathans, prometheans disrupt the fabric of the world around them, socially and physically, with their very presence. Even Marduk, who tries to live up to the ideals of humanity's better nature, can't resist this "Disquiet", and that fills them with shame and guilt, especially since they have studied prometheans from a distance and seen they generally don't want anything but the simple courtesies and rights every human takes for granted. Of course, since prometheans feed on lightning and live off of the grid anyway, Marduk's efforts to fight them almost never go the Society's way. It's fortunate indeed that prometheans are rare as a general rule and especially that they aren't allied with leviathans, as they excel as anti-Marduk shock troopers.

        ​Changelings and Marduk are pretty much oblivious to each other. With Marduk being focused on the big, splashy presence of Leviathans, Changelings just sort of slip by unnoticed. Things almost never go well for the Society if it does get involved: although their idealism means they hate the idea of breaking their word, allowing them some protection against breaking pledges, their tendency to politely disdain "superstitious nonsense" means they get whammied hard when they unthinkingly cross a line that shouldn't be crossed.

        ​Sineaters scorn the Marduk Society as absolutely everything they hate about hunters in general; that self-righteous confidence, that idealistic overenthusiasm, that persistence in meddling in affairs where they don't belong because they trust SCIENCE! can solve everything. A typical sineater attitude is that Marduk is like a great, big, untrained, overenthusiastic puppy - comes barging in knocking things over and pissing everywhere and is too dim to understand why you get mad at them for messing things up.

        ​Mummies might almost be Enemy #2 in the eyes of the Marduk Society. Like the Leviathans, mummies can cause tremendous destruction wherever they go, and are living relics of the bloody past that Marduk embodies moving away from... worse, they are often surprisingly adaptable to modern society. It infuriates the Society when they hear the cynical commentary from mummies that, for all its fancy new gadgets and baubles, human nature hasn't changed in the slightest, and that the core of the world now is no different than the Bronze Age they originally lived in.

        ​Demons horrify the Marduk Society, distorting everything they believe about the purity of reason and the power of technology. Worse, they're almost impossible for the Society to effectively counter. Demons can hide in plain sight, being almost impossible to winkle out of hiding, and effortlessly hide again. Worse still, they can manipulate technology on a level even the Society doesn't understand, making the "social warfare" of the Society pretty much useless. A demon can outhack and outcode any human technology - hell, a Dataform demon sufficiently provoked could probably ride a communication signal up to one of Marduk's satellite bases and proceed to destroy everything on that station's network, if not manifesting in its true form and destroying the whole base.

        If this makes it sound like the Marduk Society is pretty out of its depth in the World of Darkness... that's because, realistically, they ARE.

        White Hatting in a world built on a basis of Black and Gray Morality is inherently costly. Being good is HARD - it wouldn't be the World of Darkness if that weren't the case.

        Furthermore, the Marduk Society is a very "niche" hunter group. They excel at taking down Leviathans - no other Conspiracy can hope to match them on that front. But it means their tactics, their arsenal, everything they have, is specifically geared towards fighting Leviathans. Entirely different beasts present entirely different problems, and whilst Marduk could adapt, the trick is surviving long enough to do so. The difference between trolling a Leviathan by erasing its bank account and doing the same to a Werewolf is that the latter can talk to spirits and find out exactly who did it, gather her buddies, jump into the Shadow, then pop out of it in the middle of your safehouse primed and ready to rip you into little pieces. Combine that unprecedented assault, with the fact you aren't going to have the specialized weapons you need to kill werewolves effectively, and... well, it's not pretty.

        This is a problem all niche conspiracies have in Hunter as it is. The corebook conspiracies are broadly natured for a reason, because they go after everything. Groups like Les Mysteres or The Cainite Heresy go after specific quarry, so they're less effective when out of their field. It's nothing against Marduk, it's just what happens - the inherent problem with specialization.

            Which is why I made Marduk's core tactics give players a reason to be resentful.
            We've previously established that Leviathans don't deserve to be alive, and Marduk is an organization whose flaws are encapsulated by "hasn't won yet," right? As-is, I'd imagine that players would find it difficult to resent Marduk because, well, they're being constantly reminded that their characters are Atrocious Monsters who should've died out ages ago and their enemies are the Good People whose moral duty is to conquer the world.

            Remember, "progress" doesn't have just one meaning. Your "ethically run corporation" Marduk is still a participant in capitalism; for that reason, there is a not inconsiderable swathe of the human population which feels that that Marduk doesn't deserve to exist. Some would say that no singular organization can be trusted with killsats, especially one that hides its activities and doesn't submit to government regulations -- and progress enabled Marduk to do exactly that. The progress of science gave us nuclear weapons, the capacity to annihilate human society, and dissimilar rates of progress allowed for scientific racism and unethical experimentation, as well as imperialism powered by uneven technology levels. Scientific progress isn't just a bunch of benefits that go up all the time -- Marduk as an unalloyed good, and one that has, apparently, peaked in the year 2015/"the present day," is both a poor tool for generating gameplay and a poor representation of progress and reason, both of which are ethically neutral. Progress is only a good thing when it is used to support good values, and reason is only a good thing when one has good premises; if Marduk is going to be a representative of Progress and Reason and Growth instead of a Principle of Goodness with very little depth, then it needs more ambiguity, or, at the very least, shouldn't be a perfectly noble organization with all its negative influences purged and nowhere to go but up.

            (Research also shows that nomadic hunter/gatherers were, by and large, much happier than agriculturalists; in that situation, better lifespans and more control -- progress -- reduced net lifestyle happiness. How's that for progress?)

            I was thinking of longer life expectency, better medicine. Combat with court cases and words instead of swords. You know, the basic stuff that most people agree is good. Though other people might have different ideas and that's fine by me. You could change what Marduk and the old Marduk conspiracy actually believed quite easily (it's mostly left undefined anyway) and it should still work.
            That's not quite the complaint, I think; Marduk's beliefs don't quite matter if they're in the current framework, whose tactics and actions are very carefully delineated according to your personal morals and understanding of progress.

            Since Marduk and the Leviathans are fighting, a single answer would be how conflict has changed to be less harmful, more managed, and often with lawyers not swords. Reflected by Marduk's use of soft weapons to stress the Leviathan and their unwillingness to kill cultists.
            I think you've misunderstood Thamuzz's comment. Being an "interesting beatstick" is, in fact, a bad thing; players don't want a single answer, because it restricts the use of the game to tell stories.

            Vampire doesn't give a definitive answer as to whether Golconda is a real thing, even though "redemption" doesn't seem to fit with the "decaying humanity" theme; that's because "Golconda is definitely a myth that vampires tell themselves because they miss humanity" is a boring answer, whether or not it's thematic. Likewise here: if Marduk's character is carefully and clearly delineated (should the WoD really use the phrase "good guys" or the concept of evil? "Idealist faction" might be better -- it doesn't imply the impossibility of their erring, morally), then you really narrow the stories you can tell with them.

            Mostly. Princess is the game where you debate what "better" actually means. In Leviathan the details of progress is are less important. You're not exploring progress itself but the effect progress has on people. Specifically on Leviathans.
            "Asking complex questions about the themes" isn't limited to a single game line. Promethean asks about the worth and the meaning of "humanity" and the nature of condemnation; Vampire asks us to consider the plight of the monster and what happens to morality in the face of overwhelming need; Mage wants to talk about hubris, whether lofty goals justify compromises of one's own soul, and whether "hubris" is a meaningful term in a Fallen World that people are intentionally grinding into the dirt. None of these supplies definite answers. This is why they are interesting, thematically. As is, Marduk is asking and answering Leviathan's core question, and it's using only one answer: "What is the nature of progress?" "It is an unalloyed good that will save humanity." "How does progress affect people who can't adjust?" "They can't win or achieve any meaningful victories with their outdated methods, and will ultimately be stamped out."

            Games don't answer questions; they just ask them, and the players and ST answer.

            (Also, remember: Leviathan is a game. As in, a game which will be played by people who aren't you. If a bunch of people object to your current portrayal of the setting on the grounds that they don't want to play with it or feel that it makes play less interesting/fun for them, defending and refusing to alter that portrayal in any way isn't constructive. Listen to MB -- I've been following your homebrewing projects, and you do, in fact, tend to fall in love with ideas and then refuse to change them for any reason. It's important to listen to your players and other people who comment on and develop the game, and to compromise with them -- that's how good games get made.)

                                                ​Geniuses HATE the Marduk Society. Hate it madly, with a blinding passion. Hate it with an intensity that sees A.M being quoted. The reasoning is so very simple. Take the seething resentment of the socially awkward nerd for the popular, beloved school-jock. Mix with the frustrated fury of a scientist who has been laboring in obscurity and sees others claiming accolades and fame that should rightfully be his, succeeding at his experiments. Spice with all-consuming, sanity-blinding intensity of the archetypical mad scientist. And you have some understanding of why Geniuses hate the Marduk Society, for taking the role that should be theirs, for having devices that work. The Peerage struggles to keep that resentment under control, and does its best to avoid the Society. But those who won't be part of the Peerage, like the Lemurians, and sometimes even members of the Peerage who just snap and can't take it anymore, go after the Society. And then everybody in the vicinity loses. The Marduk Society doesn't understand Geniuses - indeed, by virtue of their own mentality, they cannot understand what drives the Geniuses, and especially what drives their sheer, unadulterated, pure hatred of the Society. Standing orders for the Society is to vacate, to not give Geniuses the attention or the confrontation they crave. If needs must, Marduk is willing to abandon technology in order to distract a rogue Genius, and has standing orders about committing suicide rather than being captured. They have far too many cases of Marduk agents being violated in the most obscene ways, minds and bodies flayed, distorted and torn asunder as Geniuses tried to find out what makes them capable of doing what Geniuses aren't.

                                                Fortunately for the Marduk Society, Geniuses and Leviathans don't get on, generally. Beyond the fact that, outside of the (Mad Biologists) and (Exploration Faction), few Geniuses have interests in such "primitive throwbacks", it's the standard "mad scientist casually dismisses and unthinkingly mistreats the monster until it turns on him" archetype. Of course, sometimes it doesn't happen that way - sometimes, a Genius/Leviathan pairing manages to get along despite their temperamental clashes. That's when the Marduk Society starts to sweat.

                                                But that's nothing in comparison to those nightmarish occasions when Clockbreakers(?) and Leviathans cross paths. An alliance between an entity that is a throwback to the primordial era of humanity, and a twisted anti-technologist capable of warping reality to nullify humanity's progress is perhaps the one thing that can force the Society and the Peerage to actively cooperate. The idea that, if you brought enough Clockbreakers together with enough Leviathans, or just one powerful enough Leviathan, you could undo everything humanity has accomplished and evolved into since the Stone Age... nobody wants to even think about the possibility, sane or otherwise.

Talking about "feelings" is too much like clinging to arguments about "nature", which are all too often brought up to allow humanity to shirk progressing out of the primordial mire.

                                                    Sample Ritual
                                                    Heed not the Blasphemer (Predation oo)

                                                    Mankinds ability to cooperate and share ideas is perhaps the greatest threat to the Wicked Tribe. It is this ability that led to civilisation and scientific progress, that builds nations and soceities, that allows mobs of mere mortals to rout a would-be God from their town. Communication exposes a Leviathans nature for all to perceive and births hope in the hearts of men. This ritual seeks to disable opposition by removing their ability to share what they know about the horror that lurks in their town.
                                                    Suggested Sacrifice: A living human (willing or unwilling) is subject to lingual mutilation, their tongue is pulled out and devoured or incinerated.
                                                    Sample acts : The entire ritual is performed in silence or in incomprehensible gibberish. The assembly acts out a scene where the unfaithful try and fail to oppose the Leviathan in some way and are punished for it. A cultist reads a set of commandments including a law involving secrecy or blasphemy.


                                                    Base Effect : A single wake-vunerable target with knowledge of the Leviathan or his cult is rendered unable to relay this information to others in any meaningful way. Exactly how this manifests depends on the degree of knowledge the target has : if the target has only limited experience of the Leviathan or his cult (saw a group of robed men lingering around the docks on Saturday) then they will simply avoid talking about the issue or feign ignorance ; if the target has intimate knowledge of the Leviathan and his designs (has perceived the most Wicked in Apotheosis) then they go as far as biting off their own tongue to avoid revealing what they know. Despite the vocal themes of this ritual it applies to all forms of communication. In no way does the target actually forget his experiences.
                                                    +1 Potency : The target instead is forced to express false information about the Leviathan. Again the effect depends on the intimacy of his knowledge – a survivor of a direct confrontation with a God-Monster can be made to tell any lies the Leviathan wishes, whereas those with only minor information will simply make mistakes.
                                                    +1 Potency : The ritual can be used to target a group of people. The group must have some sort of relation (the entire Kramer family, appartment block 12b, all witnesses to the robbery at the natural history museum) to a maximum number of Sheol x 10.
                                                    +2 Potency : The targets silence is contagious and self containing. Anyone who attempts to extract information from them will be subject to similar effects.
