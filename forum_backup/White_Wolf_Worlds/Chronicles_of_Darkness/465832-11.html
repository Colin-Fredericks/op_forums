                    One story ends, another begins. The close of the Berlin story, and downtime.

                    Session XL

                    Marek’s Thoughts

                    November 1922

                    Well, the meeting between the monarchs went well. There was the usual griping to begin with but I think things worked out in the end. We started with Lizette, who was rather well-armed, going and picking up Woebegone first. Apparently she was taking her role as protector very seriously. The Autumn Queen arrived at our house and we quickly dispensed with the cold iron test. Woebegone was in fact wearing a glamour and it fell away as soon as she touched the iron. Her actual appearance is that of a short woman with big ears and nose. She looked like something out of the old tales of gremlins or boggarts. I can see why she uses a glamour. While there is nothing wrong with her actual appearance it is rather diminutive and she wanted to strike an impressive figure as queen of the Autumn court. People might not take her actual appearance all that seriously, which is unfortunate but true. So, I can see why she uses a glamour. Wolfram also commented on the interesting wrappings that she had around her wrists. Apparently they gave off an aura of death and Woebegone explained that the wrappings, which looked like film, were made from Fetch skin. Disturbing though it may sound at first, fetches are just fake people make up of detritus that the Gentry find lying around. So, it’s probable that Woebegone destroyed her doppelganger and used some of the pieces for her own ends.

                    Soon after that Skadi arrived with a bodyguard. We had her and her companion touch the cold iron to no effect. After that Sascha arrived with Oneida and they both touched the iron. Nothing happened. Not that we were expecting anything to. Woebegone’s associate Rudolph touched the cold iron and released a smoke pellet at the same time to make it look like it was burning him. He was the only one that pulled any kind of joke.

                    Once the pleasantries were dispensed with the negotiating began. Wolfram assisted Alla in acting as moderators for the discussion. The arguments boiled down to Skadi wanting at least a generation to grow up remembering the war and the loss and feeling bad about it so they would make sure it didn’t happen again. Woebegone and Sascha wanted to bring the country up out of the mire and revitalize the economy and give hope to the people. I can see why they want to do that. The economy is in shambles. German currency is worth next to nothing. Inflation has risen so fast that the people are going broke just trying to buy bread. So, I can see why they want to bring in outside money and business. Skadi is convinced that if the people forget too quickly the horrors of war then they will be doomed to repeat it. This went back and forth for a while. Mainly between Skadi and Woebegone. Sascha for the most part just fidgeted and complained that he was bored.

                    The discussion was going back and forth until Dearbhail finally got fed up and interjected. She explained the troubles of her own country, Ireland, and how keeping the people repressed just leads to people who have nothing left to lose. Her people could only stand to take so much oppression before they fought back and as far as I know the fighting is still going on. Skadi asked to take a break to consider this and we adjourned for a few minutes to let the Winter Queen think things over.

                    Skadi came back from her deliberation and apparently what Dearbhail said changed the Winter Queen’s mind. Skadi agreed to allow the Spring and Autumn courts to do their best to influence the economy as long as the growth wasn’t too fast or dramatic. They agreed and Alla came up with the wording for the compromise. Now, because there are four courts in the city they will not be able to come to an agreement should they need to in the future. Sascha will do whatever Woebegone does and the Summer King will do whatever Skadi wants so they needed a tiebreaker. Someone that is impartial and would be willing to act as the deciding vote in future conflicts. We talked for a while about who would make a good tiebreaker and eventually decided on Willie Schmidt. Wolfram went to go talk to Willie and see if he’d be interested in serving in this capacity. The both returned, so it seems that Wolfram was able to talk the other man into it. Willie agreed before the fae monarchs to be the deciding vote should they need him, but wanted to make sure that he was exempt from the fall out of any decisions that he made. They agreed to that and included in the bargain some monetary gain for Willie. He did not ask for money, but they offered it as payment for being the tiebreaker should they need it and he agreed.

                    Later we all went to Saints and Sinners. As soon as we arrived Dearbhail read Holbien’s desires as far as werewolves go and found out that he just wants to be left alone. Lizette read his fears with the same focus and found out that he is afraid of being used as breeding stock. A valid fear. Uratha and Pure do not mate amongst themselves, we look to those who are not Uratha. Holbien just wants to be left out of werewolf matters which is fair as far as I’m concerned.

                    Before Dearbhail’s act started Greta joined us for the show. She shared that the Watch have attacked the Pure. Our plan to get them going after one another seems to have worked. With any luck they will weaken one another so neither of them will pose a threat to Berlin.

                    After Greta updated us Dearbhail went up on stage to sing. She was wonderful as always. She sang a number of songs and people got up to dance. The woman that I would ask to dance was the one singing so I stayed at the table and just enjoyed the show. Dearbhail really is quite lovely.

                    Once the show was over Holbien came over to talk to us. Tatiana suggested that if he ever needed help or protection from a threat to call us and we could protect him. He said that he appreciated the offer. What Tatiana did next was not as binding as a Fae oath, but it is binding for us. We had agreed on this course of action before we came to the club tonight. In the course of her promising to protect him she revealed us to be Uratha and herself to be an Iron Master. She did not say it in so many words, but the phrasing that she used is known to be Uratha and Iron Master. Holbien accepted this without comment but nodded his understanding.


                    6 November 1922

                    Hans sent a messenger to find me before I left Berlin. I was actually heading out of the city when the courier found me. I will admit that I was a little surprised by the request. It was the first time that Hans has ever asked for a personal favor, but I can see why he asked for Reznik. Apparently his sister Elsbeth married a man named Dale and they had a lovely daughter named Gerda. They had a decent life. Dale was an accountant and Elsbeth stayed home with their little girl. Apparently there was a man in the neighborhood that became jealous of Dale’s family and their success. A few days ago this man took his anger and resentment to new heights and set Dale and Elsbeth’s house on fire. I think he was intending to get the whole family, but Dale was working late and came home to find his house in flames. He also found a sign posted on the front yard that said all Jews should die. There was nothing he could do. The fire was too big. His family died. The other man, Ernst, got away with it too. He hid behind his family name and the police ignored any evidence that pointed to the man having done anything. It was no secret that Ernst hated Dale and his family, and had even threatened them in the past. There were no witnesses to say he was there. He was not even questioned.


                    7 November 1922

                    I have tracked Ernst today to see what his habits are and which places he frequents. He went to his own place of business for most of the day. He then went to a Gentleman’s club when he did some socializing and drinking. At least he left the building on more unsteady legs than when he entered it. He did not do much else today. I will keep an eye on him and await my chance.


                    10 November 1922


                    Ernst seems to have a routine which I will exploit. Most nights he goes out drinking and socializing with his friends. He usually stays out later than they do and walks home alone. I will follow him tomorrow night and take care of my business.


                    11 November 1922

                    Tonight was definitely an interesting and eye-opening night. I had started to stalk my prey as he was making his way back to his house when I noticed that I was not the only one following him. I caught the scent of someone else, who smelled faintly of bread, also tracking Ernst. When I finally spotted the other hunter I was surprised to see a somewhat short man with a slight build following Ernst through the alleys. His outward appearance was that of a small unassuming man, but as soon as I got close he seemed to be much greater than his stature would indicate. Then I saw him start to throw things at Ernst as he got closer. Big things. Things no man should be able to lift. I decided then that I wanted to find out who this man was and what his interest in Ernst was. So, I approached. I made sure to make noise so he would hear me coming. I was in Dalu form, so if he looked close enough at me he would know that I was not human, just as I could tell that there was something more to him. I greeted him and asked what his interest in the quivering man was. He said that he was “teaching” Ernst the error of his ways. That he should not target those that he sees as less than himself. I admitted to this new man, he said his name was Laszlo, that I had been hunting Ernst myself and was planning on bringing him to justice for killing the woman and little girl. Laszlo said that was fair and just asked that I let him finish what he’d started, and then he’d let me finish my own hunt. I was amused to see Ernst trying to hide, so I agreed and stood back as Laszlo took up the chase once more. I will give him credit… Ernst tried to run, but didn’t get far. He obviously was not overly familiar with the alleyways and ended up well and truly lost. I followed at a leisurely pace and eventually caught up to the pair. Ernst was once again quivering in a ball on the ground. The air was sour with the smell of urine and fear, and strangely enough a burnt smell. There was also scorch marks on the ground where Ernst cowered. When Laszlo saw me approach he stepped back with a smile. I walked up to Ernst and smiled down at the frightened man. I asked him if he’d learned his lesson yet? Ernst swore that he had. That he would never hurt anyone again. That’s all well and good I suppose. If it was just Laszlo there I believe that he would have let the trembling man live and return to his life. Perhaps a changed man. Perhaps not. But Laszlo wasn’t alone. I was there, and the note that Hans had given me was clear as to what he wanted to see happen to the scum that killed his sister and niece. It was very clear. And so I obliged. Nothing elaborate. I just used a gift to grow longer claws and ripped out the man’s throat.

                    Laszlo stood back and watched unconcerned as I killed Ernst. The other man just smiled at me when I was done, and I will admit that my curiosity took over and I asked him if he did that kind of thing often. Laszlo nodded and said that sometimes it was necessary to “teach” those that lose their way and remind them how they should act. I asked him what he was. I was being blunt, but social niceties have never been my strong point. He said that he was one of many and that they call themselves The Children. He told me that he was the mortal manifestation of The Golem and that he protects the Jews of Berlin from those that would prey upon them. I smiled at that. I told them that it was good to hear that the people had a protector. He then said that it was also good that Berlin could count a noble werewolf amongst its guardians. I wasn’t overly trying to hide what I was, but it was still a bit of a surprise to just have him say it like that. I did smile though. I then complimented him on the hunt and told him that I would look for him again the next time I was in Berlin. Laszlo nodded and smiled back. Something made me tell him that if he ever needed help then he should ask for Reznik. I told him how to contact Hans, and he would find me. Just watching Laszlo this evening I know the man can take care of himself, outward appearances aside, but I felt like I should make the offer anyway. In turn he shared with me the name and address of his family’s bakery. They live over the shop. Should I ever need to contact him I have a way.


                    26 December 1922

                    I decided to come home for the holidays this year. I have missed it in the past due to work. Usually I’ve been too far away. This year though I decided to make sure that I’d be home for Christmas. I know that my family appreciates having me home. I don’t visit as often as I suppose I should. The children are growing up so fast. Kvéta is now seven and Dominik is fourteen. Darkja is just over one and is walking now. She seems to get into everything.

                    I have annoyed Alena again, but I am fine with that. For Christmas I got each of the girls another doll, these were from Russia. For Dominik I got a compass. And for the older children I got a couple notepads of paper and colored crayons with which to draw. Kvéta’s eyes lit up at the doll and the drawing supplies. I hope that she puts them to good use. Dominik was happy with his present as well. He wanted to immediately go for a walk in the woods and see if he could find his way home. Erik said that it was far too cold out to do too much exploring, so I suggested that Domink take it with him when he does his chores or when he goes to town with his parents so he can see which direction they are headed.

                    For the ladies I got a bolt of cloth that I saw in Berlin. Dearbhail had commented on someone wearing a dress made from cloth similar and said that it was very fashionable. So, before I left Berlin I stopped at a shop and bought some cloth. I thought that the ladies might be able to make themselves something pretty out of it. Lenka and Desnisa seemed to really like it and Alena didn’t complain when I told them I found it in Berlin. For my brother I got a set of warm winter clothes. I know that he would not buy something like that for himself. I got my father a warm winter coat. The last time I saw his coat it had holes in it and I figured that he would need a new one soon. I was right.

                    As for myself, my brother and his wife got me a pair of good boots which I immediately put on. They are very comfortable. Alena and Josef made me a warm sweater in dark blue. It was very well made. I thanked Alena for her hard work and she looked at me with a hint of surprise. I can be polite when I try. The children had also made me some presents. Dominik had carved a spider using the knife that I had given him. It was pretty well done and I told him that it would have a place of honor in my house. Kveta had helped her mother make me a hat. It matched my sweater and I told her I would wear it whenever it got cold. Overall it was a good visit. The ladies cooked a wonderful meal and I got to spend time with my other pack, or my first pack I should say. We even got some good news. Lenka revealed that she was with child and believes that the baby will be born in August of next year. There was much congratulations and well-wishes. I am happy to see my pack grow.


                    3 February 1923

                    I took a job in France. It has been a while since I’ve been back here for work purposes but I thought the job warranted my attention. The target was a man named Michel Dubois, and he was a factory owner in Toulon. Apparently there were safety issues at his factory and employees that complained or tried to bring to light the problems promptly disappeared. There was some evidence that pointed at Michel being the guilty party, but again, money and family won out and he went free. If he goes missing himself the factory will transfer to the keeping of his business partner Gerard Babineaux. So, I followed Mousier Dubois for a couple of days to get his routine down. He did not have much of a social life. He was married to a stout, patient woman named Julie who greeted his return at night with something like resignation. I did not feel bad making her a widow.

                    I waited until Michel was alone at the factory one night. It appeared that he was working late, so I paid him a visit. I “borrowed” a pistol that I found in his night stand the other day when I was checking out his house. I waited until Julie was gone and acquired the gun. I used it yesterday to shoot Michel in the head and then put the gun in his own hand. It will appear that he took his own life. It won’t even be that hard to believe. From the time I’ve observed him he has worked late several times and complained to his wife about the financial stability of the factory. He kept saying that he would figure something out, but I saw no evidence that he had found a solution to his money problems. I do not know if Gerard will have better luck with the factory, but that is not my problem. I was hired to get rid of Michel and make it look like a suicide, so I did.


                    23 May 1923

                    I got a message from Adawolf the other day asking me to lend them my aid. Apparently they needed help with getting papers for the newest member of their pack, Friedrich. He had to leave his old life behind when he changed and did not have a way to procure a new identity. I have friends that can help with that, so Adawolf contacted me. I owe them a favor anyway so I told them I would be in contact with my people.


                    25 May 1923

                    I got in touch with Hans who put me in contact with his man in Berlin. I then arranged with the pack to have Friedrich come with me to get his photograph taken so identification could be created for him. Friedrich is not much younger than I am but seems to be so young. He had never been to Berlin before and was impressed by the buildings and shops. If he had his way we would have explored all over the city. I tried to explain to him that there were certain groups in the city that we needed to avoid, but he didn’t seem to want to listen. Like most young men I think he believed himself to be invincible; especially considering what he is. I briefly considered just letting him wander the city and see if he found any groups of Pure, but I did not. I eventually got him to follow me to meet our contact and we concluded our business in short order. Friedrich had his new identification. I paid the man for his work and thanked him before we headed back out. We made it back to the pack by nightfall. Friedrich was still excited about his trip to Berlin, which led to me giving the pack a warning about what was going on in the city. Adawolf already knew about the Pure, but was not aware of The Watch. So, I informed them of what was going on and how best to avoid trouble should they need to go to the city. I did not go into detail on my involvement on what is happening within the city, I just gave an overview of the places and people to be avoided.


                    8 July 1923

                    I received a letter from my brother saying that Lenka was having problems with the pregnancy. She had been to see the local midwives and they told her that she needed to rest. He asked that I come home just in case something went wrong. I agreed and headed home as quickly as I could. I decided against running this time since having a car might come in handy. I was right. Early this morning Lenka went into labor a full month before the baby is supposed to come. Andel was very upset, but Lenka was her usual calm self. We had one of the midwives come to the house but she said that there was nothing that she could do. The baby was turned around the wrong way, a breach baby, and she did not know how to turn it. So, we put Lenka and Andel in the car and I drove them to the nearest big town that had a hospital. I’ll admit that I did not pay close attention to the town names as I passed them. I just followed Andel’s directions. It has been too long since I spent any real time at home. Anyway, we made it to the town in short order and Lenka was rushed in to see a doctor. Andel and I were told to wait in the lounge area while they saw to his wife. So, we waited. Andel paced the room frequently and all attempts on my part to get him to sit down were in vain. About an hour later the doctor came back out and said that they had managed to turn the baby around and she was born just a few minutes ago. She is a little small due to her being born early, but she appears to be healthy. Andel finally sat down, in relief. We were allowed to see Lenka about half an hour later. She was holding the newborn girl and singing softly to her. Andel and Lenka decided to name the little girl Aneta, after our grandmother.


                    11 July 1923

                    The four of us heading back home much to the delight of everyone else. We had called and informed them of the good news the day little Aneta was born, but they were still anxious to see her and Lenka home safe. Desnisa and Alena offered to come over and help with the baby while Lenka rested up. The house was rather full but no one was upset about that. I offered to leave to make room, but they insisted that I stay so I did. Since they are putting me up I have decided that I should do something for them. I will make a fetish to keep the little girl safe. She is only a couple of days old, but sometimes she seems to see the heart of you with her stormy grey eyes. She is a special little girl.


                    12 July 1923

                    I spent most of today in the woods trying to find a spirit that I could bind to a fetish to protect little Aneta. I found a playful one this afternoon that reminded me of volchonok. So I made a mobile out of wood and leather. I carved pieces of wood into different animal shapes and shaped a circle out of a flexible sapling. The animals hung around the circle and I hung a dark circle in the center to represent the new moon. To the others the dark circle will just be decoration, but to me of course it represents so much more. Alena was not impressed with the mobile that I made, but then I was not trying to gain her approval. Andel and Lenka liked it and Aneta seemed fascinated with it when they hung it over her crib. That is what mattered to me.
                    I have decided that I will leave in a couple of days. The house is rather full of people and despite their insistence that I stay I know they have enough to deal with without having to worry about a guest. This evening though I was able to lend at least a little aid. Aneta woke up crying. After Lenka fed the little girl she still seemed fully awake, but Lenka was obviously worn out. Aneta had woken me too, so I offered to rock the baby to sleep. I held little Aneta and did my best to imitate the kind of swaying motion that I’ve seen Lenka and Andel use with the baby. Aneta calmed down and proceeded to grab a hold of my shirt and made little noises at me. She seemed to watch me closely so I made sure to keep a pleasant expression on my face. Lenka has told me that Aneta’s eyes will change color within a few months. I found myself hoping that they do not change. I like the stormy grey color. I think it suits her well.


                    15 January 1924

                    I was not able to make it home for the holidays this time but sent gifts to everyone. Andel sent a letter back saying that they were all appreciated. All the presents I sent this time were from Poland. I am happy that they were well received.

                    Not long after that letter I got another one from Andel telling me that father, Lenka and little Aneta had all fallen ill with some sort of lung infection. They were all coughing and had fevers. I wrote back to tell Andel that I was on my way home and would be there within a few days. I had a job that I was supposed to do in Poland. A man was preying on young women in Wroclaw. I had initially planned on terrorizing him before finally hunting and killing him. Having met Laszlo gave me the idea and I thought it would be fitting to “teach” him before killing him. That was the plan anyway. My family, my pack becoming sick changed my plans though. I did not have the luxury of “teaching” Nelek so I just followed him to his home where he lived alone and put a sniper round in his head. The authorities will find him eventually.


                    17 January 1924

                    I made it home in time to care for my family as they lay sick in bed. Everyone took special care to wash the linens and keep everything clean. Of the three affected little Aneta was the worst off. Her coughs sounded horrible and I will admit that I was afraid that she would not make it. She is so little and the midwives told Lenka that babies born early often have a harder time fighting off lung infections. I do not believe that it was their intention to worry my sister-in-law, just prepare her in case the worst should happen.


                    21 January 1924

                    Everyone seems to have pulled through the sickness. Lenka was the first to get over it, then father, then Aneta. I will admit that it was good to see little Aneta finally smile after she’d been frowning so much lately. Her breathing is much improved and she instead making noises that sound happy rather than distressed. Andel told me that Aneta really liked the mobile that I made for her and often reached for the little wooden animals. I am happy that she likes it so much. I was also glad to have made it to keep her safe. I do not know if the spirit helped little Aneta get over the illness or not, but I choose to believe that it did. The local doctor and midwives did not seem to think that she would make it. I am glad that she proved them wrong. Despite what people have said, Aneta’s eyes remain grey.



                    23 March 1924

                    I was traveling through Austria and came across stories of a haunted mill. The place had been abandoned shortly after the war due to the troublesome spirit. So, I decided to investigate. As it turns out it was not a ghost but a spirit of sadness. At least the spirit seemed rather upset and I could only assume that the war left its toll on the spirit world here as well as the physical one. It was not hard to deal with it and it almost seemed happy to be sent back to the spirit world.


                    8 July 1924

                    As luck would have it I made it home in time for Aneta’s first birthday. She is still a bit small for her age, but she has become quite the inquisitive little girl and loves to explore outside. She seems fascinated by the trees, the birds, squirrels, you name it. They do not let her wander too far, but I was happy to see that she has taken such an interest in the natural world. I made Aneta her own little train of spiders, for luck, and she seems to really like them. She has already played with them on the floor and had them marching in a little line towards the doll that Alena made for her. Darkja brought out her spiders and played together with her little sister for a while. Together they had a veritable army of spiders marching across the floor.

                    I think I well and truly annoyed Alena today. I had taken the children outside to play and after they had tired themselves out running around I told them stories about the spirit world and how we should always respect not just the people around us but the things as well.                                                     Well, we did not want violence then, foolish volchonok. We were speaking with the writer to see if the others would stop being foolish and let her join us.

                                                    Yes, they did decide to. Connie did not find anything damning in her mind, just that she is very good at seducing people and not bothered by anything that she has seen, including my transformation and the deaths of many, many of her mates. But she probably hasn’t betrayed any of them.

                                                    Well, Connie could not be certain someone had not meddled with her memories. This is a thing that could be done, apparently.

                                                    I do not like it either.

                                                    No, it is not a reason to run away into the night! There is business, and there will be for a time. We will run later, volchonok. Be patient.


                                                    Lizette’s Plans

                                                    13th July 1924

                                                    So I am resting at home after being badly hurt. May as well catch the journal up.

                                                    The trip to the Moulin Rouge was uneventful. Dearbháil and Dacian saw Madeline and Marie Rouge, so they went to sit with them, while the rest joined Constancia and Marissa. It was mostly Vampire business. Connie had a lead she was following up on, and more immediately a member of the group called the Carthians wanted to meet with us. All the rest of the Carthians we had come across were hostile to Alla, but this Bernard LeRoi was not. Though Connie helpfully pointed out he was a criminal more than once.

                                                    Unfortunately, Constancia and Marissa kept going back to burning churches, which Wolfram and I found rather upsetting. Apparently Connie burned one but does not make it a habit, which of course Marissa was trying to encourage her to do. Tatiana seemed amused more than anything else. She would. She is not Catholic. You know, I wonder why Dearbháil has not worked on Marek to convert yet. I think it would be good if more of us were Catholic. Also, there was teasing Marissa about not having contacted Greta. Their loud falling out in Berlin was the last time the two women had spoken to one another. Tatiana seemed put out that she hadn’t, but I don’t blame her. Greta is a pain.

                                                    At the end of the night we agreed to see Bernard the next day, and Connie and Marissa agreed to come with us for interviewing Nicholette before hand. I went on to sleep after that. It was a rough day, and the visions in the hospital were nightmarish. No wonder Madeline was drinking so much.

                                                    Today I kept busy working on Marlene’s dress and touching up my own. As a noble, Marlene needs to wear pastels and light colors to show she is unmarried. Dull, but I can work with it. Also touched up my, Christina’s, and Wolfram’s outfits. Aside from a brief run to a café that was my day.

                                                    I did not have time to work on a machinegun that shoots chainsaws (ultimate invention) or an autosaw. Though I realize shorter term I have not modified my car much recently. I should really get on that. I wonder if I can rig it to fly somewhere if needed. You know throw a switch or something. It could be a bomber, and I could put more guns on it.

                                                    Mayhem!

                                                    Of course, it might draw unwanted attention. Ah well, my Cadillac Sedan does now anyway some. I suspect it is the Vickers. Or because it is bright green. Or the oil slick or caltrop dispenser. I wonder if I can design something so that it drops unpinned grenades behind it too. Mayhem! Anyway.

                                                    The evening was a second interview with Nicholette with the Vampires present. Yellow-eyed Constancia would use some of her Mekhet powers on her, including reading her mind while Alla lead the discussion. To her credit, Nicholette was a sport about it. All in all, from our driving a tractor through her brain, the most suspicious thing we got was that she saw a werewolf change without losing her mind. I was starting to feel sorry for her.

                                                    Still, she has had an eventful life. She has been to Anatolia, to Egypt, all over the Mediterranean, traveling with this handsome adventurer or that. She had lost boyfriends to assassins, the desert, and traps in pyramids. She had had all sorts of torrid affairs. Though unlike Dana, she was the sidekick, always tagging along. Still, like Dana, she had seen much of the world.

                                                    They actually had Nicholette go upstairs and back in while Connie reported what she had read and they asked new questions (and conducted more mind reading) based on that. But in the end, she was hired, and moved into the Chateau tonight.

                                                    While Dacian and Dearbháil made sure that went smoothly, Wolfram, Alla, Marek and I went to investigate the whole statue thing. Tatiana wandered off. Bernard LeRoi was a sleazebag, but a friendly sleazebag. He gave us a general breakdown, and we walked to his warehouse along the quays. We immediately saw a problem when his guards were missing. Marek heard some sounds of eating from around the corner, and he and a pair of ghouls LeRoi had went to investigate. The guards were being eaten by some sort of murder Spirit. It looked like a multi-eyed flapper the bottom half of whose face was a giant mouth. The ghouls opened up, and it was hit but not hurt badly. Marek attacked and Alla raced over there in a blur. Sadly by the time I readied my Tommy Gun the Spirit had jumped into the Seine. Marek jumped after it. When he eventually returned, he had killed it.

                                                    The rest of us went into the warehouse to investigate. There we were ambushed by a second Spirit, which bit the absolute hell out of me. I had to spend the rest of the fight trying to evade it. LeRoi managed to hurt this one, and between he, Wolfram and Alla it was down by the time Marek returned.

                                                    The statue was indeed missing. The four of us looked around, and found it likely jumped down from the pedestal and walked out. I used my Contracts of Animation to look closer. It waited to leave until it attracted the murder Spirits. I realized after a moment why…it was a statue of the Indian Goddess Kali. I don’t know much about her, but I heard about her devotees in the last century. Who knows what havoc it could wreck in Paris?

                                                    Mayhem!

                                                    Wait, bad Mayhem.

                                                    Anyway, we returned rather than immediately searching. I am rather missing a chunk of my mass, and am hoping for some healing from Dearbháil. I’d like to not be wounded for days again. I shall sleep till she arrives.

                                                    If I can ever get comfortable.
