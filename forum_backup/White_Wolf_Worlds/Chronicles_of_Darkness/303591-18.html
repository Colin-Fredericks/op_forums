        Part of it depends on how literally "I was Sutek, but so were we all" is meant to be taken. When I first read it, my initial impression was to take it straight. After all, the only other reference to Sutek is that he was supposedly Azar's brother whose rivalry led to Azar's death, and that his actions then enabled Azar's ascension and return to Heaven. However, we now have a much more concrete information on that event, and it could easily be supposed that these myths were just that, and that Sutek was an invention. And if the same goes for him, well, Anpu is really the only deity that has been independently verified to exist. Everything else could be the half-baked ramblings of guilty Shan'iatu spinning stories to cover up their shame.

        I'm fairly sure the first chapter also refers to Azar as "the Judges' herald", insinuating that he might be lower (rather than higher, as the Shan'iatu would later claim to their mortal followers) than the Judges. At least, he was before he ascended the throne of Duat.

                        The "names" are an interesting point. After all, Name Magic needed to be used to create the Judges. The names the Shan'iatu planned to use weren't created until the Rite, and remember that just making up a name for Azar was something that struck them as, ahem, divine inspiration. Could be easy to assume that they desired to be the Judges, and didn't decouple their desire from what their desire was named. In other words, they used the names of existing Judges to describe the Judges they wished to become.

                        The Descent rolls are an entirely separate matter. I'm of the belief that, if the Judges the Arisen are bound to are actually the real Judges, that has absolutely no bearing on their mission, Descent rolls, and other little quirks of their nature. They met the Judges because they died and went to Duat, where the original Judges are established as going to in order to pass Judgement on the dead anyway. It's called out in the corebook's GM section that the Judges have never once made a direct reappearance in the life of any of the Arisen since that first and only meeting in Duat.

                        Also remember that, the same section I think, mentions that Arisen think magic cannot be used on a Mummy with the same Judge. They're wrong. It's Mummies who were made by the same Shan'iatu. The Judges they picked have no bearing on that part of their magic. It's a simple assumption, based on what the Shan'iatu told them, that the Judges hold the reigns over their descents.

                        But the Shan'iatu lied when it came to Irem's cosmology, so everything the Arisen know on the matter is suspect.

                        Basically, I wouldn't be surprised if the punishment for trash-talking Irem was not a Judge striking its servant for the disrespect, but rather a Shan'iatu (whatever form they now take) doing so.

                            Before the argument about if their are two sets of 42 Judges or a single set drags on I think its important to mention the following:

                            Its pretty obviously left vague so that the ST can make their own mind up about this idea. There is enough source material that can be interpreted in a variety of ways (as this discussion shows) that one can form their own opinion about it but no answer is given.

                            While I do engage with the history of the Mummy setting (one has to really to run a game) I'm vary weary of treating it as any sort of meta-plot (which it is not).

                            Its something that might be given a more definitive answer in future releases, until then I think its definitely in the "make up your own mind" camp of roleplay fluff (like the majority of "facts" in the NWoD).

                            For my 2p I think there are two sets of Judges, but that purely an ascetic choice (I like symmetry in general and the Judges of Life/True Judges vs The Judge of Death/Shan'Iatu idea appeals to me as a result) on my part based off my take on the fluff presented in the various books.

                                                            Don't confuse my questioning of what is important in an actual game of Mummy with what I think is important to have in the books. Just as the broader details of the setting might not be all that relevant on the intensely personal level of characters in a game, the details of individual characters are obviously pretty irrelevant in a book about that very setting.
