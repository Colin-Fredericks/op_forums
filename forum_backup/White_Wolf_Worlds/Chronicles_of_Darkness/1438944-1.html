Archmasters tend to be fairer game at the individual level.

I mean, if the issues ends up with having a problem that abstract entities lack a form to use in order to commune with the changeling, just give them a form to do so. It is not like the changeling is able to punch Winter in the face anyway.

        So the follow up question, in that case, would be "what is the difference between communing with a 6+ Rank entity, which is stated to be outside of the system's mechanics, and abstract concepts"? Because if it is an avatar, we can just give them an avatar.

                                    Originally posted by LostLight View Post
                                    What is the problem giving abstract concepts a level of awareness which can only be accessed by those which have an affinity to the Wyrd?
                                    You honestly don't see how much this could and would change the game? When I said it deserves its own system it's not because I want it to be a part of the game. It's because it's such a big deal that if it's part of the game you'd definitely need at least guidelines for how to handle being able to contact the abstract forces of the world and preferably something that explains why changelings don't do that all the fucking time to gain information or broker other types of deals. It's an interesting concept that I'd love to see in a game, but as something clearly explained in the core book with the rest of the book taking this into account and not something I would extrapolate from an ambiguity in a subsystem in a source book.

                                    Let's say a player character contacted the concept of Darkness when stuck in an old basement. How does Darkness respond to being addressed, and how does Darkness respond to any request or offer the PC may express? There's a non-existence of rules, guidelines and examples for dealings with such situations. The system I want is not to depict the representation in game traits but to guide the resolution of such situations, something that isn't pure ST fiat with no descriptions to extrapolate from.

                                    When a pack of PC werewolves petitions a Rank 6+ spirit, at least the spirit fits into a well known hierarchy and ecology with good descriptions of how spirits in general act and to some degree also how high Ranking spirits act. Even though the rules are "plot device", there's plenty of meat to bite into for the ST when it comes to designing and playing that encounter, not to mention plenty of distance to be covered between the players coming up with the idea and the PCs actually reaching the spirit in question.
                                    But almost none of that exists for abstract concepts in Changeling. The extremely few examples we've got, the Seasonal patrons, are wildly different. The main thing tying them together, being the four Eurocentric seasons, isn't even applicable to any other concepts.
                                    I can't really say what tools are needed, but there needs to be at least something to work off of. Aside from Bargains that have very specific and detailed rules, I consider the idea unplayable because it's wholly unsupported by the game and requires input that I would only expect in a freeform improv RPG.


                                    And another reason why I'm opposed to slapping this idea onto Changeling now even if I probably would've liked the idea if it was properly incorporated in the core book:
                                    What we know from the core book is that there's some connection between the Summer Court and Summer. The connection is hugely important because Courts informs huge parts of the setting, and taking on the Mantle of a Court directly affects the character, yet the original connection is quite distant to the characters. The founder of the Court directly Bargained with Summer, but every other Summer Courtier has simply sworn an Oath to the other changelings in the Court. Its through their actions and ideology that their Mantle increases, and they tap into pre-existing powers when they learn Court Contracts. Only the summer monarch is said to be directly affected by Summer, and only in that Summer grants the monarch the Crown. That's the only hint of a personal contact between a changeling and a Court patron in the core book, and even that's part of a ritualised system (and, unfortunately, devoid of any description of how it happens). For all other purposes Summer is more distant than the Gentry because the Gentry are at least acting against the characters and acting in the story rather than .

                                    What you're suggesting is that every single changeling, courtier and non-courtier alike, can directly and personally petition Summer, the patron that has otherwise exclusively acted through the effects of the Court and the Mantle, and whose only direct interactions with other characters are described in the history and legends passed down in the Summer Court. I hope you see the same disconnect that I see.


                                    Disclaimer: I don't know much of first edition where things might've been different, so I'm arguing purely from a 2e position.

                                                    I have not been positing that changelings have a special ability to negotiate with abstract concepts, I am saying that changelings can address a present abstract concept — an ability people in general have — and potentially make themselves the conduit for an agreement that is less personally dangerous to them if they can conduct a number of legendary acts in alignment with that abstract concept in relatively short order, or else twist their place in the Wyrd into something that serves that concept.

                                                    1e's mechanics on creating Courts in Equinox Road built on the systems for forging new Contracts, which was a multi-step process.

                                                    "To strike the pledge, the changeling finds an appropriate proxy representing the desired force. The Contracts of Eternal Spring, for instance, began with a pledge sworn in an orchard in full bloom, on a warm and breezy sunlit Spring day. There she swears the terms of the bargain, and makes her initial offering."

                                                    That initial offering is a lesser alliance pledge, in 1e's systems (i.e. you agree not to work against the other party) that you have to uphold for some length of time; the 2e system carries this forward mostly in the Founder's Mark's impact on your frailties and Anchors. After that, you make another pledge to perform a greater endeavor task (whether it be "a single extremely impressive task or a series of impressive, but not overwhelming actions," it's got to be something big enough for people to notice, which the 2e system represents by having those tasks "buy off" the Mantle altering the character to better suit the patron.

                                                    Given OA&T's examples of Carousing and Secrets, there is no reason to assume that the narrative process of striking Bargains with abstract phenomena work any different in 2e to forging Contracts with such things in 1e.

                                                        Think about it like prayer: Anybody, any time, any where can theoretically pray to a deity or saint or what have you for intercession. You don't necessarily get an answer, or even an acknowledgement at all! Maybe you feel some connection stir, maybe you see some subtle sign, but that's far from a given. Some times, most times, you pray and... that's it. You hope. Maybe it's answered, maybe not, you can't be sure. Lots of religions have structures that are supposed to increase your chances. Consecrated places, special offerings, a hierarchy of priests who have a more direct connection to leverage on your behalf, groups of adherents who can second and third and etc your prayer.

                                                        Are Courts really that different? You have the hierarchy, the symbols, the places of power, the rituals. I see no reason why any Changeling, any time, any where can't attempt to Bargain with demi god entities, because the catch is this: If everybody can attempt it, what are you doing to make your would be patron notice you and take an interest? Just because anybody can ask doesn't mean everybody is going to be coming back with a holy writ forged in fire. Casual attempts are going to fail before they even start because they're ignored.

                                                        Why don't Changelings do it all the time can be answered two (not mutually exclusive ways). 1st: They do, it just usually fails to get a response 2nd: The kind of deals the entity in question is interested in cutting come with much too steep a price for casual requests
