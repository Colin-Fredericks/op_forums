        Originally posted by WHW View Post
        As for hacks I generally use:
        Separate XP pools for Supernatural Powers and Mundane Thingies. So for example, in Mage you get Normal Experience that can be spent on Merits, Skills, Attributes and so on. You can't use this Experience to improve things that are related to your supernatural template (other than Merits, I guess).You also get Arcane Experience, that can be spent to buy up Arcanas. You may not use this Arcane XP to buy up mundane things.
        Supernatural Tolerance stats are not buyable with XP, we just advance them when time is right.
        This is in general because we like lower power levels and like mundane skills and abilities staying relevant for the whole game, instead of being super fast eclipsed and overwritten by supernatural powers. Our ideal is the situation where your mundane abilities synergize and cooperate with your super powers.
        This is just a flat out good hack. I used to do this when I ran games with heavy character progression, separating Mundane and Supernatural Experiences. Mundane applied to all the things a normal mortal could buy, as well as Social and Object based merits. Supernatural applied to all template powers, power stats and supernatural merits. It works great for keeping characters rounded and more realistic. In fact it works so well I kinda wish it were an official rule.

                Another one that I figured out is worth sharing:
                Specialties in skills that have 0 dots. Specialties in their intended, original role never felt like working; it was both kinda unoptimal to buy them, and when you *did* buy them, the extra 1 dice was pretty much irrelevant and didn't feel impactful (because people felt like they should invest Specialties into their highest skills); even if it was relevant, it just drowned in a bucket of dice and wasn't really "visible". So I allowed taking Specialties in skills that you don't have, and it works much better; people now buy Specialties in aspects of skills that they are interested in specifically, but don't care about rest of the skill package (example - our Chinese Mage has now "China Town" specialty in Streetwise, instead of dumping whole skill dots there). I also rule that if you have a specialty in such 0 skill that applies, you don't suffer from untrained penalties. This seems to round characters much, much more than just using it as floating +1. I actually see characters with Crafts 0 (Cooking), Expression 0 (Dance), Science 0 (Chemistry) or even Firearms 0 (Handguns).
                At character creation, it both helps fleshing out characters by the standard "what does this specialty mean and where it comes from?". but also by allowing wider spread of abilities and allowing more skill dot spreads that are functional in gameplay. It also encourages specializing by spending dots in one "major" skill, and using some specialties to round out lacking areas, especially on "low priority luxury skills" like mentioned Expression.

                                    Thus far, the biggest hacks I've done to the setting (as opposed to individual rules) have been to the Invisible Realms and their corresponding ephemerals.

                                    The Shadow and Spirits reflect What Is. Spirits are formed from things that have a reflection in existence. Mostly standard. Biggest change is cutting out the entire category of emotional, concept or abstract Spirits - such beings are transplanted to (as of Mage 2E's general naming) Goetia. Another change is the added "5th dimension" of Aspect (maps to Influence, usually, and thus might almost be used interchangeably), which tracks how "close" to the material world you are in the Shadow. Going "deeper" leads to the terrain mirroring the material world less and less, and reflecting whichever Aspect your are headed "towards" more. This is often where your high-Rank spirits live.

                                    The Astral and Goetia reflect What Might. Goetia are formed by thinking beings' ideas, feeling, and concepts - nothing you can directly point to, but "you know it when you see it" stuff. As previously stated, emotional and abstract ephemera have their home here. Oneiros/Individual Dream Goetia are usually Rank 1-2, rarely 3. Temenos/Common Dream has Rank 2-4, rarely 5+. Anima Mundi/Primordial Dreams Rank 4+. Doesn't "map" to the material world very well - it needs to be anchored to individual minds, or saturated areas, with everything outside those inaccessible.

                                    The Underworld and Ghosts reflect What Was. Ghosts are the echoes of dead/destroyed/gone things, usually humans (due to Spirits and Goetia soaking up the essence in most other cases, preventing nascent Ghosts from forming). Ghost usually come into existence Rank 1-2 (as normal), though they can advance to Rank 3 outside of the Underworld, provided the proper stimuli/power. A trip to the Great Below is still required for Rank 4+, though that is mostly because they need to lose all their Anchors to be able to learn how to create new ones (reach Influence 4+). Also, all dead things end up in the Underworld, including the remnants of buildings, Spirits, Goetia, etc., though in the case of other ephemerals, they've usually been so drained of their Essence prior to reaching that point, that they usually end up as little more than Rank 1-0 echoes. Usually. Another change is that I've added is that Time is somewhat navigable in the Underworld - most "visitors" bring their own and thus don't notice, but some of the "residents" get trapped/lost in endless pockets, and a knowledgeable traveller can locate things from the past by moving towards that "age".

                                    Angels, as ever, are the outliers, being "native" to the material world. Biggest change to them is changing the Infrastructure Condition to Purpose - It surrounds things and actions related to the Angel's missions, though Infrastructure can also serve as a substitute (and Infrastructure might also fill this role conceptually, but I've split it out into Purpose for clarity).

                                    Oh, and "cross-pollination" (aka Magath) is far more common and less "looked down on". Spirits with Goetic Influences, Ghosts with Spirit Influences, etc. are hardly rare.

                                                Here's my personal hacks -

                                                Everything is different. Currently, my 2e setting is actually moving away from being a fantasy kitchen sink, and focusing on five key demographics. Mortals, Vampire, Werewolf, Mage, and Changeling. All other creatures spawn from these five core demographics, or something stranger and outside PC options. I've considered Promethean and Geist, but those two are far too personal of games, with a focus on the spiritual, for it to make its way comfortably where I'd be happy with them.

                                                - All spirit worlds are now one. Yes, that's right. The Shadow, Underworld, Hedge, Astral are now just one big conglomerate. I could go into more detail, but that's an issue for another day.
                                                - All energy types are exchangable. Drinking vitae grants essence, mana, and glamour. Giving essence to a mage is the same as mana. Feeding glamour off the others drains their superantural ​fuel.
                                                - Mortal includes elements of Hunter, Demon and will include Deviant elements as well. Focused around the God Machine and the evolution of humanity going forwards. In fact, my players don't know this, but the God-Machine was actually created by humans in the distant future and thrown back in time. Lots of more story stuff there.
                                                - Vampire games have kind of commandeered good portions of both Mummy's and Geist's themes. Vampire is the big "undead" type that pretty much covers everything.
                                                - Beast kind of got cut up between Werewolf and Changeling. Hollows kind of ate how Lairs work, and the affinity for family now runs as additional benefits to pack bonds.
                                                - There are other shapeshifter types in Werewolf. Primarily, they come in the form of Hosts. Still trying to figure out the best way to do cat shifters. The spirit world salad makes sacred prey when dealing with fey and ghostly antagonists an interesting step for some of these. Also, don't use an exorcism on a werewolf. Its not pleasant for anyone involved.
                                                - Mage has lost supernal summons. On the other hand, Spirit magic now lets you call up anything native to the spirit world. Fey, angel, animistic being, elemental, demon, etc. You name it. There's a possibility of even summoning a changeling and binding them as a familiar! Altought, the Abyss is still there, and still very much real. Be warned, eff the dragons, here be Cthulhu.
                                                - Speaking of mages... "true names" are far more central. They're both a bane and a benefit to the Awakened. I like more banes in my games than just paradox.
                                                - Changelings! Now with pacts made with actual spirits!
                                                - The MiB are real. They. Are On. To Us.
                                                - The game is set as a "tier 2" model, a focus on city issues, not global, and not street level. Werewolves are formed into a Protectorate.
                                                - There's an interspecies council, a fight club called the Midnight Games, and even an acting company and a catering service.

                                                        Whenever I have a prearranged idea for a game I usually go to WoD: Mirrors, WoD: Armory Reloaded, or I survey the collection of storyteller guides and players guides looking for what systems do what.


                                                        My last vampire game for instance (v20) I used the "draw from the hat" mechanic with the caveat that THINGS will happen to your character but you can opt out or you can reach into the hat and get 2 exp. This is how I took a cadre of mortals to vampires and then dropped them into the thick of the Jyhad as starting characters. All the characters went all in (there were 3 draws each) and it left them with complications, flaws, backgrounds, and merits. It worked marvelously and really brought home the "darkness" of the world of darkness and gave them a sense of their place in the city.

                                                        Usually I do things like this to match the tone of a particular game, highlight an idea, or add some unique gameplay experience.

                                                        When I ran a the Laundry, X-files, Person of Interest, kind of Hunter game I limited the conspiracies and compact choice to the more scientific friendly ones (Ashwood Abbey, Barret Commission, Division Six, Network Zero, Null Mysteriis, Utopia Now, Cheiron Group, Task Force Valkerie, VANGUARD ) and used one of the pc's Aspiration slots for the 'company' objective. All the monsters in this game had some (weird) scientific explanation. I also borrowed some of the mechanics from Demon (namely cover and a few conditions) but whenever it came up it represented how much attention the various 'Conspiracies' were giving the pc's at any given time.

                                                        One I really enjoyed was a 1e VtR game where character creation looked like this:
                                                        Character Creation:
                                                        Relationship merit required at 1 (pg. 213 WoD Mirrors), becomes
                                                        Veteran(WoD Mirrors) and Profession (HtV) are allowed
                                                        Surging for all starting Vampires (pg. 127 VtR Chronicler's Guide )
                                                        -3 to all Resolve + Composure rolls
                                                        Merit costs reduced Common Sense reduced to 2, Danger Sense to 1

                                                        It was a very low key street level game where the pcs were struggling to make it through each night. One almost went into torpor when he went over the wrong fence and got mauled by a dog, for example. Eventually they were found and introduced into the Danse Macabre. At this point I shifted gears and converted their merits and changed the rules. It was awesome to see my pcs realize all they'd gained, and also what they'd lost.

                                                        (Session 3)
                                                        Social Combat (Danse Macabre)
                                                        Relationship -> Retainer, Contacts, or Allies
                                                        Veteran/Profession -> Status
                                                        Degeneration rolls + 1/2 Status bonus rounded max. +2
                                                        Blood Sympathy and Taste of Family automatically succeed
                                                        Gain +1 dot Haven

                                                        And I also ran a one shot Second Sight game that used the question "what scares you about magic?" to replace one on the mortal Integrity chart, and gave each of them a 'sorcerer' condition based on the archetype they picked from Second Sight.

                                                            I'm trying to run my CofD campaign as vanilla as possible, mechanics-wise, which is hard because I'd customized my long-running Mage game so heavily. World- and story-wise, it gets far more convoluted, because every game I run takes place in the same universe, and making nWod and CofD mesh is a bit tricky. So far, I've run about 6 games in this universe, trying to keep it all lined up.

                                                            My customized rules for Mage are as follows:

                                                            -There are no Mages at 5 dots or higher. There are rumors of some existing, but since they are so rare as to be non-existent, no new 5-dot Mages can be created. This was done to balance out the world and make sure my players were actually playing the game, not just waiting for their "real" powers. Also, a common theme running through the game is that the Masquerade has to be maintained at all costs, and 5-dot Mages start unraveling that quickly.
                                                            -Paradox incurs for the scene and the player. That is, the more witnessed vulgar magic flying around, the more chance of paradox everyone gets, but you only get hit with it if you're casting vulgar magic.
                                                            -Mage sight is on, all the time. Everyone rolls it anyway and burns a Mana to keep it up all day, so there's really no reason to make them go through the entire process. It also means that you can't hide as a Mage, or expect other Mages to just not see you. Occultation still applies.
                                                            -While it is mechanically possible to murder another Mage and take their Mana, diablorizing another Mage earns you an actual, real-life punch in the face. So you won't be that jerk.
                                                            -There are a lot more Mages in the world. A large city will have several thousand, and it's odd but not ridiculous to come across other Mages in your day-to-day life.
                                                            -Successes to call a spirit determine the level of spirit, so you never know what you're going to get.
                                                            -No familiars. Not after the butterfly incident.
                                                            -Custom spells are allowed as long as they're reasonable to the level and Arcana a Mage has.
                                                            -You can re-roll failed spells once per scene, if you burn a Mana (to a reasonable extent).
                                                            -Mages are welcome to have Shadow Names, but knowing a person's actual name doesn't give you a Sympathy bonus. And it's odd in my world to go just by a Shadow Name. It's far more covert to go by John than Raven Starwind.
