                        Originally posted by Sikla Alkis View Post

                        So, if we drop the four elements splat idea, we still need a way to define the 5x5 splats so that were-elementals are given form. I propose something along these lines for the primary splat, for you to play with as you see fit, BDH:

                        - Aerial: Aerial elementals are associated with weather phenomena and other forces of the atmosphere. A raging were-tornado, a noisy were-thunderstorm, and a hell-bent were-snowstorm all are examples of Aerials.

                        - Aquatic: Aquatic elementals are creatures that conjure up sea disasters and other phenomena. A were-tsunami (as BDH pointed out), a hungry were-whirlpool, and a crumbling were-glacier are all examples of Aquatics.

                        - Agnic: Agnic elementals are the children of fire phenomena. A blazing were-forest fire, a monstrous were-fire tornado, and a sizzling were-lava flow are all examples of Agnics.

                        - Aetheric: Aetheric elementals are some of the rarest and most mysterious of elementals, embodying phenomena that comes from the Deep Shadow and space itself. A vicious were-solar flare, a vengeful were-meteor, and a gluttonous were-black hole are all examples of Aetherics.

                        - Chthonic: Terran elementals are creatures of the earth, associated with natural disasters found happening on land masses. A slogging were-landslide, a raging were-cave in, and a shifting piece of were-fault line are all examples of Terrans.
                        I like these, as long as individual characters didn't have to be narrowed down to, "this character is a [were-cave in]" or "I'm a [were-meteor strike]!" It gets too limiting. But yeah, I like it. The Aetheric was a nice touch.

                        For forms, I think maybe we compact: what about three forms? Human. Elemental. DISASTER.

                        Hishu is their hishu. Simple enough. Human means human.
                        Hardunsuath is their urhan and it gives them spirit stats (derived from their normal ones) and certain advantages only spirits get (no wound penalties, etc.).
                        [Disaster/Cataclysm/Chaos] is their gauru and it is a physical manifestation of their core nature. Even being near it is a problem because it lashes out on its own, compelled to enact elemental chaos.

                        Like BDH commented, we talked about compulsion-type stuff. Maybe each character has a compulsion they must fulfill every scene, every week, whatever. If they don't, they risk their own kuruth. Maybe Agnics need to burn things, Cthonics need to bury them, Aetheric... need to... be outerspacey, I don't know! Heh.

                                                            espritdecalmar: Much like how three more options for tribes + the Bale Hounds were eventually added in as plot potential, a sidebar could be made about new elementals embodying forces related to animals, diseases (like bacteria/viruses) and plants. No doubt there would be some confusion over the appearance of such elementals, since the spirit-gods that birthed the original five had had nothing to say (as usual) on the matter. Delicious WTF reactions would probably be across the board.

                                                            @gebbethine: Way ahead of you, there.

                                                            The Paragons: Paragon elementals believe that the existence of their powers is for the greater good at all. Whether it's an Aerial acting as a helpful meteorologist, bringing much-needed weather (such as rain) to needy parts of their country, or an Agnic who tries to redirect forest fires away from people's homes, Paragons are all about the pursuit of helping others and the world. They find purpose in the survival of the planet and humanity through altruism, charity, and the protection of the innocent and weak.

                                                            The Conquerors: Conqueror elementals, in stark contrast to the Paragons, believe in the use of their powers to solely stay on top of the heap. They are tyrants, kings and overlords, whether it's an angry were-whirlpool demanding virgin tribute from passing sailors, or a Chthonic that will bring down a mountain on a tiny village if he is not worshipped as a god. Conquerors are all about the pursuit of power and keeping it, subjugating anyone under their heel they do not see as worthy or equal.

                                                            The Watchers: Watcher elementals believe themselves to be the keepers of Law and Promise. Their moral codes and rules of conduct do not have to make sense — they are the guardians, policemen and governors of myriad mortal and occult secrets, oaths and societies. One Aquatic may be a part of the Coast Guard, bringing in illegal waste dumpers with the power of the waves; another may be an Aerial hunting down mages, feeling their manipulation of weather violates Nature's Law. Watchers pursue order in the universe, and go to great lengths to keep it.

                                                            The Unfettered: Unfettered elementals revel in chaos. They are crazed, unpredictable, the most inhuman in thought and behaviour of all the elemental kin. One may be an Agnic that lights houses on fire every Tuesday, then lights people on fire every Friday just to see what that might do. Another may be an earth elemental who causes earthquakes in the middle of nowhere, laughing as scientists try to scramble and explain the devastation. The Unfettered's pursuit is doing whatever they want, and succeeding whenever they get away with it.

                                                            The Pathless: Pathless elementals do not follow any set of purpose. Instead, they are a loose collective of elementals whose ideals, ethics and goals do not fit neatly within the other established groups, or are not liked by certain individuals. Their purpose lies in whatever they find becomes it, whether it's an Agnic lighting lamps in Victorian-era London to an Aetheric providing clues to the nature of space to a little-known astronomer. The Pathless have many pursuits, but all of them are not as clear-cut as those from the other four groups.
