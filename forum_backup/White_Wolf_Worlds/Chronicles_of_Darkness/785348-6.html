                                    Originally posted by Aspel View Post
                                    You think that it was piracy, and therefore illegal, and therefore bad, and therefore should have been taken down.
                                    It was piracy by text book definitions thereof. And the thing about fair-use, is that it is effectively an affirmative defense. By claiming fair-use, you're making the argument that you infringed on someone else's copyright, but in a fashion the law recognizes you had the right to do. It's not on me, or WWP, to prove it wasn't fair-use, just that it was infringement; which is was beyond any shadow of a doubt. It's on Subnet or anyone that wants to defend their actions to demonstrate that it should have fallen under fair-use provisions.

                                    Being massive case of piracy, done with no actual respect for the game's producers (the people that get paid absolute shit so we can have our fun games, and accept that because they value our entertainment over lucrative careers doing pretty much anything else) hard work (ex: the White Wolf Wikia links - constantly - to where to by the books the wiki references and cites to help promote the games in addition to being an information resource on them; something Subnet never did), it was both illegal and bad in the long term. Any usefulness of the site weighed against the material pirated on the site squarely put Subnet in the category of harmful to the financial viability of the brand. I want more WoD and CofD books. I don't want people putting personal convenience over WWP/OP's ability to monetize the IPs, because if they can't no more books get made.

                                    First off, no, Subnet did not replace an entire product, it supplements it
                                    I never claimed it did. I claimed it replaced a significant portion of an incredibly large number (literally hundreds) of products. The mechanics are not the totality of those products, but they are a major portion of those products.

                                    People by RPG books for a number of reasons, but their primary goal is to provide support for both the RP part of the equation (such as setting), and the G part of the equation (aka the rules including the mechanics). Even if some people did not buy a particular book for the mechanics, or the mechanics are not their primary content concern, it is a vital content concern to the nature of the product.

                                    Subnet did not "supplement" shit. It copied and pasted material, and then posted it online. That's not supplementing. That's that ugly word you don't like: piracy. It even did this for books where hyperlinked PDFs are available so that argument isn't even a valid one to make about what Subnet was all about.

                                    More important, the people running Subnet never had the permission to do what they did, even if it was something of value. Just because you feel like a company isn't offering a service people want, doesn't give you the right to violate the law to do it for yourself instead; even if you don't do it for profit.

                                    People do not buy roleplaying game books solely for the mechanics.
                                    Great. Nobody is claiming this. It's a strawman you're touting. It's nothing more than a rhetorical lie.

                                    If you say the site replaced the books, then yes, you are saying that the site had the only material that anyone would buy the books for, which is patently untrue.
                                    This logic doesn't hold. Subnet can replace part of the book, removing the need for a significant portion of the customer base to buy the product, without having to replace the entire book. So, no, I'm not saying the site had the only material that anyone would buy the books for. I'm saying it had significant quantities material that people buy the books for, which is patently true.

                                    Whether it facilitates a better relationship with fans, or whether it strains that relationship.
                                    It's a no win-situation. Either they piss of the fans that actually support them by buying the products, or they piss off the fans that care more about larger agendas regarding how we handle digital material and fan derivative works in the modern era.

                                    If they do nothing to go after major copyright infractions (let alone the legal ramifications which are significant on their own), then everyone trying to do the right thing to support them by being a good consumer and obeying the law is a chump.

                                    If they crack down on a major copyright infraction, no matter how justified, they'll get headaches about how the sky is falling and all fan-sites, homebrew, and etc. will be killed off in a flood of C&D letters.

                                    They can't facilitate good relationships with fans that don't actually want a good relationship (that is, fans that only want a one sided relationship, of either stripe). So they're going to work with fans that actually want a relationship; which means fans that understand that there needs to be a balance between protecting the IPs and making money, and doing cool fun shit for the fans for free.

                                    Since they're never going to win over the people that see one website C&Ded into non-existence as a sign they're untrustworthy overlords who are clearly liars when they say they want to foster fan contributions but there are lines to what can be allowed - because clearly that's to get us all to let down our guard before the flood of C&D letters clog the works - worrying about how they feel is pointless. They're going to worry about those of us that want to see them acting as a responsible company; both as an enterprise to profit off of creative content, and as one that sees good treatment of their fan community as a priority. Which they've stumbled on a bit, but nothing to actually throw doubt that it's their goal.
