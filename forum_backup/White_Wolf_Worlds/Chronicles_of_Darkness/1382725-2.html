I'm not personally very keen on the idea of action and portrayal leaning a little too far into "Maybe they have a point"-where the Pure have a point, the Forsaken should be able to leverage that right back into their (admittedly) mixed ethos.

                        Well let's look at this way

                        The Pure, when you take what they are as a whole, cant be decent, as ArcaneArts has already pointed out. The fire touched will always believe in their blind worship and stick to spirits who have a morality even more twisted than theirs, the Ivory Claws will always hate you for being "impure", and the predator kings will kill you for simply not respecting their idea of the hunt and even use of technology.

                        But, and I am using tv tropes lingo here, but Evil Virtues can be found in the Aneshega

                        The Fire Touched believe in (their own version) redemption of the forsaken and will try very hard to turn them to their side rather than kill them. They will also fight to the death to protect the misuse of spirits, which means Forsaken alliances arent unrealistic. Not common, but more than possible still.

                        The Predator Kings, as bloodthirsty and flawed their methods are, wish for a time nature ruled supreme. They genuinely wish there were no deforestation or any of that, and believe a pure state of predator and prey and the hunt. And despite how they are, they will respect anyone who adheres to the hunt and old ways. Even a mere human (admittedly, I have a hard time with the predator kings)

                        The Ivory Claws, and this is pretty difficult because they really are the nazis of werewolves, are nonetheless honor bound and are (quite ironically) in touch with their human side. When an ivory claw makes a promise, he is expected to keep it, and if his family is dishonoured, he will correct that slight. And despite having the most bitter hatred, they know when to negotiate (correct me if I'm wrong anyone, but i believe in either corebook or The Pack, its Ivory Claws who managed to put together a protectorate with the Forsaken)

                        So, while the Pure will never, ever be decent by our standards (and I'm pretty sure Forsaken standards), they can have traits that can at least give you a begrudging respect for them

                                    Unsurprisingly, I’m a fan of 2e’s increased local focus and lack of homogeneity for tribes. The Pure tribes are antagonists and their philosophies are more alien to what we find acceptable as humans. But, to try and answer the thread’s title, what does ‘decent’ mean? If by decent you mean ‘Anshega who seem reasonable and willing to engage with outside ideas and not just murder everyone’, then the answer is yes. But if decent means ‘like human philosophies and morals’ then no - but neither are the Forsaken.

                                    Something I *really* like in 2e is that the Pure are more in control of what does and doesn’t make the grade of their philosophies on a very personal level. The Fire-Touched revere spirits, but they hunt those who disrespect the Shadow. For some, this includes hunting spirits who think they should spend their time in the Flesh. That’s disrespecting the Shadow and spirits who think Fire-Touched are automatically pushovers are in for a rude shock. Ivory Claws value respecting lineages and family. On one extreme this can be eugenic-level bullshit, on the other end it’s just families working together to make themselves the strongest they can be against the world. Predator Kings honour the hunt. Some are barbarians who live in the wilderness, make all their own things and hunt down any humans who come into their territories. Others accept that the hunt can mean more than blood and meat and grudgingly accept that humans have adapted their hunts to modern life. The Predator Kings hate it but they live with it.

                                    But no Anshega is going to be 100% decent by human standards. By simply being Uratha they’re content to kill humans without dwelling on it so much. They’ll follow old spirit laws that call for blood and pain because that’s their culture. They’ll use strength and magic to dominate and overwhelm resistance without worrying about broader society because that’s what their ancient instincts tell them.

                                    The thing is, this also applies to the Forsaken. Very few of them are ‘decent’ by human standards. Maybe none. Some of the tribes aren’t particularly aligned with human ideals, and practically none of the Auspices are. Irraka are always thinking about how they’d murder you. Ithaeur care more for the invisible spirits that you have no awareness of. Etc.

                                    But anyway, the short version of it is that tribes - Anshega or Urdaga - are global concepts with local realisations. Can there be decent Anshega? Certainly. Can there be monstrous Urdaga that make the Pure look moderate? Absolutely.

                                                        Originally posted by Krat05 View Post
                                                        Also I'm getting the impression that some of the people here believe that "antagonist" means "villain". It doesn't. Antagonist here means "a person who actively opposes or is hostile to someone or something" there is nothing there that demands an antagonist be a baddie.
                                                        This is factually untrue. You didn't read ArcaneArts's post correctly. An "antagonist organization" here is an organization in CofD rules as an example of how the fundamental creature of the gameline, in this case werewolves, can go "too far" and leave moral ambiguity behind for a clear moral failure. In this case, you are correct that the term "antagonist" is being hijacked. An antagonist in the more formal sense is not your definition, though: an antagonist in a story is specifically someone who opposes the protagonist.

                                                        In other words, an antagonist fundamentally cannot exist without a protagonist. And a protagonist cannot exist without a story. In thematic terms, characters are perspectives on a theme. A theme might be, "Love conquers all," and the protagonist might say, "yes," while the antagonist says, "no", and the story explores who is right by highlighting the consequences of one interpretation versus the other.

                                                        At your table, and in your story, it is entirely possible for the antagonist to be another Forsaken. It can be a human. These are well-established, especially in W:tF, where Blood Talons and Iron Masters explicitly consider all Werewolves and humans, respectively, to be acceptable prey. Hell, it can be your packmate, if your group is capable of juggling that. The limit isn't imposed by the gameline, but by your imagination and your players' consent.

                                                        The moral ambiguity of the "protagonist organizations", as you call them, is not in question. Indeed, that's a major thematic point in every CofD gamelne. That is crucial to ArcaneArts's point. The Forsaken aren't good guys. They're just less bad than the Pure because they have some redeeming qualities. None of the Vampire covenants are moral paragons. All of the Orders in the Mage Pentacle have explicitly called-out problems. Being the good guy is not the default playstyle for a CofD gameline.

                                                        But what is the moral ambiguity that you want to actually evoke? Is that something you want help with, or something you feel you're able to help others with?

                                                        For instance, is it wrong to kill a member of the Pure? The Oath of the Moon already covers that. Do we owe the Pure the time of day? Should we partner with them to stop the rampage of an idigam? Should we respect the borders of their territory? Do we respect the borders of our fellow Forsaken's territory? Should we concede some of their tenets and agree to eugenics programs? How far is too far?

                                                        And that's the question. That's the game. How far is too far? How far do you go down that road before you become Pure? Does your packmate's hardline anti-spirit policy, because they're overreacting to Fire-Touched philosophy, also go too far? At what point does your packmate's environmentalism and distaste for civilization stop being a personal quirk and become a parroting of Dire Wolf's proclamations?

                                                        If you want ways for Pure to stop being Pure, then that's something we could explore. If you want moral ambiguity, then play the game. Exploring those questions is the point. You don't have to, but it kinda sounds like you want to, and if you're not getting that, then it's between you and your table. People might have advice along those lines if you ask.
