                Shifting Flesh (Life ••••• ••)
                • Practice: Entities
                • Primary Factor: Potency
                • Cost: 4 Mana, 2 Arcane Beats
                • Withstand: Stamina
                • Example Quintessences: The ephemeral remains of a slain Idigam, a species that has speciated within the last generation, the heart of a human with the Biokinesis and Biomimicry supernatural Merits.


                By embedding the chaos at the heart of the world’s most adaptable lifeforms into another, the Archmaster grants a measure of that power to the target.

                The target effectively gains the Shifting Dread Power (Werewolf: the Forsaken Second Edition, p. 222), with the following modifications:
                • The beneficiary’s effective Rank is equal to the spell’s Potency.
                • Other than shifting dots (see below) the beneficiary can only benefit from one shift at a time.
                • The beneficiary may only shift dots in physical attributes, physical skills, and merits that relate to a physical trait or skill (such as physical merits, most fighting styles, and some others such as Striking Looks). These shifts, once complete, are permanent. This does not allow the target to raise any attribute above their normal maximum.
                • The target may not change Numen or other supernatural abilities.
                • The target may additionally choose to heal damage equal to the spell's potency.
                • When the beneficiary shifts to a particular form to help at a certain task, this always grants the +5 bonus as though they possessed the limited shifting ability.


                +1 Reach: Skills and attributes may be raised above the ordinary maximum, though any change above the maximum is never permanent and disappears - with the resulting dots being reassigned to a valid trait by the player - when the Shifting power is used again.

                A similar Entities spell exists for the Mind Arcanum, which is Withstood by Resolve and grants only the dot shifting ability, but allows this for any mental or social attribute, all skills, and any merit not reliant on either raw physical traits (such as Giant) or relations with others (such as Status, Allies or True Friend).

                    This one is partly based on Ascension's Augustine Aleph.

                    Universal Superior (Mind ••••• •)
                    • Practice: Dynamics
                    • Primary Factor: Duration
                    • Cost: 2 Arcane Beats
                    • Withstand: Resolve
                    • Example Quintessences: A proclamation granting wide powers to a certain individual, the soul of an absolute ruler.



                    The Archmaster grasps the Supernal symbol of authority and shapes it to their own desire, granting its subject the same sovereign power over the Fallen World.

                    This spell makes it so that the subject becomes the hierarchical superior to anyone they encounter. This grants effective Status dots in any organization whose member they interact with, at a level at least one higher than anyone in their immediate presence. Memories and possibility are no object: Any recollection is rewritten to suit, unless the victim’s Resolve is high enough to Withstand the spell, and any impossibility is outright ignored: A mortal could easily be placed within the local vampiric hierarchy without so much as a passing comment. However, when the beneficiary of this spell interacts in ways that would ordinarily be impossible - such as trying to give orders to someone who truly has no superiors, or a female-presenting person giving orders within an all-male religious organization - this will make the effects fade quickly when outside of immediate range and likely trigger Quiescence.

                    By default, this spell affects every organization the beneficiary interacts with. However, it is possible for an Archmaster to limit this spell’s scope and immunize particular organizations or types thereof. This is often done to exempt supernatural organizations and prevent the inevitable backlash that would occur.

                            I, uh...hadn't really considered it. With Withstand being a 2e mechanic absent in Imperial Mysteries, I guess there's really no canon answer (other than 'just like non-Imperial Practices', which would point to 'no'.)

                            I instinctively went with yes, and I can think of two reasons, one based on the supposed way Dynamics works and another based on the every-tricky concept of 'balance':

                            The first is that Dynamics spells are supposed to be magic that changes, grows and evolves to match changing circumstances. A fully working 'program' compared to the simple one-off 'functions' of spellcasting. This means that their powers can, in some circumstances, spread in nonstandard ways and indeed, this is how Universal Superior works: It uses the subject as a vector for a magic that temporarily overrules everyone's ordinary understanding of hierarchy.

                            The second idea is that people should have the opportunity to resist their mental understanding of what is true being overridden and, since that cannot be at the moment of casting, this seemed the only other alternative.

                            That said, these are not reasons I thought up when designing the spell, but post-hoc rationalizations to defend what I already did and as such the reasoning behind them is suspect and possibly faulty. As a counterpoint, the Mind spell 'Social Networking' doesn't seem to require any such Withstand, and in a lot of ways, it's a less-adaptable version of Universal Superior.

                                The following two are not so much spells as enhancements to existing spells, based on Hung Spell in the core but adapted to the scale on which Archmasters work. The Fate spell is actually an Attainment in my own half-finished Imperial Mysteries patch (which draws heavily on Falco’s work).

                                Imperial Trigger (Fate ••••• •)
                                • Practice: Dynamics
                                • Primary Factor: Duration
                                • Cost: 2 Mana
                                • Withstand: None (though the component spells do; see below)
                                • Example Quintessences: The disruption of an otherwise balanced system, a response system to an action that would otherwise be cataclysmic.



                                The Archmaster crafts a spell container of sorts, which regulates the status of other spells contained within it.

                                Once this spell is cast it may embed one or more spells (as per its Scale) which must all have the same target or targets, giving them a number of trigger conditions equal to its Potency. These trigger conditions may freely interact with any of the spells (giving them separate or identical triggers) and with each other (thus creating conditions which are dependent on other conditions). The triggers are dynamic, capable of operating on any trigger that the Mage could detect with any sense or through Mage Sight.

                                If Imperial Trigger ends before its component spells do, the components are left in the condition they are in at the duration (effectively ending the spell if it is left ‘off’). If the component spells end before the Imperial Trigger does, the spells simply end.

                                Add Any Arcanum 2: The triggers may also operate on any information the Mage could get through any Knowing or Unveiling spell of that Arcanum.
                                Add Time 4: The time spent inactive by component spells does not count towards their duration.

                                Example: An Imperial Trigger is cast with Potency 5 and the embedded spells Body Control, Heightened Senses, Mend, and Life Force Assault. Its triggers are:
                                • Body Control and Mend are active whenever the target actively works towards the Mage’s goals.
                                • Heightened Senses functions when the target desires it to.
                                • Mend has an additional trigger that it only functions when not in view of Sleepers.
                                • Body Control, Heightened Senses and Mend all stop working whenever the target stops considering themselves a loyal servant to the Archmaster and will not reactivate until they do.
                                • Finally, if the target takes violent action against the Mage, Life Force Assault activates and all benefits permanently stop, unable to be reactivated.




                                Adaptive Scaling (Space ••••• •)
                                • Practice: Dynamics
                                • Primary Factor: Potency
                                • Cost: 2 Mana
                                • Withstand: None (though the component spells do; see below)
                                • Example Quintessences: A complete organizational chart of a multinational corporation, a genealogy going back at least ten generations



                                The Archmaster has developed the ability to cast spells which dynamically affect a certain group defined by their Sympathetic links. The Archmaster may include spells with a total number of dots equal to the spell’s Potency. These spells must all initially affect the same targets as the spell’s Scale.

                                When adaptive scaling is cast, define a group of people encompassed by the spell, such as the members of an organization or the descendants of a particular person. Once the spell is cast, the component spells’ scaling dynamically adjusts to encompass any changes in the defined target: People who are no longer members of an organization are no longer affected while new people joining gain the effects.

                                    Annihilate Banes (Spirit ••••• ••)
                                    • Practice: Excision
                                    • Primary Factor: Potency
                                    • Cost: 4 Mana, 2 Arcane Beats
                                    • Withstand: Rank
                                    • Example Quintessences: A fatal flaw that causes a building or organization to collapse



                                    The Mage scours the target’s pattern, ripping out the Spirit’s Ban or Bane and removing it entirely. This affects the Spirit so blessed in all expected ways: a bane no longer does damage to the creature, while an action taken can no longer be against its ban. Removing either the Ban or Bane also makes it impossible to ward against the spirit.

                                    If any action is taken that would impose a new or different ban or bane, the spell goes into a Clash of Wills.

                                    +1 Reach: The spell applies to both the ban and the bane instead of one or the other.
                                    +1 Reach: The spell also works against any ban or bane imposed due to superior Rank.
                                    +1 Reach: Instead of removing the ban or bane entirely, the Archmaster creates a new, hidden one. This ban or bane cannot be researched, and the spell Clashes with any supernatural ability to discern it. By default, only the caster is aware of the new weakness. Used in this way, the spell is an Entities spell.

                                    Death and Mind variants of this spell exist, removing the bans or banes of Ghosts and Goetia respectively.
