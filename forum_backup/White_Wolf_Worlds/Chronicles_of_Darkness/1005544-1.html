    Someone attempts to melee attack you; you interrupt to move your Speed away from them. Their attack fails because you are now out of range.                 I'm not sure why you're saying "except towards a hostile party." It doesn't let you pick two locks in a turn or use two Instant-action Disciplines in a turn, either. If a game allows multiple actions per round, it becomes a must-have for everyone who wants to be passable at combat. That's why it's so rare/nonexistant in 2e.

That means the only way you can really represent the speed of an attack is by allowing it to happen first. Which Celerity does. Even better, it actually allows you to interrupt another character's action, partially bypassing the normal rules of the turn-based system (without actually breaking the action economy).

                                                        I thought Celerity was one of the best changes 2E made. It took one of the worst disciplines and made it super awesome.

                                                        I don't agree that passive Celerity is underpowered. At level 1 or 2, maybe, but so is Vigor. Also, speaking of passive power, they don't have 2 or 3 aspects. Vigor only boosts strength. Then, in turn, strength boosts attack, but that's not a different power, it's the same thing. Same with Resilience boosting Stamina and HP, HP is calculated with stamina, so yeah, duh. What Resilience does, though, is downgrade (non-sun) aggravated, which is very good, but aggravated doesn't really come up that often that it's a game changer.

                                                        Passive Celerity adds to defence. That is a direct counter to vigor, basically. One character with x in Vigor attacking a character with x in Celerity means both cancel each other and the attack dice pool is the same as if nobody had disciplines. Aside from that vigor does give a slight extra edge in making the user able to use bigger weapons which will hurt more, but you're gonna need obfuscate if you want to carry a buster sword around. On the other hand, and that is important, Celerity provides defence against firearms. Nobody gets that except broken as shit gaurou werewolves.

                                                        Now, sure, we're talking about "only" a potential 5 bonus in defence, but unless you're only fighting very powerful supernaturals, A boost of 3 to 5 in defence potentially makes you unhittable by anyone. The average Dexerity/Wits score is 2, and any decent PC who wants to fight will have at least 1 in athletics. Add celerity 3 to it and you get 6 defence. According to some supplement which I'm not sure about (maybe armory), competent but common fighters or firerarms users will tend to get a score of 2 in their skill. I think that was the score for your average soldier or something. It represents limited but professional training, so you'll have to piss off some really nasty people to have more dangerous guys than that thrown at your in large numbers. So let's say we have a sword fighter with Strength 3 and 2 in weaponry for an attack pool of 5 and a shooter with Dex 3 and firearm 2 :

                                                        In normal attacks, the fighter will have to throw a chance die to have a chance to hit your vampire. The shooter will have only 2 dice, which isn't so great (and worsened as soon as the vampire gets in range or takes some form of cover). The fighter has to use willpower to only get 2 attack dice, and the shooter, with willpower will get back what should have been his original 5 dice pool. That is a big deal. Celerity makes you untouchable against mooks.

                                                        You have to take into account the way defence is calculated now. The fact athletics (or brawl/weaponry) is added means that, since you still take the lower od dex/wits, your average defense score will only be marginally inferior to your average attack pool. The same way vigor breaks that balance and give its user a big advantage, so does celerity. The difference is that, against mooks, vigor user can still get hit regularly if they don't have the initiative and one-shot their opponent, celerity, while not making the fight faster, means you can more easily get out of it unharmed.



                                                        Of course, the most interesting part of new celerity is the new active power of interruption, which allows for tactical thinking and can ruin an opponent's day without the multiple attack madness of the CWoD celerity or the crazy styles of 1E (looking at your marksmanship). In my last vampire campaign, admittedly, the players who had celerity tended to forget they had that possibility, so my NPCs didn't abuse it, but provided combats aren't simple affair of rock'em sock'em robots, it can create a lot of opportunities (making someone drop a grenade before they can throw it for example) even outside of combat, it means you can stop someone from pressing a button or push someone who's going to get hurt away or something. The interruption thing is interesting because it can be used in many many ways. Then, there's the initiative boost, which is nice to have, but not crazy, and there's the speed boost which can make you extremely fast at high levels, like catching a car or, like the book says, passing through someone's field of view unnnoticed. It's pretty funny to think it took so many years for celerity to finally become both good and balanced.



                                                        Making celerity boost Dex would only make firearms the best choice period. Get a big enough gun, preferably with auto-fire and kill everything. Guns are already pretty underrated, probably due to the memory of 1E. One of my very average fighter players, towards the end of the campaign, finally decided, after I told her it was actually pretty good, to get and use a submachine gun and she was pretty damn effective with it, killing small groups of mooks in two turns basically, and making her pretty useful in the final boss fight.
