    Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post

    But yeah, this topic was brought up in the last thread. Humans hold the position that is does in this world completely through artificial means. If you strip a human of their technology and place them butt naked in the wild, chances are that they aren't going to survive against most threats they have to face. But if you do the same thing to one of the supernatural splats, they still have a pretty good chance at survival.
    Yes, because tool making doesn't count despite humans not being the only tool making species on the planet. Yeah, no, you'll have to try a little harder than that to convince me that tool making skills are somehow an artificial advantage. It is a natural advantage, and it's been proven that it's also a very helpful one as regards the dominance of a species in a given eco-system.

    More importantly, the idea that tools should be factored out in a full-scale argument is kind of pointless. Hell, even the original Armory made the point that monsters can make and use tools too, albeit humanity is much more properly motivated to do it, and that it's not so much levelling the field so much as changing the game. If we want to appreciate the conflict, rather than dropping a human, a changeling, a werewolf and a beast naked in the Amazon, why don't we let them bring their equipment to bear?

    Also worth noting is this weird idea of a purely physical, violent-or-survival based definition of excellence. What about the arts, the sciences, the development of communities, the preservation of history? What about innovation and exploration and creation and shit like that? On the subjects that are probably more important to grade against than "Hey, Dave, bet you I can lay around naked in the Amazon longer than you", monsters (even in a more real world context) would probably fall behind because the have a metric shit ton of problems on their plate that make it hard to focus on the same rapid evolution of things that make up the sort of eco-systemic dominance humanity has acheieved with it's ideas of civilization, even if the most prominent examples are inherently problematice-and that's before we get into discussions about how their inherent advantages do or do not help facilitate that subject(Such as how, yes, Mages would realistically technologically advance super-fucking fast by skipping through steps 1-7 on most projects).

    I'm just saying that a)The priorities and ways with which to see humanity as inferior are messed up here, reducing it to "Naked in the Amazon" when we need to be discussing how eco-systemic dominance is established, and b) fails to recognize that that's not an isolated thing-monsters in the Chronicles world are in a very essential symbiotic relationship with humanity, and the two are going to riff off of each other and build on the others accomplishments.

    PS: However, all of that neglects two things, which is a) monsters have a symbiotic relationship to humanity, but that doesn't mean that humanity has a symbiotic relationship with monsters(I mean, it can be debated, with some compelling cases regarding some of the monsters who can very easily be described as barriers to worse things), and b) the low reproduction rate of most monsters. You can argue that numbers aren't a thing that should be held as a major advantage for humanity in a conflict, and there's some case for that when all a mage has to do is be willing to self implode and otherwise evoke the Sleeping Curse big time-but it falls apart when it comes to becoming a genuinely dominant species. Just because most monsters are inherent predators of humanity, or at least in a position to stand taller than them, doesn't help when humanity just flat out breeds faster than you. Before long, you're going to their grainhouses, their synagogues, and their movie theaters, because they have uniquely spread and dominated in such a way that you find your monstrous self now has to exist within their context. There's just not enough of you to establish a real dominance, even if every single monster can kill 50 humans with ease or individually outlast them in the harshest environments.

    Monsters win on a small scale, but would clearly lose on a large scale. Is that true of all monsters in fiction? No, it's just the way that Chronicles is written, which is kind of universally humanist, in case you forgot.
