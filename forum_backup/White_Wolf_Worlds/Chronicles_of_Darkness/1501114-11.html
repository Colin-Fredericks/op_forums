    Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
    Does my analysis make sense? Besides the ST disallowing it, is there any reason why this shouldn't work as described?
    The rulebooks aren't written like programming manuals. They're written assuming a degree of shared consensus as to what the rules are for and that some degree of common sense can be used. In the case of Professional Training, the Merit designed to represent "you are invested in the practice of a particular field and active enough in its practice to cultivate networking connections," I think it's pretty evident that declaring that the skills most relevant to your field of focus (which, at four dots, you are very heavily focused and well established in) are skills that you have no practice or training in so that you can conjure maybe like five beats or so is not an intended use of this Merit, nor enriching to the game experience.

    Professional Training is there as low-hanging fruit to encourage you to give some definition to your character's everyday life experience and help work it into play. This is doing the opposite.

                                                            06-20-2024, 09:47 PM
                                                            Originally posted by cybirddude View Post
                                                            When it comes to Touchstones in general, could a character have a the unborn baby be a Touchstone while the one carrying the bag isn't. Also, if a person is pregnant with multiples, could a character have one baby be a Touchstone while the other isn't?
                                                            As previously discussed, most Touchstone systems are built under the assumption that the Touchstone is a character, which an unborn child obviously is not.

                                                            A baby in utero is not going to test a vampire's Requiem in any way that would not be equally or better represented by the person carrying the child, who is presumably not a stranger.

                                                            An unborn child does not have any manner of two-way relationship that a werewolf can draw upon to anchor their Harmony, though the potential it represents could ostensibly be used in the same fashion as more abstract Touchstones like The Locus or The Religion.

                                                            A kid in the womb is not going to teach a changeling how to trust again, and spending a scene "meaningfully interacting with" it as a distinct entity from the person it's gestating in is going to be functionally impossible or at least an exceedingly hard sell for your table.

                                                            A Synergy Touchstone represents a promise, whether relating to your Burden or your Remembrance, and while some of those theoretically have room to apply to an unborn baby, most of them are once again more reasonably tied to characters who are characters who can be engaged with in the story at the time the relationship is tagged.

                                                            If you are a monster hunter, an unborn baby is definitionally not someone you will feel safe around who can help keep you grounded, much less serve as someone you can open up to or trust with your life in a way that wouldn't be better handled by the carrying parent.

                                                            Children in the womb are not characters and thus do not have Virtues or Vices and thus are completely and utterly outside the mechanical scope of Arisen Touchstones, end of story.

                                                            You asked a variant of this question with relation to deviant Touchstones a year ago.

                                                            Generally speaking, the things Touchstones represent exist to drive social/emotional interactions with a specific part of the setting, most commonly and beneficially a character, and an unborn child itself typically contributes constructively to that tool about as well as a donor organ and with the same baked-in question about the player's choice of focuses when there's a different part of the setting wrapped around it that is more conversant or at least better able to be engaged with the systems meant for dealing with people.
