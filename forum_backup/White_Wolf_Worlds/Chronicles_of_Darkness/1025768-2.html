    I may have mis-read Mummy the Curse being in the survey but was reasonably sure it was in there.

    I think its been clear for some time that White Wolf is almost exclusively interested in old World of Darkness. Having said that they do seem to admire aspects of Chronicles, enough to keep it alive at any rate. I suspect that at worst Chronicles will suffer from benign neglect, with Onyx Path free to do their own thing and White Wolf mostly happy to let it tick over. At best, I think White Wolf could incorporate some of the Chronicles material into their plans for the main World of Darkness. I could see a Hunter video game drawing more from Vigil than Reckoning as a possible example. Requiem will never supplant Masquerade but we may see elements that Requiem does well incorporated.

    Changeling the Lost would make a better setting for a tv show or game than Dreaming, in my opinion. Same with Hunter the Vigil over Reckoning. Never looked into Owod Mummy but I doubt it is as evocative as Mummy the Curse.

    I guess we will see but I am personally not too worried about the Chronicles future; if it was going to be axed it would have been already.

            Originally posted by Gravitas Man View Post
            Changeling the Lost would make a better setting for a tv show or game than Dreaming, in my opinion. Same with Hunter the Vigil over Reckoning. Never looked into Owod Mummy but I doubt it is as evocative as Mummy the Curse.
            Changeling or Geist would work well in my eyes. Both of them would allow for interesting tales, as well as strange otherworlds that practical special effects as well as CGI would allow. However, I think that Geist would be easier - Changelings can have some very unusual forms, and unless we take the perspective of a normal human, who isn't initiated to what Changelings look like, that would be a very high costume budget to give every Changeling a unique and otherwordly look. Add onto how a Gentry's realm is typically an otherworldly place in and of itself, the Hedge is a massive forest of thorns and jst strangeness, the Gentry themselves are more forces of personalty and nature than actual people, and how each Changeling is effectively a PTSD victim...it would be hard to write for a series, on top of make visual.

            Geist, on the other hand, while requiring a good amount of work to make specters that the Sin Eaters would regularly see, wouldn't need it quite so innately. The Geists themselves could look like actors in costumes, or they could just be puppets, or CGI, and they aren't needed 24/7 for the audience. Sin Eaters also have the ability to slug off damage like nobody's business, which works for dramatic fight scenes and such, as well as rituals that can easily be modified to suit whatever approach they want for the characters. Plus, coming back from the dead is their hat, so you could have enemy Sin Eaters fall off buildings dramatically and come back in later stories without it being contrived - heck, they may not have even died from the fall in the first place.

                                                        PMárk, I will not argue with which IP had the more culturally significant or has more marketable ventures outside of Tabletop Roleplaying (because you are right, there is no denying how influential or important WoD has been on the gaming, literary, and movie media.)

                                                        What I will argue is the logical fallacy that CofD never had the fanbase or cultural impact the WoD had (again, not which was more culturally significant. But that both were culturally significant.) And I argue this because that fallacy is so dismissive to the fanbase of the CofD, and enforces the idea that Gaming Conventions & television deals are the by all end all metrics for a community of fans. Which is bullshit. The CofD community is as large if not larger than WoD, the difference is that because the CofD is a toolbox like D&D there is no unitied front for the fanbase. Being a CofD fan is often a very personal experience since each ST and player will play in any number of different worlds crafter from the CofD toolbox. There are often reoccurring themes and powers, but each one is only shackled by the powers of the groups own imagination. On the other hand WoD is very much One World of Darkness, and much easier for fans to group together over facets of it they can easily relate to or which speaks to them. This familiarity allows things like LARPS and CONS to form more easily, since each player will know what the setting's strengths...and flaws are right out the gates.

                                                        I think more than anything else, after the fear of CofD being canned goes away, it's this incredibly dismissive tone that grinds CofD fans (like myself) gears. And if it was just other fans of the WoD then it would be business as usual, but when it comes from the people who own the intellectual property to the CofD...then it becomes something to get angry about. Especially when it can push new players away from even trying CofD gamelines, and thus alienating new fans to the community.
