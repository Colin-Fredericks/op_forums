    Creating Beasts out of Monsters With Histories

    11-09-2015, 03:26 PM
    So one of the things that drew me to Beast in the first place was the thought of playing a mythological monster. Of reversing the normal narrative and turning the thing that the Hero slays as part of his journey into the player character. But the current rules don't seem to support that. Right now, despite the lack of a humanity stat, the context of Beasts is still very much, "You were born and lived a human life until you were devoured, you retain your old memories and the mindset they shaped, modified only by your new urges, etc, etc." Except I don't actually want to play a human who was Devoured. I want to play the monster that ate the human (that killed the rat that ate the malt that lay in the house that Jack built).

    I guess what I want to know is, is there any way to support this kind of character in the game as it stands right now? A thing that rose out of the darkness of sixty centuries, out of a legacy of hunger, blood, and fear that poured itself into human form to better walk among its prey. Perhaps a Beast that was once an actual, honest to goodness dragon now bound in human flesh. Maybe a Beast who of old stalked the land as a naagloshii or something similar that found a way to escape the limitations of its native condition through the Devouring. Or one that remembers having been a servant of Nemesis herself, dispensing retribution upon mortals whose hubris provoked her lady. Or one who is very much like Illyria from Angel. Can this be done? How would you handle the creature's actual memories of its previous existence: The actual history, the background of that monster and where it came from? Just make them such that most of them aren't relevant to its day to day existence, and it has to depend on the memories of its human shell? Or is there another WoD game line that would better represent such a creature?

    Hige sceal þē heardra, heorte þē cēnre,
    mōd sceal þē māre, þē ūre mægen lytlað.

      There's a section in the Kickstarter that talks about Horrors with a past- they are goetic beings, after all, and the 'ghost' of a Beast is usually an Unfettered Horror. Mage's Secrets of the Ruined Temple mentions them long before Beast was a thing, the idea of these Astral mythical creatures possessing or even Claiming a human.

      Here's the quote from Chapter Two: "Most Horrors are ancient. Their roots lie in the earliest epoch of human history, when these hairless, defenseless apes lived in terror of predators, uncaring elements, and plagues for which they had no names. Over time they become encrusted in myth and legend; the natural human response to a recurring nightmare in their midst.
      Rarely, a Beast inherits a strong Horror with a name and sense of history. She awakens with a mythic identity overlaid upon her human self. She is not just Ugallu. She is Ashalla, the Idiptu wind-fiend who unleashed hell on ancient Babylon. She feels compelled to walk among its ruins, even though she is also Ashya Dochev from Kazan, Russia. "

      So it appears as though there is a precedent for Beasts believing they are the reincarnations of the Horrors, rather than a human with a Horror. That, I suppose, is the point of the Legend, and when they become the Beast Incarnate, it becomes the source of their Myth. I can totally believe that some Incarnate Beasts with a true sense of history became that way by replaying the myths they came from, only this time they subvert the narrative they were born of and triumph over the Hero. Maybe there are even Beasts who believe they deserve priority and seniority over other Beasts because their Horrors come with a name and a history.

              There are still questions to be asked about the whole mythic identity thing, though.

              What kind of beings qualify for use as horrors with mythic identities? Should this be kept to monsters, or are gods and/or their servants fair game, too? Could a Nemesis actually be *Nemesis* herself? Should territory already claimed by another supernatural splat be avoided, or would it be totally fine to have Samael or Lucifer as your Horror, or Azazel or Mephistopheles? I assume Jötnar are fair game for Anakim. What sort of thing is kosher? What isn't? Is the truth of the claim less important than your ability to back up the legend? Guidelines would be helpful.
