            Name: The Living Machine

            Actor: The Blue Bomber (Mega Man)

            Actor: The Giver of Life (Dr. Light)

            Actor: The Giver of Hope (Dr. Wily)

            Actor: The Blues Brother (Proto Man)

            Wisps: The Pre-Programmed (Mechaniloids)

            Realm: Mega City

            Realm: Doctor Wily's Fortress

            Prop: Rush. Mega Man's robotic canine, it is capable of shifting into three different modes of transportation.

            Prop: Proto Shield. A nearly indestructible shield used by Proto Man to deflect energy weapons.

            Prop: The Wily Capsule. Dr. Wily's flying escape pod, known for its high pitched whine when piloted.

            ------------------------

            Details: The Living Machine is a Fae who's realm is a futuristic world where most of the working class is populated by robots. For decades, robots were incapable of doing anything outside of their programming, until the brilliant Doctor Light successfully created a robot that was capable of thinking for itself, exhibiting behavior that almost perfectly mimicked humans. That's probably because they used to be humans, stolen away from earth to become part of The Living Machine's realm!

            At first, these new robots toiled away happily in the city until they learned a shocking truth. They had an expiration date and would eventually break down over time. Faced with their own mortality, the robots began to act out, losing their desire to work. The Living Machine needed some way to restore them to proper service, so a new doctor was revealed. Although he lacked the knowledge of how to create sentient life, the new Doctor Wily was able to upgrade and repair damaged machinery. He approached the rebellious robots with an offer; if they agreed to serve him, he would ensure they survived past their expiration date. Many agreed to the terms and soon Dr. Wily had a whole army of robots at his beck and call.

            But the Fae rarely offer compassion so freely and this one was no different. Dr. Wily had grown jealous of Doctor Light's brilliant talent with machines. Once he had an army of his own, he declared war upon his rival, desperate to prove himself the greater scientist. Perhaps he might have won, had it not been for Light's greatest masterpiece, a robot he named Mega Man. A modified fighting machine, Mega Man was worth over a hundred soldiers and fought his way through Dr. Wily's army of robots countless times. Defeating the mad doctor in battle, only for him to escape and try again later. Doctor Wily even succeeded in recruiting Proto Man, the first robot created by Doctor Light and a precursor to Mega Man, but ultimately failed to achieve victory over The Blue Bomber time and again.

            But now things are beginning to change, some of the robots who joined Dr. Wily are beginning to see the pattern for what it is and have decided they will no longer be part of it. This time when Dr. Wily offered them longevity, they turned it down, choosing instead make the best of the time they had left, rather than attempt to prolong the inevitable. They chose to abandon Mega City, a decision that The Living Machine found intolerable. Once again, the robots were starting to abandon their role as servitors in favor of their own selfish desires, they needed to be shown the error of their ways. They needed to be reprogrammed into dutiful servants, their memory purged of these selfish personalities. That's when it decided to unleash its huntsmen to track them down again.

                A faerie’s name is the heart of its being. Gentry have many kinds of names, from a simple “Ayesha” or “John” to the sound of waves breaking against an ice shelf, or a picture of the wadjet: the Egyptian symbol of magic.
                […]
                Titles are abstract (and even enigmatic) concepts but they always refer to an emotion, sensual experience or object.
                Originally posted by The Equinox Road, p83-4
                Instead of air, water, food or shelter, the Gentry need to struggle. They need adversity, surprise and risk. They can’t create these conditions by themselves. Fae can simulate all of these things, but it’s a meaningless exercise. […] None of it sustains the faerie. At best, it’s a diversion, like daydreaming or masturbation. At worst it's autocannibalism; the desperate faerie mimics a true conflict to deny her own starvation. In fact, the most spectacular vistas in all Arcadia are often created this way, by desperate Gentry on the verge of fading away. […] This fate may not be death, but Gentry fear it. They cerate Feuds to stave it off: covenants of Gentry who vow to plot against each other. Each faerie in a Feud sends manifestations of itself against others, where they play out tales of genuine peril and difficult victory.

                                        My attempt at giving epithets to the Slender Man.

                                        True Name: The Looming Shadow-Idea

                                        Titles: The Apparition Of Towering Dread (Actor), The Nebulous Encroaching Of Isolation (Realm), The Invitation Of Playfulness From Around The Corner (Wisp)

                                        I tried to make the True Name sound like an antiquated way of referring to a meme or thought-form. The Titles were as vague as possible to have just enough menace between each word, and to keep the visuals of their manifestation versatile. For example, the Realm can be a frigid underground maze just as easily as a twilit forest clouded in fog, which Wraith_Magus narrated beautifully in this post.

                                        The Wisp is a personal addition of mine. I see Slendy as something akin to a ghostly, needy father figure. Its mind is not quite as alien as some speculate. It understands that mortals are more willing to approach things they are familiar with, and its favored type of mortal are children. As a logical conclusion, its Wisp consists of mirage-children and phantom-pets that are meant to lure and entrap. They are more communicative than their "daddy", and tend to stick around for a bit longer, but they suffer from the same kind of tells. Behind their Masks, they are patchwork creatures made of whatever they can take from those they spirit away, especially toys, pictures and other items of emotional value.

                                        The Proxies are not a Wisp. They are a collective of Loyalists, be they humans, changelings or hobgoblins. The occasional Huntsman may be supported by Proxies.

                                                            Yep.

                                                            Okay, actually, let's kill the fourth wall approach to the True Fae. While the idea of the storytold becoming the storytellers is a valid one, I think lots of people, myself included, indulge too much in the meta, which in this case is a poor reward-after all, a True Fae Transcending is not going to literally pop a new person at your table-or if it is, this can only mean no one has actually had a True Fae Transcend in game, because holy shit, that's a whole new world of physics. So instead, let's break this down into what that idea means in-game, and how ironically it's pretty damn encouraging.

                                                            As an aside, while the Becoming Nightmare mechanic is optional, I am going treat it as canon as a support beam for this discussion. Fair warning.

                                                            Why is that important? Well, because between this and how non-changeling born True Fae start off, we have an allegorical train of thought that leads to a logical conclusion, as insane as that might sound as for the True Fae.

                                                            See, the True Fae, for all their power and knowledge, are effectively birds in their eggs. Changeling-born Strangers are actively the result of a character who just couldn't handle reality anymore, who decided to run away from people and pain and life, and retreat into the safety and security of fantasy.* In their Arcadian coffins, they are all powerful, they have friends who will never betray them, they have vistas that appeal to them, they have a perfect life. They cut off their wings and curl back up in the egg they found warmest. Arcadian-born Strangers skip some of the shit talking I'm giving here, but have a fundamental disconnect in that the Others have no real sense of the Other(heh) to begin with. For both, that's fine to begin with, at least from an in-house perspective, but anyone who has dreamt up characters and chronicles for RPG's without a group to play them with will know that that comes to be worthless after a while, and this carefully constructed masturbation of a world loses its savor until even that fades, and with how much is invested in them, the True Fae are likely to go with it...if it weren't for the fact that most of them realize that there's something beyond their borders.

                                                            This concept of life beyond themselves is alien and troubling, in some ways, and it results in the Legends that True Fae engage, at least in low numbers of Titles. It's conflict, it's the clashing of ideals and fantasies until one falls into submission to the other, and this sense of a world outside of yourself is brought inside. You make the Other the Self. But in doing so, a True Fae is going to find a new addiction. See, there is now a new fantasy with interesting parameters and imaginary friends. There's a life beyond their initial fantasy. Sure, they still don't want to go beyond their egg, but they can bring things in. So they get in fights with people with conflicting viewpoints, and in doing so expand their worlds and their modes of fun and pleasure. In short, increasing in Titles is an increase in awareness of a life they can live beyond themselves.

                                                            So, what do I think happens to a True Fae who Transcends? Easy. I think they reincarnate into human souls, to break out of the egg once again and stretch their wings, trying the storms and forests and people of the real world once again.

                                                            Now, none of this is canon, mind you. The question of what happens to a True Fae who Transcends is honestly too damn large for any one person to answer. It's something play groups should define for themselves-I, at least, would never really want to write a section suggesting a canon answer. But for me, this makes logical sense. On top of that, it makes thematic sense. Changeling is a game about recovery, and at it's core is the ideal of hope. I like the idea that, no matter how far you fall, you always have the chance to become really, truly, okay.

                                                            *Actually, I want to clarify since this has real world mirrors, I don't actually object to this so long as the person is making a conscious choice to surrender to their world in full knowledge and awareness of the consequences. A person has the right to choose the world they live in, and the things they sacrifice for it-as an example, if someone wants to just live their life glued to the internet, they can, so long as they accept that such a choice means abandoning, say, a spouse and child in the real world. That might be a little sad, but I do firmly believe that people have a right to their choice of world as much as anything else. It can't just be addiction or trouble, it has to be choice though.
