                                Yeah, but I like the protect monster angle personally. Here's my try at it. Feel free to comment:

                                Cheryl Wilson, the Monster Keeper

                                “I don’t care if he is a monster. That doesn’t give you the right to kill him.”
                                An unfortunate truth many Hunters aren’t comfortable with, but usually end up learning, is that not all monsters are pure evil. While many of them certainly are irredeemable fiends, there are just as many who really just want to be left alone, or even are trying to do good. These are the ones the Noble known as the Monster Keeper makes her duty of protecting.
                                Originally a young girl named Cheryl Wilson, this particular Princess grew up in a small, but reasonably happy family, with loving parents and a little brother named Lee. She was a good-hearted kid, and got along pretty fine with him, often acting protective; they had their arguments, like any sibling, but overall they were rather close.
                                At some point when they were teenagers, however, something changed with Lee. The boy went through a Devouring, and became a Beast. Cheryl was confused about what had happened to her little brother, who was suddenly talking about a hungry monster living inside his mind and urging him to hurt people. To make things worse, when he tried to resist, the entire family started having nightmares about being chased by a hideous dragon-like creature, which always ended with them awakening and finding Lee in tears, repeating he didn’t want to hurt anyone.
                                Then came the day where a man came to the home and tried to murder Lee, insisting the boy was a monster. When she saw this lunatic trying to kill her beloved little brother she had seen crying for weeks, something inside Cheryl snapped, and she went through a transformation of her own, becoming a Noble. With her newfound power, she was able to defeat the man and save her brother. Soon after, she found out not only could she now gather the energy to feed her powers by helping people around her, but doing so actually seemed to feed whatever creature was inside Lee, diminishing its urge to hurt people. In a matter of days, the nightmares ended, and the kid could more or less resume a normal life.
                                Ever since, the two siblings have become even more inseparable than they already were, and regularly travel, looking for other supernatural beings like them who get hunted down like animals. Whenever they find one, they observe from a distance, trying to figure out if they are dealing with a real monster who needs to be stopped, or a victim of the consequences. If he proves the former, they let the local hunters do their job, and might even give them tips in the case of really bad seed. If the latter, however, the Princess does everything in her power to defend the creature. She will usually try diplomacy first, but should the hunters prove impossible to reason with, or worse, not care, she will unleash all her power on them.

                                                Originally posted by Darinas View Post
                                                I was a bit concerned with how people would react to it, since portraying Beasts sympathetically seems to raise some controversy among fans, but it seems that one works.
                                                Honestly, her brother is a Beast in name only, at least by what's written there. He doesn't feed upon human fears, he doesn't teach lessons. Maybe you just didn't get to that, but I'm guessing he uses Family Dinner to feed. (A mechanic I feel makes it way to easy for Beasts to just avoid Hunger)

                                                Incidently that's probably half the reason Princesses and Beasts usually don't get along. A Beast can't exactly justify their feeding as necessity if assisting a Princess with good works is a viable alternative. The other half is that Princesses would agree that lessons and emotions are super important, which is why they can't stand Beasts who "are doing it wrong!". With some justification, see Yuri's Group, also nothing Atavisms & Nightmares can do come close to Passion's Light & You Must Remember This for teaching an effective lesson; and that's before you get to the high level Charms.


                                                I think there is an issue though: Conflict between Cheryl and hunters is too black and white. Either the hunter are fanatics (or Chiron) and want to hurt the innocent monsters she protects, or there is some contrived misunderstanding, or there is no conflict. I won't lie, this is a fundamentally hard character to write, because if the monsters she protects are bad enough to justify players hunting them then she comes across as a bad parody of magical girl, stupid good in TvTropes terms. If the monsters are not bad enough then you hit the problem you have now: A narrow concept that only works when players are intentionally playing fanatics.

                                                My thought would be for the Princess to be trying to save a monster who's harmless now, but with the potential to level a city (pity Deviant isn't out yet). Then you could have a conflict based on "it's too dangerous to leave alive" vs "saving a life is worth a risk" (with the added complications that starting a fight might set the Deviant off so you really want to use non-violent means to get the Princess away before going in for the kill).

                                                            I think it's perfectly reasonable for there to be genuine moral conflict- monsters can be dangerous but still worthy of life. Monsters can be rehabilitated- a beast or vampire can feed with only minor harm and a demon could make fair and equitable deals. Even a spirit could gain essence in helpful, or at least harmless, ways. Do the hunters have the right to just write them off as abominations and shoot them?

                                                            Some monsters are dangerous through no fault of their own- a promethean's disquiet, torment and wasteland are dangerous, but does the promethean deserve to die for them? Possessed, changelings and deviants are ultimately victims in their own right- can we ethically kill them for the crime of being in the wrong place at the wrong time, however dangerous they are now?

                                                            Even on a deeper level, do monsters even have human moral obligations? Can a pain-spirit be blamed for being what it is? Is a vampire eating a human really any more evil then a human eating a cow? Why should we expect a millennia-old mummy to abide by modern western ethics?

                                                            And even if monsters are genuinely evil and destructive, so are many people- do we want death squads going around killing them with no trial or accountability?

                                                            There are more then enough questions in hunting to allow a genuine moral discussion around the ethics of killing genuinely dangerous monsters.
