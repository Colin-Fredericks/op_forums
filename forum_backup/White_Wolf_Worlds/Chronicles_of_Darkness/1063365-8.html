                            Originally posted by Mrmdubois View Post
                            Here's another thing about mysteries, typically people (Mages included) don't bother to look beyond the most obvious answer. We like to apply Occam's Razor. Adding even one layer of complexity to a mystery can be enough to throw off an investigation. This happens with regrettable frequency in law enforcement. For instance there was a case of a professor at a college who was murdered in her classroom in a brief period between classes. A homeless man who liked to hang out on campus was convicted for the crime due to enough circumstantial evidence. Decades later video footage came to light -that the prosecution had- of him sleeping on a bench that was time stamped for the time during which the murder had to have occurred. The prosecution had this conflicting piece of evidence, but since it didn't fit their preconception of the case or some of the other evidence they pushed for and got the conviction of an innocent man. There's probably some systemic problems going on there, but it's unlikely the prosecution intended to convict an innocent man, they just decided to ignore the video evidence that conflicted with their preconceptions. Mages are powerful, but just as prone to these kinds of human mistakes. Now, in a game, eventually, all is supposed to come to light, but a smart ST can toy with their players' preconceptions and mislead them quite easily for as long as the story requires without the need for "Your powers don't work here." or other gimmicks. (Though a good gimmick can also be fun once in a while!)
                            Would you mind giving an example for this? Because while the example of the murder with the video is realistic it wouldnt fly with players. Players see such a absolute evidence and they immediately fly into "he is innocent". While real people in the real world can and do ignore the absolute truth in favor of their pre-conconceptions, a player that shoot themselves on the leg to that level is rare and not a measuring stick by any means.

                            As for this thread original question, lower case mysteries arent a story you can really tell in mage unless either

                            A) You go full plot convenience as Ashenrogue went and put high concept things like Irises.

                            B) Nerf mages.

                            And to be fair thats something that happens in many games, some splat you make difficult of impossible to tell certain kinds of stories with a certain focus.
