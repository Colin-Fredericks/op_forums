    [Story] As Rain Falls the Flame

    01-25-2016, 06:13 AM
    "Twice or thrice had I loved thee,
    Before I knew thy face and name;
    So in a voice, so in a shapeless flame,
    Angels affect us oft, and worshipped be."
    —John Donne, Songs and Sonnets






    Kara found the library a strangely comforting place. After a semester of studying abroad, it was nice to have some peace and quiet again. This particular library had a sort of cold, calm feeling, conducive to studying or even catching a well-earned nap. Over the past weeks, Kara had been coming more frequently, pulling late-night study sessions or researching well into the early hours. That was another thing she liked: the library was open basically 24 hours. There was always that librarian, too, she seemed to work all sorts of shifts and never seemed to have a day off. At least, any time Kara came in, she saw her. Soon she began to wonder.

    One night Kara approached the librarian, after all the other patrons had gone. "Don't you ever sleep? It seems like you work here day and night."

    The librarian gave a surprised look. The girl herself was surprising: she was not a stuffy old lady, but around Kara's age. A college student, by the looks of her. Big, deep blue eyes behind smart oval-rimmed glasses, her dark red hair pulled back in a neat ponytail that left the bangs parted to frame her angular features. She was tall and slender, but graceful as she moved, each step as light as a page turning. Kara couldn't quite figure her out.

    "Sleep? Yes, of course I must sleep."

    You'd think she'd never been asked that before. What's with her? "Well, duh, Ms. Sarcastic. I don't mean literally. But you seem to work here a lot. Do you know the owners or something?"

    "No, I simply enjoy my work. At night I wander the aisles and read through random books. Fascinating, aren't they?"

    "Books? Well yeah, but I mean, you must spend your whole life around them. What do you do in your off-time?"

    The librarian blinked. She didn't even have a name tag. "I ... I am a student. I work here to help support the financial burdens of tuition."

    Kara chuckled. "You're so stiff. Why not loosen up a little? So what do you study?"

    "I study ... science. I have chosen biology as my primary academic focus."

    Okay, robot. "So, I take it you do most of your studying here?"

    The librarian's eyes widened a little. It was like she was afraid to be questioned so much, or else just really lacked social skills. Not surprising, if she spends all her time here. Still, poking at her was kind of fun.

    "Yes, that would be the most efficient use of my time," said the librarian. Then she seemed to come out of her shell a little. "But sometimes, I just read for fun. So many stories, so many thoughts, ideas, principles all held in pages. It's like you could lose yourself in them. So much history, or visions of the future ... once I started working here, I'd really never had anything like it."

    Kara nodded. "That makes sense. You sound like you really enjoy it, then. All the adventures and everything?"

    "Absolutely!" The librarian's eyes brightened. "It's like ten thousand lifetimes scattered among these books. I just find that really amazing, the capacity to create like that. And the non-fiction accounts are just as entertaining. The things that people have done really intrigue me."

    "Sure! Oh, I just spent a summer in Europe, believe it or not. Went over there for some of the anthropology studies I'm taking, came back to study archaeology here."

    "Oh, you have been across the sea, then? To other nations ... fascinating! You study human history and culture. In a way, I share that passion, as I explore these shelves. Is that not true?"

    Kara laughed and held up her hands. "Relax! I'm not grilling you here. We definitely share an interest in people." She held out her hand. "I'm Kara, by the way. Kara Bailey."

    The librarian studied the proffered hand for a moment before taking it, as if deciding what to do. The handshake was short, perfunctory, and left Kara thinking that the woman's hand was too cold.

    "I am ... Jessica Redfield. Yes, that's me." She nodded.

    "Did you forget? You hesitated there." Kara just grinned again. "Relax, Jessica. Is it okay if I call you 'Jess?'"

    A pause. Then: "Yes. I like that. Jess."

    "Okay, Jess. I think you've been cooped up in here too long. Maybe sometime you should get out, live a little."

    Jessica nodded slowly, a strange look crossing her features. "Yes, that is what I dream, really. I want to go out there and make some stories of my own."

    "I've gotta run, but hit me up if you want to catch a movie sometime. Maybe get a beer."

    Jessica nodded. "Goodbye, Kara."
    * * *


    Kara became a regular fixture in the library over the next few weeks. "Jessica" studied her with growing curiosity. She was ... amazing. So vibrant, so full of life as she talked about her travels, her dreams, the very things that made up the stories the angel so loved. In time she realized that she had begun to love Kara like she did her stories, and that thought troubled her. She often thought about her long dark hair, her deep brown eyes, the floral scent of her perfume.

    Guard the library. See that no harm befalls its contents until the appointed hour. That was the angel's mission. Use only what Cover you need. Deliver the selected pages at the appointed time. Her orders were straightforward enough. An email from an untraceable address arrived every week or two, outlining a page, or sometimes only a section of the page. As it had tonight.

    The page was from an old collection of ghost stories, all allegedly true. It detailed the account of a lighthouse keeper.

    6/14/1904
    They appear just out at the edges of the fog. First one, then four, then eight. Never more than eight. 12:01 AM. Every fourth night. All wearing white rags. I can tell they're looking at me but I can't see any eyes. No faces. Don't use a light. Their faces are dark holes. Don't use a light. Never again.

    8/3
    They're all I dream about now. Getting closer. A step closer every night. Void where the faces should be, sucking in all light. I can hear them now. Voices sound like the ocean. Can't understand their words. They keep getting closer but no one believes me. Tomorrow night I'm going to try shining the big light on them. Can't dream about them anymore. I feel like my face is getting cold.

    8/4
    Light went out. Can't feel my face. Voices gargling. Sounds like drowning. Can't breathe. Feels like water in my lungs. Lantern my only light. Can't get out.

    They're on the stairs.

    8/5
    only one way down
    god help me

    "Jessica" took the page down into the basement. Three steps forward into the cold storage room, turn left and thirteen more. She came upon a door no one else knew about. The steady thrum of machinery emanated through the floor. Exactly 12:01 AM she opened the door and stepped inside. Sound rushed to meet her, an industrial cacophony beaten by pistons and the slow grind of massive cogs. That sound used to comfort the angel, but now "Jessica" felt ... odd. In the center of the room stood the pedestal, a rusty shaft squealing as it slowly turned, topped by a broad gear. She set the page upon the gear and stepped back.

    Immediately a small hatch on the far wall opened. Out floated a small oculus attached to a delicate mechanical claw. It hovered over the paper and scanned its contents, as it always did, then gently picked up the page and disappeared into the hatch. "Jessica" knew it traveled by an arcane labyrinth of tunnels, vents, and shafts back to whatever facility the God-Machine had prepared. Her part complete, "Jessica" returned to the library.

    The early hours passed all but unnoticed. As she had so many nights, the angel spent hours wandering the aisles, picking out books almost at random from the shelves and devouring their contents. She didn't even notice that the library had opened for the day until a familiar voice jarred her from her reverie.

    "Doyle, huh? Kind of an interesting take on a 'lost world,' isn't it? I always liked that one, myself."

    "Jessica" turned to find Kara smiling at her. She closed the book in her hands, Sir Arthur Conan Doyle's The Lost World. "Yes, it's so fascinating. A realm no one knew existed until they found it. A place untouched by ... machines. Or the rest of the world. Full of impossible creatures!"

    Kara grinned. "You bet. I can only imagine what I'd do if I discovered a lost world. I think I'd freak out and squeal like a schoolgirl. What about you, Jess?"

    "Jessica" felt flustered. It was a strange new feeling, a fluttering sensation in her belly. She was just a construct, that shouldn't be possible. The feeling was like anxiety, nervousness, born of conflicting emotions. This wasn't part of her mission and she didn't know how to proceed. "My own world? I don't know. I think I'd just take the opportunity to explore it. No interference. No one telling me what to do. I've never really thought about it until now."

    "Well, maybe you should start, you know? I'll bet you have all kinds of interesting ideas."

    "I ... maybe. I guess I've never really had a chance to express them."

    Kara smirked and reached out to touch "Jessica's" arm. "I'm sure, what with spending all your time cooped up in here. Why don't you come out, have a bit of fun? You can tell me some of your ideas."

    "Oh." Jessica's heart sank. "I have to work late tonight."

    Kara shrugged, obviously disappointed. "I understand, but you know, if not for me, do it for yourself sometime."

    Jessica felt a stab of some new feeling, cold and sickly and deeply unsettling. Guilt? "I ... will. Soon. I promise. When I don't have to work anymore."

    "Okay. Well, I got the one I needed, if you want to check me out." Kara flashed her a wink and a sly grin.

    "Check you out? Oh, the book. Oh." Jessica almost sighed in relief. "Yes, of course. The book. The, ah, book."

    Jessica accompanied Kara back to the front desk. After scanning through the book, Jess smiled politely. "Enjoy your studies. Have a good night, Kara."

    "Oh, this one's purely for pleasure. I figured it was time I gave The Lost World another read-through." She took the book and gave Jess a parting smile. "Good luck on your shift tonight. Don't go too wild now!"
    * * *


    For the first time, Jessica felt lost. She grew increasingly uneasy as the night wore on. A sense of foreboding settled heavily over the library. She closed and locked the doors earlier than usual. Some grave Sword of Damocles loomed over the library, she could feel it.

    No email came until late that night. Precisely at 12:01 AM it arrived in her inbox, sent from an anonymous address.

    To: [email protected]
    From: 000101010111011010101011010101101011...onefilms.co m
    Subject: Your recent transfer request

    Your term at the local facility is complete. Please report to offices of Human Resources for reassignment. Deletion of all accounts and addresses to follow.

    Jessica reeled. It felt like the wind had been knocked out of her. The mission was complete. "Jessica Redfield" would cease to exist. She'd be rendered down into basal components for the God-Machine to later use. She was just a construct, soulless, built for a single purpose. Already she had stepped outside the bounds of her original mission. The Machine would deem her faulty, ensure that all data was scrubbed from her component parts.

    The thought made her sick—and that feeling only made her spiral further into shock and disbelief. How can I despair? How am I ... I'm not just an angel. I'm Jess. That's what she called me. Thinking to herself, for herself, it was as frightening as the realization: the library was going to be destroyed. As Infrastructure it had served its purpose, and now the God-Machine needed to eliminate it. It was possible, if unlikely, that a dedicated investigator might notice a pattern to the missing pages and pose a threat to the God-Machine's plans.

    The email browser closed on its own and the computer shut down entirely. It was but a prelude. What would come? Cultists, perhaps, or even a Destroyer. Almost certainly to burn the place down—as long as the books burned, even if the structure survived, the evidence was erased. "Thy will be done," she whispered, and the words surprised her.

    A loud knock at the front door startled her. Have they come so soon? She hurried to the double doors, only to find Kara waiting. Kara waved as she spotted Jessica through the glass and held up a brown paper bag. Alarmed, Jessica hastily unlocked the door and ushered her inside. Jessica followed her to one of the tables, where Kara set down her stuff. "What are you doing here, Kara?"

    "I brought wine. Just the cheap stuff, but I figured if you were going to be stuck here so late, you could use a glass and some company. Since you seem incapable of taking a hint." Kara's pleased smirk turned to concern. "Hey, are you okay? You look like you've been crying."

    Jessica blinked and tears stung her eyes. She hadn't even realized she was crying. Or that she could cry. "Yeah, I'm ... it's just a really bad time right now. You shouldn't be here."

    Kara approached, eyes full of concern. "What is it? What's wrong? You can tell me. Did someone threaten you?"

    "Yes. No. I mean, it's complicated. And it's just a very bad time to be—"

    Jessica froze and her eyes went wide, staring into space. Her glasses clattered to the floor. Images and sounds flashed into her head: A wiry man in a ratty, sleeveless sweatshirt, hood drawn over his crooked face. His arms had more burn scars than unmarred skin. He had a worn backpack slung over one shoulder and a lit cigarette clenched between yellowed teeth. She also saw something greater, superimposed above and behind him, a dark and towering figure with a single, pale glowing eye. Silver blades gleamed in the moonlight.

    "—ica! Jess! Hey, are you okay?"

    Kara's warm hands fell on Jessica's shoulders. The angel blinked, awareness slowly creeping back into her eyes. Revelation followed awareness: she was no longer Harahel. Now she didn't know who or what she was. "I'm ... I'm not okay, Kara! You can't be here. You're in danger."

    "What do you mean?" said Kara, her voice rising in alarm. "Someone did threaten you, didn't they? Let's go. I'm calling the police."

    Jessica protested but Kara already had her smartphone out. Strange, warbling static covered the screen. "What the hell? I just bought this thing! It should be wor—"

    "Kara, it's not your phone. There's something out there, coming for us. It's scrambling your signal."

    "What? What do you mean, 'something?' What's going on?"

    Jessica suddenly grabbed her by the arm and pulled her close. "Listen. This place is going to burn. You must leave, now. I'll let you out through the back. You're in mortal danger if you stay here."

    "Come on, Jess, it can't be that bad, can it?" She searched Jessica's troubled blue eyes. "Can it?"

    "You have to go. Now. I can't lose you, too. You have too many stories left to tell. I won't let them erase you, too."

    "Erase me ... what the hell are you talking about?" Kara tugged impatiently at Jessica's arm. "Come on, we have to get out of here, get police. Whatever's happening, I'll help you get through it."

    A crash came from the front door, followed by a crackling whoosh. Flame engulfed the entire doorway.

    "Holy shit!" Kara cried. "What the fuck is happening, Jess? Who would want to do this to you, of all people?"

    Jessica's expression turned stone-cold and determined. "I'm not 'people.' But they're going to try to delete you now, too."

    "Why? Why would they do this? Why, Jess?"

    "Come on."

    Jessica gripped her arm with incredible strength and all but drug her through the library, toward the rear fire exit. Shelves rushed past as Kara tried to gather her wits. All she knew was that she was very scared, for Jessica and herself.

    "Damn!" Jessica felt the heat outside the door before they reached it. Fire wouldn't bother her much, but Kara was a different story. More importantly, the arsonist out there had help. Something else was lending him speed, fanning the flames into a furious blaze.

    The angel—no, not an angel anymore, I don't know what I am—hesitated. If she pressed the issue she might be able to escape with Kara, but if the Destroyer was waiting for them out there....

    "Jess, what do we do? Is there somewhere we can hide? Or another exit?"

    Jessica shook her head. "Any other exits will be burning, too. He has help. Something much worse than fire."

    "How? What, like a weapon or something? A gun?"

    "A weapon? Yes, something like that. It's older than your civilization. It's destroyed more lives than there are books on these shelves."

    "What the hell? It sounds like you mean some kind of monster."

    "Yes." Jessica nodded, her tone growing colder, more distant. "A monster. An angel. It's been sent to kill me and see this place is destroyed."

    Kara shook her head, hands trembling as she held them up. "Jess, what you're saying is crazy! I mean, even if that was true, why would someone burn this place?"

    "Secrets. Knowledge. The most dangerous weapon of all." She turned at last to Kara and her eyes welled with tears. "I'm sorry, Kara. Truly sorry. I should have broken contact. I should have made you stay away. But you fascinated me. Your warmth, your spirit, everything about you made me question myself. And now it's doomed me, but I'll face damnation before I let it doom you, too. When we get outside, you need to run. Understand? Run and don't look back. Forget me, forget this place, get as far away from this as you can. Promise me that."

    "Jess, I can't just—"

    "Promise." But Jessica gave her no more time to protest. Instead she scooped Kara lightly up in her arms and threw her shoulder into the door in the same motion. It crumpled and flew off the hinges. They leaped through fire and into a rush of chill night air. In the scraggly parking lot behind the library, Jessica set Kara on her feet again. "Now go. Hurry!"

    A man's mocking voice interrupted her. "Hey now, hey now," he said with a bit of a drawl, his voice dripping with smug amusement. "Don't y'all go runnin' off just like that. You're gonna miss the party." He gestured toward the flames now hungrily swallowing the doorway. No fire alarm rang out, also scrambled by the Destroyer angel's power.

    "You ain't got nowhere to be right now, do ya? Mean, we got all this soul-searchin' to do over the fire, right? It's always so pure, ain't it? So perfect."

    Jessica stepped between Kara and the cultist. She recognized him immediately, the symbols tattooed in his craggy flesh and the rare patches of unburnt skin. Wings woven about gears, eyes atop geometric shapes, words written in a language only servants of the Machine could read. Recruited out of prison, or a psych ward, given a cause to believe in, offering up sacrifices as fuel for worship of the God-Machine. He was dangerous, because he had no reservations. To him, the pyre was everything.

    The ugly bastard smiled, and Jessica saw that his teeth had been torn out, probably by hand, some replaced with rusted drill bits. He spread his cracked lips in a hideous, barking laugh. "I got all night, ladies." He held a Molotov in one hand; the bottles clinking in his backpack suggested several more in wait. In the other he flicked open a Zippo. "Gon' be a real hot date, idn'it?" He cackled hoarsely.

    Jessica backed away, arms held out behind her to shield Kara. She wasn't afraid of the mortal man, or even of his fire. She feared what came next.

    "Oh, don't worry, darlin', this one ain't for ya. It's for me. I'm finally gon' get to go home. My faith is bein' rewarded." Then the man threw his Molotov down at his own feet. It broke with a crash and flames engulfed him, spreading out to meet those lapping at the library doors.

    "Oh my God!" cried Kara, horrified.

    Jessica shook her head. "No. Mine."

    The arsonist's flesh began to bubble and blacken, but he did not scream. He laughed, each heaving breath becoming more like the hiss of an open flame. Black iron began to rip through his skin, his features running together like melting tallow. Flame burned red-hot within the skin that remained, sizzling flesh shrinking before growing iron plates. His face contorted into a dark furnace grill, tongues of flame lashing out from within, a single malevolent eye burning above it. The Destroyer.

    Jessica turned and shot a look of sorrow at Kara, who could only gape at the monstrous visage before her. She met Jessica's eyes and pleaded for an escape. "Please, Jess, that thing will kill you, kill us both! Let's go!"

    "No, Kara. You must run. We can't escape it on our own. I will buy you the time. It's the last thing I can do."

    "No!"

    "Kara ... I am deeply sorry for what you have to see."

    A flash like lightning blinded Kara for a moment. When it passed she saw fire erupting about Jessica, whose skin paled and grew translucent. She stood taller than before, buoyed by the superheated winds. Bluish flame enveloped her body, and great razor-edged wings emerged from her back. Jessica's face transformed into a porcelain mask with eyes like twin blue stars and perfect, unmoving lips.

    Kara stumbled back and fell, crawling away with maddened eyes. She thought she was crazy, she thought she looked upon a monster, and in both ways she was right. Jessica was gone. Now it was Harahel the angel in her final moments. Kara got up and ran, screaming for help. "Monsters! Help! Help! Someone please help me!"

    Harahel turned to the Destroyer. "Hatzatiel." Her voice echoed near and far as though spoken on the winds from every direction at once.

    "She burns!" he roared with the voice of a bonfire.

    Harahel advanced, her own flame flourishing. Lightning snapped and sizzled in her gaze. "Her story does not end here today ... only your part in it."

    The Destroyer surged forth with thunderous steps. Harahel threw herself into him, plasma drives mounted between her wing-units flaring. She pushed back against the giant of malice and wrought-iron but could only slow his advance. Clanking and smoking he battered her away. She clung to him as he charged through, swooping and circling about him, lashing out with plasma-tipped claws. His armor held. He could not lay his rending gauntlets upon her but she could not stop his charge.

    At last she gave up, knowing her only hope of saving Kara lied in swift action. Her drives whirred as energy gathered within—

    —and Hatzatiel belched forth a torrent of magmatic rage. It swept over Harahel and she tumbled helpless in its clutches. She had so focused on protecting Kara that she had no time to defend herself. Harahel lay in a sizzling pool of melted asphalt and liquid fire, her plasma drives damaged, wings and mask scorched black. The Destroyer lumbered off, closing distance on Kara.

    The mortal ran as fast as her long legs could carry her. She looked back over her shoulder and ran even faster. The giant metal-thing was gaining on her. Kara knew she had no chance. If she could get to help, get to a busy street, find a cop shop, anything. The streets were empty, a seemingly endless corridor of shadow and distant, faint streetlights.

    Clang clang clang clang CLANG CLANG CLANG—

    A wave of pure cold washed over Kara from behind. She fell and rolled, her whole body going senseless at once. Vaguely she was aware that she was covered in fire, that it wasn't cold but catastrophic nerve destruction. Her eyes boiled and she saw no more. A strange sort of calm and acceptance came over her as her body burned, a distance from such physical worries.

    Hatzatiel stood impassively watching the woman die. She had been unfortunate; Harahel's attacks had damaged him enough that his first breath hadn't incinerated her outright. The fire roiled within his cavernous furnace-chest. He would burn her away so completely no ash nor ghost nor single cell remained.

    A mechanical whine filled the air, like a jet screaming through the clouds. Harahel soared past, claws ripping a blue plume through the air behind her—and right through Hatzatiel's head. Metal groaned and shrieked as he toppled, his head shattered. It was only a minor injury to an angel of his station, but it might slow him just enough.

    The wounded Harahel swept up Kara's charred form and blasted into the sky. Her wings began to come apart, rivet by rivet, feather-beams falling away. Sputtering plasma drives began to fail but she drove on, determined to save Kara if it took every last power cell she had in her.

    At last her systems failed and she fluttered down some distance outside the city. They landed on a lonely stretch of darkened highway, now lit by the pulses of the angel's damaged systems. She set down Kara and stepped back, devastated to see her smoldering body. Kara was barely recognizable as human. Her lungs had shriveled, her flesh blackened and split. What spark of her soul remained within her dying body was quickly fading.

    Harahel refused. "Kara will NOT die for me, or the God-Machine. I deny you." She poured her own flame forth, blue like a ghostly St. Elmo's Fire, not to sear and devour but to heal. Her own fire guttered as she burned the last of her energy reserves to repair the damage. At last she fell away, spent. Kara's body was restored. Whether she yet lived Harahel did not know. She feared that she had been too late.

    But Kara stirred. She refused to die as much as the angel wouldn't let her. Kara started awake, stunned to feel anything at all, her skin smooth and fair as it ever was. Then she saw the crumpled angel and scrambled away with a gasp. "You! I don't know what ... it wasn't just a dream, then. Either I really am going crazy or you're a fucking monster!"

    "Karaaaaa...," Harahel sputtered weakly.

    "What are you? I mean, were you that thing the whole time? No wonder you didn't sleep! What were you doing there, studying us? Trying to replace us like some fucking pod person? Tell me!" Kara screamed, her voice raw with pain and fear.

    Harahel rose to her knees. She looked down at her hands, delicate silver fingers ending in perfect claws. Segmented joints, silvery limbs clad in sleek white plates. Her face was smooth and shaped, a mask, not real, unfeeling. She hated it in that moment. She hated everything about herself. In Kara's eyes she saw her own reflection, and echoed Kara's fear with her own self-loathing. "I am sorry."

    With that, the broken angel reached up to grasp her left wing over the shoulder. She dug in her claws and pulled until the metal groaned and nerve-wires snapped sibilantly. The angel howled. Her plasma-claws flared and tore into the wing's housing. She ripped it free. White-hot blood spattered the road. Harahel tossed away the ruined wing. She reached up to seize the other and tear it loose. Her ring-like plasma drives fell in pieces. Bit by bit Harahel—not Harahel—clawed away her bio-mechanical parts.

    Kara watched in horrified awe. She reached out and then pulled back her hand, moved to pity as she saw the pitiful creature destroying its own body. Her own body. It's her. I mean, it was. Maybe she still is.

    Harahel screamed in such anguish the sky broke with rain. She hooked her frayed fingers, now free of claws, around the edges of her mask. Thrashing in pure agony she cracked the mask, then began to pull it free.

    "No!" Kara cried, "You'll die!" She moved, but it was too late.

    The mask fell and shattered upon the scored and pitted asphalt. Harahel's scream turned to a static shriek, slowly deepening again into a human-like cry. She bent double, limned in crackling electric blue flame. It erupted from her, spread like fiery wings behind her, shot through with lightning-feathers. Thousands of tiny white star-sparks streamed through the blaze. Harahel stood slowly. Her hair rose and drifted as if on an ocean current, a deeper hue. Rents in her skin sealed to smooth, lean perfection, pale and lightly tinged blue, occasionally lit from within as lightning danced through her veins. She hovered above the ground like a storm cloud, a nebula, a being of purest angelic fire.

    Kara stared, entranced by the terrible beauty of the creature before her. "Are you an angel?" she asked, her voice but a whisper.

    Harahel faced Kara once more. Her eyes now blazed with the same electric blue light. "I was." At that her light seemed to go out and she shrank, clothes appearing seemingly from nothing to cover her nude body. She was Jessica the librarian again, just as the very first time Kara had seen her. Jessica fell as though fainting, but Kara was quick enough to catch her. She knelt down in the rain, cradling Jessica's head in her lap.

    "I was an angel. Now that story is ended. I betrayed my mission. They were going to delete you and the library. They would erase me. I made my choice."

    Kara's tears streamed down her cheeks, splashing upon Jessica alongside the falling rain. "What did you choose?"

    Jessica reached up and brushed her fingers over Kara's cheek. She smiled weakly.

    "To write a new story."
