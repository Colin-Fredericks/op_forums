    How do you protect Status Quo for the world?

    06-17-2017, 04:34 PM
    When given godlike power, many players will presumably want to change the mundane world in a way they think is for the better, and quite possibly derail whatever campaign you had in mind for them. For example assassinating world leaders, eradicate dangerous diseases, bring back Firefly, take control of entire countries, destroy Apple.

    Only having read the core rulebook (1st and 2nd Edition) I cannot recall having actually seen much about this topic. It is assumed that mages per default will keep to their cabals and consilia and pursue mysteries all day.

    I am interested to know how you deal with ​these things, or if they have even turned up at all.

    ​If you have a method or cause for stopping players from world-changing feats, how do you go about it?
    ​Is it because various mage communities will go on a witch-hunt for that audacious mage? Is the God-Machine or the Seers constantly keeping and protecting Status Quo? Will powerful supernatural beings have predicted what the players are about to do and move in early to stop it? Will the very symbols of reality themselves twist the fate of the ambitious mage?
    ​
    Or are things like these happening constantly, and quiessence is keeping us sleepers from sensing that the world is as absurd and twisted as it should be with so many mages and other supernaturals running around?

                                      Originally posted by Aristarkos View Post
                                      When given godlike power, many players will presumably want to change the mundane world in a way they think is for the better, and quite possibly derail whatever campaign you had in mind for them. For example assassinating world leaders, eradicate dangerous diseases, bring back Firefly, take control of entire countries, destroy Apple.
                                      They'd be drastically overestimating their powers. Assassinating people is easy, but unless your country is the size of 'a small neighborhood' or a population of only a few hundred, you would be out of luck. One could potentially eradicate disease but it would require finding every instance of that disease in the world and destroying it, where you fall into the same pitfalls as groups like the WHO do. It's a lot of work and all it takes is one minor outbreak and one trip to make it so you have to start all over again.

                                      ​If you have a method or cause for stopping players from world-changing feats, how do you go about it?
                                      ​Is it because various mage communities will go on a witch-hunt for that audacious mage? Is the God-Machine or the Seers constantly keeping and protecting Status Quo? Will powerful supernatural beings have predicted what the players are about to do and move in early to stop it? Will the very symbols of reality themselves twist the fate of the ambitious mage?
                                      As a ST, if your players want to attempt world-changing feats, they should be encouraged. The game is there for their entertainment as well as your own, and you shouldn't shy away from letting them attempt things like this. It can even have long-lasting repercussions at the table if you play in the same world. If the characters in your Mage game cure AIDS, then in your next game of Vampire AIDS might not exist. But that doesn't mean you should make things easy. Assassinating a world leader would involve various political pitfalls and drawbacks, and then there's the other supernaturals to account for. And considering the length of planning some go into, it's entirely possible that assassination was just part of their plan the whole time. Just like the Lord of the Rings didn't start with Gandalf using Mega-Throw to chuck the ring into the volcano from the Shire, stories your players are in should involve challenges and obstacles.
                                      ​
                                      Or are things like these happening constantly, and quiessence is keeping us sleepers from sensing that the world is as absurd and twisted as it should be with so many mages and other supernaturals running around?
                                      People know about the supernatural, but they don't know the specifics. Even mages don't get the rulebooks, and a lot of them are working off of hope and crossed fingers. But there are magical concerns that affect people aware of magic. It's not just a matter of Quiessence or Lunacy, but these things have an affect on the Integrity of people, and cause the type of Conditions that would make it harder for them to really go out there, be in public and try to get the word out. And even if they did, people know that gazing into the darkness is just a way to get its attention. No one wants to be next.

                                      In Mage, at least, you do have an entire organization dedicated to making the world an oppressive place, and they're not only masters at dealing with the Pentacle, they have the gods of Mageworld telling them how and when to best act. So while some people may be able to make the world a better place, mages are going to face a direct challenge in their peers. And that's just counting the Seers. You also have people like the Banishers and other members of the Pentacle who would gladly kill you for a variety of reasons, even if your intentions were good.
