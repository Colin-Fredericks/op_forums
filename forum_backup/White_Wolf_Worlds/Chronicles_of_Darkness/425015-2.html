    I am running short on time right now so if you don't mind I'm going to quote myself from earlier conversations where applicable and keep it short with original commentary right now. Hopefully that'll help!

    Originally posted by Your question View Post
    How do people handle Demon 'society' in a place? As in, the 4 PCs are a Ring and there are some number (I'm thinking 15-20) other Demons in the same geographical area. How have you organised it? How do they communicate between different groups? DO they communicate? How would a Temporal Agency try to attract other Demons to it to buy Covers? How would different Agendas make contact and draw people in?
    Originally posted by Kirby Jerusalem View Post
    Yeah, my take is that it's a lot like the cell structure of Hunter. You might know a couple of demons outside of your ring in a personal way but mostly Demon "society" works through a list of phone numbers, emails and dead drops. There might be territory or specific things claimed by specific demons, but I imagine it's mostly a matter of hearsay and it's less enforced and more handled as a gentleman's agreement not to mess with each other's stuff.

    In contrast, Agencies are shining beacons of "society", organizations based on encouraging interaction and cooperation between the Unchained. By default in my games, this is seen as something to be wary of, groups that are seen as either naive enough to think everyone's going to get along selflessly or manipulative and ruthless enough to embroil everyone in their schemes. Those perceptions plus the fact that it's relatively easy to track down info on Agencies mean it's usually seen as entirely too open to being suborned by the God-Machine and only a resource you should use when there are no easy alternatives.
    Small note to tack onto this: in general what I'd probably emphasize in this environment of extremely decentralized communication is that unlike other, more political games each Ring is essentially their own greatest authority. You don't have higher-ups or societal duties by default; you might know how to contact a couple of other demons and maybe even have some associates, but your Ring's business is your own and nobody's got the right to contradict you. On the other hand, nobody's going to hold your hand and if you get in trouble there's no obligation to come to your rescue.

    Originally posted by Your question, continued
    If these Demons are all trying to hide themselves and are all so expert at doing so how does anyone even begin finding other Demons to start forming an Agency or working with likeminded Agendas at all?
    In my view, Demon society in general is very much voluntary; you get out what you put into it, even if that's a name and a phone number to reach you at. I imagine Rings pick a spokesman and these spokesmen work to get connected, putting out a title, what the Ring is about, and a way to get in contact. Once trust is established, I figure the most connected Rings set up a system - check in twice a month with updates, and then maybe your fellow Demon will introduce you to his friend when you're in need.

    Of course, once you're -that- connected, you're only a hop, skip and jump away from forming Agencies, and everyone knows how risky THAT is...

    Originally posted by Your question, continued
    Also, how do Demons find out that the GMachine is up to something with Infrastructure. Is it just through noticing strange things in the newspaper, or word of mouth or odd planning permission notices? Or is the aetheric resonance attracting them? Or are they just wandering around and just see the gears somewhere?
    All of the above!

    Originally posted by Your question View Post
    What kind of things do you let slip and when would you press for the Compromise roll? I'm looking for the nuance and why you'd press one but not the other. The obvious stuff like killing someone is already called out in the book.
    Originally posted by Kirby Jerusalem View Post
    The thing to remember about compromise rolls gained by out-of-character behavior is that it’s for when you act grossly out of character. It’s not so much for when you go “huh, I wouldn’t have guessed that” when you find out your boss knows a smattering of French, or when you go “wow, I never would have expected you to do that!” like if the previously fat programmer loses a significant amount of weight. It’s for when your octogenarian pacifist takes down five thugs with moves that would make Jason Bourne blush, or when you find the friendly pastor with a bloody machete and a bunch of chopped up bodies. It’s up to the ST and the player to come to an understanding of course, but in general the guideline I go with is that if it causes a hypothetical bystander to suffer some amount of cognitive dissonance, that’s when I break out the compromise rolls. So with that perspective a stock broker poking around a construction site is weird but it doesn’t make you sit up and go “wait a minute, a stock broker -here-? RED FLAG!” As such, demons can get away with a lot of the basic legwork for their operations before they start risking cover on doing things that they blatantly shouldn’t be doing.

        A Guardian who was sent to protect an up and coming Yakuza thug and reached a point of asking why she should be doing this at all. Concept is strongly focused on being a researcher particularly on theology, philosophy and morality and for what reasons the GMachine does what it does. Obviously an Inquisitor. Cover is an academic at a University in the philosophy and ethics department. She is playing the most 'classically' combat capable member of the group.

        Cover risking specialty: swords (under weaponry)

        Merits: library 1, resources 1, status: university 1, terrible form 4, versatile transformation 1, interdisciplinary specialty: theology 1, professional training: academic 1, fighting finesse 2, light weapons 1 (was given 3 dots for free social merits to provide links to organisations and such in the world)

        Embeds/Exploits: First Strike, authorized, password entropy, Voice of the machine (first key)

        Demonic Form: A classical angelic figure, strongly reminiscent of her form as an Angel, sword, wings, fire, and glory
        Blade hand, fast attack, inhuman reflexes, mantle of fire, glory and terror, wings, voice of the angel (she has terrible form at 4 dots but has not yet chosen the additional form options)

        Cipher Guidance: When discussing this she had a hard time coming to an ultimate idea but everything was very much a case of 'the truth will out' or 'knowing the answer to why'

        For the Cipher I was thinking Voice of the machine > Find the leak > Data retrieval > Like I Built it

        1st interlock would mean that by listening to the GMachine you can find the most vulnerable point in the local infrastructure to find out more information about what is going on

        2nd interlock something like once you find the leak you can scan the GMachine's plans and history with voice of the machine so find out what it was planning to do 3 months ago, a year ago, etc

        3rd I wanted to give a bonus to scanning infrastructure to find out its weak points, what it is for, what each component in the infrastructure does and how to take advantage of it (using the system in the player's guide for investigating infrastructure)

        Am I going in the right direction? How would you guys go about the interlocks mechanically?

        The idea here is to chain going from getting a hint about goings on to finding where to go for more detail, to being able to gather lots of specific detail with the option to use that detail to take control.

            How to stop it being god machine plot of the week?
            Well in my game which has been going on for a few months is just one plot of the god machine. Yes there are others going around that demons can find but the main one is still on going. Half the game is trying to find out what the machine is planning. If it is a good thing for demons or not. Sometimes it will be good for some and not others and demon will fight over it.
            This was my plot if it helps. It is a simple plan. 1. Build infrastructure (and take old ones that was broken by demons) in the fractures and then port them into the real world. 2. Shut down the shards of reality in the process too.

            That was it but my players had to 1st learn of the shards and how they worked. How to get in and out.
            Second is an occult matrix is built from other infrastructure. Basically plans within plans. One of my demons had a drug dealer cover, and he found a new mind controlling drug on the street. It made people aggressive and unreasonable. This was to cause more gang warfare (with the help of cultist and angels rubbing up people the wrong way) - Concealment infurstrtre

            This was to help give momentum so an angel with a cover of a politician trying to get militated police to sort the salutation out (really they were demon hunters who could be free to take out demons in board daylight. They were there to protect the matrix and distract demons from the matrix) - defence infrastructure

            One of the player demons job was to collect probability and his npc wife took fate, this was needed to make the stuff from the shards become more real and be able to exist in the real world. - logistical infrastructure

            There are more infrastructure to make this whole plan work, some that would stop the plan out right and some that would barely slow it down. My players have be investigating this project for a while (in between personal plot and someone trying to create souls) and they are close to knowing what the plot is about. They know the purpose of the drugs and the plan to put demon hunters on the street and that weird things are happening in the shards but not sure what. They have angel jacked the politician’s cover to stop that plan and are going to raid the demon hunter training ground to stop that plan (still not knowing what the god machines plan is)



            The point I'm making, it should not be easy to know what the god machine is planning and it should not always be easy to just break down infrastructure. The infrastructure might not be easy to take down as it is made of people who work at a shop. The demons might have a problem killing inncent people, unaware of what they are doing. Half the battle should be knowing anything, and the way demons are they do not like to share info for free and they can always lie. The second should be who does this affect. Who would help ypu stop it and who would try and stop you (humans, demons, angel or even vampires). Then (unless they have a certain exploit) they then need to find out to take the thing down.

            This shouldnt feel like a plot of the week (hopefully)
