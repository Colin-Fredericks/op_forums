                        Originally posted by Prince of the Night View Post
                        Is it cultural appropriation for an african or asian to recite the lords Prayer?
                        No. As in your case with Shinto, they have their own branches of Christianity to follow thanks to missionaries. Currently they are completely Asian branches, but they're born from native teachings.

                        But in general it is hard to talk about Cultural Appropriation when it relates to following religions (as opposed to just depicting those religions in media). So it isn't CA to follow a religion from another culture or region, but it may be to depict this religion in a fiction book without input from natives.

                            Another important thing to note, regarding say the Lord’s Prayer, is that most forms of Christianity are closed in only the loosest of ways. The stricter sects may require a period of education before the rites of initiation can be performed but plenty of sects consider accepting Jesus of Nazareth as Christ within your heart to be perfectly sufficient to become a Christian.

                            That’s leaving aside the historical power dynamics which come into play with Christianity having been used as a tool of European colonial and imperial power for centuries.

                            Certain practices and faiths are closed and respect for the practitioners and their spiritual truths obligate outsiders to respect that the faith is closed and that it should not be used by outsiders except in whatever ways are specifically allowed by the practitioners. Open practices or practices that are nearly open, such as Christianity or Islam for example - which both have strong proselytizing traditions as well as relatively easy initiation procedures, are going to be more flexible in how outsiders can interact with their practices although I still feel that it’s important to offer respect and dignity to the practitioners and their spiritual mysteries.

                                It's worth considering that cultural appropriation is about cultures, not places (obviously cultures can hold specific values regarding specific locations though).

                                The Lord's Prayer originated in Asia, and spread to Africa as fast as it did to Europe. It's fair to think of it as culturally European due to the influence of Hellenism and the Pax Romana in the parts of Asia and Africa where Christianity originated and spread, but Coptic Christians are one of the oldest known groups of Christians, and most certainly aren't from Europe. And they definitely have their version of the Lord's Prayer.

                                IME, a lot of people from Africa and Asia find it kind of weird that the "African" and "Asian" are used with such unspoken assumptions about what cultures and peoples are being discussed. I know Moroccan, Ethiopian, and South African immigrants to the US, and all of them have struggled with the idea that they're not all Africans in the US; even though Morocco, Ethiopia, and South Africa are all in Africa. Moroccans get treated as Middle Eastern, and the South Africans as European. "African = black," and "Asian = south east Asia," is understandable to an extent as a functional shorthand in many countries, but it's worth thinking about such assumptions when diving deeper into issues like cultural appropriation.
