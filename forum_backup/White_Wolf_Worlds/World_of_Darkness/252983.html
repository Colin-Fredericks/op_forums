    (World-Building Project) The Storyteller Universe: A New WoD/Classic WoD Hybrid

    09-09-2014, 12:56 AM
    Okay guys, I had an idea for a New World of Darkness/Classic World of Darkness Hybrid. I'm posting the project here in the cWoD forum because it will use Classic World of Darkness mechanics as I am more familiar with it, and more of the Classic World of Darkness gamelines will be involved in the story overall.

    Basically, this combines the best of both worlds so to speak. Taking the mystery and lack of metaplot found in New World of Darkness but applied to a large chunk of Classic World of Darkness, while porting over the better aspects of the New World of Darkness into the Classic and vice versa. We will break down the setting game by game, starting with Vampire and moving onto Werewolf and Mage and so on. We will not be using the Translation Guides for Vampire or Werewolf, as they do not work with the approach I am taking to The Storyteller Universe.

    The old metaplot is completely discarded, as are the Gothic-Punk trappings (at least by default, if an individual Storyteller wishes to make their game more Gothic or Punk, then I am perfectly fine with it.)

    Of course, all this is property of CCP and various other companies. I own nothing and make no profit off of this.

    The following games and editions will be mined for material in the game and are canon in some form or another to the Storyteller Universe.
    • Vampire The Masquerade 2nd Edition & 20th Anniversary Edition (V20 mechanically, 2e thematically)
    • Vampire The Requiem 1st Edition (I don't own Blood & Smoke, so I don't know how B&S handles the Covenants)
    • Werewolf The Apocalypse 20th Anniversary Edition
    • Mage The Ascension 2nd Edition (Subject to change when M20 comes out)
    • Wraith The Oblivion 2nd Edition (Ditto)
    • Changeling The Lost (replaces Changeling The Dreaming)
    • Hunter The Vigil & The Hunters Hunted (both volumes)
    • Senshi The Merchandising (Classic WoD Fan Game)
    • Street Fighter: The Storytelling Game (very loosely canon)

    These games are non-canon to the Storyteller Universe and therefore their splats do not exist in it....
    • Kindred of the East
    • Demon The Fallen
    • Hunter The Reckoning
    • Mummy The Resurrection
    • Changeling The Dreaming
    • Orpheus
    • Werewolf The Forsaken
    • Mage The Awakening
    • Geist The Sin Eaters
    • Demon The Descent
    • Mummy The Curse


    Now, as for Promethean, I am unsure on whether or not I should include it in this Storyteller Universe or not, as it would take a lot of mechanical conversion to make it work on the Classic WoD system, and I have enough on my plate with Changeling. Now, we'll start with the overviews and backstories of each gameline, starting with Vampire, which draws from both Masquerade and Requiem....

    The General Overview of Vampire

    Okay, this is the Storyteller Universe's take on everyone's favorite game of personal horror. Only this time, themes of personal horror are explicitly up to ST fiat. Now, thematically, this game is more like the first and second editions of Masquerade and less like Revised, but there's some key differences between this iteration of Vampire and any other edition of Masquerade.

    The biggest one of all is that while the Clans and Disciplines and base mechanics are the same as in V20, the sects of Masquerade have been replaced by the Covenants of Requiem. In other words, the Camarilla, Sabbat, Independents, and Anarchs have been replaced by the Carthians, Circle of the Crone, Invictus, Lancea Sanctum, Ordo Dracul, Unaligned, and the minor covenants Belial's Brood and VII.

    Another change is the backstory. The Caine myth is not objectively true, neither is the Judeo-Christian worldview, it is instead a lot more open to interpretation. The Caine story was the most predominant among Western Kindred for centuries, and the Lancea Sanctum still believes in it to a lesser extent, but it is not objective gospel truth in the setting.

    Since KOTE is removed, East Asia is fully accessible to vampires, although they have their own unique customs and variations on the covenants.

    While the Tremere successfully usurped the Salubri, the Cappadocians are still a major clan. Thematically, it is because the Giovanni's structure was so insular that it could not adapt to the Covenants of Requiem, so instead the Giovanni are an Invictus-aligned Cappadocian bloodline. The other 12 Clans are still the same as they were in Masquerade, but no longer bound to any one sect or covenant.

    I have considered bringing in the High Clans/Low Clans concept from Dark Ages into this, but I am unsure. The only difference I would think is that the Tremere would now be considered a High Clan in the Modern Nights, but were a Low Clan in the Dark Ages.

    I am removing Paths of Enlightenment from this game, and may replace traditional Humanity with the various Roads of Salvation from Dark Ages, as Roads can easily be modernized and I find them more interesting than the modern Paths or Humanity in the modern era.

    The History of the Kindred

    Nobody knows for sure who the first vampire was, other than the fact he existed. This legendary progenitor of all Kindred has gone by many names, whether it be Caine, Mikaboshi, The Crone, Belial, or simply Patient Zero for you more scientifically-oriented Kindred. Whomever he was and however he became a vampire, he sired at least one or more other people, known as the Second Generation, who were presumably destroyed by the Third Generation, occasionally referred to in archaic terms as Antediluvians or Yama Kings. Nobody knows who the Third Generation are or how many are among their ranks. All we know for sure is that they are of immense power, and it is most likely that the founders of the 13 Clans were among their ranks. As for their numbers in the Modern Nights, it has been speculated to be either three, nine, thirteen, twenty-seven, one hundred, or none at all. Not since the Camarilla collapsed have the Third Generation been confirmed to be active, though it is suspected that two powerful Kindred named Mekhet and Lucian are of this generation and still active in the 21st Century.

    Anyway, there's the matter of the Fourth and Fifth Generations, referred to by Western Kindred as Methuselahs and Eastern Kindred as Bodhisattvas, who are thousands of years old, very powerful, and while not the founders of clans or bloodlines, are certainly the ones who helped lay the foundations of our society and the way it exists today. In the West, there was the Camarilla. As the Roman Empire (and before that, the Roman Republic) sought to conquer much of Europe, the Middle East, and North Africa, so too did the Kindred of Rome seek to unify all vampires within its territories under the banner of the Camarilla. However, Kindred society is intrinsically tied to the society and structures of mortals, and when Rome collapsed in the 5th Century CE, so did the Camarilla. Though the Camarilla survived in the Eastern Roman Empire until the early 1400's, it never held the power over all Kindred like it did before.

    There was a similar organization in Asia, based in China, called the Mandate of Heaven, founded around the time of the Zhou Dynasty and achieving much of its zenith in the Han Dynasty, but unlike the Han Dynasty, the Mandate of Heaven continued to maintain a tight grip over Chinese Kindred even after the Han collapsed. This not only extended to the Kindred of China, but even held influence outside of Chinese territory, both in places heavily influenced by China's culture, such as Japan and Korea, and in places unaffected by China, such as India and Southeast Asia. However, when the Ming Dynasty collapsed in 1644, the Mandate of Heaven lost of a lot of power and influence outside of Chinese territory, with the rest of Asia forming their own unique covenants. The Mandate of Heaven, a mere husk of a covenant by the mid 1800's, collapsed entirely in 1913, a few short years after the Qing Dynasty was overthrown.

    The only pan-cultural covenant to not be dissolved entirely was the Ashirra of the Middle East, though a lot of its actual power was lost when Baghdad was sacked by the Mongols in the 1200's, and other covenants rose to challenge the Ashirra. Now, with the Arab Spring of 2011 and the current mortal upheavals in the region, who knows if the Ashirra can still survive in this day and age?

    Now, in the West, three new covenants rose from the ashes of the Camarilla during the chaos of the Early Middle Ages. There was the Invictus, a neo-feudal covenant wishing to continue the basic traditions of the Camarilla, but in a more localized way. Then the Lancea Sanctum, a sort of vampiric equivalent to mortal Christianity and the predominant moral and religious authority among Western vampires arose, and so too did the Circle of the Crone, a loose confederation of Pagan (and later Neo-Pagan) vampire sects that refused to bow down to the authority of the Lancea Sanctum and instead maintain their old traditions.

    In the Renaissance of the 1400's and 1500's, the inklings of a new covenant arose, which would fully galvanize and solidify in the Enlightenment of the 1600's and 1700's. This was a secret mystical society that sought to transcend the vampiric curse through a mix of science, mysticism, and the occult, relying on neither God nor Man to guide them to their goals. Allegedly founded by a powerful Methuselah known as Dracula, the Ordo Dracul rose to the occasion and secured its place as a major covenant among Western Kindred.

    In the late 19th and early 20th Centuries, came the last and youngest covenant, the Carthian Movement. Wishing to eschew the neo-feudal ways of vampire government, the Carthians wanted to apply new mortal political philosophies to undead society. This covenant was most successful in the Americas, but still holds the fewest domains in the world overall.

    And still yet, there are a large number of Kindred who want no part of any of the covenants.


    Anyway, that's what I have for now, I want to develop this setting further and any help would be appreciated. Next, I will post Werewolf and Mage, as well as expand more info on Vampire.

    Is this a good idea? Any ways I can improve it?

      I'm completely serious when I say I mean no offense at all by this. It's probably been said before in harsher tones, but I want you to know I'm not rolling my eyes or making fun of you. Fun games about pretend draculas aren't worth that kind of stress or judgment.

      Do you ever actually get to run these games of your preferred personal World of Darkness? Is writing a "fansplat" / setting and mechanics guidebook kind of thing what you're actually setting out to do? It seems like you make incremental iterations of the same pet project on these forums periodically, without making headway (although I'm not actually certain what you're setting out to do in these threads — crowdsource ideas, I guess, but what kind of ideas?).

      To be more direct about what I mean, what you're doing seems like it must feel really frustrating, and you might benefit from stepping back and looking for some clarity of purpose. Are you more interested in worldbuilding and game-writing, or actually playing in or running this game world?

      If the former, the best advice I can give you is not to keep asking for input but to just to keep writing, because crowdsourcing a big project like this simply never goes well, and the one person you can count on to have the interest and enthusiasm in your project is you.

      If the latter (which is honestly what I suspect would make you happier), I'd just be clearer about looking for a group, online if you don't have anyone in your area who shares your interests, and be open to compromises. I'm certain there's somebody out there who would enjoy playing in a game with a similar tone to your preferences, as long as you're open to working with them rather than sticking to a very specific conception of what you prefer as a whole. A big part of working with a group is being willing to kill your darlings and design to what's best to the individual campaign and the enjoyment of the group.
