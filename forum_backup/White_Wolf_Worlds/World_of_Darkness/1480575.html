    Too much dices

    05-09-2022, 04:54 PM
    This is pure curiosity but i've been thinking about some case where a character, may be he pc or npc, get a lot of die to roll:
    Simple exemple: a vampire with 8 dices to shoot with celerity 6 would roll 14 dices, but in the case of a full auto shoot he would throw 24 dices + the damage roll next to mesure all the damage he had done. A pc could get that level after few scenario and a potential diablery and an npc of the st could be put in the game as antagonist and may have more potent than that.
    In werewolf there's some gift that increase some stats or even double them + the totem bonus, which could get them up than 15 dices to roll for a claws attack.

    In those case where these character are so potent/powerfull as there been any system or rules in actual or previous edition that change the rolling system to roll fewer dices while keeping efficiency? Or maybe an houserule?
    Cause i'm sure some people made stories during the famous apocalypse with the firsts vampires coming back or werewolves facing the evil of malfea and it's hard for me to imagine them throwing multiple times their dice and count avery success and botch. When you have around 10 it's not really anoying but when you have between 15 and 20 or more for the most powerfull characters...

      V5 has a take half option (though doesn't get as high with the numbers for the most part), where you consider the action successful with 1/2 it's rating. This is because on average, 1 die in V5 is worth ~0.5 successes (it's a lot more complicated than that thanks to the way 10s work, and the way Hunger dice might impact things, and we haven't seen a full rule set like H5 for non-vampires).

      This doesn't really work as well for the older editions, because V5 reduced the number of variables making the math for estimating how many successes you average easier to do.

      It also doesn't help that pre-V5 (so far, V5 is just one book and while the plan is to keep things more consistent than the older editions that promise hasn't happened yet even if there's no reason to doubt it), the WoD games don't have a unified rule set, and their are subtle differences between each game that can make these sorts of rules hard to do. In V20, attacking someone in the back gets you +2 dice, while in W20 you get -2 difficulty instead. These have wildly different impacts on anything like what you're looking for.

      One of the big reasons this hasn't happened, is that combat is especially hard to simply with the way all the variables work. It gets close to the point of just being better to adapt the game to a similar but more manageable system that's designed for this (like Storypath or Exalted: Essence, which are both designed to keep the dice-pools smaller despite dealing with potentially very powerful creatures).

                              Edit: with my friends we talked a lot about it and we've decided to use an alternative dice system:
                              When a character, pc or npc make an action and his pool is equal or higher than 16 dices then he's just too powerfull to fail no matter how dificult the action is (he cannot do whatever he want, the st will tell if an action is possible or not). The effect of that success depend on what the character is doing, how, with what, under what circumstances and the ST will describe what happen.
                              Exemple if samuel the vampire with 19 dice attack Joe the hunter which has 10 dice to defend samuel will automatically wins, but the success of joe to defend will define if he die immediatly or is just injured. If Joe has enough success (like 6 or more) and an armor he will survive with 1 or 2 damage, without armor he will survive with 4 or 5 damages.

                              If 2 characters with more than 16 dice fight each other then they roll only 1 die. They add the result to their total of points and the one with the higher value wins:
                              Samuel the vampire face Death-Bringer the werewolf, samuel shoot him whit 18 dice, Death-Bringer dodge with 20. They roll 1 die each, samuel obtain 5, he add this result to his 18 dice and get 22. Death-Bringer obtain 3, he add it to his 20 dice and obtain 23.
                              Death-Bringer dodge the shoot of Samuel.

                              This houserule we made may not fit to all tables, but we're good with it with my friends, we've simply adapted the D&D rolling system to make it fit to ours. We know it's not perfect but we simply couldn't translate the awesome power of some characters like the first vampires and incarnae into game with the basic world of darkness system.
                              Maybe this will help some people to play a campaign during the famous apocalypse or with legendary characters

                                  You could take half of the dice pool, or take half over a threshold (e.g., roll a max of 10 dice; the rest become automatic successes on a 2 dice to 1 success basis, round up for vamps and down for mortals).

                                  Or you could roll any dice over the threshold (so 9 dice if your pool is 19) and just take half for the first 10. That way you always get at least an exceptional success on such actions. That seems appropriate.

                                  Or you could deduct opposing dice pools from the PC's own, and roll what's left.

                                  Or you could roll half the dice required but count each success twice.

                                  Or you could rule that when a dice pool reaches 10+, you gain your Ability score in automatic successes, and halve or roll the rest (this gives a little boost to Abilities). If that's too high, just take the Ability score as auto successes, ignore the Attribute score, and roll any other modifiers as dice.

                                  All of the above solutions only work, though, if each die averages 0.5 successes. RAW, that's at around difficulty 5, since you have weird stuff with 1s and 10s. So I'd recommend tweaking the dice so you have no more variable target numbers (TNs).

                                  If you simplify the dice rules so 1s deduct but 10s always count twice (just add an extra die for a specialty), then you get ~0.5 successes per die with a fixed TN of 6, as the 1s and 10s basically cancel each other out. I prefer this to the V5 system of only counting 10s twice if there are pairs of them, because that method adds squiffiness back into the maths, and this way lets you sometimes get two successes even on one die. Bonus.

                                  Or you can use the Exalted method, which is my personal favourite, with all dice needing a 7+ to succeed and 10s doubling. In this method, 1s do nothing on their own, but they do help you determine botches as usual. This is nice and simple, although some people prefer 6+ instead of 7+ for their TNs (and some people hate 1s subtracting), so it's very much a taste thing. The maths works out about the same, though.

                                  If using a fixed target number for all dice, then difficulty would either become a factor of how many successes you need or adding and subtracting dice.

                                  I would suggest modifying dice, a) because you want to reduce dice pools overall and difficulty increases are more common in RPGs than difficulty decreases, and b) because converting V20 difficulties to a success modifier gets a bit tricky, and I'll show you why.

                                  I would keep the V20 degrees of success to add granularity, and allow for heroic or legendary outcomes. You can beef up the complications on a marginal success if you like, to allow for V5's concept of 'succeed at a cost', which is a nice touch.

                                  That means two successes is a 'moderate' success still, and a person with 'average' Attribute (2) and Ability (2) scores will roll 4 dice and, on average, get a moderate success (average stats should mean average results on average...).

                                  So far, so simple.

                                  Now, the difficulties and modifiers are a bit trickier to convert, as changing the target number in V20 has a variable effect, depending on your dice pool. I'm using this AnyDice program here: http://anydice.com/program/5e8a

                                  You can also use this one to compare results with varying dice pools, but be warned that it deducts 1s automatically and would be a faff to change, so even if using the Exalted method, you will have to put the TN to 6 (and put '1' for specialty so that 10s double): https://anydice.com/program/10f78 (as I said above, TN6 with 10s doubling and 1s deducting works out about the same as TN7 with 10s doubling and 1s being just failures).

                                  You can see that going from difficulty 6 to 7, or 7 to 8 and so on, incurs about a +/-10% change of odds on each die. That seems nice and simple, but it doesn't result in a simple conversion (e.g., where +1 difficulty in V20 equals -1 success or whatever). On a dice pool of 5, you'd lose 0.5 successes, but on a dice pool of 10, you'd lose 1 success.

                                  You could apply V20 difficulties and modifiers as an overall percentage of your dice pool removed or added again, or an overall penalty or bonus to your final successes. But that seems clunky.

                                  It's not as neat to say, 'You have 3 dice, so lose 0.3 successes or 0.6 dice'; or, 'You have 7 dice, so lose 0.7 successes or 1.4 dice.'

                                  So let's get some averages again. The players will be rolling 2-10 dice for most things, we can say the average for them is 6 dice, giving a +/-0.6 success modifier for every +/-1 difficulty modifier in V20. But we can also assume the average stats of an average person would be 2+2=4 dice, in which case each +/-1 difficulty modifier in V20 applies +/-0.4 successes. 0.5 is sat right there in the middle of those two numbers, so I'm going to take it and use that, because it converts nicely. So every +/-1 difficulty modifier in V20 means you apply +/-1 die. So +2 difficulty in V20 is -2 dice.

                                  This also applies to the standard difficulties of 3-9 in the core book, thus:

                                  V20 Difficulty / Simplified Difficulty
                                  Difficulty 3 / +3 dice
                                  Difficulty 4 / +2 dice
                                  Difficulty 5 / +1 dice
                                  Difficulty 6 / +0 dice
                                  Difficulty 7 / -1 dice
                                  Difficulty 8 / -2 dice
                                  Difficulty 9 / -3 dice

                                  Just remember that the modifier is applied relative to the V20 numbers, not the simplified ones. Your new TN may be 7, for example, but a roll listed as difficulty 7 in V20 is still -1 die, because that's relatively more difficult than V20's standard difficulty of 6.

                                  Now, there's one last thing to consider: V20 has dice pool bonuses and penalties on top of difficulty modifiers. E.g., when using auto fire with a firearm. So while it might be an idea to cap modifiers to the difficulty at +/-3 dice (equivalent to V20's hard difficulty caps of 3 and 9), you have to also decide whether to add V20's dice pool bonuses and penalties on top of that.

                                  Letting penalties stack up would reduce the number of dice rolled, but adding bonuses in such a manner makes dice pools even larger.

                                  But, it's not as tricky as at first it seems, because now you've converted the core resolution system to one where every die is worth 0.5 successes.

                                  So, you can cap rolled dice at 10 and everything else gets halved and added as automatic successes. Or use any of the other methods suggested at the start of this post.

                                  In fact, I think most of the issues with the V20 dice pool system can be fixed by using fixed TNs as I've suggested here. The ability to take half whenever you need an average or want to avoid rolling too many dice makes everything so much easier.

                                      Keeping halves dice above 10 as auto success is a good one too, i thought about it first and make few roll with a dice roller.

                                      A've made it a bit complex at first, increasing the amout of dice that count as 1 success depending on the difficulty (diff 7 = 2 dice count as 1 success, diff 8 = 3 dice count as 1 success and so on)
                                      But i went too far and didn't find something that was satisfying. Plus the full auto shoot was not the only one test i made, i created vampire character test that was very low generations and has naturally between 20 and 30 dice to roll in a fight.
                                      After hours i just found that so much power is too hard to mesure with a classic roll, even whith a cap at 10 dice. If the pc or npc roll 30 dice but the difficulty is 9, he will get less than 10 success, and if the difficulty is 10 he may not have any.

                                      So i just changed my vision of it; in v5 i saw the different scene option and decided to just adopt a narrative description of what happen:
                                      If the character is hiding behind a wall the shooter has +3 on difficulty, but if this shooter is a vampire shooting full auto and get to 24 dice the amount of bullets + the skill of the vampire make his shoot deadly.
                                      Narratively i manage it like this: as the character is pretty well hided behind the wall i decrease the effect of the shoot but i don't cancel it. Next i check the protection of the character, he can soak lethal damages or not, he wear a helmet or not. Depedning of those informations i declare that the character lose a certain amount of health, the secondary effects like losing an eye or breaking jaw or nothing if he was strong enough to not be harmed etc...

                                      In second time i can also cap the dice to 10 and give a bigger effect to the action regarding the success, for exemple the nexus crawler roll willpower difficulty 6 and depending on the success his ability to transform the reality is more or less powerfull. Instead of asking a character to roll the same amount of success as his opponent i just define what he do the same way.

                                      The narative way seems better because of the difficulty system, at difficulty 10 i cannot let a pc or npc get 1 success each 2 dice above 10 especialy if he get to 20 or more with a full auto or because of his power.
