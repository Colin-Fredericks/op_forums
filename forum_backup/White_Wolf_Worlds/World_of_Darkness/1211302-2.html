                            Agreed. It was the best when it deviated.

                            That said, there's lots of things I liked about Forsaken. Much of werewolf nature felt far more mythical than Apocalypse. Like the Uratha clearly came off like they were half-spirit beings from the get go, there was less cringey elements involving kinfolk vs wolf-blooded, and some mutual aspects of the game were better spelled out. Even little things like how it feels to learn gifts, and that they become a part of ones nature, like sight or smell, were nice little touches.

                            However, I didn't like how the whole of the spirit world was this vile Darwinistic nightmare. It fit the themes of Forsaken, but I wouldn't lift it whole cloth into Apocalypse. Though I loved the idea of the Uratha tribes having a mythical origin and purpose, they felt really bland, and even unnecessary in comparison to the Garou's (though the Garou tribes often became terribly stereotypical and static). And, at least to me, Forsaken paradoxically lacked the same emphasis on spirituality than Apocalypse. IDK how to describe it, because in many ways Forsaken's spirits came off better as animistic reflections of their physical counterparts. However, unlike Apocalypse, the spirit and the physical worlds didn't feel as necessary to each others existence. It was more like managing an ugly marriage neither side could escape. The nature of Uratha society came off to my group as lacking any personal spirituality too, outside of the Forsaken's relationship to Luna, and even then. Same with the Pure. Their relationship to spirits was more of an alliance of convenience than having reverence that even BSDs have for the Wyrm. Taking into account their relationship to the Shadow, it's not surprising. Uratha are more hunters of monsters like slayers in the Buffyverse. So, perhaps that's the point. I haven't read as many Forsaken books as Apocalypse, but I've heard the Uratha get better fleshed out in the supplements.

                            As for the environmental aspects of Apocalypse, I did enjoy that too. The Garou were dealing with something real and not just evil cultists summoning demons for the sake of EVUL. However, especially in the early books, it came off as really hammy at times, and far too reflective of the industrial age and onward. The Wyrm, and the Triat as a whole, needed a lot more nuance.

                            Moving on to Mage's decedent, I've only started reading Awakening in the last year or so. That whole Atlantis part of their cosmology really alienated me when I first heard about it. It does lack the scope and depth of Ascension, but there's still some really cool stuff on its pages.
