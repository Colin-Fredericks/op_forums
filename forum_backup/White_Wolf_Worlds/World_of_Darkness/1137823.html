                                                  Not load your ancient grimoires onto an electronic device at all, probably. There are several steps it can go horribly wrong.

                                                  Step 1. Own the grimoire. Already, you're potentially in deep shit depending on what it is.

                                                  Step 2. Digitize the grimoire. Either you're scanning it or typing it out by hand. Either one, depending on what exactly you are and what exactly you're using, might get picked up by all kinds of greeblies and goblins out there, from a keylogging russian scammer to a Pentex bane to a curious Glass Walker who gets a sudden twitching urge to poke his head in when he smells Digital Magic going on.

                                                  Step 3. Encrypt the grimoire. This is where shit gets weird, because either you're using a conventional encryption - in which case, magic might snap it like a twig - or magic. If you're using magic, your run of the mill ipad probably doesn't come with Bloodcrypt+ pre-installed, so unless you have the nous to do it yourself, someone is going to know - and someone is going to have leverage over you forever. Worse yet, it might be more than one someone, since you're either using the kind of jailbroken extra functionality someone-in-the-know has to provide, or a device custom built using the kind of Special Components that probably get you added to a watchlist somewhere deep in the NWO, Pentex, or Glass Walkers computers. Either way, you're potentially fucked.

                                                  Step 4. Cart around a sacred, rare, and/or literally magical text in your phone/ipad/laptop to read in public. Why? What advantage does it actually offer? Does that advantage offset the part where you're potentially carrying around a massive magnet for trouble from all kinds of sectors? If it has actual magic in it, then other vampires/garou/banes/mages/whatever have a strong reason to want to gank you for it. If it doesn't, then it's still potentially a walking secrecy breach for something that conveys basically no value to having on you in public.

                                                  99% of the time, there will be exactly no advantage to a digitized grimoire over a non-digitized one. Need it to do magic? Unless you find yourself in the very strange scenario of needing to consult grimoires quickly in the field all the time, you can probably just take the actual book when you need it. The situations where there is an advantage, a temporary copy is probably a better bet - e.g. showing proof you have those missing pages from the Book of Nod to a buyer without taking the actual thing. Unless security through obscurity is your bet, and you're keeping it on just one old hard drive among dozens lying around in the storage unit untouched for years at a time under a fake name, it's not going to be much use.

                                                  Bottom line? If you have to worry about someone finding your actual Sinister Book, you've probably got way bigger problems in your unlife than how to digitize it. Like 'how did they get into my haven' and 'why aren't I dead yet?' The dangers are far less if a mortal finds it - 'huh, weird book' describes half my occult library - than another mage, cainite, or garou does.
