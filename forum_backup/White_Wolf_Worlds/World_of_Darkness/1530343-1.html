                Everyone portrays the traditions differently. As a general rule, I believe that both organizations have good intentions but are deeply flawed in their execution due to arrogance and pride, just like the very mages that form them. Good or bad is something that, to me, applies only on an individual level for each mage. The organizations as a whole try to do good, but more often than not get mixed results and always cause some harm, either intended or unintended.
                For something more specific, I represent the traditions as having a deep rift between the younger mages on Earth and the out-of-touch elders in the horizon realms. The avatar storm alleviated the problem a bit (by basically killing or making most elders disappear), but it still persists, and it's reaching a breaking point.
                Most young mages reject the violent conflict or "crusader" mentality against the technocracy. There's no denying the horrible things they have done and they must be opposed when they do wrong, but there's also no denying the good they have brought. Also, let's be honest, they may not have won the ascension war, yet, but they are the ones on the top right now. Just how realistic it is to confront them directly?
                As such, most younger mages see the technocratic paradigm not as something to destroy, but as something to make it their own and integrate into their paradigms. Their ultimate objective is to stretch it and make it more flexible, creating a world of wonder and possibility. The old guard of the traditions are mostly stuck in their old ways and don't like this attitude at all, their answer ranges from scolding to outright declaring many of these mages traitors.

                    To be fair, the official stance of Lore of the Traditions is that the Traditions had problems in the past, but have seen the errors of their ways and are each undergoing a reformation that returns them to the role of “default good guys”.

                    It's not how I do things in my Mage setting, because it involves a lot of whitewashing of the more problematic elements of each Tradition. My go-to example of this is how the writer of the Verbena chapter decided to base her take on the Verbena on real-world Wicca, but deviated from that on the trans issue: “So, there is some very weird transphobia that pops up in witchcraft spaces, and I really wanted to push back on that.”4:54. So the Verbena are based on real-world witchcraft, except when that clashes with trans rights. Similar issues crop up in every Tradition, with the book deviating from the real-world inspirations for each of them to provide a more rosy picture. In general, the “Rose-colored Glasses” Flaw isn't specific to the Technocracy; but it's not something that the writers should succumb to.

                    And again, the issue of whether they're good, bad, or hypocritical isn't determined by how well the Traditions get along with each other; it's determined by matters such as whether witches are transphobic — issues internal to each Tradition.

                    My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                            Again though, you're coming from the assumption that the Traditions are the “good guys”; and that therefore it makes sense to purge them of the more offensive negative qualities.                                         Originally posted by CT Phipps
                                        A lot of fans see the Ascension War as very much a kind of “these are not related to the current struggles and oppressions of RL, Mages are whining over magic while real people are suffering”
                                        Yes; and others see the division as occurring along other lines. And these are all caps ways to look at it; singling out one (such as the one you single out) as fundamentally being more valid than the others largely undercuts the stated purpose of this topic, as it attempts to shut down our minimize alternate takes.


                                        On the Verbena thing:

                                        In the 80s, there was a big “Dungeons and Dragons” scare, with reports about kids doing insane things after playing the game and intimations that the game was messing with children's minds and making them behave irrationally. That turned out not to be the case; the few instances where kids did behave irrationally after playing D&D, it was later shown that they were already mentally unstable before they ever cracked open a book.

                                        In a similar way, the Swords of Heimdall aren't responsible for turning people into skinheads. The people that you described already were skinheads.

                                        In particular, Mage isn't Werewolf. At its finest, Mage promotes a level of maturity and introspection that Werewolf doesn't. A core theme of Mage is to encourage its players to think for themselves and to take responsibility for their own, personal choices. When you sugarcoat things and try to push a simplistic “us good; them evil” narrative onto the Mage gamers, you undercut that.

                                        And there's evidence that “all of the factions are f'd up; it's up to you to decide for yourself” has been part of Mage from the very beginning: the very first supplement, Book of Chantries, deliberately portrayed every Chantry in the book as screwed up in its own unique way; the Traditions were not spared from this treatment. Brucato is on record stating that that was a deliberate choice, specifically because he didn't want any of the factions to hold a monopoly on ethics. And likewise, from the very beginning he was working to add nuance to the Technocracy so that it wouldn't merely be an oppressive monolith: in Book of Shadows, we were shown how an agent of the New World Order could be a good guy while a Cultist of Ecstasy could be a bad guy, with both of them playing their respective factions more or less straight.

                                                I understand what you mean. However, I think it's all about how you present it. Showing shades of gray is not the same as moral equivalency. It's important to show how flawed each organization is and call it when they do something wrong, even if individual members are good and ethical.

                                                In my games, I always let my players know in unambiguous terms that the technocracy, by their admission, does not believe in democracy. They are an authoritarian organization fully committed to having an elite group rule over the rest. I always point at China to see what the world would become if they could take over, only it would be much more extreme. That the technocracy is changing its ways to a kinder, gentler, and more open-minded organization doesn't change the fact that this belief is deeply ingrained and still present, a good tyrant is still a tyrant. They may have, for the most part, abandoned the colonizer and imperialist mindset of their forefathers, but they have substituted it for a paternalistic and condescending view in which they still believe they have to "guide" sleepers to the correct path. The methodology has changed, but not the main objective.

                                                Regarding the traditions, one thing that the books have pointed out and that I've seen very few people mention (except here) is this: many in the traditions still adhere to outdated and harmful social conventions. It is pretty clear that some traditions have strict social and gender roles as part of their paradigms, their underdog status does not make them immune to some type of bigotry. In fact, it is pointed out right that the technocracy can be much more accepting of transgender and other LGBtQ communities. The traditions are changing their approach, sure, but it is also important to not forget that their name includes tradition in it and there are plenty of mages who take it as gospel not to be questioned or challenged. That's why one of the central conflicts in my games is between the younger mages trying to reconcile their cultural traditions with the modern world and old mages trying to preserve the "purity" of tradition.

                                                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                                        Fantastic post.

                                                        +1 smiley face.

                                                        BUT I feel it could go too far.

                                                        Because, really...it's WRONG to teach that fascists are morally equivalent to the people who oppose them. Not just wrong ethically but just dumb from a philosophical standpoint.
                                                        If the situation is one where one side has gone full fascist and the other has gone full opposition, I agree. That's not Mage though.

                                                        The Technocracy has not cornered the market on fascism, totalitarianism, authoritarianism, or any of the other tyrannical isms. There are quite a few in the Traditions with proclivities in that direction as well. There are also key Traditions characters who are explicitly terrorists. Well intentioned extremism (and also not-so-well intentioned extremism) are common elements of Mage. Nor has any faction completely fallen completely down the fascism hill either, at least not by default (though depending on how far you push the authoritarian Technocracy slider, it may not be very long). It oversimplifies Mage to depict the Technocracy that way, or to lionize the Traditions.

                                                        Also, I think its very important to keep at least some focus on how any mage or faction of mages can fall down that path. Most of what makes the Technocracy bad comes not from what they want to accomplish but how they are willing to do to accomplish it. The Traditions are not insulated from that, and their goal for the world - a place where myriad paradigms can be true at the same time - could be its own brand of dystopian nightmare. A very common question most mage's face sooner or later is: "How far are you willing to go when you know you are right?." Fewer ask the important follow up questions like "How sure are you that you are right?"

                                                        And I feel like the Technocracy can't be divorced from the fact its ideology is what causes wealth inequality, global warming, and so much.
                                                        The metaphysics of Mage do not play well with global warming, to the point that trying to put the blame for it on a faction (any faction) ends up opening a whole bunch of questions that make Mage uncomfortable for some. I've seen threads where this issue spoils the game for people. Same for vaccines. Unsurprisingly, a game about subjective reality breaks down when we look at it closely and this is one of those places.
