                                            There is an actual question of how effective antimatter would be as a weapon (or as a propulsion system) because only electron-positron interactions release pure energy. When you get into the physics, antiproton-proton reactions produce pions and kaons, the pions and kaons decay into muons, and the muons decay into positrons, neutrinos, and electrons (and the positrons and electrons explode when they interact). Since the resulting particles are going at around 99.999% c, they tend to travel very, very far before they decay because of time dilation effects. While pions and kaons only have a halflife of about 12-25 nanoseconds, muons have a halflife of about 2.2 microseconds and, when you calculate the normal time dilation effects, that means that the muons travel for 46.2 microseconds before they experience 2.2 microseconds of decay, meaning that they have traveled nearly 14 kilometers from their point of annihilation (when they explode with a force of over 40,000 metric tons of TNT per gram of antimatter). In effect, you would need kilograms of antimatter before you could do any significant damage to anyone who was at the point of annihilation (though humans would probably suffer lethal levels of radiation from pion and muon interactions).
