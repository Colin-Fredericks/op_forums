    World of Darkness (Hold the Darkness)

    03-14-2018, 12:45 AM
    Am I stupid for thinking that it's possible to play the World of Darkness as less, well... dark?

    Maybe it's just that I'm an ultra-idealistic millennial manchild, but I've always seen the WoD as a great urban fantasy setting that can run the whole spectrum of tones and moods without sacrificing its famous thematic complexity. I enjoy it as a world with dark shadows, yes, but brilliant lights and amazing things as well. I just don't see the fun in making the answer to everything "It's the World of Darkness, so everything's terrible."

    Am I alone in this? Am I profoundly missing the point, or does my interpretation hold some merit?

      Not at all. 2nd Ed started really pushing the everyone is dark when you look close enough angle, but backed off later into Revised because you need contrast or it gets boring.

      20th has sort of backpeddled a bit, but there is nothing wrong with wanting a brighter WoD, and plenty of good stories can be had. Granted, it might not be for everyone, and a lot of people enjoy WoD games specifically because it is darker than most other settings, so do keep that in mind.

      Just change the theme to urban fantasy, make a few setting tweeks, and go. One game I have been tossing around running for a while is a city where Vampires, Mages, Werewolves, Wraiths, etc... have come together as a sort of city council that deals with a variety of supernatural (and mundane) threats, not to mention interpersonal conflicts, with the intention of one day revealing themselves to the world without being hunted or ruining the domain.

      Werewolves are still Garou, with Tribes, spirits, etc.., Vampires are Kindred with ancient Noddist lore, etc...
      They just all have been told to play nice, police the superatural up, and don't cause trouble as they want to try to make a better world.

          I mostly run Mage and while there are dark things happening, the overall theme is about overcoming those things and creating a better world, even if only on the scale of a neighborhood. Half the fun is the wonders (and not the item kind) they encounter.

          This is particularly true if you run with the more nuanced Technocrats of the Revised Convention Books who aren’t monolithic crushers of the human spirit, but fellow mages who made a different call than the Traditions on where they fall on the whole Liberty vs. Security spectrum and that some members of both sides of the old Ascension War are crappy human beings, a few are bonifide heroes and most are somewhere in between.

          Also interesting in this regard is the notion in Mage20 that the whole “end of the world” and its related garbage came and went. The world is still here spinning away and reports that magic was on the verge of death turned out to be greatly exaggerated. That’s a massive “hope spot” all on its own; kinda like going from the darkest days of the Battle of Britain in 1940 or in the days after Pearl Harbor when the Japanese swept in and pushed back American forces again and again and all hope seemed lost to life in 1946 when the Axis powers had been soundly crushed and the Cold War not quite begun.

          So yeah, I totally agree you can run the World of Darkness setting without making the darkness all consuming and Mage is one of the better windows on that version of the setting.

                              ​​I don't think you're stupid or alone or wrong at all for wanting a less "grimdark" WoD. You really don't have to remove a lot, depending on the setting, to get a little blue sky and some silver linings into your world. My experience is with WTA, so I'll speak mainly from that perspective. If you don't want the apocalypse to be inevitably coming within this generation, just don't make it so. You know how often people in the real world are always trying to predict the apocalypse, and how people in the real world are always seeing "signs" of the apocalypse, and how vague apocalyptic prophecy tends to be? Turn that into the reality the Garou are facing: sure, there are signs of the apocalypse everywhere, but there have always been signs of the apocalypse here and there, so it's futile to try to forecast it, and with enough wisdom, determination, and luck, the Gaian spirits and fera might have hope start turning the tide, beat back the Wyrm's corruption and the Weaver's webs gradually over time, and maybe put off the apocalypse indefinitely. It's the old "we're not dead yet" and "never give up" attitude they already have, with the "hope that springs eternal in the human breast" added to the mix because "it ain't over until it's over." For the vampires, maybe all you have to do is offer them a real, achievable hope of finding a way to keep the Beast at bay indefinitely, give the religious ones a reason to hope their souls aren't hopelessly damned, and then the only thing they ultimately can't avoid is the threat of ennui.

                              In my humble opinion, all you have to do to rescue the WoD from the grimdark mood is to remove the inevitability of the overwhelming doom that threatens the various kinds of monsters, and replace it with a very serious threat of doom, but one that they have some measure of hope to overcome. Really that's what I see the introductory comic in the W20 core book trying to do. Once you've got some real, serious hope for the characters to reach towards, you can add more subtle things, like make Pentex less powerful, make the werewolves more willing to make truces with the vampires, etc. It really can be, as others have said, a good urban fantasy setting, approaching something like the "Being Human" TV show, where survival, redemption, and virtue are actually possible.

                                      No, it's ok!

                                      I've always seen the WoD more as a dark urban fantasy setting (with the different games harboring different levels of incorporating personal horror elements), with a heavily stylized dark gothic-punk aesthetics (which I quite like) than a strict horror slash social critique one.

                                      Yes it is those too and don't get me wrong, it's reflection of real-world phenomena and in-your-face with the dark attitude are things I always loved, but truly, I always found the overly grimdark you-cant-play-anything-than-lowly-nobodies-and-can't-change-anything-and-the-whole-world-is-shit attitude some people prefer boring. Also, how long we could mourn the terrible loss of humanity and the terror of having to drink blood, truly?

                                      No, WoD is a power fnatasy too and if you have power, you could change things and if there's the possibility of change, there's hope.

                                      Also, WoD has a certain coolness factor and I like to play into it from time-to-time. Just read the fiction.

                                      So yes, I think it's totally possible to play it as less dark. Nothing's wrong with that.

                                      Fundamentalyy, I think the difference between an average lighter urban fantasy and WoD boils down to this: in an average UF, most people are good, even if they're mediocre, or fallible, but fundamentally good. Even supernaturals keep things like community, love and such on high regard (in a sense, they are more human than WoD's supnat residents) and they are willing to play nice, even in the face of long-standing prejudices. There is darkness, but that is something that comes from the outside, from demons, crazy vampires, cultists, dark mages, etc. or from the abnormal or broken individuals. In a sense, it's a fundamentally Western, dare I say, American mindset.

                                      In WoD, however, the power hungry, selfish, bordering on-or full-blown sociopathic and much more inhumane are arguably the norm among the supernatural, while the mundanes are mindless sheeps most of the time, apathetic wageslaves. People, in general are just bad in the WoD, either because humans are just less good than they think they are, or because of the influence of the supernatural. It's a much more cynical mindset and not everyone is comfortable with it.

                                      So, in a nutshell, in an average UF, the world is in the light and you defending it from the darkness. It's a superheroes' world. In the WoD, the world is dark, or at least heavily dimmed dark-gray and you try to defend and spread the rare sparks of light. Or just want to rule the darkness as one of the monsters. It's a villains' world.

                                      Of course, this is a scale and you could do your game at any point you want in-between. It's not even consistent between the games. with Vampire, I tend to lean more toward dark, with Mage, to a more hopeful style.

                                          Dude, I've been running my Vampire games as a "less dark" World of Darkness for years.

                                          No Metaplot, No Personal Horror, No Gothic-Punk stuff (my games are more anime-influenced), Vampires can be heroic good guys if they choose to do so, more Trenchcoats and Katanas than you can shake a stick at, and the 90's fan-game Senshi: The Merchandising is declared canon in my personal games, while Kindred of the East and Demon: The Fallen are declared non-canon.

                                          Long story short, it's YOUR game. Make it what you want it to be. As long as everyone is having fun, there is NO "wrong way" to play World of Darkness or any other role-playing game for that matter.

Essentially the weight of the past and of the vast impersonal powers of the present bearing down oppressively on the world and those in it, contrasted with an attitude of rebellion against the status quo of banality, surrender and ignorance. Often the dignity and dreams of the individual striving against uncaring or corrupt forces who want to crush such things. It's not dark clothes and odd haircuts; it's the ideas behind those things in the first place.
