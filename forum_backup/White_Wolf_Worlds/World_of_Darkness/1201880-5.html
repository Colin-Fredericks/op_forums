        Originally posted by Lian View Post


        We must be reading two profoundly different books, between the overt Orwellian bits to the plethora of heroic factions within the Technocracy working around the system because its broken to the Default explanation for the Disparate Alliance, I would say the default assumption is that the Technocracy is the Antagonist Faction... even in Technocracy chronicles.
        There's definitely plenty of support for playing the Technocracy as antagonists if that's what you want to do; I've never said otherwise. What I'm taking issue with is the claim that M20 presents using the Technocracy as the antagonists as the default option. It explicitly does not.

        No, the default antagonist faction in M20 is the Nephandi — even to the extent that much of what's wrong with the Technocracy and the Traditions is blamed on them.

        The default explanation for the Disparate Alliance that you alluded to isn't “The Technocracy is a threat”; it's “the Nephandi have infiltrated the Technocracy”. It's their shared belief that the Nephandi are puppetmasters who must be stopped at all costs that's bringing them together — and it's their suspicion that the Nephandi may have infiltrated the Traditions too that keeps them from approaching the Council with their concerns.

        The heroic factions within the Technocracy that are working around the system are there for Storytellers who want to tell stories along the lines of “the Technocracy is corrupt but redeemable”. It doesn't mean that they're the default approach for a Technocracy Chronicle; indeed, the section in Chapter Seven about a Technocracy Chronicle, while it does say that intrigue is featured in nearly all Technocratic Chronicles, doesn't even mention the dissident factions until the final paragraph, and then only to note that they're an exception to the way other Technocracy Chronicles work. So no, the Technocracy is not the default antagonist of a Technocratic Chronicle.

            One thing to remember about a Technocracy chronicle is, you are playing the winners. So even if the Technocracy is the lighter shade of grey, you as a technocrat are not the underdog. At best, you're the rich guy drone-striking evilish poor people with rusty junk weapons. Which inherently limits the drama of playing Techno vs Traddie from the Techno perspective.

            So a Technocracy chronicle is going to be more often about being the winner who doesn't want his victory to fall apart from internal corruption.

            That's not to say it's impossible to run a Techno chronicle where you are locally the underdog, or for that matter, nor is it impossible to run a Tradition chronicle focusing on internal corruption. But the tendency is going to be stronger towards running Techno chronicles as fights against internal corruption, and Tradition chronicles as guerilla stuff. Being the winner and loser respectively just lends itself to those results with less contrived plot-points.

                    Well modern James Bond movies are exactly what I'm talking about. Bond doesn't really fight terrorists, except as minor cold-open villains. He fights corrupt establishment figures. Modern Spectre isn't some organization of third world terrorists. It's an organization of what in Mage would be Syndicate and NWO types corrupting the establishment from within. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectre_(2015_film) The gist of modern spy movies is that the "good" guys won, and are now rotting from the inside. Hell, it's even become part of superhero movies, where SHIELD has secretly been massively infiltrated by Hydra, and even the non-Hydra leaders will nuke New York if things get hectic. Or Homeland, where the biggest evil of all is the new President.

                    For that matter, the quintessential Space Marine movie, "Aliens", has the aliens mainly be a threat because the corporation is so obsessed with controlling a new resource that they sabotage the survival of their own troops. And "Starship Troopers" has the subtext of being a propaganda movie created by a government that destroyed one of its own cities to justify a war.
