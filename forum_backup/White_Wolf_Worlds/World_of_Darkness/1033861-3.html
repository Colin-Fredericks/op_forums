    Originally posted by Saur Ops Specialist View Post

    Recall that we're dealing with beings that have frequent mystic visions warning them of looming calamities, the capacity to cheat the separation of space to cut travel time significantly, and a social-spiritual mandate to go and involve themselves in dangerous shit. They might not have every base covered, but they'll have had a rather large body of interesting experiences.
    They are still very few and still can't be everywhere. They can't be in every battle, much less in every back-room negotiating and intrigue. Even if a character was present during an event, what individuals see is, again, very limited and extremely biased.

    Look, vampire elders, methuselahs, archmages and shapeshifters, or any other critter with a very long or infinite lifespan are just not infallible, omniscient repositories of history. They know a lot more about specific things, persons and events, than what is in the books, yes, but the biggest parts of their knowledge are personal impressions, stories and anecdotes. One of them may have war stories about Richard the Lionheart, another may have known Shakespeare personally and read the first draft of Midsummer Night's dream. Another one might been present at the fall of Constantinople. All of that is good, it adds to the game, but it's very different than assuming they know every part of history infallibly and objectively. Besides, those individual immortals and old things are scattered, hard to reach and very low in numbers.

    If nothing worked, then let's think!

                Originally posted by Karlgust View Post

                I think I have a communication problem... I don't why, but so many people on so many topics get mad at me, but I'm sincerely not doing on purpose...

                Well... I'm not trying to say "don't do it". I'm trying to make some good ideas on how to do it
                I'm not mad at all. I was just uncertain about what is your goal with this topic? At first, you asked for advice about crossovers, then went to great lengths about how crossover is bonkers and that was a bit confusing to me.

                Regardless, to maintain a productive conversation:

                Another thing that crossed my mind.... Why can't exist this super-secret-conspiracy that has many different types of supernaturals? I mean... Did you ever heard that joke about a Mage, a Vampire, a Garou and a Changelling entering a bar... And creating their own organization?

                Well, the shapeshifters of the East are not organized by their supernatural "race", instead making " super heores clubs", were all breeds are toghether (except the Ananasi), and their divisions are cultural/social rather than racial... So, why can't we have the "league of extraordinary monsters"?

                What do you guys think?
                That's a great question and I asked myself that several times. The logical answer is it can't exist, because, while I on the firm opinion that the WoD is intentionally a crossover setting (the corebooks and nearly every supplement mention other critters), the games itself weren't intended as crossover. But basically we all know that, every game has it's own protagonists and focus. Moreover, different writers had different opinions about crossovers. Thus, something like a supernatural coalition, or the aforementioned Evil G5 never happened, the closes things are indeed Pentex, the Beast Courts and maybe the Tal'MaheRA to some extent.

                Now, I do think that something like a supnat coalition could exist, but not on the large scale. You would have a sincerely apocalypse-like event on the big scale to get the big groups working together in concert. Otherwise, there's too much lack of knowledge, misunderstanding, differing interests and goals, limited resources and so on.

                However, on the smaller scale, you absolutely could have something in my opinion, either locally, or on a bigger, organizational level. The Traditions as a whole are likely won't sign a pact with the Garou Nation, but there might be a Dreamspeaker group which maintains an exceptionally good relationship with the Uktena, even maybe with a lot of kinfolk mages and sorcerers. They could even originates from the same tribe and from one region. They could help each other out regularly. I think that's fine, things like that are great foundations for chronicles and character concepts.

                Or, in a contested city between the Camarilla and the Sabbat the local Bone Gnawers and Glasswalkers might have forged a very strenous, but functional (for now..) treaty and pact with the local Camarilla establishment, because at least those leeches are reasonable and tend to stay low and the evil you know is always better.

                Alternatively, while I think a super-secret allied supnat organization is very unlikely, unless you're willing to rewrite large parts of the setting (and I'm not against that, honestly, I'd like to play in a more "conventional urban fantasy" setting too, where the different groups and critters are constituting a more unified underground culture with smaller subcultures, instead of the extremely xenophobic groups of WoD - maybe we'll get a step in that direction with the upcoming One WoD). However, there might be personal avquaintances and friendships and frenemies in the upper echelons of the groups, who could make up a quasi-informal super-conspiracy.

                If nothing worked, then let's think!

                                Originally posted by PMárk View Post
                                My advice?

                                First: give up the notion of one convenient, unified timeline and the "this is how things happened and that was how the world was created" approach of other rpgs and worlds.

                                Second: base things on the fact that people are fallible. Memories are fallible, even the memories of beings like methuselahs and mokolé. Oral tradition is extremely fallible. That that garou say something doesn't make it true, it's a myth. Just because something is in a written source, like the Book of Nod doesn't make it true either, just like in real life. Oh, people are whining about Demon, but honestly, demons don't have the truth either. Their God could have been a lot of things. Just because a game was based on Judeo-Christian mythology and another on animistic faith systems doesn't necessiate that either of them have to be exclusively true.

                                IMO, when you embrace that no one has the ultimate truth, just their specific mythos, suddenly the "incompatible" worldviews and creation stories start to matter much less. Accidentally that's how the real world works. Different people and religions have very different ideas about how creation works and how it came to exist.

                                In my book the different critters having different stories don't deride the game, on the contrary, it adds to it.

                                Going back to history: to me, everything earlier than the high middle ages is extremely fragmented, just like in real life. Rome, we know a bit more about, but that's it. Individuals from those ages, like methuselahs usually aren't willing to give up their knowledge, but if they are, they are still individuals and they can't remember something that didn't happen with them personally in an age before fast travel and communication. Even the antediluvians and even Caine are just persons.

                                Or, to put it differently, what a rokea would remeber? Swimming, swimming, swimming. A Plesiosaurus here, an early whale there. Bajilion of fish. More swimming. A tasty seal from not so long ago. History tends to not happening in the deep oceans.

                                Everything before the great ancient civilizations is basically a myth and no one knows.
                                Ultimately that's what I most agree with. Crossover in the world of Darkness have three kinds of problems: Inconsistent rules, inconsistent perception of current cosmology, and inconsistent assertions on lore. But the last one isn't so much of a problem, only when such assertions can't be ignored. And the fact is that most players put a lot more of weight on those assertions than they actually have.
