    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post

    I studied it some time ago, so no sources. Then yes, if you have an expert that would be a good call, better yet if they can point a good free source.

    But long story short two things change the effects of pressure: uniformity and how much the fluid around you compresses under pressure.

    Most of our experience with pressure under air is uneven and dealing with a medium that compresses a lot. That leads to squishy results as the fluid in question moves violently to weak spots.

    Hydrostatic pressure is the opposite. Very hard to concentrate, tending to get uniformly around you, and water is stupidly resistant to compression. A drop from the surface and one from the Mariana Trenches have almost the same density.

    All in all the result is no damage. Other things do happen. Without magicking physics away, a vampire in deep water would be pinned by the weight of the water column in a way air doesn't do. Think being unable to raise even if less dense because the pressure is literally forcing you down.

    The problem with speed is chemical. Pressure changes reactivity and solubility of a lot of things that we have in the body. In this case the main culprit is your blood's Nitrogen. As you compress it will suffer some changes, if you then decompress too fast it will form bubbles in your veins.

        Started to edit the main, now I want to discuss rules for other extreme conditions related to the modern industrial world.

        Those are not places we usually consider as anything beyond cool hazards, because the human body can't survive them. But inhuman corpses may be able to withstand at least enough to explore or use many of them further than humans, and even use some as safe if inconvenient havens, where hunters can't hope to get into.

        I'm not making rules now, I want to discuss ideas about what to do with them. I'll also do research later.

        - Furnaces:

        Of course a vampire can't resist the fire of such places. But industrial furnaces include whole machines and areas of intense heat and heat is not the same as fire.

        I think vampires should be able to ignore heat at least until water boils, and probably higher. Not indefinitely, of course.

        The dangers are four: boiling, melting, cooking and burning. Each happens to a human body at a different temperature, cause different injuries and may interact with the supernatural body of a vampire in different ways.

        Which means the Curse can be used to wave the physics and damage for a certain degree. For example, we may decide Vampire Blood within the body only boils at 150⁰C, or never and the boiling itself is never a source of harm.

        It can also be used to make some serious problems into mere penalties. This is useful as a way to open up story options: if exploring an area of intense heat creates obstacles without killing, your PCs can try them at a cost.

        - Molten rock/glass:

        At lower ends, lava is less destructive than we usually think. First it's so dense it doesn't really feel liquid, second its heat doesn't transfer that well. Finally, it actually cools down at a reasonably fast rate.

        In lab experiments recreating lava, pieces of meat or chicken submerged in lava burn in the outside but remain uncooked inside: the immediate rock solidifies and insulate from the rest of the flow. A vampire should be able to survive, despite entombed in rock and with some damage.

        Glass would pose a similar situation, but more common and easier to break free.

        - Chemical vats:

        Those depend on each kind of chemical. Some are corrosive and would eat at vampire flesh, or react producing heat and fire. But others are toxic to humans for thoroughly distinct reasons that an undead body doesn't care.

        Another option for exploring those is to draw on fluids dynamics to see how different vats change movement: is it possible to swim, or is the vampire consigned to either the bottom or the surface? Is it so viscous you get totally stuck, or is it surprisingly easier than water? What about non-newtonian fluids?

        - Centrifuges:

        Here the problem is definitely something that can smash your body to a pulp, the G force. It partially fights gravity and each level makes movement harder and harder.

        Big enough centrifuges for a human body are uncommon, but not unheard of: some materials are processed in bulk in big ones, and some are built for human use in gravity tests. A fictional setting with secret laboratories has probably a number of bigger, weirder ones, or other machinery that work like one.

                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                I think that's skipping the folklore inspiration a bit.
                I'm revising this part, and with this took the time to make a proper research into the matter.

                What I can say today is that we're looking to at least two very distinct lines of development for this trope, where the distinction is in the very concept of what the revenant is.

                Folklore left remarkably little record about the beliefs behind such practices. Many others we have a lot of writings about how the monsters and phenomena are understood, their meaning, etc. For raising corpses it's almost nothing but the practices themselves.

                Current anthropologic theories for this, based in the matter-of-fact style of descriptions and the few theories on the mechanism behind it, is that corpses were seen indeed as mechanical objects rather than "rekindled" in any way.

                Some theories are eminently mechanical, like a common idea that gases building up could be them cause for reanimation, while others were supernatural, with it becoming a vessel for a spirit, possibly but not necessarily that of the deceased. But they all agree on key points: the body itself is just an unintelligent mechanism, a literal vehicle at best. Cursed dead people are the purview of ghosts and possessions.

                The common line this appears to draw among the distinct practices is that they stay purely mechanical: stuff the mouth so the body can't bite or gnaw, pierce the heart so it can't pump fluids, and remove the head and place it in the body's legs so getting up becomes impossible. This reading is enforced by the belief that risk is directly tied to the state of decomposition, not as much with preserved corpses seen as unnatural, but any fresh corpse being a greater risk as decomposition removes the problem. This isn't very different in Asia, with the original Chinese "vampires" being just stiff corpses that monks would animate to ease their travel to the body's village for burial.

                Vampires proper appear in literature later, first what we can call "proto-vampires" from 1800 onward, then something closer with Polidori's The Vampyre. And they start cut completely from this folklore.

                Both proto-vampires and Lord Ruthven are not particularly resilient. Geraldine (1800), Oneiza (1801) and Darley (1817) aren't described as immortals, with Oneiza dying from common violence. Ruthven is similarly killed by bandits, and he brings the notion of immortality coming back to life after a year rather than resisting it in the first place.

                Our first example of the immortal vampire as we know is Varney, but he's immune to all the methods. Varney dies in the end by jumping into an active volcano, the old equivalent of the character being tied to a rocket to the Sun.

                Real folkloric methods would come back only with Carmilla, but they're immediately beyond their original scope: her destruction requires a lengthy ritual with many blessings and prays. Several methods aren't applied in succession, with a blessed stake through her heart, then she is beheaded with a blessed blade, and then her body is burned. It's severely implied that anything less she would still né able to recover from.

                Dracula keeps a similar approach for Lucy, and Van Helsing's studies imply she's not the only one, but the Count himself requires something different, and although we see the last of him with a Bowie Knife in the chest, it's ambiguous in the book, after all the struggle to kill him, which parts contributed or not.

                Even setting aside my peeve with decapitation as a combat occurrence, VtM sits in a weird space in the tradition. Old stories would accept simple beheading, but they describe an entity very different from modern Vampires and, as it seems, more mechanical. New ones would not engage with beheading unless if it needed far more effort than just doing it.

                Mostly VtM's take seems to still come from Highlander rather than anything vampire.

                        Considering how long decapitations were a means of execution in history, I highly doubt there was some shift in the 1960s. France performed its last execution by guillotine in 1977.. that's a pretty long history of cutting heads off.

                        In combat, the logic of targeting the center mass has always been very simple: it's the biggest target and it's full of vital organs. Landing solid hits to the head is much harder compared to attacks to the torso. In lots of existing manuals of sword fighting in the age of plate armor, one of the default targets was to thrust to the underside of the shoulder joint (which has to be loose for flexibility) to disable you're opponent's arm and then you pull a dagger to finish them off. Helmets were plenty strong to keep the head safe from a lot of blows and those things were designed to that thrusts or arrows to the necessary sight and breathing holes were flukes rather than realistic targets. Guns didn't change the calculus. Shooting for center mass instead of the head has been the default for centuries and still is.

                        The idea this has anything to do with changing concepts of a symbolic nature to the heart and the brain doesn't really line up with how fights work, or even with when medicine and anatomy started to sort out which organs did what.

                        ---------

                        My only issue with the whole "Highlander" thing is that Highlander did not popularize the idea of easy decapitations in a fight; nor has VtM ever really worked in a fashion that makes it close to an optimal way to fight. As far as I can tell the primary factor has always been, "since vampires don't have functional bodies like humans, what exactly is the justification for them dying for good after taking too much damage?" with the answer of "when the head is functionally removed, because we already made staking incapacitation over a killing blow."

                        Highlander fights might be been a cool extra visual for the game to lean into, but the rules have never actually leaned into it as some sort of default over being what you did to kill off a vampire during the day if you don't want to use fire or sunlight.

                                        Sunlight and Daysleep – Expanded

                                        As tradition dictates, Kindred are nocturnal predators with aversion to the sun and the day in general, consigned to exist fully as a society only in the dark of the night.

                                        That said, this is not as old a tradition and not something we can't tinker with. As a thematic rule, no major balance issue occur if you alter or remove it.

                                        For the record: Dracula, Carmilla, Ruthven and Varney, the four founders of the myth of the Vampire, never got harmed by the sun, they could walk in plain daylight even if they didn't much like it. 103 years separate Ruthven from Nosferatu, the first ever to be harmed (and killed) by the sun. Way more time than what went between the start of the tradition and the first Vampire to ignore it again, the Queen of the Damned.

                                        Assumptions

                                        The assumptions you make about the game should guide your hand on deciding how much to make the day dangerous or not. Choose them carefully.

                                        Special: remember that Aggravated damage isn't just an immediate threat, it lingers for some time. Depending on game's pacing, any damage of this caliber will make things harder for the character for a while.

                                        Current Game Standards

                                        1. Sunlight is dangerous and Daysleep is incapacitating. Kindred society simply can't afford to not be exclusively nocturnal.
                                        2. Damnation adds nuance, not all monsters are equally at danger. Those closer to mortals can afford a longer stay.
                                        3. Indirect exposure matters. You can be tactical about it, you can take calculated risks.
                                        4. Mortals are dangerous. A main reason is their ability to exploit the greatest of Kindred weaknesses (this is balance-relevant).
                                        5. The day itself imposes the Slumber. If not completely incapacitated, vampires are sluggish at best and helpless at worse during the day, sun or not.
                                        6. Vampires should still be able to flee the scene if endangered during the day. This is to prevent insta-kill buttons in the game.
                                        7. Humanity is a buffer against the Slumber. The more you have, the easier to function during the day.
                                        8. Any daily activity is a fight against the Slumber, and it will win. Only the most Humane pose a chance to stay a whole day awake.
                                        9. Sleep isn't necessary. The Rouse Check of the night readies the vampire for the rest of it.
                                        10. CAUTION: excess damage flattens it.

                                        Tropes of the Game

                                        1. The sun is a dream. Any Vampire with even a modicum of capacity to actually walk in the day is both rare and target of deadly envy.
                                        2. Walk into the sunlight is both metaphor and actual suicide method, as is "greeting the sun" a metaphor and method for execution.
                                        3. A vampire is the ultimate sin. The sun is the ultimate cleanser. Only holiness or divine intervention protect the Damned.
                                        4. Servitors are needed. A Vampire without mortals to get things done in the day is missing a limb.

                                        Unused Tropes

                                        1. The sun is utterly lethal. No Kindred can dare to face it beyond mere seconds.
                                        2. A vampire is harmless during the day. Even under threat to life they can't wake.
                                        3. The sun burns sin from Vampires. The ones who avoid it can still face its rays unhindered.
                                        4. Only direct sunlight harms Vampires. Without it they can't even tell day from night better than a mortal (classic).
                                        5. Sunlight is a physical barrier. A Vampire isn't as much harmed by it as is trapped and unable to enter or, if surrounded, move. One caught in the sun is a corpse and nothing more until nightfall.
                                        6. Sunlight is dangerous, but manageable. It isn't safe to take it unprotected, but a sufficiently covered Kindred can give it a try. Their society is still nocturnal, but the odd man wandering around with too much cover in the summer may be one of them (more urban legend).
                                        7. Sunlight is a discomfort, not a danger. It dampens powers, is overall annoying, but vampires can take it. Their society is still mostly nocturnal, but it's harder to tell which of your neighbors are one of them. Maybe a whole street (original and good for some horror tropes).
                                        8. Power staves off the danger of sunlight for good. Higher Blood Potency protect the older monsters, making them even more dangerous (Queen of the Damned style, it increases the sense of mythical might of the Methuselah).
                                        9. Vampires don't sleep, except to recover from grievous injuries and other mystic activities. During the day the creatures are hiding in the dark places of the city, plotting and preparing for when lady Night frees them from captivity (good for some horror tropes, but also to get the time jumps out of your game.

                                        Proposals

                                        The first two just simplify what's already how it works in practice. The Mercy and The Aura are meant to work together and build something that actually follows the intent of the rules.

                                        Daylight Simple Rule (baseline)

                                        A vampire suffer 2 Agg dmg each turn exposed to sunlight. The ST is the final judge on what counts as exposure, but there's no partial damage.

                                        This is simpler but effectively the same as the current rules.

                                        Daylight Death Sentence

                                        A vampire has 3 rounds to go back to the darkness, turning to ashes if still exposed to the sun at the end of the timer.

                                        This removes variables and any advantage from higher stats or powers, but the vampire take no damage if fast enough, good balance

                                        The Mercy (Humanity as Shield)

                                        The purifying light of the sun punishes the Beast, not the Human beneath. If being moral protects against the sun, the rate of Aggravated damage is as follows:

                                        - Humanity 0: instant destruction
                                        - Humanity 1-2: 2/turn
                                        - Humanity 3-4: 1/2 turns
                                        - Humanity 5-6: 1/scene
                                        - Humanity 7-8: 1/day
                                        - Humanity 9-10: 1 Superficial/Day

                                        Commentary:

                                        Humanity 0: A Wight has no place under the sun, ever.
                                        Humanity 1-2: Regular, pretty harsh, run or perish.
                                        Humanity 3-4: This is still Agg, but you can brave a short run without a death sentence.
                                        Humanity 5-6: Still free Agg, but once in a while you can make a painful sacrifice to take care of business
                                        Humanity 7-8: Don't get confident, this will burn through your reserves (and skin) fast if you abuse, but you can actually function during the day despite the smell of bacon.
                                        Humanity 9-10: Daywalker. You deserve it.

                                        Most functional Vampires are between Humanity 3 and 5: they can't really afford to brave the sun outside extraordinary need, but have a chance at fleeing if just thrown there.

                                        Starting at 7 gives the PCs a taste of the day as an option, but the cost is still high: a day under the sun is another healing Agg at a cost of 3 Rouse Checks, and feeding is limited by the very fact of being Humanity 7 or 8.

                                        Vampires at enough Humanity to not be toast in seconds become both an asset and envied. They get a role in society that partially offsets the political disadvantage of being scrupulous, but many would like to bring them down eventually.

                                        Dials:

                                        1. Move every category down a peg and let Humanity 9-10 be immune. This will make for a new Kindred society where managing Humanity is a focal concern and the day is not off-limits, but mostly still stay nocturnal.

                                        2. Move every category up a peg and let Wights explode in flames, starting fires. This leaves most vampires in the old rules or close, while the really dedicated do reap some benefit and also become too unreliable to be used regularly as an asset by others. They only get the anger.

                                        The Judge's Eye (The Curse as Factor)

                                        The stronger the Curse, the harder it is to escape judgement by the sun itself. This is about escaping notice: low Blood Potency can dare a little exposure without being “found out”, while the most powerful must avoid any hint of daylight to survive.

                                        With that, this is a binary situation: a character either is under the effects of the sun or isn't. If protected, they suffer no further effects. If they are exposed, full effects apply. You can combine this rule with others for damage and/or Daysleep.

                                        Exposure Score:

                                        Start with an Exposure Score of 8, then apply modifiers as presented in following sections. A Vampire is protected from Sunlight at Exposure 0 or below.

                                        - Exposure 8: Bright Daylight ➡️ start at this
                                        - Exposure 0: Safety Threshold

                                        Exposure Conditions:

                                        - Dappled Shade -2
                                        ---- Tree canopy or translucent shade

                                        - Light Cloud Cover -4
                                        ---- Any shade that still has shadows

                                        - Heavy Cloud Cover -6
                                        ---- Heavy enough to have no shadow

                                        - Veiled Indoors -8
                                        ---- Curtains or other barrier for light

                                        - Deep Indoors -10
                                        ---- No direct light, visibly dimmer

                                        - Adjusted Darkness -12
                                        ---- Shapes visible, details obscured

                                        - Absolute Darkness -14
                                        ---- Found in the depths of the world

                                        Protective Gear:

                                        - Minimal -1
                                        ---- Long sleeves, hat or sunglasses
                                        ---- Not weird, but easy to remember
                                        - Light -2
                                        ---- Long coat, hat, glasses, gloves
                                        ---- In a hot day or place you get stares
                                        - Moderate -3
                                        ---- Add large trenchcoat and scarf
                                        ---- It's either winter or you're weird
                                        - Heavy -4
                                        ---- Add mask and prescription glasses
                                        ---- Your aversion to light is undeniable
                                        - Maximum -6
                                        ---- Full-body suit and closed helmet
                                        ---- This is specialized & unmistakable
                                        - Dark Armor -9
                                        ---- That's at least a space suit
                                        ---- You're a comic book character

                                        Curse Effect:

                                        - Add Bane Severity to Exposure.
                                        - If Exposure 1+, apply sunlight.

                                        Commentary:

                                        Thin Blood (BS 0): in any overcast day they can try to walk around if they have the right gear. Otherwise, avoiding direct light is safe enough for them.

                                        Child/Neonate (BS 2): they can brave the streets in the cloudiest days, and sleep anywhere without a non-blocked window.

                                        Ancillae (BS 3): with adequate clothes they can wander sunlit rooms as long as they remain indoors, away from open windows.

                                        Elder (BS 4): rooms with windows already pose a risk without good preparation, they feel truly safe only in the dark.

                                        Greater Elders (BS 5): darkness is their domain, even if they can try a dimly lit room with effort. It's unlikely they'll want to.

                                        Methuselah (BS 6): preparation can ease things, but safety is only obtained in the darkest pits. A coffin is for real a must have.

                                        This adds a layer of risk management and strategy to Kindred relationship with light. It is something that will permeate the society:

                                        - Vampires in need can operate during the day without being consigned only to catacombs and sealed havens. While the amount of freedom varies, most can now manage something.
                                        - Neonates are an even greater asset and a strong practical reason for Embraces, as new Vampires can navigate the day to a greater extent.
                                        - Old monsters still reced to the darkness as they see little reason to exert as little freedom at such a risk. Stratification in vampiric society becomes more visible as they literally occupy different places.
                                        - It is a risk. Miscalculation or accidents can put you in a higher exposure level than planned and end up frying you. It's not a decision to be taken lightly, but to be carefully planned ahead.

                                        Dials:

                                        1. Widen the gap using the tables below and adding Blood Potency instead of Bane Severity. This will lock each one more rigidly in their functional niches and increase the penalty for high BP at the same time that increasing problems feel more gradual.
                                        2. Give Thin-Bloods a -2 bonus. The Thin-Blood Advantage Day Drinker has no penalties anymore. A well prepared Thin-Blood can make do with umbrellas and Day Drinkers really can walk at day.
                                        3. Use the Day Drinker penalties for those at Exposure 1 instead of damage. That way Vampires can give up some power for slightly more mobility. It goes a long way.
                                        4. Use the Day Drinker penalties for those at Exposure 0 instead of protection. At the threshold vampires pay a cost to function at all, the benefits of this rule are sensibly reduced in practice.

                                        Wider Gap Exposure Score:

                                        - Exposure 11: Bright Daylight
                                        - Exposure 0: Safety Threshold

                                        Wider Gap Exposure Conditions:

                                        - Dappled Shade -3
                                        - Light Cloud Cover -6
                                        - Heavy Cloud Cover -9
                                        - Veiled Indoors -12
                                        - Deep Indoors -15
                                        - Adjusted Darkness -18
                                        - Absolute Darkness -21

                                        Daysleep Bonus Dice

                                        You can have all sorts of sources for bonus dice on the roll to awake or remain awake. It is interesting to think why it affects the Curse in such a particular way. You should expect PCs to seek those bonuses and spend more time awake during the day simply by virtue of being reminded they can.

                                        - Minor Benefit +1
                                        ---- Almost symbolic, recognizes an effect without committing to it. A Kindred can still stack those in their favor.

                                        - Consistent Improvement +2
                                        ---- That's an extra average success. It's not a game changer, but increases the average stimulus category the Kindred can respond to. For Humanity 1, make success without cost possible at all.

                                        - Major Benefit +3
                                        ---- A single source this size is serious business. Humanity 7 Vampires can expect to wake up to minor problems more often than not, and Humanity 3 have a similar chance to wake up if their life is threatened.

                                        - Regular Success +4
                                        ---- Two extra average successes makes the sting of the rule sensibly lower, but still there. Starting Humanity vampires can wake up to solve problems, most can deal with emergencies if needed.

                                        - Maximum Benefit +5
                                        ---- No single source should give all that at once. This is enough to make any vampire respond to fire threats more often than not, while starting vampires can really wake up consistently if they need.

                                        - Regularly Awaken +6 +
                                        ---- Average successes now answer for a category of stimulus by themselves, leaving the vampire to account just for reliability and further categories.

                                        Sample Bonuses:

                                        - Chronicle Default +1 to +5
                                        ---- This establishes a new baseline for how easily/frequently vampires stay awake during the day to focus on their plots and intimate affairs.

                                        - Attuned lair +1 to +5
                                        ---- May even be a new Background, the Attunement makes the lord of the Haven more active inside it for their own projects. It may extend to guests as an extra.

                                        - No Feeding in previous night +2
                                        ---- Maybe it's the hungry Beast trying to find prey at any cost, maybe it's just the Human beneath finding a little respite, but it works.

                                        - Caffeinated blood +1
                                        ---- Stimulants are drugs, too. A victim under high doses may help fight off the Daysleep, on top of other effects it may have.

                                        - Touchstone is in the scene +3
                                        ---- Spending a day with a Touchstone or observing them is a powerful motif in the life of the undead.

                                        - Hunger Level
                                        ---- This is a powerful motivator. With the Hunger level applied directly as a bonus, you'll nudge players to see the day as a desperate but valid opportunity for feeding. You'll also give them a strategic reason to risk staying at higher Hunger. Finally these are Hunger Dice, so expect the day to be ruled by Compulsion frequently.

                                        Avoid penalties. Their most probable result will just be the PCs not trying as resisting Daysleep is hard enough to be worth the trouble.

                                        Daysleep Difficulty Modifier

                                        A few sources can alter how hard it is to wake up in this manner. They differ from bonus dice in that you can't stack them to go lower than difficulty 2, although you can stack them to have an easier time waking to lighter causes.

                                        - Easy Danger Roll Diff -1
                                        ---- Waking is a little easier, if still not guaranteed. High Humanity Vampires are reasonably safe against fire, while Humanity 4 can now escape burning to death more often than not.

                                        - Easy Important Roll Diff -2
                                        ---- Vampires wake for serious matters at least as easily as for fire. This change their potential relationship with their mortal servants, as now they can be called on important matters. For PCs it means you can have the characters participating in important day events without contrived plots or much luck.

                                        - Easy Inconvenience Roll Diff -3
                                        ---- Baseline now let Vampires wake for minor problems – that may just be not so minor, as in hunters approaching silently. Waking up just because they want is now as easy as staying awake after that.

                                        - Easy Interest Diff -4
                                        ---- Awaking is Diff 2 for whatever reason. While many Vampires won't even try, the creatures regularly stay up during the day. Mortals still have the upper hand of numbers and not frying under the sun, but the undead plot their big conspiracies all the time, and the dark places are dangerous.

                                        Sample Reductions
                                        - Chronicle Default -1 to -4
                                        ---- This establishes a new baseline for how easily/frequently vampires stay awake during the day to focus on their plots and intimate affairs.

                                        - Alarm Setup -1 to -2
                                        ---- A few clocks or a servant that do muster the courage to shake the vampire awake is -1. A system of stimuli made to wake up corpses may manage just that.

                                        - Neighborhood Resonance -1 to -4
                                        ---- While this is not necessarily the same as Blood Resonance, it may be related. The important thing is that some areas in the city can be easier for some Kindred to stay awake when there.

                                        - Supernatural Beacon -1 to -4
                                        ---- Powerful effects may rouse the Kindred into vigilance all day long in an area. Powerful presences, special magics, holes into the spirit world.

                                        Special: you can decide Diff can go as low as 1. This is going to make waking up way easier and more common, but isn't bad by itself. Do consider the rule Absolutes, though.

                                        Winning At A Cost - Day Awakening

                                        Standardizing the cost to cover remaining successes is a great opportunity to make it a more accessible option and, at the same time, define effects that reinforce themes you want.

                                        - Superficial Willpower Damage
                                        ---- Each success costs a Willpower point. Simple, direct, cut on resource, that's it.

                                        - Rouse Check
                                        ---- This is a heft cost, it should be either a single Check no matter how many are needed, or a Check for 2 successes. The Rouse Check of the night will still happen as normal.

                                        - Waking Sickness
                                        ---- The character starts further capped in a number of dice equal to the lacking successes. Each relevant roll under this penalty reduces it in 1.

                                        - Memory of Deprivation
                                        ---- The shock brings back one piece of memory from their living days: what it feels like to lack sleep. The sensation causes Impairment until nightfall, and then an additional scene per success needed.

                                        - Subsumed Beast
                                        ---- The Beast doesn't wake as completely and this is bad, because it houses the curse that empowers the Vampire. For each success short of the Diff, the player gives up one trait completely until they sleep a full day again. A whole Discipline is the standard, but they may chose Mend or Blood Surge.

                                        Day Awakening Duration

                                        Regular rule is to give the character a full scene, then ask for a new roll each new scene. That's reasonable, but too easy for a game that want to limit day action severely without cutting fire defense altogether, and too cumbersome if players want to engage in something all day long.

                                        - Give the Awakening roll’s margin as extra scenes before the next roll. That will reduce the need for repeating rolls all day long.

                                        Dials:

                                        - Change from roll per scene to roll per turn and margins to extra turns. You may want to also reduce the Difficulty of Awakening rolls as this will severely limit the reliability of awakening in the day, and too many rolls will be a chore.

                                        Sleep Benefits

                                        Those reinforce the theme of the Daysleep by giving an actual advantage on pursuing it. This makes the choice of staying awake vs undergoing the Daysleep more strategical.

                                        You get the benefit only after a full day of slumber, from sunrise to sunset.

                                        - Willpower Healing
                                        ---- Heal one superficial willpower damage

                                        - Resonance Attuning
                                        ---- Increase your current Resonance by 1 level of Intensity. If it would give you a Dyscrasia, the effect is: “you know if someone in your vicinity has the same Resonance. Consume the Dyscrasia to pinpoint them”.

                                        - The Blush of Life
                                        ---- Upon awakening, activating the Blush of Life let you roll two dice and keep the better.

                                        - Shackle the Beast
                                        ---- You get +1 dice on the very next roll to resist Frenzy.

                                        - Memoriam Dice
                                        ---- The character keeps all benefits they met through the night: if they stood at the place of the memory, drank blood of a witness and held the artifact, then it's as if they were all at the same time and add an extra dice for the sleep.

                                        Sleep Deprivation

                                        The opposite of the previous, this will tie the Daysleep to basic functions of undead flesh and blood, or otherwise reduce the character as the Curse claims the price.

                                        Any interruption of sleep, delay to start or a too early awakening kicks in the penalties, and they stay until the vampire sleeps a full day.

                                        - Locked Reflexes
                                        ---- The physiology of the undead isn't fully responsive. The Kindred can't use their fangs, the Blush of Life or Blood Surge. They all still exist as functions and can activate suddenly, no cost, at ST discretion, but are outside control.

                                        - Dead Flesh
                                        ---- Similar to the Thin Blood Flaw, you're a slightly rotting corpse. Just looking at you tells you're not well, and the smell isn't any pleasant. Use those rules, but add that you're still recognizable, and people may ask questions about this malady. Beware carrion feeders, most won't bother a corpse still moving, but some are brave or dumb enough.

                                        - Monstrous Hunger
                                        ---- Blood Potency is considered 2 levels higher for the purpose of feeding. If you're at BP 10, you can only slake your Hunger killing a human.

                                        - Bestial Lability
                                        ---- Double your Hunger Dice.

                                        Absolutes

                                        Sometimes it's easier to just skip rolls and bookkeeping altogether. Don't shy from that if you have decided on what you want from the idea of Vampires awake at day, just do it and ignore the dice.

                                        You can decide, for example:

                                        - Vampires just can do it
                                        ---- Staying awake, hiding from mortals is common, vampires plot all the time, sleep is for benefit/penalty or an affectation.

                                        - Vampires just can't do it
                                        ---- If caught unguarded by day they're just screwed. This is, indeed, the most vulnerable they get, and only the most trusted servants get access to their Master's resting place.

                                        - At X Humanity they do it
                                        ---- That gives a strong moral theme, and a hard benefit for staying above your line. 7 will distinguish starting players. 6 distinguishes the two halves of the score, 4 and 5 start to allow a majority of Vampires, while 2 and 3 make sleep a mark of final stages of degeneration.

                                        - At X awakening dice they do it
                                        ---- Similar to above, but bonus dice exist and count towards this goal. Bonuses then mark methods that reliably work to wakeup a Vampire. If you use this, a source of Diff reduction can work as a bonus at double the value.

                                        - If Hunger is 0, they can do it
                                        ---- Managing Hunger now has an extra benefit, and a reason for characters to seek the slaking of the last dot.

                                        - Pay the price and do it
                                        ---- Choose one cost for it: Minor/Major
                                        -------- A Rouse Check/2 Rouse Checks
                                        -------- A Willpower point/Aggravated
                                        -------- Any Resonance/Any Dyscrasia
