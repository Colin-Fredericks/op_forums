        I disagree with the interpretation that one can only have a "meaningful game" by ignoring the population ratio. It would be fair to say that you can only run specific types of stories by ignoring the ratio, but those types of stories would work better in say Werewolf or Exalted rather than Vampire if going by fluff & RAW.

        Vampires are supposed to be rare, most cities and towns won't have any vampires whatsoever. They are the focal points of influence, power, and to a certain extent corruption. Some STs tend to write the stories where legions of neonates war with each other, when in fact the majority of the wars should be fought between ghouls and normal mortal servitors. VtM is supposed to be a personal horror game rather than Call of Duty: Fang edition, the Blood Hunt videogame notwithstanding.

        I have run games where there are only the PC coterie and maybe three other vampires encountered in the whole chronicle, and it turned out great. I have also run games that upended most of the setting and had a great time doing it. The population ratio is a feature rather than a bug, though suppose many STs are more interested in running an Avengers Endgame rather than something more personal in nature.

                            Originally posted by Thoth View Post
                            General vampire population numbers is one of those things that people get passionately very "head canon" about. For some reason they get twitchy when they realize that their grand storyline of vampire politics wouldn't happen in the first place because the mortal population to vampire population ratio is so far off it is beyond laughable.

                            "What do you mean my San Francisco chronical about a dozen coteries of Cainites fighting a dozen Wu of Kuei-jin doesn't make sense?"
                            "With the mortal population of SF, you can have like five vampires total. Rather than a vampiric invasion, you are having five goths arguing in a room, but no one leaves because they don't want to be alone."

                            Yes, I am one of those annoying people who brings up the fact that Kuei-Jin are in fact officially counted as vampires when the infamous 1:100,000 ratio gets brought up. Which means the total number of Cainites is actually about 1/3rd lower than what most people math it out to be.

                            8 billion mortals on the planet (1:100K ratio) means there are about 80,000 vampires in existence. There are about two Cainites for every Kuei-Jin, which results in about >27K Kuei-Jin and about >54K Cainites.

                            There are 13 Clans and many Bloodlines, but keeping with simple calculations, just consider all the bloodlines to count as a 14th clan. That means each clan is only about 3,800 members strong worldwide, regardless of if they are active, in torpor, or are in other dimensions.

                            That doesn't sound so bad at first glance, until you realize that a coterie/pack is between three and ten members strong, and most mixed vampire population cities have several coteries in residence on top of the Prince/Primogen/Sheriff group and then the Harpies and other vampires who are not in groups but are still part of the community. Your average ambitious vampire storyline ends up accounting for a huge portion of the vampire race.

                            Of course, this gets even more silly when you go back in time. In 1800 the human population was only about 1 billion. Which means the Vampire population capped out at 10,000 in total. This then breaks down to about 6600 Cainites and about 3300 Kuei-jin. Doing the same 14-way split, that only leaves about >500 members per clan worldwide, regardless of being awake, in torpor, or in another dimension.

                            Amusingly enough by the math, your average vampire game should rarely have vampires in it.
                            Wait a minute. Weren’t the Kuei-jin retconned out of existence in V5? So a Vampire game set in 2024 using V5 rules and lore would only include Cainites for that 1:100,000 ratio. Slightly more manageable.

                                Originally posted by Atticus Cleanclaw View Post
                                Remember to consider the population of the region as a whole and not just the individual urban centres:
                                That's correct per the game. The 100,000 figure is supposed to be for the metropolitan area as a whole. So while Knoxville itself is only 200,000, the Knoxville metro area is almost 900,000. The game reflects that there are a lot of people who don't live in a specific city, but frequently travel there.

                                Of course, any ration of vampires to humans is completely arbitrary. We have no idea how many people per vampire are needed in order to adequately allow a vampire to survive and be unknown. It's not based on any kind of realism, but simply a guide to assist STs in designing their own chronicles.

                                Since many STs love to include many dozens, even hundreds of vampires, in their games, they ignore that ratio so they can include all the characters, clans, bloodlines, sects, vampires across a spread of generations, etc. that they want. That's a valid choice, but it isn't any less arbitrary or more realistic than the canonical ratio.

                                In my experience, players really only need about 20-30 vampire NPCs for them to have the experience that they expect. Anything more than that is superfluous as players don't keep track of more NPCs than that. Extra NPCs tend to be superfluous. You could keep to canonical ratio and still have more vampire NPCs available simply by expanding the geographic scope of the game. Instead of placing all your vampire NPCs into one city, you just make other nearby cities part of the game by frequent overnight travel, conclaves, inter-domain politics, etc. The Nashville metro area can hold around 20 vampires canonically, but Memphis adds another 14, Knoxville 10, and Chattanooga 5. Add some Gangrel in the wilderness, and you can easily have a game with 50-60 or so vampires and still go by canon. Not to mention all the smaller metro areas in the state you could include. Most chronicles aren't like that, but there is no reason for that. There's a reason Dark Colony covers all of New England instead of being Boston By Night.

                                Plus even the game itself acknowledges the established ratio always holds. That's just what the Inner Circle of the Camarilla wants enforced. Chicago had way more Kindred than expected because Lodin always had to deal with hostile Primogen limiting his power, dealing with lots of rivals, and ruling a domain whose size and population was probably too big. The Los Angeles free state obviously ignored it. All Sabbat domains ignore it. It isn't hard for an ST to justify why this domain has more vampires than normal and still retain something tied to canon.

                                                    Originally posted by Lysander View Post
                                                    So in general how many kindred do you have in your cities? We thought that maybe having at best thirty max was a good solid number to work with and have a decent number of stories and conflicts with.
                                                    In my own case I ask what sort of story I am wanting to tell, then fill it out from there. After 25+ years of playing, the standard storylines of neonates raging against the system is a bit played out for me. To use video game concepts, at this point I prefer alternative starting points, different builds, and different play methods.

                                                    Imagine you are a coterie of neonates who want to bypass local vampire politics, so you pool your Resource backgrounds, and buy a nice communal haven in a city without a vampire presence. The prince technically can't stop you from leaving their sphere of influence, and once outside the city their "legal" power more or less ends. The game is mostly about setting up a power base in the new town. This means that Backgrounds are more important to direct play than many stats or disciplines. The only non-coterie vampires you interact with is someone you know from the old city or if someone wants to make a play for your new home. Thats less than 10 vampires total for the majority of a chronicle.

                                                    In a different game I ran, I had the would-be coterie embraced on a camping trip by a passing gangrel, she gave them the standard introductory speech and "good luck" slap on the rump and disappeared into the night. Well the PCs then had to figure out what to do since they were in an unclaimed zone of territory in the middle of several other small cities that each had their own power structures. Which group do you join, do you go full independent, do you just go into torpor and hope everything blows over? That game had something like 50+ vampire NPCs but each town only had like 10 or so cainites. Yes, the sire gangrel was Ennoia herself. No, they didn't figure this out until much later in the game, and all the experienced players were having nervous breakdowns at the thought of the how many vampires were going to be coming for them once that information got out.

                                                    In a third game I ran the coterie as a Daughter of Cacophony & Toreador mix that was a vampire anime-ish idol band. A player put me up to it, don't judge me . The coterie was mobile going from city to city for their concerts. This meant tons of NPCs in the game, but they would only be there for a week or two before traveling to a new place. Plus given the cutesy vampire theme they more or less could feed live on stage and flaunt many rules because it was all an act. Each city reacted differently to the coterie with different groups being outraged, overjoyed, and others trying to use them for their own plans.

                                                        Originally posted by Aahz View Post
                                                        Still I think it a bit of a different feeling if each member of the Primogen is just speaking for him self and about two other clan members, or if he represents 10 to 20 vampires.
                                                        I never believed that Primogen speak for anyone other than themselves. I dislike the idea (that seems based from LARP more than tabletop materials) that Primogen are essentially clan representatives. They aren't. They are just the powerful vampires in the city that force the Prince and other powerful vampires (the other Primogen) to take heed of their desires. They are not representatives. They are the powerful magnates and great lords that even the king must respect.

                                                        Let's look at Chicago By Night as an example. Despite the Venture, objectively, being the most powerful clan collectively in Chicago, they have no Primogen. Though Ballard schemes to be accepted as one of them. On the other hand there are two Brujah Primogen (Critias and Tyler) and the history section makes it clear that until recently there were three Brujah Primogen (including Procet whom Tyler recently secretly killed). The Malkavians also don't have a Primogen. O'Leary isn't a Primogen though Lodin honors her for starting the Great Chicago Fire that enabled him to seize power. So two clans don't have representatives on the Primogen Council, and one clan has multiple Primogen. Of course, this early on even the idea that there are Primogen advising/controlling Lodin isn't well known and that knowledge is part of the city's secrets.

                                                        Milwaukee By Night has a Council of Elders, but it's not quite the same thing as the Primogen. That Council was originally instituted by Hrothulf to help him run the city. So they were more like a collective Seneschal. After Hrothulf stepped down, the Council of Elders collectively ruled the city and elevate one of their own members as Prince. Since Mark Decker never attends any meeting as the Gangrel representative, there are fierce debates whether to replace him with another, but it doesn't necessarily need to be another Gangrel. The "one council member per clan" is clearly an artifact of local politics.

                                                        In New Orleans By Night, there is a Prince's Council that seems to be equivalent to the Primogen. But there are only four members leaving three clans entirely represented. DC By Night only mentions four Primogen excluding three clans entirely.

                                                        So the early sourcebooks show lots of domains with Primogens that have nothing to do with the clans being represented. It seems to be based more from the original MET books that institutionalized a lot of a domain's offices so players in LARP games had some position they could go after, and turned the office into some kind of clan representative (so they could have another office, being the whip).
