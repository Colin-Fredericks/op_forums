    Somewhat more realistic equipment; Make humans scary.

    01-19-2018, 02:48 PM
    First off, I don't want to bog the game down in realism, but I do think there's elements to improve in terms of weapons and armour, especially the later. I think it happens a bit to often that military and law enforcement are passed off as mooks, and proper equipment/preperation doesn't mean enough. I don't mind that some folks like to make their action about trenchcoats and katanas, world of darkness rolls with a lot of those cinematic wonders, but I think I'd like to ground a few things a little more. At the same time, I'm not sure exactly how i can go about some of the changes I'd like to make. I'd like to be consistent regardless of splat. I think military and law enforcement should be respected enemies when provoked. Your masterful skill with a blade probably isn't more scary than a SWAT team.

    The biggest offender Is armour. If we ignore splat-specific stuff, you've got a 1-5 dice of protection with some dexterity penalty in there. This isn't anything like how modern body armour works. Stuff gets rated for protection against threats. So a kevlar vest providing three dice to protect against weapons that do at least four dice of damage is simply unacceptable, and the problem is only more pronounced as you increase the scale. I get that WoD has a painfully slow combat system and steps should be taken to shorten it, but I'm sure there are other, less terrible methods of doing such a thing.

    Ballistic vests, helmets and limb protection can be rated up to what we'd call "large revolver". Typically there's a tradeoff of comfort/conceal/price. Most cops will go for a vest that protects them from what they expect to be shot with, they don't need absolute protection, they want comfort on long jobs, though they always wear enough to protect from their own sidearm.
    Hard armour typically concerns rifle rounds, though some can go up to big, armour piercing ammunition. This stuff's usually reserved for the front and back of the torso, it's too heavy for headwear/limb wear.
    Stab vests have a horrible rating system that's difficult to understand, but if the average thug has 2/3 physicals, 1 melee, and a knife,dagger or spike that does +1/2, and you're only offering 3 protection, you've got a no-sell.
    As for riot gear, it's absolute garbage for firearms and I wouldn't wear one near a sword. The merit comes from how all-encompassing it is, at the expense of dexterity and perception. Mechanics wise, you've probably got +1 or 2 difficulty for an attacker to get your weakspots/around the armour, but it won't be sufficient for something that does lethal damage.the stab vest you wear under it's still going to be the most protective feature. A full set's comparitively cheap.
    Now my question is such; What's the best way to implement more effective, reasonable armour, balanced around humans but with a mind for vampires, mages, the odd werewolf, that kid who insists she's an evil scottish goblin.
    -Just make new dice pools to reflect protection.
    -Double dicepools if we're talking the correct type of damage (Balistic vest VS bullets but not spear stabs) maybe add 2 dice to things to keep them "safe"
    -Auto-soak and have characters soak the rest as bashing, so long as the damage is within a threshold
    -something else I haven't thought of

    On top of that, conundrums like "what about degradation", where does fortitude and gifts like luna's armour come in, what if my character does a full Ned-Kelly but with potence/glabro/mage or fae-awfulness so they can wear three inches of quality steel around their entire form?!?!

    Second question
    What about firearms at above 8 dice. In the states where most games are played, getting hunting ammo for bigger game isn't that hard, and the military-grade stuff depends on location or conections. I don't particularly know why first teams wouldn't be equiped with crazy calibers to hunt down the lupine menace, nor do I think an assamite sniper would do with anything below "Anti-Material" when targetting some blue-blood. But what's the best way of making such things playable? I noticed in the mage book, a 30. MG does 12 damage, a 50. does 16, and a 30mm round does 14 dice but halves armour. I think that's too simple ("halving" something doesn't sound anything but arbitrary) but maybe there's a level where you just take of soak dice, where they're not going to do more damage, they're just going to go through you more easily. But when do you do that? Do you start replacing damage with no soak at 12, 14, 16? Do you do individual bullets which might be AP or just long and pointy.

    Lastly. What do you think of
    Removing/reducing burst fire/full auto difficulty modifiers with some guns, particularly larger ones where single shot's probably more difficult to hit with than a spray. Or giving individual weapons modifiers
    Reducing the difficulty of shooting from cover (so, largely zero until full cover) especially when using it as something to rest the weapon against.
    some kind of rule relating to using full auto/precise shooting against cover
    Rules for fire resistant gear, stuff that totally blocks out the sun.

    Be correct.

      My first suggestion would be to look at the CofD combat equipment rules and consider porting them over. For weapons you get smaller numbers (weapons provide automatic damage instead of dice of damage so they can largely stick in the 0-5 range, 0 for small weapons that are upgrading bashing to lethal), armor piercing is straightforward, the burst fire rules are simpler (and the penalties can be negated with things like bi-pods or physical mounts), etc. Though the initiative penalties are completely whacked for realism.

      The armor in the CofD is way more robust. It has a general rating which reduces most damage (allows some judgement calls) directly (though mundane armor can't reduce things below 1 bashing, so things never completely slow down), and a ballistic rating that downgrades lethal damage to bashing from bullets. So a kevlar vest that's labelled as 1/3 has 1 general armor which isn't going to to much against a knife, but if you're shot for 4 damage by a gun (a decent hit with a pistol or a graze with a light long arm) you end up taking 3 bashing damage (general armor eats up the worst damage it can first).

      It's not perfect by any means, but seems closer to what you want.

      Another option is to actually go way less realistic first. Do something like Exalted 3e, where there's only three main equipment stat blocks per type. Then you add detail back in with Tags. Make the Tags do whatever you want to help highlight what feels more realistic to you rather than trying to fiddle around with dozens of different variations.

          ​I'm not really answering the question the OP finished with, but I want to come at the starting question from a different angle. Look at it this way: no single human, no matter how well armed and armored, is going to stand a good chance one-on-one against a respectable werewolf or vampire. But to me the real advantage that the humans have is their sheer numbers. Even in the classic horror stories, a monster's worst nightmare is the entire town mobilizing with pitchforks and torches. It's not really the SWAT team that's the problem. It's the human wave waking up to your existence and coming for you that's the problem. Humans are scary in the same way a swarm of angry bees is scary.

                  To amend my previous post a bit with resent things: The weapon and armor tags in the Storypath previews are much better than Exalted 3e's approach, and meant for modern day games anyway.

                  Originally posted by Lian View Post
                  2. Guns shouldn't be more accurate. They should be profoundly less, most bullets fired in the heat of battle.. miss.
                  This is... a very questionable thing.

                  Most bullets fired in the heat of battle in the history of guns in warfare weren't aimed at a specific target. Early firearms were relatively inaccurate, so they relied on volume of fire by infantry rather than aimed shots. During the American Civil War and the introduction of the Minie ball (esp. in combination with the repeating rifle), which drastically increased accuracy over regular ball ammunition, hits in battle went up a huge amount before tactics changed and generals stopped marching their soldiers in infantry lines that could take volley fire, but were decimated by accurate mass rifle fire. Then trench warfare began.

                  Trench warfare was combined with the introduction of truly automatic weapons, and tactics like suppressing fire, strafing, and so on. A machine gun might not be trying to hit anyone, and instead keep infantry pinned during artillery shelling, so each of those bullets is a "miss" even though it's entirely intended.

                  Even as war moved out of the trenches, the tactical use of automatic and mass fire continued to not be focused on maximizing hits per shot because it's not the goal of every weapon.

                  There is a very big difference between, "in the heat of battle the vast majority of bullets don't hit a human target, because most bullets are not aimed at a specific human target," and, "in the heat of battle the vast majority of bullets miss."

                  Of course, more bullets miss than hit in situations where automatic fire, or volume non-automatic fire is used for tactical reasons. But that's better represented by not having 1 attack = 1 bullet, and tracking bullets individually, since combatants frequently fire multiple shots under stress, and some will hit, rather than everyone whiffing most of the time.

                              Armor has never been about being untouchable or removing any danger of being damaged. I haven't really looked at modern armor in these systems in ages but if you want more realism at the expense of more details to keep track of you'd probably do something like turn the first point of damage into bashing or something for armor vs the types of weapons it's effective against. I'd feel reasonably comfortable saying that if PC supernaturals wanted this to apply to them, they wouldn't get any additional processing based on that (ie, no armored vampires turning a point into bashing then halving it into nonexistence) because you're modeling different things doing the same task at that point. Armor makes delicate human anatomy a little less delicate but your vampire was never that delicate to begin with. This sort of realism doesn't really do what you set out to do though, unless you have a human PC in with a bunch of tough supernaturals.

                              The really scary thing about humans is any of them might suddenly pull out a gun, can of mace, a knife, etc. and attack you in small numbers. And if you're facing off against police and stuff, you're going to run into a lot of people. If you get the military seriously involved (ie, they actually deploy to deal with your PCs and the PCs don't run away) there's not generally much reason to even run that combat. SWAT teams can be thought of in the same way. In the real world a significant percentage of local police departments seem to be shopping at the army surplus store and some of them have gear that is very out of scale with their normal policing duties (the stated intent is to combat terrorist insurgencies and the like but sometimes it finds other uses). Some have acquired things like tanks, although the one I heard of wasn't able to handle the upkeep on it. But in the WoD who knows? I wouldn't want to be the overconfident idiot who walked into that meat grinder just to find out.

                              There's basically no supernatural power that lets you scale up the way humans can. You hide what you are, you confront them in small, isolated groups or you die. If they don't kill you, your own kind will just to keep themselves from being hunted or having their prey arm themselves. The most dangerous thing humans bring with them to any confrontation are cellphones, radios, and the ability for a crowd to disperse in different directions and call for help. And if you eliminate all communications in an area, that's noticeable in a different way. Someone might interrupt you before you can clean up a mass disaster.
