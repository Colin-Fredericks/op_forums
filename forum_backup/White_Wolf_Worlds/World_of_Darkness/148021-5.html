            I fail to be surprised when any post about Reckoning turns into a discussion about how Vigil is a better game even though the latter wasn't even mentioned in the OP. We've been hearing that Reckoning sucks by the majority of the community's standards since it was released in late '99. Let's let the now skelefied horse be, hmm? Some people love it. Most seem to hate it. I am one of the former having Reckoning be the game I chose to buy from among the WoD core books available at the time. It wasn't entirely what I expected from the marketing, but it was definitely all kinds of awesome. It let me play a human in a game line where a human was usually just food or a plaything.

            This brings me to the next point that wasn't part of the OP. Are the Imbued human? I think so. However, "We're not a blip on our own monster radar!" is shaky reasoning at best. I believe the question was deliberately left open for debate and we may never have a definitive answer.

            Now to the actual question at hand: Yes, they are all nutty. Every last one of them. By average human thinking standards, they are aberrant and strange in their thinking and worldview. It doesn't matter that the majority of the human race is dead wrong about the existence of monsters and the Hunters are right. Because they are in the minority compared to the consensus, they are insane to think something is fundamentally different from what the vast majority of humanity believes.

            I was able to play a Defender for a few sessions that didn't immediately take out all the "threats" he saw (this is how his Sight operated) because there were just so many of them and he needed to pick his fights. Also, not all of these creatures were posing immediate danger to his charge (the entire female student population past, present, and future of the college he attended), or at least as near as he could tell. It was a mixed game, so he had to play nice and I was easily able to find legitimate story reasons to do so.

            Let me clarify one point in closing: I love Vigil. It delivered on many things I was hoping Reckoning would be. I am very fond of Reckoning because I grew to appreciate what it was instead of what I thought it should have been. Vigil is a different sort of beast and a beautiful one at that. I would gladly play either one. I just am pretty tired of all the hate thrown at my favorite CWoD game.

                Agreed with everything you just said — although I do find it interesting that your chosen “defense” for HtR (which, I understand, wasn’t intended as a defense) is essentially “they’re not all bad.” I’m not so much in the camp of “Vigil is better than Reckoning” as “the Year of the Hunter and related books were more to my liking than Reckoning was”.

                But I do agree that Reckoning had its good points, not the least of which was that the Imbued had at least some protection against the “I win” buttons that had been handed out to so many monsters by that point (Delirium, the Mists, etc.): lacking any powers of his own, the only chance a human has against monsters is to keep his wits about him; so passive “mental tampering” effects are especially damaging as they undermine the hunter’s only means of survival.

                But the OP hit upon my single biggest issue with the Imbued: no matter how much you might say that they’re “still human”, they have powers that are not natural to humanity, and they don’t see the world the way normal humans do (literally even, due to the Sight). To one extent or another, every last one of them is nuts; and they’re no more human than mages are (a point that I’m grateful that Spellbound brought up). That’s why my perspective on Reckoning is that it’s a good line to mine for ideas; but it’s not a game I have any interest in playing anywhere close to as-is.

                My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
