                                Incidentally, because of the age jump, I've always liked IT as a Changeling adventure.


                                Dracula
                                Well, the obvious choice is hunters (mundane or otherwise) going after a Tzimisce who has just moved to London. That gives you the ties to Eastern Europe, why Dracula would be importing dirt . . .

                                However, you could also run Dracula as a situation of gruesome mistaken identity. Throughout the novel, Dracula never does "supernatural" stuff in front of third party witnesses. In fact, the only people who ever see any "vampiric" activity are Harker & Co. Heck, Dracula even walks around London in broad daylight. And really, the whole novel reads like an inquest into why Harker et al felt the need to chase a Transylvanian expatriate across the continent before murdering him in his home. So you could see it as a cautionary tale for other hunters about getting too obsessed with a hunt, and missing clues that your target is just a mundane human

                                The Phantom of the Opera.
                                There's nothing explicitly supernatural about the Phantom, but you could do an interesting version where (instead of being injured), he's embraced by a Nosferatu to preserve his voice. That gives you the hideous appearance, living in the catacombs . . .

                                                        Friday the 13th part one and two
                                                        Part One: Female mage - driven mad by grief over the loss of her son - devotes her life to raising him from the dead, pursuing all sorts of dark power. And then someone decides to reopen the summer camp where he died. In her madness, she can't distinguish the new camp counselors from the ones who let him drown, all those years ago . . . and decides that a little human sacrifice might be just the ticket . . .

                                                        Part Two: Behold the fruits of her labor! Her dark experiments have created her own flawed version of a Bane Mummy. Turns out Jason didn't die of drowning - just suffered massive brain damage. He lived to be a large, shambling hulk of a man before dying of unknown causes (his mother's magic, perhaps? Or maybe outright murder, a desperate attempt to get away from being responsible for him?)

                                                        But with his mother's death, the wards she place on her experiment collapse . . . and the variant Bane Mummy escapes. Still mentally damaged, and having absorbed a lifetime of hatred from her, the Bane Mummy seeks to murder the teens she hated so much. And his Ka - when manifest - appears as young teenage Jason (but only under water - remember, its a flawed experiment).

                                                        And, of course, since he's a mummy (albeit a Bane one), there's no way to truly kill him. No matter how badly his body is damaged, or how many times he's "killed", what's left of his soul will (eventually) be able to repair the damage and bring him back . . .
