    If they have a good idea, allow it.

    Otherwise calmly explain to them how what they want to do wouldn't fit into the theme or the mood of your story. Then talk to them about what they want out of their character and make suggestions...try to find a middle ground.

    Maybe the reason they want to play a garou in a vampire game is they really want to play an outsider, one that doesn't feel like they fit into any modern social structure. Get to what they really want, not just what's on their character sheet.

    At this point, if they still push the issue I'll just say 'no'. I've only had one person let it get to this point and she remained mad at me. I reminded her she's getting worked up and stressed out over "play-pretending to be a vampire". She laughed and it turned out okay.

    Basic people skills 101.

                        Originally posted by etherial View Post
                        Stats exist to empower storytelling, that means that low stats (and failures) empower character failure and high stats (and successes) empower character success, regardless of player capabilities.
                        Yes, this is true, up to a point. That approach can easily be twisted into using stats as a crutch, a substitute for good roleplaying. Just because you generated a character with tons of INT doesn't mean you are a genius, or can even reasonably portray a facsimile of one. In fact, playing a decidedly brilliant character can be pretty hard. Just throwing some bones and saying 'well, I know what to do because I am smart' is not going to cut it. I am not trying to say that to be an acting snob either... if you roll the dice and expect the GM to tell you how to come up with a sagacious plan, you aren't playing your genius of a character, you are just letting him/her be controlled by someone else.
