                Originally posted by quelsec View Post
                Calling water in a solid state as ice would require temperature change.
                Not exactly.

                The state of matter is determined by the bonds between molecules, which is a distinct property from their temperature. Heat is just the most readily available means to change those bonds. We can achieve the same effect with enough pressure, after all.

                During a transition of state, temperature doesn't change, either. The heat is used/produced by the change in state. If you could force the material to assume a solid configuration, it would theoretically retain the new state even if at a distinct temperature.

                                    Originally posted by kalinara View Post
                                    That's an interesting thought. What would a mage observe with matter 1 when water freezes? The actual substance isn't really changing. It's still H2O. The properties that do change are the temperature (which is an outside force acting upon it), or the interaction of its atoms and molecules, which would also, IMO, be more of a Forces thing than a Matter thing.

                                    My own thought is that the mage would get a snapshot of the water as it exists in the state of being water, and water as it exists in the state of being ice. They would be able to tell that, fundamentally, it's the same thing. But what would they observe about the process itself?
                                    What Matter 1, or Life 1, or Forces 1 or whatever sensorial magic tells the Mage is based on his Paradigm and his set of skills.

                                    A Mage with a PhD in chemistry would see Matter in a combination of molecular/ionic/metallic interactions, known substances and their chemical nomenclatures, beyond their physical properties (mass, density, resistance, elasticity and so on).

                                    A pre historic Mage would see things as a matter of comparison with something known, for instance "as strong as rocks but as flexible as leather".

                                    And like already explained by many people above, temperature is NOT what defines the state of matter, it's just one of the contributors. What defines the state of matter is the molecular arrangements of it - which temperature can influence, but pressure also factors in.

                                    So, changing the state of matter is a simple process of changing their molecular interactions.
