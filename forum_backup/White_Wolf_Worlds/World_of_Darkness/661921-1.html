                It depends whether you care about game balance or making the rules mesh. The former is harder, so I try not to worry about it too much.

                If it means that mages are suddenly less powerful and werewolves are suddenly more evenly matched, that isn't a flaw in my eyes.

                The important thing to remember is that PCs should be able to stand up to each other while still shining in their own right. For NPCs it doesn't matter, so you can just do away with their innate countermagick and cinematic soak if it isn't important for a particular encounter.

                Flexibility is key. If your mage is too squishy, give him armour or cool gadgets. If your mortal hunter is underpowered, let him develop True Faith or take a Merit that brings him up to scratch.

                        As I see it making such a system balanced would be near impossible and would only serve to undermine each system. As far as themes go I thing otherworldly horror like the quasi-cthulhuian mythos like in DTF would provide a good middle ground for themes as is allows for moments of personal horror like vampire(ancient gods eating your sanity), the moment of dramatic action like werewolf(fighting beings from beyond), the trans-humanism of mage(studying inhuman knowledge), and the depressive atmosphere of wraith(the slow destructive erosion of the world before your very eye's).

                        The larger problem I can see are the interactions with spirits, as each game has their own interpretation for how they work. In past cross-playthroughs we ruled that spirits have different powers based around where they come from, a high umbral spirit will have powers like that of a mage as the high umbra is a realm of concepts and ideas, the middle umbral spirits have charms and gifts as the middle umbra is made of reflections of our world, the low umbra spirits have powers like wraiths or the thaumaturgy of vampires as they reflect memories of things that have passed, and the deep umbral spirits are much like fallen having sat at the edge of creation being corrupted by the abyss.

                        Otherwise I don't mess with the systems as they are. A werewolf may be stronger than a vampire in straight combat, but that's because vampires aren't Supposed to be in straight combat all the time, they shine most when they manipulate from the shadows. A 1st rank werewolf straight from character creation may be able to take on a master vampire in open combat, but a newly embraced vampire can manipulate a city with blood alone and a matter of weeks what it would take a 5th rank garou with the most socially oriented gifts Years to accomplish.

                        Each system has it's own merits, and using those merit's properly with a group of good roleplayers can make for Delicious conflict and amazing stories. If some players fall behind because they can't use those talents it's on them, not the system. Sure it's not balanced, but nether is real life and WOD works best when it reflects real life.

                            Mechanically, like several others, I borrowed the "key mystical attribute acts as resistance bonus to supernatural powers" and simply house-ruled Blood Potency into VtM (I have some complicated house rules for revamping VtM's Kindred).

                            As far as lore, I simply pick which line is "dominant", and that canon rules over the others. Chiefly, I run either Vampire or Mage. Garou, Wraiths, and Changelings exist, but if there's a dispute between Mage Umbra and Werewolf Umbra, Mage Umbra wins and the Garou Umbra gets either a retcon or a handwave of why things are different.

                            If Vampire is dominant, then the Technocracy is a small but powerful conspiracy that operates behind the bureaucracy of the modern world, while vampire elders hold the immediate power. If Mage is dominant, then the Technocracy is the Secret Government manipulating events, while the vampires are the small but powerful conspiracy that manipulates power indirecty to avoid drawing NWO's attention.

                                    I do that, but in my own revamped "World of Insufficient Illumination" version of the setting. Basically, I tend to run my games with less Lovecraft or Kafka and more Butcher.

                                    In that setting, that Technocracy is what's infiltrated the intelligence apparatus and commercial sectors. The Camarilla is deeply entrenched in the civilian government. Both can manipulate the military when necessary but have to keep direct hands off thanks to a Task Force Valkyrie style group that's been around since World War II that tends to... purge direct infiltrators pretty quickly. Pentex is just a Nephandi corrupted branch of the Syndicate.

                                    Vampires and Werewolves are not at war, but neither do they like each other very much. The Garou divide their time between keeping the spirit world and physical world from corrupting each other until they're ready to re-merge, and stopping their brethren who've lost themselves to the Rage and want to wipe out the modern world.

                                    The Tradition Council remains largely the same, though more unified.

                                    The Abyss has not been breached, instead angelic beings have been cast out of Heaven and are forced to merge with creatures they once thought beneath them. Some are adapting, some are in denial, and others have gone mad, thinking the only way they'll find release is if the world ends, so they're trying to find ways to make it happen sooner rather than later.

                                    Wraith is largely unchanged. Changeling... I feel neither published version works well with my setting, and haven't had the time to come up with something new, so they're strictly NPCs. I tend to run them as powerful spirits that are more humanlike in some aspects but with vastly alien mindsets and persepctives.

                                            Originally posted by Prometheas View Post


                                            Meh, lovecraft for life. :P

                                            I personally try to go with settings where I change as little as possible as to preserve the individuals themes of each book. multi-leveled conspiracies and ancient horrors seem to blend those themes best(though it does mean some stories end up in settings that wouldn't be out of place as a level in Bloodborne.)
                                            I've been playing since '94. I'm bored with the RAW as far as settings go. WoD got more and more oppressive and restrictive with each edition in what Storytellers were "allowed" to do if they wanted to follow the metaplot. There are two types of NPCs, with few exceptions. They're talked up as epic badasses, but with a stat block that's comparatively weak (which works because the advancement system is designed around chronicles that last years in order to see any real advancement). Look no further than the maturation rules in any Vampire edition to see how laughably weak "elders" are. My favorite example, though, are ghouls. A couple splats talk about about how some vampire ghouled his men (exact number unknown, but implied to be a decent sized strike force at bare minimum) before a battle and turned them into an unstoppable force. Yeah, 1 dot of Potence really turns your men into an unholy wrecking crew. And unless this guy was 4th gen and emptied his entire Blood Pool into them, they didn't even have enough in their system to HEAL much less fuel that single dot for any length of time.

                                            The other type are SO POWERFUL THEY SIMPLY WIN!!!! See Antediluvians, Oracles, True Fae, and assorted others. At first that didn't bother me, because as they say "if you stat it, they will kill it." So, if they're so godlike, why isn't the REAL war between them, fighting for ultimate control with starting level PCs as pawns? "Because, they don't get involved!" Why would beings with such power not use it, especially vampires and mages? "Because the rules won't let them!" ... Whatever.

                                            But towards the end of CWoD, I had a realization. The regime that had taken over WW were AFRAID of players. They didn't stat the Epic NPCs, because that would risk players DEFEATING them. Not just slaying them BUT BEATING THEM, which meant they'd have effectively changed the setting in a definitive manner. Can't have that! But then look at the NPCs, so powerful in fluff but pathetic in stat form. Each Edition talked more and more of how this supernatural faction or another couldn't affect REAL change and how all they did was ride human trends and manipulate the masses into passivity. Even the Technocracy got hit with this. They had all the makings of an interesting Alternative History if not in the past, then at least diverging into one, and they refused to use it, bending believabilty as they reached for bigger and bigger excuses for why mages, vampires, and werewolves existed, but had absolutely ZERO impact on the world at large, even from the shadows.

                                            The final piece was the advancement system. I'd always accepted that WoD games were meant to be long-term things, and the system was designed around. But then I noticed how few people ran games that lasted longer than a year (at least in my neck of the woods). None of their characters ever saw a fifth dot in ANYTHING, unless they devoted every drop of XP they got to raising it, and they did that by barely advancing in anything else. The system STRANGLED choice, meaning you min-maxed your character for your desired role at chargen, and then never spent XP except to advance in that role. Any attempt at adding variety or purely "RP" choices was thrown aside, because unless you lucked out and got into a Mega-Campaign, you were never going be much more than what you were at the start.

                                            At the time, I broached this in the old WW forums, and got lambasted for not "Getting it," and "not understanding that being powerful and affecting change goes against the themes of WoD." That's pretty much when I burned WoD's canon and reforged my own. My ultimate vindication was seeing Vampire the Requeim 2nd Edition go for an XP system very similar to the one I came up nearly a decade before. Onyx Path seems to be taking a more moderate stance with the Edition Agnostic 20th line, and I'm VERY curious to see what they do with VtM 4th Edition.

                                                            Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                                            I'll set aside my disagreement on the matter of balance (it is too complex, and not for this thread), and that cWoD reflects real life as much as D&D. The problem isn't balance, but inconsistencies in mechanics that make the rules' interactions too hard to judge in a crossover game.

                                                            By the way, I also modified the XP system to something more like nWoD 2nd Ed. But simply throwing greater amounts of XP also does the trick to some degree. I didn't found my perfect system for any of this, though. Your ideas for some changes are very interesting.
                                                            There is no such thing as "balanced" system, and more writers try to MAKE them balanced by shoving rule after rule down our throats, the less I like the game. It's the province of the STORYTELLER to keep things "balanced" so that the players can enjoy themselves and the narrative stay focused. Take for example, abominations. They made them APPALLINGLY awful and near worthless as even NPCs all so players wouldn't want to play them.

                                                            Me, I chucked the horribly written Abomination and replaced it with something actually TERRIFYING, that both sides would want dead just for their own peace of mind. And when a player tries to hand me a background to be one, I laugh, pat them on the head, tell them "nice try, now go make a real character" and move on. If someone can't control their players without having a chapter and paragraph verse to reference to back up their rulings, then they have no business being behind the screen.
