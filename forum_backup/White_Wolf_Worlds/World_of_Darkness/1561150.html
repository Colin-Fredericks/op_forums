    Weird Cities

    06-30-2026, 11:48 PM
    I'm making experiments right now and looking for opinions and experiences with VtM being run on a Weird City.

    Let me explain: a Weird City isn't just a place with weird stuff happening and shady magical denizens, in the WoD this is just a city.

    A Weird City is one where at least one strange phenomenon is so prevalent in regular experience that inhabitants take it as normal and carry on, unaware their neck of the woods is a bizarre anomaly.

    My current ideas were inspired by "Cassilda's Song" in The King in Yellow, Act 1, Scene 2, the pseudo-play from the famous anthology:

    - The sunset in the nearby lake is a local pride. The still water is a perfect mirror, multiplying light and colors. But the lake doesn't reflect the sun in the sky: it reflects two suns, and neither looks like ours. This is not an isolated location, it's one of the hottest points in the area.

    - No one in the city ever understood why people in the outside talk about moon phases. The Moon is the Moon. It's always there, full glory, night or day, clear sky or rain. Its image pierces the clouds, and you can always find it quickly. It does have a tendency to change colors and surface, but not "phase", that's clearly nonsense.

    - At night the stars are always visible. It's too rare for clouds to stay there in mass after sunset. More impressive, though, is that the sky seems impervious to the notion of light pollution. It's as clear and full of stars today as it was two hundred years ago, which already was more than a desert. Inhabitants just got used to the most glorious night vista.

    - it's the most popular tune in town. Everyone knows it, everyone likes it. It has no lyrics, but is easy to slap anything in, so people sing all kinds of shit in it. Others just hums it. Its origin isn't a secret: just look to the sky, find the right patch, and stare the bright star Aldebaran, you will hear it clear as no instrument can be.

    - For some reason, in the 90s a documentary was shot in the sewers. No one's sure what it was about, though most say it was about the problem with snapping turtles. The film was never released, but the VHS tapes still make the rounds. Kids trade tapes and trivia as some local collectible, everyone saw at least one take and has theories about the strange ruins beneath the pipes. Since 2003 the city has an ongoing public program of distribution of free VCRs to guarantee the new technologies won't eliminate this fine culture from their midst.

    - On most of the world Obtenebration is known and feared as an alien power that draws pure malice from another dimension, its mere touch provoking unease, fear, even panic. Here it is one of the most familiar sensations anyone can describe, as playing with or being caressed by the tendrils of darkness from the Earth is an universal childhood experience, always linked to safe, happy places, like grandparents house. Your typical inhabitant finds Obtenebration cozy and warm, even soothing near the horrors of undeath. Local Lasombra use it as a subtle manipulation tool in a way impossible elsewhere, and question why their clan mates abhor do much the natives of this city.

          In my concept, what makes the Weird City relevant is the shock of absurd mundanity with which the phenomena selected is treated by locals. It isn't a secret, it isn't even strange to them, it's just life as normal.

          Below was lightly inspired by the description of Hanuda Village as having a tin mine with extra properties, but everything else I just made up:

          - occultists and alchemists from all around the world seek the materials from this place (they may mine some common metal like tin or aluminum, or source stone, low value gems, wood, any relatively cheap material) because of their strange properties when exposed to supernatural forces.

          Natives are aware of those properties but think of them just as minor quirks. People actively use them as entertainment and toys:
          -- children have dolls that talk in unknown languages with no sound device installed,
          -- objects resting in tables of this material can move of their own accord,
          -- electronic devices are preternaturally durable, but have a tendency to change owners,
          -- Blood Sorcery reacts strangely. Here, if this material is hit by magic or used in a ritual, the reaction summons a strange entity: it has the form of a normal animal found nearby the site, but its eyes are glowing, moving mandalas. It's stats are the same as the animal + 3× Level of the magic as extras to distribute among traits (any listed trait), and the same amount of dots in Disciplines. The animal won't stay close for long, but long term behavior is unpredictable, some serving today as pets to locals.
          SPECIAL: This summoning is a local thing. The material always reacts to blood magic, but the effect changes with location and is usually way less potent.

              I had a whole idea about a city where there's a water source (not the main one) that heals people, reduces recovery time from blood loss and reanimates corpses, but the forum ate it. So I'll try to sum up the important parts:

              - Mayors love it. It solves the health indexes at almost no cost, so there's constant investment and propaganda for people to drink it.

              - Local hospital hates it. Hospitalization can be ditched on most cases that would require it. It also isn't approved by national authorities and may have long term deleterious effects. Not a major thing, statistics without gameplay effect, maybe slightly higher cancer and hypertension rates.

              - The primary water source for this thing is near to an old mine. The mine can be anything but iron. The healing water looks like rust.

              - Bodies soaked in it animate. They have their old memories, but no real intelligence and pose no danger, they're just annoying. The usual way to get rid of them is beheading and drying the head from the healing water, then waterproof it before burying again.

              - Animated corpses are called Pseudo-Living. They are somehow fertile and can impregnate or give birth. Including with low-gen vampires. That's gross for both societies, but necrophilia is a thing, so that's that.

              -- For those who know the legends, yes, they're the actual original Vampyr: dumb and horny.

              - mortals drinking it heal as if they just received medical treatment at 3 successes by someone with Medicine 4, and will continue to do so as if properly hospitalized. Also recover iron levels 10× faster.

              - drinking from Pseudo-Living (they're easy to mistake for drunkards) tastes bad and drugs a vampire. Not dangerous per se, but exploitable. Drinking healing water tastes like water and is not nourishing, but don't have to be thrown up.

              - Healing water let you learn a Discipline at the ST discretion. The ST may let you pay as one clan as if it had awakened a fourth one.

              - Babies from Pseudo-Living are probably Dhampyr.

                      What Yharnam inspired:

                      - Locals are from a very particular ethnicity unique to the area. They don't shy from unions with foreigners, but to date their own particular traces seem remarkably resilient on children born here. Their eyes are particularly sunken with bigger, black irises and pupils, and the skin has paper-white "freckles" that give a palid impression from afar. Hair also whitens very early in life, and most reach teenage years already with a pattern of white streaks or dispersed strands that will then whiten more very slowly.
                      - Otherwise they're a regular native ethnicity, no greater distinction from nearby peoples. In the US it means Native American, they're only white if this city is in Europe, and "white" may be a misnomer in the Mediterranean.

                      What set them apart more strongly from other people, though, is how their bodies processes ingested blood, causing an euphoria similar to the effects of alcohol, but described by them as superior (if that's because it's more intense, or that they're too resistant to alcohol to know better, is up in the air). For that reason, blood is considered the local specialty for drinks that usually only locals enjoy.

                      The blood doesn't have to be human, mind you, or in great quantities. Like other people a local will choke on anything beyond a spoon of human blood due to clotting. Animal blood depends. They usually mix the blood on some other drink, with the caveat it can't be alcoholic or too hot, as those kill the properties they seek. Human blood is a delicacy, but animal is the standard fare.

                      Hospitals drain the dead of blood for the family at a fee, and they can then sell it. It's the only legal way to obtain human blood for drink, but a black market exists, surely, mostly exploiting poor donors and sometimes draining their own dead. It's not common for anyone to kill just for blood, as it's rarely needed to add to old regular urban violence.

                      Another quirk of locals is their burial practices. They have longer than normal mourning periods before the final destination, staying a few more days than usual with the drained dead, and after it they build a tombstone in the middle of the city, near the house or at any other place the dead liked, instead of at usual memorial places like where the body is buried.

                      What's curious is what happens when a local is turned into a Ghoul:
                      - They start with two Level 1 Discipline Powers. The Domitor must both have dots in the Disciplines and have them/it as in-clan. They must have one of the powers, but the other can be different.
                      - They pay the same cost for Level 1 powers as Vampires pay for Level 1 Discipline dots. They have the same in-Clans as their first Domitor.
                      - They can make Rouse Checks after drinking human blood. Instead of having Hunger score, they lose this ability when they fail the roll, until they drink more human blood.
                      - They can use the Rouse Check for Mending and Disciplines, but not for Blood Surge.
                      - They remain as Ghouls for 3 months, not 1.
                      - They suffer from an effect similar to Hopeless Addiction after a month without Vampire blood, although a Rouse Check can stave it off for a day.

                          What Ma'Habre inspired:

                          - Located in southern Türkiye, the city is stubbornly ancient. Stubborn as in it refuses to appear otherwise. Modern building materials and architecture just seem to refuse to work here, so the whole city is built on stone, mud bricks and bitumen, reinforced with reeds in the material. Different districts have different reception for "newer" materials, and few places let the buildings be reinforced with steel rebar.

                          It's limited to materials, not techniques. It sits in the forefront of engineering, developing ever new solutions to its curious constraints. Today they're most sought for ancient reconstruction, areas under local/climate constraints and low carbon footprint. The city is a horizontal sprawl with few buildings taller than 6 stories, efficient public transport and aggressively strict urban planning to stay functional in its situation.

                          Another strong sector is tech. The construction makes centralization a no-go for anything in the city: from water distribution to internet access, vital infrastructure has to be local and then network. Specialized wiring is quickly giving way to wireless decentralized systems, and this resulted in heavy investment in tech that could sidestep regular central systems dependency. Specialized software for engineering and material analysis is also a forte, as is local AI development now (no big cloud servers, focus on local deployment).

                          It should be impossible, of course. Even with extreme specialization and planning, this thing shouldn't be scalable to support more than 100k inhabitants at most. But things here just seem to be slightly (or not so slightly) out of the usual parameters. Bricks that support 20% more weight than they should, soil that drains water with unusual efficiency, roads that resist that much longer before they need repair. The effect is far from making it easy, but definitely makes it feasible, and the engineers know that.

                          Normal buildings are goners. Erect a wall with modern materials and in 24 hours it will be visibly sinking and inclined. A whole house will be completely engulfed in two weeks, not a trace of it left above ground. The mud brick ones simply accommodate and stay, firmer than the composition allows.

                          Most of it is just the land itself, something in the stone, the clay, the reeds and the bitumen. But there is something else in the many building rules. Every wall in the city bears inscriptions. No one's sure about the language and most people don't care. It's probably just a made-up code by a previous administration. But architects keep painstakingly engraving each finalized project and restoration (mud brick requires regular restoration), and there are rules, so many rules governing what exactly to inscribe. What, after all, is written in the walls?

                          Vampires feel more active in the city. It also has many covered areas and buildings with large internal courtyards where people meet. Even feeding feels more stimulating, and one who just fed is more dangerous than usual:

                          -- Vampires wake 1h before sunset and sleep 1h after sunrise. Rolls to stay awake get -1 diff.
                          -- Inside buildings or under cover is always considered safe from the sun, no matter how much light actually enters.
                          -- When a Vampire drinks at least 3 Blood Points / Slakes 2 Hunger in a single scene, they get pumped up: for the rest of the scene, they have all the benefits of -1 Generation / +1 Blood Potency (no drawbacks).

                          The city sits in a platform above surrounding terrain, made of stone and the ruins of previous buildings. Many, many previous buildings. At least as far back as 3 thousands years ago. It has also an abnormal tendency to attract those interested in or carrying old fragments of vampire lore, especially about early days. From Nod to Setite myths, and even the ancestral origin myths from the Laibon and from Mongol Kindred. Some say that's the city trying to gather its own history.

                                                      Unfortunately, my only experience has been with the 2nd edition, which I sadly haven't had the chance to play since about 20 years ago. I've heard good reviews about the new 3rd edition, but haven't really had the time to investigate it thoroughly.

                                                      And the gun law thing is probably because during the 1990s (especially in 1992 when the original edition was written), the majority of English language RPGs were made by American companies for American audiences, which meant that private ownership of non-military firearms was considered the status quo and it was probably necessary to remind readers that an island nation in the Mediterranean (or the Caribbean if you're playing the French version) would do things differently. (For much of the English speaking world, the right to own weapons for self-defense goes back to the 1689 Declaration of Rights by English Parliament; while much of the Anglosphere has moved away from that, especially in the past few decades, the US Constitution is, by design, more difficult to alter.)
