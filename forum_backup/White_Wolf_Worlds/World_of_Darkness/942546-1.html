    I think it shouldn't be. Throwing a punch at a moving target is always brawl, never martial arts. I'm of the opinion that fighting should be houseruled. The WoD combat system is one of way too many dice and slow turns, but the d10 system so much better than rolling one dice for complicated manovuers.

    The problem is people need to know the complex manovuers or at least have some creativity/ sense of how to fight. problem with one of my storytellers/players is that I've been in a few fights and trained in a few styles. I'm not a pro or anything really good, i just did this stuff when I was a kid/Teen- he's a couch potato who watches WWE.

    When I'm storyteller, I tend to tweak the combat rules to make them more 'real'. IE holding the enemy is a contested action and the dice you get over the enemy dictates an advantage you get in further melee actions. Hitting certain places affects the difficulty of damage/soak rolls. I add/takeaway dice/difficulty for a bunch of little things to compensate for everything so it all makes sense. Problem is half my players just wanna 'claw it'. I suppose you need people with an interest in how things work, but it's not exactly like players just wanting to claw it have taken away from anything.

    He relies on the rulebook, and things go awry if they're not in the rules. Wrestling moves (many of which are designed to not be as painful as they look, in the event they look painful) are the only thing you'll get more elaborate than what's in the books, and they'l be fluff rather than mechanics. Hitting someone in the right place usualy won't do something special. most troubling is that taking the trouble to shoot someone in the head with a gun is +2 difficulty to hit, with a chance to stun them (dictated by a d2). It's never really worth it unless you're fighting a vampire.


    But, a martial arts skill... I feel like a martial arts skill is more of a 'look at these moves i know'. I'd do something like
    Here's a few moves, difficulty 9.
    Learn the martial arts skill to reduce their difficulty by up to three.

        I love, "you can block a blade by not blocking the blade," as a counter.

        1) Combat training is not "Brawl" training, because as I've mentioned, Athletics/Dodge at a minimum are parts of basic unarmed training as well. Just like going to a four year university and majoring in a science degree isn't just getting your dots in Science, but also Academics at a minimum (if not lots of other things depending on your minor, electives, and clubs).

        2) I already addressed how the system models trying to stop a melee or firearms attack with Brawl. It just isn't with the Block maneuver. You really need to stop conflating jargon (the specific use of a word in a specific context) with general use. The Block maneuver does not cover everything that in common use might be called a "block." I can "block" someone trying to punch me with a staff, but in the system that's the Parry maneuver, not the Block maneuver. If you want to stop someone with a knife or a pistol, and you're unarmed, you don't use the Block maneuver, even if you want to describe a called shot to their arm an attempt to "block" their strike. The mechanical significance of this though, is that you can't do this as a default defensive action (so you can't abort to it), which means having Initiative is really important to trying anti-weapon unarmed tactics; which makes complete sense.

        These might not be unrelated; especially if you modify difficulties on things like damage/soak.

        This adds even more variables to combat, which leads to decision paralysis, which can be avoided by just skipping any non-basic combat options.

        I think if you want to encourage more that, "I claw it," it needs to be easier to know why to try other options. The current system is bad enough. Most players don't know if trading +1 diff for +1 damage is worth it or not. The cWOD combat system would benefit more from a simple maneuver creation system instead of pages of maneuvers. Explaining how to build maneuvers also helps explain when to use them.

                    Blocking a weapon has nothing to do with Dodge/Athleticism. It has everything to do with your skill in Brawl because you use many of the same techniques to defend against a weapon that you would use to defend against a punch or kick. The difference is that failure is much, much more painful.

                    Most combat styles teach weapon blocking and disarming techniques to allow their students to gain access to the weapon of the opponent. They usually consist of step in, block the weapon by deflecting the angle of attack with your hands, grab the wrists, and disarm the opponent (usually by breaking the wrists). You can then pick up the weapon for your own use or proceed to destroy the formerly armed opponent. The schools I have been associated with usually preferred the latter, as there was no guarantee that they did not have a second weapon. Destroying a person usually focuses on crippling the second arm and one leg, generally taking two more attacks, rather than more serious damage because someone with just one good limb was not a threat and it takes a really long time to beat someone into Unconscious, much longer than suggested by WoD.

                            Originally posted by Aya Tari View Post
                            Blocking a weapon has nothing to do with Dodge/Athleticism.
                            Well... it does. As you illustrate one of the first steps is positioning and foot-work, which are covered by Dodge and/or Athletics depending on the edition/book we're using.

                            Ok, I'm going to break this down in mechanical terms:

                            They usually consist of step in,...
                            Either you've won Initiative and are using your reflexive movement, or you lost Initiative and are using the Evasive Action maneuver (Athletics!) to position yourself advantageously for your following move (Evasive Action isn't just avoiding a blow, but doing so in a fashion that nets you a mechanical benefit for a follow up counter if you avoid the attack completely).

                            block the weapon by deflecting the angle of attack with your hands, grab the wrists,
                            Initiate a grapple, denying the opponent the ability to attack with their weapon unless they can out grapple you as well.

                            and disarm the opponent (usually by breaking the wrists).
                            Either use the disarm option in a grapple, take control of the weapons (see next section), or make a called shot in the grapple against their arm/wrist to do enough damage to make them drop the weapon anyway.

                            You can then pick up the weapon for your own use or proceed to destroy the formerly armed opponent.
                            Either using the weapon after taking control over it, or not, use the grappling option to damage them until you're satisfied they can't fight back.

                            The schools I have been associated with usually preferred the latter, as there was no guarantee that they did not have a second weapon. Destroying a person usually focuses on crippling the second arm and one leg, generally taking two more attacks, rather than more serious damage because someone with just one good limb was not a threat and it takes a really long time to beat someone into Unconscious, much longer than suggested by WoD.
                            The idea that two broken wrists/arms and a broken leg isn't "serious" damage on par with being beaten unconscious is... a bit of a questionable assumption. But the system doesn't model damage in such fine detail anyway. Besides putting most people in the Crippled damage level (-5 to their actions) will either net them zero dice and auto-fail any attacks (as well as move so slow you can easily out run them if they're using hand-to-hand weapons), or a small enough dice pool finishing the job if they keep fighting shouldn't be a problem. And this is technically easier than putting them into Incapacitated - which would be beating them unconscious - since it takes less damage.

                            And, again, you don't have to think of this as the optimal way of handling it in the system. The core point I'm making here is that the self-defense/combat tactics you're talking about are in the system, even if they're not in the "Block" maneuver. They're not impossible to do RAW.
