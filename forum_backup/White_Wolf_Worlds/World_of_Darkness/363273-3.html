                            I got Kindred of the East last year, and even though their culture is VERY ritual heavy (especially with their war-making), the Kuei-Jin seem to be a fascinating people. Sadly though, I find that the Year of the Lotus book-line is incomplete. To explain, let's see where every Supernatural race is technically:

                            Vampire: Again Kindred of the East, check.

                            Werewolf: Hengeyokai book, check.

                            Mage: I know there's the Akashic Brotherhood, but I wonder if it's the ONLY tradition in the Middle Kingdom. I know, from a book on Chinese Fairy Tales, that there were two competing camps of thought: Confucianism and Taoism. Which school of thought is the Akashic Brotherhood? If that Tradition is one of these schools, then perhaps there should be a Tradition that reflects the other school, as a sort of rival to the Brotherhood. Also, are there any Eastern orientated Traditions that are exclusive to Asia? Ooo, how about a Tradition that takes advantage of ninja mysticism? It could be centered in the Inu(?) Prefecture, where it's claimed to be the birthplace of ninjas.
                            Also, is Awakened culture in the Middle Kingdom different from Europe and America? How? Please consider making a Mages of the East book.

                            Wraith: Thanks to the overwhelming response for Wraith20, the corebook will talk about the Jade Kingdom. Will everyone be okay with what's in the corebook or should Wraith get it's own Lotus book?

                            Changeling: As far as I know, there's nothing about Changelings in the Middle Kingdom. Are their Asian-centric kiths or is Dreaming strictly Western only? If you can, consider making a Changelings of the East book for Dreaming fans, with also new Houses if allowed.

                            Hunter: As far as I know, we have Demon Hunter X and Strike Team Zero, though I wonder about something. Are there any Imbued in Asia and if so what do they do for their Hunts? Are there any new Creeds that developed in the Middle Kingdom? I think there should be a Lotus book about Reckoning-type Hunters in Asia and how they Hunt there, What does everyone think?

                            Mummy: The players guide on The Ten Thousand Immortals is great and detailed. I think that their culture should be expanded upon in their own Lotus book to make them as fulfilling as the Amenti (since the Middle Kingdom is the Asian Mummies territory). Are Shinto and Buddhist temples holy places to where the TTIs recuperate? What is their footprint in Asian history? What is their relations between the Kuei-Jin and the other shen?

                            Demon: Asian Demons of the Yama Kings have been detailed in the book: The Ten-Thousand Hells. Is this book the Demon Lotus book? If not, might you make one to complete this order?

                            The completion of the Lotus books is the only thing that I want to happen because it seems like that some game-lines became restricted geography-wise due to the classic WoD ending like that. Can Onyx Path make this happen in the future?

                            Thanks.

                                                            JackalopeVegas, the problem with Hunter and Demon is that they came after the Year of The Lotus was over. They didn't exist when the project was running so they never got any coverage. Hunter would probably work mostly as is, with the possibility of a few extra creeds, but to be honest I don't really miss the Hunters all that much.

                                                            Demon on the other hand does not work with KotE. In general the Demon line took a huge dump on all the metaphysics and backgrounds of all the other games (it's quite poor form to build up your heat by making everyone else seem like clueless idiots) and did its best bot to integrate peacefully with the other lines - which is quite contrary to the concept of Kindred of the East, which was made as a rather hollistic WoD where the factions interact and cooexist much more then in the vanilla lines. The Demons of eastern WoD are the Kuei Jin, the Akuma, the Bakemono, The Spirit servants of the Yama Kings, Banes and the Yama Kings themselves.

                                                            Dragons of the East is a pretty cool book, and it covers a lot of the things you wanted:
                                                            - The Five Metal Dragons - these guys are essentially a whole different paradigm of the Technocracy with a much more hollistic approach to Ascension which treats the Western Technocrats as their slightly stupid cousins. Also, they're quite aware they are doing magic.
                                                            - Blind tunnel dwelling shamans of Vietnam
                                                            - highly ritualistic shamans of Korea
                                                            - The Dragon Wizards - Wu Lung
                                                            - Akashic Brotherhood
                                                            - Wu Keng - while these folks get a lot of bad rap, I really like them as antagonists, and even potentional PC's. They were given quite a bit of depth in this book and made quite a bit more even in their presentation. They are essentially peasant mages who as an act of desperation offered themselves to Demons so they could protect their own people. The end result is that they are men trained from childhood to act and dress as women so they could be the perfect brides to demons. They have some pretty cool visuals to their spells and have to edge the border between Infernal servitude and protecting their own people. One of the plots introduced here is that the demons have been lying to them all along and that they need but a little push to brake free
                                                            - At least three different interpretations of what the ninjas are and are not, and one Craft that's essentially the modern ninja - Go Kamisori Gama

                                                            What doesn't kill you, makes you... stranger.
