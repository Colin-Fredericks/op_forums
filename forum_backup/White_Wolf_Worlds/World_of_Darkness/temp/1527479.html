      I don't think there's much written about it. Even the 1995 sourcebook The Inquisition lacks a section on True Faith versus Changelings despite addressing the other four splats (I assume Changeling hadn't been published yet). So you'll need to decide yourself.

      Folklore does offer some suggestions. Many fairies don't like church bells and will flee from them. Baptism of newborns will prevent changelings. Pages from the Bible, making the sign of the cross, and invoking the saints are often just as effective as iron. Some fairies express an interest in becoming Christian, or worry that they cannot have eternal life like Christian humans because they are fairy. But how do you want to translate that into the game? Just substitute fairy for vampire in the True Faith rules? Make it an intrinsic flaw like iron that requires a Merit to negate? Make it available as a Flaw? Treat is as some kind of Banality? Something else? Different STs will take different tacks depending on their own understanding and bias.

                  The Echoes flaw in the C20 book has some examples of what Black Fox describes being put into mechanical terms re: effects of interaction with church symbols and the like.

                  I do think it would be worth further examination as a subject because it is an interesting field, interactions between Fey/changelings and True Faith type things. As noted there is plenty of European folklore that has to do with the fair folk being averse to the sign of the cross, Bible, church bells, etc. The stated reasons for this, either Watsonian or Doyalist, varies. There’s an old idea that the fair folk are the angels who weren’t bad enough to be condemned to Hell during Satan’s Fall but weren’t good enough to be allowed to stay in Heaven either, which might suggest why they react the way they do to symbols of the faith. Other researchers have likened it to something similar to how iron became associated with anti-Fey qualities; the hypothesis being that oral culture remembered the use of Roman/Christian iron to destroy old Druidic/pre-Christian religious and spiritual sites and that iron-as-modernization became anathema to the fair folk as they became associated with The Past™️. One could imagine then that Christianization could play a similar role; the force of the Present (Christianity in Medieval/Renaissance Europe) being used to overcome the (Pagan) Past.

                  Other subjects of interest might also be the somewhat fluid boundaries between what is imagined as Fey or as Demonic. For instance, it wasn’t unusual for saints in folklore to have power over demons (after all, King Solomon did it), yet sometimes these relationships would be recontextualized as saints with Fey type beings instead for any number of reasons (for example: St. Nicholas commanded demonic spirits, Santa Claus has elven helpers). Also to consider was the notion that Fey spirits could hypothetically become human through marriage/union with human beings (see certain genres of Animal Bride stories or Paracelsus’ warning that many water-nymphs tempt Christian men into marriage in order to gain a soul before abandoning them). I’m less familiar with Islamic or Buddhist folklore traditions but I believe they have similar phenomena of holy people commanding/exorcising nature spirits/tricksters/demons; so I’d also be curious to see what role they could play into a larger discussion on this subject.

                                              In Dark Ages: Fae, the spread of the religion of the "White Christ", and in particular, individuals with True Faith, are the major existential threat* that the Fae are suddenly waking up to after having been off doing their own thing for a few centuries. For example, Changeling babies who are baptized never grow up to be Fae.

                                              Faith can trigger Echoes (personal rather than kith-bases Frailties) which cause any number of unpleasant effects from mild inconvenience up through bodily immolation. Interestingly for this conversation, becoming Locked (we'd say Undone) is never one of them, but becoming immediately Lost (i.e. Bedlam) is one of the most severe. So in that sense, True Faith is more like a very powerful, alien Art than a force of Banality.

                                              In the Autumn World, Unleashing or otherwise Invoking the Wyrd causes a reflexive Banality backlash. So I think it would be generally sensible if "attacking" a Changeling with True Faith basically played out like forcing the fae to Unleash involuntarily, and suffer the consequences?

                                              *My (not-inconsistent-with-the-text) headcanon is that Christianity is different and scary because of the paradigm-for-Sleepers doctrine of "salvation through faith alone", but I'm still a Mage guy at heart.

                                                      The mention of Mage gives me a notion of how one could potentially flesh out the use of Faith for their chronicles by pulling slightly from Mage and having either formal or informal ‘resonance’ tags associated with traits like Faith that would color how the trait interacts with Changelings.

                                                      People have alluded to already how a 1-size-fits-all approach to Faith doesn’t really suit incorporating the various ways that Faith (whether Christian, Muslim, Buddhist, etc.) effects Fey or Trickster I’m in Trickster /Natural Spirits in folklore. One way of handling this could be to choose the elements that best suit the themes I of your chronicle and use those primarily for that chronicle.

                                                      But on the other hand, going more off of a resonance like system, I would imagine something like:

                                                      A Changeling encountering someone whose Faith is associated with their role as a Spiritual Warrior, whether the pagan-hunters of Antiquity or à contemporary inquisitor, would be effected by their Faith in the typical fashion. That person’s Faith attribute/True Faith could be used to represent their capacity for undoing chimerical effects, undoing enchantment, and even ‘suppressing the Fey’ (putting Changelings into a quasi-undone state; whether or not this is banality proper though is potentially up for debate.

                                                      On the other extreme however, someone whose Faith is more akin to a Folk Mystic or Cunning Midwife, whether in their classical manifestations or as a contemporary reconstruction, might affect Changelings differently. They might be able to use their Faith to undo malignant chimerical effects but also empower benevolent chimerical effects. I can even imagine a category of Saint-figures whose Faith might be able to compel the Fey to a certain extent or even provide them a sort of ‘Adoption’ status. Perhaps those Fey spirits who, for example, consented to undergo a Christian baptism undertaken by a Saint-figure would be able to resist Faith-based banality/undoing but would receive a number of Oaths/Bans suited to the tenets of the Saint’s Faith: for instance they might be forbidden from using their malignant powers against any Christian child or from tampering with a church’s threshold or what have you.

                                                      This could potentially be an interesting theme to add to the history of the Sundering/Shattering.
