    The Greastest Horror in Changeling

    06-12-2015, 11:33 AM
    The thing that gave Changeling it's greatest force of Horror were the Sidhe. The game showed how irresponsible dreams create horror, misery, injustice, and cruelty. Because the Sidhe were dreams of power, the power of beauty, the power of wealth, the power of social prestige, power without responsibility or compassion, they were lovely monsters.

    Remember the Sidhe have no idea that they're being selfish. Much like the nobles of Europe and other lands they can't imagine why people don't just obey them, and see those who say no or simply don't want to be bothered as evil or mad. Watching the film Savages, the Selma Hyack character was a perfect sidhe noble. She demands that the protagonists give up their freedom and serve her as expendable pawns in an organization that will look down on them as mere scum. She is upset that they don't simply throw their personal freedom and security away for her whim and is physically and psychologically brutal to them for daring to resist. Her sevant, played by Benicio del Toro, dismisses the protagonists as savages for not following his prudish and brutal code, but sees raping O as proof of his manliness. A totally sidhe style of behavior.

    The sidhe see themselves as being like the French nobles in The Scarlet Primpernell. Victims desperately fighting for survival. But the Sidhe behave more like the Count de Evermont in Tale of Two Cities. This comes from the simple fact that the sidhe can't form much real sympathy with anyone who isn't a sidhe. The problem of the Norse Sidhe (must look up the name) is the extreme, but no sidhe on record really gets a non-sidhe.

      While certain sidhe could definitely be horrific, I'd hardly call them the central horror of Changeling. The true darkness of Changeling is that no matter what you do, no matter what you accomplish, you are doomed forget it and you're true nature. No one will ever know what kind of life you lived.

      It's actually something that hits me a lot harder these days than when I was playing the game back in its heyday. The past several years I've had to watch as Alzheimer's has slowly taken my grandmother. Now days, her body is there, but she just isn't. Believe me, that's some gut wrenching, horrific stuff, and it's the fate of every single changeling. The lucky ones are the who go out in a blaze of glory fighting some monster or an evil sidhe ruler.

                                  06-15-2015, 02:44 AM
                                  Originally posted by Astromancer View Post
                                  Remember , the Dreaming favors the Shide. Their dreams, their myths, their whims, devour your life and achievement. It's like being the kid who earned valedictorian finding everybody talking about the Captain of the football team getting a scholarship to a bigger school than the valedictorian could ever hope for. . .
                                  That's more the fate of enchanted humans, actually. Vulnerable to all of the Dreaming's dangers, but without a drop of magic to protect themselves. Their noses pressed against the window, able to see the "cool kid's party" . . .but never able to go in and have fun themselves.

                                  In many ways Changeling: The Dreaming is about creative kids in high School. The Shide are the champions of Banality.
                                  You're half right. The sidhe aren't champions of Banality - they're fae born of human stories about knights and royalty. The evil usurper who murders the rightful king, and institutes a horrific reign of terror and oppression. The rightful heir who overthrows him, with the aid of a plucky band of misfits. A couple who fall madly in love, but cannot be together, because they belong to two warring noble houses (a la Romeo and Juliet). . . These are all examples of sidhe. And not of that's banal.

                                  But you are right about the "creative kids in high school" idea. They may have a world-changing novel in them, or a fantastic new style of painting. But most of them will never get a chance to explore their talents. Or their work will be a commercial failure, and they'll have to restock shelves for Walmart, just to pay the bills. Banality isn't supernatural evil. Dark wizards sacrificing virgins, dark knights tormenting peasants, ogres eating children . . that's all right out of myths and legends - and thus part of the Dreaming.

                                  Banality . . . Banality is the dead-eyed looked of a bureaucrat who hates their job, and is just going through the motions, calling "next" as they work through the line of applicants. Banality is staying in your hometown, and becoming an accountant, rather than chase your dream of being a rock star (or actress, or astronaut). Shelving that amazing idea, because its "just not practical". Its mindless routine, conformity, drudgery. Its losing yourself in the cycle of paying bills, holding down a 9-to-5, and making sure the kids get to their dance classes and basketball practices. Of letting yourself become just a cog in the machine, when you could have been "the one".

                                  So, just as most of the creative kids in high school will never be able to realize their dreams, finding something more "practical" . . .and maybe accepting a half-assessed substitute (singing in the church choir, or being in community theater), most changelings will fail, too. For every changeling who manages to find enough glamour to hold on to themselves indefinitely, to stay young, beautiful and magical forever, a thousand are going to grow old and forget.

                                  And most fall hard. Spending your life slaying dragons and rescuing princesses doesn't exactly bring in the big bucks. Nor does it look good on a modern resume. Many end up on the streets, or in insane asylums. And a changeling consumed by Banality can't even hold to the memories of their adventures - in fact, many come to look back on their youthful "goofing off" as irresponsible . . .even with some loathing.

                                  That's the horror of Changeling. To have actually ridden unicorns in a faerie forest, and seen the majesty of a flight of dragons rage across the sky . . . only to grow old, and believe that none of it ever actually happened. And worse, to know that one day - if you live long enough - you'll be the old geezer yelling at kids to stop fantasizing about such foolish things.
