                                                            Holden posted some great stuff over on RPG.net that he would like to see in C20. I'm reposting it here because it's just too good not to share:

                                                            Carl Sagan ought to be a Dreamer, yes. By contrast, I imagine it would be acutely painful to share an elevator ride with Christopher Hitchens. Doubly so if you're a changeling.

                                                            Given my druthers, I would rather equate Banality with apathy and reductive cynicism rather than simple rationality, and certainly not with "science." One of the Kiths are a bunch of mad engineers, for Chrissakes.
                                                            It was trying, but I think it's safe to say that Changeling was a bit of a mess, and yawed around missing a lot of what it was swinging for much of the time. (I'm thinking specifically of the lead-in for Noblesse Oblige, where the narrator shudders in revulsion at the mention of the theory of evolution.) A lot of this, I think, goes back to Changeling releasing in '95 and getting most of its formative supplements in '95-'96, which is the absolute apex of White Wolf's infatuation with cosmology crossover, before they reversed course HARD around '98. That means Changeling got a lot of conceptual carry-over from Werewolf's Weaver and Mage's Technocracy that didn't necessarily belong in the game.
                                                            (source:                                                             http://forum.rpg.net/showthread.php?...g-20-confirmed )

                                                            That last one in particular speaks to me. Changeling always seemed so afraid to stake out it's own territory and accidentally step on the toes of another gameline. Changeling: The Dreaming shouldn't care that there's an enemy in Werewolf called the Fomori. Changeling should own the Formorians without apology or making excuses why the two have the same name. Dreams and Nightmares does it too. Everyone thinks Changelings are unplayable in the Dreaming because the book spends so much word count explaining how Mages perceive Changelings in the Dreaming, and only one sentence saying that's not how Changeling's see themselves. Why do the Nunnehi care about the Umbra and not the Dreaming?

                                                            I'd love to see Changeling get out of the shadow of the other game lines and stand just as independently as Vampire, Werewolf, Mage, Hunter, and Demon.
