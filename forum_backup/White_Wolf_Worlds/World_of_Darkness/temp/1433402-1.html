        c20 does not promote Vampires as having pariticularly high banality if one looks at the various stated out Prodigals,actin general prodigals even at their worst tend to be less banal than your average mortal.... this probably matches up with a certain amount of common sense I mean how can someone who lives and breaths magic be particular banal?

        On the other hand it broadly does push the idea that it is a commonly held belief among changelings that Vampire are banality incarnate and that only unseelie rebels really see it differently. Then I thought about how Vampire generally works and came to an epiphany.

        Being a Vampire is not a banal act. But things that tend to push someone to high banality is a survival strategy for the long term vampire. The long term survival strategies of changelings vs Vampire are intrinsically at odds and can create a very interesting star crossed lovers trope.

        So how does being Banal help your vampire survive... Well.. Humanity/Roads/Paths of Enlightenment. Quite simply the drive to become less engaged to day to day "Adventuring" is sort of built into the Kindred condition. A static controlled routine is generally how one engages in a night to night life for the long term for a vampire no matter their path. Its why they work through ghouls, and neonates and pawns because the less they engage in the less they have to risk having their world view questioned and thus keep the beast at bay. This is why most kindred die young. They never get out of this pawn stage and cannot control their routine.. the perfect allies and friends for your average changeling.

        Unfortunately of course as they move higher up in the board they find the routine a strong drive and the changeling of course needs.. chaos they need novelty they need to adventure. Their friendships and loves become poisons.... but such is the way of love.. just ask a Varich.

        Now i am sure many of you will point out to Paths like Metamorphosis for things that promote chaos and change... but it doesn't. It promotes a very regimented and controlled focus you in control. It even goes so far as to remove you from kindred society at its highest ends. Yes becoming a hermit temple made of flesh is creepy body horror... its also you banal as fuck after a couple of years.

        Conversely those particularly low and in unstable states on their path might gain a new found understanding by engaging with the changeling... growing ot they point of exemplarship.. or climbing up from the brink of wassail by the grace of glamour.. that they eventually turn against.. such is the tragedy of Kindred existance.

                                                I think in general vampires should be highly Banal, but exceptions exist. There are any number of reasons why certain individual vampires should be less Banal. I'd usually make these younger vampire or vampires with higher Humanity, but I could come up with scenarios of older and more monstrous vampires being less Banal. There's lot of potentially interesting stories or scenarios where you'd want vampires and fairies interacting so why hamper yourself with an overly strict rule? "Always" and "never" tend to be bad rules. The importance is to rely less on game mechanics, and think of character reasons why this particular vampire is less Banal while still keeping vampires in general Banal.

                                                I am reluctant to simply make Malkavians in general less Banal though. That is an artifact of the early game when it was assumed that Malkavians had greater knowledge of "true" reality. That was paired with their Derangement, but the underlying assumption of the game was the Malkavians "knew" things other vampires didn't. I question whether that was actually the case. First, very few canonical NPCs were actually depicted like that. Second, while that was something an ST could objectively make true for an NPC, it was always much harder to do that for PCs because you couldn't control what a PC Malkavian did, nor did you want to give preference to one PC over the others. At best, it was simply a pretext an ST could use to provide additional knowledge to a PC that the ST wanted them to have, but an ST could also figure out ways to provide that same knowledge in other ways to other PCs. Third, once the game began to provide actual mechanics to some of these things for Malkavians, it opened up the rift between those Malkavians who might know some things (because they had that on their character sheet) and those Malkavians who wouldn't (because it wasn't on their character sheet). Fourth, not all Malkavian concepts and Derangements lead themselves to the idea that this Malkavian may understand "true reality" better as the game began encouraging more Hannibal Lecter types versus the more whimsical or delusional types. One can argue a Malkavian who is schizophrenic might actually sometimes experience the Umbra or the Dreaming (that giant pink bunny named Harvey that he talks to might be a Pooka or a Chimera), but a Malkavian who is simply a sociopath or have depression shouldn't. So for me, having slightly more Malkavians be less Banal on average that other vampires is better than a strict rule that says all Malkavians are less Banal.

                                                The other guidance I would use is that not all Banality is the same. The Banality of a dour, strict, and unimaginative human working at the DMV should not be the same as the Banality of an elder vampire, even if that vampire is highly Banal. Mechanically it may play out the same, but how it is presented should be different. The first person is just an unimaginative killjoy that takes the fun out of life with their presence. The second is a force of entropy and death into this world that erodes the life and hope of people and animals it encounters. It reduces humanity to simply being a source of blood to keep itself alive, or being slaves to its desires instead of guardians of their own hopes and dreams. The two beings' Banality should be roleplayed very differently. Therefore when you do encounter a vampire that is somehow less Banal, it provides an appropriate juxtaposition to the vampires in general, and gives a hint on what the future may hold for that character (becoming more Banal as the years takes it tolls, it loses Humanity, kills more people, enslaves people through the Blood Bond, etc.)

                                                        I certainly believe that some elders are not considered Banal (or even as a source of Glamour). I think there is relatively very few of them though. The real issue is whether they legitimately act as sources of wonder and imagination (and be so ongoing, not an infrequent or inadvertent source by random chance). A monstrous elder who is on the Path of Night is much more likely to serve as one given that they believe vampires serve a higher purpose and seek to fulfill it. Their actions are more likely to be designed to keep mortals aware and in fear so they will acknowledge the great moral powers of the universe. Most elders though simply have more Banal ends - to survive and slake their Hunger. Of course, we're not limited to vampires on the Path of Night. We can come up with lots of theoretical examples (and also have vampires that theoretically should not be Banal, but are in practice).

                                                        People like to be scared - but only in safe situations where they aren't actually in danger of being harmed. If they actually feel under threat, people tend to shut down their minds and concentrate merely on survival and afterwards don't view their past experience positively. It's traumatic, not a source of wonder. Experiences with vampires tend to grind people down which is a very Banal thing. So while I can see certain circumstances where people might be left with a sense of wonder upon interacting with vampires, I think it's very limited.

                                                        It would be much more common for someone to temporarily be curious about the world of vampires before they learn the sudden and real (Banal) truth about it - they're just a meal or a useful slave. The false glamour isn't real. It's the difference between the romanticized version of the mob in the Godfather versus the more realistic depiction in Scorsese movies. Simply because it's false, doesn't mean it can't generate "real" Glamour per the Changeling mechanic. But it has less to do with understanding reality (which is Banal) than the person being so foolish they don't realize the situation they are in. In that case, is it the vampires not being Banal, or is it that their victims are actually the ones not being so? (We can apply it to many other things that are generally thought to be terrible, but some people find adventurous or have fantasies that incorporate only some of the reality while ignoring everything else. War is an obvious one.) There's obviously some room for discussion when it comes to the game mechanics. I believe in general, vampire Banality is high and probably increases over time. But there are exceptions, and a very rare few vampires might actually be sources of actual "Dark Glamour". As long as the ST really sold it to me that this vampire or this particular encounter isn't Banal, I'd be fine with it. But I don't think it's the default.

                                                        So if I was STing Changeling, and had to make a general rule, I might say "hey, in any situation concerning vampires, it's 90% Banal, but 10% of it is not". (More likely it's less than that for me.) But different STs would assign different percentages.

                                                            The idea that vampires are INTRINSICALLY banal is a holdover from 2nd Edition that is largely absent from 20th Anniversary Rules As Written. The entire parameters surrounding Banality have changed because it is now, largely, unique to the individual. What one Changeling finds Banal another would find Glamorous. Point blank-- vampires are a source of inspiration. Vampires have inspired countless books, movies, paintings, plays, and other works of art. You can use this to explore a Kithain's interest in vampires, particularly any kind of tragic/romantic kind of Cainite (it feels like every coterie has a Toreador or Malkavian these days). Conversely, vampires are also a huge source of fear-- fear can also be extremely Glamorous. Nightmares and the stories of monsters are not intrinsically Banal, so that's another angle you can pursue depending on what vampires are taking the spotlight in your story. A third example-- Gangrel and Pooka. Some vampires, and some faeries, are more connected to the natural world. Plenty of cultures around the world (the Russian vedma comes to mind) blend a vampiric entity with an animal shapeshifter enchantress.

                                                            My impulse thoughts around this issue-- Thinbloods and Kithain would make an interesting story. Thinbloods experimenting with Alchemy meeting a Nocker, wild experiments commence? Sounds Glamorous to me. In general, younger vampires (which, to be fair, recent editions of VTM encourage younger vampires be the stars!) would probably be better companions to the Kithain. Both Masquerade and Dreaming have classicist struggles as a central theme (Elders vs Neonates, Nobles vs Commoners) and that would be interesting to explore.

                                                            I would encourage you not to be discouraged about vampires being intrinsically Banal. The entire point of Dreaming is that the potential for stories are limitless. Personally I think there are some very interesting stories to be told about these two creatures finding themselves at a crossroads.
