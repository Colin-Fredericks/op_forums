    Electric Sheep

    08-19-2022, 12:21 PM
    So, I never played CtD, but have some basic understanding of the Dreaming from crossing over from other splats. My current campaign has an oneiromancer, and I had an idea for something I might need some pointers for from players / STs with a little more direct experience.

    Google DeepDream was a project that (initially) explored the possibility of taking a look inside the black box of a neural network, which is usually something very difficult to do. They side-stepped the issue by using a network trained on image recognition and made it 'run in reverse' - I won't get into software engineering details on how that is misleading, but it suffices as a generalization.

    Machine Learning software has become very prominent since then; things like AI Dungeon popped up, and currently image creators like DALL-E or Midjourney are vogue. Commercially, Machine Learning algorithms are pretty widespread for anything that involves pattern recognition, like search and recommendation engines, the visual processing of self-driving cars, surveillance and so on.

    I'm avoiding calling them Artificial Intelligence because while results get more impressive, they are still a far cry from conscious. Within the WoD however, true AI already exists.

    My point here is, could the current state of rudimentary 'creativity' of ML together with the widespread application of the technology already be enough to affect the Dreaming? Could you imagine a 'section' of the Dreaming where those created images and stories accumulate to build a new realm? Or would it be its own thing, a Chimare separate from the Dreaming?

    Extrapolating from this, would the created Chimeras be different from those created by human dreamers? It's difficult not to project expectations into those images, even though the neural net that created them has no idea what the images represent.

    Is there any official release that explores machine dreams?

              The upcoming lore of the traditions explains that the notion of the digital web is not necessarily as limited as previous editions have presented it as.                   I would always make a distinction between the Dreaming itself and any other Dreamlands style realms in the Dream Zone. Once you start including any kind of items of human imagination in the Dreaming, you quickly have a setting that is departing from what people imagine a Fairyland to be like. I don't think you can have a game about fairies if the fairies and fairyland become unrecognizable.

                  I think there is something "real" and "primal" about the Dreaming that does not apply to any Dream realms only fueled by human imagination and wonder. You can come up with various explanations for that.

                  I would allow for SOME crossover in the sense that it is always possible to somehow travel other parts of reality. So if an ST wanted changelings to somehow enter the Digital Web, that's OK. The same is there was somehow a lost spirit or group of spirits from these other realms "invade" the Dreaming, I'd be fine with that. An ST could create good adventures for his PCs.

                  But personally, I wouldn't want computers creating spirits or chimera that appear in the Dreaming. That's just my personal taste.

                          To answer this question it is necessary to understand the nature of the Dreaming (roughly, as many details remain unanswered, obviously).

                          And the first thing to understand is that the Dreaming is older than humanity, and the second that it is a realm of Glamour, not of literal Dreams. While dreams may manifest there, this is not the point.

                          What the Dreaming is is the realm of stories and emotions. It is a place that gather the feelings of everything in the world that has the capacity to feel and turns it into forms following the Laws of Narratives.

                          It is also a place of creation. It is not the Astral Umbra. When you imagine something and your imagination takes form from Glamour, it is a Chimera, but the fae aren't Chimera, and not every Chimera is formed that way. No human in the WoD thought of the Tuatha, or any of the Kiths, before the Dreaming itself spawned them. Fears of hunger and predation fueled the birth of the Redcaps, but there was no legend about Redcaps before the Dreaming made them.

                          So none of this is how the Dreaming works. If such Chimera do exist, they exist either in the Autumn World or the Near Dreaming, because Chimera spawned by imagination are spawned near their creators. The things that spawn within the Dreaming itself derive from the nature of that place and have no connection to this world, although they may one day come to it and become known to mortal legend, just as the Kiths did.

                          On the other hand, the Dreaming already has things that aren't related to human dreams. Among the Gallain we have already some prime examples, like the Dark-kin and the Inanimae. Pay attention to the Inanimae on that: they're possibly not born of the dreams about natural phenomena and physical things, but the dreams from natural phenomena and physical things.

                          By the way, currently among the Inanimae there's already the Empire of Flickers, composed of Krofted Mannikins linked to televisions, computers and other broadcasts. An Inanimae linked to a Neural Network could be either a Mannikin or, possibly, a Solimond. Learning about the Inanimae will be good for you to answer this question. But while they travel to farther reaches of the Dreaming, the Inanimae do belong to the Autumn World and can't unfetter themselves permanently from it.

                          Yes, in the end it is probable that such things already have some form of impact on the Dreaming, both because of the dreams caused by them and their own possibly-existing dreams (the Dreaming can react to imagination of things that don't have an actual imagination, because the Dreaming answers only to itself), but at the same time this impact exists within the terms of the Dreaming, and out there will take forms unrelated to their Autumn counterpart, forms that pertain only to the context of the Dreaming.

It isn't Maya, it isn't a part of the Dream Zone, and most dreams probably can't be accessed from there unless something makes them manifest there.                                       Now, saying what causes Glamour is complicated. We have things like the Inanimae and also Glades, Freehold-like sources of Glamour that exist in wild places without contact to humans. Other than that, human-related Glamour, and as such connection to the Dreaming, tends to occur where people share inspiration instead of around the things that inspire them. If lots of children share stories about a haunted mansion around a campfire, the Glamour doesn't take form in the haunted mansion, but around the campfire, even if the result may be a version of the mansion appearing at its location in the Near Dreaming.

                                      Banality is not just the lack of imagination, it is the crushing of it. Do not think of it as an absence. DeepDream by itself would probably be considered a wonderful thing by the fae. Its lack of feeling is irrelevant in comparison to how its work inspires and exists just for the sake of sparking imagination. A face-recognition software used give funny labels to people in photos wouldn't be a thing of Banality, but one used to erase jokes and nicknames and enforce proper identification for the sake of sobriety would be Banal.

                                      You should also understand the psychology of denizens of the Dreaming. They're the most likely splat to embrace any kind of new idea, adventure, curiosity, and throw caution through the window. Excessive caution is Banal, as is fear that paralyzes instead of fueling story. Whatever comes from such new phenomena, they'll hate or love, they'll try to conquer or ally with, they'll interact and spread tales about instead of just avoiding. Even their cautionary tales have always a note of daring instead of simply saying you shouldn't try something. They're bonkers, really. Also, they already have tales about meeting, befriending, vowing revenge on things like seasons, mountains, the concept of social class, etc.

So much so that people are already asking themselves if "artists will lose their jobs". I cant imagine a heavier blow to the Dreaming than this - masses of dreamers actively GIVING UP on their ability to "dream" because they are being replaced by souless and emotionless machines. "What's the point of trying to make a song or paint a painting if nobody wants to buy my work because "DeepGoogle" is so much better at it? I wont even try".

                                                          That to me would be the final blown delivered by the autumn people, and the final winter to all Fae.
