                          Except, especially as of Awakening 2e, it's not the same answer.                                   I'll leave Mage: The Awakening to people who understand that game. I don't - and frankly have little desire to - so I can only speak for Mage: The Ascension.

                                  If we're doing so, this question is...complicated.

                                  First, let's get out of the way what we mean by "flashy". One of the differences I stress between "high fantasy" and "urban fantasy" is that in the latter, the visibility of magic is limited entirely to the actual Effect being performed. If a wizard waves a hand and causes a chair to levitate in the air, the only thing you see is the wizard's actions and the chair rising. You don't see glowing runes appear in the air or the chair get wreathed in some colorful aura or cloud of sparkles. These sorts of things were implemented by creators working in visual mediums, as a shorthand to let the audience know, without a doubt, that magic was happening.

                                  Not only does this not fit so well into the urban fantasy mold MtAs is going for - where magic should be mysterious and in the shadows - it would also does a disservice to the game's purposes in two ways. One, by taking the focus away from the tools and techniques used by different mage groups. And two, by making it impossible for magic to be Coincidental, if the very act of using magic always brings about special effects. If the very premise of MtAs is to work, it should be possible for a mage to set things up in such a way that observers couldn't be sure that magic happened at all.

                                  That said, my very earlier statements establish that plenty of the Effects that mages can use are, by their nature, inherently fantastic. Or they can be, if taken in a vacuum. Mages can levitate chairs, summon fireballs, rip holes into the spirit world, teleport, turn water into wine, animate statues, sprout wings, shrug off bullets, and stop time. All of these things will appear "flashy" enough, each in their own way, just by happening. If not disguised, that is what we call Vulgar magic. And that brings Paradox down on the mage's head.

                                  But they CAN be disguised, if the mage is creative enough, picks the right time and place, and does a little set-up and pageantry. Summoning a fireball seems much more plausible if the mage happens to be holding a lighter and a can of hairspray. It IS even possible for a mage to "summon demons to save money on movie special effects", if the mage makes it look like their summoning WAS special effects. There is a skill in MtAs called Blatancy, that is all about how well a character can make impossible feats seem plausible. So yes, you can summon your demons to make that movie.

                                  It is not remotely a good idea to do so - because demons - but you could do it.

                                  Although with the amount of effort involved in dressing up a set such that it could look like a trick, and the amount of Paradox that could be garnered anyway if the mage botches, it makes you wonder if it wouldn't still be easier and cheaper (and safer) to use FX anyway. Not even mentioning how those demons expect to be paid for their appearance, and we all know how that goes.

                                          I would also argue that high levels of specific Resonances - the levels where even sleepers notice something is off - are a solid foundation for the style of a Mage's flashyness when combined with obvious vulgar magic - where any pretense of coincidence isn't even attempted.
                                          Not only do you have a gauge that tells you 'Alright, your Mage is by now so resonant that things are starting to get flashy at times', but it also delivers the apt verbs to describe the flashyness at the same time.

                                          It's actually a good way, IMO, to help new players visualize the concept of Resonance. At low levels it is merely a vibe and the fingerprint of the Mage's effects. At high levels combined with blatant vulgar magic, it becomes the signature style of visual effects of the Mage. The same way you immediately recognize the signature work of a specific character's magic or abilities in media like comics or movies, based purely on its visuals.
