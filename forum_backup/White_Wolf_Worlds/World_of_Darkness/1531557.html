    What if the Hermetic Order had joined the Order of Reason?

    02-26-2024, 07:24 PM
    Sorcerer's Crusade actually says that this was an option. What do you think would have happened? How would the Order/Union be today? What about the world? Would the Hermetics have left eventually?
    Most importantly could it have happened?
    Personally i think the Cabal of pure thought would have left asap. Already they have been burning some daedalans for heresy, i don't think they would tolerate it.

    Craftmasons would probably feel like they are undergoing a hostile takeover. I think they would have the exact same fate, perhaps earlier.

    The high guild actually likes Hermetics, from what i recall, at least to the extend that you can like an enemy.

    Any thoughts?

      *While many Hermetics would find afinity on many OoR practices and beliefs, the OoH as a whole isn't a team player. La Salle had to duel so many mages to have the OoH sit with the Traditions that he died, of Certamen, which it's non lethal (well, admitedly some may cheat and damage the opponent ... and idk if all the duels were Certamen - that was the traditional type of duel. It's still pretty impactfull) ... I think that event talks spades over the "character" of the OoH

      *The OoH at that point isn't interested in "joining" with anyone. Best case scenario, they want the others to join them. Even after being forced kicking and screaming to sit at a table with other Traditons, they see the others as tools and try a takeover. OoR may be more nice looking for some, but I would think they're still mages that need to be educated into a better Paradigm, or deployed as tools

      *The idea that the masses have to be protected and educated, that the OoH has in the modern era wasn't a thing back then. Ancient OoH wouldn't entreat the Crafmason idea of bettering the lot of mortals by giving them the tools of magick. Craftmasons founded the OoR because they wanted to face the OoH, agreed that it would feel like a takeover, it may well be exactly that

      *On the other hand, the more "inquisitorial" aspect of figthing against the supernatural to protect the masses could be approached. OoH do see the role of the mage as wizard-king, and noblesse oblige could mean something. On this end I could see some level of aproach with the Cabal, out of convinience. Albeit giving the cheeky attitude of OoH and how fanatical the Cabal it's, it's going to be a dangerous partnership

      *On Europe they were the major force once uppon a time. The Traditions, thus, would be forced to be much less eurocentrinc. They would be quite the underdogs there. Traditions could become stronger on africa, as maybe the Ngoma would've joined as a full Trad w/o the Hermetics as gatekeepers

      *The Guild will like the Hermetic, as an asset. And I think if they don't leave (likely) or take over (less likely) they may end getting liquidated by them. Nobody likes the OoH as a sort of "fifth column" of wizards that feel superior not just to the masses, but also to the other enligthened. The other factions are going to be feed up with them and join against them eventually - if they don't change.
      And the Guild could end causing their downfall because they control the "bottom line", that's something the ancient OoH really has trouble grasping (they could end having the floor removed in a similar vein as how merchants did the noble)

      Alternatively to takeovers and just leaving, I think the OoH could become the foundation of a stronger NWO. OoH are the masters of symbols, that already had a lot of covert power by creating secret orders attracting people on high places. Once the more "lunatic wizard-king" members have been calmed, the subtler, more politically oriented, parts of the order could take the role of social control and shadow behind the goverment that NWO has. Perhaps less Big Brother and more Illuminati (less transparent but less controling?)

                                      Not quite. If a Magickal Effect goes against Earthly Foundations, it remains Vulgar even if the Consensus accepts it. And vice versa.

                                      It's similar in some respects to MRev's Cosmological Constants and Historical Inertia, in that they're explanations of why some things' Vulgarity doesn't align with the Consensus. That said: in Victorian Mage, it's stated that if a Territory (Victorian parlance for a Reality Zone) gets completely depopulated and remains that way long enough, it eventually reverts to the Earthly Foundations, with all Consensus-imposed changes fading away.

                                          Earthly Foundations are highly customizable to the type of game you want to run since we have few things that are definitively stated to be foundations, but many things that reasonably could be. They can and do favor the development of science, as they push towards a certain repeatability and reliability. Whether they would always push towards a science we would recognize is less certain, as it's not even clear our world pushes towards a single presentation of science. After all, two theories which both accurately predict the same phenomena and have the same ease of use are equally valid.
