    Martial Arts, for one. Putting aside the mystical themes - which obviously don't come into play with the strictly rationalist Technocracy - an Enlightened agent can work wonders with the proper application of physical might and scientific principles.

    Speaking of scientific principles, a Technocract could employ them in conjunction with materials lying around to do their work. Basically imagine MacGuyver, except with Enlightened levels of brilliance.

    Even "technobabble" can be used in creative ways. Like a Technocrat forcing a person to puke, just by rattling off the chemicals contained in processed foods. Or altering the person's behavior by citing obscure psychology studies, so the target thinks that acting in a certain way is normal or even expected. Or causing a machine to break down by talking about its many technical limitations, followed by a well-placed slap to the side. Some of this technobabble involves providing information that directly influences people, while others are simply the Technocrat proving their authority on the subject at hand, and "letting nature take its course".
    The first two would potentially work depending on the circumstances, but I'm not actually sold on the last one. The problem with it is that there's no direct line between speaking technobabble and creating the intended effect other than, basically, magic words.

Even if the agent cites a study that is bullshit, if they can make their listener believe it, it can change how they think (if only by virtue of the target believing really hard that they ought to be thinking in a certain way).

                        Although that sort of thing could extend even to Life effects as well. The Placebo Effect is a known factor in medical science, to the point where it's firmly in the Consensus. If the right words - coming from a supposed expert medical authority - can plant the idea of a medical malady in a person's head, it could trick the body into manifesting the symptoms on its own. Mind over matter isn't just for spiritualists and psychics, but is arguably scientific fact...to a certain degree. A Technocrat won't be able to talk a person into spontaneously growing tentacles, but he could make him run a fever, have a seizure, or begin vomiting.

                                            Keep in mind that the TU sometimes runs on "Science" that is so far advanced, as to be indistinguishable from magic. In addition to that the Technocracy does some mad prepwork (or at least pretends to do it), allowing Unionists to pull off some spectacular stunts.

                                            Can a technocraft turn a kitchen knife into a gun using 'magic words'? Of course not. But he can trigger the catchphrase that will trigger the device hidden in its handle (thanks to the Syndicates efforts, alot of mundane household appliances have hidden functions) that collapses the waveform of the knife and then reconfiguring its material form to the agents need with the help of real time sattelite assisted computing.

                                            Can a technocrat stranded naked in the wilds by a reality deviant wrestle the hungry bear next to him? Well that depends on his combat training. However if the mage has benefited from proper training he can adopt certain mannerisms, which combined with his slightly altered body scent (a standart small scale physiological alteration done to field agents, usually just piggybacked onto a flu shot for convinience sake) will trigger a genetic sequence added to the species in 1907. It was added to a sample specimen and then transmitted to the rest of the species via complex quantum entanglement procedures that searched for DNA of similar configuration. Yes I know that's how sleepers told you is not how quantum entanglement works. Explaining the exact physics behind it would take you years to understand without the use of advanced psychometric learning techniques. Anyway trigger the sequence, the bear turns docile.

                                            Can a technocrat just simply step through time and space? well space is really relative and certain natural distortions can be abused. That's of course very tricky, by and all space is rather sturdy. But you can allways use the mainframe hub of your area in a bind by uttering a specific sound combined with certain muscle contractions. We have a statistical analytic programm running on the mainframe that'll predict when someone next enacts this trigger, then opens a micro-spatial-fissure, feeding you information from its side via ultrasound directly into your subconcious and reading your responses from pupil dilation. Should have seen those superstitionists when I suddenly vanished and popped back up behind them.

                                            In the end alot of what is possible or not possible is a question of ST decree, but the Union can justify just about any effect using the vast infrastructure of highly advanced sci fi technology. How far they can take it probably depends on what mood you want to use for them. I know some who think the union (aside from the VEs) should be played as basically a normal government agency whose technology is just about as advanced as modern technology, just with maybe a few years ahead and them being really good at it (mechanically represented through the benefit of spellcasting mechanics). Such an NWO agent can of course only use his Data spehere to spy on people when he has actual drones in the air and teleportation is right out of the window. That is superstitionist talk! On the other hand you can play the union as being centuries ahead of normal humanity due to their enlightened understanding (easily doable using magic) and only slowly introducing the tech to the sleepers at an ever growing pace. Such a Unionist doesn't use a spydrone because he has to or because it's particularily easier. He does so because it gives him plausible deniability. Because he works together with sleeper agents whom he exalts through his exemplary use of modern technology. Not everyone has an education in basic hypertech (yet), so everytime a technocrat is forced to use instruments a sleeper can't understand and that seem miraculous to him, the sleeper misinterprets science as magic and the timetable needs to be adjusted ever so slightly behind. This TU uses bleeding edge science instruments to inspire, not because they have to.

                                                            Exactly.

                                                            Remember, everyone, that back in the Renaissance, the Order of Reason divided itself into Outer and Inner Labyrinths. It was a secret, initiatory society, so parts of its knowledge base was withheld from members until they achieved higher ranks, like all secret societies that are built around initiation. The Outer Labyrinth - made up of the unEnlightened Brethren and those Enlightened members of Arete 3 and lower - were deliberately made unaware that what they were doing was anything supernatural, superhuman, or super-scientific. To the point where Mage: The Sorcerer's Crusade recommended the Casual Rule for all such Order of Reason members in the Outer Labyrinth. Which says "you cannot use any Enlightened Effects that are Vain, Period". They did not believe it was possible, because all Outer Labyrinth members believed they were using techniques available to all humans (with the proper training), or tools and technologies that were reasonable extrapolations of existing ones. To members of the Outer Labyrinth, there was no visible difference between an unEnlightened and Enlightened member, save that the latter may be more skilled, better trained, more knowledgeable of natural philosophy, or simply more lucky/observant/blessed by God's/The Devil's own luck.

                                                            It is only when a Daedalean reaches Arete 4 are they initiated into the Inner Labyrinth, and revealed the truth. That certain people have an ephemeral but rational power of Enlightenment, that pushes them to the peak of human potential. And that this allows rational, God-fearing individuals to push the boundaries of invention, organization, and technique, in order to better mankind's lot in life. And now that the Daedalean has reached the Inner Labyrinth, they are allowed to pursue these more...experimental disciplines and sciences. So long, of course, as the character does not fall to the ever-present temptation of Sorcery.

                                                            Naturally, the highest ranking members of the Inner Labyrinth dabble in Sorcery often - or Sorcery-like bits of questionable Science - in secret. But that's neither here nor there. For the Order of Reason, everything is explicable, or will be rendered explicable with time and the proper testing.

                                                            I see no reason to think that the modern Technocracy wouldn't follow the same general arrangement. The rank-and-file in the Union understand only the party line: what they do is nothing more than bleeding edge research, based on the most theoretical and advanced of scientific principles. All of which, naturally, is too advanced to let the general public know about, until such time as the science has been refined. Given that an Enlightened agent doesn't have access to Effects much more powerful than an experienced Extraordinary Citizen (with their focused but not insignificant technosorcery Paths), agents on the ground would be hard pressed to even effectively tell if "Enlightenment" were anything more than a loose identifier for talent.

                                                            And the superstitionists? Why, they are either frauds that cloak legitimate technology behind veneers of mysticism, or idiot savants who stumble into real scientific power, or the blind adherents to ancient proto-science that have nuggets of truth buried in heaps of nonsense, or individuals sensitive to other dimensional beings and capable of inducing such alien organisms to perform for them (likely at terrible cost in the future), or even those who wield dangerous psionic power, barely shackled and directed by ritual and primitive taboos. The fact that the superstitionist is unwilling to perform their most impressive feats outside of situations they rigidly control is evidence that they are charlatans who need to arrange their tricks in advance. That they seem to suffer bizarre backlashes from their efforts at times speaks of not being in true control of the forces they channel. That the ones who claim to speak to "spirits" adhere to insane behavioral patterns is evidence that they are either being forced to comply with requests from truly alien intelligences, or they are psionics who suffer from schizophrenia and hallucinations.

                                                            It is only when a Technocrat rises to the highest ranks that they are informed of the truth: that Reality, to some degree, responds to human belief. Which is exactly why the Union's mission of educating the masses in one world view is so vital. Of course, anyone who reaches that level has been allowed to advance because they've proven discreet, trustworthy, and loyal to the Union and its principles. That way, even as their old beliefs are revealed as half-truth, they are already committed to the Technocracy, and can see the brutal necessity of the deception.
