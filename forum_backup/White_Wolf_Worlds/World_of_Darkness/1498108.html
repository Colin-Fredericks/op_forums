    MtA/M20 Magic question

    11-26-2022, 11:05 PM
    We have an ongoing debate at the table trying to decide at what point does a spell (or the effects of a spell) become permanent? I know that with 6+ successes a spell can become permanent with Storyteller option. At what point do spells that heal, spells that create or alter something, when do they become permanent?

    - Does a healing spell from Life 2 or 3 end and your wounds open back up?
    - If I turn a vampire into a lawn chair, does he revert to normal at the end, or does the spell end and the results of the spell remain?
    - If I make a stone wall using Prime and Matter, does the wall stay a wall at the end of the spell?

    What spells are permanent without 6 successes, and which spells need those 6 successes?

    Please help.

      There is no simple answer, because the game is purposefully vague/flexible so that the ST can ensure the free form magic system stays within the tone they're aiming for.

      That said, in general, when a spell "ends" the impact of the spell doesn't go away. If I throw a fireball, the fireball disappears when the spell ends, but anything burnt by it, or set on fire in the process, doesn't have that revert. The fireball ceases to exist, but the burns it caused still happened, so need to be healed. If I turn a broken piece of steel into clay, and then remold it back into it's proper pre-broken shape, when it reverts back to steel it will be in that form.

      Basically, everything that happens during of duration of the spell doesn't get retconned out of the timeline because the spell ends. Permanent (or at least long duration) spells are when you need the spell itself to keep on going. A ward isn't much use if the spell ends before any intruders try getting into your Sanctum.

          Permanence means one of two things in Ascension. There permanent as in “indefinite duration” and then there’s permanent as in “magic that will last forever”

          Indefinite duration is when you pump 6 successes into an effect. The effect is essentially permanent with a few caveats.
          1. If the effect is vulgar then it may succumb to the effects of unbelief if witnessed by enough sleepers. While no ruleset is provided for unbelief, a common house rule is that the effect loses a few successes, either becoming weaker or losing duration. Very much up to the ST how that plays out.
          2. Duration length is up to ST discretion. 6 successes is just the threshold for indefinite duration. In theory the ST can declare that adding to duration like this only extends and effect for a year or so, this makes it essentially permanent all intents and purposes since there are precious few chronicles that span multiple years, but it isn’t generation spanning. Alternatively the ST might decide a little Time magic is required to push duration of an effect beyond the 6 month threshold, even allowing duration to shift towards the timelines chart after the initial 6 successes is reached.
          3. Your Focus can make indefinite duration a pain. If your focus requires active concentration such as via psychic telekinesis, maintaining a mental link, maintaining a prime sword, etc, then it will add to your active effects limit, with difficulty for casting other stuff given a +1 per 2 active effects. Furthermore having 2 active effects locks you out from conducting rituals, which severely limits the kinds of indefinite duration effects you can create.
          If your Focus instead externalises the magic such as machines to keep an eye on people, prayer to god to allow good fortune, or placing a thought in someone’s head that you don’t need to maintain, then concentration is not required until the duration of the spell expires, at which point it will start to contribute to your simultaneous effect limit. The same goes for effects locked in to patterns with matter/life/forces

          True permanence requires the creation of a wonder, and that is done with prime. This makes an effect which essentially casts itself so you don’t have to. At a base level, this can be done by using Prime to invest quintessence in to your effect and then using Prime 3 to make the effect ongoing but only for as long as it is charged with quint (possibly requiring a periapt to be rechargeable, or to keep feeding it tass) or Prime 4 to make it truly permanent (so even if it runs out of quint, it will still run when topped up later).
          These effects become a) immune to unbelief, vulgar or not, b) not subject to your or your paradigm, so they’ll function regardless of whether or not you are standing in the room.

          Magic which involves damage, giving or removing, tends to be instantaneous. You cast the effect and the damage is transferred or removed, and that is the end of it. This includes matter effects made to destroy or repair matter patterns.

          Effects to transform, however, are based on duration, so transforming a human (or non-human) into an object or animal will be subject to duration as would using matter to turn lead in to gold.

          Life effects can be made permanent without prime by accepting flaws or applying experience points to the change, and similarly Matter effects to change their shape and form can be made permanent through application of craft. The exception of this is effect which enforce a change of state to a matter pattern, such as changing water in to ice or brick walls in to smoke, which can only be permanent upon achieving mastery with Matter alone.

                  Mage, generally speaking, relies on the concept of "Patterns." Everything has a baseline Pattern unique to itself. Magic that overrides Patterns reverts as the Pattern tries to reassert itself (this can be violent enough to theoretically be used to kill someone if the magic is vulgar)..

                  The Pattern of the bar is to be steel, once the magic making it something not-steel ends, the Pattern wants to be steel again. Mage treats Matter Patterns as easier to alter than Life Patterns for various reasons, so access to direct Pattern alteration instead of form alteration is easier with Matter, but that's not exactly what you're asking about.

                      Originally posted by Ingeloak View Post
                      - Does a healing spell from Life 2 or 3 end and your wounds open back up?
                      A healing spell dont "end" anymore than a wounding spell gets "unwounded" once the effect "ends".

                      Healing and wounding is always permanent (or at least until the body is healed/wounded back again, naturally or not). You need 1 success to affect a target besides yourself, and each success besides that heals 2 levels of damage. There's no time limit on that.

                      Originally posted by Ingeloak View Post
                      - If I turn a vampire into a lawn chair, does he revert to normal at the end, or does the spell end and the results of the spell remain?
                      The vampire reverts back to normal after the spell's end.

                      Originally posted by Ingeloak View Post
                      - If I make a stone wall using Prime and Matter, does the wall stay a wall at the end of the spell?
                      The wall gets dispersed back again into the Ether, which cannot be collected and is lost (much like energy that ends up dissipated in the form of heat)

                      Think of it this way: a material object created from the Ether (Quintessence) without enough successes to "make it stable" (turn it permanent), is too unstable in its "ethereal fluctuations", and thus after the time passes upon which your spell bound that locus of Ether into that solid form, without the power of your spell bounding that Ether into that specific solid form, it simply dissipates back into the Ether - and lets not forget, the Ether (or Quintessence) is like heat, it is present everywhere and dissipates in the environment. And like heat, once it dissipates, it cant be easily captured. Thus, after the spell ends, that point (or points) of Quint you spent into making the wall, is lost.

                          Indeed, that is exactly the reason why transformations are temporary, but repair/damage spells are permanent. It's because of the Pattern. When you heal a living being using Life or mend a broken object using Matter, you are "Restoring the Pattern" of that being or object; thus the restored state becomes the new Pattern of the subject of the spell. Likewise, it's the same reason of why damage spells are also permanent (to either living beings or inanimate objects). Once you break a glass, its broken state becomes its new Pattern.

                          Transformations however, are temporary because they are a deviation of the Pattern of the subject. That's why the clay bars revert back to steel.

                          And that's EXACTLY what means to "turn an effect permanent": it means literally to CHANGE THE PATTERN OF THE SUBJECT.

                          That holds true even for Wonders too: when you place a "magick effect" on an object (or living being) using Prime (3, 4 or 5, depending on what is being done), what you are actually doing is "weaving" the ethereal patterns of the subject. I believe something like this is even explained under Prime Sphere, at least for Revised Edition.

                          To better visualize it: when you create a Wonder, you are playing with the "ethereal wave-lenghts" of the subject. If you think for a moment in electromagnetism, everything has electromagnetic energy, and that has a wave lenght. Change the parameters of that wave and you change the electromagnetic energy of anything.

                          If we think in this similarly to Ether, we could say that changing the "ether-wave" changes the primordial functions of the object. So lets throw in some more technobabble into it, just to illustrate: "if you wanna create a *scepter of healing*, you need to change the object's Ether Pattern to a 231 nn ether-wave. Oak pieces of wood normally have a ether-wave of 287 nn, so you must adjust your modulator to reduce it precisely by 56 nn".

                          The reason for this technobabble example is to illustrate how the creation of Wonders of any kind is nothing more nothing less than the manipulation of the Ethereal Patterns of an object (or living being using Prime 5 rather than 4). You permanently changes the Ethereal Pattern of the object (but the Material Pattern remains the same - a sword remains a sword, a rabbit remains a rabbit, but now they are "magick" because their Ether fluctuations have been permanently altered).

                          I hope that with this illustration, the Prime Sphere also becomes more clear (since it's the second hardest sphere to understand)

                          EDIT: by the way, once you turn a transformation permanent, that becomes the "New Pattern" of the subject of the spell, which means that this new form is what the subject will henceforth return to. For example, if you permanently change the sex of a person, that becomes their new Pattern; lets for example say that you turn a man into a woman permanently. If another mage tries to "revert back" and turn the now woman back into a man, the subject will simply revert into a woman after this new spell ends, because the subject's Pattern has being permanently changed (thus it would require to do another permanent change to "revert back")
