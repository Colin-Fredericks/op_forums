                    Originally posted by Ajax View Post
                    It's really really really easy to ditch Avatars altogether.

                    You can even keep Seekings in the sense of epiphanies of the soul/Eureka! moments/shakabuku or just situations on which a mage's understanding of magic makes a quantum leap of degree. And do it as a part of the ongoing story instead of some weird off-camera dream-scaping exercise.

                    They CAN be the heart and soul of the game, if that's specifically what you want and how the game is cast. Hells, the rules as is might not even go far enough. Instead of a rather crap Background where the crunch and fluff are out of whack and wildly disproportionate in how they affect the game, maybe Avatars could be as meaningful as, say, Geists are for Sin-Eaters.... Or maybe even as Shadows are to Wraiths. Why not create a system for &quot;Avatar-Guiding&quot; and let one of the other players take on the roll of your magical, occasionally asshole-ish, soul buddy?

                    They can also get the way. A lot. There is a ton of stuff that can go on in Mage game and sometimes trying to insert having a bossy invisible friend/soul-stalker/weird cat from nowhere/vague feeling that &quot;that's not a moon&quot; just takes away from all the rest of the coolness.
                    Avatar Guiding sounds really cool!

                                Originally posted by zeroninja View Post
                                I know according to both the Fallen from Demon and the Celestial Chorus, Avatars are pieces of "the One" as in when God/Whatever "smote the cosmos" it was broken into pieces itself, many of which fused with human souls to make mages, and others later causing the torrent of Avatars in the Avatar Storms in the metaplot.

                                Demon is... problematic to take as canon. While I do like it as possibly the only splat that can provide some kind of cohesive World of Darkness that ties all splats together, taking what Demon (which is a game that explicitly says that prehistory existed as multiple things all at once so it's up to interpretations) says about Mage (a game that is quite literally about things being dependent on interpretation) hardly gives concrete answers.

                                For example, Demon states that God created Man to be "as God," but the angels didn't know what exactly that meant. They only knew that early man was effectively "asleep," hardly more sentient than animals. In an attempt to save them from themselves, early angels force "woke up" early humans so as to give them agency and a way to save themselves from an impending mysterious doom.

                                Now, this can be interpreted biblically as the the serpent tempting Adam and Eve into eating of the Tree of Knowledge, or concerned angels giving the early tribes of barely evolved mankind actual sentience. As far as Demon is concerned, they're both the same things, and myriad others. However, it can also be interpreted that these angels Awakened, in the Mage sense, mankind to its inherent power that it always had. This was also before any of the cosmic calamities that sent the World of Darkness careening to the dark place that it is, but it could have been the start of it. "God," whatever it really was or could be interpreted as, was still around and kicking in this time.

                                What I'm saying is that maybe Demons (formerly the angels) think that Avatars are some kind of shard of The One, and this is what gives mages their power. I don't specifically recall reading that, but I'm also not the most well read when it comes to the Demon books after the part where their history component ends. However, Demon also implies that humans were created to have reality altering power in the first place. Of course, they could also not have been created deliberately at all, depending on how liberally you interpret Demon prehistory.
