                            First off, thank all of you for the info, it's greatly appreciated!

                            Originally posted by Ambrosia View Post

                            At first glance I like this kind of streamlining - less micromanagement of the base stats.
                            But on the second thought, and ironically from a *roleplay* perspective...it gives you less options, stat-wise.

                            Namely, you could previously play a scrawny, thin character that, however, was nimble and swift. Or a character with a high stamina and strength, that had the dexterity of a brick - the hulking giant type.
                            And whatever combinations and reasons you can come up with, really.

                            Now with only the 'physical' attribute, everybody who's a very good runner or a nimble pickpocket can also benchpress a literal benchful of people. For..some reason. A person that is good at socializing also looks great? An intelligent person also has the bestest perception?
                            As much as the system streamlines numbers juggling for the sake of RP, it also limits the different possibilities for rolls. Heh.
                            And yes, I fully realize a player could voluntarily decide to use less dice on a roll where they imagine their character'd be less developed physically, but..the majority of players probably won't.
                            That was my exact thought since I saw the BNS rules. It's easier to track in a LARP environment than the traditional 9 atrrs, but unnecessarily restrictive for tabletop. I'm glad it'll get refined, let's hope for the best!

                            Anyway, all of the other things seems actually quite good! I'm very happy they took this approach with you are what you eat instead of the more extreme ones. This one could add to the flavor of the game without constraining it and I always said something like that would be okay. The TN/required successes are going to the direction of Shadowrun's recent editions, I'm perfectly okay with that.

                            The Hunger mechanics seems to require a bit more bookkeeping than ideal at first glance (and I still think it's a little too much to have essentially two WP stat to track) but I could imagine that it is worse in paper than in practice and overall, it seems good and quite flavorful and dare I say, flat out better than the old system at first glance!

                            Some things I'm waiting for, but if I get it right weren't the subject of this playtest:

                            - merits/flaws and specializations, I hope they change it to something like NWoD/SR, since that could open up space for more interesting stuff, like fighting styles and such
                            - how more complex tasks will work
                            - how they sync the different lines' unique stats (like conscience, etc.)? They want more crossover (which I'm happy about), so I hope they're thinking about it from the beginning.
                            - how important and pervasive the narrative mechanics will be

                            Also, could any of you write a little more about the combat mechanics? How different is it from the old system?
                            Last edited by PMárk; 05-15-2017, 04:24 PM.

                            If nothing worked, then let's think!
