                I mean, it's fine to restrict the scenario to a straight hand-to-hand fight because that's the concept you want to explore. But the idea that either supernatural is more likely than not to resort to a "fair fight" over pride, honour, etc., is a bit dubious. In particular, a vampire that has spent time building up their skills with Dominate is probably not going to be one who views using it as a blow to their pride; they're going to be the ones that see it as a blatant demonstration of their superiority, and they're at least as likely to revel in that rather than turn their nose up at it, IMO.

                I think you make a better case for the Garou, as warrior pride tends to be a big deal for them. But even that is going to come with caveats; a Bone Gnawer is a lot less likely to walk into an unfavourable scenario and try and power through it out of hubris than a member of the Get. Garou of any kind are also more likely to have some kind of combat ability, because their entire society exists on a war footing, whereas plenty of vampires are pretty happy to mostly avoid trouble whenever possible.

                        If you want to explore all the available options from all splats, regardless of whom it is actually aimed at, and always rule their allowance in favor of the mortal, it is just easier to decide that yes, it is certainly possible. At that stage you have already determined it is a possibility and it will become one.

                        Could they without absurdities? Perhaps, but unlikely.
                        Animalism can sap the fighting will of anyone. It's quite nasty against mortals.
                        Auspex will warn the elder in advance, allowing them to prepare or retreat. A physical threat being the easiest to detect.
                        Celerity will make odds uneven or allow for escapes.
                        Dominate will end the fight before it starts.
                        Fortitude will allow the elder to loose a roll. It is probably the poorest choice, unless at 6+, where it might just break the poor mortal.
                        Obfuscate can end any fight or make it impossible in the first place.
                        Potence... While Celerity gets a lot of credit for power, Potence is in my opinion the real killer, in particular using combat manouvers relying on strength. Grapple with high Potence versus no Potence is the most efficient physical way to end a fight.
                        Presence have multiple ways of just ending or preventing fights. It can be fought off using willpower, but botching willpower rolls is nasty and supply will run out quickly, in particular since there are so many of them that can be applied.

                        Now, an Elder does not have all of these, but even with 10 dots divided among them there are multiple ways to stop or outright win a fight. Add clan-specific disciplines, in particular powers from Thaumaturgy and Necromancy that will kill a mortal by simply succeeding (which a single expenditure of willpower ensures), it quickly gets one sided.
                        Then add low generation and a large amount of blood, which may make an attribute match the entire dice pool of a mortal.
                        The vampire fangs are also versatile, with Potence, the bite is a death sentence to anything but the most resilient creatures, or it could just impose the kiss, which knock outs mortal resistance without a pass, and even has a chance to do the same to supernatural. And this is built in.

                        Could a mortal have a streak of good luck and win? Yes. But it is not likely. How unlikely depends on the Elder.
                        A mortal this well trained would be wasted trying to go head-to-head, but very dangerous actually exploiting the glaring weaknesses of the supernatural, both physical and social. Daytime and the Masquerade against Kindred are much more potent weapons.

                        ---

                        In Revised, though, the most efficient way to beat something head-to-head is by Retainers 5. This is simply due to the funky multiple combatants rule that increases difficulty by 1 for each opponent attacking the same target, quickly putting mostly everything at difficulty 10, at which point size of dice pools no longer matter (just watch out for those automatic Potence successes!). But I supposed Obtenebroken has the same built in feature. Unsure if this carried over to V20.

That said, while some parts of swinging weapons around can seem counterintuitive for someone experienced with brawl, a lot of it does carry over. Footwork for example.                                                             There are five editions of the WoD, and two editions of the CofD (when Paradox decided to return to the old WoD as their core product, the new WoD was renamed to the Chronicles of Darkness to reduce brand confusion keeping in mind that 'old' and 'new' are fan labels, not official ones).

                                                            None of them are 100% compatible with each other. The WoD is notorious for not even being consistent between game-lines (the Dark Ages games for Revised and 20th being the largest deviations). For example, in some of the WoD games, flanking bonuses are dice bonuses, and in others they're difficulty bonuses. In combat, as a relatively mechanically intense part of the games, what are generally minor differences start to compound the most as more mechanical complexity increases.

                                                            I personally default to the 20th base rules for the games that got them, or the Revised rules for the games that didn't, when it comes to the WoD, and the 2e rules for CofD. But there's not inherently correct answer for what you're trying to do, you just need to settle somewhere.
