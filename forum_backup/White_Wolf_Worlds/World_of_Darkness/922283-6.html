            Vampire: The Week of Nightmares happened alright, and the Red Star is still up there, and if another antediluvian rises a century from now, those guys will really be picking up the the pace compared to the last millennium. How come aeons turn so much slower than new iPhones come out?

            The Camarilla, as a whole, are hypocrites, in that they love humans in principal but aren’t social animals themselves. Their aversion to killing just means they like to keep blood dolls trapped in hellish abusive relationships for decades on end. Their strict laws about individual territory prevents them from socializing much outside of Elysium, and this encourages them to idealize one another. On the whole, each individual in the Camarilla thinks he or she is the only one who’s a horrible person.

            Werewolf: The garou religion is a more accurate interpretation of umbral politics than most other religions, but it’s still a religion. There are mages in the upper echelons of every major world religion who are in contact with ancient spirits, but that doesn't mean they know everything, either. Most spirits have poor memories and narrow ranges of experience, and like telling fleshies what they want to hear.

            The Wyrm is a cohesive political faction in the spirit world, but it only originated shortly before the fall of the Black Spiral Dancers.

            “Weaver spirits” are just spirits that have allied themselves with humanity, and don’t otherwise have a common leadership or necessarily like each other. They typically dislike shapeshifters because shapeshifters are typically dangerous to humans. Some spirits do believe in the Weaver as an ideal, but the universe only cares what humans believe.

            “The Wyld” is wishful thinking that all the spirits who aren't hostile to shapeshifters on general principle have something else in common as well. Many “Wyld spirits” have learned to take it as a compliment, some haven’t.

            It’s fair to say the Black Spiral Dancers are bad as werewolves go, but from a human perspective, even the Children of Gaia and Stargazers are in the same moral neighborhood as human organized crime families and terrorist groups.

            Until a little over a century ago, the Black Spirals were almost universally maniacal, hedonistic, rampaging monsters. That was also about when they stopped being one of the smallest tribes.

            It’s now the elder Black Spirals who constitute most of the stereotypical genocidal psychopaths, but those guys aren't happy with most of tribe. While it’s true that the BSDs have major endemic problems with drugs and rape culture, at least half the other tribes can compete in at least one of those departments each.

            The majority of BSDs join because either they’re metis, their kids are metis, or they just don't want to base their lives around garou dogma anymore. Come on, free range beef is expensive. You go to one ritual, and then you lay low and lend crash space to rampaging tweakers when need be so they’ll protect you when your old pack tries to deprogram you with a klaive.

            So the BSDs get the revised Sabbat treatment. It’s not that they’re right, or that they’re nicer folks on the whole than the other tribes, it’s that the problem is the Garou Nation’s bigotry. In themselves, the BSDs are pretty ineffective no matter how you slice it. Their main purposes from the Wyrm’s perspective are to keep werewolves “on the bench” and distract the other tribes from real problems.

            Mage: The Ascension War is over. At least the first one. Both factions are thoroughly infiltrated by nephandi and/or harbor a lot of widderslaintes who don’t know or care that they’re nephandi. No one’s sure which. Some mages have also been spontaneously going Barrabus since the Avatar Storm started.

            The Technocracy and Traditions are both rapidly splintering. The Celestial Chorus has partially joined the Technocracy. The Void Engineers look like they might go the other way. The Adepts and Euthanatos have more or less gone rogue. The Order of Hermes just doesn’t get much love since the whole Ksifari fiasco, so the Hollow Ones have become the de facto leader Tradition even though they probably harbor the most nephandi. The Iteration X’ers are still fanatically loyal to the Technocracy, if only because of their increasingly vocal adoration of the machine god Autochthon, which really creeps out the rest of the conventions. The Ahl-I-Batin are attempting to rejoin the Traditions, but they’re not sure who’s supposed to ratify that at this point.

            There are also three emerging cross-paradigm group of mages from all the Traditions and the Conventions. The first one is allied with certain demons and imbued, and they don’t have much in common except hatred of (most) non-humans. The second is trying to create a coalition of non-humans. The third faction just doesn’t understand why trafficking with demons is acceptable all of a sudden, and think demons are causing the increase in nephandi.

            Wraith: Demons helped with the relief effort during the Sixth Maelstrom, and have since been rebuilding the Dark Kingdoms in their image. They’ve also created a new type of undead to serve as Stygia’s agents in the land of the living.

            Changeling: The Shadow Court has shed its secrecy since the Second Resurgence. The general consensus is that the balance of power between the courts should be restored, but no one is sure how. The general mindset that humans are the enemy is getting more socially acceptable in Concordia, as is modern technology.

            Hunter: By 2006, the imbued had started organizing into largish cells, and every supernatural faction wanted as many of those cells in its pockets as would fit. By now, most of the imbued are allied with demons and mages and many are trying to convince themselves they’re not mercenaries.

            Demon: Demons are also losing interest in their own factions in favor of throwing in with established players. They’re floaters who don’t have much of a cause of their own since they still can’t find Lucifer. They’re basically seen as the spirit equivalent of an out of work veteran who isn’t allowed near schools driving a car with no plates, so they take what comes their way. A lot of them work for mages and a few have assimilated into changeling society, since they can enter the Dreaming, unlike the Umbra (Heaven).

            I generally use Demon as chassis for my WoD. Fera and vampires are surviving Rabisu and Halaku nephilim, respectively. Vampires, the Underworld, and the Dreaming were all created by demons as attempts to preserve human knowledge across generations. The Umbra is the ruins of Heaven. Spirits are angels, but most of them have lost their consciousness and/or sanity since God was assassinated. The Wyrm is a faction of angels and demons who believe God’s Creation doesn't deserve to exist without God.

            Oh yeah, and the first generation of nephilim are now in their teens, as is the Pure Metis. This may be bad, because some demons remember something about the original nephilim having been destroyed because when they bred together, their children were more powerful than either parent, so in enough generations they could have rivaled God. Metis infertility was probably why the fera were spared. This process already seems to be setting in with dhampir.

                I love this thread I do and am going to throw some just random stuff in about characters and the world.

                Mortal Authorities

                + For a short description of the police in my games, I go with, "Slightly more incompetent and corrupt than the ones in Gotham City." If the police arrive at a situation involving the supernatural they're likely to cut and run but if they do stay, then they're likely to random shoot at everything and make up whatever story they can.

                The police are almost always under the control of vampires in the WoD but they're unaware of the truth--instead believing they're taking bribes from the uber-rich and mafia of the city. In my WOD, every police station has a room for working over suspects, isolating criminals until they confess to whatever is done, and evidence lockers where cash as well as drugs routinely disappear. It's not at all how I feel about the police in RL save perhaps the worst places in America but it fits my view of the WoD.

                + The United States government and most governments in general have a large amount of knowledge about how the supernatural works. However, virtually all of the people who know are corrupt or working hand-in-hand with them. Generally, when someone finds out the truth, they get visited by the Technocracy or vampires to get offered to be brought into the fold and it works well.

                + Bob Schnoblin was Director of the CIA under George W. Bush.

                + Two Presidents were ghouls and Marcus Vitel used to direct the Federal government by fixing US elections.

                Werewolf: The Apocalypse

                + Endron Oil is a stand-in for Enron Oil in my game and went bankrupt in much the same way. However, this being the WOD, it got bailed out by the government and is back to 100% effectiveness.

                Well, it would be except for the fact it's now under vampire management.

                + Harold Zettler is an atheist who doesn't believe in the Wyrm and assumes all of his cohorts are worshiping the Antediluvians or demons while he milks the Pentex cow for all its worth.

                + King Albretch fully believes he's going to be assassinated long before the Apocalypse (and doesn't actually believe in it) but is trying to push whatever reforms he can before it happens.

                + Evan has to hang around Albretch because a Caucasian Wendigo born in the Suburbs is someone no one takes seriously. He's all-but-decided to defect to the Children of Gaia as soon as he can.

                + Albretch and Mari broke the Litany and both are pretty terrified about that being found out (and doing it again).

                + Samual Haight was actually an entirely serious werewolf hunter who killed 20 Garou while mortal before getting his ritual that allowed him to become a Skinwalker. When he died, he was resurrected as an Abomination by his followers before being finally put down. It's only Garou "fireside" and the Nuwisha stories which made him ridiculous.

                + Number Two in Malpheas is the Silver Fang King who began the War of Rage. A lot of the Garou's beliefs about their religion, customs, and practices come from him. The Mokole know this but trying to share that memory usually results in a fight.

                Vampire: The Masquerade

                + Lucita starred in a bunch of Spanish language exploitation films in the 1970s as she doesn't have a problem with nudity and thought they were awesome. She also starred in a few English ones directed by the Kindred Johann. How she appeared in film is anyone's guess.

                This includes an adaptation of Vampirella.

                + Most Malkavians aren't actually wacky at all but plenty of them play up the stereotype so people will keep them out of the Jyhad. If a Malkavian is doing that, he wants you to leave him the **** alone.

                + Anatole is a master of this despite the fact God and the Antediluvians literally speak to him.

                + Lodin isn't dead is actually a conspiracy theory and Edward Neally eventually had himself Vicissituded to look like his former boss.

                + Balthazar is an enormous Dukes of Hazard fan and the fact he's Canadian with no Southern blood whatsoever is a joke. He's also actually possessed of a staggering resemblance to Charlie Sheen and is quite handsome if he ever bothers to clean up.

                + Beckett is actually a black Londoner (this may be canon).

                + Vykos is a Loyalist in my game and absolutely despises the idea of Sabbat becoming like the Camarilla so he constantly stirrs shit with the younglings and undermines his fellow Elders.

                + Theo Bell is ripping off Wesley Snipe's blade and pretending he came up with it first.

                        My vampire games usually borrow from both VtR and VtM. Covenants are sects and they tend to be more aggressive with each other than in the VtR standard setting.
                        One of the key differences though, is that no vampire knows the true origin of vampirism. There's something like the fog of eternity that messes with the elder's memory.
                        Vampires in game use the term "bloodline" to refer to clans and Clan is an IC term that refers to those bloodlines that have a standing in the Camarilla and a sit in the Inner Circle. Clans within a city may offer protection to other bloodlines under their Clan banner.

                        During character creation players can choose between roughly 35 available different bloodlines (there's like other 10 more that are NPC only) grouped by the VtR categories (Lord, Haunt, Shadow, Beast or Succubi) including the 13 original clans and the 5 VtR standard clans and some of the bloodlines found in the nwod books (some have been homebrewed other merged for example Lygos and Badacelli belong to the same bloodline of blind darkness worshiping underground vampires).

                        Werewolves are Lupines instead of the de facto werewolf games. They're divided in three bigger factions: The Pure, The Forsaken and the The Reformed (working on the name). The larger faction are the Reformed which are werewolves trying to coupe with being "cursed" with The Change and try to hold to their own Humanity the most. They chain themselves during the full moon and many try to find cures or rescue other kinfolk or werewolves from the other factions or try to fight back against outsiders a lot like guerrilla style resistant groups. These are the most numerous. Then there's The Pure. Not much is changed except that the Fire Touched are more like a Tribe of darwinian gangs/fanatical group fighting each other for territory. The Forsaken do their thing mostly outside of the cities. Children of Gaia are trying to protect the earth but they're pacifists and healers. Here you can find sone other Fera but for the most part the Childeen are considered legends or folk tales.

                            How do I modify my WoD setting from the baseline? Gawd, it might be easier (though less fun) to list what I haven't modified. To start with, all the myths and legends of how XYZ came to exist? All myths, all legends. But all have an element of the truth. I created my own Grand Unified Theory (of the World of Darkness) to explain how Garou cosmology fits in with Vampire's Judeo-Christian base. For now, I'll focus on my changes to the big one; Vampire. Some details are left out, since one of my players in-frequents this board and somehow will probably stumble on to this very post and read things I'm not ready to unveil.

                            Caine is still the progenitor of his species. He still made the 2nd generation (the "Lost Generation", according to my notes). The thirteen clans are descended from the Antideluvians, who are all of the 3rd Generation. And Caine is probably the first vampire.

                            But not all vampires descend from Caine. I re-tooled the Keui-jin to use the same system as Cainites, then created other "Vampire Variants". Each of these separate lines of Vampires have different weaknesses and strengths, each come from different areas of the world (some regions even having two or three different "vampire strains"), and each claims their "Patient Zero" was the first vampire. But the Cainite "strain" is the easiest to "pass on", hence why they're the most wide-spread. Some of these lineages might only have a handful of remaining "carriers", with the rest having been wiped out in their own, personal apocalypse. Others might have hundreds or thousands of members who coexist among Cainites (including one of the official bloodlines whose origins I retconned). But most are few in number, keep to their "ancestral lands", and stay out of the spotlight for reasons of their own.

                            As for the Cainite Antediluvians, I upped their numbers to (redacted). The thirteen big ones are the most prolific, to be sure. Of the ones remaining, some never sired more than one or two childer (or none at all). Some died during some minor apocalypse or another. A couple formed small clans centered on a specific region. Some had weaknesses so crippling that they and their clans withered away to nothing by the modern nights. But even though there's more than thirteen, only the big thirteen can claim any relevance in the modern nights. Like the other "Vampire Offshoots" (except the K-Js, who have significant enough numbers), these "lesser Antediluvians" are more of a curiosity, a relic of the past, something for a Malkavian scientist obsessed with the origin of the species to research about and include in the footnotes of his papers.

                            For the small number of kindred who know about this "broader vampire world", it brings up new questions about Gehenna. If it's about the Antes rising up and eating all other vampires, does that just mean Cainites, or do the other "strains" suffer at their fangs as well? Or do their own "great-great-grandpappies" rise and eat them while the Antes are chowing down on the Cainites? Are those other guys spared? And when Caine rises up, is he alone? Is his big final confrontation with god, or is it really with these other "lineage progenitors"? And of course, there's always the question about whether the progenitors of these other vampire lineages are actually stories about Caine corrupted by time (or of course, is Caine actually a corruption of their story)? Is the reason some of these lineages are so small and numerically depleted because they already went through their own "Gehenna" and, if so, does that mean Gehenna is something Cainites can survive through, albeit in limited numbers?

                            I like to add things like these "Kindred offshoots" whenever it can add some level of doubt to the existing story. Especially when I give the caveat of "all of this, of course, might be clever Setite lies and Malkavian ramblings".

                                It's next to impactless in the game, but Avalon Incorporated, the Pentex subsidiary, employs something called the Avalon Standard Dimensions on its action figures. This means that all action figures across all its lines are of the same rough size, meaning that all weapons, vehicles and accessories can be used by all action figures.

                                Why? Because with all action figures being compatible they are more fun when playing with many of them at the same time. Which means that kids will want more of them, which in turn is profit for Pentex.
