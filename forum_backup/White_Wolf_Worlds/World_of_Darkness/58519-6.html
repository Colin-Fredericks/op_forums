    I can recommend Chloe Neil's Chicagolands Vampire series, particularly the first book, Some Girls Bite. Sort of an urban (Chicago-based) counterpart to True Blood, it occurs after the Masquerade comes down, and vampires admit they exist:

    Originally posted by Some Girls Bite
    Sure, the life of a graduate student wasn't exactly glamorous, but it was Merit's. She was doing fine until a rogue vampire attacked her. But he only got a sip before he was scared away by another bloodsucker and this one decided the best way to save her life was to make her the walking undead.

    Turns out her savior was the master vampire of Cadogan House. Now she's traded sweating over her thesis for learning to fit in at a Hyde Park mansion full of vamps loyal to Ethan "Lord o' the Manor" Sullivan. Of course, as a tall, green-eyed, four-hundred- year-old vampire, he has centuries-worth of charm, but unfortunately he expects her gratitude - and servitude. But an inconvenient sunlight allergy and Ethan's attitude are the least of her concerns. Someone's still out to get her. Her initiation into Chicago's nightlife may be the first skirmish in a war and there will be blood.
    Later volumes were a bit too . . . "motivated" in showing how vampires and other supernaturals get along, but they weren't bad. And the first book was fun, showing Merit's first days as a vampire, from her Embrace to her first big fight. The scene where she and her (human) roommate try to figure out how to drink bagged blood . . .Two grad students, hunched over the counter, poking at a bag of blood with straws. . .

    Or if you want a Hunter-esque series (nWoD or classic), Larry Correia's Monster Hunter International is a good choice. It's basically . . . the NRA takes on the Cthulhu Mythos ("We need more guns - no, the big ones!" . . . "Hey! Tim brought a rocket launcher!" . . . "Dibs on the flamethrower!" ). And the author's famously said, "the only reason a vampire should sparkle is if I've doused it in napalm":

    Five days after Owen Zastava Pitt pushed his insufferable boss out of a fourteenth story window, he woke up in the hospital with a scarred face, an unbelievable memory, and a job offer.

    It turns out that monsters are real. All the things from myth, legend, and B-movies are out there, waiting in the shadows. Officially secret, some of them are evil, and some are just hungry. On the other side are the people who kill monsters for a living. Monster Hunter International is the premier eradication company in the business. And now Owen is their newest recruit.

    It’s actually a pretty sweet gig, except for one little problem. An ancient entity known as the Cursed One has returned to settle a centuries old vendetta. Should the Cursed One succeed, it means the end of the world, and MHI is the only thing standing in his way. With the clock ticking towards Armageddon, Owen finds himself trapped between legions of undead minions, belligerent federal agents, a cryptic ghost who has taken up residence inside his head, and the cursed family of the woman he loves.

    Business is good . .
    .

    If you've ever wanted to run a Hunter game where the government knows about the supernatural, and is (quietly) sponsoring teams to hunt it down, MHI is right up your alley. Average humanity is still in the dark about the supernatural, but various mercenary firms and bounty hunters in-the-know compete for the bounties sponsored by the government. The average zombie's only worth a couple bucks, but taking down a Great Old One can net you tens of millions.

    Really, its quite at home in the World of Darkness. And I dare any Storytellers out there to read the series, and not want to rip off Decision Week.

        Only Lovers Left Alive: this movie is a little boring but great fodder for clan toreador's quiet moments and a great look at the clan's mind set.

        Once Upon Time: this show IS Changeling: The Dreaming

        The Vampire Diaries and the Originals: No seriously want to know how neonates and elders work politics and intrigue? CHeck out these shows.

        American Horror Story:

        Murder House: Great Wraith and Hunter Fodder

        Asylum: probably better for New World of Darkness Changeling and Hunter but still good material for Demon The Fallen

        Coven: despite its flaws actually a pretty look at chantry spats and inter tradition feuds

        Freak show: not so much....yeah lets just skip this one (though Twisty was still pretty awesome)

        Hotel: The Countess is a good look at just how evil and terrifying a Toreador (or antitribu) can be, and Sally and the Addiction Demon make a great look at a Wraith and her Shadow

        Gotham: a good look at World of Darkness cities

        The 100: What would happen if a bunch of Void Engineers and extraordinary citizens got trapped across the Gauntlet in Pangaea?

        Ash vs. Evil Dead: Hunter and Demon

        Agents of S.H.I.E.L,D,: Technocrats as the good guys
