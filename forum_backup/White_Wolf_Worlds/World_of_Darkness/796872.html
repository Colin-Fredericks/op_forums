    C:tD20 -- The Black Compendium: What a strectch goal it would be!

    12-30-2015, 12:51 PM
    I wanted to repeat here that my dream for a future reward would be a Book of Nod-style book for Changeling, because the game line didn't get one, and it is time.

    I would call it the Book of Compendia, because it would give us the Black Compendium in its entirety (or at least the known fragments): narrating the founding of Arcadia, the War of Trees and the subsequent departure of the Tuatha dé Danaan for the Far Shores. It would tell us of the victory against the White City of Bones, after their White Fomorian lords had imprisoned a tribe of the sidhe and transformed it into the first Thallain, of the Nine Final Battles, of the Triumph Casque of Sorrows, of the shattering of its key into nine pieces by the Red King, and of the establishment of the kingdoms of the Five Beasts of the Dreaming. It would also tell us of the Compact between the fae and humans. And it will tell us more on the Heron Women and Crawfish Men, of the twin of Arike.

    Small titbits and fragments of the Green Compendium (the one which tells not what happened but what could have happened) may even narrate the quest of Lord Gamine to free Arcadia and the Court of High King Ardlanth from the Darkening Curse, and the tyranny of his regent, Bernard Assjack. It could tell us of the rebellion of Prince Yrtalien, and of his subsequent exile.

    This book would be illustrated by Rebecca Guay, Melissa Uran, and Tony Diterlizzi, of course.

    Would you second this wish, this dream?

                                          Originally posted by Lian View Post

Then you have SIlver Record and Fragile path which try to do something different, they are a diverse collection of stories and styles.                                           I agree. The Biblical style isn't very appropriate. I also think it probably won't be on a level with Elizabethan drama and verse, but then I don't think it needs to be.

                                          There are older lays, epics, romances and ballads which might actually be better models anyway. These often exist now in translation, as many were written in French or Middle English, and that gives a good 'in' for both a writer and reader, because it allows for a more contemporary/accessible style of language. Contemporary translations of Beowulf, for instance, are rather plainspeaking but still poetic, and are much easier to read as a result.

                                          Elizabethan English, meanwhile, is dense but still pretty much the same language we use now, so there's no excuse for a 'translation' and therefore I think it will be a much harder read for a gaming product.

                                          Additionally, the older texts would feel more authentic anyway. Fae history is ancient, so you'd expect something that extends back at least as far as the Middle Ages.
