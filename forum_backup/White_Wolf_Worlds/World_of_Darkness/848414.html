    SMT x WoD

    03-16-2016, 05:32 PM
    Are there any Shin Megami Tensei fans here?

    Personally, I'm a great fan of the series.
    I also use SMT 3 as a base for my WoD games.

    so...have you ever used SMT in your games? how?



    EDIT:

    My idea was to make WoD one of the worlds in the vortex. It would be forced to go through Conception, through a cycle of Death and Rebirth.

    MtA
    In the previous Conception, the Reason that triumphed was that of freedom of thinking and Consensual reality.

    WtA
    the Triat is the equivalent of Kagutsushi.
    After Conception, the victor triggers the Wyld, and triggers the Weaver (according to his Reason).

    Gaïa, like Aradia, is one of the Gods from the Worlds that failed Conception.
    Gaïa seeks to bring Balance to the WoD and prevent the cycle to repeat itself.

    DtF
    Lucifer, like the one in SMT, seeks to destroy the Triat and break the cycle imposed by God.

    VtM
    Caine was an inhabitant in the previous world, and reborn in the new world. He felt uneasy with the new world order, but couldn't tell why.
    Caine eventually kills his brother. In doing so, he breaks the consensus, thus Triggering the Wyrm to rebalance.
    When the Wyrm is triggered, it goes through a process of balance, but fails and instead starts corrupting and consuming WoD energy....until the wheel of ages stops and a new Conception is called.


    New Conception:
    I thaught Gehenna, Ascension, Apocalypse would serve as either the "new Conception" or "the WoD turning into a Dead world by losing its energy". But I still don't know how to shape it.

      A while back, I wrote up a background for a Mage Craft (that would around 2004 get folded into the Virtual Adepts), who use "demon" (rather Umbrood) summoning programs like in the first two Shin Megami Tensei games. Heck, I even sorta made the origin of the original demon summoning program tie into the plot of the original novel the entire series was based on (basically, some Japanese student adapts Hermetic Theurgy into a computer computer). I thought the cyberpunk elements of SMT 1 and SMT 2 were fascinating, and would work well to the Virtual Adepts in general.

      (By the way, I recommend you check these earlier games out. They are a different experience from that of SMT 3, but they fit better with the cyberpunk ethos that influenced early Mage. I recommend the Let's Plays by now-retired YouTuber RaidouFrost: SMT 1, SMT 2.)

      Not to leave SMT 3 out, I had as a part of this idea be that a coterie of these Mages be engaged in creating Terminals that allowed them to access something like the Amala Network, which is a system of halls connecting to various parts of both the Material world and the Umbra. And that, by building Artifacts in those forms, they create a web connecting their Craft's members - and any other Tradition Mages willing to pay - to different locations throughout the world. Which would be very useful for folks looking to move great distances without the Technocracy having flight records of them. And they would be largely immovable pillars situated inside Sancta, so it's not a Paradox problem like trying to Correspondence yourself around. But of course, the Amala Network would have dangers, and possibly an insidious origin, to be determined by the Storyteller.

      I eventually found the ideas to be a bit too on-the-nose, but I can easily see a Virtual Adept (or Hermetic House Thig) player patterning their Spirit-heavy character around the idea.

      I also did a write-up of a race of artificial humans based on the Manikins. The way I had it, they came about in two ways: 1) a Mage or Sorcerer created them for their own purposes, or 2) the arose spontaneously from the ground, when a collection of Wraiths, that were too weak or tattered to remain viable on their own, collected in one spot and bonded together into a rough gestalt. The latter type arises from the ground, because the Earth is the element that gives life most readily. And the phenomenon would have precedent in the World of Darkness, being something that just happened every century or two. But these Manikins - born without coherent memory or purpose, yet cursed by notable and constant shudders and tics - would become considerably more common following the Sixth Great Maelstrom, when millions of souls were torn apart by winds and scattered everywhere. And that while whole Wraiths who survived the disaster would find their bodies and become zombies, those that were merely fragments would coalesce together into thousands of patchwork spirits. Perhaps Gaia sought more agents to beat back the coming Apocalypse, or perhaps a more impersonal Earth just sought to do something with all these masses of spirits. Whatever the case, hundreds or even thousands of Manikins popped out of the ground, and for the first time in history form their own fledgling societies. Clusters of twitchy, lost humanoids huddling together in the midst of regular homeless people, in abandoned buildings or under bridges.

      Perhaps some of them, with the concurrent blowing of the winds of the Avatar Storm, might unknowingly get shards of Avatar lodged in their souls, and proceed to Awaken. Individual Manikin Mages, developing their magick independently, and slowly trying to lead their Manikin communities towards greatness.

      Magestuck: The Ascension

                      Cause, from what I've red in mage (not much I admit), the 2 settings do conflict in some points.
                      I tried to make the two fit as well as possible, though I admit it was a bit difficult. Mage characters would use computers and Demon Summoning Programs as Instruments for Spirit magick, while unAwakened Technosorcers used them for much the same thing using the Path of Summoning, Binding, And Warding.

                      One of the on-the-nose parts were the fact that many within the Craft divide themselves between Law and Chaos sides, the one summoning mostly heavenly umbrood, while the latter summoning more "pagan" or wild umbrood. But in addition to being really artificial, I had a problem with the division in my treatment for the same reason I didn't care for the Law/Chaos axis in the SMT series: it's overly simple and reductionist. While there's no reason why a Craft of digital devil summoners couldn't develop and ethos that divided the whole of the Umbra into Law, Chaos, and Neutral alignments, it didn't seem very realistic, nor would the rest of Mage society likely respect such a simplistic view of spirits.

                      Then again, most Mages would find it very concerning about a bunch of punk kids actively dealing with beings they themselves view as "Demons". Regardless of the actual nature of the Umbrood, that smacks of potential Infernalism. Even the Order of Hermes prefers, when it deals with devils, to deal with the astral representations of devils, rather than the actual, living-in-hell-and-stealing-souls devils. Yes, there is a difference, when the Umbra is filled with many figures quite plainly manifested specifically because humans believe in them.


                      As for the End Of The World business, while it has merit, I personally prefer not to go into it. My write-ups were specifically from the perspective of slotting SMT elements into the regular World of Darkness, where all the supernatural elements operate in shadow wars, just beneath the veneer of the mundane status quo. You are free to explore the use of Vortex Worlds as a storytelling engine, though.

                              Originally posted by Pleiades View Post
                              Well, it's not that big of a deal. The Sabbat Inquisition tends to tag every spirit summoner/dealer as a potential infernalist and it didn't impact the story very much.
                              I'm not sure how it would go for mages, though.
                              In the Sabbat's case, it would be a much bigger deal if a Storyteller took the anti-Infernalist crusade seriously. It's meant to be an option for conflict in games.

                              In Mage, Infernalism is a fairly big deal. There's of course the Nephandi - the Mages who invert their magical souls and dedicate their lives to evil and destruction. They frequently serve Demonic, Wyrmish, or Eldritch powers. Nephandi are the one faction that every other Mage faction will drop everything to destroy, preferably by capturing them and destroying their Avatar (Mage soul), so they don't reincarnate again down the line. Tradition Mages, Technocrats, and Marauders all see them as the absolute evil...mostly because they are.

                              Basically, the Nephandi are the Mage version of the Baali.

                              Just as Vampires have non-Baali Infernalists, though, so do Mages occasionally come up against non-Nephandi Infernalists. You don't need an inverted Avatar to serve a demon (it just helps). Most Mage factions are also on the look-out for members who give in and summon demons from Actual Hell. Both because they are troublesome on their own, and because they could find their way to becoming Nephandi.

                              Again, it's as big a deal as a Storyteller wants to make it, but I would think that a group that openly claims to summon "Demons" would come under scrutiny. Even if only a few of them encounter Demons from Actual Hell, that's a chance some Mages would rather not take. It's why if I were to try to adapt SMT to Mage now, I'd make them into those Virtual Adepts (and members of Hermetic House Thig) who deal with Umbrood via technology. It's already kind of there in the Mage text already, so it's not a hard sell.
