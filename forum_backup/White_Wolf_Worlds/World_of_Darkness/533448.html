                                  Originally posted by Saint Michael View Post

                                  Page seems to be dead but archive.org saved a copy here. Weirdly the archived version sometimes turns to black after first loading the page, but here's the OP of that thread:
                                  If you haven't read the Void Engineers Convention Book and don't want to be spoiled for the true nature of Threat Null (a secret the VEs keep, and many storytellers may not want to have spoiled at their table), stop reading - because it's pretty implicit here...

                                  So since Threat Null exists, shouldn't these guys as well? Technocrats weren't the only ones on the wrong side when the Storm hit.

                                  Now obviously since these threats don't share a paradigmatic belief in "Control", they aren't held together like Threat Null, which likely contributes them to not being a COLLECTIVE threat in the same way. They're as likely to attack/lure/whatever each other as PCs.


                                  Nirvana: The Self is an illusion. These agents of enlightenment will free you of deception and ignorance. Their light removes all boundary between the consciousness and the external world. Harm to one thing afflicts everything, all hidden truths exposed, emotions and agendas melt together between enemies and friends alike. None meet them and return unchanged, and many never reclaim an individual identity to speak of, becoming part of their egoless light from their gazing third-eyes.

                                  Archons: Agents of the Source - or so they claim. Messengers and Avengers of Divine Commandments. Deliverers of Terrible Miracles and Divine Judgment. Usually appearing as angelic visions of primal flame.

                                  Liberalines: Chaotic forces of freedom from all bonds of restraint - be it authority, actual imprisonment, your own moral restraints, or the laws of physics. Rarely does the same one appear the same twice, as bizarre form modification through ecstatic self-mutilation is a norm.

                                  Moroi: Voormas & his ilk. These macabre liches carry the Maelstrom of the Low Umbra in their wake, and will bring the Underworld to the living and the rest of the Umbra to end the boundary of death all together. [I know this is an inversion of what the Euthanatos actually believe; but the extreme of that (ending the world to turn the Cycle on a cosmic scale) is what the Nephandi already have covered - so the traditional Necromancer shtick of Voormas fits well enough here. Of course Senex is still human and upholds the actual Euthanatos ideals against Voormas]

                                  Exarchs: "Will-Workers" taken to the megalomaniacal extreme. Would be God-Kings who burn with elemental power, bending the Umbra to their will as personal spirit courts. Regal Exemplars of Hubris and Arete (in the Greek sense) wearing their own manifest authority in crowns of lightning.

                                  The Pattern: Strange observers, slightly off from normal human, but often still supremely innocuous. The universe is their science experiment. Diversely strange phenomena and their often fatal results are inflicted and studied. Everyone on a void ship switches bodies suddenly. Space warps into an endless loop trapping subway riders inside while their train infinitely accelerates. A spontaneous pregnancy results in a hyper-evolved homo-neandrathalus child. All in the name of science.

                                  The Sacrifice: Blood-Soaked vassals of nature itself. Animal-skulled heads, antlers, cloaks of sea-brined hands, endless bleeding runes, etc etc mark the chthonic atavists of the Sacrifice. The cycle of nature will be upheld, and the blood of those who would hold themselves apart from its tole (like all human civilization) will grease the turn.

                                  Singularity: solipsistic utopias exist in the virtual realities of the Digital Web. Like fairy tales of old, one must resist the temptations they offer, and long-since bodiless simulators as well, or disappear into a personal paradise never to return.
                                  And in later posts they added
                                  Left out the Dreamspeakers specifically to parallel the Void Engineers.
                                  and
                                  face_p0lluti0n on rpg.net had a great idea for twisted Dreamspeakers.

                                  The Emissaries: Half-human, half-spirit hybrids, they wish to bridge the gap not only between humans and spirits, but to merge the Umbra with Earth, replacing the entire world of physicality with the symbolic mechanics of the deepest Umbral realms. Instead of keeping the peace between humans and spirits as they once did, they find it more effective to simple combine the two.

                                                  I've commented elsewhere that I was disappointed with TR Unit 8. A big part of that is that the section on Threat Null was the most detailed part of it; but it was largely a rehash of what the Void Engineers Convention Book provided. And the three parts that weren't rehashes were uninspired: “Threat Marauder: what if a large contingent of the Union is literally Mad Scientists? Threat Caul: what if the Nephandi have infiltrated the Union? The Sentinels: the Threat Null counterpart to the Void Engineers is indistinguishable from the Void Engineers.”

                                                  I would be more inclined to replace Threat Marauder with Threat Negation: that is, take the basic premise of Quiet running rampant in the Union's ranks; but say that that Quiet leans heavily toward Denial, and that the resulting Marauders are Negation Men. Threat Caul is basically another name for the Technephandi, so I see little point in mentioning them here.

                                                  And instead of Sentinels, I'd rather the Threat Null counterpart to the VEs be Aliens: old-style explorer-type former VEs who have “gone native” and are rarely encountered because they're too busy exploring the depths of the Deep Universe and no longer have any interest in Earth or its affairs. That is, until they realize that Earth's resources are something that they can't survive without, at which point they turn around and become Alien Invaders.

                                                  And as for the other four Conventions, I like El Barto's thoughts: a Threat Null that starts establishing beachheads on Earth might start to look like what he's describing.

                                                  It would make for a good Storyteller's Vault supplement, I think.
