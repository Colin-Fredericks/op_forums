    V6 alpha initial playtest report

    08-17-2026, 10:25 AM
    We played our first playtest last night. It took some getting used to the new systems, and there were a couple of things we didn't get to try, but it was overall fine and we had fun. Taking into account that this is an alpha playtest and so probably has lots of early ideas (likely way more than they'll use), these are my initial impressions:

    Core mechanics
    • TN 8+ was fine.
    • Concerns about odds of success seem overblown. The players didn't seem to succeed or fail too often. Each player had at least one painful failure, which is a product of the much smaller dice pools.
    • I initially wanted 10s to explode like VTR2e, since it gives neater probabilities (about a third of a success per die, instead of just 0.3), but since you only need one success to succeed and extra successes rarely do anything else, Quickening essentially means you're carrying 10s forward as bonus dice on later tests. It makes sense.
    • Quickening is basically just Willpower that keys off success.
    • Quickening does make it more exciting when you roll 10s, but I wouldn't say it's as exciting as rolling a natural 20, since it only gives a very minor buff (basically, +1 die per point). If it were me, I'd probably award it less frequently and make it more powerful, or at least allow it to be used more dynamically (e.g., to do tricks/gambits/feats in combat). Either way, it's not objectionable.
    • The default roll always requires a negative modifier of some kind (usually –2 to –3 dice from Difficulty), which suggests they might need to recalibrate their baseline. Average Difficulty (or Challenging) feels like it should be +/-0 dice; Routine (or Average) should not be a penalty; it should be +1 die, minimum, so they should scale it upwards from there. AFAICS, there's no unopposed roll where you don't deduct dice at all, which just feels wrong. Why not just make all Attributes one dot lower in the first place?
    • Applying dice pool penalties to every roll also feeds into the feeling that dice pools are significantly smaller. People wanted their fistfuls of dice.
    • There's possibly not enough wiggle room in the system. At zero dice, you automatically fail but at four dice you can automatically succeed at anything outside of conflict. The range between "totally impossible" and "so trivial I don't have to roll" is too small.

    Dice pools and modifiers

    It takes too long now to make a dice pool. This is due to a few things:
    • There are too many modifiers in general (circumstantial Difficulties, opponent-based Difficulties, action-based Difficulties, and hunting modifiers). They should condense the four pages of Difficulties and modifiers into a single table.
    • The order of operations for building a dice pool seems janky. Difficulty should be expressed as a modifier (–5 rather than 5), not least because the system combines Difficulty modifiers with separate dice pool penalties. There's an extra step involved when you have to first add Difficulty modifiers, then invert them to a negative, and then add the dice pool modifiers on top. If something is Difficulty 2 with a +1 Difficulty modifier, you have to remind yourself that a further –1 penalty actually brings that to –4 (-[2+1]-1) and not –2 (-[2+1-1]). Small things like this add up at the table.
    • Besides the time required to build dice pools, players felt the actual resolution was quicker once they rolled, since they didn't need to compare outcomes. But since I can roll at the same time as them, it wasn't really quicker for me, as I had to watch them building their pools every time and that took quite some time.
    • As an ST, I was busy enough not to miss rolling dice, but there's definitely more for players to track now.

    NPCs and opposition
    • NPCs felt pretty flat. One number for everything was dull. I'm hoping that's just an abstraction for the playtest and that they'll at least get different ratings for physical/mental/social. NPC Level should be an optional rule in any case.
    • Since they do flat damage, fights become very predictable. They either do no damage or their Level in damage. The only risk is if the players roll a painful failure and opt to take additional damage. PvP combat (opposed tests) is more swingy and would probably feel better as the default system.
    • I spent Drama regularly, but had three points by the end, so the game escalated nicely at that point. Because of how things panned out, they encountered three major obstacles at the end, instead of spread over the course of the session, and were very clever about managing them. I think that made it feel like they'd earned their getaway.
    • There is an issue in that you don't want to have teleporting NPCs who just arrive from thin air. I tried to make the arrivals logical, which is why the police, third wight, and hounds all essentially ended up arriving at the same time.

    Vitae
    • We didn't mind the merging of health and the blood pool conceptually, but it does put a lot of pressure on that one health track. Making more Disciplines key off Willpower was helpful there, but it does lead to some weird interactions.
    • For example, if you injure another vampire before diablerising them, you'll already find they've got less vitae in them. Meanwhile, mortals have to have a separate pool of 10 blood points that isn't affected by injury, because otherwise you'd be sacrificing blood every time you hurt a vessel.
    • Fluff-wise, there are some issues with suspension of disbelief as well. In prior editions, vitae was spent to heal wounds, so you might end up with an empty blood pool but you're fully healed. It takes a lot to beat the vitae out of a torpid vampire. But in the alpha, any injury removes vitae, so it's almost like you're spending vitae to automatically heal the damage, but the damage stays anyway. One of my players joked that it felt like he was just gushing blood any time someone hurt him, like that scene in The Addams Family.
    • I'm almost certain they'll end up splitting the health and vitae track again, but perhaps with the vitae track having the equivalent of health levels for Hunger. If they don't, that'll probably be my first house rule.

    Morality
    • The Morality system is a bit poorly worded in places, and is probably more complicated than it needs to be. They also introduce a lot of terms that perhaps add to the complexity (e.g., Beast/Monstrous, Nature/Mortal, "sliding", "re-opening", etc.). Whatever happened to the good old "permanent" dots and "temporary" points/squares?
    • The players accumulated several marks in their Humanity tracker, but I fear they'll rarely trigger frenzy (outbursts seem silly, so we weren't bothered to miss those).
    • The name Mortal Acts seemed misleading to us. It felt like that should describe something positive you do to challenge your Monstrous side, rather than merely describing harm to your Mortal side. "Mortal Sins" would be an obvious alternative.
    • Also, I think there should be some asymmetry to the struggle against the Beast—it should be easy to just let your Nature fade away, while the Beast never sits quietly.
    • You mark damage to your track if you successfully diablerise someone, but not if you merely attempt to diablerise them and fail.
    • The fact you only make one mark for murder seems way too low.
    Disciplines and powers
    • V6 has the same problems as V5 in the Disciplines department (super niche powers that don't really do much on their own, giving the illusion of choice by breaking old powers apart). However, maturing Disciplines are a great idea.
    • We didn't get to use Blood Surge, so we will try that next time.
    • It seemed there were fewer options to just have a Discipline work against mortals, so we'd like some of those to come back in future.
    • Replacing that messy Blood Potency table with a Generation Modifier is a great idea. Just don't call it a Generation Modifier—it can still be called Blood Potency.

    Playtest and pregens
    • The adventure is a bit artificial and bit more heist-like than we'd usually play, but I expect that's to allow you to try out a broad range of systems—including investigation, infiltration, physical combat and social combat.
    • The pregens seem pretty poorly put together. E.g., a character with a specialisation in light weapons only has a heavy weapon. With slightly more optimised characters, it may feel easier.

    All in all, we had fun and I think it's got good bones. It's just the add-ons that need work and refinement. I'm cautiously optimistic about the next draft.

              The TN 8 I expected to work out, Quickening working out was interesting to know. I do feel it kinda weird that you can spend it in the actions of others, but my major complaint is just the name taken from Highlander (and just because it's too copy pastey, Diablerie has always been a pretty Highlander mechanic).

              I think your point on the default Difficulties lands well, I thought the same. Even if mostly it works, always having a penalty feels punitive. It also makes that Attribute at 1 be a sentence of futility.

              Let me do some calc. Your effective range of challenge is 1 to 3 dice. What kind of character should fall in that range for a given task, and who should fall out of it for the same task?

              By the Mediocrity Principle the bulk of our experience is average tasks being performed by average professionals even if it doesn't look like it, so we can assume that most services we see in our daily lives are being performed by people with around 4 dice including tools and environment. Why 4 dice? That's Attribute 2 + Skill 1 + Specialization, which seems a good rule of thumb for a trained but not great worker. Since most services can be expected to be performed fine without issue, having 4+ dice (auto success) after Diff modifier should be ok, -1 shouldn't.

              This is not the easiest roll. There must be a point where a trainee can feel comfortable with tasks that aren't trivial for others. Yes, I think that starting at +1 Diff and progress from there to negatives is spot on if 4 = auto success. Is that the best option?

              We have to tackle this from three perspectives:
              1. Are the odds high enough that rolling is more a chore than worth it?
              2. Is the character's competence high enough that failure becomes a narrative problem?
              3. Is the difference between characters high enough to justify a given task challenging one but not the other?

              1. The chances of success by number of dice on the pool, calculated at AnyDice:
              1 dice 30% worth a try, but unlikely
              2 dice 51% pure luck, unreliable
              3 dice 65,7% likely success, but a 34,3% failure
              4 dice 75,99% only 24,1% failure, roughly 1/4
              5 dice 83,19% only 16,81% failure, roughly 1/6
              6 dice 88,24% only 11,76% failure, less than 1/8

              I can see a case for either 4 or 5 as the point of auto success in this perspective. 24,1% is lower than the minimum chance of success, but is still one failure every 4 rolls. Rolling 5 dice definitely is unnecessary, but 4 I'm not inclined one way or another.

              2 and 3. What having a pool above Difficulty penalty entails?
              -- 4 dice above is having 3 more than the minimum necessary (From an unskilled child to a working professional. From an average trainee to a strong veteran with Att 3 and Skill 3. From this Veteran to a master at Olympic level, just short of peak human performance), 2 more than dumb luck (+1 rank in both Attribute and Skill) and 1 more than a 2/3 chance of success (you had a solid chance, then bought a new rank).
              -- 5 dice above is having 4 more than the minimum necessary (From an unskilled child to a talented professional. From an average trainee to an exceptional veteran with Att least one trait at 4. From strong Veteran to absolute peak human performance), 3 more than dumb luck, 2 more than a 2/3 chance of success (you had a solid chance, then bought two new ranks) and 1 more than a 3/4 chance (already diminished returns to only now buy auto success).

              5 dice seems a bit excessive for me here. Even if you need to be a strong Veteran to have a chance, should you really have to be at a most absolute peak human possibility to be able to auto success it? I think 4 dice is ok and values more the dots you buy. Spending XP to go from 2/3 chance to 3/4 chance isn't really that fun, the auto success gives qualitative difference when numeric would be too little. Both the difference between chars and the numeric return seem to me that auto success at a 4 is a worthy time saver for unopposed tasks and a nice boost to the value of your XP.

              I think the rule is solid. Wouldn't find 5 terrible, but 4 is solid.

              How did the modifiers played? I didn't got it, the four tables, three are for completely different types of roll, so choosing which to use and the Diff within should be up to you and simple, only one is really circumstantial modifiers.

              I just mean by that I'm not picturing how it played to understand what slowed things down. I do think the Diff modifiers could have a better explanation, and the main table have some examples.

              Players having more to track is fine in my book. Less strain on the ST and more participation by the players in building the story. Overall I like it, though I can see it faltering with some types of players that can't or won't make things easy.

              The flat NPCs was a problem I expected. I usually prefer about 6 or 7 stats, 3 baselines for unexpected situations and 3 or 4 actions the NPC is good at. That's my minimum even for random mooks.

              But the Drama points system, I'm really curious about how it played out in practice. How was it to use? What it changed and what went harder or smoother thanks to it? How you'd say it fared over unguided improvisation and/or pre customization of the adventure?

              I personally think the Vitae track will work all right if fluff changes to the body healing minor injuries all the time or a clearer explanation that Vitae is a supernatural animation energy, not literal blood, and how it should look when taking damage. Overall I think the suspension of disbelief/blood shower comedy issue is the easy part to fix.

              How the pressure on a single track felt is more of an experience I'm curious about, and you have good points that "bleeding" a target by the beating is a significant change to how conflicts and predation among vampires play out. Split blood and HP for the living is a no brainer.

              Humanity I expected to play very different, and I don't think the point is to change balance, but first to feel if the proposed thematic change is interesting to begin with.

              It's not about falling to the Beast anymore, but about two conflicting inner sets of instinct and psychological pressure. None should be good or bad, balancing should be the challenge, it is not at all about the alien corruption eating your morality, but you balancing human yearnings and predator instinct, both for good and evil, a Forsaken Harmony type of thing.

              That's my curiosity in this regard. Not if their Scale works for the classic drama, but if the new conflict is an interesting play experience on its own terms.

              As a descriptive element it isn't new. It has been baked into the description of how kindred live in the conflict between human longing and social needs vs the Beast's demands for safety and paranoia, and especially how to roleplay a given Humanity value in the regular track. It's changing which aspect is the core conflict and which takes place as personal stylization.

              So, I'd really appreciate an evaluation of the proposal for a new experience itself as its own thing.

              Generation Modifier feels ok as a name for me. The point was precisely to ditch Blood Potency as something that changes with time within your Generation, going back to Generation being the thing Masquerade is about.

              Observe that when it talks about char gen and Tiers, it does have an observation that a young vampire from a lower Gen will use the Gen as basis for which Tier to use. It's poorly worded, but is a backpedaling on the Blood Potency trait and instead a simpler approach for the old Generation chart.

              I think the pregens are also built kind whatever because focus was to test char gen. I'd like to see some experience with it. Overall that was a good read for practical perspective, thank you.

              Hope you don't mind some questions and requests (that you're obviously not required to fulfill, your own experiments should be your main concern):

              - Choose Your Pain, how it played out? Fun to use? Fast or slow to decide? Are the options all interesting, or something proved evidently best of useless in practice?

              - Devil's Bargain? V5 has a heavy take on Diff partly because Win at a Cost is meant to be a core mechanic there, and I feel it actually work well. How does Devil's Bargain works as basic catch up for the steep penalties?

              - The system is not meant to use Difficulty only as a means to move you in and out of rolling range, but also to give you reason to engage with Side Resources, of which we have five types here. How did they play out as things to try and experience?
              -- Quickening economy,
              -- Assist, Demoralize, Distract and Find Flaw as action options that grant direct bonuses,
              -- Coordinate as a new Action on top of those but with its own bonus economy system,
              -- Modifiers as strategic approach to problems,
              -- Story Assets, the new simplified rule for equipment.

              - Vitae Thresholds are easy to track? Are they interesting in practice? Did the players just avoid going low, or the experience of playing at each range was fun or strategically engaging?

              - Baneful Damage eliminated damage type rollout and can now be downgraded during the night rather than in Daysleep. How it played out in your test? I like it in theory, want to know in practice.

              - Downtime rules are interesting over a more freeform take? Especially their new takes on Resources that interact with it, like spending and recovery, and reallocation, how they're going?

              - V5 had the concept of Aggravated Willpower damage that I didn't tested yet in the game I'm playing. The Playtest ditched it. How does that feel? Would a rule for Baneful Willpower damage improve this aspect of play?

              - Are Called Shots working well? And Social Conflict, is it easy to decide on rolls and dynamic to play? Conditions work well, and is the current list sufficient?

              I do have some ideas of rule changes that I'd like to see. Not just for this playtest but in general. New takes on several traditional approaches in systems.

              For one, I think it way past the time for we to get out of micromanaged seconds long turns. I think even combat turns should cover 1 to 5 minutes and represent an abstraction of what transpired after a "phrase" rather than a frame by frame slice that ends up requiring every second to be relevant.

              (Disclaimer: the link talks about dance, but choreography phrases are also used in fight scenes in movies and theater as the basic unit of meaningful movement)

              Another is that for social contested rolls I really, really like L5R 4th Edition use of Etiquette as the Defense standard. It's interesting because it captures an important aspect of interaction most games miss: standing and social maneuvering.

              Basically L5R avoids having social actions prescribe roleplay. Be it a persuasion, telling a lie or anything of the like, while NPCs can have a Difficulty for a given task to see if you did or not a convincing argument, PCs aren't required to resist such rolls in their decision making and even the ST can forego for NPCs without issue.

              Instead, you roll when you want to publicly act or argue the other's action, not because of how they affect you, but to see how your reaction is seen by the community, especially in a court setting. Can you denounce a lie without loosing face? Deny a request while giving the right impression? Counter an argument without sounding aggressive?

              We're moving past the effect of the social action in the interlocutor and moving into who can sway the social environment in their favor with the exchange, and who'll have to choose between conceding or loosing face. It's fascinating and I felt as the best social conflict system I've played just with this use of skills and test results. A Reputation stat could help in this regard, but isn't required.

                  Personally, I would probably combine Devil's Bargains and Choose Your Pain. It again cuts down on some of the many choices you have.                   It was definitely a lot to manage for a first game, but it would get easier as time goes on.                   This is probably a good thing. Aggravated WP damage was too hard to recover in V5.

                  Called shots seemed pretty straightforward. We did use those quite a bit. Social combat we didn't use. Conditions seemed fine.

                  For one, I think it way past the time for we to get out of micromanaged seconds long turns. I think even combat turns should cover 1 to 5 minutes and represent an abstraction of what transpired after a "phrase" rather than a frame by frame slice that ends up requiring every second to be relevant.

                  (Disclaimer: the link talks about dance, but choreography phrases are also used in fight scenes in movies and theater as the basic unit of meaningful movement)
                  This could work well. How would you do it?

                  Another is that for social contested rolls I really, really like L5R 4th Edition use of Etiquette as the Defense standard. It's interesting because it captures an important aspect of interaction most games miss: standing and social maneuvering.

                  Basically L5R avoids having social actions prescribe roleplay. Be it a persuasion, telling a lie or anything of the like, while NPCs can have a Difficulty for a given task to see if you did or not a convincing argument, PCs aren't required to resist such rolls in their decision making and even the ST can forego for NPCs without issue.

                  Instead, you roll when you want to publicly act or argue the other's action, not because of how they affect you, but to see how your reaction is seen by the community, especially in a court setting. Can you denounce a lie without loosing face? Deny a request while giving the right impression? Counter an argument without sounding aggressive?

                  We're moving past the effect of the social action in the interlocutor and moving into who can sway the social environment in their favor with the exchange, and who'll have to choose between conceding or loosing face. It's fascinating and I felt as the best social conflict system I've played just with this use of skills and test results. A Reputation stat could help in this regard, but isn't required.
