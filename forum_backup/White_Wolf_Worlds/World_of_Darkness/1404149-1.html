                    Originally posted by Ambrosia View Post
                    What problem would the touchstone-like mechanic solve in those gamelines?
                    Vampire: Fixing a lack of narrative structure about how vampires connect with Humanity in order to maintain it as part of being human is social and thus the struggle to maintain Humanity in the face of the Beast needs to drag vampires back to humanity in an interactive fashion. The answer to maintaining high Humanity shouldn't be "go be a hermit," as that removes such efforts from play instead of making different approaches to Humanity having different play experiences.

                    Werewolf: Fixing a lack of incentives for behavior beyond Renown farming. Too many of the downsides of Rage are easily avoided by isolationism (yes, this is going to be a running theme), so the game would be better with a game element that reward players for putting their characters in sub-optimal situations that are genre appropriate.

                    Mage & Changeling: Fixing a lack of structure around the balancing of living a double life. While lots of people enjoy running around the Umbra as mages, or the Dreaming as changelings, and getting to do wild fantasy adventures instead of dealing with the bleak and depressing normal world, the games are at their strongest when the characters can't just run away like that. Things like that Avatar Storm were done to push back against this, but it was all stick and no carrot; there is a need for something to keep these character grounded to the real world in order to properly execute their themes.

                    Wraith (& Orpheus now that it's better integrated in Wraith): As noted, basically has them already.

                    Hunter (Imbued): A lot of the places where Touchstones could address certain problems in the game are places where the game needs a serious overhaul anyway. In general though, similar to Mage and Changeling, the Imbued could use a grounding mechanic. There is no mechanical tool for pushing back against the way the mechanics of the game push the PCs to become more and more divorced from normal human life. The metagame too strongly draws a line between being 6/10 dedicated to the Hunt, and being 7+/10 dedicated to it instead of giving room for the lines to be blurrier.

                    Demon: Probably doesn't need it beyond existing things, though like Hunter the game needs a new edition badly and that may change it one way or the other fairly easily

                    KotE: Largely the same as normal Vampire in this regard. Depending on how any future implementation would go, the temptation to tie them closer to Wraith than Vampire would make this rather natural.

                    Mummy: Another basically already as them; though could use some sprucing up.

                                RPGs promote creativity within limitations (this is an extremely common approach beyond RPGs, as many people find it easier to be creative with limits over absolute freedom). Touchstones (and again, the general concept, not a specific implementation of them) are just another way to provide the limits of frameworks and structures that help most people be creative because such elements narrow things down and make it easier to decide on things to do. That's why a lot of people buy these big books with lots of rules in them.

                                A long standing debate in RPG design are social mechanics (including even having social Attributes/Abilities/Skills/etc.). Some people want to feel free to act out their characters, and others feel that player social aptitude shouldn't override how the character is written; esp. for people that aren't necessarily great at social skills but want to get to play someone that is. I'm very firmly in the second camp. And in general I say have rules and not need them, than need rules and not have them.
