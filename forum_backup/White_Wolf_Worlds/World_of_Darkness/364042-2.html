            I've never liked HtR, the concept just felt off for me and for some of the reasons Possessed has given. They were just another supernatural splat shoehorned into what always felt like a /the Crossover slot between the main lines. Now before I get a reiteration of how supernatural the other Hunter stuff was I;d like to point out that since the very beginning of the WoD (or close enough) we've been told that mortals are Mortals but some of them learn bits of the truth and try to bring down the monsters that ruined their lives, killed their friends or they are ordered to by shadowy organisations (Inquisition) but we've also been told that some mortals a rare few (and typically those that end up interacting with the supernatural) know a few tricks of their own (Numina and a few of the merits that were out there). For the longest time the best weapon against Vampires was faith, as in True Faith, that was the Mortal's go to for being a Vampire Hunter and it meshed perfectly with the setting. It also fitted nicely into Werewolf as well where we also saw a few more Numina amongst the Kinfolk and Mage did have it's Sorcerers. But suffice to say we'd been given plenty of evidence and a few small supplement books that told us what mortals were in the world of darkness and that yes you didn't have to be a supernatural "splat" to be able to tweak a little power to help close the odds by a few inches (and that's all that Numina and Faith gave you, Psychic Void was a little nastier).

            The other thing we'd been told about through Vampire, Werewolf, Mage and Wraith was that being a Hunter required a lot of planning, learning and absolute luck, otherwise you're dead, Playing a Hunter was likely to be a brief brutal experience because even if you had Faith or had learned some of the secret truths of the world to use on your side you were up against things that were vastly more powerful than you, that was the point and odds are you were doing it for some form of personal reason (a la Punisher). So for years this is what we had learned Hunters within the WoD to be. They were mortals who existed very much in the cracks of the WoD.

            HtR which was meant to be the Hunters game didn't mesh with any of this. I'm not referring to what could or couldn't be done with it but the core default of how it's presented and put together. It wasn't about True Faith, Lucky and maybe the ability to heal someone else of 2 levels of lethal damage. It wasn't about having enough WP to hold out on the Delirium well enough to still be able to act. It wasn't about tracking down journals and documents to learn about Vampiric Clan flaws or risking your life to talk with a Ghoul who might let something slip or trying to find just enough silver to bring down that Shapeshifter. It wasn't about stakeouts to work out if so and so really was a creature of the night or just kept an odd routine to their lives. Sure some of the things you could pick up as a mortal could get around fragments of that part of the game but even the Templar faith magic from DaC that let you see a supernatural only worked in a full on conversation with them and then was limited to by type. Nope. HtR was a VERY different game it how it appeared and was presented (even played). Worse still the way it was put together that the Imbued came to be sounded very very similar to an Avatar awakening in many respects which would make them not Imbued but Mages. Note that I know that HtR games could and certainly did involve sometimes some of the above but the sheer life or death, success or failure necessity of it was removed from the feel of the game.

            Remove it from the rest of the WoD and it was fine as a Stand Alone (just like we played the other line anyway all so often) but put into a Crossover perspective (where the Hunter concept often sat alongside it's mortal origins) and it simply didn't sit well. Ironically the biggest complaint I heard in some circles (typically in the online chat games) was how that the Imbued were typically weaker than the other Supernatural splats. It was a game line that had stepped over the old concept of hunters to bring in a new funky concept of hunter that was a supernatural creature imbued with powers to bring down the other supernatural creatures of the WoD for 'reasons' that one on one were often not capable of fulfilling that function. Yes it felt a little like a Buffy type attempt that wasn't as good as the licensed property doing the same thing.

                            To me, it sounds more that Possessed and Moirdryd had a bad play experience with HtR, rather than an issue with how HtR is actually designed to play, or separately what HtR can do. Many of their complaints just don't really jive with the line. Investigation, research, and stakes outs where very much expected from both HH and HtR. HtR being a fully devoted line, didn't make specific rules for it, which maybe they expected, but that doesn't mean it removed that as part of the game either. In fat, most of the fiction, both in the line and in novels, and even in the crossover style books, expressly highlights the fact that HtR cells do a heck of a lot of studying and planning to hunt, and in fact possibly more than HH, for the simple fact that they have more resources (like Hunter.net) to sift through to find the half truths vs the outright BS.

                            Unlike with many Numina/True Faith, Edges are neither understood or reliable. They are abilities that the individual Hunters have assigned to them, not that they themselves develop and learn. There are a lot of suggestions on how these can work, but the ability to just spend something from a pool and use a power on command isn't usually one of them.

                            While they do have some natural protections against the supernatural (as can HH hunters, sorcerers, etc. . .), something that needs to called out is that they are not 100% protection/immunity, not understood by the individual, and something that, having a very limited resource, especially with new Hunters, needs to be activated, and needs to be activated before hand to have any affect whatsoever, which really plays into the fat that Hunters (HtR) need to pick their fights well, and can not just act any time they see something off. The only universal immunity that doesn't require activation or an experimental effort to function is that they are immune to becoming another supernatural creature, and that's one that is more in place so that a major factor of the setting is not instantly invalidated. (An ex-HtR turned vampire gets on Hunter.net and spills the secrets to the other supernaturals). Hunters can still be charmed, or for a limited time mind-controlled, otherwise corrupted, or manipulated/abused by supernaturals.


                            Because no two HtR hunters agree on what's really going on, (who/what gave them their abilities, why, etc. . .), and there is no answer given, and the setting itself mandates a contrast between some supernaturals being able to be "redeemed" while it's up to the players to discover and attempt to find out which can not and need destroyed, there is absolutely no built in story, mechanic, or assumption that HtR are just hitmen/women out there to take down monsters with ease. It isn't there.

                            Unlike most HH hunters, HtR are random people that have been picked out from the masses, for absolutely no known reason, not given any clue about anything other than monsters exist, and an inborn mandate to do something about it. Some defend against it, some want to heal these monsters, so want to take the fight to them, and some want to try to figure the monsters themselves out.

                            So, I don't want to continue the "No, you are wrong" "No you are wrong" sort of deal above, I can see both sides, in my opinion and experience most of the evidence and complaints about HtR above is actually much more true about all of the other HH games, and I'm just not seeing how it was supposed to apply to HtR, but not HH? Kind of seems like a double standard, and I think that's why Possessed is getting so much kickback. Now, that being said, just not digging the game or setting is perfectly fine. And like he or she has said a few times, they are not going to curse WW/OP or you if H20 is a possibility. It sounds more like the argument is focused on how HtR is presented here to others, than actual issues with the game or mechanics, if that makes sense.

                            Speaking of which, being one of the final games to come out, I think HtR did a fantastic job using the ST/"D10" system to allow cool new mechanics and Virtues. I kind if wish some of this could have been ported to the other 20th Anniversary lines.
