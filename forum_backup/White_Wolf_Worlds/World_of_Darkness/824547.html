    -1 difficulty vs +1 dice

    02-05-2016, 04:19 PM
    Under what circumstances is a bonus better represented by adding a die to a pool, vs reducing the difficulty of an action?

    Conversely, is a penalty to a dice pool, injuries aside, ever more appropriate than an increase in difficulty?

    Prompted by noticing that in the DA basic rules, specialties add a die, rather than reduce difficulty or allow 10s to re-roll.

    I prefer the ability to adjust difficulties and dice pools over having a flat difficulty solely accompanied by modifiers to the dice pool (OWoD vs CoD) but I had not thought before about when each adjustment is most appropriate.

    Smaller dice pools lead to faster gameplay, but, unless you allow 10s to re-roll, an increase in dice pool is the only way to garner an increased number of successes in a non-extended roll.

      Generally speaking, difficulty modifiers are more influential than dice modifiers in the WoD system. 6 dice at difficulty 6 averages 2.4 successes. +1 dice only nets you +0.4 average successes, while -1 difficulty nets you +0.6 average successes. Not a huge change, but it's good enough to illustrate the point.

      The only times you're going to want to add dice over reduce difficulty, is if you've either already dropped the difficulty to 2 with other methods, or if you need more successes than your current dice-pool can provide (though you're pretty screwed anyway odds-wise).

      In terms of "appropriateness," the books are extremely inconsistent on this. The best way to keep this consistent (though it will not be since the books aren't) is to make difficulty modifications generally external issues that make the task easier/harder such as poor lighting penalizing a Perception check, or a killer being sloppy about evidence giving a bonus to an Investigation roll. Then make dice modifications a matter of changes in personal capacity. Wound penalties are because your body is damaged and less capable, while shapeshifting into a more muscular form gives you more Strength (and dice in Strength checks) because you're capable of exerting more power physically.

      A few points to note:

      - In DA Revised Basic, Attribute specialties still provide the bonus to 10s, while Ability specialties are bonus dice. This is actually a nice thing because it means they can stack effectively.

      - The 20th books have moved from 10s re-roll to 10s count as two successes. This is statistically more potent, but depending on which reroll rules you were using (exploding or not) might not be theoretically capable of exceeding certain thresholds.

              One way to look at it is that altering difficulty modifies the chance of the character doing her best still inside her usual ability, while altering dice-pools modifies what is her ability for the task. With less difficulty the character can achieve the same with more ease, with more dice she can achieve more.

              But otherwise, Heavy Arms' points are an excellent explanation. I would only argue that +.4 and +.6 average successes is a huge difference. And with 50% more weight, reducing difficulties is hugely more beneficial in the average chance. It isn't always the same distinction, though. The numbers are for 6 dice at difficulty 6, but they change as you change difficulties and dice-pools.

              As a rule of thumb, you can assume that at low difficulties you have more benefit with more dice, and with more dice you have more benefit with lower difficulty.

                  If you're interested M.Muir here's the system we use at our table to keep large dicepools from getting out of hand: Players may subtract a die from their pool to lower the difficulty by 1. The difficulty can never drop below 4 for any contested rolls (meaning, any rolls where someone/-thing is actively trying to resist. So, every type of combat), and there must be a minumum of 1 die left in the pool to roll. As an example, a player with a dicepool of 6 may subtract 2 dice to lower the standard difficulty to 4, and a player rolling at a difficulty of 8 with a dicepool of 5 may subtract 2 dice to lower the difficulty to a 6 (or even remove 4 dice to lower the difficulty to 4). For uncontested rolls, we use the suggestion printed in one of the Revised books, that if the Attribute+Ability score is higher than the difficulty would be, then it's automatically successful. Now, I'm sure someone will inform me how this system is wrong, but neither I nor my players are statisticians, and it seems to keep everyone happy without being too obviously broken.

                      I'm not going to say wrong. It has a goal (keeping dice pools lower without radically altering things) and it certainly does that (which is the only way you can really say a rule is "right"). As I noted above, -1 diff is worth more than +1 dice, so the system incentivizes spending your dice to lower difficulty as much as possible while saving enough dice to score the number of successes you'll need. You'll generally roll less dice, and since everyone should be doing it, the odds don't change too drastically.

                      It has some potential issues, but that depends highly on the game-line(s) in use, and access to various traits. VtM has very few difficulty reduction powers, so they'll get a lot of use at of this, WtA is mixed which is probably the worse place to be because some builds will get a slight boost over others with this, while MtA does way more difficulty reduction than dice-adders making this an OK but often minor consideration.

                      The biggest problem to M.Muir post though, is that it blurs the line heavily on trying to figure out what difficulty bonuses, and dice bonuses, actually "mean" in-game. If you can swap dice for difficulty, it's that much more unclear what situations should generate dice modifiers, and what situations should generate difficulty modifiers.

                              Ultimately, there's no "right" math on that.

                              The problem with it in terms of the statistics is that the impact doesn't scale proportionally. Trying to find a right ratio without it being math-at-the-table (as in the ratio would have to change with the size of the dice pool), simply doesn't work.

                              To be a bit more detail, at a base difficulty of 6, +1 dice increases your average successes by 0.4 (not counting specialties). It doesn't matter if your base dice pool is 2, or 12, the impact is the same. Though it is worth noting that the significance of that impact does change, after all going from 2 dice to 3 dice is jumping the average from below 1 successes to above 1 success, which means it's a big jump in just flat out how often you'll succeed at all. Going from 12 dice to 13 dice, means you'll rolling 5 successes a bit more than 4 successes.

                              Meanwhile, the impact of -1 difficulty changes radically with dice pool size. 2 dice at diff 6 vs. 2 dice at diff 5 is a difference of 0.2 average successes. At 12 dice, the difference is 1.2 average successes.

                              So, changing the ration from 1:1 to 2:3 isn't really better or worse in a vacuum. It just shifts the range of dice pools where that trade is roughly "fair" which changes when it's worth sacrificing dice and when it isn't. The only way to settle on a specific ratio as the most optimal statistically would be to orient it around a specific dice-pool size.

                                      I want to add two points.

                                      First, a suggestion, or better, a personal consideration to nothing: as a rule of thumb, it is a good idea to make options a little costly, applying the "Casino Rule" where the House keeps the advantage. If the players have the option to turn dice into less dif, you could make it -3 dice for -2 dif, lets say. That's because when you give the players a tactical option, the option itself is one more advantage for them in combat, since they can better adjust strategy. But that's not a hard rule, and if your way already works with your troupe, no reason to change if you don't see it.

                                      Second, at the same time, the method used by nothing can actually solve the problem for the OP if it works for the other troupe. Once you let the players change between dif and dice, it becomes essentially moot the ST first decision. You can make everything into dice modifiers only, starting at the same dif always, and just let the players decide how they split their dice into dif reductions and actual pools. A good Rule of Thumb if you make the exchange rate 1:1 is to make all rolls dif 9, and the standard dif becomes +3 dice (enough to play your original pool at dif 6).

                                      Those considerations are taken with only a vague grasp of the math, as much as I can muster late at night before I go to bed.

                                          monteparnas There's not a lot of combat in our game right now, but we've rewritten the entire way combat works in our games anyhow - in one specific instance, we've eliminated the addition of bonus successes adding to firearms damage. Since guns are "the Great Equalizer," we've reflected that damage-wise and instead allowed players with higher ratings to lower difficulties to use them (so a shotgun, with regular ammunition, causes 8 damage always, but a character with no rating in Firearms is rolling just Dexterity at difficulty 7 - standard 6+1 for lacking a Skill. This is before any modifiers from cover, etc. A character with Dexterity 3 and Firearms 3 could subtract 2 dice to roll at a difficulty of 4, and if firing at someone behind cover, or aiming, they would bump their difficulty back up to ~7 or 8).
