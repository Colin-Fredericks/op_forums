      There are many, many ways of forming alliances between splats, and they do happen with greater regularity than their societies recognize.

      It's just that no particular method is surefire and most of the time it won't happen.

      Why does a Garou Pack has to work with whomever? For most packs the answer is they don't, and no argument will dissuade them. Much less convince them of working with vampires.

      Sure there are exceptions, but the people involved matters a lot, and the context has to be detailed.

      Every splat is extremely resourceful in ways the others can't even comprehend, and usually the answer to any trouble is within their group, mingling with others brings more risks than it's usually worth, and frequently their long term goals aren't just incompatible, but completely alien to the others. Bridging this gap takes more than just throwing any group at a situation where working together would help.

      An exception does exist, though. Changelings will basically interact with anything and go along just for the adventure.

      On your particular examples: the average Garou Pack will just "brute force" their way out (both actual strength and their many Gifts and Fetishes) and wipe the vampires on the way out. If this isn't possible, they'll wipe the vampires and bravely accept their fate afterwards. Not compromising is more important than living. Imbued usually have a very similar approach to working with monsters and Garou are proud, so in the second scenario your average Pack won't ask for help and your average Hunters will just add the Garou to their to-do list.

      Most of the time everyone else is at least a little more willing to compromise, give or take, unless it is with a Demon, only a Changeling will be buddies with a Fallen outside extremely uncommon circumstances and extremely open-minded people.

                          Even if these Garou are trully good people that don't want to kill humans just for having gotten themselves involved with Garou stuff, I don't see "rescuing noosy humans" as the basis for an alliance.

                          If they got a First Team after them, that means they're pooking arround things they don't (or shouldn't) understand. And they're not even cappable of getting away with it by themselves.

                          They're creating trouble for Garou that have to rescue them. Trouble for anyone involved. The least they can do it's prentend they forgot everything about it and not cause more trouble :P

                          While alliances are possible, and some of the books suggest to go for it, I think it's better to not underestimate the amount of Lore that goes against the idea, and put the effort to try to conciliate the problems involved.

                          I think maybe the Traditions could give us a reasonably good example: While they're all mages, the Traditions are still a mess of different factions with different ideas about how things should work, that barely understood one another before the unification, and many were trying to kill each other. It's trully a thing of wonder that the thing marched on. Yet it's important and big enough that the books actually went into it, instead of glossing over the whole thing like they often do

                          To keep it short:

                          *The Traditions unified against a massive treath they were able to predict, but unable to stop. The rise of Order of Reason, and the predictions pointing towards the constitution of the Technocracy in the future.

                          *And even then only trough the effort and sacrifice of important and powerful members of the factions involved, that had the clouth to be listened, were willing to put their differences aside, and were willing to go way out of their way and do titanic efforts to convince the other mages to meet and talk (one of them even died figthing his own faction just to convince them to sit at that table).

                          *And even then, Mages would have left w/o an alliance if it wasn't because of serendipity: The Order of Reason attacked the gathering, cementing the idea of a serious existential treath beyond any doubt (and allowing the Order of Hermes to demonstrate power, showing they had more to put into the table than being insufferable pricks)

                          I think that, by putting a similar effort, one could have compelling, or at least passable, ideas for an alliance between supernatural factions.

                          *A common goal none can achieve by themselves. It needs to be a situation that can't be solved simply by looking inward to their own faction.
                          *Special people willing to shake a hand with the other part to achieve it, and willing to face problems within their own sects for that.
                          *A concrete situation that makes the whole argument for an alliance sink in, something to beat prejudice at least for the moment.

                          Then it would be a matter of making contacts, places of reunion, oaths or compromises, etc ... institutions that cement the alliance as more than just a passing instance of "let's kill that other guy first".

                                          Originally posted by Samuelalpha86 View Post
                                          Well, I was thinking that maybe a particularly open-minded member of the Garou pack has the plan to teach the Imbued about Garou so they'll be less "shoot first ask questions later" about werewolves and know when to act.
                                          The Garou don't know what the Imbued are. Not even the Imbued know what they are. And teaching a hunter with a "shoot first" attitude about your society will just make them a better hunter, since they're already a "shoot first" kind of hunter. Why would they listen to the "sugar-coated" parts? You're a monster, you lie!

                                          Even the hunter that doesn't shoot the Garou won't change anything, because the standard Garou response will still be to just get those guys out of the way instead of counting on their help, even in the best cases. The hunters have to first prove that they can help in any meaningful way before even the most kind Garou will treat them as anything more than helpless, ignorant and annoying children.

                                          Let's be clear on something here:

                                          As I said, alliances can and do happen, regularly.

                                          It's just that you need a specific situation to have that happen. Throwing generic scenarios at random (even if open-minded) characters won't make them work together. Specific scenarios with specific individuals can work, sometimes with even less effort than we're implying, at least for minor alliances, like two specific half-dozen-max groups.

                                          Keeping secrets is the name of the game. In the WoD most splats have a lot to hide from each other and a lot to lose if their enemies find their secrets. They also have societies that strictly impose those secrets, so any spill is also a sword over your head. The whole setting is built on paranoia, on top of all the actually valid reasons why they may cause problems to each other. Their history of problems and conflicts with each other only cements this further.

                                          Does that means making alliances is impossible? Not. But it does means "stranger danger" until further notice, every person you don't know is more likely to be a problem than help.

                                          Which brings us to the best way to build a scenario where people ally: have them interact several times with low stakes and no conflict. It is still hard, but a far lower bar to simply have them get superficially acquainted without them killing each other. Let them build familiarity slowly. Then start to build trust with incidents as minor as possible. They'll be more willing to form alliances when they have enough experience about each other to make an informed decision than when they're simply thrown in a life-or-death situation or something.

                                          For example, have the Garou watch from afar this group of hunters dealing with a Wyrm-infested corporation they can't deal with now for whatever reasons, maybe being occupied with something else. The Garou notice the hunters do an actually decent job, if not at tearing everything apart, at something like gathering information or sabotage. They may also during the hunters' mission drop their own kind of info from time to time, being as helpful as they can while they can't go in themselves. At the time the hunters know they're getting help from Werewolves, they also know what kind of fucked-up thing those Werewolves are dealing with. Now they have enough of a rapport with each other to work a little more together in future endeavors, the Garou know those hunters can help and the hunters know those werewolves fight for a good cause.

                                              I'd say it is COMPLETELY dependent on who is involved and why. No splat is ever going to ally with another splat outright.

                                              Individual members, however? More likely than you think! There is Dark Alliance: Vancouver that explores this in better ways than I can explain it.

                                              My personal advise is not to build the alliance itself. Instead, here's other things to consider:

                                              1 - Who are the two groups involved?
                                              (Keep this small scale: One Garou pack that's part of a Sept; one hunter cell made of 3 people; a couple Orphan Mages, for example)

                                              2 - Who is the mutual enemy that facilitates the need for this alliance?
                                              Just pick a couple Pentex subsidiaries and save yourself the headache. Pentex and Fomori just make good bad guys on a general level.

                                              3 - Build the rapport between the individuals who make up the alliance and why.
                                              Were they friends before this point? Were they frenemies?
                                              Is the Shadowlord Sept leader married to an Arcanum Elder Brother? (Also, Page 61 Halls of the Arcanum: Supernatural Companion 1-5 merit; Page 111 - Spoilers)

                                              Any little detail you throw in "for the funnies" can easily snowball into a bigger point of relevance and get put in the spotlight.

                                              Other people here on this thread have offered better advice than I, but I figured to not drown you in technicalities, and instead consider an angle where other people could have a similar question for their own stories for chronicles as well as Characters and character ideas.
