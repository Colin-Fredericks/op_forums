    Laser gun: 8 (3 successes) Aggravated damage, 4 successes to hit.
    Vampire: 3 successes to dodge.
    Results: Vampire takes the full damage.

    Fireball: 8 (3 successes) Aggravated damage, no roll to hit.
    Vampire: 3 successes to dodge.
    Results: You subtract the dodge successes from the fireball, potentially making it do no damage or very little.

            Assuming it's a) hostile, b) knows you know it is a vampire, c) not super powerful and d) this is a short term conflict where you are at the same place as the vampire...

            If I was a new character and not specifically made to handle a vampire I'd try to get away from it. Failing that I'd try to calm it down. Even if you're not good at social interaction, you can always beg.
            I'd also try to get a hold of the rest of my party as soon as possible.

            General advice:
            Always think about what information you need at the moment. Why is the vampire hostile? Is there ways to exit the room? How powerful do the vampire appear to be? Where is your party? If you do manage to exit the room, what ways do you have to leave quickly? Can you steal a car, or get to your own car? On a meta level, how do your gm run kindred? Information defines and provides you options
            Remember you are a part of a group. Especially when handling a vampire, depend on other pcs. If they aren't dependable, you have a problem for any dangerous situation you are in.

            Other things that can help:
            Be careful with mind whammies from the vampire. If you don't have mindwards or do not have strong willpower, at least avoid looking directly at them and listening to them
            Forces are decent against kindred. I do not remember what level of forces you need to turn invisible but I think it was 4. Even then though, if you want to get away you could use forces to mute and remove any sounds you make, which might help with stealth roles. Also if you do engage in combat, forces can be used to make some decent invisible armor around yourself (a forcefield), though you might need life 1 to 'lock it' around your pattern since you want it to move with you. Also you might want to do it as a ritual so you get more successes.

            Note also you can just get normal body armor and wear that. This is usually a good idea if you want to engage in violence anyway. The force shield have the advantage of not necessarily being visible though.

            If you want to just snap your finger and set a kindred on fire, you will need not just a paradigm that lets you do that, but also prime 2 in addition to forces, to conjure fire from nothing. Some sts might also decide that you need matter 1 but I don't think it should really be needed. Also it's vulgar so be careful with paradox. If you only have forces, try to carry a lighter around so you can just set things on fire. Also in either case think about the room you are in, and try to not burn down an appartment complex and get a bunch of sleepers killed.

            Other than that you just have your normal selection of combat and escape rotes. Prime 2 lets your attacks deal aggravated damage against supernatural, life keeps you alive, correspondence is great for fleeing but can also make aiming easier, getting into the penumbra with spirits can be useful as well, general physical fitness is good for both dodging and running, matter can quickly help you get trough doors or strengthen doors against people hunting you.

            Lastly, if the kindred appears to be powerful, or if it is not engaging you directly, but trough intermediaries, check if you have a mentor that you can ask to help you. Even if your pc do not have any, check if anyone else in the group has one, or if noone has any, check if you have in game know any npcs in the traditions you can ask for help or advice, or any pull in the traditions and can get them to help you.

            -

            As a side note I honestly do not understand why it'd be any more difficult to jump out of the way of vertical lightning than horizontal lightning.

            "Yeah, the weird freak started to chant a bunch of ooga booga, raise his to the sky, then his chant reached a crescendo and he started to clap his hand together, so I jumped out of the way and then I shot him. He had some forcefields up so he took less damage, but it still looked like it hurt. Serves him right, the damn nerd"

            Though there's a couple of things here that makes the whole thing feel skeevy to me.

            Summoning a lightning bold from the sky seems extremely different from me than most standard forces attacks.

            1. I'd count the distance of the spell as the highest between either its point of origin and the target, or the mage and the target. So probably you want correspondence two to shift the effect that far up into the sky.

            2. Generating an actual kilometers long lightning bolt is pretty huge. If a mage wanted to generate a several kilometers long pillar of fire, or a several kilometers long spear out of matter even temporarily, I'd require a large amount of successes. The only difference with lightning in that context is lightning might be coincidental. I'd still require a large amount of successes. I think it'd be easier if they just made sure to check the weather report and go out in the middle of a storm. At that point using forces or entropy to make the poor sod more likely to get hit by lightning seems a lot easier.

            Possible if you had forces 5 and a thematic as a weather wizard I'd wave most of the hoops you need to jump trough and let you automatically call down lightning on people and might even wave their ability to dodge unless they are not mooks. Otherwise you might want to do that specific effect as a ritual.

                                        Originally posted by Vandarl View Post
                                        OK, I am sure it has been asked before, but my search-fu has failed me. Other than not be in the same city with them, what is the best way for a fresh out of the box Mage to deal with a hostile vampire? Straight chargen, no additional XP. How would you do it?
                                        Forces 3, Prime 2, Mind 1. Forces 3 provides the basis to perform a number of very useful things. First, make yourself invisible to the visible light spectrum (can't hit what you can't see); make yourself levitate over the battlefield (can't hit what you can't reach, even flailing around wildly); reduce gravity in the area and/or levitate target (Celerity and dodging don't mean much if all the target can do is writhe and flail around in one spot.) Caution: direct levitation might be resisted, but it more easily conceptualized, whereas reducing gravity in a small area moves the effect into the category of 'irresistible indirect effects', but may be resisted by some ST's as too amorphous despite HDYDT explicitly stating that this sort of thing is doable with as little as Forces 2. Mind 1 provides the ability to erect a Mind shield and potentially resist or avoid getting Dominated or Presence'd into submission by fledgling with Dominate 1 who has figured out that "Surrender!" is a useful one-word sentence.

                                        So those cover your defense, as well as neutralizing the target's potential defenses. Offense using Forces 3, Prime 2 has been tread so thoroughly above that I won't get into it. Needless to say, if you can sit in your invisible sky bubble to cast for a couple of turns and spend a Willpower, odds are good the vampire's not going to have much for that. Another interesting tactic would be to imbue a firearm with aggravated damage via Prime 2, then put the firearm in an invisible and sound-dampening bubble, then levitate it so that it it's right right behind your target's head, and use telekinesis to pull the trigger. +1000 points for Gryffindor if you use a sawed-off 12-gauge shotgun loaded with buckshot, and aim for the vampire's neck. You're targeting the neck, yes, but you're also firing from pointblank range with a scatter gun. You just need three damage past the soak to decap.

                                        I agree that this is nonsense. The ease with which one may Dodge (which uses Athletics or Acrobatics) an attack, per the book, depends on factors like how far away the thing being dodged is from the dodger, and how much room the dodger has to move around in space. So without a doubt what is occurring here is that the person is changing their position and location in three-dimensional space to avoid being contacted by a thing which is traveling towards them. It's a priori that being able to perceive a thing is a necessary precondition to reacting purposefully to the thing. While there are plenty of magic attacks that operate in a manner that lend themselves to being perceived and reacted to by most people (and we've named a few), there are plenty of others that absolutely do not work that way.

                                        If my target and I are facing off, and I use Forces 3 Prime 2 to conjure an invisible laser that originates at an elevated position behind above my target, and approaches my target at a downward 12° angle--I don't know how (absent their having magical senses and the like) they're going to know to avoid that. Even if they know I can cast magic, I'm gesturing wildly with one hand and waving a staff with the other, and speaking in tongues... how are they supposed to know where an invisible, inaudible attack is coming from? Well, the game basically provides the equivalent of an Auspex rating because they can roll Perception + Awareness to perceive the attack somehow--and since Awareness is a Talent, at a minimum absolutely everyone is rolling Perception. However, even if I think this is lazy, it's still a game about wizards and vampires, so the fact that everyone on the planet now has the shining for the purposes of dodging true magick (and seemingly only true magick), is as valid as the existence of were hyenas. What I find incongruous is another portion of the very paragraph that describes the Per+Aware rule on pg. 544 of M20:

                                        Really obvious attacks – lightning bolts, clouds of deadly gas, and so forth – are easy to see coming. Invisible ones – flesh-eating spirits, silent curses, Entropic ripples that collapse a bridge, that sort of thing – may be detected with a successful Perception + Awareness roll, difficulty 8.
                                        I will readily accept that people will have their sixth sense triggered by having a curse levied against them... but the purpose of this paragraph is to discuss dodging magical effects... so... how do you physically dodge a curse? Are you telling me that if someone is going to curse me with Entropy or Life (i.e., Blight of Aging, or Rip the Man Body, or both), the curse is being literally flung at me, such that if I jump out of the way, someone behind me might get the curse instead? Really? Then why even make a distinction between physical and non-physical in the first place?

                                        I think one of the big issues with M20 is that they didn't want to futz with the underlying metaphysical mechanics of magic, but they also didn't want mages to be nearly as powerful as they were in previous editions simply by dint of sphere magick giving mages capabilities incomparable to whatever means non-mages have of defending against attacks. So they basically put in rules that dispense with even attempting to make some kind of sense within the metaphysics of the game--in other words, they created escape hatches for non-mages that you have to just accept and not try to make sense of; they're not internally consistent with the rest of the rest of the rules.
