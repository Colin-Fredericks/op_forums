        The ST system is not meant to be a physics engine. Attempting to make it into one gave us Exalted 2nd edition, i.e. a buggy mistake that falls apart whenever pressure falls on it. Particularly with the feats of strength, it's generally better to go by a textual description than a listen maximum weight, because the core of the system is "roll if something is uncertain, using an Attribute + Ability", not "you can lift up this exact quantity of coinage", the latter of which is only really important if you're looting a place and gaining xp for each gold piece you can get back to town. If you aren't playing a character advancement game that revolves around treasure, exact weights are not particularly important to the game's typical operations, and you can eyeball it.

        Auxiliary Note #1: W20 explicitly allows for a variance of Willpower rolls to lift more weight, as well as to potentially carry it for as long as a given feat takes to achieve.

        Auxiliary Note #2: The lifting table seems to assume a military press, since it allows you to throw something, and it's generally much more difficult to do this than to, say, carry around a load of weight on a frame specifically designed to carry gear, like a rugged backpack with a solid framework, or, presumably, most tactical apparel. Also, different muscle groups are involved in these tasks, so a character might get away with a lower Attribute rating than they would need if they were lifting it over their head.

                                                    You need to prepare having in mind what the characters can and can't do. Doing so otherwise leads to the "evil mages turned my Elder into a chair" type of problems (and it's many variations, for instance, "A werewolf ripped X appart with total ease") without anyone being a powergamer...perhaps those players merely expected their powers to work as intended. A good resource it's to combine antagonists from all games, I.E: the Correspondence scrying that would've worked so well against the BSD may be countered by a Nephandi.

                                                    To be prepared for this kind of eventuality, you need to be versant on the rules of all games (and supplements) implied.
