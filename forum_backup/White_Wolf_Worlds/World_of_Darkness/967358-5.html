    Hardly. Experimental evidence, verified by thousands of experiments that were desperately trying to find an error in the foundational theories, supports the majority of the positions. What evidence do you have that the belief of human beings have changed the physics of the world or really anything? The majority of humans believe that in a benevolent diety, but there is precious little evidence of that belief being worth anything throughout history.

    As for the Native American issue, it is the weight of numbers. The best estimates have the population of the New World in the 1400s being 25% of that of the population of the Old World. Unless the paradoxical position is taken that the number of people who believe in something has no impact on how the Consensus shapes reality, the fact that 80% of the people believed that there was open water between England and Japan meant that the Consensus (if it shapes reality) should have long ago destroyed the Americas. It logically follows then that either a) the Concensus shapes reality and America was created by the European Consensus to serve European needs or b) the Consensus does not shape reality and the Americas have always been there. Since there has never been experimental evidence of belief shaping reality, b) is presumed to be correct.

    On another note, while it is a common fallacy that European people thought that the Earth was flat until Columbus, that is not true. It was well known fact that Europeans had known that the Earth was a sphere since the time of Pythagoros, 6th century BC, and that only lunatics believed in a Flat Earth by the 8th century AD. While the idea had spread throught the Old World through the trade networks that connected to Europe by the 2nd century AD, there were some minor religious communities that believed in a Flat Earth took a while to be convinced. In fact, the support for the hypothesis of a spherical Earth was one of the first recorded scientific experiments in human history.
