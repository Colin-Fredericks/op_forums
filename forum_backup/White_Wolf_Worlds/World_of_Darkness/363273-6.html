        For Vampire: The Masquerade

        Clanlessbook: Caitiff - I want to write this book myself. Examining unlife as the bottom feeders and dumpster-sleepers, harried and hounded by the scourge, the second-class citizens of the Camarilla, the proud stooges of the Sabbat, the first casualties of the Jyhad, and how they survive night to night. All told in far more detail than Outcasts or Time of Thin Blood, and detailing the Caitiff's responses to the persecution of the Final Nights.

        Guide to the Inconnu - In a similar vein to VII for Vampire: The Requiem, a book presenting three or so different versions of what the Inconnu might be in the World of Darkness, as well as guidelines for creating your own spin on the mysterious sect. Would include the known history of the Inconnu during the nights of Ancient Rome.

        Jus Sanguinis: A Guide to the Bloodlines - A book that gives each of the minor bloodlines a bit more depth and history.

        Wages of Sin - A book focusing on Necromancy, to help bring it up to the level of Thaumaturgy in terms of Paths and Rituals. More information on the Underworld as presented in Vampire (with obvious shout-outs to Wraith).

        V20 Storyteller's Compendium - Something along the lines of the 1st Edition Storyteller's Handbook - with advice on storytelling, crafting chronicles and the like of course, but focusing on painting outside the lines presented in the existing rulebooks. System permutations and alternatives, ways to incorporate elements of other WoD games, ways to alter the setting (whether in geography or chronology), and other fun stuff to get people brainstorming on how to make the V:tM setting their own.

        For Demon: The Fallen & Wraith the Oblivion

        The Harrowing: A re-imagining of Hell in the World of Darkness so that it's not so... well... boring. Instead of having demons more or less all standing around in an empty room being bitchy to each other, give us Hell as a setting. It doesn't have to be all Judeo-Christian, but we need something more than "God stuffs you in a bag of holding." Perhaps Hell was originally a void... but as the eons passed and the great Wheel turned, cracks began to form at the periphery, allowing the weakest of demons to touch the darkest recesses of the Shadowlands and draw in plasm for use in constructing a new world, a perverted mirror of the Heaven that birthed the Demons.

        I have images of Spectres and Wraiths who fall too deep into a Harrowing brushing up against the fracturing wall of Hell, where monstrous, impish clawed arms flail and grasp at them - possibly pulling them through the barrier and into the hands of Demons who have been waiting ages to have something to mold and shape... where the last thing the Wraith sees are the spires of cities built upon the plasm of the darkest of human souls. Or perhaps the Damned Soul is dragged into a coffer where his soul and the souls of other Damned are bartered as currency - whether as raw materials, slave labor or perhaps entertainment for a noble of the Demon courts.

        Perhaps this version of Hell is only a pocket in the deepest, darkest pits of the Umbra, where a small number of demons have managed to slip through and forge an overlapping plane between Hell and the Shadow - a sort of grand trick to grant them some illusory semblance of freedom, where the "gravity" of hell doesn't pull them, but where they can once again create.

                                            I'd like a Kindred of the East: the Great Leap Outward sourcebook. The Great Leap Outward - a.k.a the kuei-jin invasion of California - was the defining event for an entire generation of kuei-jin. It brings them (in force) to the West for the first time, putting them in direct contact/conflict with Western supernaturals. It allowed the young ones to escape the reach of their elders, gives the Bamboo Princes room to maneuver . . .and its the first thing the Quincunx was able to agree on in centuries.

                                            And it was an incredibly dynamic time for . . .well, everyone. Borders were changing nightly, San Diego returns to the Camarilla, and one ticked-off elder (or mandarin) could plunge an entire city back into war.

                                            A major crossover event, lots of potential for adventure and advancement . . .and yet White Wolf broke up the events among something like a dozen city and splatbooks. You'd get a line in one book about how the kuei-jin were on the verge of attacking, and then, in the next, they'd be talking about how Monkey Trip Wu was planning on naming the seized territory the "Quicksilver Court". Then, in a third, you'd get a brief mention about how he'd botched the ancestor rite . . .

                                            Such a seminal event deserves its own sourcebook. It wouldn't even have to be updated for 20th anniversary - it'd be great to be able to play a wu in the first wave - or during the heady days of their early victories. Things are in such flux, it'd give Storytellers a lot of freedom. Just pick (or create) a mandarin to lead the assault (on a particular city), and start the campaign with the first moments of the assault, as the kuei-jin erupt from the ship's hold. . .

                                            And you could crossover with so many other settings - unlike Cainites, kuei-jin know exactly what to do with caerns/nodes/haunts/freeholds . . . And so many of the resident supernaturals are just so tasty . . .

                                            Plus, if Onyx Path does want to add in 20th anniversary material, there's plenty of opportunity there, too. We never did get much on the "New Promise Mandarinate" (kuei-jin California) under Han Hui, for example
