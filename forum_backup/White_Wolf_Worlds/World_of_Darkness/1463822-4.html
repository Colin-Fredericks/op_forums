    Honestly, who or what gave the imbued powers was irrelevant for the game itself. The biggest "revelations" came from short references within other games drunk on their self importance or the pretty crappy Time of Judgement book that tried to do everything at once. It was something to be built upon and explored within the needs of the chronicle. And just like all the other games had different takes on what was going on in them compared the others, Hunter did a lot of its own. That was its charm and that allowed you take what you wanted and what you needed. So saying that their "mysteries" are stuck in lost games making them irrelevant is a bit silly, as that's the least of their problems (and one exacerbated by fans with extensive libraries and desires to make things click together).

    As for what the new Paradox White Wolf crew is trying to do. Sure they started as ascended fans and hired a number of people with sterling RPG credentials, but they had a very clear vision of what this new WoD would be - this would be a WoD they felt was "correct" and damned be anyone who thinks it should be something else. And while vision and drive are great if you need to build something up and get new people onboard, their vision pretty much fizzed out with Vampire 1st edition as the be-and-all of the WoD. They saw it as the pinnacle and perfection without caring much where things evolved along the way. This was reiterated with their commentary about other lines where they literally went by memes and outrage interpretations of the cringiest material White Wolf had made and actually tried to fix through the ages. Watching their approach is not encouraging in the slightest as it's a way that says that there is only one way to play these games - theirs.

    Justin Achilli is someone I trust on topics Vampire. On anything not Vampire or anything crossing over with Vampire I don't. In general It's a flawed perception to believe that people writing these games have extensive knowledge of other game lines or even necessarily care about things beyond their contribution. Look at Phil Brucatto, his mage was very much a statement to his own views and what he feels the game should be. It's sure as hell a step away from what Bridges had as his own vision. A lot of the people writing this stuff were doing it for a paycheck and given guidelines whats expected, they did that and were done. Occasionally they introduced clever bylines and references and stuff that made them happy, but the majority of the work was informed by the people that set their goals and the need to finish a product. This is the same with the New White Wolf crew.

    Edit: Please note that this is not a diss on the people writing for these games, they are usually passionate and have their own ideas and visions on how they want to make the games work. Their contributions are what made these games better and more interesting and I appreciate their work, even if I had ongoing holy wars when I was younger and dumber with some of them. I'm just slightly cynical about how the sausage is made, how much time people have, how much money they get and who makes these final decisions on what a final product has to look like.

    The funny bit here is that each of these games evolved quite a bit along the way. They evolved within the halls of White Wolf and later Onyx Path, but they evolved at every table and every part of the world where people convened and played these games to give them more life and corrected course towards what they needed. This is pretty much why people care so much about the WoD - its something they built up through the ages along with the material that evolved with them. Again, this is something I feel Wizards understood when they made 5E D&D as they did. And this is something Paradox doesn't seem to get so far.
