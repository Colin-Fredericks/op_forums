    Aleph Older editions rules are as confusing as M20, if not more. I believe the p.20 entry means the sum of all effects? So a wonder with Prime 5 effect and a Forces3/Prime2 effect would cost 8?

    Anyway, after reading and thinking a lot and talking to people I believe I managed to understand how the system works without ignoring neither the table nor the explanation. And your insight about the difference between dots and points helped a lot.

    Ok, first, there are 3 different counting systems. Let's call them, for clarification:

    - background points: the pool of points you get to spend in wonder cost. Each freebie/background you spend in the background Wonder, gives you about 1-3 background points as per the table.

    - Wonder rating: How many dots a wonder has. Regularly it equals wonder arete (arete can be increased) and highest sphere lvl effect. It gives a notion on how powerful a wonder is.

    - wonder cost: the cost of each wonder with background points from the pool. The cost follows the description I posted in OP. So, wonder cost= highest sphere lvl x2 + any increase in Arete.

    Now, you spend your background/freebies points to get dots in the wonder background. According to the table, you get about 1-3 background points per dot. You spend that pool of background points buying wonders, that costs their wonder cost.

    It's actually simple after you get the concepts. So, in my OP example, the wonder rating 5 have 10 wonder cost and needs 10 background points, which you get by buying 4 dots in the background. So option B was right.

    Note that wonder has no $ and is not a doubled background. I always thought wonders were very expensive. A lvl 5 costed 10 freebies, that's a lot for an item that someone can break. It happens it costs 4 freebies. That's a lot better.

                    I'm personally in favor of XP costs for Backgrounds, but also with the Sanctity rules in place (if the story strips you of a BG, then you get refunded full cost). Had applied that on my games even before CoD gave it a name.

                    But I also don't think every thing that can be represented by a BG should be so, it only defines who has primary control over the thing, the Player or the ST.

                    That said, I think a mixed approach is valid. A player that wants the Wonder enough to invest XP should have an easier time getting it and some sense of being secure in its possession (the Player, not the Character). For an item the player won't spend XP on, then the ST is free to make it as hard or easy to get it as the story demands and do with it afterwards as they please.

                    Yet, I think in general Wonder Creation rules have always been more complex than they really need to. In the end the rules only govern putting into it effects the Mage can already cast, so as useful as they are, they're not going to really add new powers to the Mage. And even for a Mage who don't have access to those effects, a Wonder is far more static than actually saving the XP for personal growth. The other lines have far less juice out of their magic systems and yet work perfectly fine with simpler and cheaper magic items, Mage is so complicated to get something that really doesn't give you a fraction of the benefits.

                    My personal preference today is to ditch completely the rules for Wonders in any edition and adapt the rules for Imbued Items and Artifacts from Mage the Awakening.

                        Yeah. One of the things that I have on my M5 wishlist is for the Wonder rules to be simplified, streamlined, and — in terms of how wonders are to be made — folded into the rules for extreme Feats.

                        And I would definitely borrow some ideas from Awakening, such as the Imbued Items rules or the “sufficiently powerful Magick costs XP to perform” concept found in Imperial Mysteries: generalizing the concept, practically permanent Effects, or even Effects that are so big that they'll have lasting consequences on the world at large long after the Effect itself has technically ended, shouldn't just be a matter of how many Successes are needed; there should be precious resource costs, such as specific kind of Tass and/or XP, associated with such “great works”. Not to restrict the Magick per se, but rather as a way of encouraging story: if a working requires you to use a particular kind of Tass, then you must somehow acquire that Tass; and unless you already have a ready supply of it, that involves going out and finding it.

                        You should be able to model feats such as shifting a Reality Zone using the Magick rules: not because shifting a Zone should be something that can be done with just a series of dice rolls, but because the Magick-working game systems should incorporate narrative-driven elements into large works. Riffing off of Clarke's Law, a sufficiently powerful Magick should be indistinguishable from Story.

                        And making Wonders should be an application of that, not a separate game system.

                        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

So, it ain't that relevant in the end - but it has the rigth spirit.

                                Imperial Mysteries definitely does arch-magic rigth. I believe that what makes that system great, more than anything else, it's Quintessence. Quintessence isn't a random resource you get over time. Quintessence it's a story. It's mistery that, by it's unique nature and it's relation to the spell, very much causes the spell to become a story. You're literally fueling your spells with story.

                                Let's keep that in mind...

                                Making things cost XP in the current state of the game will not encourage story because XP you will get over time, regardless of the kind of story you're playing. XP main purpose isn't to encourage story but to regulate character grow rate. On the contrary, I don't like Backgrounds costing XP precisely because, as things are now, it disincentivizes story: If you need a Cult, instead of roleplaying, roll those social attributes, etc. you're entitled to pay the XP you won in the last Technocratic raid

                                Now, I know the ST should ban that expenditure unless the player did something to justify it, but the fact it's that typically XP is expended taking in count off-camera "downtime actions" (because nobody want's to- say- have to roleplay studying STEM all day to gain Sciences. It's not going to be an important part of the story). ST can policy this, but it's a negative solution. I would rather not move the story with the ban hammer.

                                However, the way XP is rewarded does encourage story in a more positive way. An easy and concrete example being the "mystical xp" that Awakening rewards from discovering misteries. That encourages players to seek that kind stuff, moving stories foward (oWoD uses XP to encourage story too, but in a more general fashion). The key here it's not so much how XP is expended, but how it's gained

                                If Wonder making requires an XP gained in a particular kind of story, or a story that has a specific elements in the plot, that does encourage to include such stories or plot elements. For instance: The story where you got the special Tass. Special Tass and special XP could be related (or even be the same thing).
