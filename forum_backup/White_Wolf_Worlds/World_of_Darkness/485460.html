    Dark Ages: Hunter setting riff

    04-19-2015, 12:47 PM
    My Lord Bishop,

    I write this missive from Pont-Audemer, or what was once said village. The tales are true, and more besides, may God in Heaven have mercy upon us.

    I confess that when I first let Évreux a mere fortnight ago, I dismissed the rumors as mere peasant stories, frightening tales told in taverns and in front of hearths. So much of our holy work involves such nonsense, as you well know, and nearly always the trail of the tale wends its way through many mouths before vanishing into the mists of "Two villages over, they say..."

    But my certainty was shaken along the road to Pont-Audemer. There, while resting at the abbey in Corneville-sur-Risle, I was woken in the night by a great clamor in the village square. Rousing myself, I hastened to see what was the cause of this noisy disturbance.

    It was a family of farmers who dwelt in Pont-Audemer. There were four of them, the farmer, his wife, and two young children - four, where there should have been six! When myself, the abbot and the local knight had calmed them down, which was no easy task, we learned that the farmer's elder sons had both been slain by some sort of wild beasts.

    What manner of beasts? asked I.

    Like unto men, but men who had long been sleeping in their graves! answered the farmer.

    All of Pont-Audemer was overrun with such goules. They themselves had but barely escaped.

    There was, as you no doubt expect, great consternation at such a tale. The farmer was known to a few of the people of Corneville, and all vouched for him as an honest, God-fearing Christian. Moreoever, the farmer's wife and the elder of the two youths both swore the story was true upon their very souls. The youngest child was not yet old enough to speak, but was clearly greatly afraid.

    The hour was late, nearing the midnight, but action was no doubt called for. The knight, Charles d'Aubert by name, had himself armored and saddled, and along with four of his guards, rode off with me and one of the monks of the abbey to investigate.

    It is some two leagues from Corneville to Pont-Audemer, and we had traversed perhaps half that distance when we came across many more scared peasants, all telling the same tale - the dead had risen from their graves and were rampaging through the village and the country alongside.

    At hearing so many voices tell this story, I felt no small amount of holy dread. Was this, then, the opening page of the Parousia? Would soon the angel sound his trumpet?

    At such times, a man - this man at least - can only heed his heart. And mine said Ride on! There may yet be work you can do.

    So I told Sir Charles, and he rallied his men. We rode on. It was not long, not long at all, before the truth of the tales was made clear to us.

    As we passed by a copse of trees, there did emerge three figures. Men, by the first look, but the moon then graced - or cursed, perhaps - us with better light. They had been men, but the peasants spoke true. These were the dead risen from their grave slumber. In one case, it had been a long slumber, for what shuffled towards us was little more than flakes of papery flesh over lumpy bones, as it is said the Pharoahs of old look in their tombs. The other were but newly dead, but that made their visages no less horrible.

    We made ready to cut them down, but there was no need.

    From the woods behind them, a man emerged, a living man. He held a humble pitch fork in his hand, no great weapon against three goules. But when they turned and attacked him, the fork blazed like a torch, nay, like the white heart of a forge, and each blow set its target to burning. In but a moment, all the goules had been destroyed.

    Once this was done, the man asked "Did the angels guide you here, too?"

    * * *

    It is the Year of our Lord 1230, and a Reckoning has come for the children and servants of Satan...

    In other words, let's see if we can build a Dark Ages version of Hunter: the Reckoning, shall we?

    "But isn't that kind of what DA: Inquisitor is?"
    "Not really. There's hunters and there's Hunters. This is about the Imbued, not Inquisitors."

    "So you want to have the Imbued show up 750 odd years earlier?"
    "Pretty much! And all the apocalyptic goodness that goes with them. The Red Star, Thin Blood, the Sixth - okay, Second - Great Maelstrom, etc etc."

    "What about the web and HunterNet?"
    "Okay, some things obviously won't translate over..."

    * * *

    Some ideas and questions I've had:

    * Possibly adapting the KotE backstory about the Wan Xian (also alluded to in a couple revised VtM clanbooks) so it ties into Judeo-Christian mythology like Vampire does. F'instance, say that the Nephilim from Genesis were the Wan Xian, or the Wan Xian were the Nephilim if you prefer, and the Flood was as much to punish them as it was to destroy the First City.

    * Fiddling with the Cappadocian/Giovanni metaplot so they're tied into the Second Great Maelstrom that unleashes the walking dead and triggers the return of the Imbued.

    * An alternate Week of Nightmares. Maybe Ravnos is still the one who rises first, but it can't be the Technocracy that finally kills him, since they don't exist yet. If only there were powerful mages mostly unfettered by Paradox who could step into that role... Or maybe Set or Haqim rise up instead, what with the Crusades and all that?

    * How would a society that's much more open to the existence of the supernatural, yet also much more culturally and religiously homogeneous, deal with the existence of the Imbued and the mystical mayhem that goes with them?

    Whaddaya think, sirs? Ideas? Comments?

      Not really. There's hunters and there's Hunters. This is about the Imbued, not Inquisitors.
      To-May-To, To-Mah-To. I generally go with the notion that the powers of the Imbued are the powers of True Faith, but divorced from the notion of religious devotion. In fact, I generally scrap the True Faith merit, per se, and handle the Faithful (both pre- and post-Reckoning) using a slightly tweaked version of the rules for the Imbued (the tweaks focusing mainly on incorporating suitable mechanics from Dark Ages: Inquisitor, such as the notion of Superior Virtues that enhance regular Virtues and a Piety trait that serves to rein in the excesses of too much Conviction). As such, “the Reckoning occurs nearly a thousand years ahead of schedule”, to me, amounts to people spontaneously becoming Inquisitors, without the involvement of the Church. Possibly replace Inquisitor's Blessings with Hunter's Edges, or adapt the Edges to work as Endowments that are Virtue-based rather than faction-based.

      As for the trigger, you could simply say that the solar eclipse that the Dark Ages line was set up to build toward has proven to be far more significant than it was historically,

      My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

              Both of you raise good points. I could see groups within torn over the matter of the Imbued, some of them seeking to gather and train as many of them as they can, others viewing them as just as corrupt and tainted as any of the other supernaturals. And that's not including those Imbued that aren't Christian.

              That said, there is still plenty both groups cover and that's really the main problem. In the modern day the Inquisition is a shadow of itself but that's not the case in the Dark Ages. At this point they're in their prime and there's really not a lot the Imbued could bring to the table that they don't already cover. Without the hunter.net they lack what little cohesion they have in the modern era. Yes, they could set up a similar network through mundane means but to do so would take a great deal of time and effort to set up and maintain as well as some organization which starts to bring it into the realm of the Inquisition.

              If I was to bring the Imbued into the Dark Ages, I would keep them just as they are HtR, small scale but with the isolation factor ramped up as they don't have the means of long-range communications. Small groups of 'blessed' individuals fighting against the monsters in the night with no real idea just how dark the night truly is.

              Though I do admit, just the idea of the Time of Judgement happening back then rather than in the present day has a lot of potential.

              Homo sapiens. What an inventive, invincible species. It's only a few million years since they crawled up out of the mud and learned to walk. Puny, defenceless bipeds. They've survived flood, famine and plague. They've survived cosmic wars and holocausts. And now, here they are, out among the stars, waiting to begin a new life. Ready to outsit eternity. They're indomitable. Indomitable.

                      All right, let's work off that then. The Inquisition is slowly getting a handle on what it faces in the shadow crusade - and then just as strange new stories of walking corpses spread from one end of Christendom to the other, and even beyond, a strange new force emerges, ordinary folks (almost all of them peasants) granted weird powers and a burning desire to confront the Inquisition's enemies.

                      Who are they? What are they? From where do these powers come? Are they magicians? Have they made pacts with the Devil and his fellow fallen angels? Or could it be that they are the warriors of God, an army meant to fight the battles of Armageddon? But how can this be, if they are being called in Saracen lands, in India, even in Cathay? Surely God would not bless those who do not even recognize Him?

                      From the Imbued POV, they're asking themselves many of the same questions. The Messengers that speak to them - what are they? God's angels? Satan's? The third host, those who fought for neither side in the war in Heaven? What is their purpose? For the ones that dwell in Asia, why this strange sense of both kinship and hatred between them and the night-walkers of the east?

                      The potential for cults forming around the Imbued is probably a lot greater in the Dark Ages than the modern day, which only adds to the tense relationship between Imbued and Inquisition. (Tangent - a three way war between Tzimisce, Shadow Lords and Imbued & allies/followers in Transylvania would be a cool campaign premise, I think.)

                                  Eastern Orthodoxy is a denomination of Catholicism. And please reserve such blanket statements on similarity, because this can be considered quite insulting. There is a major cultural and geographical bias that ended up shifting the two variants quite a bit in how they actually operate. Just because they both include the Bible and Jesus does not make them "very similar".

                                  The only variations of Orthodox "Inquisition" came about among the Russians as a cause celebre when they had schisms and heretics to deal with, in the late 15th and late 19th century. It was on a smaller scale, and was never a fully sanctioned and organised order as was the Inquisition. The Inquisition was literally an organisation created by Rome to propagate and control Faith in the majority of Europe and fight the rise of heretic faiths, we didn't have any of those in the rest of Europe. Of course, you could always argue that the Byzantine Empire was quite big on murdering people and torturing people of wrong faith for geopolitical reasons, but they never really had an organised arm of the Church for that.

                                  There were witch-finders, witch-hunters and assorted smaller groups of "Inquistors" across the whole world and across all the cultures and religions, but never with the organisation and geographical influence of the Inquisition.

                                  What doesn't kill you, makes you... stranger.
