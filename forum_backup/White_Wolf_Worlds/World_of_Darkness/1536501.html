      Which qualities are you looking for?

      For material hardness, I interpret the Sphere levels as:

      Matter 2: Turn soft steel into carbon steel
      Matter 3: Increase density to increase hardness
      Matter 5: Turn material into Unbreakium (or increase density permanently)

      I reserve Matter 5 for material with really outlandish (ie magic) properties - Kryptonite, Vibranium, Unobtainium etc

          I agree with voidshaper.

          In a more general sense, with Matter 2 you can alter one substance into another (of the same phase, for... reasons). With Matter 3 you can alter the common properties of matter, like density, hardness, conductivity, shape, phase, etc. Matter 5 lets you give the less common properties (like radioactivity for some reason) and create "impossible" properties or substances (this brick passes through glass, but not stone).

                  I think those restrictions were well meant but futile attempts by the writers to prevent player shenanigans, and so seem pretty arbitrary. Turning something into plasma or BE-condensate is on paper a simple change of phase. With a little math you can discover that creating baby black holes is just a question of density to which the answer isn't all that unattainable on small scales. Degenerate neutron star matter may be a simple Matter 2 effect away. Or anti-matter, strange matter, and a multitude of other things from the Book of Bad Ideas.

                                              The issue is more in how things are phrased by the authors to us the players of the game.                                                   Originally posted by quelsec View Post

                                                  Not sure if it is what you are asking, but if you are looking for something like a table that would translate a dice roll into hard values that quantify the effect you are out of luck. Like Heavy Arms mentioned, the system shorthands attributes you would also have to consider outside of the general scope of the spell.

                                                  That's what I meant when I said earlier that I would tie the effect to the purpose: If you want to make something harder to protect yourself, your effect is 'armor', not 'harder material' per se. The resulting effect in game (soak) is something different than, say, you want to make a plank of wood stronger to carry more weight. It's a lot easier to translate a roll into whatever the spell 'wants' rather than trying to simulate an effect with hard values.

                                                  Edit: An exception might be an Enlightened Scientist whose Focus is tied to such things as concrete physical values. If they study an effect, they would likely have tests that measure what it does- but even in that case, it would still be more practical to abstract those values into game values that match how the result is used.
