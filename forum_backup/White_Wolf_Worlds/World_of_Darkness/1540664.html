          The idea that reality is wholly shaped by humanity doesn't even stand up to scrutiny on Earth. There are Earthly Foundations that serve as a baseline of reality that the Consensus cannot change, for example.

          I'm not surprised that the Umbra tends to be free of human meddling.

          My own view on it is that the Gauntlet shields the Umbra from the effects of the Consensus. And the Umbra itself has a second layer of defense in that humans who migrate to the Umbra tend to turn into spirits, losing their Avatars (Awake or Asleep) in the process. So the Umbra proper, as opposed to Umbral Realms that have their own form of Gauntlet between them and the Umbra itself, is unlikely to ever be colonized by humans.

                      Yes. To me, the world of Mage is Subjective - FULLY Subjective. There's really no room for a middle ground here, because either the Moon exists irregardless of my solipsism, or the Moon exists only when I'm looking at it because I expect it to be there.

                      Now, sure, the Earth's tides don't rely on my fishing schedule alone (or does it now?), which is why this fabulous thing called "reality" is built based on a CONSENSUS.

                      But a Consensus of what exactly?

                      And 2nd ed gave this possibility, and indeed it makes sense, that humans are not the only ones - and probably, not even the most relevant ones - agents of Consensus.

                      AKA if you want to change gravity, first you'll have to ask permission for all spirits to change their minds.

                      All of WoD has heavy Gnostic ideas, with the 2 major offenders being Mage and Demon the Fall. This idea of subjective reality comes straight out from gnosticism. Mage also has the idea of "the One" and of "Avatars" as "shards of the One". This is Gnosticism 101. In fact, "we are all God", everything you see and hear and feel around you is also God, and essentially all the "reality" is just God having a drug induced "bad trip" crazy dream. "Ascension", the most Gnostic thing I have ever seen, is reaching unity with "the One" or with "the Whole", finally realizing that everything is in fact the same thing (God).

                      Therefore, a "gravity spirit" which "believes things should always fall down" is constantly reinforcing the "Consensus of Gravity". Mages call this "Earthly Foundation" simply because there's too many spirits reinforcing that idea, so much so that the whole of humanity wouldn't be able to change it.

                      But, since me, you, gravity spirits, rocks and plants and the dirty beneath our feet and the air above our heads, since that all is actually the same thing and that thing is "God", then that simply means that "God" is much more stubborn about gravity in his drug induced dream where the almighty thinks he is an infinitude of things (rather than the single one all encompassing cosmic unity), than he is about which kinds of magic should be coincidental or vulgar. There are many many more pieces of God dreaming about gravity than there are pieces dreaming about a day where cellphones will no longer work.

                      Therefore, I wouldn't say that "Consensus" or "Earthly Foundations" are things 'set on stone', funnily enough. Things (probably) are more complicated than they seem.

                          Allow me to clarify something here that seems to be getting lost:

                          Originally posted by Bernese View Post
                          how much does human thought affect umbra? there are places and things in the umbra that contradict the idea that reality is wholly shaped by humanity (i.e. the deep umbra and the aliens) is it only earth and the moon?
                          Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                          The idea that reality is wholly shaped by humanity doesn't even stand up to scrutiny on Earth. There are Earthly Foundations that serve as a baseline of reality that the Consensus cannot change, for example.

                          I'm not surprised that the Umbra tends to be free of human meddling.

                          My own view on it is that the Gauntlet shields the Umbra from the effects of the Consensus. And the Umbra itself has a second layer of defense in that humans who migrate to the Umbra tend to turn into spirits, losing their Avatars (Awake or Asleep) in the process. So the Umbra proper, as opposed to Umbral Realms that have their own form of Gauntlet between them and the Umbra itself, is unlikely to ever be colonized by humans.
                          The question asked, and my initial response, were both about humanity's influence on reality. Is it possible that reality in Mage is wholly subjective, but that there are more participants in defining that subjectivity than just humans? Sure! While I won't go so far as to say that Mages reality is 100% subjective, I have no problem saying that that's a possibility.

                          But again, the original assertion wasn't that reality isn't wholly subjective; it was that it isn't wholly shaped by human beliefs. And there's no doubt about that. Maybe I should have said "the human Consensus" instead of "the Consensus" in my post in order to be more clear; but in my defense, the books always use "the Consensus" to refer to collective human beliefs.

                          Is the larger reality determined only by beliefs, with a larger Consensus that includes gods and monsters as well as humans? It's possible. But there are some things that the Consensus has little to no say over, and it doesn't matter if that's because they're fundamental truths or because God said so. The practical effects are the same.

                          And since there seems to be a tendency to jump to assumptions here, let me clarify one other thing: Earthly Foundations aren't absolute. They can be overruled — by individuals, no less; because in Mage, an individual Awakened is more capable of effecting change than the entire Consensus is. The Consensus cannot overrule Earthly Foundations; a Mage absolutely can.

                                  Some things are almost always vulgar, no matter who's doing them, where they're doing them, or how they're being done. In game terms, acts that vulgar these Earthly Foundations contradict the baseline reality for Earth.
                                  • Sudden Large Alterations of Physical and Metaphysical Mass or Space: …
                                  • Obvious violations of normal physical laws: …
                                  • Cross- dimensional rifts: …
                                  • Messing with the time stream: …
                                  M20 pp.612–613

                                  ​​​​​
                                  There you go: four Earthly Foundations given in M20, specifically right where the concept of Earthly Foundations are first presented. There are caveats in there (e.g., "almost always vulgar"), and you might disagree with one or two of the examples given (which, given Storyteller's prerogative, means that they're not Earthly Foundations in your games); but that doesn't change the fact that four examples of Earthly Foundations are given.
