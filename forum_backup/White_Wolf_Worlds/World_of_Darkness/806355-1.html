                            Originally posted by Fat Larry View Post
                            But that's the problem. I've never had issues playing some of the parts of the WoD people have had trouble playing.
                            It doesn't matter if you personally haven't had a problem with things. It matters that you acknowledge that other people have problems with the same things. A white man doesn't need to suffer the racism of the US criminal justice system personally to acknowledge it exists, or say, "This doesn't directly impact me, but I can see why it should change."

                            All the stereotypes that EXIST in the real world,...
                            There is a huge difference between playing in a game world where discriminatory stereotypes are reflective of the ones in our world, and one that makes discriminatory stereotypes metaphysically true.

                            That's the line that really gets to people. Are drunken Irish werewolves a thing? Sure. So are drunken Irish humans. The thing is that the Fianna aren't actually just drunken Irish werewolves, and they're not forced to be so by fluff or by mechanics. There's no trait in the game that says, "You're either Irish, or of Irish decent. You have to spend WP to resist getting drunk whenever you have the chance, and you lose 2 dice on social rolls if you don't talk in a strong accent or in Irish Gaelic. All ethnically Irish PCs must take this to be considered truly Irish."

                            The game doesn't go off the rails just because characters happen to intersect with real world stereotypes in various ways. The games become problems when they force stereotypes on characters.

                            That's the difference between the Assamites, and a signature Ventrue who's feeding restriction is triggery. You don't have to play a Venture that only feeds on rape victims. Such a Ventrue never has to be in any of your games just because the books have one in them. You can't use the Assamites without bringing in game-enforced anti-Arab/Muslim/Middle-Eastern stereotypes.

                            Someone is always going to find something to be offended by, regardless of their location, regardless of their ideologies.
                            Cop out response. The correct response to this is not, "so screw caring about judging the situations, and just be careless about how offensive you are."

                            This is not a black & white, yes or no, issue. We are capable of looking at a complaint about something in the games, recognize why people had a bad experience because of the game's writing, and then making rational judgements on (1) if the complaint is something truly wrong with the game, or the game and the individual being a bad fit - plenty of people just aren't going to enjoy the subjects the WoD touches on - (2) if the issue at hand can be fixed for future use by addressing the problems and rectifying them with better text, or (3) if the issue really is something that can only be fixed by excising the material and starting over.

                            I just want to play the games, man.
                            What makes you think the rest of us don't?

                            And another issue? As per my example, I don't consider the Fianna a problem. So there's nothing to acknowledge. I don't see a need to "fix" it. It's not broken, to me. Of course, that's just one example. I guess the katana would be another.
                            What's to acknowledge is when people make valid complaints about the Fianna as presented in the books. Or valid complaints about katana stats.

                            All you're doing is saying, "My opinion is all that matters, so if someone presents a reason why my opinion might need to be reanalyzed, I have to attack such positions to preserve the primacy of my opinions."

                            You come off as caring far more about the purity of your idealized concept of the games, and shouting down changes (even if you'll acknowledge they might be positive overall changes), than actually having people game on.

                                        The original corebooks did a good job at describing the general society of a worldwide supernatural group. We could assume there were local differences, but the Camarilla was a worldwide organization, the Litany was the prehistoric law of the Garou, the Traditions were an alliance that incorporated all magical traditions, and ghosts were dead no matter what (although the various depictions of mythological underworlds were explained). I really liked that.

                                        Then, as the game developed, people kept shrinking what these groups were and said they only applied to the West. Everyone else was their own thing, and often had completely different game mechanics. They took the late twentieth century concept of muliculturalism and retroactively applied it back even though it made no sense. White Wolf thought they were doing justice to other cultures when it was just the opposite. It made the game less cool. As bad as Asia was, Africa was equally so.

                                        I have no problem with there being some mysteries in the game, and saying a chronicle set in Asia would be different than in the West (well, duh) is fine. But it was just became overboard. Given the long timeline vampire, I never understood why the Kuei-jin were so unknown in early editions, but it was so minor it could be easily ignored - and wasn't any worse than any of the other bloodlines. Then it all became cheesey nonsense.

                                        In my own games, I ignore most of these later supplements.
