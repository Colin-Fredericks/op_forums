                                              I've been gearing up for a Changeling game, so I was thinking about stuff for inspiration for that series at least.

                                              Amagai Brilliant Park and Chuunibyou are both pretty good inspirational sources for Changeling the Dreaming. Amagai Brilliant Park involves faeries from another world who have to subsist on joyful human emotions, so they run a giant amusement park to attract people, which wouldn't be impossible for a group of Changelings to do - some place like that might be a cool location for your freehold.

                                              In Chuunibyou several of the characters think that they're powerful witches, demons or angels (they're just normal people) but sometimes when they fight or do action scenes, they have cool magical battles and such (in reality they're swinging around umbrellas and soup ladles, but in their minds they're throwing out all sorts of cool attacks) and it's very easy to imagine the characters as Changelings and viewing their cool supernatural stuff as actually happening.

                                              The visual novel Umineko: When They Cry (the anime series was pretty terrible and just ends halfway through the storyline) is a fantastic example of Changeling the Dreaming. The primary themes are things like reality, banality, imagination and hope. It's also very meta-textual, in that while one story is happening, much of that is part of another story (similar to how you have two worlds in Changeling), plus it's magic system matches up rather nicely with Changeling. Indeed, it's probably the closest thing I've read to a Changeling the Dreaming story.


                                              More generically the Fate franchise (Fate/Zero, Fate/Stay Night, and other stories in the same universe like Tsukihime or Kara no Kyoukai) involve a world with mages, who hide their powers from normal humans, vampires who secretly feed on people, church inquisitions to hunt down those who reveal the secrets of the supernatural to normal people, etc. The setting has a lot in common with the World of Darkness and it's not hard to grab something from there to use as inspiration.

                                              Finally there's Dance in the Vampire Bund (the anime was it's own original adaptation and rather different from the manga). In this series an ancient, powerful and ludicrously wealthy vampire purchases all of Japan's national debt. In exchange, Japan allows her to purchase an island where she can set up her own, largely separate nation with its own laws, where vampires will be allowed to openly live (the Vampire Bund). She has a werewolf for a bodyguard, and the series has a good mix of action and political maneuvering. It's not a great fit for Vampire, but you can probably find something in there.
