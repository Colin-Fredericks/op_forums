                CoD's use of Power/Fuel/Morality across the board was to facilitate crossover between lines, something cWoD fans had always wanted, but had always been a mechanical headache because MR*H and co. didn't really design the first editions of the games with that in mind.

                The character of the Incredible Hulk was conceived as a sort of Dr. Jekyll/Mr. Hyde meets "walking nuclear bomb", a nuclear physicist caught in the blast of his own creation and turned into a monster. Warren Ellis actually did a pretty cool riff on the concept in his Planetary series, both explaining why a guy building nuclear weapons would be considered a heroic (if slightly tragic) protagonist in the early 1960s, and working in the series's overall concept of reality altering forces by having the man "ride the wave" of the device he'd created and use his genius to "rewrite" his body into something that would survive the event. The original, with its themes of mutation via radiation, fits really well with Werewolf as some sort of unique and freakishly powerful Wyrm monster that would challenge a mid to high level pack as the main villain of a long chronicle (say one where he's planning to create some sort of mass nuclear event to create more of his kind). The other works really well with Mage, either as a unique accidental creation by a scientist on the verge of Awakening (as a Marauder, perhaps), or some sort of Void Engineer or Progenitor experiment gone wrong.
