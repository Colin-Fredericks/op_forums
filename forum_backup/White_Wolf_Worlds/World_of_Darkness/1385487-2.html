    My take on it is that the Traditions have always believed that it's all Willworking; but that that's their thing, the thing that distinguishes them from both the Technocracy (who at least in TR very clearly do not believe that Awakened Magick and Enlightened Science are the same thing; TR says as much in no uncertain terms) and the Crafts (who, in my view at least, mostly tend not to care, one way or the other).

    Where I firmly agree with you is that the Awakening or Enlightenment is fundamentally a realization that you have an innate quality that gives you the power to change the world, literally. And yes, MRev got it wrong when it said otherwise. But that realization does not innately come with the understanding that everyone else who can shape reality is doing the same thing you are. If that was something that every mage instinctively knows, then it would have been a ubiquitous truth since days long past, before the dawn of history; it would not have been something that the Council of Nine discovered in the 1400s.

            You'll note that the post that you quoted has been completely rewritten, because I posted it too fast, and based my response on a literal misreading of what you said. I refer you to the rewritten version.

            In particular, I agree that all of the Traditions agree that the inherent power to change the world is Willworking. That agreement is what the Traditions were founded on. That's one reason you'll find “it's all Willworking” in every Tradition Book.

            But outside of the Traditions is a different matter. Sure, there's still a universally shared experience by everyone who Awakens that they have an innate power to change reality. But the interpretation of the nature of that power, outside of the Traditions, is a lot more varied (except in the Union, where it's not surprisingly fairly regimented — but in a way that's quite different from the Traditions' Willworking notion).

                In terms of the Technocracy's view of it, let me point out three items:

                The Technocrat term for “Avatar” is “Genius”.

                The Technocrat term for “Arete” is “Enlightenment”.

                In the Revised VE Convention Book, the fundamental nature of Tychoidian Cosmology and the Enlightened Anthropic Principle refers to humans as beings who create reality through the processing of information.

                Taken together, the Technocratic conception of “the power to change reality” is rooted in comprehension and imagination rather than will.

                As for the Disparates, well, it depends on the Disparate. The Batini, Children of Knowledge, and Hollow Ones have all been closely associated with the Traditions and tend to share the Willworking mindset that the Traditions embrace. Heck, the Batini helped invent it. Other factions tend to have explanations in keeping with their Focus: the Sisters of Hypollyta might interpret the power to change reality as something that's rooted in Spontaneity, while the Bata'a might see it as something that derives from a Sensitivity to the Umbra.

(Although certain things Etherites do certainly would fall under strange theories of Psionics..but only part of it).

                        Even dropping instruments does not necessarily equal a Mage starting to believe that they are just working their capital W Will in a literal sense.
                        A Verbena I've been RPing is a pretty devout fanatic to an old God. Manifest Avatar kind of helps with that. If she starts dropping instruments, it's not because she realizes "I can work my will!". The opposite, she'll realize the deity is trusting the priestess more and more with potent gifts, while demanding less ritual and etiquette to be granted those blessings.

                        Likewise (And I forgot who mentioned it), somebody in another thread mentioned how dropping instruments can also simply mean, in the mind of the mage's paradigm, that they are letting go of training wheels - an Akashic not needing to perform a certain series of Katas anymore in order to attune their Mind for certain things; But this, too, is less a matter of "I am doing Willworking!" and more a matter of simply "My Mind is trained enough to reach the state of <fancy term here> easily now". It's still well within their first, defined Paradigm.

                        The Traditions, as a coherent group, saying "We're just doing willworking guys!" does not mesh.

                                                The Traditions are people with drastically different views of reality with opposing goals and endgames.
                                                Just because they've worked together for a while and played nice with each other isn't indicitive that they truly trust each other. If they ever took down the Technocracy (They won't) then they wouldn't have the cohesion that a common enemy brings and they would go right back to squabbling and fighting amongst themselves with humanity as the group that suffers. The Disparate Alliance is fully aware that each magical group has different, and sometimes diametrically opposed goals, and as such isn't trying to standarize magic or force each other to try and mesh world beliefs together.
                                                Also, while the Technocracy does do bad things as a whole, they are also dealing with extinction level threats and are the only group with /humanity's/ survival in mind. It doesn't excuse their sins, but they technically at war for the future survival of reality. Reality Deviants are very dangerous and its impossible to say the Technocracy hasn't improved the lives of humanity as a whole vastly. To them brainwashing people, including their own people, is literally a safeguard to prevent the world from turning into horror fantasy nightmare that the Traditions could bring about.
                                                Even before the Technocracy existed the awakened were taking advantage of sleepers through magic and megalomania. Yes, some mages treated sleepers with respect and protected them. But many abused their power and caused suffering and pain. The Technocracy is the outcome of such abuse of power. They are trying to give humanity a life of relative peace and stability, give them the gift if ignorance to the evils in the world that they would have no defense against. They gave them tools and weapons that can be used against those evils should they find themselves in situations that could mean life or death.
                                                The Traditions are all about themselves. Believe me, I actually love the Traditions in different ways, but it wouldn't be that farfetched for the Traditions to use the same methods the Technocracy uses if they were in power. In fact I'd personally expect it to come about.
                                                Mages and more specifically magic, by nature, are abusive towards humanity and reality. Hence Paradox and Backlash.
                                                The Technocracy at least tries to be humanity a world were they can thrive, unfortunately they have felt like a drastic set of rules and punishments are needed. Hence why reformation of the Technocracy is probably humanity's best chance at survival the World of Darkness.
                                                I personally think that the Technocracy truly has humanity's best interests at heart but some bad apples have gotten mixed in for purposes of power and also the Nephandi of course.

Yes they'll be able to communicate between them easier, but at the cost of their magical identities.
                                                Thats why I personally like Pillars and Foundations and tend to view Spheres as a metagame mechanic when I do play (Which admittedly isn't often and its been a while since I played with others besides my husband.) I also feel the flexible paradigm system seems more like it would belong to the Disparate Alliance and Orphans.

                                                Tldr; Traditions are only a thing because of the Technocracy and without it would immediately turn to squabbling and fighting amongst themselves over who's paradigm would be supreme.                                                     This, with the added caveat that being ecumenical essentially boils down to “there are a few key points that we can agree on; and in the larger scheme of things, what unites is is more important than what divided us.” That's why I keep harping on the “We Are All Gods in Disguise” Paradigm from Book of Secrets: while it's not a perfect fit for inclusion in every Traditionist's Focus, something like it should be.

                                                    In particular, I did not intend to imply that Traditionists don't believe in their own Focus; that's literally a contradiction, seeing as how one's Focus is founded on what one believes.                                                     I would also submit, though, that if you have a mystical Focus (as opposed to a technomantic one), it should be hard for you not to believe in Willworking, as even an Arete 1 Mage is capable of spending the Willpower and accepting the increased difficulty to Work Without Focus — at least, the way the rules are written. I have a bit of a problem with this, myself, which is why I have a house rule about recasting mystic vs. technomantic as internal vs. external: that way, if your Verbena truly believe that her power comes from the gods that she serves, she has an external Focus and can't Work Without Focus. But that's about as far as I intend to go along that tangent here; the main point is that “I believe in Willworking” isn't the same thing as “I believe that my Practice and Instruments are pointless”.
