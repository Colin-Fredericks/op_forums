                              The majority of the cores books don't have a cap on Merits, just a suggested 7 point cap on Flaws. People putting caps on Merits are generally making a house rule, not using the RAW (within reason, the limits on Merits and Flaws is RAW flexible to the ST's judgemental calls).

                              There's also the Natural Linguist Merit which makes characters that speak more than four languages much cheaper to do (and gets you additional benefits which could be applied to translating languages you don't know but should have a chance to decipher).

                                          The usefulness of languages is one of those super-subjective things, which is why it's actually best as a Merit instead of the old placement in the Linguistics Knowledge.

                                          I also highly disagree with the idea that languages are a binary thing where either you never need it (but then you never have to take a Merit), or every player is going to take the Merit.

                                          I've been in plenty of games where only a few characters heavily invest in Languages, and it's always been useful for them to do so. The most common setup I see is the "face" character that speaks all of the common modern languages, and as the best social character is doing a lot of the talking with NPCs anyway, spoken in the setting of the game, and the "academic" character that has a good smattering of ancient languages to get in the occult investigation stuff. Both character archetypes get value for the investment, and can do enough that there isn't a need for everyone to get it.

                                          Of course, it's situational. In Mage games, the Language Merit is exceedingly common to the point of mandatory because so many groups use language as part of their Instruments, but there's lots of ways to avoid it if you need to. A Chorister doesn't need to be fluent in a language that they pray in to use it for magic.

                                                  Given the nature of any point-buy game, part of GMing is adapting the setting/etc. of the thing you're running to what the players make. If nobody makes a combat capable PC, you don't throw lots of combat heavy stuff at them. If you do have a combat heavy PC, you include combat because that lets that player have fun.

                                                  So to me, the question is sort of a chicken or egg sort of thing. Unless your ST is very set on a specific story path you follow and you have to get past the NPC that only speaks language X by talking to them without any ability to get help, it matters as much as the group makes it matter... which is true of anything in the game.
