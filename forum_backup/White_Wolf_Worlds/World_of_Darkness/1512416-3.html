                    OK, so here's the thing about the 10 dice pool human brawler:

                    It doesn't matter.

                    Using V20 rules, and being a bit generous with averages, and a 10 dice attack with Strength 5 that's bashing? Let's say they roll 5 successes (the vampire didn't take a defensive action, at all). That's 9 dice of damage, so let's say they get 5 successes again. That would get reduced to 2B damage dealt if the Vampire failed their soak roll completely.

                    That's how little of a threat a maxed out human's punches mean to a vampire. No dodging or soak, no Disciplines, no blood spending, no nothing but being a vampire... and the best human fighter in the world is doing the high end of average just to deal two damage.

                    That attack also means no defense, so our vampire is counter attacking - and humans don't halve bashing damage (or soak the aggravated damage that vampires can deal with bites) - against no defensive action either.

                    While the stat blocks posted above seem to be relying on the Revised implementation of Disciplines, it's not a huge deal. The vampire's best strategy is to take the max-human's opening hit(s), maybe spend a blood to heal if they need it (though most likely they don't and can spend it on Str instead), and then grapple as a counter. Potence 2-3 w/blood easily makes up for the difference in dice pools, and the vampire can start agg-biting.

                    So not min-maxed to high heaven, not using something you even need to spend a ton of freebies on to be able to do, no prepping before the fight to pump up your physicals to 6/6/6 and snacking on someone to top up; just a decent fighter with a supernatural edge completely overturning everything the maxed out human can do. It would be a pretty slow fight by WoD standards, but time is on the vampire's side here.

                    A fairly combat oriented but hardly optimally so neonate... with huge potential to get way stronger... is absolutely the statistical favored to win against a human that's reached the max a human can reach.

                                                Eh, short of something like fate or destiny, the presence of magic in a setting doesn't give you the excuse to write off the mundane. Questions like "why is Samwell Tarly still so fat" or "How does the Witcher continent maintain it's london-like ethnic diversity after thousands of years" don't become invalid because somewhere, miles away in that setting, there's a few dragons and some corpses became monsters.

                                                And it's a natural reaction to consider problems as things to solve. Oh, my gun barely leaves a dent in it, how much bigger does my next gun need to be, should I buy armour piercing rounds? Since prehistory, if something didn't go down when you hit it with a stick, you needed a bigger stick, or a sharper stick, or a rock, or metal. Just accept human nature and accept that if vampires or aliens or dragons or demons or whatever else is unknown suddenly became very real our monkey brains would delight in inventing stratagems to kill them.
