                                                I've had trouble with Changeling. I know that part of the theme is that they don't belong anymore, the Banality and all that, but they feel thematically out of step. For me Changelings were a casualty of time; Fae still exist but are powerful, alien and inhuman. Them appearing in our realm is a rare thing indeed. Modern WoD is an "age of men" situation, the Elves are long gone.

                                                Demon appeals to me, but I'd need to make some big mechanical and lore-based changes. Firstly, we should get it straight that the memories of Demon's origins are garbled and unreliable; some Demons truly believe that the Abrahamic God made them, some think that their memories of these mythic events are the result of potent mortal belief; Demons reflect Western Concepts of Demons because of the influence of Christianity on the world, basically.

                                                Same with Werewolf; this Spirit can say with certainty that it has personhood, but this is just projection on the part of personal creatures who interact with it. Spirits don't, by and large, have personhood. It's only thanks to the immense power of the Garou nation that Spirits all swear by all that's holy that everything the Garou believe is 100% true.

                                                So my personal fix for Demon and Werewolf is that a lot of the things spiritual beings (from Fallen Angels to spirits) believe is... questionable. Spirits aren't the creation of belief, but belief does shape them. Kupala would be just one example; the intense belief of a group of powerful supernaturals (potent Tzimisce Elders with their own primitive Magics) mutated the spirit of a bit of geography into a sort of blood-god-spirit. Whether Kupala was a "spirit" or an Earthbound is irrelevant; the faith of the Tzimisce has reshaped her.

                                                Why do I like this? It makes the true origins of everything murky. It makes people who think Abrahamic religions are bullshit more comfortable inhabiting the world; it isn't all built on something they've rejected. It leaves room for outside perspectives. Have the myths be just that; myths. A murderer was the first Vampire; was he "Caine"? Who knows! Demons claim that he was, but their memories aren't exactly cast-iron. Maybe Caine inspired Cain. Maybe Cain was the origin of Caine. Maybe God actually exists! Or maybe he's just a potent spirit, like all human gods. It also gives legitimacy to Werewolves (and Fera) who refuse their role in existence; they aren't fighting reality, they're fighting a system.

                                                It also means that the Setite who thinks Set = Caine and Osiris = Abel has nothing contradicting them directly in Word of God. Same for the Garou who thinks that Shifters are biological phenomena. Same for the Demon who thinks that their memories of the "start of time" are the trickery of some powerful ancient demon.
