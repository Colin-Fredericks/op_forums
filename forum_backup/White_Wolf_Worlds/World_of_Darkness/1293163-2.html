                            Is the issue that you have a hard time envisioning a psuedo science that says a man can perform these feats with the right training or motions? Or is the issue that the book implies things that go that far are somehow not enough like Science?

                            If it is the latter, I'm going to say ignore the book on this one, because there have been a lot of strange theories over the years that seem magical to us now but at the time were treated as real subjects of scientific inquiry (or at least as inquiry into the natural sciences). In Galileo's time it was believed that a drum made of lamb skin would not make a sound in the presence of a drum made of wolf skin. Lamark proposed that animals mutated over their lifetimes and could pass on those mutations. Within the last 200 years spontaneous generation was considered a valid explanation of how maggots formed.

                            There really isn't a dividing line of strangeness where something stops being a science and starts being magic because some sciences were (and are) far, far stranger than magic. If the technomancer thinks he is operating of of strange natural laws or principles that allow an effect to happen, that's scientific enough to count. Even if it makes him sound like a character out of a comic book.
