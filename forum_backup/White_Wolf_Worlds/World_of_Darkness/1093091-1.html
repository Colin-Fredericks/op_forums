    Well, it depends on your interpretations of the stories. Yes, evil and corruption are human things (or humanoid things) but that is because the Elder Gods and Great Old Ones are so powerful that they step on humans much like humans might step on ants when crossing a field. Do they intend to step on humans? No. Do they care that they step on humans? No. Would they avoid stepping on humans if they noticed them? No. They are evil in their indifference to humans because they are willing to destroy humans (even all of humanity) without a second thought. Earth does seem to have some minor importance in the Lovercraftian stories though, even if humans do not, as it seems to be a cosmic meeting place where lots of things end up.

                        It's like people walking down the street.

                        Humans know there are ants on the ground. And we know that when we step on ants, they die. But most people walk down the street looking up and ahead, and don't bother looking down. If they step on a bunch of ants, well, tough luck. There are humans who look down as they walk, and try to avoid stepping on ants, but those people tend to be a bit unusual.

                        There's no doubt that from the ant's perspective, the humans are evil and malefic creatures who simply step on them. Even the humans who try to avoid them are still scary and dangerous because they make the earth shake and sometimes they step on ants anyway. And ultimately to the humans, even the ones who try to avoid stepping on ants, ants just ants, and is anyone really going to lose any sleep over stepping on a few?

                        That's how I tend to imagine the Great Old Ones - from the human perspective they're absolutely evil, but from their own perspective humans are completely insignificant.
