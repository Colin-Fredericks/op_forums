                                In reply to some of the ideas inMagus Maximus ‘s posts (with the disclaimer that I’m following the ‘Sewer Gator’ principle of WoD brainstorming/setting writing)

                                re: Early Church-Gnostic Split

                                Of the two major schisms you describe in post #16, it might be interesting to seed various other setting threads that one could run with in their WoD. One interesting hypothesis that could be relevant to storytellers with an interest in the gonzo is the concept that the Roman Empire rewrote the surviving gospels around the times of the various councils that established the early church’s theological positions (the more radical version of this theory is the notion of Jesus of Nazareth being a wholesale invention of Rome) so as to make the gospels more amenable to co-existence with the social and political necessities of the empire. If such a thing was even partially true in one’s WoD, this could provide fodder for a clique of axial age Roman Awakened who could be seen as pre-cursors of the Order of Reason/Technocrats (at least, in the form where they lost resemble the Exarchs of MtAw) in their aim to place limits upon the Gnostic currents. Or, alternatively, it could be a fun concept for the claimed history of a clique of neo-Roman philosopher-mages!

                                Even if that particular story is untrue for your WoD it does draw inspiration imo from a real phenomena that was the process of socio-political conditioning the religious movements are drawn through if they are to harmoniously interweave with a historical polity. One could draw up analogous histories for the development of ‘pagan’, Islamic, Buddhist, Taoist, etc. beliefs which interwove with the socio-political/economic context of their polity. In fact the tensions which often exist within religious traditions as practiced by different segments of society (and reflecting the contradictions between the interests of those segments of society) is a rich vein for mining drama and narrative. Clearly, in the case of the Christian Church, a faction prevailed which would have to ensure that the Church could not only co-exist with the Empire (and later, the barbarian warlords that followed the empire), but synthesize with those political units.

                                One might represent this dynamic by having a faction of Church-awakened (whether or not we want to call them the ‘Celestial Chorus’ proper, I’ll call them the “Curiae” for now) whose interest was to create the world that aligned Empire & Church and who were thus naturally opposed to the Gnostics, the (often Apocalyptic) ‘Populares’, Heretics (miscellaneous heterodox groups), and whatever other factions one wants to include within the Church as vessel for Awakened politics.

                                re: Order of Hermes

                                I def agree that in the various crack ups of the renaissance and early modern period, Hermetic philosophers and the various philosophers who would align themselves with the Rosicrucians would be great fodder for depicting a shake up which would provide the material and ideological bases for something like the Order of Reason to form.

                                The Rosicrucians as described in this early modern period as a secret brotherhood dedicated to aligning the spheres of True Philosophy and True Theology and bringing them together in a harmonious synthesis, which was of course indebted to the general trends of the Reformation, which as one strategy of its critique of the Church had spent a considerable amount of energy in criticizing the various superstitious, crypto-pagan practices which the pre-Reformation Church was accused of either having endorsed or tacitly overlooked. The Counter-Reformation of course swings back and perhaps the Jesuit Brotherhood are the best Catholic counterpart to the role and function of the Rosicrucians in this time period.

                                Now irl the Rosicrucians as described in the classic pamphlets may never have actually existed outside of the idealized dreams of some Protestant German philosopher-theologians but this hasn’t stopped the speculation of latter day occultists that various Protestant philosopher-theologians of the day were in fact involved in private (or even secret) reading and discussion societies which, if not inspired by Rosicrucianism, certainly rhymes with its stated values. Some have even proposed groups such as these as being the antecedent of such organizations as the British Royal Society and the ideological ferment behind such storied figures as Sir Francis Bacon, Sir Isaac Newton, & John Dee. A prime recruiting pool for an Order of Reason type organization.

                                As well as being of a similar background to individuals who would later become involved with Masonic societies after their proliferation (with the Rosicrucians perhaps as among the earliest “Para-Masonic” societies).

                                re: Masons

                                The Masons are a very rich vein and one very useful imo for imagining the sort of influences on an Order of Reason type organization as well as how such an organization could eventually evolve into something like the Technocratic Union, because, as some of my favorite fringe historians have argued, a selected reading of the Brotherhood’s history can in fact be used as mirror to chart the course of modern western society’s development.

                                One of the observations that fascinates me about the Masons, and even if one might quibble with its real world applications I think it’s good for narrative reasons, and might also offer partial explanations for your experience MM, is this heuristic for Masonic history and the historical position of Masons relative to society at large: let us posit that the politically-active Masons are generally aligned with the ambitions of organized cadres of the (petty) bourgeoisie.

                                According to the writer who I first heard this idea from, this can be shown by showcasing a few various tendencies of historical Masonic groups: in the 18th century Masonic Lodges provided means, opportunities, & connections for the class of ideologically & politically motivated bourgeois cadres which would play key roles in the American, French, & Haitian revolutions; using lodges both as technology of social organization and a hot house for developing critiques of feudal and clerical order (for the ur-example of this, see Adam Weishaup’s Bavarian Illuminati).

                                In the 19th century Masonic Lodges could be split between those territories where the above hothouses of revolutionary liberalism were still relevant and thus Masonry could be historically progressive, such as in the Bolivarian revolutions of Latin America or with Garibaldi & the Carbonari in Italy (which, as an aside, some mafia historians have posited the hypothesis that the organization of the Southern Italian mafias as para-Masonic organizations may have their origins in the contact between revolutionary carbonari & rural banditti during imprisonment in 19th century Italy, which is a juicy notion); while in places where bourgeoise society was established Masonic groups took on a conservative or reactionary character (for the former consider British Masonry under Victoria or the proliferation of Masonry within the American political classes, for the latter consider the paramasonic nature of confederare & post-confederate groups such as the Knights of the Golden Circle & the first Ku Klux Klan; also note the curious political slipperiness of Popular Anti-Masonry in 19th century US politics).

                                By the 20th century Masonic Lodges are so infamous for their capacity to harbor nodes of intelligence organization that most mass political movements, whether progressive or reactionary, suppress Masonry upon taking power so as to forestall restoration or counter-attack from the central Bourgeois core powers; despite this such mass political organizations, whether progressive or reactionary, often tend to also make use of many of the social technologies of Freemasonry and could perhaps even be called para-Masonic in their inner cadres. Meanwhile within a core bourgeois power such as the United States, Political Freemasonry remains a live current for the active political and economic classes for a time (my grandfather who was in government work was the Catholic equivalent, a Knight of Columbus); but by the end of Fordism, the Neoliberal turn, and the decline of classical Political Machines in the 1970s, Freemasonry has gradually lost its vitality and potency until today, in the 21st century, most US Masonic Lodges are probably indistinguishable from their local Elks Lodge or Rotary Club counterparts.

                                Now of course there are some exceptions to this. There has always been a conservative, even reactionary, wing of Freemasonry. The Bavarian Illuminati famously caused a split in Continental freemasonry because many lodges were unwilling to tolerate and co-exist with the revolutionary implications of the illuminated lodges. This dynamic is depicted in an interesting fashion in Alan Moore’s “From Hell”, where in a blink-and-you’ll-miss-it plot point, the principal antagonist of the novel, a British Mason, invokes the threat of ‘Illuminism” to justify his extreme tactics to his reticent superiors.

                                Anyhow, regardless of one’s feelings on the heuristic given above and its relation to real world history and politics, I think that it offers an interesting vein to draw inspiration for the depiction of not only how the Masonic thread can be used in the presentation of the Order of Reason type group but also how it can be used to explain the development and transition of the Order of Reason into the Technocratic Union.
