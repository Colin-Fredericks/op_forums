              In Mummy (the VtM supplement) 1st and 2nd Edition, completely destroying the body simply does an extra 5 levels of damage that must be "healed" before the Mummy can be fully resurrected into it's body again. It's not possible to truly destroy them. Even reducing the body to ash just postpones their fixing it and returning. Unless they take precautions, their lifeless body (when they are "dead", sort of similar to being in Torpor but having the ability to Astral Travel), does rot, decompose, and get eaten by worms or whatever. It doesn't matter, because once they have the spiritual energy to return to their body, they must first heal and fully rebuild it.

              Vampires can drink their blood, it just doesn't do anything for them.

              In Mummy the Resurrection (3rd/Rev), I believe it's still the same, except that vampires not only can drink their blood, but get double the BP for it and it sort of taints them with a semblance of mortality. It's both very addictive and also generally very undesirable.

                      a corax picks its eyes,
                      flies consume its flesh,

                      From a practical standpoint, a mummy is truly immortal. Mummies can shift power between their body and different parts of their soul, and to truly kill one, you have to completely annihilate every aspect of their being. And some of those parts exist on different planes of existence

                      So, that means an enemy seeking to kill one has to be able to (more or less) simultaneously destroy:

                      a) their body, in the living world/Skinlands
                      b) their ka, which exists in the Shadowlands, and which will fight to protect the body
                      c) their ba, which manifests only in the depths of the Underwold (Sunless Sea/Tempest/Dark Kingdoms level)
                      d) any physical record of the mummy's true name, on any plane of existence, along with any memory of that name

                      Anything less than that, and the mummy survives. In practice, that means only certain high level mages can reasonably hope to kill one. . . although even many supernaturals don't know that. There just aren't a lot of mummies to experiment on, and it can take centuries for a mummy to gather enough energy to fully heal itself (and it does have to fully heal, before coming back to life). So many may assume they've successfully killed a mummy, when in fact they'll be back (albeit long after their "killer" is dead)



                      Edit :In your example, the mummy's enemies are just trashing the body - arguably the least important part of the mummy. At some point, though, the ka will manifest to protect the corpse . . . so maybe they'd be able to destroy that, too. Either way, though, that level of damage to the corpse will take a long time (and a lot of energy) to heal. (Mummies warn each other not to tick off werecrocodiles for exactly that reason)

                      But that might be enough, depending on their goals. A destroyed corpse (and ka) will strand the ba soul in the depths of the Underworld for centuries, with little option to strike back. But sooner or later, a mummy will always come back.

                                              Originally posted by Dzyhanas View Post
                                              Long ago in one forum under topic like 'how to kill Mummy completely' someone answered as 'being eaten by Redcap'
                                              "Killing" a mummy is easy - they're basically hedge wizards practicing an antique style of magic (one heavy on making magical artifacts). They have no ability to soak aggravated damage, and heal human slow. Really, a street punk with a Saturday Night Special (i.e. a cheap .22 pistol) would be enough to kill one .

                                              The problem is keeping them dead. A mummy is a lot like a self-aware cancer - if any part of them survives, it can completely replace the rest. Corpses unrot, shredded organs pull themselves back together again, damaged pieces of the soul regrow . . .

                                              That's the great power of the mummies. No matter what you do to them, they'll always, always come back. It may take a couple decades, centuries, or (in one case) millennia . . . but they will be back.


                                              Edit: However, if you really wanted to screw with one, Mind magic (and other ways to tamper with memories) is a good choice. Mummies already have issues with memory - the average one is thousands of years old, and the human brain just isn't designed to hold millennia worth of experiences. Hit 'em with an amnesia spell, and burn their personal libraries to the ground, and you can really mess one up.

                                                          Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
                                                          Did the Mummy books ever address what happens if a Mage turns their physical body into another type of matter? Like, say, stone? Or, indeed, what would happen if they used high level Prime magick to completely discorporate the body into raw Quintessence?
                                                          To truly discorporate (that is, truly destroy) a mummy with Sphere magic, the mage has to use Prime:5, Spirit:5 (neither sphere, alone, has the power to utterly destroy a mummy -- he'll eventually reform/resurrect, even if it takes centuries). That's from both Mage and Mummy:tR. Even then, it's not easy, because the mummy gets any appropriate rolls that someone else might be entitled to, as appropriate (Willpower in some instances; they may have active spells in place to resist "x"; they may have amulets/what-have-you to offer protection; etc.), as well as always can default to rolling his Balance as a defensive pool vs. the mage's magic, in addition to any other defensive rolls allowed (mummies can attempt to counter or at least mitigate True Magic(k) with their Balance as a dice pool/action, particularly Vulgar magic; the Prime:5/Spirit:5 cited is also indicated as being quite Vulgar, so the mage is not only attempting to destroy a being who likely has multiple layers of resistance but said mage also has to worry about Paradox, as well). So no casual feat by any means, and usually something that any mage capable of doing so would likely choose *not* to do due to the difficulties involved.

                                                          As far as transforming the mummy via "x", into a form that isn't "killing" them, as such, but simply "suspending" them: the books don't cover that, as such, but given that a mummy can be kept "indefinitely dead" by keeping their body in a state of constant/persistent harm (the oft-cited "throwing the mummy into an active volcano", which is out of the book itself, wherein the khat is constantly damaged and therefore cannot regenerate/resurrect -- save for when the Ka/outside forces act sufficiently to remove the khat from said situation), transforming the mummy into a statue can be as "permanent" a fix as it would be for almost any other target. However, few mummies are without friends/associates (and often very powerful associates), so that statue better be tucked away somewhere. Similarly, if the mummy was dead and then the mage transformed the body into a statue (to effectively prevent the mummy from returning), remember similarly that the Ka can work towards undoing a great many situations -- even supernatural ones -- that the khat may find itself stuck in; depending on edition, the Ka may also cast spells/use powers just as the mummy normally can when he is whole, and if sufficiently powerful/knowledgeable might be able to undo the transformation on its own (via the appropriate spells/rituals/what-have-you). If nothing else, destroying/pulverizing the statue (tree, alternate animal form, what-have-you) can allow the Ba/Ka to gradually transform/resurrect the mummy as normal, re-constituting the khat back to its original form (again, it might require years for this to occur depending on specific circumstances/details... but then again, mummies have time on their side...).

                                                          *****

                                                          As far as the mummy being eaten, the same general methodologies apply: eventually the digested parts will be excreted, and that excrement will eventually makes its way through the air (due to circumstances, but mostly due to the power of the Ka) and the mummy will resurrect from even the smallest traces of it's original body, in whatever form. Only if a mummy's body is destroyed on the atomic level -- *utterly destroyed*, with nary but a few trace atoms here and there, per M:tR -- will he then be unable to resurrect; even then, he still exists in his pseudo-wraith identity, and unless utterly destroyed similarly on a spiritual level in The Low Umbra, he'll spiritually resurrect again back into his wraith-form. While this for most functional situations makes the character now a "wraith" -- not a Wraith, proper, as the mummy still operates under different metaphysical rules from Wraiths -- he still might come back again if his spirit is drawn back and a simple variant of the New Spell of Life is enacted by sorcerers in the physical world via a new body/merging, a la' M:tR, with the New Amenti.

                                                          If, however, a mummy is consumed by a being who doesn't excrete/have a conventionally "normal" metabolic process, then his khat can be in a functional state of "being damaged", vis-a-vis the volcano citation from the book. As long as the khat (all or at least the commanding majority of it) is in that state, then the mummy can't resurrect; his Ka will have to do what it can to manipulate events to as to free the khat while the Ba works on things in the Underworld. (A number of mummies no doubt take this situation as a "six of one, half-dozen of the other" sort of thing: while they are indefinitely barred from resurrecting, they consequently have considerable time to work on matters in Duat and similar, possibly forging new alliances, dealing with lingering past issues, or garnering even more mystical power/knowledge. Those who don't properly understand mummies' life/death cycles often fail to realize *just how active* mummies really are over the millennia... even when dead, they can often be up to as much as when they are alive....) BTW: there are one or two examples in M:tR of beings who might completely or partially devour a mummy, leaving them in the exampled situation; on the flip, if say a horror-movie zombie were to eat a mummy (>paging Rick Grimes<), the mummy might actually be able to resurrect (eventually) out of the zombie b/c in that case the khat isn't being "constantly harmed" as the zombie has no active metabolism to inflict such harm. Of course, the image of a newly resurrected mummy bursting forth from a zombie's stomach -- possibly still having to fight the zombie that ate it as it happens -- is simultaneously both grotesque and highly comical.

                                                          Hope that all helps!
