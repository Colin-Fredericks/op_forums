                                Originally posted by Whithers View Post
                                The Story Telling system is already a universal generic system.
                                No, it isn't. It's never been made into one. It has been expanded vastly beyond Vampire, but there is no support for the systems for countless things, and there is no version of the system published as a truly generic/universal system with setting and setting supporting mechanics either removed or treated as a toolbox to build with. There is no ST system SRD like has been done with D&D (and all the OGL based games built off it) or PbtA. There's been no generic core like BRP, EABA, GURPS, Tri-Stat, Unisystem, Fudge/Fate, Cypher, Savage Worlds, etc.

                                Could the ST system be join those ranks? Sure. The people in control of it have never bothered though. And Onyx Path seems ready to take that place for people with the Storypath system being done for Trinity 2e and Scion 2e.

                                When the economy suffers, and the more it suffers,...
                                Irrelevant. RPGs have opted for the cycle of editions model for decades rather steadily without significant deviation based on the strength of the economy. D&D has been doing it since the 70s (esp. with the "basic" D&D and AD&D editions coming out in parallel during the 80s). WW is talking about WoD 5e, but that's completely ignoring things like the DA Revised system which was significantly different from any of the current time WoD editions. Fate is on its 3rd Edition and is split into regular and FAE. Shadowrun is on its 5e. GURPS in on 4th. Hero is on 6th.

                                The point is pretty simple. This has continued through good and bad economies.

                                The Story Teller System was meant to create the most credible suspension of disbelief possible.
                                No it wasn't... I mean VtM 1e makes it pretty clear this isn't really important to the inception of the system. One of the most decried aspect of the early ST system (and many systems that came out at the same time) is that they completely ignored ensuring the systems created credible suspension of disbelief and relied on the game users to ignore the rules when the results bucked suspension of disbelief. I've never seen a signal interview of the original ST system team that communicated this, compared to a lot of things that they have expressed as their core design goals; which are mostly about making RPGs that focus on storytelling (hence the name..) and how to create a holistic design aesthetic to inspire that focus.

                                The ST system at its best is still when this is the primary concern.

                                Through the limitations of economy and the determination to reduce support. A dry well in the Sahara quenches no thirst.
                                The limitations of economy has not born out to the CofD to cease being sold, or the only visible public metrics on its performance to have suffered. If anything the CofD has been doing better (though correlation, not causation) since the 20th books became a full line proper, because the CofD was moved to stand more on its own merits (and has a stronger design in 2e for it) once the 20th books were also being made.

                                There was no determination to reduce support to the CofD because of 20th that anyone has ever stated as an official stance of the original WW, CCP era, or new WW.

                                There is no dry well for either the WoD or CofD because both are selling.

                                Since there is no support for the CWoD Meta in the NWoD materials that is untrue.
                                1) What do you call the Translation Guides exactly?

                                2) Even with that, it doesn't matter. If you've invested in the CofD, you haven't invested in the WoD meta. If you wanted the WoD meta, it already existed in the older books (the 20th books have not added significantly to the WoD meta, they have just collected it in less books). The 20th books as written/designed (obviously they could have done something more drastic) can only obsolete the earlier WoD books, they can't obsolete the CofD. If you want to play Vampire: the Requiem, V20 does you no good what so ever.

                                Something which is never tried being dismissed because there is no positive evidence of it working since it was never tried.
                                I think BRP represents plenty of evidence of this having been tried. BRP was designed exactly as you describe (a core universal/generic system, with lots of different settings made with it) and lasted 21 years before it finally got a new edition (and had another edition come out more in the 5-10 range that's common for longer running systems after that). One data point isn't a lot, but we've seen what happens in at least one circumstance. And it was that eventually BRP needed to incorporate 20 years of rules experience into their core system, as well as any marketing concerns.

                                                    -VtM20 only mentioned the Revised-era changes to the clans like Assamite, Gangrel, Malkavians, and Ravnos in the back of the book, buried in the Bloodlines chapter. Which for me, is not an appropriate place to put them.

                                                    -MtA20 makes the most reference to metaplot...but it's all wrapped up in the Future Fates format. Where it offer suggestions for what you could do with metaplot elements: either a. a world where this metaplot point hasn't happened yet b. the metaplot point happened exactly as it did back in the older editions c. the metaplot point happened...but it happened in a slightly different way then it did as written. Which again, offers a lot of freedom to GMs and players on how much/little they want to use the metaplot.

                                                    So yeah. With the way how the corebooks treat them, nothing there says that I have to use the metaplot as written. In fact, they go as far as encouraging the notion that I don't *HAVE* to use them. Which is how it should be.
