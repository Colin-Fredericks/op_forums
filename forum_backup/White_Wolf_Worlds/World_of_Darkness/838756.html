    Damage, Damage, Damage: Looking for a more accurate system/numbers for weapons/armours/ places you hit.

    02-28-2016, 03:45 PM
    So I'm thinking something like...

    Weapons should go off the caliber of the bullet, rather than the class. It bothered me a little comparing the 20 books and seeing some bigger bullets doing less than smaller bullets, in one instance the bullet was switched around for assault rifles (5.56 vs 7.62) and the damage was still the same. Anyone got the knowledge of firearms to make some accurate set of advanced rules? (IE if .22 rounds did 3 damage and a .50 did 12 or 16 or whatnot. )

    Also: hitting specific parts of the body. The way I've been doing it, and to some extent my friends have been doing it, is to increase or decrease the soak difficulties (and in my games the damage difficulties) rather than add extra dice. I think this works a lot better/seems a little less gamey. But, y'know, some GM's aren't that imaginative and often don't reward that kind of stuff properly ("I severed his spine, Surely he should be paraplegic now!" "nope, Having your spine severed is normal to garou, these guys walk around with their kidneys hanging out like it's nothing!"

    I was also trying to figure our a system for silver bullets (among other bullet types); I'd imagine that, especially on larger and faster firing bullets, the entire bullet isn't silver, but rather just the tip. Otherwise it'd be to expensive for the user and too mean to garou players/kind to garou killing players. Ideas so far:
    -The entire bullet is silver, but since silver isn't so hard and heavy a material as normally used, the bullet does less damage
    -The bullet is silver tipped, roll and soak normal damage, but double up on the unsoaked damage with levels of aggravated silver. If no means to soak silver is available, but all the bullet is soaked, add one silver damage.
    -The bullet is silver tipped, Increase the soak difficulty if they don't have silver protection.
    - The same as above, but instead lower the damage difficulty. Or do both.
    -The bullet is part silver. Split the damage pool. Silver and not silver. Not-silver is always soaked first.

    Any thoughts? Also to consider: Incendiary rounds for vampires and primium bullets for... well primium is far to valuable to be used as a bullet. Gold rounds would be the same as the silver, Iron rounds I'm not sure about.

    Apart from this, we could also consider the mechanics of (Insert Bane Here) Melee weapons. If you smack someone really hard with a burning torch, it's just bashing with some aggravated on, same could be said if you used an ogre with an iron club: Some of that damage will be horrible, horrible iron, but a lot of it will also be your insides compacting against your further-insides. Like yeah if you got skewered by a klaive that's all a big silver cut, but if someone shanked you with a combat knife, one edge silvered, then is it all silver?

          1) You can't really do damage based on caliber. Caliber is actually one of the worst ways to judge how damaging a bullet will be. It's only really useful when looking at firearms where everything else is roughly the same. The most important things are mass, and acceleration (because that is what determines force). A .30 caliber round that has more mass, and more acceleration, will have more force (and thus do more damage) than a .45 caliber round that has less mass and moves slower (easily comparable with rifles in the ~.30 cal range vs. .45 pistols). The reason caliber becomes a short hand for "power" is mostly something you see in hand-guns. In a semi-auto pistol, the length of a cartridge is limited by the clip's need to fit inside the grip. This means the amount of space to make the bullet longer (adding mass), or to pack in more gunpowder (adding acceleration) are limited. So a bigger caliber means more mass, and more acceleration, because you're expanding in the one direction you have left (though also generally small rounds per clip for it).

          When you move to rifles, you have a lot more flexibility in terms of the shape of a bullet. Most rifles generally use lower caliber rounds, because they can add mass and acceleration by making longer bullets, and longer cartridges. This means more shots in a clip, as well as better aerodynamics (long and thing vs. short and fat in flight should be pretty obvious).

          2) Changing called shots bonuses to damage/soak difficulty modifiers could be done, but seems annoyingly complex to balance out. Adding damage dice is relatively straight forward for the shooter's player to judge the benefits of the trade in difficulty on their shot, because extra dice has a constant impact on average damage dealt. If I'm taking +1 difficulty for +2 damage dice, it's the same risk/benefit for a damage 4 gun, a damage 8 gun, or a damage 16 gun. If I'm taking a +1 difficulty to shoot, for a -1 difficulty on damage it's a completely different consideration; difficulty modifiers scale with dice-pool size. It's actually more "gamey."

          3) Silver weapons/bullets in the books need to be mostly silver. Silver coatings, or silver "tips," are generally not considered sufficient. I don't see a need to change this. Besides being really expensive, silver isn't actually a bad metal for making bullets and certainly not enough to warrant a change in mechanics. In many respects, its physical properties are actually good for monster hunting in that they're more likely to lodge in a target than pass through.

          I don't see a reason to change this. Iron and gold bullets would be an entirely different issue. Incendiary rounds are overrated since vampires aren't inherently flammable. They should burn a bit, but they're not like hitting something with a Molotov cocktail. Incendiary rounds only really work when shot at something relatively flammable. Primium bullets are magic and thus awesome bullets with special effects; a wizard did it.

          4) In general, on banes, it's better if the majority of the blade/striking part of the weapon is the bane in question. Edging shouldn't be enough to have system based damage.

                  I'm going to stop you right there Heavy Arms, and have to tell you you're wrong. The most important thing in firearms is purpose of the cartridge, which is then followed by energy content of the round. A rifle round is smaller and faster than a pistol round because it cares about one thing, penetration. Rifles have to deal with body armor nowadays, so they are designed to over penetrate the target. This causes less impulse and thus less energy transfer. Pistol rounds are a different matter however, you want them to hit the person and stop inside them, transferring their kinetic energy to the target. They aren't pointed for a reason, as pressure is higher in a point than a flat point so more energy gets transferred, and thus the likelihood of transferring all their to the target. Pistols are all about stopping power, while rifles are about piercing the target.
                  Now normally you'd ask why doesn't a rifle do less damage than a pistol, and this comes down to impulse and energy content of the round. Kinetic energy is E = 1/2mv^2, so a round traveling fast is going to have more energy by a large margin over the round with more mass assuming equal increase. So how does this work? Well it comes down to how you hit the target. If you're shooting a guy in the stomach with a rifle round chances are it won't do much of anything. This is because there's not bones to slow it down and the round travels through unimpeded. Now there are rounds specifically designed to break apart as it moves through a fluid, like human tissues creating a much larger energy transfer and spreading the damage over a large area (7.62mm rounds do this). Now a pistol round will hit in the stomach and have a greater chance of stopping than rifle rounds.
                  This brings me to another point, cartridges type is what determines energy type, specifically grain count and load type. This means that a 9mm round fired from a submachine gun is identical to those from a pistol, assuming they're the same cartridge type (there's a slight difference in muzzle velocity but it's not enough to matter, talking a few percentage points). Now for actual numbers, a .45 ACP which is used in M1911's has a cartridge energy between 400 and 800 J depending on the specific grain and the load mass. Meanwhile the upper range of .308 has around 3500 J. So if the .308 hit a target and transferred 50% of its energy to the target, that's more than twice the energy than the .45ACP, and which means a hell of a lot more damage to the target.

                  So when you say it has to do with caliber size or force of the round, you're very wrong. In addition, the cartridge used in a gun has nothing to do with the length of the round or the output slot. Those are designed around the cartridge used, and not the other way around.

                  Now when you actually analyze silver bullets, you should be inflicting less damage because silver is extremely light way compared to lead that is used (should do 1 less point of damage). We're talking a huge loss of energy content for the round because of a 10% change in density and the exit velocities are nigh identical. Now one should note that silver is slightly better for penetration over lead pure lead rounds, but not enough to make a mechanical difference. But silver is very much so less accurate since its mass is less and thus it can be swayed by forces more easily after it leaves the barrel.

                  As for vampires being flammable, they in fact are according the Pyrokinetic rules in the Sorcerer Revised book which has them at difficulty 4 to ignite, while humans are difficulty 9. In addition, flammable rounds tend to be super illegal, normally cause they use white phosphorous, which will burn through a human body in seconds unless you remove it ASAP (it burns at 2760°C, which is thermite levels of hot).

                          If you really want, the only really functional method is to set a "default" bullet style, and then modifiers for different rounds. So hollow points are worse against armor, but better against flesh. Armor piercing rounds deal well with armor, but have over-penetration issues, etc.

                          The CofD book Armory has a fairly decent listing of options for this that's not too far off from WoD's combat systems to modify in.

                          Recoil (the penalty you'd get with burst and full auto fire) (also, Honestly I think it'd be a nice thing if physical disciplines/magic/transformations/augmentations could have some effect on recoil)
                          The system doesn't do recoil. At all. The burst fire rules could be seen as having some recoil intent, but without any mitigation from stance, bipods/mountings, etc. it doesn't make any sense as such. As well, recoil has a much greater impact on your next shot, not your first shot.

                          If you want recoil, something I've done in the past:

                          Calculate Recoil. If Recoil is equal to or greater than your Strength, gain a cumulative +X difficulty to your next firearms attack for each firearms attack made until you spend a full action not shooting. X is equal to 1 + the difference between Recoil and Strength.

                          For supernatural powers, this is pretty simple. You don't need new powers. Anything that increases Strength helps deal with Recoil, anything that decreases (or negates penalties such as Clashing Boom Boom's Totem bonus) difficulty can mitigate the impact of recoil, and so on.

                          Base Recoil = 2 for damage ratings between 4-6, and 3 for 7+.
                          Penalties:
                          +1 using a one-handed gun one-handed.
                          +2 using a two-handed gun one-handed (if you even allow someone to do this).
                          +1 using poor shooting postures (such as holding a gun sideways, or being forced into bad position because of restraints/etc.).
                          +1 to 3 for burst fire based on level of burst.
                          +1 if a weapon that normally has a stock, has the stock folded or removed.
                          Bonuses:
                          -1 for specialized postures (such as a speed shooter's competition stance, or being prone while using a rifle).
                          -1 to 2 for extra equipment (bipods, stocks on weapons not normally have one included, etc.)

                          Gnosis cost (how many bullets you could take before you'd get a gnosis hit)

                                  The White Wolf games were/are not too concerned with accurately portraying most weapons (or combat), especially firearms, as they often have other thematic goals (even Hunter, which is all about fighting back, decided to "dumb down" antagonist splats rather than revamp combat). A few rulings I have added over the years, and add to games as appropriate, are:

                                  - Limbs, targeted or otherwise, that receive more damage than the character has Strength (after soak) are useless/shredded/destroyed;
                                  - Firearms and recoil are represented by the multiple action method, but are not multiple actions (e.g., a gun with a rate of fire of 3, shot #2 is taken at +1 difficulty, -1 die, shot #3 is +2/-2, etc.), and the modifiers can be "pushed back" or even eliminated by things like proper stance, grip, etc.;
                                  - "Silvered" weapons: if the weapon is less than 100% silver, it only causes 1 point of Aggravated damage (in conjunction with normal damage);
                                  - "Dragon's Breath" rounds: characters must wait a turn between shots for the barrel to cool, unless they want the barrel to melt after 3 shots (and cannot be used in automatic shotguns as they jam).

                                  I'm not familiar with any other incendiary munitions for handheld firearms, as the ones I know are all artillery. Oh, and silver weighs about the same as lead, as mentioned above, but is slightly harder, so silver bullets won't "mushroom" as much but will fragment easier. Not really enough to make a difference, but I thought I'd mention it.
