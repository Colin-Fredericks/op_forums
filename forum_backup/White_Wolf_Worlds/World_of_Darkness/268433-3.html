            The problem is that the DA playable splats all clearly address how to address this. Human social standing generally doesn't matter, and their own political factions are generally not defined solely by the noble/commoner divide. High Clan vampires and low Clan vampires belong to the same primary political organizations against others, rather than dividing themselves along that line. The Silver Fangs have always had to balance the Garou idea of leadership being something that needs to be earned and defended with their position as the Alpha Tribe; if the Silver Fangs tried to act like the Sidhe and make their Houses the primary political units of DA werewolf society, they'd just get killed for it. Mages really don't give a shit about this. The Inquisition makes everyone equal regardless of who they were born to as they now serve the Church. Etc.

            While DA characters might have to play parts in front of normal people, political power in the DA splats are not simply noble > commoner.

            If you make the Sidhe House the primary political units of the game, you're inherently making it Sidhe > titled Commoners > untitled Commoners in a fashion the game does not present as the default.

                I agree with Heavy and re-iterate that what you are doing sounds perfectly fine as long as everyone playing in your game knows about it before or at character creation, as this is NOT the primary axis of play as identified in the core rules or in much of the supplemental material.

                                                Though CtD is the only RPG I still play, I'm completely aware of its problems and incoherences. As a storyteller, along these almost 20 years, I could point out what bothered and/or confused more my players:
                                                1. What's the main conflict of the game? I mean, is it about a dying race living and drowning in a noxious world that infects and deteriorates its own very nature as the years go by? Or is it about people that refuses to lose their innocence so they keep childish for as much as they can? It can't be both.
                                                2. More identification with fairy tales. The main kiths are ok, but as the line has developed, more narrower and specific kiths started to appear, it was a chaos! Nunnehi, menehune, hsien, dark kin, inanimae, chimeras, etc, they all try to explain mythological creatures but actually none generated a trully identification with any fairy tale. Sometimes it's ok to be not very original.
                                                3. Cantrips. Realms are just so limiting, arts are just so incoherent (specially Primal, Legerdemain and Chicanery). And WHY changelings can't cast illusions as Ravnos' Chemistry ou deal with dreams as Wraith's Phantasm? All I see in the Core Rulebook is how Changelings deal in a daily basis with dreams, but they don't have anything (in rules terms) to deal with it actually. On the other hand, the whole story tells about fairies hiding behind illusions (and it is also highlighted in other WoD books), but the truth is that changelings can create just dull illusions.
                                                4. What's the point to put technology = banality and anachronism = glamour? It's not only the Nockers that can be inspired by technology.
                                                5. Chimerical x Real. It must be more simple. Players, in general, get tired of asking what they can or cannot see/do along the game session.

                                                There's another things that bother me and the dozens of players that I played with, but I think these points are constants.

                                                            I always had an issue with Banality in Changeling as the force of human disbelief, and setting it up as the dichotomous opposite of creativity and imagination. It kind of represents a "liberal arts" understanding of science and mathematics to suggest that these disciplines somehow undermine any sense of wonder and joy with which we view the universe. This is Measuring the Marigolds mentality, and comes across as hostile to those that find joy in biology or love the explanatory power of mathematics. We see much the same thing in early editions of Mage in which the bad guys are the force behind the scientific paradigm that gave us indoor plumbing, antibiotics and Taco Bell.

                                                            My objection to a fundamental premise of the game suggests that my solution for "fixing" it involves scrapping the entire thing and building up from scratch. This problem is not present in Changeling; the Lost, by the way, which is why I tend to prefer that game to Dreaming.
