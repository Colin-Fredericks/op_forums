            Two house rules I suggested on RPG.net for a book I've never even read (though reading V5 helped because I know the basic math of the system):

            1) Edge rolls should be difficulty 1 and scale with your successes; with the caveat that the ST can cap the maximum level you can hit based on the situation. So if you're rolling for Fleet, 1 success gets you a beat up clunker, 2 successes a decent common vehicle or an OK specialist vehicle, etc. Something I thought of between then and now, is you can offer success-at-cost by reducing a Perk on a fail instead of getting nothing.

            2) Edge rolls should happen when dramatically appropriate based on your character's personal concept of the Edge rather than things like defaulting to the start of scenes to see if you have them for the scene (which as noted on RPG.net means back-to-back scenes can get weird since knowledge can technically disappear between scenes the way Library is written). If your character has a high-tech experimental metallurgy based silver sword that's supposed to be way more functional in combat than normal silver in their personal Arsenal, it makes way more sense to roll after a fight to see if your sword broke than it does to roll to see if you could to bring it in the first place. If they rely on someone else for their specialist gear, rolling ahead of time is more appropriate.

Just as the whole reading of the cells as rightwing antiathoritarians is not the intent of the writers. If we focus on the games where protagonists are the locals and show what's there worth fighting for and how much they have to be proud of their homes it stops being as awkward and gain substance. Sure, popularity of the Narcos shows must be inspirational for folks, but if we focus on what's cool in Mexico and how badass the Mexicans can be I'm pretty sure it would make for a good game, one that could even handle the whole tendency of oWoD to only ever write about Americas and utterly mess up the rest of the world.

These folks have been living in this World for a long time, and there must be people in the know. I'd focus on just one of the states - one near US is not a bad choice due to cultural blending, and the problem "how do we Hunt someone who just ran into USA" sounds like loads of fun - its history, culture and architecture. The mannequin vampire is a good start of directly tying monsters into places, but not all places need to be filled with monsters.

                                                    The problem with setting the difficulty in the new system is that it also puts a huge amount of stress on auto-succeeding and/or taking half. The risks of failure/complicated rolls generally outweigh the potential bonus successes from critical hits. If you set the difficulty to what a lot of us would probably want to see, and keep the stress on not rolling, you lose any reason to roll unless the ST makes you roll; which runs the risk of causing tension between ST and player because it makes it feel like calling for rolls as trying to bring up chances for the dice to screw the player rather than exciting unknown outcomes.

                                                    Decreasing the difficulties only really pays off if you also have a more roll happy play style contrary to the advice in the books. V5 and H5 both putting the difficulty fairly high means taking half isn't enough, and the system, not the ST, is making things dangerous.
