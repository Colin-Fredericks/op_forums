            It does, however, point out one of the potential differences between what the Technocracy does and what everyone else does: the Technocracy eventually gets its innovations into the hands of ordinary citizens. That is, after sufficient “terranorming” (if I'm understanding the term right), things that started out as Inventions that only Enlightened Scientists could use can eventually end up as common, everyday fixtures in everyone's household. While theoretically the Traditions could replicate this feat, in practice they don't: if you want to benefit from faith healing, you have to rely on the generosity of a faith healer.

            To the extent that the Traditions care about the common man, it's largely in reaction to the Technocracy's very real inroads in that regard. But absent that reaction, Tradition goals tend to focus more on empowering the mage, with the Sleepers benefitting from a sort of “trickle down effect”: if mages are empowered, then the communities that they favor will benefit from that: if the master does well, so will his servants. In contrast, the Technocracy tends to try to empower the Masses directly (or at least they did, until they went off the rails).

            And while the Technocracy isn't responsible for any technology prior to their appearance in the mid-19th century, they weren't the first Enlightened Scientists in the setting. Heck, even the Conventions of the original Order of Reason didn't pop up out of nowhere in the 1300s: the likes of the Artificers were around long before the Craftmasons recruited them to their cause. You can't credit indoor plumbing to the Technocracy or even to the Order or Reason; but it may well have been something that an Artificer came up with long ago. And in China, the history of the Technocrats goes back thousands of years.

            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                Also worth noting in regards to the science vs. magic split is that, before they founded the Order of Reason, the Craftsmasons were members of the Order of Hermes’ House ex Miscellanea (one of the reasons Clan Tremere always has influence over various Mason organizations in my Vampire-exclusive games... ever wonder why they do so many blood drives?).

                The Craftsmasons decided they wanted to democratize magic by pushing a paradigm that allowed for repeatable results independent of the user. During the Sorcerer’s Crusade period c. 1450 many of their devices were subject to Paradox, but they took their lumps from it and kept pushing little by little to get technology anyone could use into the collective beliefs of humanity.

                Also worth noting is that the Craftsmasons were purged when the Order of Reason was reformed by the same sort of elitists who believed they had a divine right to rule... only now they would rule over the masses via their mastery of technology.

                The Technocrats cut out the soul of the Order (“make the common man the equal of mages”) in the name of power (“the common man is too ignorant to rule himself, we must use our technology to control him for his own good”).

                I find the Technocracy can best be explained by a C. S. Lewis quote;

                “Of all tyrannies, a tyranny sincerely exercised for the good of its victims may be the most oppressive. It would be better to live under robber barons than under omnipotent moral busybodies. The robber baron's cruelty may sometimes sleep, his cupidity may at some point be satiated; but those who torment us for our own good will torment us without end for they do so with the approval of their own conscience.

                “They may be more likely to go to Heaven yet at the same time likelier to make a Hell of earth. This very kindness stings with intolerable insult. To be "cured" against one's will and cured of states which we may not regard as disease is to be put on a level of those who have not yet reached the age of reason or those who never will; to be classed with infants, imbeciles, and domestic animals.”

                The Technocracy essentially became the very thing the Order of Reason was started to oppose (the elitism of the mages against the common man) while the younger generations of the Traditions have started to come around to the Craftsmasons line of reasoning... that the common man can be elevated by sharing the wonder of magick with them.

                History is cyclical for a reason.

                                                Guess why bows went out of style? Because to properly teach an archer, you had to “start with his grandfather”. Archery required a lot of physical conditioning and training. When black powder weapons came on the scene, they didn't really have any more range, lethality, or rate of fire than longbows had. What they did have was that anyone could use them with minimal training.

                                                Even in the mystical paradigms where prayers work for Sleepers, it isn't that just any prayer will work for anyone; that remains the purview of the Awakened. For a Sleeper to be able to work miracles without the intervention of one of the Awakened, the Sleeper needs to be initiated into a religious order that will teach them how to properly pray (in game terms, the Sleeper needs to jump through the hoops required to become a Sorcerer, and then learn appropriate Paths). This isn't as hard to do as it is to get someone to Awaken; but it's not trivial either.

                                                The main reason why the Technocracy has been so successful has been a one-two punch of Devices and Terranorming. The first part is that the Technocracy can mass-produce Devices, whereas every Talisman that a mage makes is an intensely personal affair (as reflected in the requirement to invest dots of Willpower into the Talisman, plus personal Quintessence into the crafting process). This is true even of the Sons of Ether and Virtual Adepts. (Personal aside: I suspect that the secret to this mass-production lies in Primal Utility, making it something of a Syndicate trade secret.) The second part is that if the Device produces an Adjustment (i.e., a Coincidental Effect), it can be used by anyone with minimal training, as opposed to Devices that generate Vulgar Effects which can only be used by Extraordinary Citizens or Enlightened Sciences. By engaging in Terranorming, the Technocracy can greatly increase the selection of Devices that can be used by anyone.

                                                The Traditions can do the Terranorming thing, too; though it's possible that the Technocracy can take it a step further than the Traditions can here too, and with sufficiently advanced Terranorming techniques moved some Devices from Coincidental to downright mundane, where they're not even Wonders anymore (meaning that Sleepers can make them as well as use them) — though that's just speculation on my part. But as I said before, the Traditions can't mass-produce Talismans.

                                                And they can't mass-produce sorcerers, either. If there's a way to do that, the secret to doing so is more likely to be found in the Technocracy's paradigm than the Traditions': the Technocrats can mass-produce Principiae, too; the Traditions cannot mass-produce Grimoires.

                                                And while I've been saying a lot about what the Traditions can't do, a case could be made that they can't because they haven't bothered to try: mass-production of Wonders isn't something that interests the Traditions.

So your point that the Technocracy is bad because it is somehow holding back some kind of universal panacea, but the Traditions wouldn't still fails. So we're back to the fact that they didn't help babies.

                                                        One of the tensions in the game is that despite the fact that PCs and NPCs in Mage can do all these wonderful things, that essentially nothing that can be noticed by ordinary people seems to have changed from our own world. That isn't unique to the World of Darkness. It's found in most urban fantasy settings and mainstream superhero universes. It's just a part of certain genres. Because if you did allow major departures in the game world, you don't have the World of Darkness or a "world outside your door", but some kind of obvious fantasy setting. Any historical deviations as a result of mages, vampires, and the like would have to be unnoticed or of trivial importance. Instead it becomes a trick of trying to figure out how real world history happened in the game because of all these supernatural forces out there.
