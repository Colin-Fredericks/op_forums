                                                    I finally managed to finish rereading the Wonder Creation rules. Even managed to finally figure out some old doubts like what's the difference between a living Periapt and a Soulflower.

                                                    For our exercise, what matters:

                                                    1. Wonder creation breaks the process into 4 phases with distinct rules: Foundation, Energy, Purification and Quickening.

                                                    2. Difficulty follows normal rules, actually, so we start from 3-5 + Sphere and get modifiers that can sum up to ±3.

                                                    3. It does give greater control over modifiers, though. Purification in particular gives you any opportunity to add a unique-to-Wonders up to -3 Dif modifier, making minimum difficulty way easier to get. It also increase the bonus and penalty from Resonance.

                                                    4. More importantly, by breaking the process into phases, it eases success accumulation. This is big. Each phase isolates stakes, making for more refined risk management and prep strategies while keeping individual rituals within reasonable targets.

                                                    I think we can use all that in some manner. And the Difficulty part we can tackle by making the Purification-analogue phase count outside the usual limits. So you can only have up to -3 Diff from usual sources, but this phase gives extra -3 that can bring you down to -6.

                                                    Fast break down:

                                                    1. Foundation uses Ability rolls to build the thing that will be the Wonder. Use whatever Craft system you like.

                                                    2. Energy requires 5×level Quintessence plus a Prime effect to lock it in the Foundation. Level of Prime (and so Diff) depends on source. Successes = 3×level.

                                                    3. Purification works as 0 to 3 independent Prime 2 rituals. Complexity scale exponentially with number of rituals, but that's fluff. Target successes scale with Foundation complexity and how messy the Resonance is, but no examples are given.

                                                    4. Quickening requires a ritual to infuse the Effects. Use highest Sphere to set Diff, target successes = 3×level.

                                                        Now bear in mind that for Spheres 6–9, Sphere+3-to-5 is difficulty 9-to-15; and even that 9 assumes the extremely rare case of an Archsphere being used to work a Coincidental Effect. Maxing out the difficulty reductions, that's Difficulty 6–12.

                                                        With the rule that 1s cancel successes, that's a best case scenario of 4 successes per ten dice, which very quickly drops to one success per ten dice; and then you start having to overcome a Threshold, needing an extra one to three hits to get one success. If you don't optimize for lowest possible difficulty, that Threshold can climb to 6 or even 9 additional hits per success, in a range where the total number of hits you're likely to get is maybe one per ten dice rolled, if you're _lucky._ And with a cap on the number of attempts you can make, the upper levels become exercises in frustration even for mages with maxed out Arete.

                                                        That's why I suggested recalibrating Archspells to trade off more successes required for rolling at a lower difficulty: so that working Archmagick doesn't feel quite so much like a Sisyphean task.

                                                        (By the way: a reasonable alternative to the Threshold rule would be to stop at difficulty 9 but double the number of successes needed for each step after that. That still means that what would nominally be 18 to 30 successes balloons to 18, 42, 96, 216, and 480 successes, if we don't decide to tone it down.)
