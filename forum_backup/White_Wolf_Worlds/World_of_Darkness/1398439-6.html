        Originally posted by Sergeant Brother View Post
        Back to the topic of Werewolf. I think it was fine for W:tA to diverge from both Hollywood and traditional werewolf myth, it basically had to in order to make a functional RPG and werewolf society. I problem instead was that they made it so that werewolves kill vampires on sight and that a starting werewolf can kill like 10 elder vampires at once. Both the intense hatred and extreme power differential made vampire and werewolf crossovers almost impossible and in fact made crossovers with most other WoD creatures really hard. Both of those things could have been moderated and not reduced W:tA playability at all.
        The cool thing is, it was indeed possible to do this. Chronicles did it, Forsaken werewolves are still extremely strong in their own right, but they're no longer an instawin against vampires, and they did this by giving a limit on how long you can spend in Gauru (Crinos) form and also not giving werewolves a whole bunch of free aggravated damage. A Forsaken werewolf, if he fails to kill the vampire in Gauru form, the tables can turn quite quickly because all his tankiness has just disappeared.

        Giving WtA werewolves a massive power advantage over VtM vampires and then practically making it canon literally did nothing for either game but cause a huge amount of cross-splat mudflinging and stupid arguments about power levels of supernaturals who were literally not internally balanced against one another.

                                                    I’m not sure it was the worst idea ever, but the original 1st edition premises for each game in their formative years never really meshed well.

                                                    Vampire: The Masquerade’s ‘personal horror’ motif was largely based on the existential horror of being an eternal being in miserable world of darkness that gave no spiritual release - it was all there ever was for the characters to ever experience. So, when Werewolf, Mage, Wraith and Changeling all came along to detail a colorful and varied spiritual multiverse to play in, it sort of underscores the horror of the original game. Likewise, Mage: The Ascension’s premise of a fluid reality - especially one that is nominally dominated by a cold, rational Technocracy, doesn’t really support any notion that vampires are running the world or that any other supernatural critters would be running around influencing events. Wraith doesn’t really take place in the same setting, being mostly set in the Shadowlands, and Changeling is so tonally different that again it just seems to undermine what went before. In short, the Classic World of Darkness doesn’t make sense.

                                                    All this comes with some caveats. The first being, as a long time fan, that obviously I like all these games even though they don’t seem to mesh well. While the NWoD/CoD games do mesh a lot better, its always the original games I seem to gravitate towards, largely because they seem to have more thematic brio. Secondly, the fact that they don’t fully make sense actually fits with an overarching theme of 90s postmodernism. It's the lack of perfection that, in some ways, actually gives these games brio and makes them more raw and resonant of the times they were written in.
