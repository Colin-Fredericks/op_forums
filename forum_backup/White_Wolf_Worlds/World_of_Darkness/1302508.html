    Attribute Specializations re-done; Specialties and Frailties

    04-04-2019, 06:41 AM
    I'm not a fan of the default system for placing a Spec in an Attribute. The specs are either far too broad for such a powerful bonus, or are so vague that you're not sure what sort of rolls they'd actually apply to.

    The system works like this, as far as I can understand. You reach 4 in Dex, you choose Graceful as your Spec, and when you're trying to do some Graceful Dancing, you get 2 successes from 10s.

    Aside from these bonuses being too broad (Graceful attacks? Graceful dodging? Graceful leaps and bounds?) or perhaps too narrow (the ST essentially doesn't let the bonus apply to anything that matters, because the idea of 'graceful attacks' scares them mechanically), and often too vague (What roll is Unforgettable Looks actually applying to? The roll that happens 50 years later when a guy's grandson asks him who the most beautiful woman he ever met was?), these bonuses also step on the toes of Abilities to a certain extent. Rolling 10s and getting 2 successes works very well for Abilities, because the Specs can be specific and work well in this sense. If your Spec is in Climbing, it's obvious when it applies. Same to Hacking, Lockpicking, Pottery, Peaceful Way Do, Parrying, High Umbral Etiquette, Rifles... and these two systems sort of... overlap a bit. Since the latter is very well executed and the former is a mess, here's my solution.

    - If you have 4 or 5 in an Attribute you get a Specialization that reflects the flavour of your Attribute. Your Strength might be "surprising" (reflecting being a small character who doesn't look strong), and your Charisma might be "flirty" or "friendly" (reflecting what sort of impression you give people through your actions, and what you're naturally good at). The recommended Specs in M20 usually work well for these. Your Intelligence could be analytical, your Dexterity could be graceful, your Stamina could be tireless...

    When you do a roll where your Spec applies (say you try and seduce someone, Charisma + Seduction, with Flirty as your Spec in Charisma), you get one bonus die.

    This adds yet more flavour with many Attributes. Your Appearance 4/5 could be photogenic (+1 die when being photographed or modelling), cute (+1 when inspiring pity or affection), sexy (+1 to seduction and flirting naturally), intimidating (+1 to rolls where you use your looks to intimidate).

    In some cases these bonuses even apply to other Attributes being rolled. Someone with Charisma 4 (Flirty) and Appearance 4 (Sexy) would get +2 dice to their Charisma + Seduction roll, for example.

    - If you have 1 in an Attribute you get a Frailty which reflects the flavour of your low stat. Your Stamina 1 might be Fatigued, Sickly or Frail. When you make a roll where these frailties apply, you lose a die. So if you are trying to continue running, or performing a ritual, and you're tired, your effective Stamina would become 0. If you are Sickly and you're trying to soak damage from an infection or illness, your effective Stamina is 0. If you are Frail and you're trying to soak damage from a bat to the ribs, your effective Stamina is 0. The idea is to play up how bad having 1 in a stat is. These traits are also roleplayed and described. The sickly Mage is constantly sniffling, has bad hayfever, and gets the flu every other year. The frail Mage complains of aches and bruises. The fatigued Mage complains about the strain of climbing staircases and walking (or, gods forbid, jogging!) any sizable distance.

    Charisma 1 could be Annoying or Boring, Manipulation 1 could be Obvious or Unconvincing, Appearance 1 could be Unhygienic or Bad Teeth or Bad Skin, etc. etc.

    - Attributes of 6+ get incredible benefits as described in the Legendary Attribute merit. These can give automatic successes in certain types of rolls (like trying to convince someone to party and have fun against their better nature with Charisma, or trying to sell an outrageous lie with Manipulation).

    - Attributes of 0 (usually only possible with Appearance) are reflected by a really terrible or crippling condition. With any Mental stat this means a catatonic state. With Charisma or Manipulation, a serious inability to communicate normally. With the physical Attributes, extreme impairment, sickness and immobility. With Appearance, something akin to the Nosferatu curse. These simply mean automatic failure in certain rolls.

      I'm not sure I consider the double 10s that powerful of a bonus in the end.

      To the general concerns of vagueness though, I actually prefer the assumption that Attribute Specialties apply. Ask players when they pick one, "what are two or three situations where this doesn't apply?" If they can't articulate even that many? Their interpretation of what it means is too broad. If they easily get a few and more start coming up (that aren't just variations on a theme, 'some Dexterity + Athletics rolls' is one, not one for each possible meaning of that dice-pool that doesn't apply) it's probably too narrow.

      I find this to be generally a satisfactory solution because it's really just giving players and STs a formal framework to reach a mutual understanding for fair rulings in play.

      I actually feel quite the opposite on the mechanical side of things though. I much prefer Abilities to get into dice-bonuses and crossover applications. I don't think we need more blurring between the social Attributes, we certainly don't need Dexterity getting more powerful, and it's far more common in my experience for players to feel bad when as an example an Engimas specialty in "Ciphers" and a Computer specialty in "Hacking," don't combine if you're hacking past someone that uses ciphers to disguise their passwords.

      The lack of range makes further penalizing Attributes at 1 questionable. With 0 (which RAW already represents a hugely crippling state), leaving 1 to cover all levels of 'functional but below average,' why do we need more of a penalty here?

      If anything, Frailties should be more free floating. It's way more fun - even if it infringes on Merits and Flaws a bit - to keep something like Frailties open to any level of the Attribute. My Manipulation 5 character might be both very Logical and uses strongly reasoned rhetoric to show people the best way to do things, and Cold where that sort of lack of emotional appeal can be off-putting to many people. Keeping Attributes as double 10s, also means that you can apply Specialties and Frailties to the same roll more easily if a situation is a mixed bag.

      I'm not really sure why we need 6+ to add additional benefits. Mages, generally, only get 6+ via magic (directly or not, and Awakened or not), and if feels like those bonuses should either be part of the magic in question to give it more flavor than just higher ratings (esp. as a reward for big rolls), or at least be super standardized. Having to come up with a custom bonus every time someone pumps up with Life 3 or Mind 1 is a pain. Vampire and Werewolf do just fine without this and starting characters can easily temporarily boost to such levels all the time.
