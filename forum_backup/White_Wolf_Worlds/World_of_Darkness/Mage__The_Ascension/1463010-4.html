        How Hitler justified it to himself or his people isn't really the issue. Germany was one of many within the military pact. Most of them wanted Japan in because they wanted Japan to help them box in Russia (justifiably, a lot of them ended up Soviet states after WWII) and friends to help fight the communists before the US got in on that was enough for them to put aside any prejudices around Asian peoples and join in with the original three Axis powers. You also have Thailand that joined the Axis (yeah, that's a detail that gets skipped a lot) because Japan wanted to be able to attack British forces in Myanmar/Burma and Thailand was given a choice of invasion or joining up so the Japanese military could move through their land without impediment. It's not like the Thai government at the time liked European colonial powers as neighbors (Japan promised to help Thailand reclaim French colonial territories), and the Japanese were keeping the Chinese from expanding too.

        The point is, as you noted ideology falls to the side of practicality when it comes to these sorts of alliances. Leaders will compromise to defeat external enemies as long as they can sell it well enough to their followers.

They're less heavy handed because they lack the pure magical power of their predecessors, but their organizational authority is still rather powerful.

                                    Originally posted by tasti man LH View Post
                                    ...which even pre-Avatar Storm, I have to disagree with, considering a lot of the Traditions don't strike me as the type to abide by such a hierarchy.
                                    But they all agree to abide by the hierarchy imposed by the Council. Their looser internal politics don't matter to agreeing to follow the larger organization's rules.

                                    It's like being a member of the EU. The EU doesn't eliminate all the sovereignty of its member nations, but being an EU member nation comes with a bunch of rules you need to follow for the EU to grant the benefits it offers. The EU, for example, doesn't stop your country from being a constitutional monarchy, a liberal democracy, or (not that any of them actually are) a purely socialist country. But regardless of how a country administers itself, the EU requires certain standards in outcomes.

                                    As to your three examples

                                    Half of the potential Dreamspeakers refused to join the Tradition at its founding because they hated the idea (there's a reason there's a lot of Crafts that would fit in with them) and the lack of respect for the diversity of culture outside of Europe.

                                    The Verbena are absolutely a hierarchy friendly organization. They're more local community focused than many of the others, but social status within their chosen communities is incredibly important to them.

                                    The Virtual Adepts are also obsessed with hierarchy, even if through the lens of subjective communally assign merit rather than other forms. As well, the VAs are the most recent Tradition, that have surviving members that are defectors from the Technocracy. They understand the strength of organization even if they feel the Union's gone too far.

                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                    I think the hierarchy exists mostly in theory anyway. A Chantry exists mostly as an island to itself. A Euthanatos, Verbena, Celestial Choirster, and Hermetic may have their own place but none of them are directly answerable to anyone else.
                                    Um... no?

                                    The Council has had an entire Chantry executed before. Chantries are absolutely not islands, even if the Council doesn't have the practical ability to nanny state them (one of the reasons why the Council's actions tended to be extreme is because it can take so long for them to consider bad actors within a Chantry that said mage can easily have gone from a questionable killing to throwing in with the Nephandi or guilt-exploding into a Marauder before they act).

                                    Like my EU example above, the Council does not dictate how a Chantry organizes itself, but does dictate outcomes. A Chantry's members are answerable to whatever form of leadership they establish for themselves, and that leadership answers to the Council if the rights or duties of any members aren't respected.

                                                            "Is the Disparate Alliance interesting?" is ultimately subjective. I find them interesting, clearly others don't. The big thing for me is that I find the WoD's default to having two main warring factions with some side minor factions for flavor rather boring. Adding factions, even if in this case just one, lets you tell more varied stories.

                                                            "Can the setting?" I honestly don't know what this means exactly. Can you run a Disparate Alliance game where all the PCs are members of it? Then sure. I don't buy the idea that they need something like Guide to the Technocracy here. GttTech, like GttSabbat, had to work hard to make factions that were largely defined by being violently antagonistic to the default PCs and make them compelling as protagonists. The Disparate Alliance is taking groups that were already compelling protagonists - even if limited in what sort of stories you could tell with them - and having them work together instead of staying separate. It's an entirely different development lift.

                                                            And the Disparates are supposed to be a tenuous organization that might crash and burn before it gets anything significant done. M20 core already both acknowledges that and puts it forward as one of the main reasons to play them over the others: taking a long shot at trying something different in the hopes of getting better results.
