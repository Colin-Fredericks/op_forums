    Hmm. How's this work for a frame? I think it's all defensible given the cosmology information we have, though obviously others may differ.

    Think of the various worlds as colors of light. They're stacked/intermingled, but the Gauntlet separates them out into three Umbrae and one material realm.

    The Gauntlet ensures that the material world - red light - is separated from the other colors.

    The Horizon is where the light unites (or, going the other way, separates). Beyond it, it's all white light, for the purpose of this metaphor.

    (Umbral stargazers see the Umbral heavens. Possibly three different flavors of them, in fact - the Aetherial Realms vs. the Shard Realms, for instance.)

    I think that Tegmark lensing wasn't necessary to explain why Sleepers don't see umbral events, because - using the metaphor - material reality only perceives material space, the space illuminated by red light. Material probes travel through red-space, and the Anthropic Horizon reflects spaces where the Gauntlet has been pushed outwards, splitting the white light.

    That is, that the Anthropic Horizon doesn't create more red light/material space, but creates a larger volume of space where the material and spiritual are separated.

    If you were in the Anthropic Horizon, your telescope would still see only material reality, because there's a Gauntlet. You can also reach this place physically, which is indeed how the various space probes got there. Correspondence could probably teleport you there.

    If you had crossed the Horizon with DimSci, your telescope would see everything, and if you turned it back towards Earth, you'd see both material Earth and the three Umbral Earths, stacked on top of eachother, because you're in a place where the colors of light aren't differentiated.

    As you returned back into the inner solar system and passed back through the Horizon, you'd be sorted into one of the various otherworlds, and your observation of Earth would be limited accordingly.

    Does that make sense?

                                                        Conventional Space and Realspace are the same thing. It's not the presence of absence of the Gauntlet that matters; it's the presence or absence of Umbrood and, for lack of a better term, Umbral physics. In Conventional Space, you don't have to worry about Void Adaptation, and you do have to worry about Vulgar Magick. On the other side of the Gauntlet, or on the other side of the Spatial Horizon (which amounts to the same thing), you do have to worry about Void Adaptation, and you generally don't have to worry about Paradox. As well, you can't just fly past the Spatial Horizon: it's a barrier to travel that either requires Spirit/Dimensional Science or the full weight of the Consensus backing you up to cross (and the latter isn't so much a case of crossing it as it is pushing it back, which really shouldn't be easy to do). The biggest difference between the Gauntlet and the Spatial Horizon in this regard is that crossing the Gauntlet is described as “Stepping Sideways”, where your three-dimensional position doesn't change but you move to a parallel layer of reality; crossing the Spatial Horizon is more a matter of “stepping forward”: you don't shift to another layer, but instead move outward from the Sun across the literal edge of space into the Void that lies beyond it. It's the fact that you have to leave regular space when you cross the Horizon that is why you can't use Correspondence to cross it.

                                                        The biggest drawback of saying that normal space extends indefinitely is that it lets the Void Engineers set up bases on deep space that would have no problem coping with things like the Avatar Storm; their only problems would involve maintaining supplies and keeping their technology running while travel to and from Earth is hazardous (which can be a challenge, but is nothing compared to the near certainty of Void Adaptation mere months after being cut off from Earth). And even that's not much of an issue if you have some teleporters in place: without the Spatial Horizon acting as a barrier to travel, there's nothing preventing them from just building a Stargate-like network to keep their far-flung colonies connected without risking any of the dangers of the Umbra.
