                    Chaos Magick (the style that inspired Mage itself, that's an accepted Practice in M20) states that pop-culture symbols are ways to power as potent or more than age old symbols. As long as it's meaningful to you, you can use it. That's why you see real life chaos mages summoning their spells by dressing as superheros or combining superhero symbols into their costumes. Magical girl props can be used as symbols too, and while the underlying principles of the magic hasn't anything to do with love, you're supposed to "embody" the character - hence the concepts that go with it do matter.

                    Now, I understand why some would think that to play with a magical girl breaks the mood they want for their games, and thus would ban it, however if your group doesn't want to murder you just for suggesting to play such cute concept: I would suggest to make an Extatic. Why?, because Love it's one of the Sacred Passions - it's an old and revered Instrument/Practice for the Tradition. Friendship can be understood like that too. After you've declared these as the sacred powers you commune with to break free of reality, props can be justified as a personal choice based on the story of the character - these symbols have power because they are meaningful to you, it doesn't matter if they don't mean a whole lot for other people, or so Chaos Magick says.

                    For instance, maybe she awakened as a child when her and a friend were playing as magical girls. Maybe her friend was attacked by a supernatural monster, she casted a spell in total desperation and it actually worked - after that se was approached by the Tradition and nowdays she uses props as Instrument because they make her remember her powerful friendship that managed to break the walls of posibility (they're a tool, a symbol. Not of some dusty old credo but of a feeling that's, literally, atemporal. Sure, they're silly, but that also hepls -> breaking the somber 'mood' of WoD it's exactly what you want to bring forth another reality).
                    To put it into Focus (as an example): If Everything is an Illusion, Chaos Magick and Art of Desire could be your Practices.Use the props as a Chaos Magick way of summoning the feelings that are all that the Art of Desire needs to 'transform' your ordinary persona and do things that are impossible. You have subtle ways of doing things (like to inspire feelings that positively influence the results of actions) for everyday stuff and more vulgar ones (like blasting stuff) for when shit hits the fan.
                    ​
                    That's already more elaborate and grounded than some paradigms I saw in Mage's sample characters.

                        I can understand a Storyteller not allowing a magical girl into a Mage game, for the sake of tone. That's a valid choice a group can make, and I won't tell someone they're doing it wrong.

                        That said, the magical girl is also not an invalid way of approaching Mage. No more so than playing a wizard or a martial artist or a cyberpunk hacker. Mage: The Ascension, more than any other game, is quite flexible when it comes to theme and tone. It, more than any other game in the World of Darkness (Old or New) aside from Changeling: The Dreaming, can diverge from the bleak, morally complicated nature of the setting.

                        A magical girl in Mage doesn't necessarily violate the principle ideas of the World of Darkness. It's merely a counterpoint to it. Indeed, a character that stands for positivity and light can serve as invaluable contrast, and make the conflicts within the WoD more meaningful. A mage that patterns herself as a magical girl is not immune to the titular Darkness - some of the most lauded magical girl shows in the genre are deeply deconstructive and tackle intense themes. This makes a magical girl's goals - promoting Love, Peace, Friendship, and Hope - into dramatic stakes.

                        It's for this same reason that Princess: The Hopeful has merit over in Chronicles of Darkness, despite seeming to not fit the setting. A magical girl mage must fight for her ideals. She must struggle for the better, brighter world she desires to create. Far from the unfitting element thrown in, the magical girl in many ways epitomizes what Mage: The Ascension is all about: Tackling insurmountable odds to make your vision of Reality the truth.

                                        Pretty nerdy question, but I wanted to have a character that possessed most of the conventions and trappings of the Japanese Magical Girl genre, which Tradition, Convention or Disparate do y'all think would best fit.

                                        For those not unfamiliar with the genre, typical Magical Girl themes and tropes are;
                                        - Main character is almost always a young girl
                                        - Only being able to do magic after going into colorful transformation sequence that gives the main character a sweet outfit
                                        - Having knowledgeable familiar/guide that usually takes the form of a cute animal or pushie
                                        - Main character's powers come from love, hope, kindness, friendship, etc.
                                        - Instruments usually take the form of staffs, wands, or medieval weapons
                                        - Often fighting creatures made out of despair and sadness
                                        - Magical abilities are pretty diverse but I think use of Forces and Prime spheres are almost universal in the genre
                                        - Calling out attacks in an elaborate and dramatic fashion is also common
                                        What kind of magical girl do you want? Are we talking about classical magical girls like Sailor Moon and Card Captor Sakura, or are we talking a more gritty deconstructionist version like Madoka Magica where the magic companion is actually the villain?
