    Paradox Only Around Sleepers

    09-20-2020, 07:38 PM
    In Awakening 2e, Paradox is no longer a thing that automatically happens except around Sleepers. A mage who's in an abandoned warehouse can toss fireballs to their heart's content. A mage can also voluntarily accept Paradox to make their spells more potent.

    I've long enjoyed MtAs's setting and have only played in one MtAs game. I'm toying with the idea of GMing one and adopting this setting hack. When I played my mage PC in that game, I was largely disappointed at how costly magick felt to perform. There was this disclaimer...

    Beyond all of those factors, you also have several core themes of Mage: courage, imagination, hope, struggle, sacrifice, progress, consequences, and interconnectedness. None of those themes would be supported--and most, if not all, of them would be undercut--if magick were a quick and easy thing that every hero could just DO. The theme of Paradox enhances the other elements of Mage. If that doesn’t work for you, then you’re probably playing the wrong game.
    ...but in my experience, it mostly resulted in cool things I wanted my PC to do having prohibitive-feeling costs attached, so I just didn't do them. The game also didn't didn't use any chantries/sanctums/reality zones/mythic threads/excursions to the Umbra/etc., so that might have biased my thinking--there was basically no way to avoid Paradox. And when the setting's goal is to keep vulgar magick rare, Paradox succeeds, since players will usually go out of their way to avoid paying costs they don't have to pay. I'm just not sure what it adds to say no (free) vulgar magick around other mages and night-folk. Paradox around Sleepers ensures mages can't openly rule the world.

    So what would Ascension's setting look like if this was the case? How would games be different?

    Blood and Bourbon, my New Orleans-based Vampire chronicle.

              In Mage Made Easy, Phil 'Satyros' Brucato suggests subtle/uncanny/extreme as ways to see magick. Ignore witnesses when considering the effect. The difficulties are highest sphere+3, highest sphere+4 and highest sphere+5, respectively. Subtle is going to work if not used too often and if it's close to the local reality. Hence, you might get away with some stunts in an action movie if you're in an area like Hollywood. That being said, a lot of the crazy action movie stunts or magic stuff are going to be, where the locals don't believe them, uncanny. Think the crazy car stunts in the later Fast and the Furious franchise like them dropping out of aircraft compared to more subtle stunt driving early on. Extreme--well that's opening planar gates and other stuff that just isn't there. See pages 17-19 of that lighter tome by Mage's main developer for more ideas. The +3/+5 difficulty suggestions works well--if you want +4 it's for those 'it's crazy and bends the laws of physics but at least you can b.s. that'

              Honestly, in practice in Mage: the Ascension it is best to spend a Willpower to get a success on a roll or to avoid a botch on a magical effect. A Sanctum for crafting and for Correspondence sensing magic also stop a lot of Paradox. Vulgar magick per M20 is only 1 Paradox unless you botch, so it's something you can cast without great fear if you are careful. Keep under 5 Paradox and 1 should bleed off per week of game time. If you get more, you're getting into a lot of fights or perhaps just don't understand how to make your magic Coincidental (or subtle).

                      This is my current set of M20 houserules. Most of them seem relevant for the topic at hand.

                      Backgrounds can be bought and raised with experience points.
                      Avatar Storm and Disembodiment do not exist.
                      The maximum amount of Quintessence that may be expended to lower magickal difficulties is the highest of Avatar or Prime.
                      The magickal pool is Arete + highest Sphere involved in the effect.
                      Everybody important can soak lethal damage like bashing.
                      Final modifier to magickal rolls cannot exceed +/- 3; all modifiers, including Abilities enhancing magick and Quintessence expenditure, can be combined to reach the maximum net amount.

                      Magickal effects can be Hidden, Disguised, or Defiant. Hidden magick is any effect that is cast when not in the presence of Sleepers, or that the Sleeper population around the mage accepts as normal or fails to notice. Effects that are accepted as a believable event by the local Sleeper population are considered Hidden magick. It also includes those effects that would be surely recognized as magical if there were a Sleeper witness but there is none at the moment. For Hidden magick, the creator does no effort to cloak it, and only cares about the success of the effect, so it is the easiest to cast. Yet it is fairly safe because the effect is part of the local Consensus, there is no Sleeper around, or they are not in a position to acknowledge it. In the absence of Sleeper witnesses to reinforce it, local reality usually has to yield to the Awakened will and power of a mage (if they properly shape the effect) and accept magick as possible. Therefore Hidden magick causes no Paradox if it is successful.

                      Disguised magick takes place when one or more Sleepers witness and notice it, but are fooled about its true nature. When casting a Disguised effect, the mage shapes it so that Sleeper observers interpret it as a normal, if unusual, event or the product of chance, or the effect itself makes them unable to notice. This kind of effect happens when Sleeper observers are present and would notice the magick but the mage makes an extra conscious effort to disguise it as a normal event. This may be done by shaping the magick to appear as something that could conceivably happen, or by cloaking it with a conjunctional illusion or distraction effect. In casting Disguised magick, mages choose to make an additional cloaking effort, hence they suffer a higher difficulty. However it does not cause Paradox if successful.
                      Defiant magick occurs when there are Sleeper witnesses that notice it and deem it an impossible event according to their paradigm. It is not so easy as Hidden effects (Sleeper presence makes local reality much less flexible to Awakened will) nor so complex as Disguised magick (the mage does not have to care about cloaking the effect) but it carries a serious Paradox penalty that grows ever heavier the greater the number of Sleeper observers.

                      Base Casting Roll
                      Effect is… Difficulty
                      Defiant 7
                      Disguised 8
                      Hidden 6

                      Paradox Points Generated
                      On a Success
                      Defiant One point + one per factor of ten in Sleeper witnesses
                      Disguised None
                      Hidden None

                      On a Botch
                      Defiant Two + Two per dot in highest Sphere + two per factor of ten in Sleeper witnesses
                      Disguised One per dot in highest Sphere
                      Hidden One per dot in highest Sphere

                      Paradox Backlash Roll
                      Storyteller rolls one die for each point of Paradox in character’s current Paradox pool, against difficulty 6.
                      Successes Effects of Discharge
                      Botch All Paradox points discharge harmlessly.
                      No successes No effects, but no Paradox points discharge.
                      1-5 One point of Paradox discharged per success. Mage also suffers a Burn or acquires a trivial Paradox Flaw.
                      6-10 One point of Paradox discharged per success. Mage also suffers a Burn or acquires a minor Paradox Flaw.
                      11-15 Usual Paradox discharge, as well as one of the following effects: a Burn; a significant Paradox Flaw; a Paradox Spirit visitation; or a mild Quiet.
                      16-20 Usual Paradox discharge, as well as two of the following effects: a Burn; a severe Paradox Flaw; a Paradox Spirit visitation; a moderate Quiet; or banishment to a Paradox Realm.
                      21+ Usual Paradox discharge, as well as two of the following effects: a Burn; one drastic Paradox Flaw; a Paradox Spirit visitation; a severe Quiet; or banishment to a Paradox Realm.

                      A Burn is an harmful discharge of Paradox energies that may inflict one of the following: one level of Bashing damage per Paradox point discharged; one level of Lethal damage per two Paradox points discharged (round down); or one level of Aggravated damage per three Paradox points or fraction therein. The player can usually choose which form Paradox damage takes. All Paradox damage can be soaked with Arete + Avatar. The player may be often able to substitute Avatar with an appropriate Sphere, such as Prime, Life, Forces, Entropy, or Mind, depending on the form the harmful discharge of Paradox takes.

                      Common manifestations of Paradox typically take the form of the Burn, Paradox Flaws, Quiet, Paradox Spirit visitations, or banishment to a Paradox Realm. In almost any case, Paradox manifestation never have a permanent duration, short of a mage having lasting alterations to one’s pattern that are noticeable to Sleepers and seemingly impossible according to local Consensus.

                      A few ways do exist to shed Paradox, but they all require forswearing magick use and/or active presence in the world for significant amounts of time, or alternatively advanced use of Prime and Quintessence expenditure.
                      Bleeding: given time, Paradox energies bleed off and fade away from the mage’s pattern. It takes one week per Paradox point for the energy to bleed off, provided that the mage earns no more Paradox during that time.
                      Abstinence: if the mage avoids doing magick for a time, the Paradox bleeds off at double speed: one point every three days. Any magick cast during that time immediately undoes the process, leaving the Paradox Pool at its previous level.
                      Purification: the mage has to retire from all companionship, attune himself to the universe and the self, and perform some sort of cleansing or centering ritual (prayer, fasting, bathing, trance, meditation, athletic or mnemonic exercises, mathematical calculations, poetry composition). The character has to meditate alone for one day per point in their Paradox Pool. The mage cannot do anything else but attend to basic needs during this time. Otherwise, the character loses any benefit from the purification ritual and the Paradox Pool returns at its previous level.
                      Nullification: Masters of Prime can use a Prime 5 effect to channel Quintessence and purify their pattern from Paradox with the energies of Creation. The character has to expend Quintessence to remove Paradox on a one-point-per-each-point basis.

                      All Effects cast within a Sanctum by its owner and other mages with compatible paradigms are considered Hidden magick, regardless of the presence of Sleepers. A Sanctum reduces the difficulty of spells performed in its area by half its Background’s rating (round down). The Effects of rival mages with incompatible paradigms to the area’s prevailing reality have to be cast as Defiant or Disguised magick, but only as long as the owner is present and able to notice them. A Sanctum has an Arcane rating equivalent to the place’s Background rating. As long as they stay within the protected area, characters enjoy the protection of this Arcane effect too. (A character who already has the Arcane Background gets whichever Arcane rating is highest – the rating of the Sanctum or the rating of her Arcane rating – not a combination of both.). The Gauntlet is one level lower in mystic Sanctums and one level higher in a tecnomagickal Laboratory than it would be otherwise. And a Technocracy Laboratory has a Gauntlet rating of 9 except with regards to Dimensional Science procedures, whose Effects are considered Hidden in an appropriate Technocratic Lab.

                      For mystics, Arete •• makes all instruments optional for one-dot Effects; Arete •••• makes them optional for two-dot Effects; and so on, with every two dots of Arete pushing the number of dots where instruments are optional up by one. For technomystics, you start the process at Arete ••••• • and push the threshold up by one for every dot of Arete.
