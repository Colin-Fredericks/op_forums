    This perennial question seems to arise from the supposition that one could formulate a Procedure so simple that any dope could do it.

    Why would we assume this? The Guide to the Techs notes that, of course, rank-and-file citizens lack the education and insight to execute the scientific methods behind a Procedure properly. Why should we presume that this is stuff that any random grad student or bystander could understand, much less operate? There are rules for hypertech objects which are simple enough that a Genius is not required to operate them, and the process of their creation is difficult and expensive. Only repeated iterations of design are sufficient to simplify a device to the extent it works in Sleeping hands, and there's a whole subunit of a Convention devoted to getting that work done.

    In any case, a Technocrat can probablytell someone how to calibrate a laser rifle, hand it to them, and it will work - the mage rolls the Arete, the civilian handles the Instrument. With sufficient Data, you could do this remotely.

                    I am genuinely confused that anyone who read the foundations of the Technocracy could come to the conclusion that they're unaware that they're performing magic. It's outright stated in the first precept.

                    Order, Stasis, and Consensus
                    Bring Stasis and order to the Universe. Predictability brings safety. Once all is discovered and all is known, Unity will be won.

                    Magick, by its very nature, is unpredictable and capricious. Elite Technocrats work with experimental and unpredictable technology, but their ultimate goal is to standardize that tech. Their futuristic innovations are tested and iterated until those inventions become safe and predictable tools that can benefit humanity. Think of the Technocrats, if you will, as the ultimate alpha testers – the earliest of early adopters.Instead of “spells,” “talismans” and “rituals,” Technocrats develop Procedures, Adjustments, Devices, and Processes, training their operatives to minimize the inherent risks of Enlightened Science. Although vulgar willworking is possible (using what agents call blatant Procedures), it’s actively discouraged. Coincidental willworking (or as agents call it, subtle Procedures) is far more predictable, largely because it almost always appears as advanced technology or scientific applications. This way, the impossible becomes commonplace and predictable. Instead of a world of supernatural horror and endless mystery, the Union tames a world in which everything can be defined, classified, quantified, and ultimately controlled.Thanks to Anthropic Field Theory (a Technocratic explanation for the effects of human beliefs upon reality), human consciousness directs the flow of Earthly phenomena. Technocrats do not program that flow so much as they direct it along the most desirable outcomes for humanity at large. Thus, although a technological elite leads the Technocracy, many agents believe that their Union fights for a world in which reality is shaped by the common populace – by the Masses, not by an anachronistic alliance of mages. The world these agents create is the world that humanity wants – in Technocratic terminology, the Consensus, the ultimate manifestation of the Anthropic Field Theory. Eventually, the Masses will get to share the same advanced technology that the Technocracy employs. Once the Union’s power becomes fully invested and integrated into human discourse and its Consensus, such radical measures will no longer be experimental. They will be predictable. They will be controlled. In short, they will be the very opposite of magick.

                                Originally posted by PazuzuAxelf View Post
                                I am genuinely confused that anyone who read the foundations of the Technocracy could come to the conclusion that they're unaware that they're performing magic. It's outright stated in the first precept.
                                Note how the quoted section essentially says “magick is inherently unpredictable and capricious; what the Technocracy does is only risky when it's experimental.” The whole section delineates between Magick and Enlightened Science, putting forth the notion that there are fundamental differences between them.

                                There's also the question of how widespread the Anthropic Field Theory is among the Conventions. You won't find it in any Mage book prior to M20, save only for the Revised Void Engineers Convention Book where it was introduced. That said, the NWO has been the main proponents in the Union that belief shapes reality and that if you can control the Masses, you can control everything. I would say that Anthropic Field Theory is widespread in those two Conventions and not so much among the Syndicate, Iteration X, and the Progenitors.

                                I would also point out that the Enlightened Anthropic Principle, as outlined in the aforementioned Convention Book, does not posit that Reality is subjective; it does state that there are fundamental laws of Reality that exist independent of human belief. Where it gets dangerously close to the Traditions' view of a subjective Reality is that it admits that Reality is influenced by human belief.

                                That said, in my games another part of that theory is an Enlightened version of some interpretations of Quantum Mechanics, specifically “wave Function collapse” interpretations: “what is possible” is constrained by “what is”; it's only when “what is” is in doubt that there can be a Wave Function to collapse. And it's only when there's a Wave Function to collapse that whatever humans can do factors in. As for what that is, the Traditions say that it's a matter of Will, forcing Reality to conform to your beliefs; what the Conventions contend is that it's a matter of Observation.
