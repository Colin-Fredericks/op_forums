        What do you mean by this - "some paradigms will have it easier than others"?
        Okay say that to give a heart attack you need to have someone's blood on you. For many, many paradigms this works perfectly, the blood being the key to someone's vitality. However for someone who believes that all of reality is an illusion, or someone who thinks that reality is one big computer program, or someone who thinks that reality is one big machine "you need someone's blood" feels weirdly restrictive and unthematic. Part of what's nice about Mage is everyone doing the same stuff in a totally different way, from killing someone with a pin through a doll's heart to killing someone through manipulating probability to create a deadly mugging to killing someone by smiting them and erasing their pattern entirely. What's nice, however, if that they are all bound by the same rules, even if the incidental details do vary (and the players are choosing to use different mechanics, though they do all have access to the same rolls if they so choose).

        Well imagine you gave each Paradigm a specific type of connection they need to kill someone from afar. Imagine one had blood, another had hair, and another had their True Name, hypothetically. Those three things vary greatly in how difficult they would be to acquire, and picking a list of things that are of vaguely equivalent difficulty isn't going to be a simple task.

                    There's also the big question: why would this mage want to be messing with other Supernaturals for fun? What is he or she getting out of this?

                    I mean, I'm sure a player could come up with a suitable justification, if they want to. But it's kind of game derailing. Old, powerful vampires tend to be paranoid and manipulative themselves. And even if the mage has a correspondence link with one vampire, that doesn't mean he or she necessarily has a link to every one of the vampire's allies, childer or ghouls. I think it a skilled storyteller could turn it into a fun little interlude in a Vampire chronicle, as a break from the usual challenges.

                        Well if this is a starting character then Arete 3, Correspondence 3 and Mind 3 are all doable. With Mind 3 and Correspondence 3 you can map something much much larger than a city block (potentially a whole city) and your powerful mental capabilities can sift through all the data you have. Let's say we're a VA who thinks the world is a hologram or video game or simulation or similar. They run a search through the city using Mind 1 to boost their mental capacity (better ways to do it I'm sure, you can have multiple minds at once at level 3 I think?) and Correspondence... 2 I think? Definitely 3 or lower. Then you just filter out all things that are mundane. You're outside the range of Obfuscate, so you're finding every Nosferatu in no time at all. Then you just need to examine the fact that the Nosferatu are split into two or more groups (Nosferatu form family groups, it's worth noting). From here you gradually build up info on every Vampire in the city... why? Well in my games the Traditions like having files on supernaturals, but that's just me. If VAs can have a file on every Vampire in a city they're going to do that, if only so they can help other Mages avoid them. So he's on a payroll to find and file every Vampire in the city, at least at first.

                        Next our VA uses their Correspondence 3 to look for good opportunities to extract objects that will give C links. Since they can spend as much time examining a haven before dropping in as they like, they are unlikely to fail... if they had Matter 2 it would be as simple as conjuring one of their minor personal affects (one tool in a set of lockpicks, one tie from a tie rack).

                        With the C links for all of the Vampires who are plugged into Elysium sorted out, they prepare a big, big ritual. Risky. The ritual is a brief but vivid hallucination for half of the Vampires detailing a raid by the Sabbat killing the other half. Then repeat with the other half.

                        Next ritual is to take the same links and create hallucinations, essentially a potent derangement or unyielding mind control where they register the other half of the Vampires in the city as generic Sabbat thugs.

                        NOW I know that this is a HUGE undertaking and very, very risky... so my question isn't "is this realistic", it's "how can I make this BETTER?" How do we shift the odds more in our favour? Let's assume that this is all happening in Downtimes, that a Session is taking place once a week in-universe, we have 6 downtimes (one per day) and we're gaining 3-4 EXP per session. What's the trajectory? What are we aiming for?

                        As for why we're doing it; fun? I mean people kill Vampires for fun canonically (roughly 10% of Hunters are thrillseekers according to Hunters Hunted ll), can't our Mage do this simply for the love of it? As a side note it's a great excuse to increase Arete at the end of it if successful; you've essentially hacked the NPCs (if we're taking a video game analogy) in a city to all go Rage-Mode on eachother, which is pretty nice modding/hacking... and that hints at the mutable underlying nature of the "game", meaning a quest to increase Arete isn't ridiculous.

                        Now, counter-intelligence... if a Vampire sees past the mind control, perhaps thanks to Dementation or Auspex or high Willpower, what do we do with them? For starters, no one will believe them at first, so you have a few days to deal with it. I'm pretty sure Mind can make someone Frenzy pretty badly... would that be level 2 or 4? Simply hit them into Frenzy when they are trying to convince other Vampires, they'll seem unstable at the very least.

                        As for fighting the growing suspicion, spread a rumour organically (a small thought in one person's mind) that it's all the work of a powerful Malkavian Elder on the other side who wants them to question their commitment to the fight. Are we going to let some Malk Antitribu trick us? No way!

                                        I'm still a bit hung up on the "why" of all this. Just from a storytelling perspective. I can imagine a lot of fun chronicle ideas involving mages as vampire hunters. But to me, the idea of having one Mage (with perhaps a cult) in a room somewhere or even in the Umbra, casting wide net spells to make two sects of Camarilla fight each other/mistake each other for the Sabbat...that doesn't strike me as a fun scenario for my players. All the real action takes place off screen with opponents that don't have much of a chance.

                                        Because I agree, while I think it would be very difficult for a starting Mage to accomplish all of this, it doesn't take that long for them to become incredibly powerful. Especially if they have time, ritual space, a plan and means to prepare. But how do you construct a game around that without the players getting bored at the lack of challenge?

                                        Honestly, with the set up of a superpowered being manipulating vampires to fight a needless war from afar, it kind of seems like you're using Mage to play a Vampire game. Which is fine if that's what you want to do, but it seems kind of unnecessary.

                                        To me, the scenario sounds a lot more fun/interesting/challenging as a Vampire game, with the players taking the role of vampire instead. Some vampire elder from another city messages the players' Prince/Bishop/whatever and explains that there's something very weird going on, and the players then have to figure out what exactly is happening. As relatively weak newcomers, they're not likely to attract the Mage's attention right away, and therefore, they neutralize the Mage's biggest edge: preparation and long term rituals for successes. Even if the Mage starts to realize that the player characters are a threat, he's going to have to find a way to anchor his Correspondence sight to them. In the mean time, clever players can make alliances, sabotage his mortal resources, and do a lot of other damage to his long term plans.

                                        It seems like it'd be more fun to be the underdogs taking down an unbeatable opponent than it would be to be the very powerful mage picking his targets off with no real challenge.

                                                        Tremere, being vampires from a mage tradition, probably have some rituals that make them harder to track. I'd rule Obfuscate functions a lot like Arcane when trying to use Correspondence.
