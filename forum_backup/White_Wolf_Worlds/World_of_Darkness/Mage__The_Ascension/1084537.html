                  And like mark says, I'd love to see more material on the underlying paradigms or belief structures within each group. The system is fantastic, and I feel like looking at both Dark Ages Mage and Ascension's systems side by side is a lovely illustration of the changes in Magic over the years. (DA Mage reflecting the ease and potency of magick in the Mythic Age, both the broad individual scope and potential power, with Ascension's system representing the waning of individual power, but a greater focus on universality, communication and commonality.) But the fluff doesn't do it justice.

                  I mean, the success of the Sphere system in universe is because it finally gave Mages a common way to conceptualize magickal effect divorced from their paradigms. The Foundation/Pillars are supposed to be more heavily rooted in paradigm. I wouldn't want to change the system, but I'd love to see examples in how to express it in more individualized ways. It should be incredibly hard for a Valdaerman to even remotely conceive of what a Chakravanti is doing with his art, and vice versa. And that goes beyond just having different names for their Pillars.

                  I'd also love to see more systems for other lands. The Ngoma, the Akashics, the Wu Lung, for example, all would have been active in this time period, and while I understand that it's probably unfeasible to publish Foundation-Pillar systems for all of them, I'd love to see some rules or guidelines toward creating balanced systems for these and any other group.

                  This is probably silly of me, but I would love to see some rules about converting a Dark Ages Mage to a Sorcerer's Crusade Mage. I've always enjoyed the idea of playing a Dark Ages Mage and carrying them into the modern day (through either longevity or reincarnation), and while I probably could convert a character by hand, it would be lovely to have a system to use/cannibalize.

                                              Originally posted by Zennis View Post
                                              It's time we get rid of this ridiculous four pillars per fellowship shit and give the dark ages some magic that makes sense!
                                              A lot of people are gaga over the Foundation/Pillar concept vs. the Arete/Sphere. (Waits for Just John to magically appear.....)

                                              And their reasoning is sound on several levels. Foundation/Pillar bakes the Focus right into the magic system in a way that Arete/Sphere is pretty much inept at doing. It's just too generic. It's GOT to have a stack of additional rules to accomplish the same thing. Which, thank the gods and little fishes, has FINALLY codified in some manageable forms as Focus in M20. But even that is mechanically less successful on the level of simplicity in getting hip deep into the way a group does magic. Oh, and it is is mechanically more in line with the fundamentals of the WoD This+That system of, well, everything. So none of the vague hand-waving that Arete requires unless the Mage game is designed to be lone of long term rituals to do anything effective.

                                              And the Minor Spheres system, no matter how you bust it out won't take away those plusses for the Foundation/Pillar system. Nor will it addresses the minuses inherent to the Arete/Sphere system.

                                              The problem with Foundation/Pillar is one of sheer scale. When I start seeing things like "just make more Foundations and/or more Pillars to cover everything and everyone", the failure is to see the level of work involved. You want enough Pillars to deal with all the different aspects of all the different Houses of the original Order of Hermes? Okay.... probably doable. But it's probably more like adding at least four more. But, even the developers have admitted that the Old Faith = Verbena and Spirit-talkers = Dreamspeakers, because trying to handle those concepts even on the level of JUST the region of Dark Ages Europe would actually involve breaking both of those into several smaller Fellowships, each with it's own Foundation and Pillars. Admittedly, some Pillars could be re-sleeved between the various more relevant new, smaller, Fellowships that would fall out of those ur-Traditions. And there would be some continuity and potential, dare I say it?, "fellowship" between the groups that, collectively would comprise a group of similar Fellowships that all have similarities to identify with one another to some degree as a community of mystics with similar views. (Sort of, but not quite, like the similarities shared by the Houses of Hermes, but more on the nature of a continuum of relationship as opposed to an organized group based on a core of common practices.)

                                              Then one can start to unpack what "everyone" can mean. As in, move the boundaries of the sandbox out to encompass the rest of the World. Which roughly equates to "Pillars/Foundations for the Traditions". Then you hit the above problem magnified. Most Traditions are collectives of like-minded folks that have some kind of commonality, but that commonality is really broad and encompasses very different kinds of mysticism from vastly different cultures. Moreover, some Traditions were cobbled together from vastly different sub-groups. Even if you are reskinning Foundations and Pillars to reduce workload, there isn't going to be pretty much ANYTHING in common between the Madzimbabwe and the Chakravanti and the remnants of the Eleusnian Mysteries/Orphic Cults (e.g. the Pomegranate Deme). There is no actual "Euthanatos" in the Dark Ages. That's a bunch of groups that got tossed together, largely because, it seems, the Eurocentric leaders at the Grand Convocation just didn't know what to do with the various groups because they were outside of their experience (or, perhaps, didn't like them and didn't want them to sit at their table in the cafeteria, e.g. the "death mages" of Europe that really seem like they could/should be Verbena.) In essence, its going to take dozens of Fellowships with many different Foundations and scads of Pillars to cover even what will eventually add up to the constiuent parts of the later Traditions (and a chunk of the future Disparates).

                                                  Originally posted by Ajax View Post

                                                  And a lot of people aren't.

A DAM20 is invariably going to have to pick between one group and piss off the other. For those of us that don't want to see dozens upon dozens of Foundations, and hundreds of Pillars, a DAM20 that works like Sorcerer's Crusade is going to be what we always wanted: Mage tweaked for the end of the High Mythic era. But it will be a spit in the face to anyone that's a huge fan of the original DAM. If DAM20 is a repeat of DAM, it can be a bigger better version of that that can make people that love it have even more to play with, but all of us that don't like the Pillars set up it's just wasted page count.

                                                  The Foundation/Pillar system isn't any better at getting deep into a given system of magic. It's just superficially more flavorful. Seasonal Pillars don't make any you any more deeply into the magical belief systems of the groups the Old Faith is meant to represent. It also hand-waves all sorts of things, like why ever Fellowship uses four "core" Pillars and then some of them sometimes have more of them... but complications. The Pillars are hand-waved to hell and back to justify why they do what they do to try to cover all the magical spells you're supposed to be able to cover.

                                                  And the WoD system has always had single trait rolls for things: Willpower being a prime example.

                                                  The problem with Foundation/Pillar is one of sheer scale.
                                                  That's one of the problems.

                                                  There's also:

                                                  1) The poor definition of Pillars and how to accomplish effects that aren't clearly spelled out by the books.

                                                  2) The obnoxiously cheap ability to master magic when there's only four Pillars, but jacking up the price makes them obnoxiously expensive individually.

                                                  3) The ill-fitting nature of Pillars when trying to force them into a limited number while making them tied to group belief. A limited number is easy when they're not tied to a specific group's beliefs, but letting belief dictate Pillars makes it nonsensical for them all to have the same number.

                                                  And yes, there is a reason why all of those are about Pillars, not Foundations. Foundations aren't the issue, since they're just Arete with bonuses you get beyond more dice.
