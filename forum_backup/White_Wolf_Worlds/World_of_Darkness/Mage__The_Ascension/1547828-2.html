            Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
            I used to argue this a long time ago, but that's taking a game mechanic and turning it into an in game metaphysic. It also leads to some strange consequences, like mages abandoning their beliefs and morals as they rise in Arete.
            IMHO, this is one of the areas where M20's more nuanced take is immensely helpful. In earlier editions, there wasn't much clarity as to what "paradigm" referred to: it was assumed to be a mixture of how you think the world works and how you go about working Effects. M20's Focus rules give you Paradigms that define how you think the world works, and Practices that define how you go about working Effects. In this context, a Path to Ascension arguably says nothing about abandoning one's Paradigm (e.g., Might make Right or Everything is Data); but it arguably does still at least imply that following the Path to Ascension involves overcoming the need for your Practices.

            Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
            Also, there's a common trope in fantasy and sci-fi about the characters getting so good at their at their skills they no longer need the same rigid steps they used to. Sometimes this is them growing beyond their previous paradigm, but often this is just because the steps were just crutches to make magic easier or easier to understand.
            …and that's essentially the same as transcending your Practices.

            Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
            And this is the direction M20 took. Mages aren't abandoning foci, they are internalizing them. The oracle stops needing drugs to get into an altered mental state not because he doesn't get into an altered mental state, but because he doesn't need to use drugs to get there anymore. The martial artist doesn't need complicated forms to direct the chi flow in his body because he can do that directly, but his paradigm is still based around chi flow.
            Is it, though? Chi flow (or, more generally, Energy; M20 p.591) is an Instrument that the Martial Artist uses in his Practice. When he surpasses the need to use that Instrument, is his Focus really still based around chi flow?

            When the Martial Artist no longer needs to use any of his Instruments, is he really still Practicing Martial Arts? Or has he moved beyond his Practice to sheer Willworking? Granted, he retains access to those Instruments if he wants to use them; but the issue is that he no longer needs to.

            Also: I've never said that raising Arete to 10 is what Ascension is. The books do seem to indicate that increasing Arete is helpful, though.

                                Also, raising your Arete isn't "seeking power"; and raising it doesn’t automatically interfere with the Path to Ascension. A case could be made that raising Spheres above 5 is "seeking power" and locks you out of achieving Ascension; that latter part is certainly what Masters of the Art said, though it seems to conflate "seeking power" and "rigidly adhering to your paradigm to the exclusion of all else".

                                I think we need a more nuanced view of what a Paradigm is in order to speak sensibly about this. I would recommend Charles Siegel's Prism of Focus for this, particularly his Tenets system, which provides structure to Paradigm. In particular, one of the Tenets listed there is Openness: to what extent does the mage's Paradigm allow for the possibility that other Paradigms might be true? This is an extension of the MRev notion that paradigms can be Open or Closed to varying degrees. This, and the notion that Paradigms aren't inherently set in stone: it's entirely possible and appropriate for a mage's Paradigm to evolve over time.

                                What I've been describing as "growing beyond your paradigm" is more properly described as "evolving your Paradigm to be more Open". The material about what Ascension is strongly implies that in order to Ascend, you must evolve your Paradigm into an Open one: mages with Closed Paradigms cannot Ascend; and it's arguable that even Hierarchical Paradigms ("your Focus is entirely valid; but my Focus is inherently better") would have difficulty with Ascension. Archmasters are blocked from Ascension because their Paradigm tends to become more Closed as they advance.

                                You can develop your understanding of "your Paradigm" as much as you want; and I would argue that that, not "pursuit of power", is what Arete models. It's why raising Arete involves Seekings which challenge the mage's worldview. But the reason why raising Arete isn't sufficient to Ascend is because Ascension demands Openness; and if your increases in Arete aren't resulting in your Paradigm becoming more Open, it's not moving you towards Ascension. This is another way of describing what I meant by "broadening your paradigm": a broad Paradigm is an Open Paradigm, and vice versa.

                                And yes, this means that Technocrats tend not to Ascend, because Technocratic Paradigms tend to be Closed. But then, Technocrats generally don't pursue Ascension. Neither do the Disparates, for that matter; the pursuit of Ascension (in a personal sense) is nearly always a Traditions thing. So it makes sense that Ascension is uniquely compatible with the Traditions' emphasis on Open Paradigms.

                                                Nyremne's approach appears to be "Masters of the Art doesn't make sense to me, so I'll disregard it and everything that follows from it, such as similar content in M20." And it's perfectly fine if you want to run your Chronicles that way. I'm trying a different approach: can I make sense ​of Masters of the Art? If so, that validates an existing published book instead of tossing it, and it opens up more possibilities for how the game can be played. With this in mind:

                                                1. Raising Arete is helpful for Ascension. It's not necessary to get to Arete 10 in order to Ascend, and you might not be able to Ascend even if you get to Arete 10; but the higher your Arete, the more likely you'll be able to Ascend.

                                                2. Raising Spheres can be helpful, harmful, or neutral to the Path of Ascension. Unlike Arete, which is a measure of how attuned you are to whatever Ascension is, Spheres are all about what you can do. That doesn't automatically make them a trap; but it does mean that a mage that focuses too much on them has fallen into the trap of power. Masters of the Art describes Exemplars as mages who become overly focused on the Spheres and block their Path to Ascension in the process. This works reasonably well as is; all that's needed is something to indicate that the mage has developed an unhealthy fascination with the Spheres, and is no longer seeing them as a means to an end but as an end in and of themselves.

                                                3. Focus can also be either helpful or harmful to the Path of Ascension. Masters of the Art talks about "growing beyond your paradigm"; and it claims that Archmasters have become so focused on a single paradigm that they've blocked their Path to Ascension. Being a transitional book between 2e and Revised, it doesn't actually have M20's concept of a Focus composed of a Paradigm, Practices, and Instruments; it just has a paradigm, and the foci that let you actualize it. (Well, technically each Tradition provides a Philosophy and either a Style or Theories and Practices depending o on the edition, which are roughly analogous to Paradigm and Practice; but they're joined at the hip, meaning that each Style or Practice flows from one Philosophy and the two are extremely easy to conflate.) So the concept needs some refinement for use with Revised and M20. Since I'm referring to both M20 and earlier editions, I'm going to capitalize Focus, Paradigm, and Practice when referring to M20's concepts, and I'll use lowercase paradigm, practices, and foci to refer to the concepts in the earlier editions.

                                                In the Revised edition, Bitter Road introduces the Paradigm Styles: Rigid, Closed, Open, and Liberal. Guide to the Traditions builds on that, introducing notions such as how deep your grasp on your paradigm is (Surface, Shallow, Strong, or Deep), what Spheres the mage prefers to use, and how the Avatar's Essence influences the paradigm. It then has a section on Altering Paradigm, noting that paradigm can evolve over time, not just becoming deeper but also shifting it. This is where you see the notion that a paradigm can have both depth and openness, and that the two don't necessarily align.

                                                I think this distinction between Surface/Shallow/Strong/Deep and Rigid/Closed/Open/Liberal is key to understanding why we're taking part each other. 1e and 2e conflated these, saying that as your Arete increases you get both a deeper understanding of your paradigm and a broader understanding of other people's paradigms. MRev and M20 don't exactly abandon this, but don't really embrace it either. This is where I think Lord Revan is coming from: he sees Arete as being a measure of how deep your understanding of your paradigm is; so the idea that raising Arete broadens your horizons feels wrong. Nyremne sees Arete as being about broadening your horizons; so the idea that Arete 10 isn't the same as Ascension feels wrong.

                                                Openness does seem to match the 1e and 2e notion of "growing beyond your paradigm" reasonably well, in the sense that the more open-minded you are with respect to others' paradigms, the more it makes sense that you'd be able to adopt their practices for your own use. Your own paradigm gradually ceases to be the be-all and end-all of how magic works and becomes just one model among many that are at your disposal. Where 1e and 2e assume that everyone develops this naturally as Arete increases, MRev allows for more options: that's where "Archmasters are stuck in their paradigm" comes in, with the idea being that the more they fixate on their own way of doing things, the more Closed and then Rigid their paradigms become; and that their fixation on their own paradigm is what prevents them from being able to Ascend.
