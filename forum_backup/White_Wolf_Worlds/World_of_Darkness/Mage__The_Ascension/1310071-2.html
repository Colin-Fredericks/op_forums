    It seems that all your arguments revolve around the questioning if a mage knows how many effects he can cast and if he can differentiate them.

    We disagree on the semantics of in and out game of "effect". In the case of the FAQ contradiction, you could change out-game "effect" to "Arete roll" and in-game effect to "spell". My points stands.

    You are using the instruments and the action of using them as being considered "effects" as well.
    Let's talk about in-game only, since it's where we are having trouble:
    Magick, more than anything, requires Will and an intention. The mage fisrt must have an intention in mind. "I want to burn this guy". Then he will use the methods he knows to turn that into reality. He might use his necklace and visualise his target burning, or whatever. He might also do more than one thing, like visualise holding his necklace and the do a little dance with a moonwalk and finish with a backflip and pointing at the target. Note that he made lots of things, but they are all part of the method he uses to burn people and he knows that. As soon as he finishes all this crap and sees his target burning, he knows his spell ended. Each spell equals an intention and that's how they can count them.

    Now, if the mage wants to get more than one result (out game effect for you) in one single use of his methods,this means he must have this intention in the first place."I will burn and wind push this guy all at once". He then proceeds to use his methods for this as well. He knows that he made just one thing because he just used his methods to reach that goal one time.
    If he want to burn someone, visualise, dance,backflip and point and the guy is burnt, and after that he wants to wind push the guy and do all that again he knows he has casted 2 spells. Each spell is directly related to the magr intention.

    Your university example is bad, because in Mage, spells tend to be more direct. We don't see effects like "i want to get rich" or "i want to get promoted in my job".
    Also, in Mage getting a degree isn't magick because it isn't directly changing reality. But if we try to fit into magick I would say that your intent is getting a degree, studying is your instrument and getting the degree is the result. One effect.

    Once you understand that a mage CAN differentiate and count his spells, all my other arguments will make sense to you.

        Actually, the main point of contention is whether the game should be driven primarily by the rules. It's not that the mages in the game can't sense the rules in the Mage gamebook; it's that they shouldn't have to. To understand our point of view, you should think of the game as being an approximation of what's “really” there, which is why Rule 0 exists. It's why I bend the rules for Wonder Creation and allow six or more Effects in a Wonder is that's what makes sense in setting.

        But more to the point, the converse of this is that the “five Effects in a Wonder” rule is an approximation — a “rounding error”, if you will: the accounting system in the rules that determines how much a Wonder costs doesn't allow for fractional point accounting, so there's no way to get a discount for having less than five Effects. This is essentially the same kind of “rounding error” that exists with how much you can lift: in the rules: Strength is measured in dots. If we were to accept the idea that the rules completely define the setting, that would mean that everyone would be able to carry 20 kg, 50 kg, 100 kg, 200 kg, or 300 kg — never 45 kg or 55 kg or 63 kg or anything other than those five values. That is, of course, absurd. In a similar way, the “five Effects in a Wonder” should be viewed as a guideline for what's reasonable, not a hard-and-fast capacity that can always be filled, and must be filled unless you're an idiot.

        And frankly, that very last part is what I object to the most: not even the idea that there's a hard-and-fast capacity of five Effects per Wonder, but that it is wrong not to fill it all the way.

        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
