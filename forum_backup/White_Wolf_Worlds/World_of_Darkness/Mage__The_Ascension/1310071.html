    Custom Wonders

    05-05-2019, 02:26 PM
    Even though M20 books (and revised) have some wonders examples, there are lots of effects that require a custom wonder.
    Now, the rules presented in M20 says that a wonder can have up to one effect per wonder dot. While the books examples doesn't seem to follow this rule, when choosing a custom wonder at character creation, do you?
    From a player perspective, the more effects a wonder has, the better. And paying 6 background points in a 3 dot wonder, or even 10 background points in a very powerful 5 dots wonder and having just a couple of effects seems like you are not having what you paid for.

    Also, I know the highest spehere effect will determine how much dots the wonder requires. But what about the other effects? Couls they be the same sphere dots, for example, a 4 dota wonder having 4 effects of level 4 spheres? Or should they "secondary" effects have lower sphere effects?

    According to the rules, it seems they can all be the same sphere level, equak to the wonder dots.

                                      Originally posted by Aleph View Post
                                      Well, don't be: Mage it's not a wargame and thus Wonders should be built with flavor in mind - that's not a rule but rather the philosophy behind the rules. WoD authors were always against optimization and powergaming, they just don't care about that stile of play.                                       I love this post so very, VERY much. I want to hug you.

                                      Whenever I write character backgrounds, wonders, rotes or what-have-you, I try to approach it from a simple perspective: "Would this thing/person/whatever be really interesting, fun and/or intriguing in a TV series/movie/novel, or would it be bland and too simple?"
                                      It's a good guideline to actually make flavorful things, and even better: It's *fun* to come up with creative things along those lines.

                                      Min/Maxing is boring. Take your points, squeeze in as much as you can, minimize any actual flaws and downsides, and then think of any actual in-game reasonings after. It makes everybody and everything more of the same, almost as if the characters themselves read the rulebook, until you get the RPG variant of a minmax Metabuild.
                                      It's fine in videogames where it can at times turn into an art to optimize builds, but in anything that's supposed to be collaborative storytelling (or one-sided storytelling in the case of pasively watched/read media), it starts getting oh so one-dimensional.

                                      It's the same reason I always get wary whenever discussions about maxing all-encompassing damages/protections/what-have-you begins. My thought always goes back to "Yes, but is that character even the kind of person to do that? Is that kind of thing even part of their personality? Does their belief and practice even teach such things?"
                                      I guess it's the old make-most-use-of-game-mechanics vs. Roleplaying (capital R) conflict.

If I played WoD as a wargame, with an ST that carefully balances "encounters" having an efficient use of the rules in mind, then I would have to agree because getting a 5th power for my 5th level wonder may well be the difference between being helpful or not when the party gets overrun by vampires.                                                       For instance, the Time Watch:

                                                      If I were to make a watch that includes the Time 5 effect to step out of time, and the fluff it's that the time outside moves according to the hands and only the user it's "outside" that (allowing me to move when the hands are still), I would never add an effect to slow time that would affect someone else, or a Time 4 that would affect a spell. Those would be against the flavor of the watch as I envision it.

                                                      I would include a power to Rewind time when the hands are forced counter-clockwise. If you're sent to the future, the date will be correct and the hands will move accordingly).

                                                      I might add an effect to ascelerate time, but it would ascelerate the time outside, not the time of the user (no Celerity) - not sure how that can be useful, but it surely goes hand to hand with rewinding when the hands move counter-clockwise and keeping time still when the hands are still

                                                          Originally posted by Synapse View Post
                                                          Ah, the age old false dichotomy of optimization vs narrative attractiveness.
                                                          It is not a 'vs' at all. IMO. Narrative atractiveness and Optimization are no opposing rivals. They can well co-exist and one can come from the other.
                                                          But, narrative attractiveness should not harshly suffer under optimization; So, in that regard it is more a weighting of the two rather than actual opposition. When a character makes decisions strongly based on OOC mechanics rather than in-world events or their personal past and experiences, for example, is when the scales get tipped too much.
                                                          Likewise when a character or item clearly is built with the mechanics first, and any story or in-game reason for doing so second instead of the other way around - at least in my opinion.
                                                          As Aleph said, to each their own, and there might be game table where a sort of 'story second' powergaming might be necessary...but it sounds rather strange to me.


                                                          EDIT:
                                                          To add, it also boils down to things and characters with flaws and reasonable abilities instead of Overpower ('abilities' depending on context..) being just more interesting to observe and, IMO, to act out.
                                                          Now, there are always exceptions to this; My absolute favorite example is the series "One Punch Man":
                                                          If you'd have asked me at one point in the past "Hey, can a series about an invincible guy who can destroy any enemy in a single punch be entertaining?" I'd have gone "Lolno."
                                                          But it is an utterly entertaining and fun watch; Not because the character lacks RAW POWAH, but because he is just adorably ignorant when it comes to social contexts, that he just ends up in absolutely bizarre situations. It works excellently as a comedy series. If the series actually was a *serious* one, with this kind of character at its center, however, it would not work.
