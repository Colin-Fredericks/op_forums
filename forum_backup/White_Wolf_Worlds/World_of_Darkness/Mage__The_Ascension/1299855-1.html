    Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post

    I thought the +3 limit didn't apply to those Willpower-based difficulties? I only say that because the examples talk about +5 difficulty totals, that sort of thing.

    And yes if someone has Willpower 9 and is a supernatural being, it's difficulty 12 for starters, possibly difficulty 15 if you're doing "DANCE PUPPETS DANCE" style magick. But my question would be does the +3 cap actually apply here?

    But knowing that -3/+3 cap is applied at the end of the equation is very helpful, thanks!
    Good question. My take it's that Willpower-based difficulties supersede the usual limits on difficulty modifiers only in regards to the +3/+5 you get for trying to use Mind on nightfolk.

    This superseding it's only mentioned in the "Mind-Mages and Night-Folk" section as an exception: "Yes, this supersedes the usual limits on difficulty modifiers, and yes it makes it very hard to force Night-Folk to perform acts they are not willing to perform." There's no other instance where a +5 to diff it's discussed, except in regards to sleepers performing social conditioning w/o magick.

    Other difficulty modifiers are never mentioned as superseding the usual rules, and thus one could argue that these don't supersede the usual rules. Hence the maximum diff for forcing a mortal to do something he doesn't want to would be +3, and for nightfolk +8

        Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
        True enough, though I'd argue that a mage's ability to drop particular Instruments is in inverse proportion to have dependent the mage is on that Instrument. On the grounds that heavily a relied upon Instrument turns into a spiritual crutch, stifling the mage's growing enlightenment (at least as far as that Instrument is concerned). Otherwise the player could use one Instrument for everything initially, then drop that Instrument at the first opportunity and never have to worry about them.

        If a mage uses a Unique Instrument often enough - and indeed because it's so useful and personally important to the mage - it gets overcome LAST. Unless the mage didn't use it that often in aggregate, or had a compelling role-play reason for why the Unique Instrument is suddenly not so vital.


        Depends on the Sphere, and the Effects used from it. Forces has a high number of potent and varied applications, both offensive, defensive, and utilitarian.

        By contrast, the mage might only use a given Sphere for a single Effect. But that Effect might be really useful, or be employed really often. Time, especially, is known for this.
        Well, since you can have any number of rotes and use amy number of instruments in your casting you could simply start using your sropped instrument in your effects, considering it is generic enough.
        I gave an example where the mage doesn't used gems in much effects. After dropping it first (because he didn't used a lot) he began to implement it into his effects. He stilk needs the blood to buff his strength, but now he realised that using the gem in conjunction makes he focus better. He couldn't do it without blood, because he still think it is necessary, since it is his primary instrument.

        And still, specialized instruments don't cover all effects like a a personalised (or maybe unique) do. And they need a very good explanation too.
