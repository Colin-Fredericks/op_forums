            I mean, the simple answer as long as you're not extended beyond sensory range for anything other that perceiving the book fragments: you do it in a properly aligned Sanctum. No need to come up with any sort of contrived situation. Unless it's common knowledge to Sleeper society that only one copy of the book was known to exist, and that it was since destroyed, there's no risk of you taking it out of the Sanctum and problems cropping up like if you give yourself wings and then leave a Sanctum. Any magical attributes of the text that have been reconstructed aren't (probably) inherently vulgar, so that's not a problem either.

            The coincidental/vulgar line is what a hypothetical average observer would consider as they watch you cast the spell. No amount of plausible explanations after the spell is cast justifies it if the book just pops into existence next to you just because no real Sleeper is there to see it; even if you want to have such explanations in handy for later.

                                    Originally posted by Muad'Dib View Post

                                    It has to be noted that the vast majority of M:tA is written strictly from the perspective of Tradition Mages - in fact it might be even more narrow, with the average narrator or protagonist of a M:tA book being a Hermetic, a Chakravanti, a Chorister, an Akashic, or a Verbenae. Stories or descriptions written strictly for and in regard to viewpoints of different Mage groups - Mystic Nephandi Mages, the Technepandi, Hollow Ones, Crafts, Technomancers (from the Traditions), or various Technocrats - are rather rare. Though some are of course of excellent quality, as it is Mage: the Ascension and WoD after all.
                                    I would disagree a little bit in here. I'd say that overall, Mage is largely described in Hermetic terms, but also on very heavy Technocratic elements. And that holds true for both the official books and for how the player base communicates. And when other Paradigms are in play, this "Techno-hermetic" language gets "translated" to it.

                                    The best example is perhaps, how the Sphere of Entropy is understood. Entropy is perhaps the least understood Sphere of all exactly because it is best understood in "technocratic" terms - but Mage is a game where you ALWAYS start as a "Traditional Mage" rather than trying to figure it out from the pov of the Union from the get go.

                                    So, Entropy is, in a sense, "quantic manipulation", there is, you make tiny, miniscule changes at a quantum level, that add up to a monumental effect, which the mystics call "destiny" or "fortune". Even the name itself is from a scientific therm, rather than it being called "Fortuna Sphere", so right there you already are receiving technocratic jargon. And then, once the concept is understood, it gets "translated" to other paradigms (oh, so it's the manipulation of Fate/Destiny/Chance? Cool).

                                    There are several other examples thou, and they cut both ways: Spirit and Prime for example are mostly explained in hermetic terms, and once it is understood by the players, the Guide to the Technocracy "translates" them to technocratic verbatim.

                                    And the reason for this is simple: "technocratic paradigm" is basically our middle school science, mixed with a little bit of techno-babble for style, and "hermetic paradigm" is mostly pop culture mysticism. So that's why those too are the used "codes" for Mage the Ascension, because it is simple and easy to understand, and easy to compile into complex ideas, made digestible enough to be used in a game of RPG, rather than requiring a PhD in Natural Sciences or in Anthropology to play. So, what is Prime? It"s "Meta-Magic", the Sphere that deals with "magic fluctuations" (know as "Mana" in many games, "Quintessence" in MtA). What is Entropy? Manipulation of chance. So why not just call it "Fortune" or "Destiny"? Because you need to understand a bit of middle school science in order to understand each one of its levels. What is "Spirit"? Manipulation of "ephemeral" entities (commonly know as "spirits"), and so on, so forth.

                                    Do you wanna see other Spheres with heavy techno-languages in them? Matter, Forces, Life. Forces may even be a better example, since to understand what Forces do, you have to know what is Kinetic Energy, Thermal Energy, Light, Sound and Electromagnetic (oh yes, and Gravity, almost forgot that one). Again, all of that, middle school science.

                                    For Matter, think about Matter 5... Change *density*... Basically everything Matter 5 does relates to some knowledge we earned in middle school science.

                                    And then, all of those get "translated" to a mystic paradigm. The player using Matter 5 to create a "Sword of the Heavens" will claim to the Master that he wants to "bless his sword" IN GAME, and OFF GAME he explains to the Master he's using Matter 5 to "increase the density" of the sword.

                                    A techno-boy explains to the Master that he's using "Spirit (Dimensional Science) 4" to "trap the spirit (alien anomaly)" in the ship's warp drive, and thus explains how he uses his "quantum modulators" to attune the anomaly wave-lenght to that of the drive, thus concluding the procedure.

                                    See? It's a common techno-hermetic background that gets translated into whatever babble the Mage's paradigm is.
