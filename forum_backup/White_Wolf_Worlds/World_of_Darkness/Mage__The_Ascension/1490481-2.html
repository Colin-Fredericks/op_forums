                That's why Traditional Chantries are usually cross-Tradition, with Choristers, Verbenae, Etherites, Hermetics, etc. working side by side as colleagues, often even working Effects together that blend their individual approaches to Magick to produce results that none of them could achieve on their own.

                                        That's definitely how the Technocracy sees it, yes. And in my preferred take on the Mage setting (where Enlightened Science is built on Earthly Foundations, which is essentially what your comment about “the creative use of the wheel or fire” is all about), they're not wrong.

                                        But they're also not right. While I'll agree that the Traditions at their worst are more interested in the pursuit of Ascension than they are about the well-being of the Sleepers, they're not “set in outdated ways”: what the Traditions do works. Sure, it tends to be more Paradox-prone than Enlightened Science, which only has to contend with the Consensus making something Vulgar, as opposed to having to contend with both the Consensus and Earthly Foundations; but it still works.

                                        And just as importantly, “the Order of Reason puts people first before their magic”, and “they're not lords and they're not bothered by ordinary people having a realistic means to challenge them because they're not ruling through fear” are just plain false. There were Enlightened Scientists once upon a time who thought like that; they were called the Craftsmasons; and while they helped create the Order of Reason with that goal in mind, from the earliest days, they spent much of their time fighting the tendency of their fellow Daedeleans to ignore or lord over the Masses. (And to the extent that the Technocracy doesn't rule through fear, it's usually because they have more subtle and effective ways to rule.)

                                        In the 1600s, the Craftsmasons were destroyed by the Order of Reason, precisely because the other Conventions rejected what they stood for. By the 1800s, the Order of Reason was rapidly transforming into the Technocracy — an apt term for them which refers to “rule by technical elites”.

                                        There are still some in the Union who hold to the ideals of the Craftsmasons; but they're a minority. Of the majority, the most benign are the ones who ignore the Masses and spend all of their time seeking to advance Enlightened Science — ironically, the very thing you accuse the Traditions of doing. The more problematic ones are moral busybodies who actively seek to interfere in the lives of the Masses, “for their own good”; they're convinced that they know better than the Masses what's good for them.
                                        Of all tyrannies, a tyranny sincerely exercised for the good of its victims may be the most oppressive. It would be better to live under robber barons than under omnipotent moral busybodies. The robber baron's cruelty may sometimes sleep, his cupidity may at some point be satiated; but those who torment us for our own good will torment us without end for they do so with the approval of their own conscience. They may be more likely to go to Heaven yet at the same time likelier to make a Hell of earth. This very kindness stings with intolerable insult. To be "cured" against one's will and cured of states which we may not regard as disease is to be put on a level of those who have not yet reached the age of reason or those who never will; to be classed with infants, imbeciles, and domestic animals.

                                        —C.S. Lewis
                                        This is the great flaw of the Technocratic Union: a benevolent tyranny that ends up treating the people it claims to help as if they were subhuman.

                                        I think this is where Grumpy RPG Reviews is coming from: seeing the Technocracy mostly portrayed as “your sex life is your business and not ours” runs directly counter to the Union's tendency to stick its collective nose into everyone's business. I can see that as the official position of the Void Engineers; they tend to have bigger concerns to occupy them than trying to regulate who sleeps with whom. But the other Conventions? Not so much. Each likely has an overall view on sex; and those views are almost certainly not “if it's not harming anyone, do as you will”.
