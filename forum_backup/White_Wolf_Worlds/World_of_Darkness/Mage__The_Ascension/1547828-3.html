                        For Divine Order, Earthly Chaos or Have Faith, I'd take inspiration from the end of St Thomas Aquinas' life. A lifetime of writing exhaustive treatises on theology and morality (ones that are still considered some of the most comprehensive to this day), and then a few months before the end of his life he received a vision of just how much deeper the reality of God goes. After that he never wrote again, saying all of his works seemed like "so much straw."

                        There's no indication that he repudiated his works or thought they led people astray. Instead it seems that he either felt that anything he could write would fall short of what he had seen or that he could not translate his understanding into words anyone else could follow him on.

                        That latter possibility, by the way, is a good explanation for why mages who Ascend don't stick around. It's not that they are whisked away, it is that their new point of view just doesn't compress down into the world in a way anyone else can access.

                                Not the person you've asked, but I've been keeping an eye on this thread, and I have some thoughts on the matter.

                                I think the mistake is acting like the "Might is Right" Paradigm, as written in M20, is synonymous with "increasing your personal power." If you read the entry, "Might is Right" as a Paradigm concept is not just a matter of your personal strength, but rather a belief about the nature of strength and how it relates to the rest of reality. It's about Social Darwinism, the idea that strength, regardless of who holds it, gets to determine what is right, or what is true.

                                I haven't read the stuff in MotA that talks about Archspheres getting in the way of Ascension, but the vibe that I get from the part you quoted from M20 is that it's criticizing the idea of reducing Ascension to just raising Spheres and Arete. And that applies to "Might is Right" just as much as any other Paradigm. In the case of someone who believes in "Might is Right," increasing their own power is an obvious course of action, but if Ascension involves embracing and engaging with the philosophy of your Paradigm beliefs, then it's not enough on its own.

                                Going back to the idea that "Might is Right" as a Paradigm is about Social Darwinism, it's not just about increasing your personal power, but demonstrating it, exercising it, proving it. A Mage could raise their Spheres and Arete by isolating themselves in their research and avoiding conflict, but that does not fit with the idea that "Might is Right." If you want to become a paragon of that ideal, then you need to use that power, prove its supremacy. A "Might is Right" Mage would need to use their strength to establish dominion over their "inferiors." They would need to seek out challenges and overcome them in order to prove their strength. And what about this disgusting fad of "democracy" that's spreading across the world like a cancer, pretending that weaklings deserve to have as much say in how the world is run as their betters?

                                Believing in "Might is Right" can also lead to some philosophically challenging questions, like how you handle being confronted by someone who is indisputably more powerful than yourself. Do you submit to the authority that their power bestows upon them? If you refuse, then you either need to abandon the idea that power alone confers legitimacy, or you need to commit yourself to proving yourself the superior being in the long run, maybe even risking your life in your refusal to submit. You can study the Spheres and raise your Arete without necessarily engaging with those kinds of issues, but they come with the territory of truly believing that "Might is Right." And confronting those issues, and finding resolution to them, is how a "Might is Right" Mage follows their path to Ascension; improving your magical abilities is an important start, but it can't be the entirety.

A desire for personal power that does not engage with any bigger or deeper questions about the nature of reality is too narrow-minded and egotistical for the kind of transcendental awareness demanded by Ascension. I would probably reject any path to Ascension that fails to confront those kinds of Big Questions, not just paths focused on personal power.

                                                            I haven't spent as much time considering technmantic paradigms, so I don't have a lot of ideas about a physics-oriented Etherite. But I imagine that their route to Ascension would probably be tied up in their efforts to create a satisfying "Theory of Everything" that fits with their concept of SCIENCE! And eventually, their understanding of the system reaches a point where they Ascend to either become a larger part of that system, or to observe it from a higher level than their (comparatively) mundane embodied existence can allow.

                                                            I am glad that you asked me about Chaos Magic, however, because that happens to be one of my favourite Paradigms, and I've actually been reading up on it a bunch lately! A Chaote's path to Ascension would probably involve really embracing the infinite possibilities of Chaos, how existence can be understood and engaged with in countless ways, including paradoxical or contradictory ones. It would also entail an understanding of the mutability of the Ego and identity; like the universe, the Chaos Mage can be anything, rejecting any fixed notions of who they are or ought to be. I feel like the route a Chaos Mage would follow to achieve Ascension would probably include the "Transcend Your Limits" Paradigm listed in the Book of Secrets.

                                                            Bringing it back to Sphere knowledge, each of those examples can be used to illustrate different ways that a Mage might develop their powers on the route to Ascension. With an Etherite who believes in a Mechanistic Cosmos, it would be pretty likely that their Sphere knowledge would be consistently increasing as they followed their path to Ascension, because the way they believe that their magic works is closely tied to their understanding of the way that the universe works, and their path to Ascension is closely tied to that increasing knowledge. But that would simply be an unintended consequence of their search for Truth, not the point of what they are doing.

                                                            Meanwhile, for the Chaote that I described, increasing Sphere knowledge might be useful in some respects, but their path is much more philosophical. The kind of understanding that they need to transcend their conceptual limitations and expand their awareness of the cosmos would not necessarily translate into knowledge of specific Spheres in the same way that the Etherite's quest for SCIENCE! would. A Chaos Mage with lower Spheres and Arete, but a deeper understanding of the principles that their Paradigm espouses, could be closer to achieving Ascension than one with higher Sphere knowledge that has failed to really pursue the greater Truth to be found within their metaphysical framework.

                                                            All of this rambling has helped me think of a way to maybe sum it up. I see Sphere knowledge being related to questions of "How?" while Ascension is more like questions of "Why?" If you're too focused on the "How?" questions, without ever thinking about the deeper "Why?" questions, then you will never reach Ascension. But it's quite possible to find the answers to "How?" questions while you're trying to understand the "Why?" questions. Again, power comes as a byproduct, or merely a means, to a greater end.
