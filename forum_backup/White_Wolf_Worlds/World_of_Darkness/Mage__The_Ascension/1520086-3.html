                Originally posted by travisjhall View Post
                Nobody has made a claim that Focus dictates spheres. I have simply been pointing out that the rules do not require that Focus always be ignored when considering spheres - not that I’ve found, anyway.
                Yes, the rules are (and always were) clear that focus is always ignored when considering spheres because the spheres depend only on what the mage is doing from an OOC point of view and not by what means he does it.

                The spheres are determined first, and in that step, foci are never discussed.
                After the spheres are determined, you will go on and look for foci and the rest.

                Also, visual effects do not require spheres, it can be added as fluff and Resonance. You don't need Forces to make a "lay on hands" healing that glows a light from your hands.

                Also, process based determinism (PBD) doea not have anything to do with foci. It has to do with choosing what spheres are required based on what process is happening. From the M20 example:

                "Summoning" a cab using PBD (the default rule):

                – Grab an existing cab: Correspondence 3/Mind 2/Entropy 2 to find and make the taxi go your way (the cab has to be already there, you're just making it go to you)

                – Create a cab and a driver from thing air: Matter 4/Forces3/Life 5/Mind 5

                Or

                - Change the odda of a taxi showing to you with a LOT of successes and big waiting time (of course, you have to be in a city and etc.)

                Now, Result based determinism (RBD) doesn't care for what is happening, just what the result you want, which is going from point A to point B, so:

                -Correspondence 3 to go from A to B and the ST invent some bullshit for that to happen, for example, a taxi happens to appear.

                Note that neither option say anything about foci. You will not find anything on M20, or outside of hdydt, that involves foci into how to determine spheres for an effect.

                            To be fair, there's a playstyle for Mage that disregards most or all of the Merits and Flaws; and technically, Merits and Flaws are supposed to be optional anyway. So “that's what Crucial Component is for” is mildly problematic.
