              The distinction with radical changes has less to do with mass and more to do with form. The line is blurry, but the examples usually don't count size changes as radical transformations. Presumably a big enough size change would count, but whether quadrupling the weight counts is a decision for the table.

              You shouldn't need Prime 2 to add mass because Mage isn't trying to preserve conservation of mass and energy. That's why mages can enhance or suppress fires with just Forces, and grow wings with just Life. Those spells take patterns that already exist and altering them. Prime 2 is involved when mages create new patterns that don't already exist. It is drawing in quintessence to weave that new pattern.

              Or to put it another way, if we did decide to conserve mass and energy, Prime 2 would need to be added to a lot of spells that don't currently use it and that's a lot of unnecessary bookkeeping.

                      No, changing into an ant or tiger is definitely a radical change. Species changes are specifically the domain of full Transformation, which is Life 5 to change others. Changing into a werewolf is harder to determine.

                      Oddly enough, most of the changes into Crinos fall under Alteration. Claws, fur, fangs, even size. Changing a 90lb contortionist into a 450lb professional wrestler isn't out of the question (though different gaming tables may decide differently). Theoretically all of this can be done to others at Life 4.

                      For my part, though, I would still go with Life 5, both because of the degree of change and because with Life 5 you can include impossible biology like firebreathing or transformation. That is, with Life 4/Time 4/Other you could inflict an alteration curse that turns on at the full moon. With Life 5/Time 4/Other you could permanently make someone a creature that transforms under the full moon.

                                          I'm right up there with you. I too am not a fan of "do it to others" requirements of an extra Sphere level. "Do it to others" is already embraced in the table of successes needed, as you need ZERO additional successes to target yourself, but 1 extra success for any other target. Not to mention that there are plenty Spheres that have no such arbitrary requirements. Do you want to get a "lucky shot" using Entropy 2? Fine, that's zero extra successes to target yourself, or 1 additional success per extra target other than yourself; you don't need Entropy 3 to give good or bad luck to others.

                                          The only Sphere upon which I fully agree that there should be a difference in levels to target self and others is Mind, because we can actually change our own minds merely with our thoughts directly, without even using Magic. So, it does makes sense that I can make myself smarter using just Mind 1 or create a mental shield with just Mind 1 - because I'm actually in direct control of my own mind! However, in order to do the same with others, you have to sort of "mind rape" them (and that would be the term, even if what your doing is for their own good, you still have to get inside their minds and twist it for your purposes). So, requiring as much as Mind 4 (which is what allows you to really "mess up" - or "reeducate" - somebody else's mind) makes perfect sense.

                                          Even for Life thou, I cannot just simply wish a disease away or instantly heal wounds with happy thoughts. Much less can I jump from a roof with a strong conviction that I can turn into a bird, no matter how much I identify as a bird and deny reality. Our own body is "external" to our "personal being", which is our mind. Everything we are is inside our mind, our body is just the "hardware" that contains it, and much like a computer can't just simply decide to upgrade his transistors, neither can we consciously desire our body to change. Therefore, it doesn't make any sense for it to be one level easier to target, when it is already easier to target yourself in a required successes base.

                                          As for rewriting Life, yes, I'm fully in favor. Not just of that, but one of the other problems I have with Life is that you can't really create NEW LIFE. I mean sure, you can create weird stuff on you sanctum lab, but those are pretty much unique monstrosities.

                                          For all of the talk about "genetics" for the Techie boys, their version of "genetics" seem to be pretty dumb, since our proud boys in black coats are unable to create newly genetically modified species.

                                          Life's fatal flaw is: your creations cannot procreate.

                                          That's what I have always think that was missing for Life 5. I believe that Life 5 should allow your lab pets to pass on their "genes" (or "pleura" or whatever mystical-babble equivalent).

                                          Yes I know, dread all the "Ubermensch" super boys in black, genetically modified to become the "Homo superior" new species but hey, that just makes it even better...

                                                      For a given value of permanent, yes. What the chart doesn't make clear is if permanent means the spell is permanent but the effect ends if the spell is dispelled or if the effect is permanent even after the spell ends. Nor, for that matter, does the Life 5 writeup make it clear if it is referring to the permanency in the charts or something else. As I said, there are other ways of interpreting Life 5, they just make less sense to me.

                                                      Of course, the reference to permanency in Life 5 may be yet another remnant of a previous draft that slipped through editing. There's a lot of that in M20 and even more in HDYDT.

                                                      In most games I don't think the distinction between permanent spells and permanent effects will come into play so I don't know how important it is. That said, let me give an edge case.

                                                      Doctor "I Just Get These Headaches" (henceforth referred to as Dr Head) decides to make a bunch of these werewolf approximations. The first (subject A) he generates in a lab from nothing. It's a pure creation spell. The next two he makes from helpful "volunteers" he "volunteered" into the back of his white van. For subject B, Dr Head only rolls enough successes for a six month duration. For subject C, he gets enough successes for the spell to be permanent.

                                                      Subject B is clearly under the effects of a spell. When the spell runs out, his werewolf affliction ends and he goes back to being a normal person. If the spell is dispelled early, he goes back to being a normal person early. Subject A, though, is probably not under the effect of a spell. He just exists now and is not subject to being dispelled. At least we generally don't think of created people being subject to that. I think a few of the Technocracy books have mages that were initially created in a lab before they awakened, and they probably weren't intended to be one successful unweaving away from death.

                                                      Subject C, though, is tricky. On the one hand his condition should be permanent like subject A. On the other hand he was created like subject B, which means the spell should function like that of subject B, just with a permanent duration. Not only that, but if he is like B, there should be a way Dr Head could make him like A instead. If it isn't rolling enough successes, what is it? If it is rolling enough successes, what is the threshold for permanent effect vs unending spell?

                                                      Is this an important question? I have no idea. Will it arise at some gaming tables? Definitely.
