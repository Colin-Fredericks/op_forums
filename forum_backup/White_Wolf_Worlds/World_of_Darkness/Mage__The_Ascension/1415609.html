    How do you use Acolytes?

    09-26-2020, 06:18 PM
    Hi!

    Narratively, how do you use Acolytes?

    I have decided to give more depth and personality to most of my Sleeper NPCs...but almost none of them are actually Acolytes. Acolytes do exist in my chronicle, but I dont know how to use them as "part" of the Traditions. I wouldnt like them to be just hanging in the background.

      Remember that Acolytes are people who not only buy into the Paradigm of whatever faction they serve, they're also people who may have ambitions for something greater than merely being helpers. Unlike the common Sleeper - who doesn't even know vast mystical power is even on the table - Acolytes are to one degree or another aware that the Awakening is possible.

      Obviously, not every Acolyte might be informed that a person can just have an epiphany and start doing magick. Some groups or individuals keep that sort of thing close to their chest. But the Traditions are among the groups that would plausibly give their Acolytes enough information to have them know that, if they're lucky, hard-working, and dedicated enough, they too could rise to those heights. Especially if it's a faction that makes initiation into unAwakened Sorcery an option.

      Ergo, Acolytes may have ambition to become Mages. Exactly how they think it works will dictate where their energies will be channeled.

      If they think it's a matter of study and dedicated to learning the Art, Craft, and Theory of their faction's Paradigm, they will spend a lot of time and energy doing that. They'll be trying to gain access to the right books, materials, or even mentors. Any of which a PC could be asked to help with, since Mage PCs obviously know something the Acolyte doesn't.

      If they think it's just a matter of pleasing those with power - and, by proxy, whatever power or principle those Mages purport to serve - you're going to get suck-ups and eager assistants. They may volunteer to help whatever the PCs want, if it means potentially gaining favor with either those PCs or whatever gods, spirits, or transcendent AIs their Paradigm posits is the gatekeepers of magickal power. Of course, Acolytes will only tolerate so much abuse and exploitation, if they don't think it's helping. And other members of the Tradition may take issue with the PCs leading Acolytes on just to benefit themselves. (Your mileage may vary, of course).

      If they've taken to heart the idea that the Awakening comes from personal experience and wisdom, they'll take every opportunity to tag along with the PCs. In the hopes that vicarious exposure to concentrated weirdness and transcendental truths will trigger something inside them. They're less concerned with pleasing the PCs, save in the respect that the PCs are well disposed enough to let the Acolytes continue coming along.

      (Related to this group are the Acolytes who tag along because this world of magick and mystery is exciting. The Awakening is less important than just the experience itself.)


      Over the course of any and all such adventures, an Acolyte might begin to grow frustrated by their inability to Awaken. This may lead to disappointment, bitterness, betrayal, and/or desperation on the Acolyte's part. Such a character can begin to instigate story threads, as their desire to Awaken or negative feelings over failure to do so boil over into confrontation or drastic action.

          Any character that's represented by your Allies, Retainers, Cult, or Familiar background(s) can conceivably be an Acolyte. Likewise the adversarial background of Apprentice (the inverse of Mentor) from revised edition. Exactly which one of these you use to represent the Acolyte is going to impact their relationship with the PC(s). Your significant other who knows you do magic even if they don't exactly understand the ins and outs of it would be an Ally. Your long time housekeeper who happens to know you're a bruja and believes in at least a part of your paradigm would be a Retainer. Your collective of lab technicians who help you do Science! are probably a cult. The talking cat that follows you around everywhere is a Familiar. The hapless Initiate you've taken under your wing is an Apprentice.

          My list of Acolyte concepts/recruiting pools per each Tradition:
          • Akashic Brotherhood: Martial arts aficionados, Followers of traditional Asian medicine or religions, Humble seekers of enlightenment, Honest public servants, Anti-materialists, Members of long-established servant families
          • Celestial Chorus: Seekers of faith looking outside of mainstream religion, Social reformers, Righteous servants of their fellow man, Those rescued from the darkness, Guardian angels
          • Cult of Ecstasy: Thrill-seekers looking for something deeper, Those looking to escape the chains of modern life, People looking to violate societal norms, Followers of charismatic or euphoric religions, Glamor-obsessed star-f***ers
          • Dreamspeakers: Members of one's own tribe, village or neighborhood, Activists involved in indigenous rights or environmental causes, Students studying indigenous or ancient cultures, Survivors of encounters with the Invisible (Spirit) World, Bound spirits
          • Euthanatos: Modern followers of gods of fate, change and/or death, Healers both traditional and modern, Survivors of near death experiences, Death-fixated teens fated for a wake up call, Those chosen by Fate, Ghosts
          • Order of Hermes: Members of occult orders, secret societies or covens, Seekers of arcane and obscure knowledge, Hopeful apprentices looking for power and enlightenment, Traditional artisans and craftsmen, Members of anciently bonded servant families, Bound demons and spirits
          • Sons of Ether: Extremely open-minded and courageous lab assistants, Personal assistants who take care of all of the boring stuff that distracts from Science!, would be sidekicks to science adventurers, engineered robots and bio forms
          • Verbena: Extended members of ancient families, Supporters of modern environmental, animal rights or feminist causes, Followers of modern pagan beliefs, Devotees to traditional healing and handcrafts, Those touched by the fey and other ancient beings
          • Virtual Adepts: Those devoted to the freedom of the individual and of information, Obsessive technology users, Paranoid conspiracy theorists, Those investigating the very building blocks of reality, Sentient AIs
          • Hollow Ones: Hangers on to the Hollower clique, Teenaged runaways, Followers of eccentric modern subcultures (Goths, horror fans, body mod fanatics, Satanists), Eccentric weirdoes, Other pariahs of the night

          And some of the Crafts:
          • Ahl i Batin: Those seeking spiritual unity with Allah, Crusaders for the peaceful unification of mankind, Ignorant pawns, Bound spirits
          • Bata'a: Followers of the various "Voodoo" faiths, Wealthy clients indebted to the Loa, Lesser spirit servants, Zombi both living and dead
          • Knights Templar: Devoted members of the Christian faith, Family members, Future knights-aspirant in training, Unawakened squires, Those rescued from the darkness, Divinely blessed companion animals
          • Lions of Zion: Members of one's own family, Members of the local Jewish community, Hopeful young students, Loyal business associates, Golums
          • Taftini: Muslim apostates, Followers of Zoroastrianism or ancient tribal faiths, Collectors of wondrous objects, Antisocial loners, Bound Djinn
          • Wu Lung: Members of one's extended family, Dutiful servants, Fellow Triad or business association members, Ancestor spirits, Lesser members of the Celestial Bureaucracy


          And a few Acolyte organizations I've used in games over the years:

          The Arcane Archive - Record keepers, historians, conspiracy theorists, and archaeologists devoted to recovering and preserving knowledge and lore, especially that threatened by Technocratic revisionism. They seek out ancient artifacts, try to save dying languages, and uncover hidden or forgotten history.

          The Daytrippers - Explorers of altered consciousness and mental expansion. Members investigate the realms of psychotropic drugs, vision quests, lucid dreaming, meditation, and out of body experiences. It's not all that uncommon for them to develop psychic abilities.

          The Earth Outreach Network - A mix of environmental activists and protectors of endangered cultures. Their work balances both material and spiritual conservation and healing. (And yes, this is specifically designed to facilitate Werewolf crossover if one wishes.)

          The Entertainment Underground - Counter culture artists, musicians, writers, actors and philosophers who fight a never ending battle against the modern pop-culture monolith. Their work is usually small scale and intimate, distributed through alternate methods which bypass the current gatekeepers of most major websites, streaming services, and publishers.

          Friends of the Revolution - Those wishing to take a more active role in the Ascension War. This includes civil disobedience, radical activism, and acts of terrorism, as well as providing information, logistics, and sanctuary for Awakened Ascension Warriors.

          The Lynx - An extremely secretive network of users, hackers, and cryptophiles from across the Traditions and some Crafts who share information and coordinate activities between Chantries around the world.

          The Nightwatch - An underground alliance of guardians and vigilantes who do everything they can to protect the innocent and helpless from predation by supernatural forces, including spirits, demons and ghosts, the Technocracy, vampires and were beasts.


          I originally designed all of these for Acolyte centered games, but I found that they help with establishing what various Acolyte NPCs may be involved in or focused on when designing Chantries or cities.

              Ascension's Right Hand has a lot of good flavor for Acolytes and other retainers. M20 Gods and Monsters also has useful material.

              Acolytes have rarely come up in my games, as most of the players have been either insufficiently interested in the various associated NPC backgrounds for their character, or tend to use those backgrounds in a pretty shallow fashion.

              In my present game, there are three active PCs. The Verbena has the Cult background, defined as musicians, but that background has barely been used. Hopefully we'll get more play out of the musician's Irish bard group--I did make up in small detail the character most useful as an apprentice, Mick. He has been barely used. The Virtual Adept has 3 dots of allies, which finally got defined as a master thief, an attractive male businessman in the medical industry (essentially a business manager for a small private medical practice channeling 'Dr. McDreamy' from Gray's Anatomy) and a mercenary good with a variety of weapons and who knows how to get them. I don't expect much development for those characters, as this is the least engaged PC and those characters are more to provide support than roleplay. The Akashic Brotherhood character has an apprentice, Matteo. Matteo could be a mage or end up becoming a grog (fighter acolyte).

              Matteo has the most background, as the player wanted a craftsman who spoke Chinese as part of their larger plan to spread meditation and Akashic ways throughout the campaign city, Portland, Oregon. Matteo has been the source of several plots--first he was saved from the horrible Alternate Reality game 'Dungeon Chef' and showed his willingness to die to save people and lack of fear of magick. Then, Matteo was in the Verbena's vision from the Blessing background as being in danger because he was smitten with his art teacher. I linked this to the 'serial numbers filed off Duncan McLeod' Verbena from the past game and this created a lot of drama because the Akashic character is celibate and believes his power comes from celibacy. Central Casting had generated a lot of background stuff and Matteo, the devoutly Catholic young man who had the hots for his teacher, was having trouble meditating. The Akashic character made Matteo's training worse by pushing meditation and the celibacy angle--then leaving for a while to study rotes at his old chantry. Matteo rebelled indirectly, as although he did not act on his urges, they occupied his thoughts and he could not meditate effectively.

              Then, after Matteo had to do a required college sexual harassment training that I had delivered by Lily Porter from the Changeling 20 Ready Made Characters because one lecture is faster than a long training, Matteo and his Akashic mentor got into a disagreement about things. Matteo got put back in his place as a student, and the Akashic was asked about his teaching philosophy again by the chantry's master, a Hermetic former college professor. Matteo ended up, as befits someone with Martyr/Traditionalist as their Nature and Demeanor and who really wants to learn the Akashic Way, giving in to his mentor's demands. Fortunately, the art teacher went to another area, but the judgement of the Akashic Brotherhood character was questioned by the chantry's NPCs (and the Verbena PC who thinks he's a misogynist white boy idiot).

              Then, as the 'Dungeon Chef' plot with the Nephandi who was foiled by the PCs and seeking revenge against the Akashic heated up, and the chantry started improving its electronic and physical security, they realized that they didn't know much about Matteo. A security check by the chantry while the Akashic was off fighting a certamen against a Hermetic he had angered by forcing the Hermetic to flee without his library lest he become a victim of the Technocracy (borrowing from Watch the World Burn book on Storytellers Vault) revealed Matteo had a fake ID and had entered a U.S. college under false pretenses. While Matteo did pass the security check, it was revealed he was 1. a Progenitor hybrid of human and dragon DNA 2. had the Catholic Church in his home country, El Salvador, as a patron 3. Had angered some minor Marauders in the area around El Salvador

              The Akashic character had never done even a basic security check on his apprentice and despite offering to do so, was out of his element and denied the right to investigate by chantry leadership (they'd concur with the Verbena's assessment of the Akashic except consider his idiocy to stem from ignorance rather than lack of capacity).

              There's also an apprentice of the House Fortunae financial planner and finance manipulation NPC member of the chantry who is a math genius. The apprentice, Linnea, has done little but a mathematical computation for the Akashic character on which Order of Hermes member will be admitted to the Council of 9 as their representative. Said Akashic wants to go to see mummies in Egypt and will win points for helping the House Shaea Primus become the Primus of the Order of Hermes. Linnea developed a good equation, but needs more data. I'm not sure how she'll end up. Part of my story for her is that with the rise of Augmented Reality and the realization the Internet is for summoning Basilisk and promoting evil, not just porn and cat lol memes, is that House Fortunae needs some skilled mathematicians to help them figure out the new Internet and its financial aspects to prepare for the coming cryptocurrency wars. . .

              We have an adventure about securing a Node to finish up, and then an adventure where the Verbena PC will be playing a benefit concert to aid the Big Springs Chantry and their goals in the Las Vegas area. I plan on acolytes being recruitable there--The Children of Springtime seem the most likely group to encounter.

              Back to the OP's question, Acolytes are going to be those NPCs associated with the PCs who believe in the magic they cast and the cause and work to assist the group. Acolytes could definitely make a game interesting, but whether they are worth developing in depth depends on how much the players intend to interact with them (unless you just enjoy creating characters, in which case, keep 'em coming).

                      The Akashic's apprentice was handing out flyers and posting flyers for the Akashic's meditation and yoga studio. I guess there's technically a few more associates, who are not Acolytes, in the campaign. The Master of the Chantry has 4 dots of Allies represented by mercenary fighters who come from families with decades of service to House Shaea. However, they are basically in the background to patrol the city and provide some protection on a larger scale. Most recently, the 4 Hermetic gunmen went to El Salvador to check on the Akashic's apprentice. The Akashic also has a servant, basically Alfred from Batman, running his business on a day-to-day basis. I make a 'job roll' each month for the Akashic's business and PC actions do affect it and determine if it was a good month or break-even month or bad business month.

                      I would think that if you have minor NPCs doing day to day stuff, they can provide some information or be scenery. Maybe they ask a mage how they are doing. How much you delve into the social life of NPCs is really going to depend on the scope of your game--if it is high adventure I doubt most players will want to spend a lot of time on what the NPC checking their equipment does as long as they are competent.
