    Originally posted by Aleph View Post
    Not long ago this would have been impossible because you needed the Traditions as a guide to understand what kind of mages you can make, but as Dataweaver says, this is made feasible thanks to M20 decoupling Tradition and Focus. That being said, I don't think that the Core should waste pages emphasizing the "importance" of Independants. Obviously they won't call themselves Orphans. That IS a derogatory (this ain't new, several old books already talk about this), and shouldn't appear in a book that's not written from the perspective of a larger faction. But that doesn't need to (and in my opinion shouldn't) mean that the factions, or the War, are somehow less important for the world.
    And this, mostly. In particular, my argument hasn't been that the core book should sing the praises of the mages formerly known as Orphans and denigrate the ones who have joined the Ascension War. But M20 is over 600 pages long, and that's way too Long for a core book; M5 is going to need to be shorter, which means a combination of off-loading a lot of material into supplements and ruthlessly streamlining what stays. And a major page-saver would be to put off most of the Ascension War and related factions material for supplements.

    And that leaves the default protagonists of the core book being the independent mages, with the Traditions, Technocracy, and Disparates appearing in a minimal-sketch form that's enough for gamers coming from earlier editions to be able to play them (which, again, is possible because the Arcane Approaches do the heavy lifting for everyone equally, whether they're Traditionists, Technocrats, Disparates, or none of the above) while gamers who are jumping on with M5 will get a chance to “get their feet wet” with mages who aren't immersed in the Ascension War before moving on to that later on — or not, if they decide that they're fine with things without it.

    And frankly, I don't consider “independent mages usually aren't Orphans” to be “emphasizing the ‘importance’ of independents”, especially since I don't think the point would need to be explicitly addressed. It would be handled implicitly, by the fact that the term Orphan wouldn't even appear in the core book (and note that I haven't suggested an alternate name; there oughtn't be a need for one) and by the fact that the earlier editions' factions would be presented as an optional element of the game rather than as a central feature.

These differences are likely because of a different vision by the prior IP owners when BNS licensed VtM - where they were fine doling out aspects of the game for different lines and worlds, letting OP do V20 and BNS do a LARP ruleset. Given that W5 is going in a direction that seems to line up with, or at least is parallel to, the setting established in the LARP rules, and given the trailers we've seen, the old divisions/subsplats for Werewolves likely still exist as clans still exist for vampires, so I doubt the traditions will go anywhere, but they definitely will be suffering in the wake of the Second Inquisition (possibly less than Vampires, though).

                                M5's Magick system needs to be as flexible as possible; one of the primary draws of the game is how versatile the magick system is. Some things I'd change about the current system:

                                First, swap the placement of Spheres and Focus in the “Casting Magick, Step by Step” section: Focus: How your character does it should be presented in Step One: Effect, as part of how you describe the Effect; and Spheres: The Foundation of Each Effect should be in Step Two: Ability as part of determining if you have what it takes to attempt the Effect. I'd also move the Wild Talent section from the end of the Sphere write-ups to the end of the Spheres: the Foundation of Each Effect part of Step Two: Ability, as Wild Talent isn't so much about defining Sphere-like traits as it is about knowing when it's appropriate to ignore the Sphere requirements for crafting an Effect and what to do instead.

                                Second, Step One: Effect should have a subsection about the magnitude of the Effect. This section would be where you discuss how many successes the Effect should require: that is, setting the Magnitude would answer the question of whether this is a Simple, Standard, Difficult, Impressive, Mighty, Outlandish, or Godlike Feat. This would also be where you point out the implications of a given Magnitude of Effect: Magnitudes below Mighty might be doable in a single roll; but if you don't want to risk it, you can cast them as Rituals, which count as rites that will take minutes to perform. Mighty, Outlandish, and Godlike Feats will almost certainly have to be cast as Rituals, with Mighty Feats counting as ceremonies that take hours to perform and Outlandish or Godlike Feats counting as Great Works that are measured in days. You don't have to go into the specific rules at this point; just indicate that if you don't think your mage can achieve the Effect in a single roll, expect that crafting the Effect will take a while.

                                The idea here is to bring Magickal Feats more into the forefront, rather than being something that's buried in the middle of Step Three: defining an Effect should involve deciding how “big” that Effect will be. In this same vein, I'd have M5's Effect write-ups include hope big of a Feat each Effect is: even if 99% of them say “Standard Feat”, it gets the reader in the habit of thinking not just in terms of how many Sphere levels are required, but also how many successes are needed.

                                Rotes, Procedures, and Other Prepared Effects and Knowing and Learning Rotes would go to Step Two: Ability, while Coincidental Magick vs. Vulgar Magick would move to Step One: Effect. Both sections would undergo some changes: I'd at least consider the possibility of replacing the Coincidental vs. Vulgar split with the Subtle/Uncanny/Extreme split that Brucato describes in Mage Made Easy. There would be other changes to the Paradox mechanics; but in Step One, the focus would solely be on deciding how subtle or blatant the Effect is.

                                In the Step Two: Ability subsection on Rotes, I would make the controversial decision to add system benefits for knowing a Rote, and an experience point cost for learning one. The fine points would be addressed later; but at this point in the process, the key feature would be that knowing our having access to a Rote can substitute for insufficient levels of Spheres and even Arete. That is, if you don't have enough dots in your Spheres to craft the Effect, but you do have access to the Effect as a Rote, you can still perform it. And if you have access to the Rote, your dice pool is the higher of your Arete or the highest Sphere in the Rote.

                                The primary motivation for this change is to provide an alternative to Sorcery, so that you don't need to write a massive new edition of Sorcerer in order to include acolytes and extraordinary citizens who know a trick or two (or three or four) but aren't Awakened. And the idea would be that the more you raise your Arete and Spheres, the less important Rotes become, possibly even to the point that you get a “refund” on the experience points you spent on them in the form of a discount to the cost of raising your Spheres to the level that the Rote is no longer needed.

                                I would split up the Mundane Skills and Magickal Effects section, with Abilities Enhancing Magick staying in Step Two: Ability while Magick Enhancing Abilities would be moved into the section in Common Magickal Effects — i.e., what can you do with Magick? I would also add an entry for “suitable Ability: -1 to -3” on the Magickal Difficulty Modifiers table, under Tools and Rituals, while streamlining its entry in Step Two to just “if you have dots in a suitable mundane ability, it can help you with the crafting of the Effect”, with the system for doing so being incorporated into…

                                Step Three: Roll
                                First, I'd scrap the notion that how subtle or blatant the Effect is has any bearing on how difficult the casting is; the differences between them would be handled solely by how much Paradox they generate. Set the Difficulty as “highest Sphere in the Effect + 4”. Second, I'd scrap the “modifiers can't adjust the modifier by more than ±3” — but I'd keep the Minimum Difficulty of 3, and reinforce it as a universal limit, not just “if the Effect is cast on Earth”.

                                Step Three is where the reference charts come in, and I'd want this section to have a paragraph for each one (with a handful of exceptions, like handling all of the Instrument-related modifiers together). The point is that there shouldn't be an entry on any of the Magickal Reference Charts that doesn't get addressed in Step Three: Roll.

                                I'd also rename the Magickal Feats chart as Magnitude, and revise it with two additional columns: a Difficulty Modifier column (which would replace the “outlandish to god-like feat” entry on the Magickal Difficulty Modifiers table) and a Ritual column (which would be one of rite, ceremony, or great work). The idea would be to put all of the system effects of choosing a particular Magnitude in one place.

                                A few entries on the Magickal Difficulty Modifiers chart would be revised:

                                • “Using personal item from target” would be moved to the Correspondence Sphere Ranges table, and would represent how the difficulty of the Effect is influenced by the level of Connection you establish with the subject, assuming that you incorporate even a single dot of Correspondence into the Effect (though a single dot wouldn't be enough to let you extend the range of your Effect; you'd get the Difficulty Modifier, but only if the subject is near enough to affect without Correspondence).

                                • “Spending Extra Time”: this ought to be -1 per additional turn on an instant crafting, per additional five minutes on a rite, per additional hour on a ceremony, and per additional five hours on a great work.

                                • “Domino Effect”: I'd scrap this, and replace it with a system where excessive use of subtle effects eventually start being counted as if they were uncanny and start generating Paradox.

                                The Degrees of Success table should revise Partial Success from “roughly half of the Suggested Successes” to “at least one success, but less than the Suggested Successes”. You might also need separate tables for instant effects and for Rituals, as “mage can keep going” doesn't really make sense for an instant Effect.

                                I'd rename the Base Damage or Duration table as the Base Potency table; and I'd add columns for Area and Targets. As with the current table, you only get one column as the Base for a given Effect. I'd also rename the Optional Dividing Successes Rule table as the Additional Aspects table, and add an entry for Area. I'd also scrap the “permanent” Duration, both in terms of Base Potency and Additional Aspects: if you want an Effect to be permanent, you need to use something like the “crafting a Wonder” rule.

                                I have some thoughts about the “I Disbelieve!” table and Countermagick; but I'll address that in a separate thread.

                                Paradox:
                                Replace the separate dice pool with Paradox dice, similar to the Hunger Dice in V5: if a Paradox die comes up as a 1 or 10, you get a Backlash, with each such die counting as one “Backlash Success”. (If you have more Paradox Dice than the dice pool you're supposed to roll, replace the extra Paradox Dice with “Pure Paradox Dice”: roll them along with the Paradox Dice, but ignore them when determining the roll's success or failure; only pay attention to them for the purpose of determining Backlash.)

                                The idea here is to streamline, and hopefully speed up, the casting-and-Backlash system by combining the two dice pools: assemble one pool, roll it, then read it twice, once for the Effect's successes and again for the Backlash.

                                For the effects of a Backlash, scrap the existing system entirely. Instead, the Storyteller comes up with an Effect conforming to the character's Focus and related to the Effect that the character was just trying to craft. An Onset of Quiet is a mind-altering Effect; Paradox Flaws are effectively Life, Matter, or Forces Effects; Paradox Spirits and Realms are Spirit Effects, based on summoning and stepping sideways respectively; Physical Backlash could be just about anything. The Storyteller shouldn't feel too constrained by the mage's Spheres; a Backlash is, in effect, a malicious Wild Talent, so he's justified in going a few dots beyond the mage if he thinks it to be appropriate.
