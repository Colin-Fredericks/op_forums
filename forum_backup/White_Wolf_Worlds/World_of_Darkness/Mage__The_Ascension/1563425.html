    Hot-Shotting

    08-24-2026, 03:54 PM
    We all know that Mage has a lot of weird rotes, often contradicting the core rules. I'd like to know if there is any rote that you feel is this way and what you do about it. Just ignore it?
    At the same time, I want to bring one present in M20:

    Hot-Shotting
    Anyone who’s ever watched a movie from the last few decades recognizes the ominous “CHK-CHK” of a cocked shotgun, the click of a pistol’s hammer, the “SHHHING” of a drawn blade, or the clack of a rifle-bolt slapping into place. By modifying her weaponry and then playing upon the psychological effect of media-instilled dread, a Technocratic operative can get her target to stand down, increase the efficiency of her attacks, or project a storm of fire so intense that only the strongest Reality Deviants can stand against it. System: A correctly trained operative – that is, one with the right Spheres – can use a simple Crafts roll to modify her weapon so that it makes more noise and gets a bit of extra kinetic kick. The Forces Sphere enhances both the sonic timbre of the sound and the kinetic damage of its strike. The resulting effects upon the target could be seen as psychological warfare, skillful applications of physics, or both. Each success reduces the difficulty for the agent’s next attack by -1, and adds one automatic success for the Forces Sphere. If she so chooses, the player may apply that bonus to an intimidation-based attack (to get the target to surrender without a fight); to damage (thus hitting the target harder than usual); to targets (allowing her to hit more than one of them with the next attack); or to a mixture of them all. For details, see the Optional Dividing Successes Rule, p. 538. The addition of Mind 2 turns the Hot-Shotting Adjustment into an explicit psychological threat. In this case, the ominous sound is enough to make most targets surrender right away. The player rolls against a difficulty of the target’s Willpower; if her successes equal or exceed the target’s Willpower, then the target gives up without a fight. If not… well, then, that first shot still has a lot more force to back up the agent’s argument.
    So, while default rules says that you can increase/decrease the damage of an attack with Forces at the rate of 1:1; or increase/decrease difficulties also at the rate of 1:1, this rote seems to have its own rule.

    Each success grants you a -1 diff reduction (although the defaul limit of -3 should apply) AND a bonus in damage, intimidation or targets in your next attack.

    Thoughts?

      Euthanatos Tradition Book has a couple of real jewels. Almost all have something.

      Here's some from Revised.

      Death in Life, among a tree of effects that don't make sense with the amount of successes required and such, allows you to halve incoming bashing damage

      This one it's interesting because it's ok to say that Spheres can change the nature of your body, and it will take the properties of whatever you morph into. You have to know that undead halve bashing damage

      Gift of Prana
      Each success creates a "pool" of two Health Levels, that then get spent when you suffer damage to heal you. It's understood as an enchantment (like a Charm?), and if it was ruled like a Wonder it would be ok, but it isn't using Wonder creation rules nor it's considered a Wonder.

      The mere idea that this sort of "complex programmed effect" it's possible w/o spending all you need to spend to create a Wonder...

      On 2 edition

      Burning Lotus
      Sacrifices the mage on a big explosion that disintegrates everything on it's aoe (about 22 feet per success - which it's ruled ... where?), then as a reward for dying it doesn't just cause the normal damage but adds one extra health level of damage per point of Quintessence on your Quintessence Wheel.

      In general I treat them as "secrets of the trade" of the Tradition (or group of mages). Stuff that only they know how to do. And if a player wants to create something like that, it would have to be a Rote carefuly studied to the effect (aka, give me time to think)

      But it annoys me.

      It like: Yeah, Mage has rules - but they don't do what we want so we will ignore them or make something up. And we won't say how you can do the same, just have some imagination.

      Some "feel" like *hidden rules* - like you just *have to know* that if you give your body properties of the undead then you halve bashing (and what about treating bullets as bashing? - that's not how undead work on Mage?). Don't try to extrapolate from this that you can do a different spell to halve Lethal or Aggravated.

      An interesting "hidden rule" it's how numerous Rotes that cause damage at level 5 (Antimater from the Etherites and the Dragon Fire from Cruzade are examples, if I'm not mistaken) cause more damage than what the table indicates. This is sort of a necessity because otherwise spells that require higher level Spheres would cause less damage (because of the increased base difficulty) - but of course nothing in the rules suggests this is the case (1e did had rules on this regard, and so does DA: Mage).

                  Hot Shotting is confusing in its verbiage but I think the problem is two words.

                  Originally posted by M20 Hot Shotting
                  Each success reduces the difficulty for the agent’s next attack by -1, and adds one automatic success for the Forces Sphere. If she so chooses, the player may apply that bonus to an intimidation-based attack (to get the target to surrender without a fight); to damage (thus hitting the target harder than usual); to targets (allowing her to hit more than one of them with the next attack); or to a mixture of them all. For details, see the Optional Dividing Successes Rule, p. 538.
                  The problem words are "that bonus" because it makes the bit about damage and targets sound like something it is not.. Really, damage and targets just come from the Optional Dividing Successes rule. If that rule is in play the mage is already able to spend successes on targets or damage. "That bonus" makes it sound like the reduced difficulty is somehow buying extra targets or damage.

                  Tell me, if instead of saying "that bonus" the section said "those successes," how would you interpret the rote?

                  I'd actually say the harder part is the next bit with the optional Mind addition, because that makes it sound like you first make a mental attack, but if that fails (or even if it doesn't) you get to use the same successes to boost the shot.

                  There's a similar problem in the next rote, Hands of Death. The basic rules are using Life or Forces or both in increase the damage of a punch and the mechanics are adding damage to a punch as per the Damage and Duration table. No problem there.

                  However, the optional Entropy add on lets the mage exploit weak points to reduce armor, durability, or soak by one level per success. Here there is no mention of splitting successes, so does each success do damage AND remove one level of armor, durability, or soak?

                          There's nothing in the description of rotes for previous editions or M20 that indicates they are inherent more powerful or have a different system for using successes. The benefits mostly center around avoiding the fast-cast penalty and naturally working well with the user's paradigm.

                          While M20 and other editions will often state different options for spending successes on effects (reducing or raising the difficulty of another roll, subtracting or adding successes to another roll, damage, duration, range, etc.) I don't think any of them have ever comprehensively set out a complete list or general rules for how these interact.

                          One further issue is that often extra spheres are added in for seemingly good reasons, but without being flexible with success use we can't represent those reasons mechanically. The 'Entropy to exploit weak points' idea we just saw in Hands of Death is a good example, because if we just use the standard Violence as a Focus rules, it doesn't do anything.

                          We can see this on pg 413, where Secret Agent John Courage's Stone Shattering Fist-Strike adds in Entropy 1, but just uses the basic success rules for damage, which is the same result as if the spell didn't include Entropy.

                          But in other places, we can see the effect doubling up, such as in the Hands of Death rote, where successes are counted twice.

                          Finally, we can see what I think is the most reasonable method, which is counting the successes towards the application like normal, but adding one extra effect for the use the Sphere. This I see on on page 440 where "Damage-inflicting effects from the Entropy, Matter, and Prime Spheres undermines the Durability trait- Entropy by exploiting weak points..." Here, in addition to the damage, Durability (an object's soak) is also reduced, but just by a single -2, it doesn't scale with # of successes.
