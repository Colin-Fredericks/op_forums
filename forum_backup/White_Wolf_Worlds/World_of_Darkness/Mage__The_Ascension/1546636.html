    Rebel Spirit

    12-05-2024, 08:25 AM
    Hello.

    One of my players defeated a werewolf antagonist and claimed her fetish: a rifle imbued with an electricity elemental. To make things not that easy, I made the spirit rebel against the character since it doesnt recognize him as the true owner of the fetish.

    It would be cool if the character earns the spirit's respect somehow (he doesn't have enough dots in Spirit to force it), but Im out of ideas about the "how".

    What kind of challenge or test could the spirit accept to cooperate?

      I think the most straightforward approaches are bargaining and chiminage. But I think I think the more fun approach is to forge a relationship with the spirit through actions. This has a lot of potential because spirits are not human and may appreciate some strange things.

      For instance, if the spirit is one of lightning, it may respond positively if the mage yells at the fetish frequently. The more volume and bluster the better. The mage becomes the thunder and the lightning spirit finds this comforting.

      A more mechanical version of this is that the mage has to, on three occasions, make a loud and attention grabbing noise at a dramatically inappropriate time. The noise has to play a significant role in the scene and there can be no attempt to hide it or mitigate it. It can be played for advantageous way, such as for intimidation or distraction, but I wouldn't tell the player that. I'd suggest once in a tense social situation, once in a stealth situation, and once in combat. Once this happens the spirit will respect the mage.

      An electricity spirit might be a bit of a prankster and so engaging in an audatious prank war might gain its respect. This could be with another person or, if the mage is creative enough, even the fetish itself. If the mage is clever with his own pranks and respectfully bears the pranks of others, the spirit may be impressed. When the mage has punished an enemy with a prank, taught a friend with a prank, and either stoically bears a prank or learns a lesson from one, the spirit will respect him.

                • Survive a thunderstorm while using the environment to their advantage.
                • Safely redirect lightning or use an electrical device creatively in a dangerous situation.

              2. Defend the Spirit’s Honor
              • Challenge: The spirit was likely bound to the werewolf for a reason. The character could investigate the spirit's past and uncover its grievances or commitments. Perhaps an old enemy or unresolved duty still lingers, and completing this task earns the spirit's trust.
              • Goal: Show the spirit they are a worthy successor to its previous master.
              3. Prove Worth Through a Duel
              • Challenge: The spirit could manifest a temporary avatar (or use the rifle against the character in a controlled way) to test their skill. The character must fight or outwit the spirit without harming it.
              • Goal: Demonstrate courage, adaptability, and respect in combat.
              4. Make a Personal Sacrifice
              • Challenge: The spirit might demand the character give up something meaningful—a personal item, a deep secret, or time and effort in a task symbolic of humility and dedication to their bond.
              • Goal: Show the spirit they value the partnership over personal gain.
              5. Recharge the Bond
              • Challenge: Since the spirit is tied to electricity, it might demand the character reconnect it to a powerful natural or mystical source, like:

                  The spirit may have agreed to be bound into the fetish if the werewolf would accomplish something that enhanced the spirit's purview. Doing something to facilitate that goal would go a long way.

                  Again, spirits can have strange motivations and oddly specific purviews, so the sky is the limit. Anything from "change all the lightbulbs on two blocks of Main Street from fluorescents to LEDs" to "Re-enact the epic love story of two teenagers from rival families torn apart by generations of feuding. She's direct current, he's alternating current, can their love survive? Music by AC/DC."

                  Yes, I wrote that second one to be funny, but it strangely has a lot of potential. And no, that was not meant to be a pun.

                  Depending on how said play needs to be re-enacted, the mage might have to:
                  • Find the lost manuscript
                  • Find a venue
                  • Find appropriate actors
                  • Properly wire the stage (each actor can only be lit by lights using the current type of their respective family)
                  • Get rid of meddling safety inspectors (because that wiring is not going to be to code)
                  • Stop a rival spirit from messing up the production.
                  • Etc.
                  That rival spirit one is particularly important because it's probably the biggest issue. While it is somewhat funny to imagine werewolves trying to manage stage production, they'd have the spiritual security locked down. Your mage won't.



                  I seriously did not intend for this post to be 90% a pitch for a behind-the-scenes production drama about a ridiculous concept. That probably won't fit most games. What the spirit wants doesn't have to be silly, though. It probably will be strange, if only because the spirit is not human and doesn't think like a human. Most importantly, though, it should be tricky and have a high chance of causing unexpected problems.

                      Without using a "brute force" approach involving higher level Spirit magicks, this sort of thing would be very much a role playing matter with no "standard" answer. Spirits might be very single-minded entities, especially so when it comes to elementals, but many aren't unintelligent beings without personalities of their own. A good example of this would be the differences between Grandfather Thunder and Typhon. They're very close in nature, Typhon actually being a sort of offshoot of Grandfather Thunder, but their motivations and personal ethics differ considerably.

                      It's also worth considering how much experience this character has in dealing with spirits at all. That's fairly specialized knowledge, represented by a few skills, which really figures into that character's experiences and background. Gaining this new possession might spur new character development, as he learns more about certain aspects of reality in the process of getting it to work for him! He could consult on it, and might get very different sorts of answers and advice, depending on who he asks. Dreamspeakers and many Hermetics both deal a good bit with spirits, but in very different ways overall. Some Etherites would gleefully lend themselves to the examination of such an item, but might not be able to offer any practical advice.

                                          Previous to M20 there were a number of highly specialized knowledge skills which dealt with different sorts of spirits. Personally, I liked this, as I consider highly specialized areas of knowledge to be good foundations for fleshing out character backgrounds. Now they're mostly covered by Esoterica, Occult, Cosmology and Enigmas specialties. and I suppose it's enough that some of these apply more readily to different sorts of spirits than others. Specialties in different areas of anthropology could also apply meaningfully when dealing with wraiths of different backgrounds. We have only one character in either of my Mage games who deals with the restless dead much, and she's a Verbena who mostly reserves that for the ancestors of those belonging to her coven, and generally only at ceremonially appropriate times.

                                          Some mentors and masters may also be very cautious about teaching their students to interact with spirits, or with the different spirit worlds in general. A mage can get themselves into worse trouble with these sorts of workings than most others, and a teacher would be entirely wise to make very sure their student knows exactly what they're doing in this area. An example of this is one Hermetic character, who has been trained intensively as a seer. The reason why his master has been slow and reluctant to begin his training in Mind and Spirit studies is that the student shares an unfortunate Curiosity flaw with his master's former master, who ended up turning barabbus after his astral voyages resulted in an encounter with something he was unable to cope with.
