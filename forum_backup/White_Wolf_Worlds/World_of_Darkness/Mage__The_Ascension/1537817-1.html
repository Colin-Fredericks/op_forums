            The way I see it, Correspondence Alone is very clearly stated in exactly the position in the book a Correspondence rule should be. It has a consistent logic behind it, it tracks with the stated powers of Correspondence and the general approach to calculating requirements. The contradicting statements are harder to find and lack explanation.

            Now M20 is a bit of a mess. This is hardly the only time contradictions and ambiguities arise. Like I said earlier, I think the magic rules were overhauled at least once during writing and a lot of artifacts of the older rules were missed in editing. Over time I've developed a few guidelines for working through the mess. There are three relevant ones here:

            1) Spheres should be able to accomplish their primary abilities without help. When Entropy 4 is given as the level for Altering Life, it can Alter Life. It doesn't need help from the Life sphere. Any exceptions should be clear and easy to understand.

            2) Mages don't need Pattern Spheres to target. That is, a mage can cast a Forces based pushing spell on objects without using Matter and on creatures without using Life. The same is true for most if not all effects. Locking Spheres are a thing, but Targeting Spheres are not.

            3) When dealing with ambiguities, give more weight to the lowest reasonable sphere requirements.

            For all of these reasons, I use the whole pattern rules on Page 513 and I sideline or ignore contrary statements elsewhere.
