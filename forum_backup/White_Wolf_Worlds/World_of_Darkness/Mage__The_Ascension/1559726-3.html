    Originally posted by DarkKing View Post
    See, that's something I just can't accept, or wrap my head around. Consensus has to be true, as there's no other explanation for why the Traditions would have fallen into decline, while the Technocracy rose to power.

    It had to be the result of a paradigm shift in humanity's collective viewpoint. Nothing else makes sense.
    ​
    Do you know every other possible explanation to say that?

    Simplest explanation possible: not everything is Mages.

    Traditions and Technocracy aren't the only Mages around to begin with, and Mages aren't the only (or main) force in the universe.

    If the Consensus is real, it's still coming from the masses of Sleepers and not from Mages, so the Union may just have overestimated how much it was their work rather than humanity doing it by itself and the Order of Reason just got lucky.

    But if it isn't... Something else is happening to reality and Mages blundered by thinking they're so important while the actual problem is out there. Not a terribly hard concept, really, the idea that reality is its own thing and things may happen because of something else.

    As for evidence:

    - Paradox, Consensus and Backlashes are NOT constant forces in the setting. The rules of reality are changing with time since way before there was an Order of Reason. This is not just rules of the game, but in-lore changes in reality itself and how it reacts to magic, that Mages felt and wrote about. At least 4 changes have been recorded, for a total of 5 eras so to speak.

    - the masses are demonstrably superstitious, religious, averse to the scientific method even when embracing scientific discoveries and full of magical thinking. There was no reason for the Consensus to follow a scientific Paradigm when the reality is that most people think bad things happen because their gods are angry.

    Don't forget that if reality is shaped by human belief, then the most powerful countries in the world are China and India, since they each have more people than the whole of Europe and North America combined.

    Social control and Reality Zones are still real reasons to fight, though.

                                                    DA Mage let you get dice-pools up faster, but the final end-points are pretty much the same; so learning magic was - at least by the mechanics - much easier/faster but not necessarily less difficult to do. DA Mage also let you buy Pillars higher than your Foundations which added to potential difficulties as those mages could learn magic before they had the self-mastery to apply smoothly. Learning faster didn't make magic easier, but generally harder as it opened mages up to more avenues of hubris.

                                                    It also shifted who decides "whether or not something should be possible." In DA, the mage themselves is the most potent source of that. The Foundation/Pillar system means mages have whole effects they couldn't learn without the intense and arduous process of learning Pillars from other Fellowships because that's what it would take to expand their personal ideas of possible beyond their starting Pillars conceptualizations of what magic can do.

                                                    During the Sorcerer's Crusade (and I feel it's worth noting that the Order of Reason and the Technocracy have been conflated a lot in this discussion, but there are meaningful differences between them esp. regarding their views on what Consensus even is and what to do with it), Reality Zones became the dominant factor of what counts as possible. This very much lays the groundwork for the Ascension War because the Order of Reason couldn't really execute its desires in light of how much power this gave to the newly formed Traditions. Most of the Traditions came out of regional groups which meant they had immense home court advantage when in friendly Reality Zones. The want to homogenize humanity to break down the Reality Zones and make the Traditions less able to dig in and endure lead to the eventual restructuring of the OoR into the Technocracy.

                                                    It's only after European colonialism (and esp. the sun never slept on the British Empire) largely succeeded in forcing most of the globe into a more shared reality with the Technocracy's aid that humanity as a whole started becoming the primary arbiter of possible. But even then Reality Zones are just reduced, not fully eliminated.

                                                    Those shifts in who the primary determiner of "possible" was also follows the trends in what happened if you violated possibility. DA resulted in a very clear, "I must have screwed up and now magical energy is wrecking myself or my stuff," as it was more personal. In SC scourging followed the logic of the local Reality Zones. Modern times as phrased above... things just got weirder because things got more abstract from so many individuals giving "feedback" at once.

                                                    This is also mirrored in other parts of the setting's metaphysics. In DA mages didn't even know about Avatars because Avatars were so seamlessly merged to the mages that there was no perception of them as a separate being; also leading to a lot of confusing them for souls or similar concepts. In SC Avatars start to become distinct but still fairly consistent in manifestations by local standards. By the modern era Avatars are weird with little rhyme or reason to the vast variety in ways they appear to their mages.
