                    Originally posted by TravisLegge View Post

                    I'd submit that all this talk of a mage who "just wants to learn necromancy and nothing else" is (consciously or not) trying to shove Mage into a standard fantasy box, and it's just not that kind of game. Mages don't approach magick in those terms in M:TA, because *that's not how magick works.*

                    Paradigms can (and should) limit how you manifest effects, absolutely. That's why there's a benefit of casting aside tools as your Arete increases. But a lot of the vitriol here seems to be setting up a strawman that Mage never claimed would stand in the setting, then tearing it down angrily.
                    Agreed. While it's very possible for a Mage to pursue, for example, Necromancy as a specialty, this doesn't mean they couldn't or wouldn't learn magical tricks outside that narrow box. Partly because pre-modern magical practitioners kept ALL their knowledge close to their chest, so any given Mage took whatever knowledge they could get. And partly because their Focus isn't a narrow band of thematically related powers (even Sorcerer Paths aren't always that, as evidenced by the flexible nature of Hellfire), but a holistic approach to viewing and interacting with the world around them.

                    To be a Mage is to adhere to a lifestyle that carries with it both a broad understanding of their Reality, and a certain measure of responsibility. Dreamspeakers and Verbana are often most associated with this, as they have a duty to both their communities/families and to the spirits/old gods. Not to mention the practical applications of their Magick, for themselves, which leads them to learn a broad swathe of Rotes.

                    For Necromancy, you might look at the Euthanatos Mage. They may specialize in dealing with the dead, whether their spirits or their corporeal remains. But they're also interested heavily in Fate. Learning how to read and manipulate Fate as it pertains to mortality, by necessity of their world view, also means acquiring the tools and framework for reading and manipulating Fate in other ways. These are not necessarily extricable, because the mystics and culture broadly that developed these tools did not neatly divide one application from another, nor consider it advantageous to do so. If your forebears went to the trouble learning how to reanimate a corpse via mastery of the principles of life (or Life), they wouldn't balk at using those same principles to heal the wounds and illnesses of themselves and others. Nor at using them to empower their bodies.

                    Euthanatoi, as a Tradition, is also very interested in employing their Magick for the purposes of giving the Good Death. Whether that's helping a sick person find rest, or eliminating a wicked person so they can no longer do harm. There are many ways in which learning the fundamentals of their Focus, for use in interacting with the dead, could also be employed to MAKE people dead. Since giving the Good Death is considered a utility/responsibility unto itself, no Euthanatos would balk at learning any tool they can to accomplish it. (Indeed, the Chakravanti consider over-reliance on death Magick to be dangerous, as it can lead to Jhor. So being "more balanced" is advantageous in itself).

                    The strength of being a Mage is in not just having a hammer, and seeing everything as a nail.

                    (On the flipside, if a Mage or their Player WANTS to focus their knowledge of a given Sphere only on one application, for thematic reasons, there's nothing stopping them from doing so. It just becomes an in-universe and out-of-universe choice. If the Necromancer wanted to gain dots in Time solely to see into the past or future, or to age someone to dust, that's fine. It just shouldn't be seen as a failure of the Sphere system if you think all your powers should be in one progression track. If that's what you want, create a few Minor Spheres related to Necromancy.)

                                                    I think you're all too fixated on my 2 simple examples, but those were just the first 2 that came in my mind. And you're too focused on the idea that "Necromancy" or "Alchemy" are almost just an RPG trope or even a simple "style" - which isn't exactly true, after all there are many Thanatoic religions throughout the world that have their worldview based on what we could describe as "Necromancy" for simplistic reasons, and "Alchemy" is not merely trying to turn lead into gold, but a rather metaphysical worldview about the changes in the cosmos. So no, they aren't exactly "just sorcery material", but ok, I understand where you're coming from.

                                                    The 2 unique Spheres of the Technocracy are other examples however.

                                                    I can also come up with other easily understandable examples, and examples that BREAK THE 9 SPHERES WHILE BEING COMPLETELY CANON.

                                                    2 examples: Space/Time "Sphere" and "Biochemistry Sphere".

                                                    I'll explain: imagine an "Enlightened Scientist" that sees Space and Time as fundamentally the same. So, for this Techie boy (or maybe, Techie-mancer), there is no "Correspondence" or "Time" Spheres, but rather he studies the "Sphere of Space/Time".

                                                    And how could that keep up with canon?

                                                    Simple. The player tells the ST that "my guy sees Space and Time as the same thing".

                                                    And, to that, the ST simply answers "Very well. Therefore, you can NOT buy Correspondence or Time individually, you HAVE TO buy a level on BOTH any time you wish to increase your knowledge about Space/Time".

                                                    But wait, the player himself can do it, even if the ST himself dislikes the idea; the player can simply state "Because my Enlightened Scientist believes that Space and Time are one and the same, I'll only ever buy a level on either Correspondence or Time it I simultaneously also buy the same level on the other Sphere".

                                                    BAM. You just demolished the idea that the cosmos has a metaphysics that is divided into 9 Spheres; essentially, you reduced the number of Spheres to 8, while having one of them that is harder to grasp/learn (because the "hacked" Sphere of "Space/Time" costs double the XP - but XP is also just another game mechanics abstraction).

                                                    The same kind of hack could be applied to an Enlightened Scientist that believes that "Matter" and "Life" are fundamentally the same - chemical reactions with different degrees of complexity. Thus, the player could also "hack the system" in order to create a "phantom Sphere" of "Biochemistry".

                                                    And that's the thing, Spheres are just game mechanics abstractions, they are NOT the "laws of the cosmos".

                                                    The other example we could "hack" in the exactly same way without moving even a millimeter (or an inch for my American friends) from official canon, would be to create an amalgamation Sphere of Mind+Spirit for my Mystical Mage that sees no distinctions between mind and spirits... For my Mage, a person's Mind IS HIS SOUL, and Spirits are essentially "ephemeral Minds". My previous example on the 3 Spheres for Ecstatics and Dreamspeakers illustrate this quite well - and hell, the idea of them having only the 3 Spheres of Body, Mind and Sprit can also be "hacked" in much the same way.

                                                    So, we actually CAN use the abstractions of the game mechanics in order to change the number of Spheres the game has, but this is limited to only grouping the existing ones to form new ones. Space/Time can be created by purchasing Time+Correspondence on a single package, Biochemistry by buying Life+Matter, and the single "Mind=Spirit" Sphere by buying those together. Since it's all just abstractions, that's not just feasible, but it also strictly follows the holy canon laws to the letter.

                                                    I'm simply stating that this same idea should be applied in a much broader range, for example, instead of creating new Spheres by merging the existing ones, to allow this process to be done by splitting them or taking few pieces of several different ones into one single amalgamation; Necromancy, for instance, could be the amalgamation of small pieces from Spirit, Entropy, Prime, Matter, Life (and perhaps others).

                                                    That's all that I'm suggesting, an expansion on the scope and the capabilities described by the game mechanics abstractions that the official Spheres represent.

                                                    Another example that I really like, is the "Minor Sphere" of "Memetics". Initially called "Dogma" (Sorcerors Crusade), then called "Propaganda", and now called "Memetics". This is the study of how ideas are spread throughout societies, and thus to use that knowledge to influence it. That's the main "secret weapon" of the Technocracy on my games, and their mastery of this Science is how they managed to get on top of the Ascension War.

                                                    Mind would be a poor fit for it, since you can't really affect millions of individuals all at once (since you would need millions of successes per RAW). The only more or less adaptable kind of effects for this would require, well, Entropy 5!

                                                    Instead, I use the Sphere of "Memetics". Bam, problem solved. An entire Sphere dedicated solely for the brainwashing of entire societies through the "magikal" use of mass propaganda. It also makes the Technocracy so much more scary and pervasive in the game. And you won't have master propagandists in the Union who also happen to be "mister Lucky Agent" because of the silly requirement of also needing to have everything else that comes with Entropy 5 just as a prerequisite to become a "master propagandist" in the first place.

                                                    That's the kind of mechanics that I want, and I see no reason as to why those can't or shouldn't be implemented, particularly, as I said, you can already merge Spheres together on a practical manner if you wish so
