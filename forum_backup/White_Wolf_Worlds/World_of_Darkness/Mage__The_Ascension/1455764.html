    Mars, terraforming versus human genetic engineering

    07-29-2021, 05:35 PM
    If colonizing Mars is possible, more so in a universe of Magick, a choice presents itself between two methods. Somehow less controversial is the idea of completely transforming the planet to suit humans as we are, a mega-industrial project. The one that makes the masses go, "Ick!", is the idea of genetically engineering a species of humans who are adapted to the conditions there. In Mage society, which method would each individual Convention and Tradition favor? Which would oppose the adventure in any form?

    Thank you for passing time with me in conversation. My Hacks.

          - Iteration X views might be similar to those of Progenitors in prefering to tailor the people to the enviroment - through cybernetics in their case, obviously, what should lead to competition with the Progenitors over what method is more suited to the situation, practical to the Conventions resources and so on.

                              There's actually a third way to colonize Mars, albeit as an extension of the first: Para-terraforming.

                              Which is to say, creating enclosed environments on Mars that are livable, and just expanding that until most of the planet's usable surface is enclosed. The whole "live under pressurized domes" model, except on a larger scale. That, or having everyone live in tunnels underground.

                              This is a far more achievable process, that won't take millennia to play out. It's a matter of construction, not of slowly altering atmospheres (not to mention it doesn't require creating a worldwide magnetic field, the lack of which is the cause of Mars losing its atmosphere to solar wind in the first place). It's still a massive engineering project, except to a certain extent it's not. Rather, it could be a series of smaller projects that can be built on over time. You start by building a few small bases, perfect the technology needed to keep it all contained, then add onto those bases or built more.

                              In this light, I imagine paraterraforming would be of great interest to Iteration X. Specifically the Macrotechnicians, for whom large engineering projects are their jam.

                              Moreover, paraterraforming is not, as a rule, mutually exclusive to the other two methods of colonization. It's a closed environment, meaning it doesn't stop them from either terraforming in the traditional sense, or of modifying people to live in the original Martian environment. Obviously, you would still need to choose one or the other, but paraterraforming can be a concurrent project that doesn't hinder either. Indeed, it's a lot easier to terraform if you've got bases on Mars already, that can slowly release amounts of air. The only terraforming method that paraterraforming wouldn't be good with are the ones that involve nukes.

                              Yes, there are serious talks about using nuclear explosions to facilitate terraforming.

                                                  Scientifically speaking:

                                                  Terraforming isn't feasible. Despite being actively researched, even in a best case scenario it would be a thousands of years undertaking under current scientific understanding. The problem is the sheer amount of additional matter and energy to make it and kickstart a new climate cycle. Altering humans to live under common Martian conditions indefinitely isn't feasible, either, it lacks fundamental conditions for life. Covering the entire planet in domes is actually worse than Terraforming it, instead of initiating a process you're going to make a planet-size building?

                                                  From a practical perspective those options are thought exercises more than anything else for the foreseeable future. But building there in small scale is doable and more than enough for a colony (seriously, even for the entire human race, we're many, but on a planet scale not THAT many), so establishing a city isn't too much of a stretch. The major problem would be acclimating to low gravity, so curiously genetic engineering would still be necessary.

                                                  And since there is still much to be learned from Mars, no scientist really wants to make any of this in too large a scale, anyways.

                                                  Now, from Mages' perspectives:

                                                  - The Technocratic Union would want to establish research colonies in a model similar to the one stated above, so to not stretch too much the Consensus. Those colonies would have some Enlightened Science, but would mainly serve as the next step in the Pogrom;

                                                  - Astral travelers of several Traditions would want to secure colonies to help them protect their interests on Martian soil, that would probably boil down to preservation;

                                                  - Other Traditions would be likely to develop an interest in Mars when the travels begin in earnest, to explore, develop more their Paradigms and establish strategical positions;

                                                  - Etherites will try to Terraform anyway.

                                                  One important factor to keep in mind is the ease to establish a local Paradigm when far from big population centers. Mars is as far as it gets. Be prepared for colonies that completely ignore things like air pressurization, preferring to breath the healthy Martian air, and actually being healthy that way.

                                                  Interactions may also change considerably depending on the situation on the colonies at a given time. Like an alliance between Progenitors and Dreamspeakers to fight the destructive secret drilling joint project between the Order of Hermes and the Syndicate.

                                                  Mars is likely to be very similar to the overall situation in space beyond the first Horizon even when it slips to within the Consensus, the difference being using rules for the Material Realm instead of the Umbra.

                                                      Bear in mind that Mars has a more complex Umbral landscape than most of the planets, with only Earth outdoing it and only Venus rivaling it. It's explicitly stated in Infinite Tapestry that most of the creatures and environments featured in early science fiction make their abode in either the Penumbra or the Umbra; and the Traditions who visit Mars are more likely to interact with this aspect of Mars than with the cold, dead world that the Technocracy is likely going to deal with. Infinite Tapestry does its usual thing of pointing out that is an interesting place to visit, but don't try to live there. Depending on the story you want to tell, you might want to be a bit more lenient — say, by giving Etherites a Spirit technique that lets them set up bases in the Martian Penumbra that, when properly maintained, can stave off Disembodiment indefinitely.

                                                      Meanwhile, there are the Earthly Foundations (well, Martian Foundations) to consider: I suspect that even as far out as Mars, you don't get to shift the Reality Zone to the point that the Martian environment is hospitable; not without going into the Penumbra, as mentioned above. Ever since the divide between Etherspace and Conventional Space was pushed out beyond the opportunity of Mars (a process that probably started with the Void Engineer Tychoides at the start of the 20th century), the idea of a habitable colony on the material surface of Mars without substantial life support technology has been Vulgar and likely resistant to paradigm manipulation (that is, you don't get to make it Coincidental just by importing a bunch of colonists who have been indoctrinated to believe that the air on Mars is breathable, or more generally that the environment can support life). Prior to that, all of Mars was “submerged” in the Umbra, and there was no cold, dead surface to visit, unless deliberate efforts were made to force a region to separate out its Conventional Space, as per a Procedure to that effect that can be found in the Void Engineers Revised Convention Book.

                                                      But that's effectively ancient history now; since at least the 1950s, the Spatial Horizon has extended out to the Asteroid Belt, and the inner planets (Mars included) have had surfaces in Conventional Space that are hostile to life.
