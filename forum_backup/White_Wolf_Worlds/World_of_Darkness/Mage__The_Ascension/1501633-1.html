            I think you might be thinking of the Albigensians.

            The Gnostics were a pre-Christian group with a tendency for borrowing imagery and names from existing religions. Some branches of gnosticism (there were quite a few) did profess that the material was evil and the spiritual good. However, the Gnostics were largely on the way out by 250, well before the Church was in a position to persecute anyone (or could be called Roman for that matter).

            The Albigensians were a heretical Christian sect (or extra-Christian religion, depending on who you talk to) from 12th century France. They professed the physical to be entirely evil and in so doing denied a number of core Christian beliefs such as the incarnation of Christ and the resurrection of the body. They also thought sex and marriage were evil (Marriage is a sacrament in the Catholic Church) and suicide was good.

            They were indeed declared heretics and excommunicated, more because of the aforementioned denials of doctrine than anything they said about the Church itself. They were also persecuted, though that was driven as much by the political ambitions of the French and English nobility (see Land Snatching) as any desire to combat heresy.



            All that aside, the Albigensian idea that the physical world is fundamentally evil (and especially their alleged promulgation of suicide as a right and proper means to escape it) could also work as a Nephandic motivation.
