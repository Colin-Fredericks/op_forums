    So, I'd like to have some opinions, if possible, because this is crazy...

    10-03-2024, 08:54 AM
    On one of my chronicles of Sorcere's Crusade, a mage is in love a vampire lady. He adores her, but he also wants to have an heir and she can't possibly give him one (for obvious reasons, if I'm not mistaken there's only a way for a vampire to have a baby "normally" that is with the merit Blasphemous pact). So, the player came to me with a plan to collect what would be called nowadays "his dna" and hers and somehow insert their "combined biological material" in another woman to have their children.

    I mean it's absolutely crazy, but as much as I like crazy ideas, I told him no at first and then told him I would consult with other experiencd ST like myself to see if anyone would have a favourable opinion to this crazy idea. As much as I find the idea interesting in a crazy way, I don't see how it would fit to any paradigm of the 16th century (where the story takes place).

    What do you people think about it?

          This is quite possible in the paradigm of the time. While DNA was not known, Aristotle posited that a male's seed mixed with a sort of substrate secreted by a female to produce offspring. For males, that was semen, for females, menses. So as an alternative to using time magic, if his lover can have a period, he could do a ritual to mix those two substances and implant it in a surrogate mother. That's Life 5, if he doesn't have to filter out vitae. But he may have to prevent the surrogate mother from influencing the child. At least during later periods it was believed the mother's thoughts and feelings during pregnancy could influence the child. So you'd need a Life 4/Mind 1 effect to block out that influence, and possibly Mind 3 to connect his lover to the child.

              What you are describing is a homunculus, a sort of mainstay of alchemy for several hundred years. The term was first used by the Swiss philosopher, physician, and alchemist Paracelsus in his 1537 work De Natura Rerum, but the concept probably predates that.

              That the sperm of a man be putrefied by itself in a sealed cucurbit for forty days with the highest degree of putrefaction in a horse's womb ["venter equinus", meaning "warm, fermenting horse dung"], or at least so long that it comes to life and moves itself, and stirs, which is easily observed. After this time, it will look somewhat like a man, but transparent, without a body. If, after this, it be fed wisely with the Arcanum of human blood, and be nourished for up to forty weeks, and be kept in the even heat of the horse's womb, a living human child grows therefrom, with all its members like another child, which is born of a woman, but much smaller.

                      Not only would the male mortal's semen need to fertilize the female vampire's egg, they would need to be able to remove it from the vampire and implant it within another living woman's uterus. For me, there are just too many steps, and those steps have some unknowns that would need to be settled or risks eliminated before it could work. So rather than bother, I'd just say "no" were I the ST.

                      First, neither male or female vampires have any living sperm or eggs. They would have died when they died/became vampires. Woman never create new eggs (ok, there is some evidence this may not be true, but it's really speculation). They just have those that they are already born with. Men do create new sperm, but sperm take 50-60 days to develop. So I don't think Domino of Life would work in that regard as it takes effect for only one day. I understand some STs might want to handwave that, but I think that is a very different situation than saying you can emulate eating, breathing, live flesh tone, etc. which seems like a weaker version of vampires on the path of humanity engaging in the Blush of Life.

                      Second, even if you assume the vampire's existing sperm or eggs can be used, won't there be a significant chance that it's so damaged from the trauma of being undead that either it is useless or will result in some terrible defect? If living women who wait too long to have children end up not becoming pregnant even though they still have eggs, what must it be like for (dead) eggs stuck in a vampire's dead body?

                      Third, how are you going to make sure the egg gets fertilized? In actual IVF procedure a doctor takes multiple eggs in order to make sure that even one can become fertilized. And fertilization is not guaranteed even with normal sex. There is a good chance this will fail even if you allow it.

                      Fourth, how are you going to retrieve the fertilized egg (or even a theoretically healthy but unfertilized egg for later fertilization) without damaging it? Such surgeries would be common place and safely and routinely done by trained medical professionals with lots of modern equipment. How can this be done in the era of the Sorcerer's Crusade even assuming the vampire cannot be harmed during the process.

                      Fifth, how are you going to do the embryo transfer? Again, this requires a degree or medical knowledge, training, and equipment that I do not believe exists in the era of the Sorcerer's Crusade. And unlike concerns about the vampire's health that you can dismiss, this procedure could very well kill the surrogate.

                      Some STs can always come up with excuses for these objections, but for me there are just too many unknowns and risks. Far simpler to just not allow it. Even if the chronicle took place in the modern day when some of these objections could not apply, I still think there's some fundamentally wrong things with it to work.

                      However, per No One of Consequence, a Mage could create some kind of homonculus from a combination of the man's sperm and the vampire's vitae. That would be appropriate for the era of the Sorcerer's Crusade. However, I would not make the issue of such a blasphemous and horrific union a real human child, but some kind of monster or abomination. Maybe the child looks human, but there would be something wrong with it. This is the origin of things like Widderslainte.






                      So unlike some of the other posters here, I wouldn't handwave it just because it's Mage. I may not be in the majority.

                          I think whether or not this turns out well should be tied to the morality of the mage and the vampire. If the vampire is trying to be a good person (in other words, has high Humanity), their love is true and genuine, the surrogate undergoes the procedure consensually and is on good terms with the mage and the vampire, and the mage uses their power for good deeds, I think it's entirely appropriate to treat this as not blasphemous and horrific at all. The process of loving and raising a child, a true mortal child, could pull the vampire closer to mortal virtues. If this is the case, the child should be a normal child... or maybe even just a little bit blessed.

                          On the other hand, if the vampire is low Humanity, their love is poison, they force the surrogate into it, and the mage abuses his power, the product of their union should be appropriately warped and evil.

                                                          Find what is available to you. The silents were more faithful to the book, the newer ones are tawdry at the expense of theme and story.

                                                          I can't give you more help than that because these films are only irregularly available in the USA or English speaking countries generally.

                                                          It might be useful to know that Alarune means Mandrake. Mandrakes were often used to make homunculi. The aspect of the legendary Mandrakes refered to in the story is the association between the gallows and Mandrakes. When men are hanged by the neck until dead, in the death process most males ejaculate. It's assumed to be caused by chaos in the dying brain. When the seminal fluid of dying men is spilled on the ground Mandrakes are supposed to take root.

                                                          In the story the seminal fluid of a dying man is collected and used to impregnate a prostitute. Alarune is thus a homuculus born from death.

                                                          In most forms of the story she seems to have some kind of unconscious influence on fate, bringing riches or ruin. She also seemed to have the ability to manipulate people's appetites for sex and adventure and to cloud their judgment. Again she seems to have no conscious control of this power.
