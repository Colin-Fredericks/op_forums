    Questions about Cabals

    10-10-2017, 09:29 PM
    Reading M20 let me with a lot of questions about cabals. The book lacks informations about this topic, so maybe can you help me!

    1) How common it's a multi-tradition cabal?

    2) How many mages in a city will be member of a cabal? Almost all, some or few?

    3) Old and experienced mages, like adepts and masters, usually are members of a cabal? I always think cabals are for beginner mages.

    4) Do cabals often have mages of different levels of knowledge and experience? Like a master and some other members of varied degrees of experience?

      1) This one depends really on the individual cabals and their constituent members. Basic logic says that Tradition mages will prefer to form cabals and Chantries with other members of their Tradition, and especially with other members of their particular Faction (if you're Hermetic of House Tytalus, you'll get along best with other Tytalus magi, for example). But often times, preferences must bow to the pressures imposed by the Mundane World. Yes, even Mages must deal with regular people problems.

      Like, for instance, where one lives. Note I didn't say "where one chooses to live"; some people have few options for where they CAN reside, or where they can reside while maintaining the lifestyle they are accustomed to. If a mage has a job - yes, mages with regular or semi-regular employment exist - that job dictates where they live, because not all places of employment are flexible about geography. Not everyone can work from home. Other things can dictate a person's residence, like enrollment in a specific learning institution (either because a particular one is important to the mage, it's the only one that offers the program the mage is interested in, or it's the only one that would accept them). Or like particular ties to a region that can't be dismissed; one Verbana might own land passed down in the family, while a shaman might have their city as their Totem.

      All this means is that, sometimes, a mage cannot always choose which of their peers are local. You can't always choose your neighbors. While one city might have a huge coven of Verbana, another city might only have a single (Awakened) witch in residence. While it's always possible to travel to other areas, not everyone can do so easily; they might not have enough Correspondence, or not have a Sanctum that would make teleportation to another city's Chantry Coincidental. And driving or flying there might be a major or expensive undertaking. In any case, the mage can't hook up with members of their own Tradition on a regular basis, but only on special occasions.

      Since problems can arise at the drop of a hat - especially with the Ascension War - Tradition mages need to create ties with who they can. If you're the only Hermetic in the tri-state area, and the Technocracy comes calling, you'll often have to make due with the irreverent Virtual Adept, the half-mad Dreamspeaker, the stoner Ecstatic, and the retiring Akashic that happen to be in the neighborhood. Even if you have nothing in common with them, and probably have severe ideological differences with.

      Other times, multiple Traditions can be found in large numbers in one area. But circumstances might incline or force members of different Traditions to collaborate. Maybe the Hermetic has been censured by the Order, and needs to get help from mages outside it. Maybe the Etherite needs the Dreamspeaker's help to smooth over relations with the local Umbrood, after a particularly disastrous Spirit experiment. Maybe the Akashic decides that the Virtual Adept's attempts to learn martial arts are embarrassing, and insists on taking over his training herself. Maybe the Euthanatos can speak to a ghost, but only the Hermetic can speak its language. Maybe the Verbana witch needs the Chorister's help getting the local religious conservatives off the backs of the local Wiccan community.

      Or maybe there's a single problem that affects all the mages from different Traditions, and they need to collaborate for mutual self-interest.

          2) Again, this depends on the situation. It can vary greatly.

          Do the mages in a specific locale have great reason to hang out? Or is everyone mostly tending to their own interests?

          (It's probably also important to distinguish between a Cabal and a Chantry. The latter is usually associated with a particular place - like a Node or a defensible position - whereas the former is formed for certain purposes, and implies active collaboration. Different mages may all be members of the same Chantry, but not regularly meet or work together as a Cabal. Or vice versa. In some cases the two might be one and the same, while other big Chantries might be divided into several Cabals.)

              3) I suspect most Cabals are made of relatively younger mages, because they're the ones who need the most backup, and do the most adventuring. Older mages tend to be more established in their lifestyle (and old age tends to make one reluctant to adventure), and more experienced mages tend to be powerful enough that they don't need help (or if they need help, they've got more Consors, Cults, powerful Allies, Apprentices, Spirit pacts, and Wonders built up over their careers). Plus, the farther along one your personal path to Enlightenment, the more you tend to want to pursue that path further, through private study, experimentation, and self-reflection.

              That said, older or more experienced mages can still be in Cabals. Some of them could have established relationships with other mages, and their Cabal status could simply never have lapsed (they still keep in touch or assist each other). Others might have developed Cabals for purposes that they still continue to serve (like establishing and maintaining a base on the moon or a Horizon realm, or researching a large magical working, or running a dragon breeding program, etc). Other mages might form new Cabals with their peers, if the need is great enough, and the youngsters can't handle it.

                  4) I would assume that some Cabals are made up of members with differing levels of experience. Especially if the purpose of the venture necessitated an expansion from one or two founders to a much larger group. Or because a Cabal ran into a problem they lacked the ability to solve, and brought in a specialist to assist (and then they just sort of never left). Or simply because different mages of different experience levels just happened to all have the same problem, and came together.

                  For the record, I don't think such a disparity is a good idea in Player cabals. Generally, if a game group gains a new player in the middle of a campaign - or an existing player needs to make a new character - they should be allowed to make a PC roughly equal to the other PCs. It allows the new character to not be a burden, and makes it so the player doesn't have to start from the very beginning (potentially all over again), while everyone else is doing much cooler stuff. In these cases, just give the new PC a lump sum of XP to roughly match everyone else, so it doesn't cause problems. Because it's the game group, realism should bow to playability and group cohesion.

                  Edit: Another thing. There's an unspoken understanding in a group that, in groups with members of disparate experience levels, the most experienced will be in charge. Not always, of course - a Virtual Adept is unlikely to casually defer to a Hermetic, even one three times his age - but it can happen. Actual age can be less of an issue, but experience (and, with mages, power) will create a social pressure in the greater one's favor. Unless there's a particular reason why a less experienced mage is the one spear-heading the operation, it's often assumed that the more experienced one is the boss.

                              Originally posted by Mark Logan View Post
                              Taking into account the answers so far, would it be correct to say that the idea of cabals functions more as a way of uniting players than something widely used by members of Traditions in general?
                              No, I wouldn't say that. Player Characters aren't the only ones who unite for some common purpose.

                              There might be research cabals, who combine their efforts to figure out some magickal area of expertise - yes, even members of different Traditions may do so. The Guide to the Traditions book has a list of cross-Tradition Factions - like the Virtual Adept/Hermetic group The Linguists, that research the power of language, both mystical and programming - that undoubtedly began as Cabals. If there's a particular topic that characters want to study, and this topic is of relevance to multiple of them (even those of different Traditions), they may pool resources, compare notes, and engage in philosophical debate.

                              Speaking of the Guide to the Traditions, a different section pointed out that something else might unite different Tradition characters: religion. We like to think that every Tradition represents its own faith and no mixing can occur, but this is simply not the case. The book uses the example of Christians, who can be found not just in the Celestial Chorus, but in the Dreamspeakers ("Spirits are angels, sent by God to keep His creation operating!"), the Order of Hermes (more "mainstream" Hermeticism for centuries was painted with a Christian brush; invocations to the Greco-Roman gods became prayers to Archangels), and even Verbana (not all the European pagans gave up their practices, yes, but not all of those who clung to the Old Ways completely rejected conversion either; the Revised Celestial Chorus book listed a witch among its sample characters, who maintained that her power came from wisdom passed down from God). A character's Practice does not preclude them from adhering to a particular religion, if their Paradigm can be made to accommodate the two.

                              If a bunch of characters in a locale are from different Traditions, but share the same faith, they may find more in common with each other than with members of their Tradition. Especially if their religion is atypical for the in-group, or is at odds with their Tradition mates at hand. A Christian Verbana might go to her fellow witches for advice about herbs and knotwork, but will be hesitant to participate in rites calling upon the power of Hecate or the Green Man.

                                  Great posts by Bluecho!

                                  I'll add that cabal membership is going to be more likely for mages from factions that promote working together to accomplish goals. It's hard to see a Hermetic in good standing who's not part of a cabal. Even the Arch Master might want to talk to or work with other Arch Masters on projects, though they could spend just a bit of time with each other. Cultists of Ecstasy and Sons of Ether have very individualistic traditions and practices and tend to emphasize voluntary cooperation, so members of those Traditions might do a lot on their own if they haven't found the right people to join forces with. As for the Technocracy, they are about control, so it would be a rare Technocrat who wasn't assigned to an Amalagam, the Technocracy equivalent of a cabal.

                                  From a metagame perspective, cabals serve like packs in Werewolf, motleys in Changeling and coteries in Vampire. Cabals are the basic group organization unit and if you have a group of players, their characters need a group so you aren't running a bunch of individual sessions while the other players wait. Also, it's nice to have backup.

                                  In my gaming experience, it's been mostly mixed cabals. I was in a PBP game where two of us played Hermetics, but usually each PC in games I've played came from a different Tradition and had different Spheres and abilities so that the cabal had more options.

                                      I think Bluecho covered nearly everything pretty well.

                                      Speaking personally, whenever I've designed a Mage city/region setting, most of the Awakened are in Cabals of three or more, partially for a "strength in numbers" thing, but also out of shared goals or interests. Some research, some fight the Technocracy, some quest for personal power, and so on. Said area will have one or more chantries, each made up of one or more cabals. These chantries will each send a representative to a local council that helps adjudicate disputes, negotiate with outsiders, make Ascension War strategies, and so forth. The members of the council are effectively a Cabal, though members may be members of other cabals as well.
                                      I like using Cabals for NPCs because they let me fill various niches and can serve as allies, rivals or outright antagonists for the PC cabal. And they can be single-Tradition (or even single sub faction, like Hermetic Houses) or mixed, depending on what you want.
                                      One of my more fun NPC groups over the years was a cabal of Hermetic Alchemists who, after a long and distinguished history of being a single Tradition group, were forced to open their ranks to outsiders by order of Porthos Fitz-Empress, only to eventually grudgingly admit that the Etherite "atomic alchemist" and the Chorus grail seeker actually made them better at their own pursuits. All of this playing out as occasional background fluff while the PCs were off doing really important adventuring and politicking.

                                      Ultimately, it's your game and you can tweak it how you like.

                                              Something else worth remembering about cabals is that they aren't mutually exclusive. They're not magical in and of themselves... they're social constructs. Depending on the number of other mages around and their own inclinatons a mage might be a member of several, one or no cabals.

                                              For example, one of the mages in the game I'm running is part of a cabal called the Errants. The Errants are a multi-tradition cabal that formed to fight a particular Nephandi and stayed together because they became family. One of the members is also a member of the House of Hash cabal, a Hermetic (and largely Ex Miscellannia) exclusive group that meets at a restaurant of the same name (think Waffle House only hashbrowns) to share their research and current projects. Several other members of the House of Hash are part of other cabals as well along cultural lines (the House Hong Lei mage is also part a largely Akashic cabal whose interests are promoting and protecting their interests in Chinatown) or just mutual interests (another is a member of the Hidden Game, a mixed cabal that places bets based on the outcome of events like city elections and then uses their magic to try and bring that outcome about).

                                              Cabals can actually be an opportunity to weave a network of connections through the mage community of your campaign world that helps make the setting feel less small and isolated.
