                    This has been around for a while, but it's still important. Remember Facebook's attempt to create a solar-powered drone plane that would provide cheaper internet access to remote areas?

                    A means of extending the range of broadband connections to even underdeveloped parts of the world is massively relevant to Mage's interests. After all, if this plan gets off the ground, the Technocracy stands to extend the reach of information technology into lands formerly the sole province of Traditionalists. After all, it's far easier (and therefore cheaper) to fly solar-powered drones around, than it is to lay cable. It also marks a leap forward for the Technocrat Paradigm in those areas. The easier it is for people to get signal, the easier it is for high technology to not stop working because of local belief. Pretty soon, locals WILL believe, because all these fancy computers and smart phones will work.

                            Okay I'm going to stop after this, because I think I could spend all night just reading WIRED articles and gushing about them. But I need to share this, because it is Relevant.

                            Manu Prakash, Stanford bioengineer, and his team have (thus far) created two mind-blowing medical innovations: the Foldscope and the Paperfuge. They are fully functioning microscopes and centrifuges - respectively - that are made of materials totally less than a dollar in cost, each. Literally a paper microscope and paper centrifuge (along with certain necessary, cheap additional components, like string or the equivalent of a watch battery). For comparisons, your standard lab centrifuge costs thousands of dollars, is heavy as hell, and requires electricity to run. The Paperfuge is operated by hand, and can fit inside a pocket. The Foldscope is similarly tiny, elegant, and practical in its design compared to standard lab microscopes. And both of them give results comparable to the industrial standard. These are not baby's first biochem equipment; you can do actual Science with these things, and they each cost less than a dollar.

                            I won't bother telling you how revolutionary this is from a health care standpoint. The articles themselves do a pretty good job of it, and even without them I assume you smart people have already grasped the implications.

                            So instead, let's talk about what this would mean in Mage. You now have lab equipment that can be built on a child's allowance. Naturally, this is a stunning boon for Progenitors (and some Void Engineers, probably), as the two above inventions have applications in Research and Education, as well as in health care and disease prevention. You just know they are giddy about the idea of getting these things into even the poorest of public schools (let alone in third world countries, opening up the pool for potential recruits). The Syndicate is likely pleased, as anything that makes the Enlightened Science of their peers cheaper, without significant loss in accuracy, is a good thing.

                            But it's also a boon for Tradition technomancers. Imagine you're an Etherite biochemist, who struggles every day with the costs of research equipment. Suddenly, you can afford basic machines for pennies on the dollar, that you can probably built yourself in an hour. And both the Foldscope and the Paperfuge are tiny, slim things. Imagine an Etherite walking around with a fully-functioning lab in her pocket. All of which, thanks to publications like WIRED, is now Coincidental.

                                Here's a little known piece of information that makes the paper instruments even more relevant. This is the Bangui Definition of AIDS*. It is the WHO's guideline for diagnosing someone with AIDS in the absence of actual HIV tests (which it clearly says are preferable). Until recently it was the primary means of diagnosing the disease in large parts of Africa. Unfortunately there are a lot of diseases in Africa that throw up enough symptoms to trigger false positives for AIDS. That, in turn, made it difficult to determine which regions and populations were hardest hit and where to send medical supplies.

                                That centrifuge is going to save millions of lives.

                                *I'm using the 1985 version because it lists the symptoms. The 1994 update stresses the need for an HIV test if possible.

                                    Back to WIRED again (because I can't get enough of it now). Here's three articles in rapid succession.

                                    -Physicists Teach AI to Identify Exotic States of Matter: Using a variation of the same computing technology that makes it possible for Facebook to automatically tag a face in a photo (and, by extension, allowing the Technocracy to spot known Reality Deviants in social media), two different teams of physicists have trained neural networks to examine materials and identify their physical states. They can theoretically discover states of matter previously unknown to scientists. Sounds like Matter senses to me.

                                    -Now’s Probably the Time to Consider One of These Burner Phones: One of those lists of X number of items related to a theme. This time, it's burner phones, prepaid devices that leave a limited paper trail (so long as you pay in cash). When you're on the run from the Technocracy, it helps to be able to make a phone call without it being (easily) traced back to you. The items here range from the very basic, to phones whose cost is so comparable to regular smart devices they shouldn't be considered burners at all (yet can still be prepaid). Most of them cost $30 or less, indeed many less than $10 (not including data refills). One of the items isn't a device at all, but an App that automatically runs calls or texts through to a proxy phone number (that can be given out and discarded at the user's convenience). The Technocracy probably has ways of getting around burners, but they might at least make mages harder to find.

                                    -MIT Made a Wearable That Knows How a Conversation’s Going: Using a combination of temperature sensors, voice analysis, word parsing, and an AI, this smart watch is capable of looking at how someone speaks and determine in a general sense whether a conversation is "Positive" or "Negative". It's not perfect - interpersonal communication is hard - but it's a step towards a very Mage-like ability to sense a person's emotional state. Again, all in the form of a Smart Watch. They're working on getting it to function on standard smart watches, but I can assume that Virtual Adepts and Technocrats alike have something similar (though obviously much more advanced).

                                                    Funnily enough, the Paperfuge is based heavily on a toy from India, called the Whirligig. A toy whose design dates back thousands of years. And when they measured it, it turned out it spins the disk at the center really really fast (that's a scientific term). Given that centrifuges work by spinning samples really really fast, it was inevitable that the two technologies would be combined, so long as there exists economic incentive to do so. It worked for the printing press, which Mage: The Sorcerer's Crusade maintains was probably an invention by sleepers (Gutenberg and his team), created from several existing technologies. As such, there is precedence for this in WoD.
