    Abandoning Instruments:

    When using this house rule, you don't have seven Instruments; you have as many or as few as you like, as long as you have at least one. Instruments don't get set aside individually; instead, they get set aside based on the highest rank required for the Effect:

    • If a mystic has at least twice as many dots of Arete as the highest Sphere needed for the Effect, he can skip the instruments if he wishes.
    • If a technomancer has at least five more dots of Arete than the highest Sphere needed for the Effect, he can skip the instruments if he wishes.

                I also think the magick system itself is broken (relatively). Because higher level powers are capped by three factors (obtaining required Sphere level, rolling the higher difficulty and obtaining more successes for a basic effect than for a lower level power), they're always weaker than lower level powers.

                So I'd make the base difficulty of all magick equal to the local Gauntlet or the Willpower of the subject. This makes lower level powers harder, but stops higher level powers seeming relatively low powered in comparison once a person starts gaining Arete.

                I'd require the expenditure of Quintessence for vulgar magic (one point if there are no witnesses, and two points if there are). You might prefer to raise the difficulty by +1 or +2.

                Finally, I'd make more consistent rules for successes, which would take into account the fact Sphere levels already limit Effects.

                If you look at the Magickal Feats chart, for example, you'll note the ascending number of successes roughly mirrors the ascending Sphere levels. In effect, it's not necessary to require five successes for a fifth Sphere level Effect if you're already restricting such Effects to a high level of mastery. To do so is to make level five powers doubly difficult.

                So I'd make one success a basic success for any Effect appropriate to its Sphere prerequisites.

                So a roll for a level five Sphere will achieve a level five Effect with just one success. However, I'd make more concrete rules about allocating successes to duration, range, etc. So you'd still need multiple successes for anything but a fleeting spell, but having a level five Sphere would at least make it easier to create more powerful Effects.

                Does anyone else have any suggestions for fixing the magick rules for higher level Spheres? I'm still looking for a really elegant fix, which must exist somewhere.

                    The simplest fix I can think of, actually, is to put all difficulties at 6 (7 or 8 for vulgar) and ignore Sphere levels.

                    Instead, to achieve the more powerful Effects you just need to roll the number of successes on the Magickal Feats chart. You can make this an extended roll, but can only roll a number of times equal to your current Sphere rating in the lowest Sphere used (each failed roll makes the next turn's roll increase by +1 difficulty). Those with lower Sphere levels will still struggle, but everyone has a chance.

                    For rotes, which require understanding as much as raw talent, you have to have the relevant Sphere levels, or the action counts as an Effect instead.

                    It allows for more flexibility, but the low Arete levels involved stop it from being overpowered at lower levels.

                            As Dream_Walker said, there's no need to complicate things too much, but I disagree with the storm being undefendable. If the magick is cast in such away it has to connect, then it has an attack roll and a defense roll can be used to avoid it. If the magick is cast in such a way it just lands, then there's no attack nor defense roll.
                            "I chant the Names of the Fire Servants to conjure a fireball that speeds towards my enemy". This dude will roll an attack (most likely dex/athletics) and the other dude can defend.
                            (arete serves only to calculate damage, attack precision applies)

                            "Breathing deep, I channel my chi into my lungs with tainted essence, then breath it out in a cloud of poison". This dude will roll an attack (most likely stamina/athletics or do) and the targets in the cloud will be able to avoid it or hold their breaths in time.
                            (arete serves to make an impressive effect, probably damage over time or some debilitation, attack precision does not apply)

                            "I release my glass-ether wasps, who fly into the sky and accumulate energy around them to hurl a lightning bolt from above at my enemy." This dude will roll an attack (most likely int/hypertech) and the other dude can defend. The victim will likely not see it coming though(high above, not visible), and not be eligible to defend. If the victim is aware, however, he'll defend normally. Tricks like this don't tend to work twice on the same target.
                            (same as fireball)

                            "I etch the Rune of Pain deep enough on my skin to draw blood, and smear it on her face to lay a death curse." This dude will roll an attack (most likely dex/brawl) and the other dude can defend.
                            (same as poison breath)

                            "I scan the target and infect his motion algorithms with my favorite incandescense malware. If he moves, he'll lit up like a bonfire." This dude will not need to roll an attack, and the other dude can't evade. The fact this particular magick has an "escape" clause (don't move) is particular. Some witch could evil eye someone into a heart attack, for example.
                            (arete does everything. it's an impressive effect that just hits)

                            And, of course, countermagick applies against all cases.

                                    In the "violence as a focus" system it seems that, if you use it to deliver a direct damage effect, then the damage of the magick overrides the damage of the weapon.

                                    This seems odd to me. If you increase the kinetic energy of a bullet, and add Prime to it, (like in the example, in p.563) you're going to cause the same damage than doing the same with a sword or a punch (albeit the punch will need Prime 3 or a glove). Nor to mention that the caliber of the bullet, or the potency of the weapon, are irrelevant to the damage.

                                    It seems that, when magick it's used, it is literally true that what matters it's not the weapon, but the warrior who uses it

                                    However amusing this oddity may be, it generates certain problems.

                                    For a thing, it benefits the mages who use less complicated weapons or techniques (that is, weapons or techniques that have an easier difficulty to hit), over big and damaging weapons.
                                    I don't think this it's a good thing. I understand that, in Mage, the "mood" it's more important than the "practicality" , but there is nothing to gain in giving those who use simple punches and kicks a magickal advantage over those who use more complex martial arts techniques. It doesn't benefit the mood...and may bother some players

                                    Also, in some cases (specialy when using enchanted weapons), it may be that using "violence as a focus" to deliver an extra magic(k)al pain, end causing less damage than would have caused the weapon.
                                    It depends on luck, and on what enhancements has the weapon, but it's an oddity that can happen. For instance, using Forces 2 (without Prime) to enhance the kinetic energy of a katana causes Lethal damage...but, what if the katana was enchanted previously to cause aggravated?. Rules in hand, nor the damage nor the type of the damage, of the weapon apply.
                                    My house rule proposal on this it's the next:

                                    While using "violence as a focus", and after delivering the hit with success, and checking for Arete, we must compare the result of the Arete check, with the potential damage of the weapon (like, Strength + 3, for a katana).
                                    If the potential damage of the weapon it's higher than the damage caused by the magick, then we add to the damage a number of damage dice equal to the difference.

                                    Also, if the spell would cause Contundent or Lethal damage, but the weapon causes a more damaging type of damage, then the type of damage of the weapon may override the damage type of the spell (using Forces 2 to argument the kinetic energy of a katana causes Lethal damage, but if the katana normally caused aggravated...why should cause Lethal when striking whith more force?). Of course, exceptions may arise, dependant on the nature of the magick.

                                    If the attack roll scored more than one success, the extra successes can be added to the damage, like in revised edition (a good aimed magickal shot may cause more damage than a bad aimed magickal shot).

                                    If this system seems too dependant on rolls, it may be possible to just sum all the extra damage dice than we've counted, an add half of them (rounded down) as direct damage

                                    This way, using complex and damaging techniques isn't penalized, and enchanted weapons can still benefit of their magick when used as foucs.
