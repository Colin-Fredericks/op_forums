            I would have the cosmology be composed of the physical universe, the astral plane, and unmanifest existence. You could have classic animist spirits dwelling in an incorporeal form in the physical universe. Most other types of spirits, such as gods, angels, and demons, would reside in the astral. Pretty much every distinct spiritual realm would compose a segment of the astral. Importantly, the astral would be non-physical, but it would still have manifest forms that would be fairly straightforward for a human to understand. Finally, there would be unmanifest existence. This would be the dwelling place for unconditioned entities, and would thus reside outside of the realm of game statistics. It would not be a place, but a state of being. The Spirit Sphere would be used for interacting with spirits in the physical world, and for interacting with the astral. The Mind Sphere would also have some uses that related to the astral.

            I think that that covers all of the essentials whilst still leaving things undefined enough for a wide variety of paradigms to be compatible with it. It would not work for all paradigms, though.

                                                    The harsh truth is that while the canon model for the Umbra can work, the developers should have taken lessons from D&D's Planescape.

                                                    1- We have a lot of metaphysical explanations, little actual descriptions of the places the PCs are exploring and very few examples of the things and entities they'll interact with. The core book specially should have less word count on the metaphysics of the Umbra and more on describing places to explore;

                                                    2- Get rid of the Vidare. The idea is good, but it only makes things hard to explain, understand, and more importantly, to describe. At most the Vidare could apply to the Periphery, but once a group travels to the Penumbra, everyone should see the same place. Better yet would be for the three Vidare to become distinct Penumbrae;

                                                    3- Define Transition Realms. WoD's Umbra does have them, but they are ill defined, unexplained and inconsistently addressed. Take time to actually explain what is it like to be in the Middle Umbra without being in any particular Realm;

                                                    4- Astral Umbra must be redesigned almost from scratch. The place is a helluva mess to actually use on play. Take the time to define it's own Transition Realm, then look at how D&D works with the Outer Planes, as they're exactly the kind of Realm found here. The place may be abstract by nature, but we need it filtered to a physical experience so we can actually explore it;

                                                    5- Make a fucking map. It isn't cheap to commission and it kinda goes against WoD's style, but that's a huge help for STs to wrap their minds around the concepts. It doesn't really matter if no map can accurately describe the Umbra, they can describe a vision of the Umbra that's good enough to start things around it.

                                                    However the Umbra works, the problem is that the books devote a lot of space trying to explain metaphysics and little translating it to descriptions and rules actually useful for the game.

                                                        Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
                                                        That's the idea. We can leave room for [the cosmologies of the Night Folk] if we want, but for the purposes of this thread there's no reason to limit cosmology by what they need.
                                                        Room for them is provided implicitly by the fact that you need to be able to account for mages with cosmologies that align with the various Nightfolk. Shamans who see the world in a manner similar to how werewolves see the world will access Otherworlds similar to Werewolf's Umbra; necromancers who see the world the same way that wraiths do will have access to the same Underworld that Wraith describes; and enchantresses who view reality the same way that Changeling's fae do will be able to access the Dreaming described in that book.

                                                        But not every shaman will see the world the way the Garou do; not every necromancer understands death and the dead the way wraiths do; and not every “Faerie Faith” mage sees the world the way changelings do. And there's no guarantee that werewolves, wraiths, and changelings even exist in a particular Storyteller's World of Darkness — or that if they do exist, that they'll bear any resemblance to the other gamelines. This cosmological model strives to be agnostic concerning the existence of nature of the other gamelines.

                                                        Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
                                                        I like this as it gives the impression that there is a lot out there and no one is even close to a complete map. I probably would have overlaps between broadly similar concepts, particularly things like the Astral realm. That doesn't mean everyone is going to the same part of that place, but it allows for conflicts in those regions. From a gameplay standpoint, it allows more interaction between mages in those regions.
                                                        In this regard, I might group Otherworlds according to which Sphere is used to access them, possibly even introducing a paradigm-neutral “interstitial space” for each: Mind lets you access an Astral Space, an interstitial space which contains Mind-friendly Reality Zones that provide portals to the so-called Astral Realms (and, if it exists, the Dreaming); Entropy lets you access an interstitial space where themes of death and the dead are prevalent, with Reality Zones that provide gates to various Afterlives; Correspondence lets you access “virtual space” with Reality Zones that provide access to various Digital Web sites and even physical locations (with the part that links to the latter more commonly being referred to by the tech-inclined as Hyperspace), but also to Reality Zones that link to a Platonic reality where mathematical and geometric concepts have a tangible reality; Forces and Matter let you access interstitial planes containing Element-themed Reality Zones; Prime gets you to an interstitial space tied to “Resonance Realms” which are defined by “actions have consequences” (among other things, Heavens and Hells would be connected here); Time lets you access a “Timestream” interstitial space with Reality Zones representing significant moments in time and with access to Everett Volumes; and so on.

                                                        In all of these cases, I'd present the interstitial spaces as mostly empty in and of themselves, with what little definition they have coming from the Sphere used to access them, and any denizens mostly consisting of natives of the Realms that the interstitial space links to; that is, no native population. The premise for them would be “it's a boring place to visit, and I wouldn't want to live there.”

                                                        And some or all of them may conceptually have a “center” which, if you can find it, provides easy access to all of the associated Realms: the Correspondence Point for Correspondence; Eternity for Time; an All-Mind(?) for Mind; Oblivion for Entropy; and so on. These centers aren't easy to reach, and likely will destroy you if you're not properly prepared; access to them is likely restricted to Masters, or possibly even to Archmasters. Call them Nexi (singular Nexus) when you need a collective term for them.

                                                        Conversely, Paradox Realms would be “cracks” in these interstitial spaces, the conceptual inversion of the Nexi. They're disturbingly easy to get into, and very difficult to get out of.

                                                        Finally, I'd be inclined to redefine Spirit in this cosmology, to give it a distinct role: perhaps make it less about “contacting spirits” and more about “the state of being spirited” — that is, a Sphere for feelings, as distinct from Mind which is a Sphere for ideas. If Werewolf-specific cosmology exists as part of this, it would specifically be tied to Rage, whereas the interstitial Spirit plane would also include regions and Realms dealing with joy, sadness, fear, disgust, and so on.

                                                        Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
                                                        This is making me picture the spirit world, in general in a more Orpheus sense, where there isn't a gauntlet between the spirit world and the physical world. Instead difficult lies in getting from there to the deeper, more specific spirit realms. That difficulty could be a barrier like a gauntlet or Orpheus' Stormwall, or it could be something else.
                                                        Pretty much what I was thinking, yes: no “Penumbra” per se; just insubstantial entities existing in the physical world. In particular, that's where I was suggesting that Reality Zones might contain gateways leading to appropriate Otherworlds.

                                                        But it would also be true that being in an appropriate Reality Zone might also allow for Liminal Spaces (where you wander into the Liminal Space from one side, gradually lose contact with the world you left, gradually establish contact with the world of your destination, and finally emerge from the Liminal Space; more like crossing a bridge than walking through a door), or Stepping Sideways to an appropriate Otherworld, or projecting your consciousness into the Otherworld, with the access to each of these defined by the nature of the Reality Zone you're in and of the world you're trying to reach.

                                                        And just like the nature of the Reality Zone determines what Otherworlds are accessible and how they're accessed, it would also determine what sorts of spirits, if any, tend to hang out in that RZ.

                                                            One of the problems I have is that I always want to keep "spirit stuff" within the purview of the "spirit powah". That is, I would like to keep all of the "weird 'otherwordly' stuff" within the Sphere/Domain of Spirit.

                                                            I'm... Not a fan of having those being touched by other Spheres... On the other hand thou, I also don't like the idea that, once you've left the 'Material Plane', Spirit just becomes the "Unified Field" of Spheres, capable of doing everything out there - for which I mean, I still want a lightning to be controlled by Forces, thank you very much, even if it is technically a "spiritual lightning". I particularly have a problem with the fact that, aside from the stuff that you somehow "ephemerized" from the Material Plane, Matter Sphere becomes useless.

                                                            I'm not so fond of the idea that Spirit becomes the "Sphere of emotions". That leaves Mind to be just a sphere of pure cognition, and that is... Weird. Emotions are part of the Mind, even more so than memory and cognition.

                                                            I think Matter SHOULD affect the "matter" of the otherworlds, even if they are "just" 'ephemeral matter". And perhaps, even Life should affect the spirit beings directly. Spirit perhaps thus, should affect something deeper, something more... Conceptual. For instance, you could 'shapeshift' a Spirit with Life, but with Spirit 5 you change that Spirit's "concept", therefore 'truly' changing it - aka, with Life the thing would suffer Pattern Bleeding, Permadox and whatnot, but with Spirit it would be a "true" shapeshift.

                                                            Anyway, those are indeed some hard aspects to consider, and my opinion isn't set on stone on them on either way
