    Sorcerer Paths - Revisions

    12-29-2016, 10:19 PM
    Not to be confused with Sorcerer Revised. This is sort of a Revision of Revised, since off the top of my head I can think of ways that the rules for Sorcery Paths could be improved. Or at least expanded upon. I'll probably expand this further later.

    Path of Hellfire
    It occurred to me that some minor bits of utility magic ought to be within the power of an unAwakened Sorcerer, and yet don't neatly fit anywhere. I'm speaking primarily at the moment of manipulations of Forces in a minor, non-offensive way. Things like creating a light source, summoning a small cloud of obscuring smoke, running a small electric shock to short out electronics, warding an object against water damage, etc.

    Given that most of these elements are covered in Hellfire as options for offensive attacks, I figured this Path would be as good as any to bundle them into. I'd make them Rituals, mostly level 1. They do no damage, so they shouldn't be harder to pull off than the two levels of lethal damage the Path does as a base. It would also make the first level of Hellfire alone worthwhile to invest in.

    Another revision for this Path is just to expand the possible areas of expertise the character can learn. Light is an obvious one, as a non-Ritual application. It doesn't do damage by itself, but can be used to blind or disorient opponents.

    Darkness as an element is mostly covered by the Path of Shadowcasting. But I figure one could justify using Hellfire to use the element offensively, which Shadowcasting can't normally do. Hit them with a kind of weighty dark (gonna be honest, I'm cribbing wholesale from Dark Souls and its system of Hexes here). Given that Shadowcasting doesn't use the standard format of Aspects and Rituals that most Paths do, Hellfire could serve as a compliment. If nothing else, it gives more room for a shadow sorcerer to grow besides the one Path.

    Gravity may be a little too esoteric for Sorcery, but I'll list it nonetheless. It does damage by applying crushing weight to targets within an area. Rituals could be spun off from this to redirect where gravity pulls, allowing the Sorcerer (time permitting) to cause things to fall up or to the side. My philosophy concerning Sorcerers is that they aren't incapable of all the Mages do as a matter of course - many of their Paths at high ranks give powers comparable to Awakened feats - they just need to work harder and longer to make progress on specific Effects.

    Sound or Sonics is an underutilized application of force, but an effective one. Whether pulled off by a witch's banshee-like scream or a technomancer's Bass Cannon, it has the ability to rock bodies, shatter glass, and deafen. Rituals might be used to create a field of silence (or one where a conversation won't carry beyond a small area), or to effect specific disruptions of the body (the infamous "Brown Note").

      Well, shadows should not really cause damage, unless you are tapping into the Abyss, so I think that Hellfire would be inappropriate. Gravity is also one that cannot really be focused and, as there are no real world examples of focused gravity, it would be impossible to conceptualize for a Sorcerer, so I do not think that it would be appropriate. Sound would be valid though.

      I think that Protection Against (Element) would be a valid ritual set for Hellfire and here is what I would suggest. Level one rituals would increase the soak of a specific target against non-magical forms of (X) by one per success and would last week day per success. Level two rituals would to the same for an area, with the area having a radius equal to ten yards per success and would last one week per success. Level three rituals would give the same soak as level one rituals, but they would apply to magical and non-magical forms of (X). Level four rituals would give the same soak and area as level two rituals, but they would apply to magical and non-magical forms of (X). Level five rituals would give limited invulnerability to (X), granting immunity to non-magical forms and magical forms of (X) for one week per success, with each.magical attack of (X) negated reducing the duration of the ritual by one week.

                              Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
                              Why shouldn't a Sorcerer be able to tap into the Abyss? Abyss Mystics do it all the time, and vampire sorcery is as Static as mortal sorcery. More so, perhaps, because vampires are fundamentally Static creatures. And that's assuming Sorcerers even draw upon the Abyss at all.
                              (Just jumping in...)
                              One of the occasionally-cited-but-not-always-elaborated-on facets/factors of Static Magic(tm) (vis-a-vis Mortals) and Awakened/Dynamic Magick(tm) is that the latter does reach "deeper" into Reality(tm) than the former does. While hedge sorcerers/those gifted with variou Numina may perform acts that for a true mage would require using "x" "deeper-delving" Spheres/Pillars/what-have-you to make "x" happen, they aren't doing so for the same metaphysical reasons nor to the degree that true mages do. Tapping into truly removed, "deeper" extra-dimensional power is largely beyond the purview of hedge sorcerers (this is why contacting/summoning/binding extra-dimensional beings is a singular -- and often very difficult to master -- Path for sorcerers that takes longer and usually requires more effort on their part than for a similarly-minded mage, or how very few sorcerers have the power to properly travel to other dimensions/realities barring have level 6 in a given path -- the super-level of Ephemera, if transplanted over from the older system, or of S/B/W, for example). Sorcerers are having to for the most part work "within" the contexts of Static Reality(tm), even when there are mythic threads and similar allowing them to do "x", and consequently don't tap/access said deeper aspects which would allow them to, say, tap into the power of the Abyss, proper, or may other such realms/powers/et al. Additionally, cross-comparing vampiric blood sorcery overlooks a tenant of metaphysics which has, for the most part, played out true across all the editions of the various splats/WoD: the power backing "x" splat (vampires, in this case) *does* in some manner connect them to a deeper level of Reality(tm) than would be possible by a mortal sorcerer under usual circumstances. The vitae of Caine sources back to both long-ago mythic times as well as into the deeper underpinnings of "how things work 'round here", so to speak. It's both metaphysically and game-mechanically consistent with how, dot-for-dot, vampiric disciplines/blood magic usually (not always, but for the most part) are more powerful than their mortal sorcerer/numinically-gifted counterparts (usually by the equivalent of a dot, sometimes two, "above" or "deeper" than the sorcerer has), though mortal sorcerers' magic is still usually more inherently flexible within "x" purview of a given Path than a given vampire's supernatural power, which is consistent again with the general advantage that a living sorcerer (or mage) has a more dynamic essence/power than the undead do for most relative purposes. It's again a sometimes subtle but important aspect of metaphysics that can be easily overlooked....

                              Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
                              I tend to take a "Why Not?" stance when it comes to Mage (and, by extension, Sorcerer). Should a Sorcerer be able to draw upon elemental darkness and use it to smite people? Why not?
                              In general spirit, this is an okay approach for most lower-to-mid-level cross-power concepts. However, referring back to my further up paragraph, the underlying aspects of metaphysics come back into play. That is, it is conceptually "okay" for a sorcerer to be able to do "x" in concept relative to the general effects-as-manifested by "y" power that a vampire/what-have-you might demonstrate, but they are again (generally speaking) accomplishing "x" for different reasons and under different rationales than how the other splat does it, and usually (not absolutely always, especially for lower-level effects, but for the most part) not quite to the same level of potency and/or "deeper-delving" metaphysics as a given splat might. Example: you can use Obtenebration to create/control shadows, muffle sound, inspire fear, and similar-such; mortal sorcerers can accomplish similar vis-a-vis Shadow Casting (or a variation of Hellfire, for those not cross-editioning from different eras, or whatever). However, while SC/whatever is making "x" happen for the sorcerer with the effect being essentially the same as via a vampire using Obtenebration (that is, shadows doing whatnot and freaking people out and other stuff), it isn't delving as far into things nor being quite as potent as vampire-empowered Obtenebration is (though in many cases/instances this distinction is unimportant on an "did it do what I wanted it to do" end-result scale). A similar-if-slightly-different thing occurs, again, relative to such as Abyssal Sorcery: for a hedge wizard to "properly" accomplish "x" in "y" manner as Abyssal Sorcery does, metaphysically-speaking, the wizard would have to use S/B/W at level 5 for smaller effects (those much more easily accomplished at lower levels via SC/H/whatever), via summoning a (D)ark entity from the Abyss, or level 6 to summon a more powerful entity from there to then accomplish a more powerful effect more along the lines of "x-y-z". (While XP-wise the mythic "Level:6" is something achievable without herculean effort, the narrative threshold of that level of power being properly "mythic" acts as a stop-gap on how often such power truly manifests in the game setting, even back in the DA setting or earlier, due to the rarity of it. There are far more vampires running around -- *far more* -- who have 6+ dots not only in "standard" disciplines levels but similarly in "My Blood Magic", than there are *any* sorcerers who have even one path at level 6 contextual to their scaling of things.)

                              The underlying point to that is that the "Why not?"-factor more accurately applies to things such as "Why not have a sorcerer be able to light candles/make a floating ball of light/perform what classically would be called cantrips, etc...", or to general concepts ("Why not have a sorcerer be able to more directly affect/manipulate near-Umbral spiritual entities via how Ephemera does vs. using S/B/W, or have a Necromancy path, proper, etc...") vs. the implied-and-stated "deeper-running" power acting behind (M)ages and even vampiric sorcery (via the latter's connection to the "deeper reality" by way of Caine's ultra-mythic vitae). Again, an important set of distinctions....


                              Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
                              I think the moment scientists started talking about how different planets have different gravities, it opened the Mythic Threads to all sorts of gravitational shenanigans (assuming Mages hadn't been doing this sort of thing for centuries already). I could see a lot of technomancers devising theories on gravity manipulation.
                              This is true -- at least relative to technomancers, small "t" -- within the context of supporting a given (P)ath or sub-set of such to accomplish "x" under a given set of metaphysical thought processes/perceptions. That still, though, doesn't in any way nullify the inherent potency-difference between what True Magick(tm) (or, again, vampiric blood-magic) has going for it vs. what the general potency of Sorcery(tm) is. (That is, per another poster's scaling in this thread, "gravity manipulation" would likely be at least at level 4, and probably 5, relative to what/how "x" would be accomplished under the general scaling of (S)orcery, particularly as applied-aspects of that concept overlap with those of Conjuration and Conveyance.) Just saying....

                              Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
                              Moreover, we don't have real-life examples of a lot of things, yet Sorcerers do it anyway. So long as they can figure out a way to justify it within their world view, mages can make it work (and lay the ground work for Sorcerers to follow suit).
                              Agreed. But again, that doesn't do anything to mitigate the still-existing power difference/inherent potency disparity between the two metaphysical levels in play: Mages(tm) will always have, generally speaking, more "oomph" in their power, in the broad, than most Sorcerers(tm) do (the case-by-case exceptions for the latter being when/where the cross-conceptualization of how, exactly, hedge sorcery works making it so that a given sorcerer can accomplish "x" with "only" level 5 or 6 in a given Path -- Alchemy, for example, that can allow for "immortality" regimens or even indefinite effects -- that, for a mage, would require several dots in several Spheres to accomplish similarly, due to the applied metaphysical-paradigm differences involved). Exceptions exist due to applied metaphysics, but for the most part mortal sorcery is -- as it always has been -- the less potent little brother/cousin of proper magic(k) (as well as more-directly-empowered powers/sources, such as again vampiric blood magic, etc.), save that the paths have a degree of applied flexibility in many instances that combine metaphysical aspects that require more knowledge/experience for, say, a mage to simulate, and of course it is all-but-unheard of for Sorcery(tm) to ever invoke Paradox(tm) or "proper Backlash(tm)", as such.

                              Distinctions, distinctions, distinctions....
