    Mind Sphere Increasing Ability Scores

    07-13-2022, 10:28 AM
    I posted this question on the Mage Discord to mixed responses so I thought I would post this question here as well.

    Reading the Technocracy book, they have a device called the "Sleepteacher". It basically lets you buy ranks in an Ability without the need to train them in game. It got me thinking about the Mind Sphere in general, and how it would interact with Abilities. Someone's skill at something is basically housed in the long term memory portion of the brain, meaning most of it is mental. It's why people who get short term amnesia can still remember how to drive a car and things like that. Mechanically we know the Mind Sphere can raise your Mental Attributes just like Life can raise Physical Attributes. Would Mind be able to temporarily raise Ability scores though? On one hand it feels like it should be able to since how strongly someone's skills are linked to their memory, but nothing in the game (Other than the Sleepteacher) ever really talks about Spheres affecting Abilities at all.

      There are no rules that would allow to do that, AFAIK. So, by RAW, I don't think there's a clear way to do so. In general, Mage handless these by lowering difficulties - the result often looks the same: More success on a roll, and whatever that means. So, one could argue that's not even necessary for general purposes

      But I get that in some situations one could be better than then other, and in general it's frustrationg when the system refuses to engage with a mechanic for no specified reason (if you have free-form mechanics to raise one set of Traits, it's only natural to think about raising others).

      I would allow it conditionally depending on the Ability, and the explanation given. Problem is, unlike Attributes, there's no clear divide between the "mental" and the "physical" or even social aspects of an Ability (which ties well to the system allowing varied combinations of Attribute + Ability).

      For instance, what Brawl does needs you to train your body and your mind. Your body can be shaped by the hardcore training to figth, giving you physical capabilities (muscle memory, muscles tied to punching power) that an equaly athletic person may not have - this isn't something Mind can give you.
      While things like "following the rithm of your opponent" would seem more tied to Perception or Wits, conceptually, but in a way that an equally perceptive scientist could lack.

      Knowledges seem mental, but also may have physical aspects (Medicine to be a surgeon needs trained hands, Dexterity) or involve social aspects (Albeit these could be still handled by Mind, most likely, so it's a bit nonsensical)

      So, I would determine the Spheres and the levels to raise an Ability by looking at the Ability and the Rote, rather than grant one for all.

          The way I see it, Attributes represent the aptitude, while Abilities are trained. Meaning, while it is certainly possible to magically increase your aptitude for something, increasing your learned Abilities is a different story. You would have to magically manifest knowledge, practice, and talent to raise the stats for an Ability. That's difficult.*

          The Sleepteacher Device is basically Dexter's Omelette Du Fromage record player: a thing that implants knowledge into your brain while you're sleeping, something that is claimed to work IRL by some. But it doesn't create the knowledge from nil, it just blasts your brain with it until it soaks it up.

          There are ways to use Mind for enhanced learning, though. By increasing your Attributes (ie, aptitude) it becomes easier to learn and may shorten the time to do so. You could also use it to transplant knowledge from someone to yourself. I don't recall if there's a rote for this atm, but I think I remember something like that as an example in one of the books.

          There's also the option to use knowledge of a past incarnation (through the Past Lives Background). But in all cases, knowledge doesn't come from the void. It seems one of the fundamental rules is that you have to put the effort in.

          *Edit: To clarify 'difficult'. There's a paradox in summoning up knowledge of something you don't know. It gets a bit hazy with Divinations here, but generally I would say they are different processes.
