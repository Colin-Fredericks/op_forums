                        My general understanding of the orthodoxy of the Akashyana (formerly the Akashic Brotherhood), without flipping through my books and going entirely from memory, plays out something like this:

                        •The Akashic Record exists and it serves to catalogue the action, inaction, and interaction of everything that has ever existed in the history of everything; including the ancient masters and the great cosmic forces. Through appropriate rites, an Akashi can gain access to the Record and correspond with all of cosmic history and use that knowledge to guide themselves and to predict the most likely results of present actions upon the future.

                        •Enlightenment: the world is imperfect but through training of the mind and body one can gain enlightenment as to the true nature of the world and how to move through it. There is a way and that way is Do. There is a Do for all things that is perfect and good and through enlightenment and understanding of the Do one can achieve anything.

                        Daoism is tightly interwoven into the traditions of the Akashyana alongside elements of Buddhism and a variety of other East Asian philosophies and spiritual practices. Their specific focus on Do and the Akashic Record is what generally distinguishes them from the Chakravanti, Wu Lung, Wu Keng and other crafts.

                        The Akashic Record is a real mystical concept and the paradigm of the Akashyana is explored both in their tradition books and on several pages of 'Anders Mage Page 2.0'

                                                    So I ended up doing some reading on Dreamspeakers for a new chronicle and I have a slightly different perspective now.

                                                    Kha'vadi has its origins in the first Dreamspeaker book. It's not presented as an "original name" in the same way as Akashayana or Chakravanti, but it is given as a synonym and defined as "those-whose-visions-shape-the-world".

                                                    I don't know if it's meant to reflect any real world languages, but it does have its own context within the Tradition.

                                                    I think the key aspect of the Kha'vadi/Dreamspeakers is that, unlike a lot of the other groups, they literally only exist because they were shoved together by the European majority despite no semblance of a common origin, because the Hermetics were obsessed with the number nine. Their history is full of clashes with the rest of the Traditions, particularly the Hermetics and Choristers, who favored European colonialism and Christian conversion over the Dreamspeakers' own native concerns.

                                                    It does make sense that they'd want to use a name for themselves that isn't the vaguely patronizing one that the Traditions saddled them with. And it makes even more sense that they'd look for a name that isn't European in origin. There are a lot of cool synonyms in that section, but I can definitely see the appeal of Kha'vadi over the others, because it takes the vision/dream element of their original name but frames it in a more active way. I think I will start using it for my games, where I didn't before.
