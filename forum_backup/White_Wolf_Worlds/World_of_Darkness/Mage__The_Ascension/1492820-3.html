                                                One side effect of putting expertise front and center is that you quickly discover that no one can be an expert on everything.                                                         Yes, Room 101 can still work in this framework; in some ways, it's essential. Mind you, the NWO goes overboard with Conditioning, taking it beyond its core function and turning it into a tool to impose conformity. They also apply it to the unwilling, which isn't good. But at its core, what Conditioning is all about is converting an Avatar into Genius. You go in a Mage and come out a Scientist. (Note that the Void Engineers practice a more lax form of Conditioning; enough to transition Avatar to Genius, but mostly lacking the conformal indoctrination of Room 101. I would also apply this to the Syndicate's Special Projects Division, and to the Harbingers of Avalon, who specialize in breaking Conditioning.)

                                                        The advantage of this is that it explains why Genius doesn't cover the same range of possibilities that an Avatar does: Genius is almost always Circumspect (house rule: make Circumspect Avatar a “0-point Merit”) and never Manifest (which means that you cannot take your Genius as an Ally, Familiar, Spirit Mentor, or anything else that implies that it's a separate entity and not just part of your personality); in fact, most Supernatural Merits and Flaws are incompatible with Enlightenment without creative reinterpretation; it would probably be easier to list the ones that are compatible than to list the ones that aren't. Awakened Magi with Supernatural Merits and Flaws that are incompatible with Enlightened Science either can't be converted at all, or are harder to convert and lose the incompatible traits in the transition. For instance, a former Mage with a Manifest Avatar will never see his “imaginary friend” again after conversion, outside of an Epiphany (though chances are good that he'll develop the Demented Eidolon Flaw); but if the Mage with the Manifest Avatar went so far as to take his Avatar as an Ally, and/or has the Phylactery Flaw, his Avatar cannot become Genius. If Avatar-to-Genius is impossible but the “conversion” is otherwise successful, the former mage still loses the incompatible traits, and loses her Arete without replacing it with Enlightenment, becoming an Extraordinary Citizen. That's not necessarily as big of a problem as it might seem at first; as I see it, the vast majority of Technocrats are Extraordinary Citizens, and the Enlightened Scientists are the elite few who find it exceptionally easy to innovate.

                                                        The Traditions view this transition from Avatar to Genius as being “merely” a shift in Focus, albeit a more extreme one than even the difference between mystics and technomancers; and the least gracious among them compare it to how a Caul “inverts” an Avatar. That is, they tend to consider a Genius to be a suppressed Avatar. By contrast, the Technocracy views technomancy as an Avatar emulating Genius: mages pretending to be scientists. Enlightened Scientists view this as the “worst of both worlds”: the technomancer is nearly as dependent on tools and techniques as the Enlightened Scientist is, and labors under the restrictions of Willworking, and thus gets the full benefits of neither. Technomancers who consider such things, meanwhile, view themselves as a bridge between Enlightened Scientists and Awakened Mystics.

                                                        The defectors from the Union were still Enlightened Scientists when they defected. Their recruits, on the other hand, are mostly technomancers; and “reverse Conditioning” (from Genius to Avatar) is a thing; so someone who starts out with Enlightenment can undergo a process to become a technomancer. So these days, the Etherites and Virtual Adepts are mostly Awakened Technomancers rather than Enlightened Scientists — though the Etherites still call themselves true Scientists and say that Technocrats aren't Scientists. And with the rise of the Mercurial Elite, there's now a movement within the VAs to abandon even technomancy and go full-on mystic (though given their origins, the Mercurial Elite have a disproportionate number of Techgnosi in their ranks). That said, you can still find the occasional Enlightened Scientist in the ranks of these organizations.

                                                        Navalon, on the other hand, probably still practices a more benign version of Conditioning, more akin to a Void Engineer's Conditioning than to Room 101: there's some basic education to ensure that the recruit has the overall mindset of a Scientist, but only what's necessary to allow the transition from Avatar and Arete to Genius and Enlightenment. For now, at least, the group is making efforts to remain a faction of Enlightened Scientists and to avoid drifting into Willworking the way the Etherites and VAs did.
