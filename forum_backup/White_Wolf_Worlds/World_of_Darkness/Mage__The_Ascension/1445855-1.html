    I don't think Prime getting axed is the right move.

    If you're trying to figure out how to expand Prime a bit from just being the Sphere that governs magic, you could look at Awakening 2e for some inspirations of effects to put under Prime (and Awakening took out the Prime 2 for empowering other Arcana thing, made magical senses standard mage powers, and has plenty of ways to gain Mana without Prime or even spells). Two of the big things Prime governs in Awakening besides magic are purity and truth.

                In my extensive pre-M20 house rules, I split primes fuel effect and break down into quintessence off into their respective spheres and gave the rest (prime body, making weapons deal agg, etc) to Spirit (and refined Spirit as specifically dealing with the Middle Umbra while Mind acts like Spirit for the High Umbra and Death -see below- interacted with the Low Umbra).

                I dunno if I do that again in M20, but that’s more because my interests for magic have evolved more towards something like DA’s foundations and pillars approach that deals less with “what’s the nature of reality/how does everything fit together?”

                            Originally posted by NemoGG View Post
                            This is exactly the kind of experience I was looking to read about. How did the game flow? Was it too powerful giving Prime effect &quot;for free&quot; to the other spheres? Did it take away from the metaphysics and the &quot;fluff&quot;debates?
                            It flowed very well and actually seemed to help the metaphysics, honestly. The fact that with adept rank you could pull in quintessence from the cosmos to create a thing and with mastery in a sphere you understood it sufficiently to break it down... and that by linking it to spheres made the idea of, for example, using mastery of Mind to dissolve a mental concept into raw quintessence... I felt reinforced the idea that reality was composed of quintessence patterns and that mastery of a given sphere allowed you to manipulate those patterns directly. The capstone that really sold it though was the rank 5 prime effect of creating a node, because with it distributed among the spheres it allowed mages to concentrate the aspects of their mastery into nodes with more character and resonance... a Forces node was not a Mind node was not a Death node.

                            This also played into my meta-setting’s (I’ve run a virtually continuous Mage campaign in the same ongoing setting for 25 years now) focus on Anchorheads; expanded from generic thin spots to the Umbra into nine mega-nodes (one for each sphere) that anchor reality and that slowly shift position based on where humanity most associates that sphere (ex; the anchorhead of Forces was located in the Ubar Fields in Saudi Arabia... the largest source of oil in the world) and the war between the Technocracy and Tradition masters over their control (some go missing for a time as the consensus changed over the years and a race to claim it is on) since it’s believed that controlling the majority of them swings the nature of the world in your direction.

                            Anyway, in my experience, Prime was largely a “feat tax”; something you had to put dots into if your magic concept worked with Forces, Life or Matter to get the same sort of results that one would expect from Entropy or Mind or Spirit or Time without any such conjunctions needed.

                            Basically, everyone either got two dots in prime to store quintessence/generic counter-magick/fuel patterns/deal agg and then forgot Prime existed because it was only ever used as a conjunctional add-on or to refill the “mana bar” -or- the occasional PC who wanted to craft items and/or pattern rip would go all the way to 5 dots as a hyper specialist.

                            The last thing you want in a system is a “feat tax.” It’s just a speed bump applied to certain abilities for reasons (i.e. “no, you don’t get the cool toy X you were expecting, first you have to unlock Y to use X’s full power”).

                            Besides, once M20 clarified you can fly or use telekinesis with Forces 2 by redirecting gravity it also became really obvious you could just use the transmute level to defacto fuel Forces patterns by transmuting gravity or ambient light into heat or lightning and defacto fuel Matter patterns by transmuting the air or the upper molecules of every nearby surface, etc.

                            The result being that all adding the Prime elements to the spheres really did was simplify the explanations; instead of converting gravity to heat, you just create heat; instead of converting air to steel you just create the steel.

                            So, yeah, I mostly found that dropping Prime just meant that item creation, vulgar pattern rips and node creation weren’t locked up behind one specialist who could pretty much only do those terribly specific things, but instead available in limited ways to everyone... the master of Mind could create talismans related to mind magic, the Forces master could make staves that channel lightning, the Life mage could brew healing tonics (or viscous poisons), etc.

                                Awakening is a different game/setting with different assumptions, so direct transfer from it to Ascension is always going to be a bit clunky.

                                Awakening doesn't deal with consensual reality, so hieromancy like that isn't directly in their examples of how to use Prime to express using truth as a form of magic, but there's no reason you couldn't. One of the example Prime spells in Awakening 2e lets you speak Truth, that is, express something you strongly feel is true with such power behind it that the Truth behind your statement is so powerful everyone that can hear you knows what you're saying; ignoring things like language barriers.

                                If you added that sort of effect to Ascension's take on Prime, you could definitely build it up as part of pushing local Reality around. It also makes the Chorus as the Tradition holding the Seat of Prime make a lot more sense since that sort of soulful expression that transcends human boundaries would totally be something that would make Prime a major focus for them.

                                Another example that mixes the Magic and Truth themes of Prime in Awakening 2e is a spell that lets you circumvent attempts to encode magical items (not just mage made ones either) with specific actions, phrases, or whatever to make the magic in them happen. By commanding a wand of fireballs to express its Truth (it shoots fireballs), Prime can get around pesky things like having to intone a Latin phrase to get it to work.

                                This sort of "reveal magic for what it truly is" sort of effect could be a very interesting part of Ascension. The Technocrats trying to "debunk" mystic Reality Zones by revealing the Truth of a spell is not the mystic trappings the Traditions use and that Sleepers start to believe in, and the Traditions causing Union hardware to go nuts as the veneer of technological plausibility gets broken by revealing the true nature of their devices.

                                            Nitpick: is not so much about everything being equally true as it is that the question of “what is true?” isn't so easily answered as casting a spell. There are some things that are definitely true no matter what you personally believe; for insurance, Avatars exist. But what, specifically, an Avatar is, beyond “whatever it is that empowers Mages”,9 is open to debate, and is not the sort of thing that can be established through a Prime Effect.

                                            On the other hand, I could definitely see Prime's repertoire being expanded to include “illusions” — which I put in quotes because it's not about deception. You can do the “deception of the senses” thing with Forces. What the Prime version would be like would be more along the lines of Glamour: think of it as step one for creating something out of the Pattern Spheres, but without the Pattern Spheres. The downside would be that such Prime patterns would be susceptible to countermagick and Unbelief; the upside would be that you could do more with less: “illusions” of things wouldn't require as many successes to create as the things themselves.

                                            My own gut instinct would be to make this sort of thing conjunctional magic, assort of inversion of the usual rule about needing two dots of Prime to create things with the Pattern spheres: instead, you might need two dots of, say, Forces to create an “illusion” of fire using Prime. But that's just me: personally, I prefer that all of the tangible effects of Prime involve some sort of conjunctional use with an appropriate Pattern sphere. But then, I get plenty of use out of the intangible applications of Prime.

                                            And in a similar vein, I've got a hack (check the classical world of Darkness section in my sig) for spell books: a sort of hybrid between a grimoire and an artifact for talisman that lets Mages cast wrote that they don't have the appropriate Spheres for. In conjunction with the aforementioned sorcerer the paths of power, I would praise him in terms of granting access to the sorceress Rote Merit So long as you have the spellbook handy, and to aid in acquiring said Merit through study. The relevance to Prime is that you need it in order to create a spell book.

                                                            Well, the reason I'm stressing Prime as Truth is more of giving Prime additional flavor so it feels less like the"meta-magic" Sphere; as is the original complaint about it in the thread.

                                                            This idea doesn't need to be about the setting have a single overriding Truth above all others. Awakening doesn't even have that thanks to Archmages mucking around in the Supernal.

                                                            In this case it's more "revealing things for what they are." It's not a violation of Ascension's metaphysics or themes for the idea of revealing the truth to include letting people see what a stage magician is actually doing instead of being caught up in the show. Ascension frequently points out that Sleepers confuse the tools for the magic; something that is essential for mages to work coincidental magic in unfriendly Reality Zones because they can adjust their tools to disguise what they're really doing.

                                                            As well, the "lie detector" effect from Awakening is easy to work into Ascension while maintaining Ascension's themes: it's detecting reality vs. the target's ideal Pattern. There's a reason the Awakening "lie detector" spell can't detect serial liars like Mind can, but can spot someone that's using hair dye, or a falsified form: the reality of a thing is in contradiction with the Pattern of the thing, even if in a small enough way to not have major metaphysical consequences.
