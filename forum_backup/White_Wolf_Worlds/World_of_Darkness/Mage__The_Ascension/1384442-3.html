    Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post
    I do wonder how they’ll present the NWO in particular on the question of “Globalism” vs “Nationalism”. The Union has in past presentations leaned heavily toward internationalism (or Western neoimperialism masquerading as internationalism, depending on your viewpoint), but that was against the backdrop of a post-Cold War neoliberal reality that H Bush literally called the “New World Order” (que the right wing conspiracy theories about UN black helicopters the Technocracy is largely inspired by).

    Today that international order is crumbling before neonationalism, so the real world context has completely changed. There are a lot of ways this could play out in-game (ideally without casting authoritarian neonationalism as a Tradition victory and potential good thing).
    I share your interest in how they will present this. Though I would argue the shift hasn't been one in which international order is crumbling or even separate from the original intentions of the Technocracy for a few reasons. First, that would presume that previous iterations of global governance operated under the desire for, or even achieved, some sort of stability. Instead, contemporary moves towards internationalism have always relied on the tools of the empire and colony--dispossession of Indigenous peoples, occupation of soveriegn nations, and the political/economic destabilization of the other to maintain the supremacy (and stability) of the metropole or colonists (within the settler nation state). For example, the imperialist moves China has made in Africa have coincided with its internal moves towards ethnoracial nationalism a la prison camps for Uyghurs. So it seems strange to separate their tools of conflict from their goals of stability since they are both often used and desired jointly and interchangeably.

    Second, while previous champions for this system (e.g., U.S., U.K.) are seeing new waves of ethnoracial nationalism rhetoric in the public discourse and national politics I would hardly say that they have given up their global perspectives on the empire or colony. Instead, these moves towards nationalism have been to support internally consistent rules of governance (e.g., Racial contractarianism) to ensure the capacity of the nation state to act globally as per usual (Mills, 1997). We are just seeing the truth of the relation between nationalism and the empire (i.e., Yes, U.S. elected a black president, but it is, and always has been, a white enthnoracial settler colonial nation state and it is more than happy to oppress people within its borders or help others oppress people elsewhere to ensure that). Yes, I'm aware of your parenthetical statement, but my words here seek to resist attempts at reducing real systems that hurt real bodies to viewpoints (I'm not saying this was your intent).

    Lastly, I would argue that taking an imperialist or colonialist understanding of reality actually allows for the tensions we see between contemporary reality and the philosophies of the technocracy to be reconciled. Specifically, I have always seen the Technocracy as operating under a system in which the dominant political (i.e., NWO) and economic (i.e., the Syndicate) realities of the world are clearly understood to be inexplicably linked to the dominant cosmologies (i.e., Void Engineers) and epistemologies (i.e., Progenitors and Iteration X). While individual Technocrats may not agree with the action of one convention they would be lying to themselves and others if they said they can't see how they are complicit--they are mages after all. So the only way they could perceive themselves as noble scientists, industrious technologists, or champions of liberal democracy is if they were operating under the same blinders as we all do, but only magnified and reified. The colorblindness of the Union is a perfect example of this. The NWO (and to an extent the greater Union) is so caught up in the magical symbolism of mutually, modernity, and progress that even in their attempt to address their participation in racism they actually fall whole ass into it. The NWO would argue that because they truly understand reality they can look past racism, but in that process they reveal their inability to see the fictional causal relations that have been made real as per the racecraft (Fields & Fields, 2012). This ignorance to how systems of imperial and colonial control operate isn't a failure in their logics, but merely an indication of their reliance on systems that demand epistemological blindness. Namely, modernity and postmodernity both require ignorance of ongoing dispossession, occupation, and violence to keep going. Coloniality as we understand it was co-developed in loving embrace with modernity, not in spite of it (Mignolo, 2011). Even when they attempt to take responsibility for their actions, the Technocracy neglects to take any steps towards addressing them (e.g., rematriating Indigenous lands, ending economic exploitation) because doing so would disrupt their internal theories of reality (even if they are built on fairytale and dead bodies). By not making these moves, the Technocracy reveals that instead of being agents of objectivity or reason they are in fact hyperreal agents of the historical relations of our reality. It seems like the only Convention that gets it is the Syndicate--not surprisingly. They openly admit that their paradigms are built on magical logics of death, disposession, and exploitation (i.e., calling their agents Magic Men). I wouldnt be surprised if redlining wasn't one of their creations or maybe its the reverese.

    Let's also not forget that the Virtual Adepts and Society of Ether were totally fine with imperialism and colonialism until it came home to roost. So, as we are wondering how we can play moral or ethical champions of modernity, science and technology, maybe we just shouldn't worry because those fantasies are built upon fantasies.

I think something that I tried reflecting on in my original post was what does the Technocracy believe in, what do the Conventions believe in, what do they want the consensus to believe in, and how are they expected to show that belief? Moreover, I hope this new book elucidates the supremacy of Conventions over others in certain realms (e.g., the Syndicate taking more control in directing global governance through international corporate capitalism) and how their paradigms are more explicitly incommensurable (and in turn may weaken the paradigms of their colleagues). The introduction of terranorming provides an interesting avenue for explaining how a single Convention might implement possibly dissonant paradigms to achieve there goals. This is all to say I am left wondering if the belief in the Technocracy's ideals is more important for them or for the Consensus. Does the Technocracy need the Consensus to believe in the platonic ideal of a thing or just go along with the material relations of it? If their goal of global governance is based upon systems of control and dispossession, does it require that some people believe in one and be subject to the other? Can some Conventions be blind--in all their power--to the barriers to achieving their goals because of their implacable faith in their paradigms?

                                            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                            The thing about making an edition that's designed to cater to the Storyteller's preference is that you don't get to dictate what that preference will be.

                                            With that said, the tone I've been getting from the manuscript so far has not been letting the Technocracy off the hook for anything. There has been acknowledgement after acknowledgement that the Union has done terrible things in the past and continues to do terrible things even today. So I have no concerns that the book will end up being tone-deaf.
                                            Like I said, it helps. But you're going to have a tone, like it or not; the consensus of MageAsc's reality may be malleable, but the gameline itself is only so much. You can't have the Union both do and not do a terrible thing, and by definition it has done terrible things if it's supposed to have brought about the current state of the world. (You can say the Union doesn't have the level of control it pretends to, but even that only goes so far as a mitigation device; push that too far, and you have a bunch of lucky idiots, not an interesting group of technological Awakened.)

                                            I can't say that I needed the Technocracy to ever play a science-focused Mage, even outside the two explicitly technological traditions. The thing to remember is the Technocracy isn't actually about technology, they're about control. They're the army claiming to bring freedom while pillaging resources; the empire that declares peace by killing all that oppose them.

                                            Or, at least, this has been their consistent portrayal up until now. Individuals under the Union's sway might care about science and technology very much, but the Union itself is mostly about making sure they stay in charge.
