    Originally posted by Ajax View Post
    I don't know how germane it is to talk about about which Vampire was a Mage or not. What "being a mage" means is such a porous term. Modern mages have a lot in common with mages during the Renaissance. A lot alike, but not quite. Both are quite different from Dark Ages Mage. If "being a mage" means using Sphere magic(k), I'm not sure there can be an answer to that question when there were no Spheres (which are both a game construct and a reality in the actual World of Darkness).

    Were these guys from the Dark Ages mages, like Tremere and his buddies? In the modern sense, probably not. Were they sorcerers, again, in the modern sense, maybe. Were they something that was both and neither? That probably hits closer to the target. Same for the bronze magic types all twisted up in the Assamite history? Probably even less likely to fit into the modern paradigm. And Baba Yaga, back in the Neolthics? Who knows?

    I'm guessing that the term Mage vis-a-vis modern magery probably MOST applies back in the whatever-never-never-long-ago of early Vampire history. Back in those days reality was probably pretty malleable. So many, if not most of the original 3rd could be considered mages in the modern sense. And not just Tzimisce and Cappadocius, who seem to have a sorta "casting spells" rep, but also Saulot (because being a seer is definitely something pretty magelike, Time Sphere?). How about Ventrue? If NWO's social engineering is "magic" how about one of the first guys to ever exercise his will to become a leader of humans and creator of cities? (Didn't they eventually give us all these guys actual names at some point).

    BTW, In looking back at all my Order of Hermes stuff, there were DEFINITELY Primi of House Tremere who were not Tremere. Is there anything anywhere that explains how he could be the long-living leader of his House and also not the leader of his House?
    Very, very good point. Probably the safest standpoint to take when ascribing "magic type" to people who were magical pre-Embrace.

                                                            I like to draw a distinction between “the Consensus” (which is how humans believe the world works) and “reality” (which is how the world actually works). Needless to say, the Consensus can influence reality; but it is not all-powerful. These are, after all, Sleeper Avatars we’re dealing with here: while they are able to achieve in aggregate what individual Sleeping Avatars cannot achieve, some things are harder to change than others — and some things are so hard to change that even the cumulative belief of every Sleeper currently on the planet may not be enough to change them. Turning every vampire into a lawn chair “simply” by making everyone on the planet believe that vampires are actually lawnchairs is probably one of those things; likewise with “disbelieving” them out of existence.

                                                            And bear in mind that it’s largely a moot point, as the Consensus isn’t anywhere close to saying that vampires don’t exist: there’s a heck of a lot more to the world than Western society. So whether or not a unified Consensus could eradicate vampires is an unanswerable question, because the Consensus isn’t unified. Which is not to say that the Technocracy isn’t trying.

                                                            To take another example, the Consensus has no say as to the nature of the Umbra; at best, it can influence the nature of the Gauntlet and make the Umbra easier or harder to reach. But the Umbra itself is outside of the Consensus’ sphere of influence. If you could get large settlements of humans with Avatars living in the Umbra without turning into spirits and losing their Avatars, you might eventually change this; but that’s a lot of conditions that we’re nowhere close to achieving. Spirits (and by extension werewolves) probably have additional protection against the Consensus due to their connection to the Umbra. Wraiths are safely hidden away in an Umbral layer and are beyond the Consensus’ reach.

                                                            Changelings are an interesting case, in that they arguably are being edited out of existence by a hostile Consensus of humanity; that is, after all, a major theme of Changeling: the Dreaming: people by and large don’t believe in fairies anymore, and the fae are suffering for it. Put another way, Banality is arguably a manifestation of the Consensus — at least in part. The fae are probably unusually vulnerable to the Consensus due to the strong connection between the Dreaming and human dreams, which are themselves closely related to belief.
