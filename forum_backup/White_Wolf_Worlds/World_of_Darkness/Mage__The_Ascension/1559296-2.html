    The true key to the flaws of the Technocracy was summed up by William Blake "May God bless and you keep/from single vision and Newton's sleep." The Technocracy is the most powerful group of Mages stuck in the "my way or the highway" mentality. And when someone takes that attitude towards you the highway always looks good.

    The Technocracy sees itself as holding the one true answer, the Void Engineers aren't nearly as narrow minded as the other four so they seem the most human. If the other convenions perceived the benefits of multiple viewpoints they could reach out to the Traditions and find ways to promote a better more sane world.

    If that happened, the Disparates would step up and be the villains. After all, about half of those organizations are as close minded as the Technocracy.

                Originally posted by DarkKing View Post
                The masses don't get any of the 'hyper science' goodies the Technocracy has up their sleeves. Allegedly under the pretense of Consensus not being able to accept their existence at the moment.
                It depends on how you define the state of Consensus in your game. In M20, 3 scenarios are proposed for the ST: 1) The Technocracy Won: the pervasive belief in technology subjugate any mystical paradigm and it would be something like you said, which would really cast doubt in the Technocracy's good intentions in not giving their hypertech to the Masses; 2) Technocratic Belief Dominates Certain Parts of the World: this one punch holes in the notion of a Technocratic Consensus and the existence of Reality Zones would make the Technocracy be more careful with when, where, and how to introduce hypertech in regions of the world where it would generate Paradox/Rejection/Correction; 3) Reality's in Flux: this scenario laughs at the idea of any Consensus, giving the opportunity to make vulgar magick into coincidental magick and the opposite for ceratin kinds of technology.

                Another thing I don't think is accurate is to frame the conflict between the Traditions (or any mystic Mage) and the Technocracy as an issue of the existence of technology. Since Guide to The Traditions, technology is said to be present in the ranks of the Council, even beyond the halls of the Society of Ether and the Virtual Adepts. The presence of technology in the Traditions is even more pervasive by the time of M20 in Lore of The Traditions, even being a major source of inner conflict in some groups like the Verbena. The distinction between the Traditions and Technocracy is not one of "Technology Bad vs Technology Good" (if it was sure, let the VAs and Etherites have at it), but of what paradigms are allowed to exist and which ones should shape Consensus. The Technocracy is not merely the party of technology, but the party of Order, Stability and above all Control. The Science of the SoE and VAs is rooted in technology/technomagick, but is utterly anathema to the Technocracy because is not "ordely", stable and thus it cannot be properly controlled. The need for sanitization before any introduction of technocratic hypertech, as you called it, is founded on these principles. Sure, every technocrat would welcome progress with open arms but it needs to be stable progress. A SoE/VA victory would sure have technomagick (and could be fun, I don't deny that), but the technomagick of those compared to the Technocracy is so distinct that it cannot be equally compared (and I know everybody in Mage is doing the same thing, the same magick and all that, but the conflict is still there and they literally kill each other on that perceived difference).

                Besides these distinctions, the reason the Technocracy sanitizes its hypertech before introduction is rooted, beyond any relation to Consensus, in a deep distrust of the Masses, If they didn't harbor such distrust, they wouldn't even exist, they would allow any paradigm to exist and any discovery to be championed and let the Masses figure it out for themselves. This distrust is so great, that among the possible antagonists proposed for technocratic characters in metaplot options in Technocracy Reloaded is the section titled The Malignant Masses.

                                                    I'm pretty much with Ramnnesis on this one. Even though the Convention books do present Syndicate as being about personal wealth, I think they are more interesting as being about setting up the system as promoting Quintessence as Value to be shared, grown, or taken through mediums of exchange than as just wanting the wealth for themselves as dirty Capitalists.
                                                    Capitalism may be either the most efficient way of doing this, or may even be a Sleeper representation of their higher economic magic, in the same way Sleeper religions are not a one for one match with Chorus or Batini paradigms.
                                                    The Etherites are more pushing a utopia where work done generates and distributes Quintessence. The value of an item is the work put into it rather than the end sale or resale price. Intrinsic value instead of defined by exchange. The difference is as much about what shapes and defines reality as about mundane economic models.
