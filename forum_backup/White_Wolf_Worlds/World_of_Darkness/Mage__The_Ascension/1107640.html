      1. Depends a lot on the Euthanatos in question. What's their Essence? How does their Avatar manifest and interact with them? And, very importantly, what TYPE of Euthanatos are they? The Euthanatoi are a grab bag of different mystic practices that are only tenuously connected to each other along a continuum of shared beliefs without really having any set of beliefs common to the entirely of the Tradition. Was the character trained by those associated with the Chakravanti? Then a point of Arete would probably represent a greater understanding of their individual Dharma and, perhaps, the way it relates to World as a whole. How about the Pomegranate Deme? Then probably it involves some sort of mystic representation of a death/rebirth cycle in tune with the Orphic Cycle/the Eleusian Mysteries. If they're more Madzimbabwe in nature, then it should be something about their ancestors and remembering and actualizing the strengths of the civilization from which those ancestors are derived. And so on...

      One Euthanatos in one of our games pretty much had to physically die each time and come back with some sort of ordeal of the soul along the way. One of the ones was having his soul trapped in an Atrocity Realm from WtA where he experienced the battle in which he died again and again and again until his cabal was able to get him back. We figured out with the time differential it was about 40,000 deaths...

      Another was a collector and smith of guns. Her Avatar pretty much was The Gun. Her fate was entangled with a set of magic six guns from the Wild West, The Bastard's Guns. Her Seekings involved acquiring and mastering each of the five guns (Trustworthy, Trickshot, Ghost-Killer, Retribution and Grief). Eventually, she discovered that she actually was the co-creator of the guns, except for Grief, the Gun-That-Was-Never-Made and the pattern for the others. Then realizing that Grief was The Gun that was her Avatar. Then smithing the Sixth Gun, Loss, the Gun-That-Never-Was, which was the expression of her soul. That was for Arete 6. By which time she had become "The Armorer of Hell" and was pretty bad ass.

      2. Those sound like fine ideas. They need to be embraced somehow in the Focus and to a great degree in the particular type of Euthantos this one is, either from the past "types" that were thrown together and labeled "Euthanatos" by the Eurocentric leadership of the Nine and/or the particular path this particular Euthanatos is coming from. Banishing "bad" ghosts is about half the purpose of the Madzimbabwe.

      BTW, Miranda, the gun woman from the above, was definitely trained by a hard-core Chakravanti type, but, as you can see, she definitely took all that in a very different direction. For that matter, so was Robert, but he stayed VERY Chakravanti-like for the entire game.

      3. I'm not sure what you mean by this. I can't really speak to it from the examples above, because those were mages from earlier iterations of the game. I guess Miranda would definitely be all about guns, but I'm not sure that any of the guns would really count as a "unique focus" as per the M20 rules. Maybe Trustworthy, as she started the game with that one? But she was hell on wheels with all of the other guns (except Retribution.... Retribution was kind of terrifying) and with any other firearm and not so bad with the usual gambit of more archaic weaponry either. (She started the game in her mid-60s too. So pretty much no one was expecting the old woman to pull out a sword, gun or crossbow and blow them away...)

      Robert definitely didn't have a unique focus of any sort. I remember he typically carried around a hockey bag full of various implements of mayhem, both modern and archaic.

                              Also, who told you that you can't get revived after death? This is Mage. Bringing people back from the dead is entirely possible, though perhaps not easy. So long as a sufficiently Life-skilled mage is around, the character can bite it and have a chance to come back.

                              Further, we need to define what "death" means. Part of standard recruitment for the Euthanatoi is to either find someone who briefly died or was near death, or to put them through same as part of their initiation. They have an entire Rote - the Agama Sojourn - built around putting someone to virtual death, so their spirit can wander the Shadowlands for a time. It would be totally appropriate for a Euthanatos's Seeking to involve an Agama Sojourn, probably administered by an elder Wheel-turner.


                              As for Unique Instruments, it depends on who your character is and what they value. What object would be important to them? It could be their father's favorite lighter, an ancestor's skull, a necklace strung with the knuckle-bones of the ones who killed them (the first time), a Hindu statuette plundered from an ancient tomb, a scroll handed down from their Master, or even a one-of-a-kind Wonder. So long as the object matters to the character, to the point where they feel especially comfortable channeling magick through it, it can work as a Unique Instrument.

                              Magestuck: The Ascension

                                  • Medical equipment and other instruments of healing (ancient, antique and modern)
                                  From a cultural standpoint, these things are usually going to be Hindu/Vedic, Western and Southern African, Classical Greco-Roman, pre-Christian Irish/Celtic, pre-Christian Norse, Mayan, or various "Voodoo" cultures of the Caribbean and Brazil. They may or may not be mixed in some fashion with one of more flavors of Christianity as a matter of cultural evolution or just hiding old ways in plane sight.

                                  The aforementioned gods include (but are not limited to):
                                  • Shiva, the destroyer and transformer, and his wife, Parvati, goddess of love, fertility and devotion, as well as her manifestations as Durga, the demon slayer, and Kali, goddess of time, change and destroyer of evil

                                  • Ganesha, remover (and placer) of obstacles and lord of learning
                                  • Hades and Persephone, king and queen of the underworld, and she also being a goddess of the natural cycle of life, death and rebirth as embodied in the changing seasons
                                  • The Fates, who spin out, measure and cut every person's life and destiny
                                  • Legba, lord of the crossroads, patron of magic users, and controller of luck
                                  • Papa Guede, the lord of death and the death, the baron of Saturday, and psychopomp, and his wife, Maman Brigitte, protector of the dead and of graves

                                      Being Euthanatos is not an easy trek. First of all, there really isn't a Western European cultural archetype that's easy to grab on to like there are for the other Traditions (Order of Hermes "wizard", Verbena "witch", VA "hacker", Etherite "mad scientist", etc.). The closest are of limited helpfulness. Necromancers as they are portrayed in modern fantasy aren't really a thing in most mystic practices (and aren't really all that easy to do in Mage as it is.... Mage the Awakening's Death Sphere covers them very nicely though). They're almost entirely a modern conceit. And the lone assassin type is out there in our zeitgeist, but for the most part, is non-mystic in nature (very Technocracy - James Bond), or would just as easily or more likely belong to some other sect (Ghost Dog street assassin or one of the two Boondock Saints boys as Choristers of a particular sort) .

                                      Whether the Euthanatos have become the mish-mash of cultural mysticism that has often been portrayed in Mage (which is evident by the comprehensive list No One stated above) with a lot of stuff added in because, ya know, ENTROPY (whatever the Hell that's supposed to be) is sort of a moot point. In my games that's kinda-sorta a thing, but more artifactually, as Euthanatoi borrow from each other to try to grapple with some magical effects that aren't really in the oeuvre of their "parent type" (Chakravanti don't really do ghosts so much, but the Madzimbabwe do them hard core; the Pomedgranete Deme-ers are the aces at transitional states between worlds and probably are a bit more mentally oriented than the others; etc.). Whether that's a muddle that gets in the way of Focus development, it's a point of "generational tension" between the old guard and the newer sorts or it's a growing sub-faction within the Euthantoi is up to the vicissitudes of the game in question. (Being an anthropologist, I do overtly push for some adherence to some version of a historical mystic "type" with pretty much all the Traditions, but that's just me and my games... unless it's Etherites in space, in which case all bets are off....) The other way it works is the intersection of the modern and the mystic, with weapons going beyond the thris, the garotte, etc. and embracing the hand gun, the sniper rifle and so on. Perhaps another point of tension, but, it's really more of an adjustment. Sure, you're still essentially a Chakravanti but you've added a Glock-17 to balance your talwar and you're using both of them for relevant effects.

                                      Another point when considering the sub-groups is that even those larger groups that were there for the first Council and got shoved together into the "weird non-European spooky guys" Tradition can embrace sub-sub-types. The Chakravanti per se, not only include those who follow the practices of the historical Chakravanti sensu stricto but also probably embrace some version of the thugee archetype and the Aghori mystics as well, along with others (as an aside the "Fellowships" of pre-Traditions India would probably be sorted in terms of modern Traditions into mostly Cultists and Euthanatoi with a nod to some very ecumenical Chorister types, which is literally and figuratively whitewashing a set of ancient and modern cultures to a great degree... but that's what happened at the First Council, since the leaders WERE those European types...). Each of those, or, again, some synthesis of them, would suggest appropriate ways to approach various foci and depicting the praxis/in game description of effects for the various Spheres.

                                      If you start mixing and matching with Graeco-Roman Mystery Religions, African ancestor cults and so on (I guess vodoun Ghede types might fall in here, but, if the Bata'a are a thing, most of them types with such Foci might bend that way), you can see where the difficulty in building a Focus by Practice and, particularly, Instruments can come in. Which of my seven are from Ancient Greece? Which from medieval strega? Which from Aztec or Maya sacrifices? Which from the various groups of India? Which are modern takes on the above or just modern? Which are from voudon? Which are from Ancient Egypt (a group that NEVER gets mentioned as being one of the antecedents of the Euthanatoi, but, really, seems like a lock)? Are they from some group of common elements? Are they a mish-mash? Are the "pure" to one type? How individualized are Foci in this Mage game and how much do various members of a Tradition keep it "tight" to have some sense of commonality to work from? (And, if they don't do that, then what does it mean to be a Euthanatos or any other Tradition?)

                                      Of course, as a completely different take on the whole thing, its not too hard to see both of the main protagonists in the movie Mr. Right as Euthantoi and they don't really delve into ANY of these issues.

                                          Generally, the Euthanatoi fall under the archetype of Psychopomps, guides of the recently dead, and agents of Fate who help guide others to their destiny. Sometimes this means being an assassin who kills people. Other times it means being a healer, or a bodyguard, or matchmaker, or diplomat, or an exorcist, or a psychotherapist, or a teacher, or a monster slayer, or what ever else Fate decrees.

                                          The general commitment to this idea - that there is a Wheel of Life, Death and Rebirth, and that shame, suffering and other mental trauma interfere with souls journey on this Wheel - is what united the Indian and Greek factions during the Hellenistic period, and the two of them and the African faction through the Aksum trading network. And at the Grand Convocation, this already formed fellowship met more like minded groups from Northern Europe and Mesoamerica. Part of this may be the result of some sort of barely remembered prototype Ur-culture like the Akashic's Mount Meru or the Verbena's Wyck. Or it's the result of their Avatars reincarnating in clumps in certain regions. Or just random chance that worked out in their favor. It depends on how you want to set up your own game's setting, I guess.

                                          As for the cultural diversity, I've always been of the opinion that the five major cultural factions - the Chakravanti, the Heirochthonoi, the Madzimbabwe, the Aided (and Keepers of Yggdrassil), and the Yum Cimil - prefer to keep a certain level of cultural purity, in so far as a member of the Natatapas is going to going to stick with the Shaivism derived methods of yoga, ritual dance, and martial arts she's familiar with and was taught during her initiation, while a Yum Cimil shaman will stay with traditional Mayan rituals, tools and symbols. But it's inevitable that cultures shift, change or intermingle. So an ex-gangbanger from Chicago who Awakened during a near death experience after being shot on a street corner (i.e., a crossroads) and "brought back" by Baron Samedi (who he sees his Avatar as) to join the Ta Kiti as a conjure man and agent of the Loa, was probably trained in Voodoo ritual magic by his mentor, but also incorporates symbols of the Christian faith he grew up with, as well as his gun (because he's intimately aware of it's power as a symbol of life and death, and of the gateway between the two). And he may also dabble in some aspects of technomagic, just because he finds it interesting. Or not. He's still drawing a lot of his base material from the Madzimbabwe's African belief systems, even if via the Afro-Caribbean culture of voodoo, but there's still something of a modern urban American spin on it.

                                          I recall a reason for Egyptian lore and beliefs not being included in the Euthanatoi given somewhere. It may have been in the book of Crafts, or in one of the early write ups of the Ahl i Batin, in which the two groups still had some sort of eternal grudge match going on.

                                          As for Entropy, I don't use it. For almost a decade, I've gone with Fate instead, as I feel it's a better representation of what the Euthanatoi (and the other Traditions in their use of the Sphere) are about. (I have long suspected that Entropy was used as a sphere name because around 1992 and 1993, it was a buzz word among those who liked to talk about complex mathematical stuff after Jurassic Park made chaos theory trendy.) On the other hand, I do use a sixth Euthanatos faction, the Entropians, who are modernist technomancers entirely focused on the mathematical aspects of Fate and probability, as well as doing the whole high tech "Flatliners" style exploration of death and the afterlife.

                                          Such are my musings for today, at least.

                                          Voltaire, "Tolerance" (1764)
