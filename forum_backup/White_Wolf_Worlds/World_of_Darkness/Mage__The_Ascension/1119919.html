          In revised:

          1. They look similar but are very different.
          Sorcerers use a framework or paradigm of sorts, much like mages, a techno-sorcerer won't throw fireball uttering a spell, but use a contraption as "focus". The difference it's that Sorcery is static. If you know how to throw a fireball, you don't know how to throw a thunder nor how control fire, you buy these separately.

          2. By paradigm, kung-fu people go to Akashicks, Hackers go to VA. In general Traditions/Technocrats do not allow Sorcerers to go up in rank a lot. In those factions that have a power ladder, Sorcerers are stranded in the first steps, they usually are above normal humans, but never ascend to the levels of Masters and Adepts.

          3. Yes, it's possible. A lot of Hermetics start their careers as Sorcerers

          4. Technically, it's a separate form of Numina (human powers). But they're very similar to Sorcerers, they use Willpower to cast and, ussualy, can't do rituals (but sometimes they can).

                  In revised, the number of awakened mages is severely reduced because of the Avatar Storm and over meta-plot related tragedies. Because of this, Sorcerers should have a much greater relevance as they are much greater in number, and so much more readily available for whatever the traditions may require to be done.

                      1) The way Sorcerer Revised describes it is the difference between "Affinitive" (Awakened) and "Linear" (Sorcery) approaches. The former - the power of True Magick (with the 'K') - is open-ended and flexible, as the Mage reweaves Reality according to her particular desires at the moment. The latter - the Static power of Sorcery - comes in more direct, rigid Paths (much like how Vampire does its blood magic), where individual powers are delineated in order. Rituals afford Sorcerers with some degree of flexibility, and the regular Path powers are not completely fixed. But these ultimately take more time and effort to develop, than what a Mage can do on the fly. Both a Mage and Sorcerer might be able to throw fire balls, but if they wanted to wreath their swords with fire, the Mage can do it relatively quickly (depending on their Focus and how far they need to stretch their theory of Magick), whereas a Sorcerer will need to find or invent a Ritual that can do that, which requires a great deal of research. Moreover, in all but a few cases, the power ceiling for Sorcerers - the upper reaches of what they can achieve - are lower than what a Mage can, and require more of an investment in Experience. Many individual Sorcerer Paths create five ranks of advancement out of what a Mage might get from gaining their second or third dot in one Sphere, depending on the Path and Sphere.


                      2) Sorcerer Revised makes the Linear practitioners an integral part of many Traditions, Technocratic Conventions, and Disparate Crafts. They are the broad bedrock of initiates into their respective Paradigms, standing just above the Consors and other non-magical helpers. Many people on this forum will scoff and whinge at the idea of the groups being populated by Sorcerers, but I consider it perfectly logical. A person only requires time, effort, and instruction to become a Sorcerer - its powers come from the observance of rotes, and thus depend only on knowledge and skill. A Mage, however, is harder to find, because their power is, in a huge part, a product of their enlightened understanding of Reality. The Awakening, itself, is a bottleneck for Mage numbers. It's only reasonable, then, that when Mage groups depend upon the spread of their Paradigm for their long-term viability, they would have a vested interest in teaching as many people how to use their Paradigm's magic as is practical.

                      The degree to which the groups make Sorcerers varies from group to group. The Verbana pass along magical theory and spells from one generation to the next, as a matter of course. The Society of Ether, meanwhile, is restricted in how many technosorcerers they can produce, as every Etherite's understanding and application of Science(!) is idiosyncratic. The Order of Hermes, meanwhile, probably has some of the best recruiting frameworks available, and teaches High Sorcery to all initiates (since that just makes for good Hermeticism, regardless of Awakening). But Hermeticism is complicated, and there exists an ethos within the Western Occult Tradition that discourages spreading knowledge too freely; they make learning Hermetic magic harder than it perhaps needs to be, in order to weed out the dabblers, the stupid, the lazy, the insane, and those just looking for quick power. They see a responsibility in doing so, for the greater good. A similar thing happens with Akashics and their instruction in both magic and Do (which, let's face it, are partly identical in their mind); learning Do requires a life-style change and iron-clad commitment.

                      All this meaning that while groups have a lot of Sorcerers, others might have fewer.


                      3) It's entirely possible for Sorcerers to Awaken. When this happens, their pre-existing Focus and body of knowledge transfers from Paths and Rituals, to Spheres and Rotes. The original Sorcerer book had actual rules for transfering dots in Paths to other supernatural powers, in the event the Sorcerer Awakened, got Embraced into vampirism, or died and became a Wraith. The Revised book doesn't have much of that, but it shouldn't be all that hard to do for a Storyteller on their own. Just convert their Paths and Rituals into Experience, and let them buy Spheres (and perhaps other supernatural Traits), with a mind towards logical places where knowledge would transfer over. (The Paths of Shapeshifting and Healing would both contribute to the Life Sphere, as would some of Hellfire, if the character had a version of it related to animating attack plants or something).

                      Not all Sorcerers will Awaken - a Sorcerer might achieve heights of linear power without ever Awakening - but it does happen. Usually when a character makes a breakthrough in their studies that completely reorders their understanding of their Art.


                      4) Psychics are mechanically identical to Sorcerers, save that the foci/Instruments they employ are their raw mental concentration. That, and they can't use Rituals (unless they take a Merit that says they can). A Psychic is either a person with some inborn talent, or who worked to unlock their power with training. Or both, since a character might develop one psychic Path naturally, before working to expand their repertoire through effort and study. There are a number of Paths built more around psychics, but Sorcerer Revised leaves players and storytellers free to transfer Paths from regular Sorcerers into Psychic ones, and vice versa, if they really want.

                          The key difference is, Hedge Magic is essentially an alternate form of engineering, whereas Magick is reality-warping pretending to be alternate engineering.

                          That is to say, there are certain practices that still work in the modern world, and while they are sort of magic, on a certain level they are just as mundane as building a car. If you think in a certain way and pattern and sequence while also chanting in a certain way and sequence and waving your hands in a certain pattern and sequence, it produces results that most would call magical. These things are difficult to learn because you have to precisely control not only what you do, but what you think and feel. But other than that, what Hedge Mages do is more or less as mundane as fixing a car.

                          True Mages, on the other hand, warp reality. They may do some of those alternate engineering things hedge mages do, but doing so is just a psychological crutch to help them summon the confidence needed to use their reality-warping power.

                              Hedge Magic is using gaps in reality to push through something 100% real; when a Sorcerer conjures fire, you can disbelieve it all you like but that DID just happen and nothing is going to happen to them (well apart from the logical consequences of their actions). Sorcerers don't deal with Paradox, but they are limited in what they can do, they are following linear rules that exist already in reality.

                              So a master of Conjuration can move an Elephant about the room and pull his antique pistol (mounted in his office) out of his coat pocket while running from a Vampire, but he CANNOT teleport. A Mage with the Correspondance and Forces necessary to pull an Elephant around a room or conjure a pistol would be able to simply teleport away or explode the Vampire in a fiery blast...

                              Sorcerers have to pay Willpower to use their powers also, and they live in the real world and have much more low-key stories... they're fighting threats that matter to them, not something as metaphysical as a battle for reality.
