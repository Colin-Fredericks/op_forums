    My problem with the Nephandi

    04-04-2016, 06:01 AM
    Almost finished the Mage 20, and been loving almost all of it. The idea of so many everyday actions - such as putting on perfume before a meeting - being actual rituals, is fantastic.

    ALMOST all of it. Do you think there are many humans who simultaneously have all the following qualities:

    1) They are suicidal, seeking their own destruction and that of the world.

    2) They think over the long term.

    3) They are highly disciplined, discrete, and good at hiding their own personality.

    4) They are immune to social pressure from those they interact with day after day after day.

    5) They are wantonly sadistic and enjoy the suffering of others.

    6) They coordinate perfectly with each other, and have an efficient hierarchy that disseminates information and instructions while leaving minimal traces. This means they have minimal infighting.

    7) They are mages, so filled with hubris and arrogance.

    And so, the Nephandi fail for me. Big time. Finding one suicidal-planner-discreet-disciplined-sadistic-bureaucratic-hubristic person might be possible, or a small group, but a whole faction? We should believe that, alone of any large groups of humans throughout history, ones who have solved the whole "efficient organisation with minimal overhead" problem are the self-destructive devil worshippers? If you think of them as human beings with their own twisted wants and agendas, ask yourself "what makes them work together better than any other group"?

    Basically, we've never seen anything like this in human history, and the Nephandi are supposed to be able to keep it up for millennia. It's beyond ridiculous. And if the Nephandi had that level of organisational coherence, there would be no need for infiltration, they could take down the others in quasi-open battle.

    And adding them as infiltrators only seems to lessen the interest of corruption or amorality in the other organisations.

    The Technocracy reignites the pogrom! Is this the moral thing to do? How are the leaders grappling with the difficult decisions of... oh wait, it's just Nephandi infiltration.

    The traditions fall apart and some start really exploiting the sleepers! Is this the inevitable tensions in an organisation on the brink? Where does expediency start and morality end? Should we... oh wait, it's just Nephandi infiltration.

    I think you get my point. Now, *accusations* of Nephandi influence are perfectly fine, or the occasional Mage going to far into demeonology. Humans accusing other humans of being demon worshippers is credible and likely, as is some turning to immorality for an extra edge. But for me, as an organised group, they achieve the rare double feat of being simultaneously unbelievable and uninteresting.

                              The Nephandi chapter, because it is meant almost exclusively for NPCs, is riddled with propaganda and fearful story hooks. There's no need to present the great misunderstandings of the past the way the Tradition and Technocracy chapters did, because the Nephandi aren't running from their past. There's no need to point out where their master plan is weak because there is no master plan. The result is that they look like powerful, perfect infiltrators who are always lurking around every corner.

                              And I have to say that the chapter really bugs me for precisely that reason. The Nephandi aren't special. They aren't better than anyone else in Mage, they are prone to the same hubris and the same mistakes. So it really drives me crazy that the chapter often implies they are just so much more successful.

                              However, there are two things to remember that mitigate some of the problems.

                              The first is that the Nephandi are not a united front. So to revisit your list, you don't find these traits in all Nephandi:

                              Originally posted by Cheesefondue View Post
                              Almost finished the Mage 20, and been loving almost all of it. The idea of so many everyday actions - such as putting on perfume before a meeting - being actual rituals, is fantastic.

                              ALMOST all of it. Do you think there are many humans who simultaneously have all the following qualities:

                              1) They are suicidal, seeking their own destruction and that of the world.

                              2) They think over the long term.

                              3) They are highly disciplined, discrete, and good at hiding their own personality.

                              4) They are immune to social pressure from those they interact with day after day after day.

                              5) They are wantonly sadistic and enjoy the suffering of others.

                              6) They coordinate perfectly with each other, and have an efficient hierarchy that disseminates information and instructions while leaving minimal traces. This means they have minimal infighting.

                              7) They are mages, so filled with hubris and arrogance.

                              1) is always true, and 4) and 5) are mostly true, simply because the Nephandi are fundamentally and metaphysically altered by the Cauls. It's not that they are uniform or incapable of changing their minds, it's that their minds and souls have an inevitable gravity towards those (self) destructive impulses.

                              2) and 3) are traits they can have, but are actually not all that common.

                              6) is just wrong.

                              They can cooperate, but can also gleefully feed each other into the woodchipper. In fact I think there are more than a few lines in the chapter that imply their infiltration has more to do with sacrificing their pawns and scapegoating than to actual mastery at fitting in. There's really nothing a Nephandus won't destroy, including herself if it pushes her agenda.

                              7) is the real problem. The chapter really presents the Nephandi like they are the only ones who know what is going on. They are not only better and more honest than everyone else, they are the inevitable winners because the world is so corrupt they barely have to push to get it to topple. Everything is rapidly falling apart anyways, its just a stubborn refusal to admit it that keeps people going. A paper thin rationalization that's been waiting for someone to tear it apart...

                              For the last 10,000 years...

                              The second and most important thing to remember about the Nephandi chapter is that it is a lie. It's the World of Darkness, not as it is but as the Nephandi often see it. You are getting a little piece of the madness that makes them what they are. Far from lacking hubris, the chapter is thick with it. They are certain of their victory. They are certain of their rightness. There is no room for the possibility of failure, because how could that even happen?

                              And I'm sure they thought the same thing before their defeat at the end of WW2.

                              Are the Nephandi poised to start some serious shit? Sure. Are they the strongest faction? The best infiltrators? The ones about to win the entire pot? That's remains to be seen.

                                      I think a lot about the Nephandi ability to be "successful", despite having low numbers relative to other groups (at least in terms of Awakened Mages; this is not even counting the Sorcerers, Infernalists, cultists, and dupes the Nephandi surround themselves with, secretly direct, and sacrifice like pawns), is the fact that the Nephandi have a different Win Condition. In that sense, they are fighting a different battle than all the other factions, on a battlefield that favors them.                                               Originally posted by Ajax View Post

Based on the reasoning being used, the Traditions should have the easiest go of it.                                               Yes, because it's really that easy for someone - anyone - to Ascend, let alone their entire membership.

                                              The Traditions also have more than just personal Ascension as their goal. If that was it, they'd all retreat to Horizon realms and let the Technocracy have the world. What the Traditions want is to make their brand(s) of magick work on Earth again. They don't want to live in hiding, too afraid of Paradox and the watchful eyes of the Technocratic Union to perform anything but the most coincidental spells. Mages have the potential to do and be anything, yet are held back. They can't accept defeat, or surrender, or obscurity. They want to take back the world.

                                              Moreover, individual Traditions have moral and philosophical reasons for wanting to spread their Paradigm. The Celestial Chorus has its various religions, and want to combat the godlessness and sin present in the world. The Dreamspeakers have their various tribes, etc, that must be defended from an imperialistic mono-culture, one that would happily either destroy the ethnic groups or destroy their cultural identity. The Euthanatos see the Wheel in imbalance, and desire to serve its proper turning; healing or reforming those who can be saved, and giving the Good Death to those who cannot. Etc, etc.

                                              Not to mention bloody-minded pride and deep-seated animosity. The Traditions have been fighting too hard, for too long, to let the battle end with anything but victory.

                                              What this means is that the Traditions don't have the option for such a pat, easy Win Condition. They want many things, and the Technocracy is directly in competition for those things. Power in many forms, resources, newly Awakened Mages to mold, the hearts and minds of the common man, the ability to dictate what is right and wrong, and many more things besides. These are battle grounds on which the Traditions and the Technocracy (and the Disparates) compete. They have Win Conditions that are incompatible, but only because they are finite.

                                              The Nephandi do not share those as Win Conditions. They don't want to control anything...they just want to break everything. And it's far easier to break something than it is to build. Building is what the other sects want, while Breaking is what the Nephandi want. In this, they have the flexibility to choose their battles and battlefields. While the Traditions must react if the Technocracy tries to claim ground in the former's sphere of influence (and vice versa), the Nephandi can attack in ways and at times no one is expecting, for purposes that no other rational actors would consider. It is for this reason that Nephandi rarely engage in "open" conflict with other sects: why fight the enemy on the field when you can undermine them from within? Why fight over a well when you can poison it instead?

Claiming every piece of destruction or degradation as a step towards the final Absolute sounds good, but it is empty propaganda. It denies the actual challenge in front of them.

                                                  Sure, turning a building into a pile of rubble is easy, but keeping it a pile of rubble is next to impossible. People rebuild, they learn, they grow. Every small victory is wiped away as humanity rises again, often to even greater heights. Win or lose, as long as the board exists sleepers and mages will play on it. The Nephandi line about the world being corrupt and dying? It's not true. Not yet.

                                                  That's why the real Nephandi struggle is always about changing the game itself. Summoning abominations from beyond to reshape the world, desecrating the very nature of humanity, and infiltrating and corrupting the other players aren't the easiest paths to victory, they are the only paths to victory. If it sounds like the Nephandi are pursuing Ascension level struggles, it is because that is exactly what they are trying to accomplish: Mass Descension. The world isn't what they say it is, so they have to change it.

                                                  Far from having the easiest win conditions, they have the hardest. Destruction is easy, making it mean something is the difficult part.

                                                      Cheesefondue - there are two main things that make the Nephandi in general believable to me (setting aside the M20 "they're everywhere!" thing).

                                                      First - remember the Cauls. They alter your soul so that destruction and decay is beautiful to you. Think of the thing that elevates your mind - fine art, exquisite wine, the love of a loved one; that's the feeling a Nephandi gets when they break someone down, when they harm. They're "immune to social pressure" in the same way that you don't stop loving Starry Night just because your circle of friends don't like art. They're "wantonly sadistic" in the same way that a person who loves amazing wine is likely to be subscribed to Wine Spectator and have an excellent wine cellar - it's the natural outcome of the internal change. The evil of the Nephandi is startlingly natural once they've got the inverted Avatar.

                                                      Second - also about the Cauls - the inversion cannot be undone. Every time they corrupt another, deeply and truly in the Cauls, they permanently gain an ally, even after that Avatar's current host dies. All the Traditions and Technocracy can do is cut off the damage with Gilgul, and that's if they're lucky. In the truly long term, the Nephandi always win the numbers game.
