    Are there characteristics that increase the likelihood of Awakening?

    11-10-2016, 11:06 PM
    Earlier I made an ill-advised post about why I house-rule a minimum of 3 Intelligence when I run Mage. There was strong and pretty unanimous disagreement there, which is fine. It made me wonder, though--do people here think the only thing that distinguishes the Awakened from Sleepers is Awakening itself? Is it completely random?

    I've always seen mages as being exceptional even before Awakening. Like nascent Exalted but to a lesser degree. For one thing, the starting Willpower for a mage is 5 at minimum, which if I remember right is above the 90th percentile for humans. So there's at least one attribute that helps stir Avatars to Awaken or attracts Avatars that are likely to Awaken. Backgrounds aren't really a set trait, but the fact that mages have higher starting Backgrounds than any other splat suggests that mages have a breadth of experience above what is normal. And of course, like every other WoD splat, they have higher-than-average Attributes though they can be distributed as diversely as anyone's.

    The mechanical trait differences end there. There are no Nature requirements either--but is it equally likely that a Follower or Conformist would be a Mage as a Rebel or Architect? That seems implausible to me, I'd expect certain types of people to be more prone to rejecting Consensus reality than others. The fantasy trope of the generic protagonist who's awesome by sheer luck doesn't seem to work very well in Mage.

                                      To re-orient the conversation a bit:

                                      There's something worth thinking about the history and metaphysics of the setting that were crafted to create the current "now" of the setting. Who Awakens and how they Awaken is something that's supposed to have changed radically over the centuries. In a setting where belief and reality are codependent reality shifted in response to belief; as mages came to believe that the old ways were being lost and the continuity of their traditions were under threat, their fears became manifest.

                                      To give that some context, in the Mythic Ages, mages awakened in a seemless fashion as the Psychopomps knew exactly the right avatar to link to the right individual such that when prompted by their cultural beliefs in how one achieves enlightenment occurred, the mage's perceptions of the process were one of a contiguous process. Early mages didn't realize there were such things as "avatars" because the fusion of human and avatar was so perfect it never felt like an external addition to their being once it gain power along with them. For untold times, teachers instructed those with potential until their power awakened, and it seemed as natural as any other craft or endeavor for this to be the case.

                                      Then things changed. For whatever reasons the avatar-human matching process began to crack. Mages began to be aware of having an avatar as a separate entity because their avatars were no longer indistinguishable from themselves. The Order of Reason had something to sink their teeth into besides being splinter Hermetics; evidence that mages were playing with forces far more malleable than previous generations really thought, and reality itself was the playing field mages were actually fighting on. As the OoR's success in cracking open reality bore fruit in their war with the Traditions, it also meant that the why of who awakens changed as well. The growing mismatch of avatar to person, and the formation of Reality Zones, created the space for more and more unpredictable and seemingly random awakenings. The old studious methods still worked in their way, but it became more and more clear as the Enlightment era kicked in that it wasn't how things worked any more and was just one path among many.

                                      And change continued. Spontaneous awakening from personal epiphany grew to be the norm, with experienced mages fighting to find and recruit over bolstering their numbers by training. The dichotomy of avatar and mind grew larger and increasingly frayed perceptions of nascent mages to force epiphanies away from the tutelage of others to generate such events. The new generations of mages bucked against the ways of the older generations because the world had literally changed and the rules of reality they had to deal with were not bound by ancient wisdom's teachings. The continuity of mentor to student frayed as it simply became less realistic and few of the older generations were capable of adapting to stay relevant (though a new middle generation born to this new world are beginning to be the new teachers with that test in front of them).

                                      Which leaves mages where the game starts. Anyone might have the spark of awakening in them. Individuality has taken over from tradition to constantly refuse predictability. Only the must basic fundamental metaphysical truths dictate who can awakening in a near tautological way.

                                      And of course, stepping back out of the game, this is completely on purpose. The game wants mages to be anybody from every walk of life. And most of what I said is later additions and retcons the game as offered to explain how things got the desire point of the original premise.

                                          Is it random (inherently)? No. Can it be random? Absolutely. Some Awakenings are the proverbial definition of random -- a causal-but-not-preconceived event happens which leads to the Awakening of a mortal's Avatar/Fount/Eidelon/Sahu/part-of-them-that-is-god/what-have-you. No rhyme, no reason, nothing "special" about the event/mortal in question (save, of course, for them now having the (A)wakened capacity to work magic(k). For others, on the opposite end of the spectrum, there is a conscious pursuit of mystical (A)wakening/understanding, where in the pursuit of "magic(k)", proper, or the enlightenment of the self/soul which as a byproduct stirs the mortal's capacity for mystical power/awareness. And for a number of others it can be for a variety of facets/reasons: they happen to have as part of their spiritual make-up a powerful god-shard (Avatar:4+) which has a degree of bias towards Awakening due to its innate nature, memories/mystical workings from the past, being proximal to other god-shards that resonate strongly with it, possessing a powerful (D)estiny or being meta-consciously aware of its own previous incarnations, etc and et al; they have no innate pre-bias towards (A)wakening, but happen to -- via family, friends/acquaintances, or similar -- be raised/keep company with other supernatural beings and consequently find their (A)wakening somewhat more likely to occur than if they had lived a more mundane life; and lastly (though rarely), some mages (A)waken because of the direct mystical workings of others, with the intent of their (A)wakening being the goal (in some cases because such is sought in the specific individual in question or alternately/more abstractly as a blanket pattern attempting to suss out an (A)wakening *somewhere* in *somebody*, and then indoctrinated (or at least guided/swayed) by those who sought out such (A)wakenings caused by "x" efforts on their parts).

                                          Some/Many of those things have much to do with innate drive/(I)ntelligence, some do not. Seeing, however, (M)ages as inherently having Int:3+ is itself a matter of paradigm/bias-of-perception, which relative to an in-narrative source is perfectly fine (it often occurred that many Hermetics would reject less intelligent/savvy pupils -- and all-but-disregard out of hand the "savage" practitioners, such as would be identified as Dreamspeakers/Spirit Talkers, Verbena/"The Old Faith", and similar -- who didn't meet their "academic" level of intellectualization/understanding... this, of course, was hubris on their parts, and a number of DS/ST/V/ToF, et al, mages were every bit as (A)wakened as they were, if not as intellectually gifted (might only have Int:1-2, reflecting less of a properly intellectual understanding of things)), but on a meta-mechanic level is an indication of not grasping the inherent diversity of what, exactly, The Awakening can mean, as well as the myriad ways it can manifest with or without any regard to the mortal through whom such (A)wakening manifests, (Again, see Nan from AHS: Coven, as she -- though there are other examples in various fictions -- is a very good example of someone who likely "only" has an Int:2 (possibly one, given that her Per might be rather high, and her Wits -- though she could be surprised/flat-footed -- might have been a 3 if she had survived to blossom into her full (A)wakening.)

                                          Well, as far as the Backgrounds go, I can totally help you with that one: back in the Long Ago, in the Before Time (the old, very original forum, pre-2004 -- and, more specifically, at a couple points between 1998 and 2003), The Powers That Be (at that time) commented on the extra points for Backgrounds -- they were to give a little room for adjudication relative to the Avatar Background. One could not put anything in it, if one so chose (because some mages have only the "spark" within them, rather than even a single-steady candle flame), but the inherent mechanics involved were more tied to the game structure than, say, with Generation relative to vampires. So a couple extra points were added to potentially flesh out their mystical background and/or capacities. It went hand-in-hand with how Mage characters have double the number of starting dots compared to the two other major splats (vampires/werewolves, who started normally with 3 dots in "Shit I Can Do"), balancing how most all other splats have a range of inherent powers (healing, shape-changing, superhuman stats, etc.) that mages don't innately have or start with. The extra Sphere dots and points in Backgrounds were just that: a game-mechanic balancer, with no other inherent meaning beyond that. (It's similar to how PC mortals, in older editions, start with 21 Freebie points vs. the standard 15, so has to give them a little more flexibility of (b)ackground that their other, inferior dot-spreads and lack of supernatural ability handicapped them with.)

(Note how when using the Equal For All option for Sorcerers and other "mortal" characters *everybody* starts with the "standard" spread of points relative to what they are: ordinary mortals get the 7/5/3, 13/9/5 and 15 Freebies spread, with certain tweaks for magic (6 points for Sorcerers vs. more limited points for other "mortal" characters -- gypsies, and similar -- who might have other inherently mystical abilities/perks/what-have-you relative to Joe Public) and similar.

For a considerable number of others, however, their innate talents may simply blossom (para)naturally via their innate souls (Avatars) Awakening(tm) and they in turn manifesting the capacity for True Magick(tm). No studying. No pre-existing bias towards mysticism (or, in rarer cases, meta-science). No active pursuit of magic(k) in any way, shape or form. *Remember*: a great number of members of many of the Traditions came to be so not because of the tutelage that they, the Traditions, offered, or were pursued to via the character's seeking *them* out, but because *they* discovered a given "mage" (who may or may not have identified themselves by that or similar terms) and swayed/indoctrinated her into their fold/cause. Many house-witches, street-shamans and road-warriors (A)waken to their magic(k) without any predisposition (that is, as understood within a mundane context; as mentioned further up, their inherent spiritual make-up may have had plans since millennia before they were born... but that's a separate concept...) whatsoever. Even the mechanical conceit of the Willpower:5+ rating (the average person having a WP in the 2-3/4 range, per several books indicating so) doesn't necessarily reflect any active drive on the character's part (though it can ,of course, for any *given* character in particular), but rather is another relative concept reflecting how the character likely had to have some above-average sense of determination and/or self and/or mental focus and/or "I'm not going to lose my mind to this crazy shit" about them so that (credibly speaking) they didn't just go insane out-the-gate when they Awakened(tm), in whatever manner, and discovered that the world isn't quite what they thought it was.

                                          None of that has to involve the the fantasy trope of the generic protagonist who awesome by sheer luck -- there are a great number of other concepts that exist far outside what you are suggesting... though, relative to Mage, there *are* some of the above lucky devils/Harry Potters/the sisters from Charmed/what-have-you, wherein they are awesome because they have high Avatar/Destiny ratings (Harry and the sisters), and have an essential shit-ton of helpful backgrounds out-the-gate (such as with the Charmed girls, who started with a Chantry, Sanctum, Node (one of the strongest in the mundane world for their mythology), Library/Wonder (their super-spellbook), and helpful spirits of their preceding witch ancestors, along with the Lucky and/or Charmed Existence merits... the latter, of course, humously appropriate; Harry had... well... yeah -- he had *a lot* going for him, actually, even if it doesn't list the same, exactly, as with the other example). In those cases, the "XP spent" wasn't always so much about gaining raw power/strength (though in some instances it was), but more so coming to understand *how* to control or utilize the considerable strength/range of powers they had from their initial "awakening" (the sisters had a shitload of starting dots, magically-speaking). That trope, while I wholly agree is over-used by many people and often used as a mentally lazy "just 'cus" excuse for some players (and not a few STs...) relative to their characters, isn't invalid on-its-face as being legitimate relative to Mage's setting -- it *is* just as legitimate there as the one you are suggesting, and especially so compared to the "linear, flat-earth" settings such as in DnD (not bad-mouthing DnD, by any means, but simply cross-citing its metaphysics because they are "flat earth", and not the deeper, more flexible understanding of Reality(tm) that Mage(tm) deals with, in general).

                                          As far as Natures... there can be a sizeable number of Conformist mages, ones who were indoctrinated into a given mystical order (the CC, OoH, and certain other groups are more likely than others to fit this bill) and express their Enlightened Will(tm) by supporting the belief system in question. (They can be Conformists, Supporters, Architects (which, in-and-of-itself, doesn't necessary mean anything that causes the mage to "press against the world"), Traditionalists, and even the likes of Child, Loner, CareGiver and similar-such, can be a mage's inherent Nature, and said character in no way "challenge" Reality(tm), as such, but simply support their own beliefs in ways that can be subtle.)

                                          All of these are very important distinctions....

                                                          Which leaves mages where the game starts. Anyone might have the spark of awakening in them. Individuality has taken over from tradition to constantly refuse predictability. Only the must basic fundamental metaphysical truths dictate who can awakening in a near tautological way.
