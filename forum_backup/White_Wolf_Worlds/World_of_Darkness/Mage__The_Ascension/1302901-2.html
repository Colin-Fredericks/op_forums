                                    Firstly if the 'laser gun' is a focus for a technocratic mage then there are many reasons it can't be used by a random citizen - they don't have the skills, training, aptitude, etc. This is extremely easy to hand wave, a mage's laser gun focus cannot be a mass produced item because there aren't any mass produced laser guns in the consensus.                                                             Yeah; that explanation never really sat well with me, either. I tend to prefer the take that Technocrats specifically (and to a somewhat lesser extent, technomancers in general) make heavy use of Wonders, particularly Gadgets and Devices, and most of their gear falls into that category rather than Instruments for their Spheres. This is supported by the fact that Technocrats have a way around the Willpower point cost of creating a Device, and the fact that they're the only faction in the game with enough resources to justify the Requisitions Background. Indeed, if not for the needless nerfing of mortal protagonists in the game (e.g., not only do they lose out on getting Arete/Enlightenment and Spheres, but they're also short-changed in terms of Attributes, Abilities, and Backgrounds), one could arguably play a highly successful Technocratic agent who is an Extraordinary Citizen rather than an Enlightened Scientist.

                                                            Which is not to say that Technocrats don't use Spheres; they do. But they generally don't use the Spheres in the field; and when they do, it should generally represent either some sort of McGuyvering or gadgeteering (i.e., cobbling together a gadget), or it should represent a procedure, with the central element being a process that they go through to get a desired result. That is, their Instruments aren't as important as what they do with them.

                                                            Another “house rule” that I'd consider would be using the Spheres in a manner similar to Adventure!'s Dramatic Editing: the mechanics are the same as a mage casting a Coincidental Effect, but the justification for it is that the Technocrat is just lucky or competent (“Magick Enhancing Abilities” would be a big part of Technocratic Dramatic Editing, with various Coincidental Effects negating difficulty penalties with which the agent would otherwise have to contend). As with how it was described in Adventure! (as opposed to Trinity Continuum's Talents, where it's described as subconscious probability manipulation), play it up as just a metagaming system to play up a genre convention, as opposed to being a measurable in-game phenomenon. This is also similar to how more recent games like Fate and Cortex Plus have narrative-driving game systems in them, without even a hint that expenditures of Fate Points or Plot Points represent any sort of in-game phenomenon.

                                                            (I'd also allow an Enlightened Scientist with the Requisitions Background to use it as a “Schroedinger's Wonder”, using a minor variation on the above Dramatic Editing option to specify on the spot just what he requisitioned before heading out on the mission — the difference being that the Effect doesn't have to be Coincidental; instead, he makes his Enlightenment roll and, if he succeeds, retroactively assigns points from his Requisitions pool and whips out a Gadget or Device that in turn performs the desired Effect. He then has that gear for the remainder of the mission.)
