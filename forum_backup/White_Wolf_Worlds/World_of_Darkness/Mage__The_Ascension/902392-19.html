    405) To Be Proud Of My Time [ Paradox Flaw | Time ] When the affected Mage asks other persons about what time it is - whether about the time period of the day ( before or after the noon, close to evening etc. ) or the hour - they don't hear the answer clearly at all, not getting the information about what time it is currently, unless they ask four or more times in a row. This occurs regardless whether a person is saying a correct or an incorrect information about what time it is.
    This Paradox Flaw only applies to questions that the affected Mage asks about current time ; it doesn't apply to inquiries about at what time an (for example) event has happened in the past or an (for example) event will happen in the future. The affected Mage has to use her own means of checking the current time, instead of asking other persons about it ; they can also use other persons' means of checking the current time - like looking at a watch belonging to another person.

            407) Roots Run Deep [Flaw or Burn]: When a mage draws upon the strength of the earth, that connection can become quite physical. Especially prevalent among Verbana, Bata'a, and other nature-focused magicians, this Flaw causes the mage's feet to become stuck to one spot, rooted to the ground. Literally, as in with physical roots that erupt from the soles of their feet. The character is, in that way, as much planted in the earth as the vegetation she manipulates. If the mage allows herself to remain where she is rooted until the duration ends, this backlash is merely a Flaw. If she tries to uproot herself, however, it becomes a Burn, as pulling the roots out is a painful process.


            408) Plain, Not Vanilla [Flaw]: The mage's palette is stymied, rendering all food and drink as flavorless. For the gourmand or the sensualist, this can be a devastating affliction, any sense of taste vanishes. Can be even worse for a mage whose magickal senses take the form of taste sensations, as this Flaw blunts the tongue.


            409) Choking For Fashion [Burn]: When garment choices are ascribed with magickal power, the forces of Paradox can use that power against the mage. Articles of clothing wrap around their wearer's neck, attempting to strangle them to death. Common clothing items at risk are collars, scarves, loose sleeves, and sometimes even belts. Some woven pieces - like sweaters or the like - will even unravel themselves, and wrap their threads around the mage's neck like strings of piano wire or thick cords.

            Magestuck: The Ascension

                    411) Adrift In The Wind [Flaw]: Reacts to when a mage uses magick to change into air, mist, or other insubstantial material, or when she lessens the effect of gravity on herself. The mage becomes susceptible to wind, with even the lightest breezes pushing against her as if she were composed of flat paper or a puff of smoke. Buffeted by the breeze, she finds it difficult to remain standing, and strong gusts can send her flying against her control.


                    412) A Reverse Last Unicorn [Flaw]: Sometimes, purity in thought and deed can work against a mage. Afflicting mages of the Celestial Chorus, Knights Templar, Wu Lung, Akashayana, and Verbana, the character takes on characteristics of the mythical Unicorn (or its Eastern counterpart, the Ki-rin). A single horn sprouts from her forehead, which grows longer as the backlash worsens. Fur of white or sometimes other colors grows in patches across the body, thick as that of an equine. Feet may shift into hooves, while the face may elongate. The most severe forms of this backlash leave the mage half-horse, barely recognizable as a person and in no way fit to wander the mortal world. Which is probably for the best, as the unicorn-esque character may find herself subject of hunts, by both mortals and supernatural beings.


                    413) A Real Hot Rod [Burn]: Automobiles - powered by internal combustion and known for "burning rubber" - are elementally associated with Fire as much as Metal. When a mage channels Awakened power through a car, truck, or other motorized vehicle, this magical correspondence can manifest to the mage's detriment. Upon touching the metal chasis of their car - or another metal object, if appropriate - the character is burned as if by red hot metal. The spot in the metal where the skin touched will glow bright and hot for a few seconds after the Burn; the character may look up from their scalded hand, only to find a hand-shaped spot of red hot steel where they touched the car.

                    Magestuck: The Ascension

                                416) Amber Preservation [Flaw or Burn]: Time is the most notoriously tricky of Spheres, when it comes to Paradox. Perhaps more often than any other application, mages are obsessed with the idea of slowing or freezing time. Whether it be to suspend magick for another occasion, halt adversaries in their tracks, suspend people in a sleep of ages, move around in a sea of stopped time, or, most especially, to arrest the march of age upon their own bodies and minds. The sands of time are often the enemy of the Awakened, and they seek to stop those grains from slipping through their fingers. Paradox, ever the force of dramatic irony, will at times answer this desire.

                                With the force of this backlash, a reddish orange sap erupts from the mage's body, often from the nose, mouth, or pores. It flows across the body and coats the mage, skin and clothes and all. The greater the backlash, the more sap. Once as much viscous liquid as is called for appears, it hardens rapidly into a thick sheet of amber. More than anything else, botched uses of immortality magick bring this backlash about, so the ties to Life are obvious. The amber is heavy, and restricts the character's movements. Minor manifestations only cover individual limbs, while greater ones lock the whole body in place. In the event of a dramatic, lethal backlash, the mage is fully encased, even to her face. Suffocating to death is almost certain, unless the amber can be cleared away or escaped.

                                If it cannot, the mage perishes, preserved for all eternity in a life-sized prison of rock, like some ancient insect. At least one such specimen can be found in a Technocracy warehouse, an idle academic curiosity for Enlightened students.

                                Magestuck: The Ascension

                                    417) Frosty Skin And A Chilling Grasp [ Paradox Flaw | Matter, Forces, Matter and Forces ] Any inanimate material surface that the affected Mage touches with their bare skin will feel cold and chilly to other persons - somewhere between the chill of ice and snow during an average January and the cold of common metals like silver, iron or copper. This lasts for a minute or two for each surface that the affected Mage touches ; and after this amount of time this sensation of cold and chill fades from the affected surface.
                                    The affected Mage does not at all feel the chill and cold induced by this Paradox Flaw.

                                        418) Let ME Do It! [Flaw? Quiet?]: The ways and even nature of the Avatar are mysterious. Apparently, though, their general goal (if an Avatar can be said to HAVE any goals) is to push their mage towards Ascension. Depending on the mage and her Focus, this could involve dropping hints and riddles, giving council, berating the mage, or even being antagonistic. According to the Avatar's Essence and the mage's beliefs, the Avatar interferes in the mage's life, in the hopes of making the character grow as a person. In all cases, though, it's assumed that even with the most domineering of Avatars, the MAGE is the one in charge (if, indeed, there is any separation between the two).

                                        This backlash, however, shakes up the dynamic. When a mage's Avatar rating is particularly high, Paradox may cause this being (or alternate side of the mage) to step in and assume command. This can occur most especially when a mage has performed some greatly Vulgar magick the Avatar disagrees with, or botched a working that sets the mage back greatly. Whatever the case, the Avatar gets fed up with the mage's behavior, pushing the mage aside and taking control personally. The mage may black out, or else watch in horror, as their body and magickal power is hijacked by the Avatar, who proceeds to spend the duration of the backlash doing it "their way".

                                        On the positive side, the Avatar will often be far more adept at using magick than the mage herself, employing Spheres higher than the mage's usual maximum. Often in combinations the mage would never have thought of, or by applying their tools in novel ways. And at the end of the day, most Avatars want what's best for the mage, and will take pains to not allow immediate harm to come to them.

                                        On the negative side, the mage's power is not in her control. And while the Avatar won't let immediate harm come to the Mage, this courtesy is not extended to anything or anyone the mage may happen to care about. The Avatar assumes control because it sees problems before the mage; barriers to the character's spiritual development. An Ecstasy Cultist may be addicted to drugs; her Avatar may see the mage's dealer as a direct impediment, to be disposed of. Or a Primordial Avatar may be frustrated by the mage's attempts to take indirect paths to solve her problems, while the Avatar would simply take a direct (often blunt and destructive) route, because all this "subtly" business is a waste of the mage's time. Meanwhile, the Avatar of a Chorister, an angel from on high, might have no patience for the sorts of compromises the Chorister has to make with non-religious or morally gray allies, and deals with them like a figure of (supposed) moral authority would deal with a pack of sinners and pagans.

                                        In any event, this is a result of Paradox because the Avatar's interference, while motivated by the mage's long-term development, is rarely good for the mage in the short term. Or, contrarily, an immediate difficulty might be surmounted, but in a manner that causes other lasting problems. A mage who was seen immolating a person alive, in front of a dozen people, will face some sort of reckoning, sooner or later. Nor, for that matter, is the Avatar necessarily correct in its course of action. The Avatar is an embodiment of the mage's soul and a personification of her Paradigm, or else to her history of incarnations. While the Avatar's actions WILL, in the end, lead to teaching moments for the mage, the Avatar is what it is and obeys its own internal logic.

                                        Magestuck: The Ascension

                                                    421) Rolling Stagnation [Flaw]: Often afflicts Bata'a and other mages who make their home in wetlands or swamps. All water in the mage's immediate area becomes stagnant and filthy, like it just got pumped from a bog. This usually reverts when the backlash's duration ends, but it can leave matters awkward and stomach-churning in the meantime. Let's not even get into the biting insects that also tend to appear.


                                                    422) Swamp Zombies [Spirit]: Speaking of bog-mages, a more severe backlash may summon Paradox Spirits in the form of bloated, rotting, slime-covered undead. If the mage strays close to a body of water, hands will spring out and try to pull the character in. If the mage keeps her distance, the swamp zombies may grow impatient and come after her. Rising from the water - which usually also suffers from the previous Paradox manifestation - these waterlogged cadavers display the signs of intense and protracted putrescence. A swamp zombie's strong odor alone is offensive, requiring a character to make a Stamina roll to avoid retching. They are slow and stupid, but their goals are the same as before: seize their target and drag them into the muck. If at any point the character goes into the water, they must succeed in various rolls to avoid drowning or being dragged to the bottom.
