        Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
        M20, p.613:
        Throughout our world in this 21st century, the Technocratic vision of reality holds the coincidental edge, not simply because of the Union's indoctrination (although that does have something to do with it) but because science-based technology is rooted in following the laws of Earthly physics. Unlike faith and magic, it's based upon repeatable, verifiable, consistent results, not mere Enlightened Will, so it tends to work more reliably than mystic magick does.
        And the second bullet point for things that are violations of Earthly Foundations is “Obvious violations of normal physical laws”.
        I can't read this section as the Technocracy rooting its work in the Earthly Foundations, for several reasons. Firstly because the suggested list of normal physical laws is so thin. The bullet point you mention has three:

        1) Water not flowing uphill, which I presume is just gravity since it is actually trivial to make water flow upwards using real world physical laws.

        2) Sudden apparition.

        3) Turtles not running really fast, which I presume is a proscription against sudden acceleration since turtle speed would be an oddly specific EF.

        That's not a list that encompasses basic physics. It's barely enough to make a Christopher Nolan movie paradoxical. Clearly there can be more than are just listed here, but there don't have to be. They are also very general, they only specify that something generally happens or does not happen, they don't say how or why.

        That's important because when we think laws of physics we are usually thinking of things that are considerably more numerous and specific: thermal expansion, equal and opposite reactions, Bernoulli's principle, etc. Those aren't EFs, though. They are the hows to the EF's whats. They are either mythic (technic?) threads or technocratic (and pretechnocratic) principles.

        I certainly agree that the Technocracy is making it's principles consistent with those three ideas (and any others that may exist). So is everyone else. The witch having a demon move her broom, the sorcerer enchanting his carpet, and the engineer designing his wing and engine system are all doing the exact same thing: recognizing that things fall and carving out an exception why this time they don't.

        I certainly agree that the Technocracy is having a better time getting people to agree with their particular style of carving out exceptions, but their success doesn't seem to have anything to do with the suggested EFs. Indeed if I had to point out the elements that make them successful it would be the indoctrination and the reliable results.

                            Let me clarify, as it sounds like what I was saying might have been unclear.

                            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                            M20, p.613:
                            Throughout our world in this 21st century, the Technocratic vision of reality holds the coincidental edge, not simply because of the Union's indoctrination (although that does have something to do with it) but because science-based technology is rooted in following the laws of Earthly physics. Unlike faith and magic, it's based upon repeatable, verifiable, consistent results, not mere Enlightened Will, so it tends to work more reliably than mystic magick does.
                            And the second bullet point for things that are violations of Earthly Foundations is “Obvious violations of normal physical laws”.
                            It looks to me like you are pointing to the underlined section as your strongest reason for why the Technocracy is rooted in Earthly Foundations. Specifically you are interpreting the reference to earthly physics as a reference to EFs and specifically to the kind listed in the "obvious violations of normal physical laws" section. Now the way I see it, there are a few ways of reading the part you underlined:
                            1) There is the way you are interpreting it: "Earthly physics" is a reference to Earthly Foundations.

                            2) The underlined part isn't referring to EFs, it is referring to the more detailed physical laws that people are familiar with. These are quite solidly entrenched in reality, but not at anything like an EF level.

                            3) "Earthly physics" are a technocracy party line that they've managed to convince most of the world of. The Technocracy isn't playing better with reality, it is playing better with the reality it has convinced people of. Not my favorite view, but definitely one I've seen.

                            My previous post was largely an explanation of why I can't agree with interpretation 1. The EFs are too general to lend themselves to a specific type of paradigm. Certainly the Technocracy is accounting for them but so is everyone else. We can, of course, just say that the Technocracy is better at it, but there's no indication of how so that seems arbitrary. We could also shore this one up by assuming more specific and numerous physical EFs, but I'm not going to do that without compelling reason.

                            I don't like number 3 either. While I do think the Technocracy would still claim to be using earthly physics even if they were knowingly making it up whole cloth, it doesn't match the kind of research and development methodology they pursue. It also isn't an edge, and I presume that the three things listed (indoctrination, rooted in physics, and results) are all supposed to be edges.

                            It's the second interpretation that makes the most sense to me. It fits the Technocracy's approach and gives them a firm foundation. Other factions will have similarly entrenched foundations, true, but that's where the Technocracy's other two edges come into play. It also happens to play best with the section on Deep Wilderness and failing technology. This is the one that fits what I know of Mage and the Technocracy best and requires the fewest assumptions to make it work.

                                    There's definitely a bit of hazy terminology in the Reality Zones. Sometimes Primal Reality is just the non-technocrat equivalent of Technocratic Reality, and I think we should consider that a part of some other grouping. Other times it earth in a closer to 'natural' state, or beholden to some ancient power, or an otherworldly place.                                     Mars as an Otherworld or series of Otherworlds would work, but leaves it entirely up to the ST how to define that. There's an opportunity for a kind of mystic wild west here, though.

                                        It's been established that Mars has a “Spirit Wilds” Umbra, and that it's populated with the sorts of aliens seen in early science fiction (Barsoom, Malachandra, Wells' War of the Worlds — although there's also a suggestion that there might be a Hollow Mars, and I'd be inclined to put the latter there). That gives us a place for everything.

                                        My thought on this also has to do with the nature and development of science fiction stories pertaining to Mars: as late as the 1930s, Mars tended to be depicted as a more or less habitable and inhabited world; this tends to coincide with the idea that the spatial horizon had not yet been extended out that far, and there was no distinct physical Mars to visit; so all such visits were to the Martian spirit wilds. As the spatial horizon extended out to mars, started to get story of a more desolate planet, still inhabited by Martians living underground; this is where you get things like Ray Bradbury's Martian Chronicles. Then, after the pioneer mission, all pretenses of Mars being inhabited than it, and subsequent stories almost exclusively deal with uninhabited physical surface.
