    Ditching Traditions/Crafts for just Practices?

    07-17-2017, 03:50 PM
    What would you guys think about getting rid of the specific Traditions/Crafts and just having the Traditions (faction) being a hodgepodge of mystic & technomagic practices? There would still be a Council of Nine, with each holding one of the sphere associated seats, but they wouldn't be automatically the big powerhouse groups. I like the idea of the Traditions (faction) be more inclusive. It also seems to me that the Traditions/Crafts fill a similar role as Practice. And since Practices are more diverse, the obvious solution is to get rid of the Traditions/Crafts and let Practices be the divisions of what kind of magic you do. Obviously this might not be for everyone, but it feels right for my vision of Mage.

    NEVER MIND

    For anyone who doesn't see my later post, I've been convinced that my viewpoint was flawed and I've since decided to keep the Traditions around.

      The Order of Hermes already brings together a lot of practices, so you could try their model. There are many houses and a few core beliefs, but Criamon from Ex Miscellena, Flambeau, Xaos (formerly Thig) and House Shaea are quite different but are all Order of Hermes. Other Traditions, Crafts, Technocracy, and Nephandi are groupings of like-minded people with similar practices and histories.

      If you have a Council of Nine, with a seat tied to a group, then if the Council of Nine is relevant, who sits on it is relevant. If the Council of Nine is mere window dressing, it doesn't really matter what the Traditions are. However, eliminating the history and politics of the Traditions would create a different tone to the game. Moving from Mage 1e to Mage Revised was watching the change in Tradition and Technocracy politics as events happened. Doisstep is very different without all the Tradition infighting and skullduggery.

      However, not all games are tied to large-scale politics of the mage factions, so in some games a change in how the Traditions work isn't really that impactful.

      It's your game, so what's correct is what works for your table.

                  Originally posted by CrackedMan View Post
                  ​The culture isn't dependent on the Traditions.
                  No, but Traditions/etc. are the source of mage-cultures in the game as is. If you remove them and don't replace them, nothing fills that role any more.

                  You can still have groups who practice witchcraft without the Verbena. You can still have social units who teach each other without the term Tradition. Why would they NOT group together and associate with people who do similar types of magic? That'd be like saying that people wouldn't gather to play football together if the NFL didn't exist. They would, just not on a large formal scale.
                  Yes, you don't need the Verbena to have witches, because not all witches are Verbena and not all Verbena are witches. The problem is that mages of a shared Sleeper cultural background are going to congregate over that more than Practices. A Druidic mage practicing Witchcraft, a Druidic mage practicing Faith, and a Druidic mage praticing High Ritual, are going to gather together for their shared Druidism. The Druidic Faith mage isn't going to go hang out with an Evangelical Faith mage because of Faith.

                  Or in different terms, Paradigm and Instruments matter too. Once you have a social unit that creates mage-cultures via a common set of closely related Paradigms, a few compatible Practices, and shared style of Instruments... you have the Traditions and Crafts even if you don't call them that. Then removing the names of the groups is just a cosmetic change, not a meaningful one.

                  And that's the thing. "Practice" doesn't imply anything close to a similar type of magic, because Focus as a whole describes a type of magic and even then ten characters that take exactly the same Focus can still have meaningful differences in type of magic. The NFL thing is off. What you're doing, is saying that if you take away all the names of individual sports and call them all Sport, all athletes will hang out because of their shared participation in Sport; after all swimmers, wrestlers, and baseball players are the same Sport player types.

                      Yup.

                      A Christian Gnostic that does do High Ritual and a High Ritual Brahamin are going to have more in common with Faith, or even Maleficia, people of their cultures than with each other.

                      A Faith mage that believes in Returning to the Golden Age isn't going to have the same interests as the one who has Faith in that the World it's an Illusion. It has sense for people of very different Practices to join together into "Bringing Back the Golden Age" as long as they kinda agree in what that Age it's, and if you put syncretism into the plate it has every sense for witches, mystics, wizards and even scientists of very different cultural Background to put their differences appart.

                      In the end, Traditions aren't just amalgamation of Practices, they also share a history, objectives, and a few Paradigms (I.E: Euthanatos have several distinct Practices and Paradigms, but all care about justice, retribution, death)... If you loose this common threads, it happens something similar as with Mage: The Awakening, where mages mystical paths are very personal.

                              In my game, I made the decision to replace the Traditions, as I feel they didn't age particularly well. I got more work than I bargained for, really. I didn't fully consider how many decisions I would have to make, and how much writing I would need to do for my players' benefit. I still like where it's going, and it's really pushing me as a ST, but it's work.

                              So, while I sympathize with the desire to remove the Traditions, I can't imagine running a game without something to replace them. Practices are a great addition to the game, but they don't answer key questions:
                              1. What does the character believe is the appropriate use of magick?
                              2. What change does the character want to see in the world? Mages by and large are not content with the status quo, and in Mage ambition influences magick.
                              3. What is the underlying belief structure that fuels their study of magick? Being a Mage is dangerous and difficult; most aren't in it just for personal power. Compare the reasoning of a Verbena to a Euthanatos.
                              4. What is the method of training apprentices, and do experienced Mages have an obligation to teach?
                              5. What, other than raw power, decides who is in charge? How are disputes settled?
                              6. What is an Avatar, and what does Ascension look like?
                              This is just a sample, but it's the kind of questions I'm having to answer right now. I think if you simply remove the Traditions, you will find your PCs asking these kind of questions of themselves and of the NPC Mages in your game. Even experienced players can find it handy to have a foundation for their character; Traditions provide foundation.

                              Human beings generally group themselves around "why" and "who" rather than "how", so you might find yourself ad hoc rebuilding Traditions anyway. Even if you organize the groups around 100% real-world cultures, you're still building groups with beliefs and goals, and natural player curiosity will result in you needing answers. Both a neo-pagan and a voudon may use the same Practice, but they have very different beliefs about when and how to use that power, and those things make characters, and characters make stories.

                              The only way I could see this working out is if the number of Mages in the world is incredibly small. If the world consists of a few characters like Gandalf, Pug, Senex, and Circe, then you scrape by on defining them by how they do magick, and let their individual personalities cover the rest. Essentially, only in a world where there are too few Mages to have a culture can a narrative thrive without Mage culture.
