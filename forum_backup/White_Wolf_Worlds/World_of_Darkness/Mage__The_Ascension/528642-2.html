        This doesn't seem right thread, since it's really not on topic, but here goes:

        Scion and NWoD definitely had meta-plot, though the hand was much lighter than with CWOD. Scion with the overall framing device of a new Titanomachy. That was a set of boxes into which everything in the game was shoehorned and it was a set of boxes for which several of the mythologies depicted were ill-suited to shoe-horning. The rules definitely took this metaplot into account all over the place. (Why aren't the gods running around the mortal world? Fate-binding and Legendary Deeds.... <rules, rules, rules> So which Titan (implicitly tied to which General Purview) is matched up with which pantheon and who gets picked to be Avatars... etc. etc.

        The NWOD it is more background (pre-God Machine), but there is a coherent world out there and while not all the games plug together perfectly, there are continuous narratives discernable across several games.... Mummy and Geist and Mage and Prometheean have different views of the same Underworld which lock together into a coherent storyline.... some devastating world cracking prehistoric event shaping the way spirit and material interact from Werewolf and Mage and Hunter..... how the Watchtowers of Mage relate, and how they don',t with Geist, Changeling, etc. There may not have been the eschatology of CWOD, but there was definably something that linked them all up. And that was reflective of and a reflection of the fact the rules were designed to work together for the most part. That's metaplot and it's rules affecting. Those are rules and they affect metaplot.

        I have stayed out of Exalted since it started (aside from playing it a couple of times) My PhD dissertation topic was Bronze Age related so the setting was grating to me at the beginning (like being a lawyer and watching TV shows or movies based around legal matters.... I just start shouting "Objection!" at the TV screen....). Then it swtiched over completely to a more anime-simulation vein and that wasn't so much to my taste. So I can't bring anything to illustrate from there, if it even exists.

        CWOD used metaplot narrative repeatedly across the games that had more than one edition to describe how the world changing was reflected in the rules changing or by bringing forward some new aspect of metaplot to represent the aspect of the rules changes. Or having some explanation of why this and that don't jibe across different editions of the game. (Celerity in DA vs. Celerity everywhen else). That's a feedback loop, of course, as the metaplot would also drive rules changes as much as it reflected them.

        There IS a metaplot in M20. It's going to affect what is subsequently put forwards. We've even heard it straight from the horses mouths (as it were), when developers start talking about things like "the Grand Alliance" of the few good Technocrats, the part of the Traditions that is able to pull their collective heads out of its collective asses and the "New [Fragile] Hope" of the Disparate Alliance vs. the Nephandi as "the new vision" for the game.

        Now, has M20 done BETTER than any CWOD game in providing a tool box with which to customize the level of old and new metaplot are in play? Sure. Absolutely. Thanks to all the gods that may be for the advent of the Purple Boxes. But the new metaplot will have an effect. WW and Onyx Path don't produce games that are almost complete crunch, like say, Fate or Pathfinder or D&D Xth Ed. Those system are metaplot neutral. You can take those rules and pretty much do anything with them. Which has its own good points and bad points. But the rich lushness of White Wolf has always been that the rules are meant to part of a game with the story and setting built in.

        To that end, perhaps it would be cool to explore what to use and not to use from M20 and previous materials to create Technocracy games with less of a Technephandi feel? Can you do it with M20 proper? If so, are there simple tweaks needed? How about integrating it more closely with the Technocracy-thru-Rosier-Colored-Mirrorshades of GttT? What would be the points to hit on? How about the role of the Rev. Convention books? What does it mean to separate out the "distinguishable downsides" of each Faction and throwing each one of them up as a warning saying "Do Not Enter" (albeit usually hidden behind some vegetation, sometimes knocked down and half-buried...)
