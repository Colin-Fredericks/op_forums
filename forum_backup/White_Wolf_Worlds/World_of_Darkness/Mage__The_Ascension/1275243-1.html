                    Originally posted by Aleph View Post

                    Besides, Life sphere it's very, VERY, clear about it affecting "living organic stuff". All Life levels describe effects affecting organic living beings. And while Life becomes Matter at the moment of death, no place in the book says that the cycle also goes the other way with inorganic stuff becoming Life (The methaphysics of magick define the interaction of patterns, and the arrows point the way. It's a one way street).
                    There's a certain logic to it, which I would be willing to listen to as an ST. A mage still needs Life (in addition to Matter) to do anything with necromancy, at least in the sense of fucking around with the undead as opposed to ghosts or other entropic spirits. It wouldn't be the first case of Sphere overlap -- case in point, Entropy, spirits of entropy and death, and the "underworld". Or, for that matter, Mind, the astral plane, and the spirits which inhabit it.

                    What a more orthodox interpretation of Life yields, is the sphere grants mastery over animate, self-sustaining and -replicating, Patterns compromised of organic compounds that are organized by cells. So we've established what is a "Life Pattern"; that's all well and good for most circumstances, but that still gets a little murky on the fringes. The books are pretty clear Life covers prokaryotes and viruses, but what about prions and ribozymes, or even enzymes in a vacuum? We can infer "yes", but the logic behind it gets a little iffy when you stop and really think about it.

                    So, what if we have a Progenitor stationed aboard a Voidship, who decides a Really Neat Experiment would be to design a lifeform that could survive on Titan? Methanogenic life that synthesizes acetylene instead of glucose, etc. We're still dealing with organic compounds, so no brainer, right?

                    Well, what if instead of Titan, that Progenitor starts their little experiment for Venus, or more appropriately as Venus is too cold, what astronomers currently believe Kepler-96c to be? Well, we're talking about silicon-based life that metabolizes silicates and sulfurates about which we can hypothesize the chemistry, however unlikely it is to be in the universe. They'd definitely need Matter and Prime to get the ball rolling (and DimSci for obvious reasons), but once our hypothetical Progenitor gets the compounds to a point of self-replication, at what point (if any) would Life then apply?

                    So, that's within the realm of "chemistry we've been able to figure out, or at least hypothesize about". Let's start talking about the REALLY weird shit. Ten or fifteen years ago there was an experiment with argon plasma to replicate early-Earth atmospheric conditions. What happened, was the plasma formed cell-like structures that were actually capable of growing, self-replicating, and exchanging information with other structures. So now we have the (remote) possibility of some kind of weird-ass prokaryotic-ish(?) plasma-based life that could proliferate in gas giants and brown dwarves perhaps, go figure. What the fuck kind of spheres you'd use for that, I could only speculate, but we're at the point of Forces taking primacy over Spirit, Mind, or even Matter, as a conjunctional Sphere to Life.

                    Really, "Life" in these terms has basically the same definition as "porn": you know it when you see it. Well, in this case in my opinion, the Consensus knows it when it sees it.

                                    Fire does not maintain homeostasis nor is it organized, two of the most important elements of life. Also for the purposes of Mage it mystically, scientifically, and colloquially belongs to the Forces sphere.

                                    It seems to me you are falling into the continuum fallacy here. Just because the definition of Life is more vague than we would like doesn't mean it is nonsensical. Life is an organized system that metabolizes, grows, and maintains homeostasis, or to put that in non scientific terms it is grows, adapts, perpetuates, and maintains itself. Yes, despite your earlier statement eating (and to a lesser extent crapping) are very relevant to life.

                                    You can get to machines that do approach Life as we understand it, but they start to look like the Replicators from Stargate or the more advanced Transformers. The self driving cars from earlier don't qualify because self driving cars don't heal, or consume and synthesize materials to maintain their integrity, or any of a number of complicated things that even the most basic of single celled organisms constantly do.

                                    If you had a self driving car that did, though, I'd say it would be reasonable to heal it with Life.




                                    The issue with HDYDT is that it included Spheres that did nothing for the spell just because it sounded good. So Pyrokinesis required Mind despite the fact that it is functionally indistinguishable from a basic Forces 3/Prime 2 spell. The point of the Spheres system is that you can use it for almost any kind of magic without changing the rules. Paradigm does not affect the spheres you use because paradigm is an in universe belief system and spheres are a gaming abstraction.
