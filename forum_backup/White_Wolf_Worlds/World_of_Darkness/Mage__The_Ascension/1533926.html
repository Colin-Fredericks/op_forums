    Elevator Pitch - Mage: The Quintessence (Mage IN SPACE)

    04-09-2024, 11:14 PM
    So I was watching that Dark Side of the Moon/Eclipse animation contest runner up that has been making the rounds and I was hit with an idea I have to imagine dozens of people have already had: Mage in Space! Hell, the Revised Storyteller Handbook even had a version of it.

    Still, for a game that often has Moebius style artwork in its books the examples of Mage in Space I've seen have usually been relatively constrained. Maybe do the Biscocchian Coprosperity Sphere, or add Mage to Aeon/Trinity Continuum, etc. My thought though is if someone is going to do Mage in Space, then they need to go ALL OUT on the idea. This can serve as a contrast to the mainline series, where the authors generally implicitly insist that the primary play style should be gutter punk street mages, even when they say they want the game to be open to wilder Chronicles.

    Now I'm still working on my other book project, and if I'm going to do this right this one would likely be 3 times or more bigger and need brand new, commissioned art for it, so this is probably way out of my range to do as a solo project. Still, I'd like to work out an elevator pitch for it.

    Here's what I got so far:
    • Title: Mage: The Quintessence
    • Synopsis: Near the mid 21st Century, Earth and the Solar System become doomed, ironically not by human hands, but because of the other Night Folk in the cWoD settings having their respective Time of Judgement scenario happen simultaneously. Fleeing the Antediluvians, the Wyrm, etc., Various Awakened groups take as people as they can and flee to the stars. 500 to 1000 years later (haven't fully decided what the time jump should be), the Council of Nine Techno-Mystic Colonies and Technocratic Union of Planets butt into each other in space and the Ascension war begins anew. Throw in various aliens, the usual Nephandi/Marauder shenanigans, and a 2nd Disparate Alliance into the mix.
    • Inspirations:
      • The Incal/Jodoverse: Duh
      • The Fifth Element: Also Duh
      • Other Moebius works: Also also Duh
      • L'Engle's Time Quintet: Alien worlds, space/time travel, microverses
      • Eclipse Phase: Technocratic Union Colonies
      • Cowboy Bebop: Lived in multicultural futurescapes
      • Star Trek: Exploring new worlds, space diplomacy
      • Aeon Flux: Pseudo religious scifi
      • Xenogears/saga/blade: More Pseudo religious scifi, The Gnosis as an Alien threat. Alien vistas.
      • Saga Frontier: Technological and Magical worlds co-existing in a broader universe.
      • Gravity Rush: More Moebius like visuals, void monsters, umbral realms
      • Trinity Continuum: Aeon - Storyteller derived Scifi
    Thoughts would be appreciated.

          If you want a re-emergence of the Ascension War, you need to have some kind of resource they are competing over. If each faction gets its own full earth/umbra combo there's not going to be much reason to fight for a long time, and maintaining the right cultures on each planet will be much more important than trying to manipulate the cultures on a different one. If, on the other hand, the planets are much thinner and weaker versions of earth (low on quintessence, shallow umbras, etc) then factions are going to need a lot of small and difficult to defend outposts.

          Also, the Technocracy is severely hindered in this scenario. A lot of their biggest advantages go away when the local reality doesn't back them up. They're going to have to lean heavily towards hypertech and will soon invoke Clarke's Law and become another form of Space Wizard. This is another reason to keep quintessence sources low as Primal Ventures will become a much needed leveler.

          I'll add one more inspiration: Alpha Centauri. Even though the game is centered around one planet, the different approaches and ideology driven conflict fit this idea.

                                                  You should think a bit about the Cosmology of the setting. Mage features an Earth-centric cosmology where even in the 21st century, you run into a barrier if you try to climb into a rocket ship and fly out of the Solar System. For an interstellar system, I could see recontextualizing this to being how every star system is: the Deep Umbra is interstellar space, and every developed star system exists inside a bubble of Conventional Space.

                                                  Less developed systems limit their bubbles of Conventional Space to planetary orbit, the way the Cosmology was described in the earlier editions of Mage; or even just to the edge of the atmosphere, as was the case back in the Sorcerers Crusade. Worlds that haven't been settled yet may exist within the Umbra, without any Conventional Space. The descendants of the Void Engineers are looking to change that, as there are considerable benefits to operating within Conventional Space, such as not having to protect yourself against Void Adaptation; but it's a big task.

                                                  The Void Engineers Convention Book argues that if you don't want to Void Adapt, you need to establish some sort of connection to an Earth Node. Fair enough, as far as it goes; but then, the Syndicate Convention Book introduces Primal Ventures, where organized human activity can function like a Node. I could see the crew of a starship functioning that way, with even a small ship's crew being adequate to provide enough of a Node to maintain an Earthlike environment on board the ship; though I'd recommend that such crew-supported Voidships maintain regular contact with each other and with human colonies, or else they risk a sort of gradual reality drift.

                                                  You might consider introducing something like Stargate SG-1 as a way to let humanity spread to the stars without making them dependent on starships to do it. I think that most of the Traditions would appreciate this option, as the Stargates wouldn't necessarily have to be technological. I could see any world connected to the Stargate network essentially sharing the same Conventional Space, even if divided among distant “bubbles”.



                                                  But then there are the actual aliens, who don't come from Earth at all, and who come from alien environments. I'd use the STV Changeling product Countless Dreams as the basis for this: you have Kites (Abyssal or Celestial aliens that live either in the sky or the depths of the ocean; good for populating gas giants and waterworlds), Silicates (Aidoneus or Digital entities that are either machines or residents of the Digital Web), Forgotten (Ancestors and Explorers who represent animalistic aliens or inhabitants of interplanetary space), and Oppies (inorganic aliens).

                                                  My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                      Population has a relationship both to Paradox and to the difficulty of casting.

                                                      According to one of the Story teller guides difficulty of preforming magic increases when a planet's Population hits certain milestones. Populations of five million, fifty million, and three hundred million all corespond to increasing casting difficulties. Thus a recently settled frontier world might attract Mages wanting freedom from Paradox (which doesn't seem to exist below populations of four hundred million) and reduced casting difficulties.

                                                      Paradox wasn't evenly spread over the planet Earth even in the early 21st century. At least according to cannon sources. So a world with five settled areas, each with a population of around one million and those areas are, a Void Engineer scout base, an Hermetic University, and Etherite Utopian settlement, a Wu Lung cultural recreation settlement, and a Ngoma agricultural settlement, might have different rules of reality in each area. Especially if the areas are far apart and densely settled.
