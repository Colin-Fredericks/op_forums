    I was thinking that a lot really depends on when the Nephandi corrupted the Technocracy (or the Traditions). If the Nephandi came in on the ground floor, so to speak, (as entry level Order of Reason or Technocrat folk) then I think there is a problem in terms of the Nephandi suddenly being to blame for the Technocrats' bad decisions, because we can't ever really be sure which decisions came from the Nephandi and which from straightforward Technocrats.

    But if the corruption is a fairly recent phenomenon, I don't think the same problem exists. The Technocrats are still 100% to blame for their worst acts in the name of progress or protecting humanity that took place before the corruption. (For example, the worst of colonialism, the more terrible parts of the industrial revolution, the purges, Room 101 and so on.) Really, if you think about it, they're still to blame for anything the Nephandi/Technocrats are doing now, because these higher-ranked members made the choice (knowingly or not) to fall to Nephandic corruption.

And metaplot, while eminently toss-able, does have an effect not only on how the rules are written for this book, but will have an effect on how future books are created.

                                    Sure, I CAN say that my Technocracy is not riddled with Nephandi like swiss cheese (if swiss cheese had a black hole at the center of the wheel), but if a Technephandi is the baseline supposition, any Technocracy book is going to be written with that in mind. NPCs will be constructed with a mind to their level of complicity in the corruption. Rules will be in place to develop the Nephandi influence further. There will be a few purple boxes here and there to help me out to de-rot the Technocracy, but the balance of anything Technocratic from this point forward looks like it is going to assume a rotten core and rotting peripheries with the PCs interacting with those assumptions in some way.

                                                    Core conceit: It's us (for various values of "us" from single cabals all the way up) against the Nephandi.
                                                    Traditions: These guys are almost utterly clueless about the important fight. They are all about instrospection and image modification. Basically idiots if not tools. Still stuck in us vs. Technocracy and no one else is important. Nephandi? Convention books.

                                                            Please read through my previous posts carefully as I repeatedly stated that I was more than willing to adopt all, some or none of the parts of the new overarching metaplot, depending on the needs of whichever chronicle we happen to be running next.

                                                            Underestimating the role that metaplot has on shaping future books is not something borne out by any evidence of past White Wolf or Onyx Path game production. White Wolf games have been metaplot heavy from the get go. The rules and the metaplot have always been tightly interwoven. When the rules changed the metaplot turned and vice-versa. I doubt there is very much chance that we will be seeing metaplot in only an occasional book here and there. That's just not how things have ever been done with these games. In fact, that's one of the things many players LOVE about White Wolf games. The STORY to it all. It can make it worthwhile to buy ALL the books even if you can't play the game. Just to keep up with the soap opera.

                                                            So, I COULD have the Disparates fall apart, which, all things being equal, they probably should.... if Yugoslavia couldn't work, this kind of plurality of multiply dysfunctional ethnocentrics is a disaster waiting to happen. Particularly since they are all running around trying to keep it secret. If I DO, I'm betting pretty much 100% what happens in my game will be close to 180 degrees off of what would be happening in later supplements

                                                            Going back to the OP (again), the centrality of the Technephandi (and Nephandi overall) just isn't to a some of people's taste. To them and to me (mostly, depending on the game) the Nephandi were one cautionary tale and the Technocracy represented another. Now they represent the same thing.

                                                            And, yes, of course, they/we/I can ignore it. That's a given. Golden Rule (of White Wolf games) and all. But the M20 setting was presented as being, if not meta-plot neutral, more meta-plot optional or even meta-plot toolbox/sandbox fashion than it turned out to be in some ways. Not that I am dissing the purple boxes. Awesome, overall. I would probably just want a little more shoved up into them.
