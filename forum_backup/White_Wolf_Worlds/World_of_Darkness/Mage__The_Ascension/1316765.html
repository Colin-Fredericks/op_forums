    Devices and Machines Instrument

    06-06-2019, 01:09 AM
    I'd like to know some things regarding "Devices and Machines" in M20:

    1) Can a mystic character (hermetic) use this instrument? As I believe they can, would they necessarily use them like exemplified in House Thig descriptions, not bothering in how a device works but only using it more as a symbol, or could they use it like an etherite, knowing the science behind the tools? Or both?

    2)Devices and machines are very vague and pratically any effect could be cast with this instrument. As M20 says, there are lots of ways of casting an effect. Considering that you could throw a fireball by using a wand or a fire device, or fly using meditation or a turbine machine, how would drop this device work? I mean, I can see any kind of effect being done with an appropriate device/machine. Wouldn't dropping it make all other instruments kinda obsolete?

    As I posted in a thread some time ago, I am interested in a character that is a mystic hermetic, having his magickal aspect focused in his body and will, but in the same time heavily dedicated to science and technology in his concept, often finding reasonable explanations for his spells. So, I'm flirting with the possibility of adding this instrument into the list.

              "I can see any kind of effect being done with X. Wouldn't dropping it make all other instruments obsolete?" It's the risk you have taken when you've chosen to leave "what instrument allows what effect" to pure interpretation and personal judgement.

              In older editions dropping was related to Spheres, and thus all droppings were balanced and no dropping would allow you to do that (you only drop the instruments for one Sphere)

              This balance issue has been debated among the players and authors when M20 came out. For some the advantages to roleplay outweigthed the risk to game balance. After all Mage isn't a well balanced game anyway, and tying Instruments to Spheres while interpreting that you don't know what Spheres are was kinda odd.

              M20 warns against this kind of attitude: Saying mages don't chose convinient Instruments and counseling the ST to forbbide to drop first the Instruments the player intends to use the most

              In other words: Yes, as much as I personally cringe, you totally can.

              If your Table it's on board and the ST hasn't any problem with you discarding all your instruments at once, that is

                      Let me remake the question. When playing a char that use an Instrument as vague as "devices and machines", there is any effect that can't be casted with it?
                      That depends on your Paradigm/Practice, and your imagination. It has little to do with the Instrument itself.

                      Your Machines can be as versatile or as limited as you want, it only depends on the justification you use to explain why/how they work within your mystical paradigm.

                      A typical Technocrat would have to find a really good excuse to be able to shot curses trough Machines...but YOUR mage it's a Mystic. Who in the nine hells knows what "machines" imply with him?

                      He may well believe that the principles that allow cursed items to cause misfortune can be harnesed as a form of negative energy. Perhaps witches curse trough a form of wave that can be replicated trough a special radio or voice modulator.

                      Or maybe not, maybe curses are a resonance emited by the evil intent of humans focused trough words and actions, and thus an ordinary (unfeeling) machine would be woefully inferior to your own voice-box fueled by your own bad blood.

                      It all depends on how you justify it.
                      All Instruments are about that. Think that before Mages weren't forced to have 7 Instruments, that's an arbitrary number. "One Foucs" mages were odd, but entirely doable. You decide how machines work within that Practice/Paradigm of yours, *nothing* but your Paradigm and imagination determines how much or how little can your machines accomplish.

                      For instance, I was more worried about Meditation than Machines. Because for some mystics "Reality it's an Illusion of the Mind", what *can't* be accomplished if you master your mind?. Meditation allows to fly, create matter, go backwards in time. A traditional "old-school" mage wouldn't shoot lasers trough meditation...but what about a Star-Trek oriented techno-buddhist that experiments with generating light with his mind?
                      If you feel you need guidelines about how limit yourself: Think about the place Machines and the other 6 Instruments have on your Practice/s and Paradigm. Then determine what things machines should make *easier* for your mage. If it's harder for machines to do something than it would be for yourself, then using them doesn't make sense (Machines are only there to help, otherwise you wouldn't be a Mystic).

                      Many mages make "machines of Quintessence manipulation", but perhaps your mystic thinks that Magick it's easier to work by training body and mind. There Machines don't make any sense, not because they *can't* be built to do that (as actual Devices), but because they won't help you as Instruments

                      But if you don't limit yourself, nothing will.

                          Any Instrument could be used to cast nearly any effect, the important part is how the mage is doing it. And dropping that instrument doesn't mean the Mage no longer thinks the tool works, it's just that they realized they can work around it. A Hermetic knows that a wand is simply an instrument to direct their enlightened Will long before they ever drop the instrument mechanically.

                          As for blending technology and mysticism, that's basically House Thig and Verditius' whole idea, but you'd probably find a lot of inspiration in the Order of Reason books for Sorcerer's Crusade. Honestly though, the Hermetics in general have that as a main theme, depending on how you define science. If you mean using the scientific method, the Order of Hermes has dedicated about 1300 years to breaking down the mechanics of reality (which in Mage includes spiritual realms and magic). They might understand, as a whole, the "physics" of the WoD better than any other group, as they're willing to take basically any workable, or at least testable idea, and try to adopt it. Think of the houses as research divisions.

                          Symbolism is absolutely important, but they won't hold themselves back by refusing to learn about the 'mundane' science behind it, because science and magic are one and the same in this universe. Sure, their warded computer might have its circuitry made of alchemically purified gold on silicon wafers arranged according to sacred geometric principles, but they'll still need plenty of the Computers and Technology Abilities to build or even 'conjure' one. Their alchemists are just as educated as any Technocratic chemist, and might even more exacting about their concoctions, even if their insights are based on less 'concrete' phenomena.

                          ** Should have refreshed before posting, Aleph's covered the top bit far better than I did, but I'll still try to add to it. You can also use different instruments to achieve the same effect, and in the past that's how I've explained moving beyond an Instrument in paradigm. Brief meditation in place of a Resonance-inhibiting machine might seem simpler, but it'll never be as natural or easy as the familiar old method.

                          And even if your character completely believes something is possible, they might not do it. You could levitate a stone with Forces simply by envisioning it rising into the air and Willing it so, but maybe you just feel more comfortable with a wand. Why jog a couple miles when a bicycle lets you do the same faster and with less effort? And if a basic lever would do the trick, why bring 'magic' into it at all, mundane or not it's still an act of Will.

                              Book of Secrets says that whichever Instrument you use the most will be the last one that you surpass. So if you incorporate machines into all of your Effects, you're not going to be surpassing your use of machines any time soon.

                              And as Athanasius Digges said*, surpassing an instrument doesn't mean that you can't use it anymore. In fact, the whole reason why you get a -1 difficulty for using a surpassed instrument is to encourage players to keep using them even after they no longer need to.

                              (Personally, I sidestep the whole issue with a house rule that you don't surpass instruments one instrument at a time; instead, you surpass instruments altogether, one dot at a time: at Arete ••, you can surpass instruments for one-dot Effects; at Arete ••••, you can surpass instruments for two-dot Effects; and so on.)

                                                  Originally posted by Lord Revan View Post

                                                  That is something that I was thinking. If the mage find it easier to cast spells with those instruments (dropped) wouldn't he begin to use them more often if not, as much as possible?
                                                  I don't think this was elaborated enough, but the rules clearly assume that's not the case.

                                                  Your primary (and secondary, and tertiary) instruments should be part of the swagger you want your mage to have all the time. Then it won't be cumbersome to apply them most of the time. Also the player should answer when it's appropiate to use each different instrument, and asked to imagine effects that would be likely with an instrument and others that would be less likely - as I say this depends on how the player rationalizes his character's magick.

                                                  My take it's that the fact that you drop Instruments and not the rest of the Focus needs to be stressed out.

                                                  There's no mechanism to leave the Pardigm or the Practice aside, hence your "tool-less" magick still needs to be justified within a Paradigm (at least 'til you drop them all).

                                                  For instance: You don't need to "impose your hands" for God to grant you healing anymore. Faith it's in the heart, and healing comes from God. The ritual it's a formality, and you've always know this but you needed the ritual to reinforce your faith. Also, you still like to do the ritual because it's the respectful thing to do (hence the bonus)...You've dropped a tool, but still believe in God and the Christian practice.

                                                  If that general worldview it's stil in place then your Primary Instrument (the most fundamental, the one you drop last) should still be Primary, and thus the one you use the most.

                                                          But Mage is a dynamic game, mages can change their paradigm, even that this is very rare. But I wonder how rare is the variation of amount of use of the particular instruments.

                                                          Now, Players I can see feeling tempted to use these a lot more after surpassing them...and that's exactly why I think this deserved a lot more love & care.

                                                          Saying "oh, and you leave last the instrument you use the most" it's useless. If players *don't* want to, it requires the Master to act as watchdog in order to not be reduced to irrelevance (always a bad idea)...

                                                          ...and narratively it's not always appropiate. Many mystic beliefs would be better represented by surpassing first the instruments you trained the most rather than the ones you only dabbled.

                                                          For instance, a Zen Mage uses repetition of katas to reach satori. Eventually he will expect to be in a permanent state of satori and don't need the katas anymore...but that's the purpose of training and repetition. The less he uses the katas the less likely he will reach that state of superation. I imagine most Akashick mages would think that way and would find very odd - insulting even - that the Instruments they internalize first are the ones they train the least.

                                                          Hence it's not a rule I like.
