    Various Mage Houserules

    03-26-2019, 05:30 PM
    - Affinity Sphere is tied to Arete rating. So if you buy Arete 3, you have to set up your Sphere dot spread so that you have 3 dots in your Affinity Sphere. When you do a successful seeking you pay the normal cost for Arete increase, and your Affinity Sphere rises to meet your Arete rating.

    I like this because from a story-telling/cinematic perspective, there is something so awesome about a person gaining new powers when they have that epiphany. Your Arete increases to 2, your Life goes up to 2, you find you can heal yourself, that sort of thing. It also means players feel a really strong connection to the Affinity Sphere since it becomes part of those special moments.

    - EXP gets split into Mundane/Arcane EXP. Mundane buys Abilities, Attributes, Willpower, Mental/Physical/Social Merits, backgrounds that Sleepers can have. Arcane buys Arete, Spheres, Supernatural Merits, and backgrounds like Totem and Sanctum.

    The beauty of this is balanced growth is ensured. If you give 2 M and 1 A each session, for example, people develop the Abilities of their mage's practice over time, investing in the WAY their mage does magick, not just in dots in Spheres. People feel empowered to invest large amounts of EXP into things like Etiquette, Expression, High Ritual, Finance and Politics, because they know that they're not "falling behind" in terms of raw magickal power. They know that everyone's got the exact same amount of EXP to put into spheres/arete, so they don't have to sweat the details or worry about the risks of not min-maxing in a competitive game with opposing PCs, for example. I'm seeing the PCs just... flourish, basically, under this system, with loving care going into little details like how they train their abilities and what they plan to do in the future, and even people spending points on frivolous things that don't feed into their magick directly. Plus Attributes and Willpower are so expensive in terms of EXP that I can't imagine PCs investing much in them otherwise, when they can get so much more bang for their buck with magickal stuff.

    Essentially the reason I love this change is that it makes sure that magick is more than what spheres you have, and what arete you've reached. It helps direct the attention of the PCs towards how their abilities feed into their magick, and ensures that the abilities that form their practice get some love. And on top of all of that it means you can give loads of EXP without being scared of the repercussions, and without having to carefully curate what your PCs purchase. Mundane EXP is safe (short of ludicrous amounts), Arcane EXP is potentially dangerous if you give too much, so you can keep that in mind when balancing what you give.

    - EDIT: Neglected to mention a big houserule. I give the same EXP to all players at the end of a session, and I include players who aren't present. It just ensures that all PCs advance at roughly the same rate, and doesn't punish people for being busy IRL. And if you're having a bad day and struggle to RP well it doesn't mean you'll miss out on EXP. It's just simpler, never been a fan of WoD's 'earned' experience system, although the guidelines might be useful for deciding how much EXP to give the group as a whole. So if it's been a big important session I'll just say "everyone add 4 mundane and 1 arcane to their sheet".

    - Totem is broadened up somewhat; Shamans are still the masters of it, but you can have a Saint as your "totem" if you like, or one of the gods in your pantheon, or one of your ancestors. It doesn't have to be Tiger or Spider or similar. It just suggests a bond with a spirit, and the nature of that bond varies, as long as you aren't in charge of them (if you want to be in charge, get a spirit familiar).

    - I like the Data Sphere, but the connections table is a total mess. Several hours for each roll? That is just the opposite of fun. I simply rewrote the normal Correspondence connections table to have Data-style connections instead of the standard connections. Essentially at my table Data has a few downsides and requirements (computers need to be a major part of your practice, work is a bit slower and more deliberate, it's much harder to justify working without tools, you can't form a connection with anything that has no connection to the world of digitized information (from antediluvians to hermit mages in the andes)), and a few cool upsides (typically coincidental because technology is winning out in most reality zones, supported by a huge global connected network, links to the digital web, allows interaction with computers more easily than correspondence, lets you work with connections that wouldn't work for correspondence, you can exploit information easily found online).
    *
    Successes needed Range; distance between operator and targeted place/system/object. Connection; what the operator has that connects them to the targeted place/system/object. More successes are needed if the connection is weak.
    1 Line of Sight, Shared Network Body Sample, Source Code, Information on subject from database
    2 Target is Very Familiar Close possession or companion, sample code, personal email or social media account
    3 Target is Familiar Possession or casual friend, legacy code, sockpuppet, throwaway email or social media account
    4 Target was Visited Once Casual acquaintance or object used once, derivative code, IP address
    5 Described Location or System Briefly met or touched object or person, code from same language, rerouted IP string
    6+ Anywhere on Earth or Digital Web No Connection
    *
    - Anyone with a hypereconomic practice can take Primal Utility, it is not the personal property of the Syndicate. The VAs have more people who are into cryptocurrency and digital economy as well.

    - Just pretend that the Blood Treachery rules on Ghouldom, and the optional Avatar/Arete-melting rules in M20, don't exist. I can see what they were going for but Ghoul is a 6 point *merit*, not a 6 point *flaw*. It is also a merit with a lot of downsides already tacked onto it. It doesn't need a metaphysical destruction of the mage's control of reality that slowly happens over time. A smarter story about addiction will use actual IRL negative consequences of addiction rather than making up supernatural ones. The latter is easy, but cheap. What makes this even more silly is that all of these rules were invented to stop Ghouldom from being too powerful. "What if they put points into Thaumaturgy, then we'll be really screwed!!!". I just... I can't see what they were going for there. There's nothing threatening about a Mage who spends 20 EXP (or whatever extortionate rate Ghouls have to pay) to use low level static magic. All EXP put into Fortitude is EXP you could be putting into Arete and Spheres. Besides, if you don't want them to have Celerity... just... tell them they can't have Celerity. Issue solved. No need to say yes and then plot to steal their Avatar rating for daring to purchase a merit *you let them have in the first place*.

    The tl;dr would be that if you don't want a PC to take the Ghoul merit, don't let them take it. That's just way easier.

    - The Arcane Background is capped at 1 at character gen, and only granted if it suits the character. It's far too open to abuse in my opinion, and the opinion of one of my Mage ST friends. The mild effect of blending into the background and slipping out of the foreground in people's memories is quite cool. I also like the idea of someone just being naturally forgettable and sneaky kind of by giving off less of an "I Am Here" signal. But at 3+ it's just a case of existing in a world without consequences where you're sort of detached from RP simply by being so hard to notice. So it's simultaneously too bad and too good, and all at the cost of 5 freebie points for some reason.

      I take the Primal Utility thing one step further: I allow other Practices than Hypereconomics to access it. In particular, my Choristers have access to a variation of it where instead of providing access to Primal Ventures that are exceptional examples of industry and innovation, it provides access to Primal Fellowships that are exceptional examples of compassion, prudence, and faith.                   Exactly. Though also, it's worth noting that the “example of industry and innovation” bit was in the original write-up of Primal Utility in the Revised Syndicate Convention Book, but got lost in translation in M20; and I consider it to be a vital restriction on Primal Utility, as it keeps the Syndicate from tapping into every small business nationwide and instead causes them to focus on a select few ventures that have a special quality to them, as well as to focus on getting more personally involved in their Ventures in order to safeguard their investments.

                  The Chorister equivalent of this is that you can't just harness any and all religious centers, as the vast majority of churches, synagogues, and mosques aren't the focal points of religious virtue that they claim to be: especially in the World of Darkness, far too many of them are spiritually dead or corrupt. With Primal Utility (which the Choristers would likely have a different name for, reflecting their view that it's not so much about Utility as it is about virtue), the mage would be well-equipped to identify those true fellowships that actually do exemplify the religious beliefs that the Celestial Chorus espouses (and this qualify for the equivalent of “Primal Venture” status, which also needs a different name when views through the Chorister Focus), though his next task would be identifying just what it is about that particular congregation that makes it special and then doing everything he can to nurture and protect it.

                  For what it's worth, I singled out the Celestial Chorus for this initially because traditionally they've held the Seat of Prime; but it also makes sense for their take on just what Prime is to be one that's rooted in human beliefs and activities as opposed to the “mystical energies” that a Hermetic make tends to think of when she contemplates Prime, albeit different beliefs and activities than the ones that the Syndicate favors. I'm reminded of an old GURPS Religion supplement that took the standard GURPS Magic system with its Mana Levels and Magic Items and achieved a more religious flavor through some simple relabeling: Mana became Sanctity, Magery was replaced by Power Investiture, and Magic Items became Holy Items. Shifting the Celestial Chorus from Prime to “Primal Virtue” has a similar effect in Mage.

                  And other aspects of Primal Utility, such as the ease of creating Wonders, apply to someone accessing the Sphere Variant using Faith rather than Hypereconomics; it's just that Chorister Wonders are going to be religiously significant items rather than technomantic ones.

                  My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                              On the Arcane Experience thing: part and parcel with Arcane Experience is the matter of how you go about earning it; and I think this might be a good way to emphasize the Avatar a bit more. That is, consider tying the means by which Arcane Experience is earned at least partially to your Avatar Essence. This is an off-the-top-of-my-head idea right now; but the dynamic between Essence and Resonance really ought to be something to the effect of: you develop Resonance based on your actions, but your actions are influenced by your Essence. Granting Arcane Experience based on Essence-appropriate actions would put some crunch into the latter part while still leaving the character's agency entirely in the player's hands. On the other hand, making Arcane Experience rewards be entirely dependent on your Essence might limit the mage's arcane development a bit too much; so maybe activities relating to all four Essences can earn Arcane Experience, but activities relating to your Avatar's Essence earn more than the others do.

                                              The trick, then, is to come up with activities for each Essence that involve interacting with the supernatural world (otherwise it wouldn't be Arcane Experience) but do so in a way that plays into that Essence.

                                              If you're familiar with Promethean: the Created, you might also consider moving that game's Milestones system to represent lessons your Avatar wants you to learn. Or not; the Milestones system can be rather involved. The idea, though, is a sound one: use Arcane Experience as a way to bring the Avatar's agenda a bit more front-and-center in the game, allowing your magickal potential to develop as your Avatar guides you.

                                              My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
