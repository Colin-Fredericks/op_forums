    Another possibility is that the entirety of the Otherworlds were once all just one Reality Zone, split apart into its various components for some unknown reason and forces. Many of the rules from the original survive in the separate regions, as they were once the same. Over time, some of the rules in some parts changed, others did not. In fact, who is to say that physical reality is even the center of everything. It could just be another level, and something else is the true center, such as Midgard/Alder Bore/etc. It is possible that there is way to connect every part of the WOD's Otherworlds into something more coherent. I've been working on something of late to that effect. Every one of my six sections of Reality, with a separate central locale, share a few basic rules, with individual realms either adding or subtracting rules when necessary. Beings of enough power could change this set-up as needed. This includes Mages at times. Just because something seems to be a rule at a certain time, doesn't mean it will stay that way.

        Originally posted by CajunKhan View Post
        Well there have to be "earthly foundations", otherwise science would have only ever reinforced the consensus. Science is about searching for what is actually true, and what would actually have been true was whatever people believed was true. Back when everyone believed the world was flat, the world would have actually been flat, and scientific experiments would have only found evidence that the world was, in fact, flat.
        That is how I run things: back when everyone believed the world was flat (which was several thousand years ago) the world was flat. At least the parts of it coherent enough to have a shape were flat. It makes surprisingly little difference to history in Mage.

        I also have some objection to your description of science, especially as it pertains to Mage. Science isn't clean, even in our world. There are still pet theories that are given more leeway than other, less accepted ones. Scientists still make claims they can't actually back up. Galileo, for instance, made a famously bad claim that comets were atmospheric effects, prompting the Jesuits to comment that if he thought that he must have never looked at one through a telescope. As it turns out, he hadn't*.

        *The entire story between Galileo and the Church isn't a simple science vs religion thing as people make it out to be. It is a soap opera of personality clashes, stubbornness, and mathematics.

        The point here is that scientists are not prevented from working with incomplete or inconsistent information. There's nothing about the world of Mage that would stop them from trying to bring forward theories that didn't fully match the data and changing the world as they did so. While I do agree that some things like gravity and inertia (call them Earthly Foundations if you like) need to be more robust than others, they do not need to be unchanging or unchangeable. Indeed, changing paradigms make more sense if these things are only consistent in the broad strokes.

            To be fair, Earthly Foundations aren't unchangeable; a mage changes things whenever she performs a work of Magick that is Vulgar according to the Earthly Foundations.

            As for whether they're unchangeable in the long run: I tend to think that they are, and that this is what the Technocracy does whenever it successfully incorporates one of its innovations so thoroughly into the fabric of reality that it no longer even counts as Magick anymore; not even Coincidental Magick. But that's a slow and arduous process, even moreso than changing a Reality Zone; and there's more involved than just altering the Consensus. At the very least, you need to maintain the Consensus in a state that's at odds with the underlying Earthly Foundations, for long enough that the latter adjusts to match the former. That's easier to do when your making small, incremental changes, which is why the Technocracy has a Timetable: said Timetable is their estimate as to how quickly they can get their improvements incorporated into the Earthly Foundations so that they're not available just to Enlightened Scientists and Extraordinary Citizens, but to everyone.

            Going back to my original premise: some good points have been made here, and I'm tweaking my thesis. There are indeed Umbral versions of the Earthly Foundations. But there's a different set of Umbral Foundations for each Umbra: the Dark Umbra has one set; the Middle Umbra has another set; the Astral Umbra has a third set; and Etherspace has a fourth set. I still say that the Deep Umbra is characterized by its lack of Umbral Foundations. Umbral Realms are the Reality Zones of the Umbrae; and they can exist anywhere, including the Deep Umbra (see the Realms of the eldtrich abominations that reside out there). But even in so-called “unformatted Umbra”, the are a handful of rules that exist that are characteristic of the Umbral “layer” (Dark, Middle, Astral, or Ether) such as who gets to hang around with minimal risk of Disembodiment; and those are the Umbral Foundations.

                            Originally posted by Erinys View Post
                            Edit: See, my difficulty with Mage consensus cosmology is that it seems to say nothing is true, or everything is equally true. Then we can't say anything definitive about anything. But yet there have to be definite game rules.
                            Mage cosmology isn't about everything being equally true or false. It's more about truth being non-binary, and more up for grabs. As has been pointed out by others, there are Earthly Foundations which resist being redefined by the Consensus, meaning that the Consensus isn't always right — which is a good thing, because the Consensus has had some pretty dumb ideas in it at times.

                            All that said, it is harder to say anything definitive in Mage; that's kind of the point: to a large extent, Reality is what you make of it. There are also some meta-rules, such as the game systems for working Magick; but what these mean it's something open to interpretation.

                            Originally posted by Erinys View Post
                            The spirit worlds collectively are a heck of a lot larger and deeper than the material worlds, so it's hard to argue that the material world is more fundamental. Being a place of spirit-less matter, the material world is the odd duck out. Or the material world and the Shade Realm of Matter. Assuming the former isn't somewhere inside the latter.
                            M20 introduced the notion of Reality Zones, which means that there isn't a single, global Consensus; instead, there are a lot of local ones. This means that it doesn't matter how vast something is, for the most part; the residents in New York City have no say in the Reality Zones in upper New York State, and vice versa. And that's with New York city's population being much larger than those of the upper New York State zones, individually and possibly even collectively. So the fact that the Umbra is far larger than Earth doesn't give them dominance in the reality shaping game.

                            On top of that, there's some question as to whether or not spirits contribute to the Consensus at all. On Earth, animals don't; only creatures with Avatars do. That is, humans. And for the most part, it's agreed that spirits don't have Avatars. There might be a few exceptions, such as the fabled Pure Ones; and those entities at the very top of the spiritual hierarchy probably do have the ability to shape reality. Yes, this includes the Triat. I'll get back to this a bit further down.

                            Originally posted by Erinys View Post
                            The science+technology = Bad concept wasn't a good design choice for either game, really. I have a friend who loves the CofD but can't stand the cWoD because of it, and I can't really blame her.
                            Neither can I, which is why I've put so much effort into countering that narrative. It is, for example, the main reason why I latched on to the Navalon craft: as I envision them, they let you decouple the Technocracy's Science from the Technocracy.

                            Originally posted by Erinys View Post
                            But the Technocracy are also all about control, about limiting possibilities and options, about censoring knowledge as a weapon to remove things from reality, and about explaining everything in reductionistic ways that eliminate spirituality, souls, intuition, and lots of other details that they know do actually exist. Those goals are also Stasis. (In Werewolf terms, Stasis and the Weaver would be the same thing. By definition, nothing but the Almighty Creator can be bigger than the Weaver because it's inherently 1/3 of all reality.) If Dynamism, Stasis, and Entropy are each 1/3 of reality in Mage, then the same is true: you can't have something bigger than Stasis, except for God/the One.
                            As you say, Werewolf's position is that Stasis and the Weaver are one and the same. By contrast, Mage takes the position that the map is not the terrain. The spirit of a tree is not the tree. And the spirit of Stasis, the Weaver, is not Stasis.

                            Also, arguments for and against the existence of the Christian God apply equally well to the Triat: how do we know that they exist? Has anyone met them personally (and can we trust those who claim to have), or have they only interacted with spirits that claim to serve them? Sure, there's such a thing as Wyrm taint; but that doesn't mean that there's an overall self-aware entity responsible for it. Yes, werewolves believe it to be so; but that doesn't automatically make it so. Not in a Mage game, at least. In fact, M20 specifically leaves it up to the Storyteller to decide what's at the top of the spiritual hierarchy.

                            And even if the Weaver does exist, that doesn't mean that the Technocracy serves it. It may just be that they have goals that overlap to an extent. Emphasis on “to an extent”: just because the Weaver has a particular goal, it doesn't automatically follow that the Technocracy does as well. And there are differences:

                            Originally posted by Erinys View Post
                            Edit: And how can the things Technocrats tell the 'Masses' not be lies? They say magic is impossible, that "reality deviants" and spirits don't exist, but they know all three are real and do exist. They insist there is no spirit world, but they know it exists and how to enter it. They censor scientific discoveries that they don't like.
                            All true, to an extent. And the organization as a whole is problematic. But two things: first, why should the Storyteller nerf Science because Technocrats are jerks?

                            And the Union doesn't do what it does because it wants to crush the human soul. Technocrats are motivated by a desire to help and protect the Masses. The things that they oppose, they don't oppose because they don't believe in them; they opposed them because they think that they're dangerous. Even the control that they seek isn't a goal in and of itself; it's a means to an end. They don't trust Sleepers to take care of themselves, and they think that they know better. Technocracy Reloaded goes so far as to say that they now consider the Masses to be the greatest danger that the Masses face.

                            I wasn't saying the Technocrats created the Gauntlet or Shroud, of course. Some spirits could know, but most mages don't talk to spirits, and some spirits could be lying. And if a spirit says "this god created XYZ" to an atheist mage, the mage'll just ignore what it said.

                                                        Anyway, the main point I was going for before I got sidetracked was that Stasis could use some revision in the context of Mage: as Ramnesis pointed out, all mages are inherently “dynamic”, in the sense that they're looking to change the world. If we're going to give a single word for what the Technocracy is trying to do, I'd prefer Pattern to Stasis: the latter has the connotation of being opposed to change, whereas the former has the connotation of preferring structure.

                                                        And in that regard, I tend to view the Timetable as a conservative thing: not in the Stasis sense of resistance to change, but in the more Pattern sense of being careful not to screw anything up as you advance: building out from the existing structure and making sure that what you add doesn't have unintended consequences that will screw up what you already have. In that sense, I could see calling the Technocracy “conservative”. In short, I see it as having everything to do with their science, while having very little too do with their policies.
