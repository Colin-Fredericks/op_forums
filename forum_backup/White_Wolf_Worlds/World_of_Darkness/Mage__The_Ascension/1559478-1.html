    Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
    Changeling is my weak point in all this. That said, I did know that the Chimerical and the Material existed side by side, but I didn’t consider that to preclude there being a Dreaming Penumbra especially when, as you say, there are areas of geographical correspondence in the Near Dreaming. In fact, I’d ask what are the qualities of those regions that keep them from being penumbral? It might help with how to handle Mage and The Dreaming.
    Partly my fault, but by "mapping" I only meant it has a spatial relationship when you have a way to cross between.

    Basically, the spatial relationship between Trods is usually similar in both realms. It's also similar if you enter the Dreaming through other means.

    Outside that, though, the Near Dreaming has no resemblance to the Material, unless you count "it kind of pretend to follow natural laws".

    It can also have several places mapping to the same spatial area, but completely different in the Dreaming, depending only in the Path you follow to reach there.

    You can have a Path to roughly the area of New York, and it leads to a 18th century village full with small green goblins. Another seems to go to the same area, but leads to an Archology all cyberpunkey, but mounted in a living giant apple tree. A third leads to alien ruins in a moonscape that don't really fit any description of the area and has Trods leading to New York, Ankara, the actual Moon and a cave in Antarctica.

    The place is way more Middle Umbra than anything. It's similar to the Material only in the sense of being mostly stable, though sometimes it's "shaken" by Dreamquakes that change the environment completely.

    What I'd suggest about Frequencies is that they're less a "this place" thing and more a "you" thing. All three exist everywhere, but in a given place they'll be more or less populated, have more or less features. Maybe even they do have individual Gauntlet Strengths: it's the same Gauntlet, but the ease to transpose it is frequency-dependant.

    You may add the need for "frequency equalizing" effects for Mages trying to travel to one they're not attuned to, but that wouldn't be a huge thing, I'd put it in Spirit 1: it's part and parcel of being able to perceive them in the first place.

    Magic also probably default to equalizing any target to its own Frequency: if a Verbena uses an effect to carry everyone to the Penumbra, a Mage attuned to the Astral will still travel to the Middle Frequency, as that's the Frequency of the effect.

    The problem with Frequency being a local thing in Mage: it passively challenges the Paradigm of a Mage about the Other Worlds, as it creates independently verified evidence of a specific structure to reality. In Ascension this is a big no.

    It can also be the case that other Frequencies exist, maybe as distillations of the main three. A creature attuned to one of those may face a few differences, which would explain why the Traditions and Garou face a distinct Gauntlet Difficulty from the Technocracy and Ananasi, even when traveling to the same Frequency.

    Everett Volumes are alternate versions of any Realm that may or may not be distinct time lines, and may or may not exist in the proper Realm. Alternate Earths are Everett Volumes, but Zones like the Dream Zone and the Web are also Everett Volumes.

    They're the main unity of Other Dimensions for the Void Engineers, so everything that's not the Conventional Space is an Everett Volume, even if in practice you won't call the "main" volumes of the Umbrae that day to day, would be needlessly confusing.

    Conventional Space for them is the realm of the Enlightened Anthropic Principle, which is a misnomer but whatever. Basically their reasoning goes as that:

    1. If something has a suitable mathematical model, it exists.

    2. Living beings, through thought/imagination, add mathematical models to reality.

    3. Brains are quantum computers, and so most of those models are suitable, hence, they exist.

    4. The Masses naturally produce the minimum conditions for their existence, forming the base state of the Consensus.

    5. Whenever something exists that contradicts the Consensus, it's placed in another "reality". It is actually just existence that is a suitable model, but was rejected by the Consensus.

    From there you can guess that Everett Volumes agglomerate compatible models/existences and shut incompatible ones into other Volumes and so on. And for them, this applies to every reality, all of them being first the leftovers of Conventional Space.

    An important note: since Conventional Space is governed by Consensus, it's only as uniform as Consensus is, so models created in Reality Zones remain if compatible there, and between the First and Second Horizons some Umbrae cease to be distinct dimensions as the models that comprise them become suitable. Beyond the last Horizon there's no Consensus and the extant models are unknowable. Those places can still have Everett Volumes, though.

    Moving to an Everett Volume always requires to cross the Gauntlet, they're aware of it and do account for it normally.

    People in different Vidare see each other because Mage wanted to have the cake and eat it. That's basically a way of saying you see whatever you believe your should see, but won't split the party.

    I think changing it to be a property of the spell achieves the same effect better: you see what you shouldn't, but that's because you got there by the shenanigans of that weirdo, no big deal.

    Jumping from dreams to the Dreaming is already a thing Changelings do, so I agree. Whatever the connection is, it exists and can be exploited without much effort.

    Be it clear, though, that Changelings don't travel in the other direction and don't enter dreams easily. Doing so requires a specific Art and the dreamer to be nearby. When they get out of the dream at the other side, they appear close to the Dream Bubble and there are Paths to other parts of the Dreaming.

    I don't remember if other Dream Bubbles are visible nearby, but I never saw evidence that this area is really in the Dreaming and never heard of a Path leading there from other places (Paths are not necessarily two-ways).

    So I'd say traveling to the Dreaming through dreams, including Demesnes, is perfectly doable. Just remember that, just like traveling to the Umbra turns you into Ephemera, travel to the Dreaming turns you into Glamour. That's official.

        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
        The problem with Frequency being a local thing in Mage: it passively challenges the Paradigm of a Mage about the Other Worlds, as it creates independently verified evidence of a specific structure to reality. In Ascension this is a big no.
        I agree at least partially, though I think we need to more clearly state the goal. Because Mage does have specific structures and elements that do seem to exist regardless of paradigm, and this is particularly pronounced in how it handles the Otherworlds. For that matter it has resonance which can infuse an area, so there’s already precedent for some magical actions being easier in some areas.

        So having structure itself isn’t the problem, the problem is making sure there’s still room for paradigm and viewpoint to matter. And having viewpoint be a dominant element of which frequency a mage enters accomplishes that, but it is not the only way. Nor does dropping the important of viewpoint deny it.

        I actually don’t think that making frequency depend on location and metaphysical state invalidates paradigms any more than having three to four main otherworlds does. But if we want to apply paradigms to this, other possible ways are that a mage’s paradigm could open up specific actions, places, and resonances that help that type of mage get to a specific frequency.

        From there I agree that for gameplay reasons, if a mage casts a spell to bring multiples over, everyone will end up in the same frequency. I also think that clever uses of consecration or other low rank powers could help mages find each other if they don’t have the means to cross over as a group or in the same place.

        Also, for gameplay reasons I think that magically brute force travel into a frequency that doesn’t match location, resonance, or temperament should be harder either in difficulty or in sphere rank. I’m actually partial to sphere rank in this case since I think brute force is actually doing something fundamentally different than using the associations that are already there.

        That’s for travel, though. Just seeing and communicating should remain the same sphere ranks and at most have a difficulty spike.

                                    The WOD version is expectedly very removed from the real world concept. There are also quite different perspectives on it from different splats.

                                    Mage (in Dead Magic II) describes it as "a Shallow Realm created by shamans for their Dreamborn (the term the section uses for spirits) kin", covering most of Australia. It is distinct from the Dreaming but connected at a higher level, and it also is not the Umbra.
                                    The connection between Dreaming and Dreamtime is described as, and I quote: "Think of them as two different cable channels: [the] Dreaming is MTV, and the Dreamtime is more like the Discovery Channel."

                                    In order to access it, a new initiate needs to be 'attuned' to the realm with a specific Rite, after which they can enter the Dreamtime by stepping sideways with a difficulty depending on location and condition of the practitioner.

                                    The section also has a Rote and rules to create similar 'Sleeping Lands', areas "between Gauntlet and Umbra". Overall, the concept is (imo) very similar to a Penumbral realm, especially the Shadowlands. The book directly states that Shallow Realms are a variation of Bastet Den Realms. As with the latter, the main difference to Penumbrae is that they usually do not connect directly to the deeper Umbra.

                                    The takeaway for the purpose of this thread is that it is a liminal realm that requires attunement to access - different from the usual Essence types - which can provide some rules and mechanisms for Penumbrae that function similarly.

                                                        Penumbrae aren't superpositions. It's not just physical beings that ignore the materiality of spirits and objects in them, but also the reverse in all cases.

                                                        Not with Chimerical substances, which would be actually a more fitting name than reality. If you step in a Chimerical flower or insect, they are crushed. If you move your hands in a way that holds a Chimerical object, you handle it. You're solid for them, all the time, all of them.

                                                        And if you can perceive them, you're in to it, no matter who you are. Enchantment is only the easiest way to notice Chimerical substances. Sometimes drugs do the trick, or other mental states, or being too young. Sometimes mood is enough, though that's rarer. But just by seeing them, they're solid for you. You can touch hold or ride a Chimera. A Chimerical beast can lift you without a Material counterpart, once you can see it.

                                                        Some can lift you regardless. It's rare, but is an effect of some Chimerical substances and forces called Wyrd.

                                                        Finally, the Mists don't separate anything, that's not their effect. They take memories. To be affected by the Mists is to forget all interaction you ever had with Chimerical substances of any kind, and to have the Mists lifted is to get your memories back.

                                                        Nothing "pierces" or "traverse" the Mists. They are not a "thing", this is just a name. It's just that when you forget, it's said the Mists have settled in. They're the reason few people talk about fairies even among the supernatural. The Changelings and Chimerae are cosmopolitan, they interact with humans and Prodigals all the time. They're witnessed. They kidnap people at random for weekend parties and return them. Yet no one remembers.

                                                        If a Mage is ever affected by the Mists, they will not just stop seeing the Fae, they'll forget every interaction they ever had. Able to rediscover an interaction happened only through roundabout evidence. A note scribbled will remain, memory gaps can point to something, but the memories are unavailable until a Mind Effect, not Spirit, pierces the Mists. Without this even a memory Effect will fail as the forgetfulness just reassert itself immediately.

                                                        Want to remember without this? You'll need to interact indirectly forever, or find a permanent way to lift the Mists. That won't affect senses, only memory. It's just that if and while you can perceive Chimerical substances, the Mists lift naturally.

                                                        So, there's no Gauntlet. There's really no reality to cross. There's invisibility and forgetfulness of phenomena you actually interact with all the time without noticing. And the difficulty others have to interact with this, aside it being the fact most players know Changeling the least of all lines, is just that it's all counterintuitive. That a typical Mage is precisely trying to pierce reality when they just need to look around with new eyes...

                                                        And a Mind 1 Effect, maybe with Prime 1, to see Glamour. That would give complete ability to interact with Chimerical "Reality", no other requirement. A stronger Mind Effect would be necessary to hold onto your memories after the Effect is over, rolled against your own rank of Banality.
