                              Additionally, when it comes to the World of Darkness, and even more so with Mage, one needs to define "real."

                              If they exist as Umbrood, as there's a whole class of Umbrood that are spirits of human beliefs, is that real?

                              If they exist as a form of bygone or similar unique entity that is unclear if they were formed by said beliefs or if they've taken on the forms of those characters to have enough power to stay in the material plane even if they don't actually do all the legends, are they real?

                              Is the aforementioned Marauder Santa real?

                              There's lots of ways that things can exist in the WoD. Whether that means the human beliefs of them are "real" is an entirely different issue. Also, as noted by monteparnas, common in Western culture is not the same as Consensus.

                                          The advantage of some options like an Umbrood is that it doesn't really relies on the Consensus, local belief is more than enough.

                                          And it may also result in several entities that are "aspects" of them, as very different legends that are thought as the "same" entity nowadays may exist separately and yet answer to the same modern name.

                                          Especially with Santa, that is the most widespread of those in "some" form. The others, though, are far less ubiquitous even as different versions. The Tooth Fairy is the third more common and is already pretty much not that ubiquitous and much less consistent. The actual idea of a "tooth fairy" only exists in north Europe and some parts of England, and in the US. Historically even those regions have teeth-related superstitions more than fairy-tales.

                                          In romance-speaking Europe a similar legend sprang during the later half of the 19th century, but it is a magical rat or mouse, not a fairy, and even then it isn't as common as in the US and didn't reach every romance country. One I can say it never got to be popular in is Portugal, there and here in Brazil we don't have this legend except as an occasional borrowing from Hollywood. It isn't a thing in the rest of the world, although those teeth rituals are pretty common, consisting generally in burying or throwing them.
