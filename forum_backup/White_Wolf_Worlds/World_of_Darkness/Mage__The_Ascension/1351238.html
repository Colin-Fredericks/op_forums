    Using Mythic Lands

    11-27-2019, 04:10 AM
    So here we have that fascinating umbral realm of myth and fiction and very little in rules or discussion about it. With how developed communications are today, a lot of young mages could be using more mythological/fictional characters for their spirit magic rather then crusty and dusty Goetia demons. Adepts of the Silver Portal already do interact with such realms extensively, why shouldn't even more dynamic mages do so? Not to mention quite a few fictional being sort of expect to be summoned and used in such a way like LoL/Dota champions or Fate servants,

    So, how would you go about stating such beings in a game? How much quintessence, how to eyeball power levels, some rules for manifestation, paradox, etc. Maybe what you would think some of those creature's reaction would be to the WoD?

          I'll try to explain.

          Mages with Spirit sphere can summon various umbrood/spirits/aliens etc.
          Generally, it is assumed they either summon local spirits that stay in Penumbra next to the real world or contact various religious beings.

          There is an umbral realm called Mythic Lands. This is where the non-religious figures of various mythologies used to live. However, with the explosion of fiction and communications the Mythic Lands also experienced explosive growth. Now Mythic Lands house Star Wars, Star Track, My Little Pony, etc. Including even more popular fan-works.

          So, why would a modern mage, steeped in modern culture and fiction risk summoning those cranky angels and demons born thousands of years ago? Sure, they would be more powerful and much, much more knowledgeable, but the denizens of Mythic Lands are much more understandable. Lore about them is freely available too! Not to mention some of those beings are pretty much designed to be summoned. The oneiromantic sorcerer tradition of Silver Portal already does that, though mostly treffiking with even more ephemeral creatures. And superman is a paradox spirit, with other superheroes continuously generated from Wyld.

          How I personally would see it that you either need Spirit 3 + Space 3 to drag a stable character from Mythic lands, or Spirit 3 to go there and manually walk one back to Earth. Maybe you could also play around with Life and Prime for more stability. For summoning you would need a correspondence link in the form of a fictional work from which you are summoning it. Also probably divied them in ranks of 1 to 5 as other spirits. The most popular ones like HP could be summoned of any rank, bu the less popular/powerful ones would not get higher ranks.


          So what are your thoughts about it?

                                      Originally posted by Tytalus View Post

                                      What book/page reference do you have?

                                      I would think such a land would have beings in the collective conscious of humanity for centuries, not just a couple decades. So "new" beings there would be stories circulating maybe from the late 1700s, early 1800s at the latest.
                                      Consulting my antique copy of The Book of Worlds, the Mythic Worlds are described on pages 63 - 65. They aren't a single realm, but a descriptor for countless realms born out of human vision and imagination. They start as small micro-realm dream bubbles in Maya/The Dream Realm, and most simply dissolve after a night or so. But ones that keep getting revisited, by either the original dreamer or those that share the idea (or just a very similar one) grow, and eventually turn into full fledged realms. Some of these realms are born out of myths and legends. Others out of what could be described as a cultural zeitgeist or collective ideal. And yes, some do form out of modern fiction. The issue though is that when I say these realms are countless, I'm not exaggerating. And trying to find the specific one you are looking for can sometimes be very difficult. To use Camelot as an example, you can go looking for Malory's version and end up in Twain's. Or in the movie Excalibur. Or the original Welsh story. And so on. The same thing can happen with Star Wars. If you find a realm based on it, is it pre or post Disney? Pre or post prequels? Pre or post Empire Strikes Back? How much expanded universe? Etc. You might even stumble across some obsessive fan's fan-fiction version where Han Solo and Jabba the Hutt are happily married and Luke is an intergalactic pizza delivery boy. And speaking of obsessive fans, one of the types you are most likely to meet in any Mythic Realm based on a fandom are the dreaming fans of that property. And they may react negatively to outsiders who aren't "one of us".

                                      Summoning anything from the Mythic Realms is likely to have some of the same issues as finding the realm you want. Summoning Luke Skywalker might end up giving you novice farm boy Luke or cranky old hermit Luke just as easily as it will get you Jedi Knight Luke. Because the Mythic Worlds spawn from Maya, certain issues involving Changeling and its chimera may come in to play, with the unawakened destroying them with disbelief or possibly not even being able to see or interact with them at all. Creatures summoned are likely similar to Bygones, and suffer the same problems many of those beings can have in a reality they don't really belong in.

                                      As far as who would actually do something like this, Orphans certainly might try it. Hollow Ones also, provided that the fandom or fiction in question is sufficiently "cool". The Dreamspeakers of the Ghost Wheel Society may have members who study this sort of thing, as might the Baruti. The Hermetic House Thig might also dabble with it, but probably more in counter-culture myths than pop culture fandom. Etherite Ethernauts may also try to study aliens or technology from certain sci fi realms. (I think there is a good argument that the Gernsback Continuum is a Mythic Realm.) The Wu Lung's realm of Kun Lun is actually a Mythic Realm. I think the Sisters of Hippolyta had a similar Realm, but I can't recall off hand and don't have my copy of Book of Crafts handy to check.

                                                          Apropos to nothing: Most of the Mythic Realms I tend to use in my games tend to be really broad conceptions of their subjects rather than specific ones, and sometimes this means they will, on occasion, abruptly shift appearance, mood and even genre while characters are there.
