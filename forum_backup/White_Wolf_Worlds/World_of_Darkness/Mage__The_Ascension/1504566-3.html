        The Syndicate are fundamentally economists, and economics is the study of incentive-structures. Incentive structures can be difficult to balance. For example, back in the day the Vietnamese government tried to incentivize the killing of rats by offering a bounty on rat-tails. The populace then proceeded to farm rats for their tails, chopping them off and then releasing the tailless but alive rats to bred. The rat population became worse than ever.

        Setting up incentive structures can be deceptively complicated. Set up a competition based incentive structure, and the first winners attempt to shut the door to the top behind them, forming monopolies and oligopolies that destroy the very system they were intended to serve. Set up a bureaucratic economic system, and the bureaucrats are incentivized to favor themselves, their families, and friends, to fudge economic numbers, and to erode any method of supervision and transparency that might threaten their position.

        Really, much more of the Syndicate fluff should be about dealing with the complexity of incentive structures, and less about pure cash.

                    There's a tension between Capitalism-as-System and Capitalism-as-Ideology. The Ideology of Capitalism - the one sold to the public to trick them into accepting their exploitation - is that small numbers of people owning the means of production is good because Competition. But as a policy matter, as an outgrowth of how Capitalism works when unfettered, it inevitably trends to Monopoly. Capitalism is, by its nature, ANTI-Competition. It will get as close to Feudalism as it is allowed to. That's not a bug, but a Feature. It's why the wealthy and powerful adopted it as a system in the wake of violent revolt against Feudal structures.

                    One of the greatest deceptions of the Neo-Liberal order has been convincing people that it and Capitalism are non-ideological. When in fact, it's as much a measure of Faith as any that came before it.

                    Ergo, while there's plenty of economists among the Syndicate, they are economists working within a paradigm (and Paradigm) fundamentally wedded to Capitalist power structures. It's a cancer that consumes and recuperates everything, up to and including criticisms of Capitalism. There are many Syndicate economists who, in a different reality, might have been using the same Hypereconomics to argue for non-Capitalist economic modes. As it stands, they're working within an Ideological framework centered around Capitalism, just as Celestial Chorusters work within a Monotheistic framework, and the Order of Hermes works within a Hermetic framework. They are as much motivated by Ideology as anyone.

                    Where internal differences may exist depends entirely on how much an individual Syndic believes that Ideology, and how much they acknowledge that it's a scam made to maintain existing power structures. The former operate under the belief that if they just do Capitalism hard enough, all its shortcomings will iron themselves out. That if they get enough people believing in Competition and Bootstraps, Competition will happen and people will actually elevate themselves through hard work.

                    The latter know that's not really the point, and it's all about keeping the Sleepers under control. That they make loads of money in the process is just a fringe benefit, you see. Trust them.

                                As I'm fond of pointing out. Mage posits that physical reality is mutable, it never actually makes the claim that moral reality is as well. Indeed, Mages don't act like this is true. They don't try to cast spells to change morality within the local area. They don't act like they are currently in the wrong but will be in the right if they change enough minds.

                                As for the soft sciences, they tend to be studies of human systems. Changing them at any kind of important level requires changing human nature. While its never directly stated, there's a tacit assumption that the Consensus does not change itself. Most people in the WoD believe that reality is unchanging, but the Consensus still alters it. So while there are plenty of mind control and manipulation effects, actual changes to human nature are the stuff of Mass Ascension.

                                We really wouldn't want it any other way, either. Mage is supposed to be about motives and stakes we at the table understand. Change too much about humanity and we remove that connection.

                                                It is important to differentiate between explanations and the things they explain; between the 'why' and the 'what.' It is the 'what' that is likely outside the reach of the Consensus.

                                                Now I certainly agree that human nature is hard to define except in the broad strokes, yet we can still say quite a bit about it. People are going to form bonds and attachments, have a sense of right and wrong, have a sense of fair play, claim ownership, claim belonging, have an inner life, feel pride and shame and compassion and injury, form opinions and customs and beliefs, seek to communicate, etc. etc. These are the kind of things that cannot be transformed, truncated, or removed.

                                                *This list is not exhaustive and focuses on the behavioral over the spiritual though those should be present too, and Mage assumes such.

                                                Now the degree to which these elements are expressed in a culture can change. Theories for why people are the way they are can change. Governments, cultures, economics can change. Those are all on the table in the Ascension War. The underlying human nature would not change, even when denied.. That's what gives the appearance that the soft sciences are immutable.

                                                Originally posted by Aleph View Post

                                                The problem is one of definition; Capitalism is a hazy term. There are definitions that encompass most human action throughout history and Capitalism in that sense is likely inevitable. There are likewise definitions that mostly describe our modern economic systems, and those forms are anything but inevitable. That's why some people can describe Capitalism as a natural extention of human nature and others can describe it as inevitably violating human dignity and each group will look at the other as if they are crazy.

                                                The solution in most cases is to define terms, but in this case that likely would derail the thread.
