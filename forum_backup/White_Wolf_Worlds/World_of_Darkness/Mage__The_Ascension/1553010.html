    The Euthanatos Tradition and the Sphere of Entropy

    08-08-2025, 11:51 AM
    Hi there!

    (Please forgive my English, as it is not my native language.)

    I have a hard time understanding the relation or link between the Euthanatos and Entropy.

    On one hand, the Tradition mages are the ones who sees if someone’s life should be ended or if it should go on. How does one see this with the Entropy sphere? The tradition’s description shows them mostly as “ death-mages “ (death, in a neutral sense, not a bad sense), where they kill someone who’s time has come, or kill someone who disrupts the natural way / the Wheel.

    On the other hand, the Entropy Sphere is mainly about, fate, fortune, and luck. Sure, it is also about decay and weak spot. The sphere is mostly about affecting probabilities and decay. But where in the sphere description does it shows how to determine if someone’s time has come (to die) or if someone’s time has not yet come?

    With the Life sphere, you can see if someone is sick, and you can choose to heal him/her, or not.

    But in the Entropy sphere, in the description, I don’t see how an Entropic mage can determine where someone’s position is on the Wheel…

    So, Story-wise and rule-wise, how does all of this is determined and how does it works?

    In mainly use the M20 edition, but I went and checked in the second edition and in the revised edition. And I still didn’t find the answers I’m looking.

    Maybe it’s obvious and I just didn’t understand what’s in front of me in the books, so I apologize in advance for asking this question.

    Thanks in advance for your time and patience to all who will answer!

      Well, Entropy 1 can see wheter someone it's close to death. Just like you can sense danger. But that's not what Euthanatos mean when they say things like "kill someone who’s time has come".

      That's mostly a judgement call based on their own moral code, that I don't remember exactly, but can be roughly summarized as, so to speak: People that cause a lot of suffering and make the world worse are a waste of air and need to do better, send them back to the cycle to see if next time they do better.

      No Sphere can tell you "when it's morally correct to kill someone". At most, you could do some research, and then convince yourself that whatever information you now have justifies your actions.

      Important note: While particularly evil humans may be up for the good death, this judgement it's often to mages that have accumulated too much death ressonance, and this is accumulated mostly by killing people with magick - so an Euthanatos that it's too eager to kill bad people with their power will end on the list as well. Killing it's understood as a last resort.

      But why Entropy?: It's the sphere of fate, and Euthanatos often see themselves as "hands of fate", so it makes sense they would have powers bestowed to them by fate. It has ties to necromancy trough how the underworld and the concept of decay relates to magick (which it's a little contrived, but it's what it is), and Euthanatos come from people that tended the dead. In a Doylist sense, it's because of the whole "heat death of the universe" mythic that surrounds concept of entropy - ultimately entropy will bring the "good death" to the universe itself

      But, really, all the Spheres assignation to a given Tradition are a little arbitrary, hence why you can choose other Spheres to be your main on M20.

          Originally posted by Lightbringer1 View Post
          I have a hard time understanding the relation or link between the Euthanatos and Entropy.
          In addition to everything Aleph said, it's a matter of understanding three concepts (I'll use the Hindu concepts instead of the in-game ones, because I think they're actually more didactic):

          1- Entropy is also the Sphere of complex systems, their dynamics and evolution. This is, in the end, what unifies fate and decay as a single concept. For the Euthanatoi, the focus is to understand the Wheel as a system. While its purpose and goal is abstractly important, their focus is in its workings right now;

          2- The key to understand what the Euthanatoi call The Wheel are the concepts of Samsara and Moksha. Basically they believe every incarnation has a personal purpose of being a means to learn and change, and eventually achieve Enlightenment. The Wheel is Samsara, the eternal cycle of reincarnation where everyone is constantly living, dying and coming back, slowly moving towards a Liberation from this unending cycle, which is Moksha;

          3- Aside from the personal goal of attaining Moksha, every life has a role to fulfill for others and for the community, or Dharma. For the Euthanatoi this includes both current Dharma and the notion of fate, but not as in what will happen to you, and instead as in what good you'll bring in your future. So beyond the goal of achieving Moksha, you have a duty to help (consciously or not) other to do the same.

          Usually happiness has a strong role to play in all this, but it's not necessarily so. Most Euthanatoi will avoid to kill someone who is and/or will make others happy, but this isn't always the case.

          Once you get all those concepts, what the Euthanaoi understand is that the Wheel, Samsara, can become temporarily clogged. Sometimes people reach a point where, in their current life, they're neither advancing towards Moksha, nor helping anyone to do so, neither being happy or making others happy or helping community in any way. In some cases, they're actively hindering others in such a way that whatever good their lives are producing, it's not being worth it.

          So the Euthanatoi assume the role of unclogging Samsara by ending this life, so the person can reincarnate again and return to advancement. It can be a bad person that hinders others as well as themselves, but it can also be a patient so ill that continuing life will only bring pointless suffering to all involved, a person whose fate from now is to stop advancement, or any other number of things. The point is the system of Samsara, its health, so to speak, and if a person's life benefits it, or if cleaning the slate is a better course of action.

          Remember that in all cases, they believe in reincarnation. Actually eliminating the Atma, the real immortal soul, is possibly the most terrible sin, only to be carried if that soul's continued existence is truly anathema to everyone else.

                      It's also part of the setting that the revelation that Avatars and souls have evidence of not being the same thing is relatively new to mage society. For most of history, mages didn't even perceive Avatars as separate entities from the self, and the commonality of experience of Avatars exerting independence to the mages they're bonded to didn't really even set in until the Enlightenment era. This was one of the Time of Judgement plot threads as the Revised metaplot accelerated this dissonance between mage and Avatar to the point of representing an existential threat to there being new mages in the future.

                                                      Originally posted by Verlan View Post
                                                      Don't really think the Euthanatoi's implied connection to cycles of reincarnation was ever really intended to be linked to their specialty Sphere?
                                                      From their own POV, probably not, especially as editions went on.

                                                      But while the rules used to reinforce them through price cuts in XP, the specialty Spheres exist primarily within the setting as the Seats in the Council of Nine, which is a social/metaphysical construct.

                                                      So, while this construct isn't strictly Chakravanti, but mostly derived from Hermetic thought, there's still a mystical reason for them to be linked to Entropy, however any particular member feels about this reason (or the link itself).

                                                      They're first and foremost linked to Entropy because they were made by the devs to represent Entropy, so there is at least a thematic link in the first place, not a practical one. Then there's a lot of aspects of the game and the setting that reinforce the concept of each Tradition being intrinsically linked to their Sphere, despite M20 deviating in how strong it presents this link (it used to be a huger thing).

                                                      At the end of the day, it is both artificial and metaphysical. No Tradition has to be constrained to a given Sphere, and in fact none was when they came up. It is both an imposition from the rules (for a Specialty Sphere) and from the Council of Nine (as an institution within it). Yet, it is in a way present in them since always, both as their Doylist raison d'être since inception, and interpretation of some defining aspect of them that justified them being chosen for that Seat. It is a mystical position they assumed based on their identity, and Mage being Mage, the distinction between symbol and symbolized is blurred at best.
