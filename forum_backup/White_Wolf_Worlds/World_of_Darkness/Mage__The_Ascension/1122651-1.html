    Sorry, using something like Forces 1 to detect the small shifts in the room... Obfuscate 1 is *telling you that there isn't anything going on*.

    A human thinks someone is in the room, and takes pictures. They check the pictures. They see nothing (for now), as Obfuscate is *telling them they are alone*. Same applies to someone watching a video if they are in range. The Spheres are just another source of info, and one that you will ignore because of Obfuscate.

    Mind can break Obfuscate, certainly. The other Spheres? Well Spirit means one of your spiritual allies could tell you, since Obfuscate only works on "minds", not ghosts and spirits. Correspondance, Matter, Entropy, Forces, yes there are problems in the room, small things aren't right (Correspondance might be saying "hmm these two objects look closer together than they are, I think my senses are being warped") but Obfuscate IS you ignoring that. I mean for crying out loud your EYES and all your other senses are screaming "VAMPIRE" as loud as they can and your brain is ignoring it because Obfuscate is fundamentally not about the senses in its basic concept.

    Personally if a Mage character had no Mind, and had Awareness, I'd let them roll to get a "chill up their spine" or something similar so that they can teleport away or something and not get melted by the blood-vomit of the Assamite who just snuck up behind them.

            Originally posted by vicky_molokh View Post
            Obfuscate, at least below elder levels, is telling the mind not to see the vampire. It doesn't prevent detections using other senses. In fact, it says:
            "Since the vampire fully retains his physical substance, he must be careful to avoid contact with anything that may disclose his presence (knocking over a vase, bumping into someone). Even a whispered word or the scuffing of a shoe against the floor can be enough to disrupt the power."

            So electromagnetic sense, echolocation, mystic spirit-awareness etc. should be quite disruptive for Obfuscate.
            Obfuscate can be disrupted by making loud noises or disrupting other objects, yes. Speaking, wading through water, stepping on a creaky board...

            But the reason this breaks Obfuscate is that it removes the pretense that there is nothing in the room. When a board creaks the "I'm not here"-ness of Obfuscate becomes difficult to maintain (so they have to roll to keep Obfuscate up). If someone *has good reason* to believe that they are not alone, then they can attempt to See the Unseen, but this is an active decision that someone actually has to make themselves, at which point practically any Sphere would do, yes. Realistically though, when a Mage hears a board creak, they don't whip out a Forces 1 effect to see if there is a weight sitting on the floor other than them, or use Spirit 1 to perceive local auras.

            So yes, Obfuscate does not edit the Vampire out of every sense available to those around them, but it does convince them that the Vampire isn't there. If they smell the Vampire's wet clothes, they don't immediately break Obfuscate. If they hear a floor creak, they don't immediately break Obfuscate. They rationalize those things away, not because of the power, but because of the way people just *behave* when they hear odd things. If you want to 100% override this mind control you need Mind, Mind 1 to reflexively detect that your mind is being controlled, and whatever level of Mind is needed to resist mind control would be needed too. Either that or the Vampire needs to mess up the Wits + Stealth roll when they step on the board.

            Now if a Mage randomly decides to construct a spacial map of the room they're in in their head using Correspondence, closing their eyes and building everything in their surroundings inside their mind... do they break Obfuscate by sensing the object moving around the room they're in? I think that's up for debate. On the one hand they're still getting the "I'm not Here" signal, and I think that would hold. On the other you could argue that Obfuscate edits Vampires out of mundane senses only, and if someone *happens* to do a detailed supernatural examination of their surroundings they will automatically break Obfuscate.

            Note that this is all from a MTA perspective. If we go by VTM rules then it's a simple Perception + Awareness vs. Manipulation + Subterfuge, difficulty 7 for both. This rule is in there since the VTM book knows that comparing ratings and differently balanced powers won't always work and that it's easier to just go by rolls.

                            Originally posted by vicky_molokh View Post
                            Ah, true, the consequences of lacking a unified framework on which all powers are built.
                            Since I run VTM I just give everything powers that work in a VTM framework to be honest. Mages still have the Traditions and the Technocracy and "Paradigms", but if you're playing Vampire the LAST thing you need is Paradox and Spheres and all that malarky. Werewolves keep stuff like Red Talons hating humans and Silver Fangs being uppity, but lose a lot of the mechanical stuff (Spirits as CCTV, Gifts).

                            Like one of the Vampires in my game has a Technomage Fangirl. He's her Husbando; she has a technocratic paradigm and works out of a basement for the VAs. Her job? She looks at supernaturals and writes up reports on them. Using CCTV, local news, online messengers (and hacking) she finds Vampires, Werewolves etc. and writes up reports on them. If a VA (or allied Mages) need to know about the Anarchs in Manchester, she's got a file on them. And in the process of learning about this PC, she falls in love with him. Like her, he's emotionally distant. He's got an eyepatch and is very handsome. He's blank enough for her to project all of her tastes onto. She has a psychiatric report on him pinned to her wall, and she's put love hearts around the various neuroses he has, since they're similar to her own. Basically this led to her submitting a report which gave the Anarchs a lower threat level than she should have done, and she's been cleaning up after their digital footprint in her spare time. Players only know about this via "cutscenes". Her powers? Technomancy 5, and some other powers (Auspex 2, Presence 4, Levinbolt 2) that are distinctly "tech-based"... so she can short-circuit a machine remotely, or scan an aura by running some CCTV footage through a special program, but she can't just conjure up lightning from thin air or see someone's aura with her eyes.

                            Werewolves? Well besides their Healing Factor and their Forms, they get Disciplines. Theurgists get "blood magic" reskinned. I do this with every splat, and I keep the lore and themes which work and don't get too bulky. Wraiths are a thing, and have Fetters, but lack Shadows, as just one example.

                                    Originally posted by Lian View Post

No amount of extra time will give the vampire more dice. A mage can spend the time building up his "Vampire hunting ritual".                                     Quite right, and I get the spirit of what you're trying to do. I agree..Vampires, Werewolves, etc....they are the instant-action splats. The always-ready-for-go-time creatures.

                                    But leaving Arete as-is for those rolls means Mages can't win any contested actions that involve magic as they will potentially always have a much smaller dice pool than the other for every round of contest, and are also the worst splat at counterspelling. It's kind of ironic, really, if you consider the nature of those two things.

                                    So it's kind of a dilemma, as while the theme of the other splats being more instant-action is perfect, the ironic flipside is one of Mages being the worst at magical defense without an almost paranoid lifestyle.

                                    I consider the 'Arete counts for twice the sucesses in contests and counters vs. splats' to be a good balance - it evens those odds between the splats for basic things, yet while *still* allowing the other splats to toss out their whole pack of abilities near instantly.
                                    A vampire can pump their strength, have passive extra bonuses to dex, and toss out a dominate effect in the same round without any preparations - instant alpha strike, and without any tools, gadgets or practices needed - while this still remains very hard for a mage to do, not mentioning justification with a good RPed paradigm. They can go for Wonder creation (expensive) or Time 4 (needs preparation and paradigm again), and both take effort, investment and RP.

                                    And yes, I think a Mage's counterspelling needs paradigmatic action - it's not reflexive, IMO. If a Mage doesn't have their tools, or even if they have their tools but are lazing on a chair with the hands in their pockets, they have a hard time justifying a counterspelling action from my point of view. Another benefit of having managed to drop an instrument, but unless a lot of instruments are dropped, the array of instantly counterspellable things is narrow.
