                This gives me an idea. The goal, for me, is to have something (I used the ever-present force of Paradox) erase memory. Let's suppose a group of Technomancers have the time, luxury and believing Sleeper participants to do a Conjunctional Magick Ritual to erase the memory of witnesses to Vulgar Magick within a long range of the place the ritual was done. I will go through the effect, Sphere by Sphere.

                Time 4 Triggered Effect: The effect will be activated under certain conditions.

                Prime, at the level needed to detect Paradox, combined with Mind 2 and Entropy 1 to detect the number of witnesses. The effect only goes off if there are many witnesses.

                Correspondence 3 to extend the range of detection, as well as the range of delivery of the effect.

                Mind 4 is the main effect. Erasing or editing the memories of the witnesses. Correspondence also extends the area of effect.

                Forces 3 could be added to make the effect more coincidental and believable for the Technomancers doing the casting. The explanation is that a satellite is sending the instructions to the brains of the targets via microwave signal. Add life 3 to specifically target the brain.

                Story-wise, this is less punitive than having goons show up out of nowhere every time the players try to do something and it maintains the consistency of the Consensus. The players realize they have not achieved anything by their flagrant use of Vulgar Magick and they know the goons will start to look for them. Time to retreat, cover their tracks and plan a more subtle method for changing the Consensus.

                                Of course its possible that a suitably powerful and appropriately specced mage might try and do something horribly vulgar in front of a massive crowd to influence the masses and thereby change the consensus. You could build a campaign around this as either technocracy (trying to stop them) or traditions/crafts (trying to stop them/trying to help them).
                                The assumptions we make going forward depend ENTIRELY on the game you are playing. No NPC does this. Its simply too dangerous, difficult, fatal.
                                If you want to stop it then give the rebel mage allies, have him be very powerful (high arete so he can actually pull it off), even have it happen, then the PCs have to handle clean up. If people see something very flashy then maybe the PCs need to run point on launching hologram technology into the consensus a decade early and control the narrative that it was a public entertainment test. If it was something dangerous where lots of people died then maybe it was a bomb and hallucinagenic gas launch, and they have to scrub the digital traces, track down and neuralzye key communicators, etc.
                                If the PCs want to be the ones to do it then you'll need to throw all sorts of creative traps and hurdles at them. How do they manage to do it, (high difficulty, high numbers of success, high paradox). How do they escape the wrath of the traditions AND the technocracy. What else crawls out the woordwork afterwards? Vampires/Garou/Ghosts, depending on WHAT they do to achieve this mass vulgar event, any other group might also show up. How do they spread the message even further, how to they work against the technocracy cover up attempt.

                                All this "It wouldn't work" "It would work" discussion is pointless. The setting isn't telling a story, it is the setting for YOUR story. Tell us how it worked in your game or how your players stopped it.

                                        Agreeing with most of what was said above, that the setting *can* be broken it's not necessarily a bad thing. If it couldn't be broken, then the Traditions and other faction's figth it's for naugh.

                                        What does matter, I think, it's if it it makes sense for the setting to have survived 'til now. It's only fair to consider how this plan could have failed, and why. It would be a bad thing if it was so easy, that someone should already have done it. Or if it was such a good method that trully being subtle seems dumb.

                                        But precisely because these kind of plans have a lot of moving parts and opposing forces, I think it's reasonable to think it's not that easy, and it - not being impossible - just failed up until now.

                                        The Taftani aproved the plan, tried it, and got mix results: They're too small a faction to change the world - but, HEY, they managed to keep their ugly desertic Reality Zone. It's not like the Technocracy didn't tried to wipe them out entirely, but the Craft managed to make it too expensive to bother - Technocracy was like: "who wants that desert anyway?, keep it!" - For the time being, of course, because the Technocracy it's in a very complex war against multiple forces.

                                        M20 goes with the Taftani have starting to favor subtler methods, to infiltrate the modern world and expand their influence beyond their small place - I don't like it, but it is what it is

                                        The Traditions are much more powerful than the Taftani, but also have a lot of organizational problems. Some mages would be opposed to break the Rule of Shade for moral reasons and others because they don't want to eat 'dox for "the cause", while surely others would figth for what Paradigm get's more camera time (or just use the whole thing to throw political rivals under the bus, any bus) ... I say this to showcase how convincing them to go for it would be a political feat of no small magnitude (and prob. the kind of thing that takes decades of blood and tears, with no small amount of assassination attempts) - and that's just to start the plan with some backing

                                        If you don't get a faction behind and do it as a rogue or a band of rogues...well. I see a problem if the Technocracy it's so strong that they can just obliterate the Traditions, but I see not so much of a problem with them being able to overpower a loner. I hope you have some serious personal power and a realy good plan...

                                        And you really should account for things like future sigth (Nephandi and other vile mages do that - they obscure their personal timelines so often, some mages would become suspicious of you if they can't see your future. You should, at least, be that careful. Probably more, I would ask the ST if I can add Mind to project a fake future to those that would try to peek)

                                        While Paradox doesn't erase memory (and doesn't care much about the number of people). There's at least one case I can think of where Paradox was so big, it could have had the secondary effect of erasing the memory of a fact: rogue archmaster Czar Vargo's case.

                                        That case it's interesting, because in some books it was stated that the Technocracy defeated Vargo and then erased the memory of everyone in the biggest cities of the world. But if my memory doesn't fail, in Revised it's suggested that the Technocracy wishes they had that kind of power, that Paradox erased Vargo's fleet from reality and it was like it never happened. People forgot that time when a crazy scientist almost takes over the world...

                                        Let's note that this wasn't Paradox affecting the mind of Sleepers, but rather a fact being erased from reality affecting the memory of sleepers.

                                        I happened after a Vulgar fight against the Technocracy, that probably accumulated quite an amount of 'dox in separate instances. It would be a break from the normal rules ... unless, perhaps, Wrinkle was involved (as erasing someone from time does cause the secondary effect of having people forget...since the erased never existed)
