                For archspheres, I like setting 6 as “whatever 5 still doesn't cover”; and 7+, if they exist at all, shouldn't be new Effects (there simply aren't enough of those to spread out over two levels let alone four or five). Instead, have archsphere ranks start providing ways to make successes provide more bang for the buck. Prism of Focus has a chapter suggesting some options in this regard.

                As well, one you start getting into the Archsphere levels, the difficulties start getting absurd: a rank 6 effect has Difficulty 9 when it's Coincidental; Vulgar is either Difficulty 10 (as in, the number of dice you roll doesn't matter; it's always a 50/50 crapshoot) or Difficulty 9 with an added success needed. By the time you get up to Rank 9, even taking the maximum difficulty reduction (–3) isn't enough to reduce a Coincidental effect below a Difficulty of 9. For this reason as well, I don't think there should be Effects that require more than 6 dots in a Sphere; and even that's pushing it.

                So more than 6 ranks shouldn't unlock more Effects; it should just let you do better with the Effects you can perform — which, at that point, could be all of them.

                                                Matter in M20
                                                Matter has a rather funky division that constantly trips me up. For Forces and Life, altering simple substances starts at rank 2, while transmutation requires rank 3. For Matter this is almost the reverse. Transmutation of simple matter starts at rank 2, but a mage can’t alter many properties of matter until rank 3. I see some game balance logic to this, but that doesn’t make it less tricky.

                                                415 –A Life 3/Matter 3/Entropy 1 that explicitly uses Entropy to spot weak points. Great. Life and Matter make the spell damaging to vampires and Life makes it Agg damage. However, it is explicitly Life 3/Matter 2 to damage vampires with Life. Also, Entropy isn’t accomplishing anything mechanical here.

                                                416 – Matter can turn clothing into light armor. No rank is given. I think it would be Matter 3. This is confirmed in the trinkets section.

                                                418 – Life and Matter don’t affect spirits unless they take on a material form.

                                                422 (Thunder Punch Sidebar) – The Matter 2 variant is to damage materials.

                                                437 – A mage who understands demolitions might be able to shape and direct explosions with Matter or Forces. Assuming that the Matter version means fashioning the explosive to affect the direction that it will explode in, this is plausible.

                                                438 (Dodging the Blast sidebar)– Matter is one of the spheres that can be used for the optional Dodging the Blast rule.

                                                441 – Using the optional structure and durability rules, Matter is required to magically repair structure.

                                                440 – If using the optional Durability and Structure rules, Matter attacks reduce object durability by -2, and Matter augmented transmutation spells reduce it by -2 per success for the purposes of overcoming Durability.

                                                440 – Forces 2/Matter 2 to make an attack on a pillar. Forces for the attack, Matter to reduce its durability (Optional Rule)

                                                443 (Awakening Substances Sidebar) – Same issue as it was with Life. It’s Matter 2 to enhance a substance, or Life 3/Matter 2 to enhance it if it is in someone. Unless it is part of their pattern that doesn’t make a whole lot of sense. I will admit, it is much easier to think of an ingested substance being part of their pattern, though.

                                                464 – Matter 2 or 3 can be used to make enhanced materials. This isn’t wrong, per se, but it needs explanation. Matter 2 can perfect simple matter by transmutation and Matter 3 by changing its form in optimal ways. Matter 4 is required to perfect complex matter, though, and Matter 5 is required for impossible materials.                                                         I think I have some idea about why people make funny faces when talking about Entropy, and it is the whole "Death" stuff. Which is the most asinine thing that unfortunately MtA brought through.

But sadly, this silly concept have being ingrained in it for whatever reason, to the point that they created the "Death Sphere" in Awakening, which in my opinion is the most stupid idea ever.

                                                        Entropy deals with chaotic systems. In physics, chaotic systems are not "random"; they are deterministic. What "chaos" means is our incapability to make accurate predictions in long spams of time.

                                                        For example, if I throw a dice or flip a coin, the result is NOT random. It is a measure of the applied force, angular momentum, gravity, air resistance and so on.

                                                        If we knew all of the variables involved, we could calculate the results EVERY SINGLE TIME.

                                                        Likewise, things like the weather have the same characteristics. If we knew the movements of every single particle of air, of every single particle of water, of every single photon emitted by the Sun, then we could make weather predictions with exact precision forever.

                                                        But that's NOT the case. We can't possibly know all of those.

                                                        Therefore, for any type of system, there's an upper limit of time upon which predictions have trustworthy results. For the weather for example, that goes for a week; for the movement of galaxies, that goes up to 1 million years.

                                                        After that, new data and new measurements must be acquired and calculated for new predictions to be reliable.

                                                        This is what chaos means. It's our incapability to have all the relevant data about any system - and that's not a matter of equipment precision or better computers, but rather that it is IMPOSSIBLE to have all those variables (in fact, you would need to know the position and momentum of every single particle in the universe).

                                                        THAT'S what Entropy controls. It control all of those tiny variables that affect any system.

                                                        The beauty of it is that, since those are Deterministic systems, you can just as easily call it DESTINY or FATE.

                                                        Thus, Entropy 1 allows you to see those hidden connections between apparently disconnected variables

                                                        Entropy 2 is the alteration of variables on a small scale... What does it make to have a dice go 1 or 6? Small changes of angular momentum and applied force could make all the difference. Or the difference of a bullet hitting or missing its mark? One tiny millimeter of difference during the gun's recoil, or some tiny variations of air resistance could be all that it takes.

                                                        For Entropy 3, let's make a thought experiment. Have you ever dropped a glass cup, but it didn't break? Sure, most of the times it's the announcement of a bad day, but on rare occasions, nothing happens.
                                                        But why does that happen?
                                                        "Luck" some would say.
                                                        In terms of physics, it relates to the strength of the impact, angular movement, and perhaps you were just "luck" that the cup fell in a stronger point.
                                                        Well, Entropy 3 deals with all of those variables in a matter pattern. Sure, when we see a Mage turning a castle to dust with it, we can't understand this and that's what gives us the impression that Entropy is just an "amalgamation of weird random "death and destruction" powers". But that has nothing to do with it.
                                                        Entropy 3 deals with the natural processes inside of matter patterns, with all the tiny hidden variables within it.

                                                        For instance: how long does it take to make an iron gate to get rusty?
                                                        And what is rust to begin with?

                                                        Well, rust turns out to be nothing more than Iron Oxide. That is, atoms of Iron combining with atoms of Oxygen in the air.

                                                        That process occurs in a "random" (meaning: chaotic, which is, deterministic but undeterminable, as in, we have no way to measure with precision). Water molecules in the air can also speed up that process.

                                                        Thus, turning Iron to Rust is a NATURAL process that occurs over time.

                                                        What Entropy 3 does is to speed up or slow down those variables; nothing more and nothing less.

                                                        On a molecular level, if we could see the results, we would see the oxygen molecules in the air that are constantly moving and due to that, sometimes combine with the iron atoms forming rust due to "random" motions, we would see that sequence of interactions being accelerated, and the "randomness" of the process being strongly tilted towards forming more (or less) rust than should be expected.

                                                        Of course, when a Mage throws a very vulgar effect that disintegrates a sword in an instant, we immediately just think about "death magic", but that's NOT the case AT ALL.

                                                        In such a vulgar display of Magik, the Mage is simply accelerating this process towards supernatural speeds, in a way that should NOT be possible.

                                                        But hey, that's exactly why such use of Entropy would be highly VULGAR, because it goes above any possible means of a natural occurrence; nevertheless, the process is the SAME as the natural process, just with the randomness accelerated and direct into unnaturally impossible outcomes (hence why it is "Magik", vulgar).

                                                        Now, living beings are directed by the same "random" mechanisms are solid matter, with the difference being that living beings are massively complex biochemical systems with constant chemical interactions. But be sure, all those "random" processes occur inside our bodies and, in fact, our bodies have evolved to capitalize on them.

                                                        For example, our hemoglobins don't just "conjure" oxygen. The molecules of oxygen that enter our lungs are transported RANDOMLY inside of our blood. But the molecules of hemoglobin have a high affinity towards oxygen, therefore, when a molecule of oxygen randomly approaches the hemoglobin, it has a HIGH PROBABILITY (but NOT CERTAINTY) to combine with it.

                                                        Given the trillions of molecules of hemoglobin that we have travelling randomly inside of our blood (inside of the red cells of the blood), that means that the probability of combination between oxygen and hemoglobin is high enough, given the vast amounts that we have, that it is sufficient to transport the necessary oxygen that we need to survive throughout our body.

                                                        Well, we could consider that to be a result of several previous experiences that person has throughout its entire life.
