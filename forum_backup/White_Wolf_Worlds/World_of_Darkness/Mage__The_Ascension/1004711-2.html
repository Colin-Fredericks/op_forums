            Originally posted by visigoth View Post

            These attempts only happen because nephandi are cohesive, organized, have an hierarchy and are apparently rational actors in the ascension war. Thus I as a player am baffled by the lack of rational justifications or reasons for their objectives. What they see in the cauls that lead them to act like they act? What they learnt from oblivion demons that they ended like this?
            School shooters, serial killers, rapists are lonely people with mental illness, not an organization with an agenda. Mafia, criminal organizations, militant groups are "evil" or "wrong" as long as they are marginalized, excluded or working illegally. Nephandi are none of these things.
            There's an odd bit of reasoning that usually crops up where the Nephandi are concerned. We know they have a coherent ideology because they are so good at working together. We know they are cohesive and well organized because they are so good at meeting their goals. And we know they have coherent goals because they spring from a common ideology. I'm simplifying to underscore the circular reasoning, of course, but my point is we often ascribe more rationality and capability to the Nephandi than they deserve.

            The heart of the problem is this assumption that the Nephandi have intricate and well thought out goals. I don't think they do. Instead, they have very broad and general goals, like causing pain, destabilization, corruption, and despair. Those goals are easy to meet; the Nephandi could spend more time fighting each other than the rest of the world and they'd still be able to say they achieved something. Since others of them can use that pain, destabilization, corruption, or despair to power their own works, it might even be a valid claim. It's not a complicated plan though, and it doesn't require much organization.

                            I agree with Ramnesis and Saikou. The Intricacy of a plan isn't necessarily relevant to how well it goes off (indeed, there's something to be said of having too many moving parts, because it creates more places where the mechanism could fail). Even a single layer of obfuscation is enough to cause damage, which is one of the main things Nephandi as a group work for.

                            Send a package in the mail to a random person who is down on their luck. It contains a stack of money and instructions to go to a certain house in the city and leave a note enclosed in the package. With the cash (and maybe a little Mind enchantment on the note to nudge the person towards compliance), the patsy is told that they can continue receiving rewards for further "errands". The stooge has no idea they are helping facilitate a campaign of psychological warfare against someone by doing their seemingly innocuous tasks. Psychological warfare that could be working to isolate a Mage from their fellow Awakened, or making a Technocrat agent doubt their sanity, or make a common office worker have a nervous breakdown (which may or may not result in them taking a shotgun to work one day). If the patsy just so happens to already be a drug addict, who suddenly has a steady supply of cash to feed their habit for very little work, all's the better as far as their dark manipulator is concerned. They can do whatever it is they want to do, and further ruin a poor bystander's life in the process.

                            The best part being that, even if the target somehow tracks things back to the patsy, the latter has no idea who they work for. Not even Mind or Time magick helps illuminate the issue, since the Nephandus was no where near their puppet. If the Nephandus is unnecessarily paranoid, they could create a second layer of obfuscation by having all the letters be copies transcribed verbatum by a person who doesn't understand the language, and who also never met their "client". But, again, this isn't really necessary.

                            And this is when you're getting a little complicated. I definitely overthought that plan. A Nephandus could get the same effect by arranging for a mental patient to escape confinement, and appear to them as a figure from their delusions, telling them to kill the whores or whatever. Most Mages who even bother to investigate a random serial killer would need to make a huge leap in logic to assume the guy is being manipulated, instead of just obeying the voices in their head.
