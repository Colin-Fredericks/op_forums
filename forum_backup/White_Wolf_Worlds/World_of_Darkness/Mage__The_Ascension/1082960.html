          Not great, but not in the way that most folks are going to comment, I'd wager.

          Consider Czar Vargo as a model of what the pre-split Etherites are like. He's well-meaning, adventurous, courageous, but also small-t-technocratic. When Vargo liberated the world, his ultimatum was not that the people would be free to choose their own way of life - it was that the governments of the world would surrender the reigns of power to Vargo and to "scientific" control. Vargo takes it for granted that those that possess the appropriate philosophical and educational qualifications will be able to "solve" governance, like it's a math problem. This strand of high modernism is part of the thematic underpinning of the Union, but it's clearly present in the early Etherites too - and the things that temper that thread are born of lessons learned when they were on the outside as a Tradition. Lessons they would not have to learn, if they were a dominant Convention. Look at 50s and 60s sci-fi. Not for the cackling mad scientists - there may be a few, but it's not important - but for the scene where the lantern-jawed white guy scientist is put in charge of a military formation, or negotiates with an alien race on Earth's behalf.

          Globally, lots of technocratic communism and isolationist "Third Way" fascism. Ether-based utopian technology empowers these societies to provide for their needs, but the Etherites in this time line never get an anti-authoritarian streak, so while they may disapprove of Etheric North Korea (a militarized dictatorship whose borders are sealed by impenetrable Etheric Shields), they don't have any Utopians agitating to do something about it. The statist Etherites smother the anarchist Difference Engineers in the crib. Without a push for a unified globe, there's no global internet, but a series of national "telenetworks", which means no "digital frontier" for the Virtual Adepts.

          The technocratic influence means that every major government devotes a lot of funding to scientific research towards practical ends - super-crops, fuels, etc. - and that these "science ministries" have a lot of social and political clout, something akin to a state religion. The "Etheric N.W.O" convention is made up of and influences these "scientific advisors" instead of controlling faceless bureaucracies or top-secret intelligence agencies.

          Space stations and bases on the moon and mars. Space remains a nationalist and militarized endeavor, so the "etheric void engineers" are a secret conspiracy within various national space programs. They trade information and coordinate, but they don't share resources - there are Russian voidships with Russian crews, and American ships, and sometimes they work together to deal with an alien threat, but with gritted teeth. There's also less "esprit de corps" and a greater focus on the individual - modernism deified the astronaut as the soldier-scientist-explorer, so the "ether marines" are composed of a bunch of people that each see themselves as the hero of their own movie.

          ItX and the Progenitors don't change much, but they no longer have a Syndicate that determines which projects to fund or a global capitalist economy to internationalize R&D and manufacturing, they're much more embedded into their surroundings - they have to make a case to their local amalgam why some Primal resources ought to be dedicated to their project, and that local amalgam is probably made up of people from the same country. So it's less "why does the Union want to fund this" and more "why should we divert Primal resources from Russia to pay for your project, comrade?"

          The Etherites don't have an ethics board until the 40s, and don't start to acknowledge the contributions of women and minorities until the 60s. This Union isn't mustache-twirling about it, but there's almost certainly a glass ceiling - the regional board that coordinates Union efforts in a given nation is upper-class, it's male. It's probably white, and where it isn't, it's made up of the dominant ethnic group. Ethical oversight is slapdash and ad hoc; nobody is Conditioned, and the sort of experiments that would get you executed in Nation A may be overlooked in Nation B.

          tl;dr: Instead of one Technocratic Union, you have the Technocratic Society of [America, Russia, etc.], and the Societies work together to ensure that Science and Reason guide the world, but don't have any globe-spanning agenda - each Society may resent or work against the others. No girls allowed.

              There are a few elements of Technocratic Etherites that I think would rise to the forefront.

              1) Most research would be driven by Individual scientists - The Etherites are remnants of a time when a lot of scientific research came from wealthy individuals rather than grand industrial cooperation. That's an element the Technocracy phased out after they left, to the point that Jobs and Wozniak starting a tech giant in their garage is the exception rather than the rule.

              2) Scientific study would be more elegant - I think the idea of Science as a form of personal expression happened after the Etherites split from their former allies, but they've always preferred theories that are elegant and imaginative over those that are expedient. Indeed that was part of what led to their punishment. In an Etherite led Technocracy clinical and unemotional science would take a back seat to dramatic and compelling science..

              3) The Technocracy would be even more authoritarian than it is now - The SoE embodies a lot of classic science fiction idealism, which includes things like scientific idealism and fascist utopias. Having to rebel against the authority of the rest of the technocracy changed that for them, as did their time with the Traditions, and even then one of their most famous members tried to take over the entire world to impose his ideas of peace. I suspect that if they'd never had to confront the possibility of a flawed scientific hegemony they'd be much, much worse.

                  If we believe in their book, one of the big things Sons of Ether wanted to disclose to the world, against the wishes of the Syndicate, was "free energy", that added with the rejection of ether led to the formation of the Etherites. This two facts make for a case that an Union leaded by the Ether it's going to be less focused with a global agenda and more tolerant with individual Masters that want to push their agendas (but not necesarily with agents of the lower levels). The Technocracy it's going to be more elitist, and focused in big, bold, leaders and heros that will make the politic.
                  Oh, and there may be "free energy" in some places of the world and there's no "outer space devoid of life" and more "etherspace where people can breath".

                  However, if the Sons of Ether have had one characteristic even before leaving the Union, it's their flamboyance. This would mean Paradox. We would have a Union much less scared of Paradox.

                  The Traditions would have to fear bolder attacks of an enemy that doesn't care so much for the consequences (if this would play better for the Union or the Traditions it's to be seen).

                  Technological advancement would be more unpredictable, more prone to failure, as the Etherites push to advance the Time Table a lot faster than the NWO. While this may deteriorate the trust in bleeding edge researches and stuff, it will also increase the technological advancement. Depending on how well this it's handled it may erode the exeptiscism in utopical science, or weaken the belief in science. Think on physic gabinets, scientists disclosing their (not the, but their) late milacre of science, sometimes it may not work, but those who do will generate a lot of prestigee for the genius behind the product.

                  We would be exploring a space with aliens where people can breath! We will live in a hollow earth!. Of course, the might decide to not disclose all this data, to not scare people with the dangerous beings that live out there. However, I would say that, at least, they will try to disclose the existence of the less dangerous aliens, to prepare people for the inevitable colonization of the etherspace (that, again, they will try to push sooner than latter).

                  Dependant on how people reacts to all of this and how Paradox reacts to all of this, we may live in a much more fantastical world, or not, but surely it would be more unpredictable than it already is. At the very least, I imagine a world where people worship geniuses. Science and corporations would be less of a stable development of the "system" and more the product of great people. It would be a more elitist society, in that regards, with (even) less patience for "the incompetent and the lazy".
                  Also, we may live in a world less interested in products or useful tools, and more in theory and knowledge. This may sound nice and dandy, but it also may mean that you wouldn't be able to buy a computer and expect it to be "intuitive" (that's for the lazy, wanna use MY new and original computer? learn the theory behind it).

It's not so much that the SoE influenced the Union as the whole Union becomes a set of Conventions that break out and more fully develop some of the SoE tropes.

                      There are a lot of ways this can go, but a lot of it depends on the starting parameters.

                              Assuming a greater focus on individual scientists and scientific pursuit and a greater focus on utopian ideals and authoritarianism, this is what I see happening to the other Conventions.

                              Iteration X is greatly changed. There was always a lot of overlap between them, the Electrodyne Engineers, and the Difference Engineers. That was fine when the other two were minor Conventions, but with the Etherites in ascendancy they don't have much of a place. They are still one half of the Military Industrial Complex, but with less ability to predict the next revolutionary leap that Complex is diminished. I see them becoming an applied science division. Someone needs to translate the diverse products of the rest of the Technocracy into reliable and trustworthy devices. They might keep being the robotic faction, though.

                              The Progenitors are hit in a similar way, but they weather it better. Partly because making things for sleepers is what they already do and partly because the need for medicine and other treatments is a constant. Their techniques might be different but their focus is largely the same. On the other hand, the Progenitors have always straddled the ethics issue, being both the conscience of the Technocracy and the Convention most tempted to cross the line. The ideals of the Etherites are going to put that struggle into stark relief.

                              The Void Engineers will thrive under the Etherites (or be subsumed into them). The independent scientist and the independent explorer are two sides of the same coin so they were natural bedfellows. The VEs will still have much of the same focus they already do. Although perhaps they will have a greater emphasis on sanitizing the new frontiers rather than defining them.

                              The Syndicate, as the other half of the Military Industrial Complex don't enjoy quite the same power. They're the ones that have to tell everyone else no, and that is harder to do in an Etherite world. Not to mention that the Etherite dream of free power, while it fits into Syndicate ideals in the long term, severely cuts into their ability to manipulate society in the short term.

                              The NWO though, they're doing really well. Someone needs to be there to turn those utopian ideals into a coherent and stable government and the NWO more than willing to do the job. While they do lose quite a bit of direct control, they end us as all but equal partners with the Etherites.

                              The Difference Engineers will rise and fall and rebel. Computers are a cool new technology but their utility is deeply tied to the vast infrastructure the classic Technocracy pushed forward. There will eventually be an internet, but its probably going to come more slowly, and computers may be a very different thing by that point. More than any other faction they need reliability and predictability and they will be rebuffed when they ask for it. So, the way I see it, while everyone else is focused on what can be done, the Difference Engineers and ItX will be threading applied technology with standard practices that eveyone can understand. When the time is right and their protocols are too deeply entrenched to uproot, a big chunk of them will jump ship.
