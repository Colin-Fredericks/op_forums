                        It's an M20 supplement. That means that it makes an effort to support whatever style of play the group wants. From TR:
                        although many features of Technocracy: Reloaded remain familiar to longtime Mage fans, we've approached this book and its material with a broader spectrum than previous books have provided. Mage 20's emphasis on Technocratic player-character options informs every aspect of this book, which provides material for players at Storytellers alike. Within that insider framework, many elements have been left deliberately ambiguous, with the final decision being left to each individual Storyteller. Depending on that Storyteller's desires, then, the current Technocracy may be:

                        • A fine idea gone horribly wrong.
                        • An archaic empire crumbling under its own obsolescence.
                        • A flawed but necessary bulwark against a world filled with monsters.
                        • A monstrous paranormal Empire with genocide all tendencies and great PR.
                        • An implacable engine of global domination where in a few idealists struggle with in a totalitarian system.
                        • An icon of paranoia where in a wrong move or word might lead to Fates far worse than death.
                        • A vast, corrupt conspiracy in which good principles justify horrendous acts.
                        • An extremist Juggernaut at war within itself.
                        • A Titanic pawn in a Nephandic endgame.
                        • The last, best hope for human survival.

                        These possibilities are not, of course, mutually exclusive.
                        So no, it's not going to stick to the Union being an unalloyed evil; though there's plenty in the book that you can use to make that so if you so choose.

                            My own hopes for the M20 line is that it will last long enough for us to get books like Technocracy: Reloaded for the Traditions and for the Disparates (“Traditions Reborn” and “Disparates Unbound”, maybe?), and a book like Book of the Fallen for the Marauders (for historical reasons, “Book of Madness, M20 Edition”).

                            The difference being that TR is designed with the notion that the game's protagonists may be from the faction under discussion, and so is sympathetic with their worldview¹ and doubles as a sort of Player's Guide; whereas Book of the Fallen is designed as a sort of “Black Dog” game where the faction under discussion is in no way intended to be player characters and is mostly there to facilitate a discussion about a disturbing topic (“abuse” in the case of the Nephandi; “mental illness” in the case of the Marauders) and to equip not just the player characters but also the players to know what they're looking at when they see it and to deal with it appropriately².


                            ¹ Which is not to say that their worldview is embraced wholeheartedly; I fully expect that if we get an equivalent book for the Traditions, their flaws and failings will receive plenty of attention, too.

                            ² And the first thing that I'd want the hypothetical M20 Book of Madness to address is “what is a mental illness, vs. what is merely a mind that works differently than the societal norm?”
