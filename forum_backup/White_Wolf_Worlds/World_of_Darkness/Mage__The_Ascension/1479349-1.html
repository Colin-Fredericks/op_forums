        I tend to adopt the same ideas of structural but also 'human/demographic' changes being equally factors, but those are honestly arbitrary boundaries and shaped by my preference to tie everything together even though various ST settings don't *quite* fit perfectly.

        'Structural' basically means the way the world (or cosmos) is set up. One major 'structural' factor I think is that many systems treat the cosmos (including time) as cyclical. KOTE was the biggest influence here, but I think ti can apply to others (since there were times Gehenna was *also* cyclical). On a cycle you're going to have different changes to the environment and that will include in the ecosystem. Energy may change in many ways but you'll always have a high point, a low point, and periods in between. In an ideal system this might continue indefinitely (or just a very long time given entropy) But many settings (and here I borrow more from DtF, CtD, WtA and not just KOTE) have the world corrupted or fundamentally damaged in some way. It's a world that isn't perfect and it probably doesn't renew itself in the way originally intended (either entropy became a thing, or it accelerated because of the imperfections.)

        Under that assumption, each 'cycle' could mean the energy available to the system decreases (maybe it gets stored someplace it wasn't intended, and it causes further problematic effects. There are lots of ways you could reflect this - the problems with the underworld, creating factors that disrupted the Triat, etc.) and the ecosystem will likewise start to change. Eventually you might get certain magical flora, fauna or resources that get consumed or never replenish or may go extinct because they fail to adapt to changing circumstances (and become bygones). Some areas of the world/cosmos will be less affected by this than others obviously so you can have areas that more magical than others (ie Horizon, or various places on Earth that become nodes, caerns, etc. Another possible side effect of this is that 'pure' quintessence may get bound up in forms that are less easily accessible or renewable (matter, or even just different kinds of energy. Chi, Mana, Gnosis, etc.)

        An imperfect environment might be bad enough by itself, but human nature could likely make it worse (kinda like real life - collectively humans don't do well planning long term, we don't always conserve or adopt sustainable practices when we should, we easily ignore subtle and gradual changes and resist making long term, systemic changes that can require effort. WoD humans are likely to be *worse* rather than better.) Consensus plays a big role here, but human habits and those of our offshoots (magical or otherwise) also play a role. More people, the crystallization of belief in certain ways (that might be toxic to other things - wonder, awe, creativity, etc.) and accelerate a pre-existing downward-spiral Vampires even be a non-trivial 'human' factor - humans played a role in the creation of vampires, and vampires are one of the few supernatural entities (apart from the dead or zombies) who might be said to have been prolfiic to any degree. Nevermind the worm, demons, the Akuma, etc. - Vampires probably can be both the most mundane of supernatural threats and the serious in terms of binding up dwindling reserves of supernatural power in less-usable forms. Consensus by itself might not completely kill magic, but Consensus (as opposed by the Technocratic Union) PLUS a deficit in magical power in a region could probably do it for at least some kinds of supernatural entities. It's also a sort of setup why magic could be problematic in a number of ways even if a non trivial percentage of humans believe in magic (its still gonna be hard to do much in the way of magic if there's no magical energy to fuel it.)

        One reason I like this view is that it can incorporate a wide range of causative factors without any one necessarily being decisive (even the ones I described). Or if someone wants certain causative factors to be more decisive it can be adjusted in various ways (Even removing ideas you like.) The broad idea is that the environment was intended to be sustainable and for whatever reasons gradually became unsustainable, and the impact of humans on it (again in various ways) made the situation worse. It's the sort of thing that can bring you to some mega-crisis point (the various settings apocalyptic 'endings' - even if you buy into a cyclic view, some 'endings' could be worse than others, with a failing system you could face a single, catastrophic ending.) And since V20 and V5 were a thing I adapted that thinking to the idea that the 'mega-crisis' was survived (or is still ongoing in some form) but it did not prove to be an *end* (at least not yet. It could be a delayed outcome, or perhaps some of the causative factors were mended or altered, or something else came into play.) - the 'cycle' may still be ongoing and things might be terrible but the world didn't come to an end. Things might even have evolved or changed (a possibility left in many of the various 'end game' scearnios which can also play into changes between the original revised and later iterations that got evolved)

                        Yep. Just to clarify: I'm not arguing that the Consensus doesn't matter, or that the Consensus can't change Reality Zones. The whole reason I came up with the idea of “soft” and “hard” RZs was to facilitate the Storyteller being able to fine-tune the relationship he wants the Consensus to have with reality — possibly even handling it differently in different parts of the same setting. Want to emphasize the role that the Consensus has in shaping reality? Feature a “soft” RZ where everything must conform to the Consensus. Want to emphasize that we have to deal with reality as it is, instead of how we wish it would be? Feature a “hard” RZ where the Consensus has a comparatively small impact. You might even frame the Ascension War itself at least partially around this notion, with the Technocracy mostly favoring the hardening of RZs (after shaping them to their liking, of course) while the Traditions prefer to “soften” RZs.

                        Nor am I saying that there can't be global influences where the Consensus is concerned — though I see that as more of an ideal being worked toward rather than an established fact. And ironically, the group that has done the most to promote a global Consensus isn't the Technocracy, but a Tradition: the Virtual Adepts, who pioneered the Internet and broke down the barriers of space that used to isolate RZs from each other. But despite RZs not officially being a thing prior to M20, I would say that they've always being implicitly there.

                        As for the nature of the Consensus: I agree with the notion that it's the collective result of Avatar-empowered beliefs — meaning that humans contribute to it while dogs and cats don't; but also meaning that individual Sleepers don't influence reality nearly as much as individual Awakened do. I generally don't consider the Awakened to be a significant part of the Consensus, because their ability to alter reality is mostly represented by the Magick rules; and when they're not willworking in a way that's best handled through said rules, they're no more influential than a Sleeper. In short, I tend to view the Consensus as the influence of human Avatars, ignoring the deliberate activities of the Awakened. Otherwise, a mage could never “go against the Consensus” because the “Consensus” would be whatever he's trying to do.

                                    The WOD seems based on Tolkien-like ideas about the world becoming more mundane over time, and Neverending Story ideas about the slow death of imagination. They came up with the whole consensus thing in an attempt to justify it, but it never really fit as an explanation. If the world is based on consensus, then we'd have never left the world of caveman folklore, and placating spirits would be as mundane a skill as engineering and medicine in the real world. The original writers seemed to work backwards from their conclusion, first deciding that their universe was a place of increasing mundanity, then deciding that it was science's fault, which then lead to scientists being The Man. It's the sort of chain of thought that makes sense when you are writing fantasy like LOTR, but which doesn't hold up to fridge-logic. Especially since the most tyrannical forces in our world are very much against the scientific method.

                                    Ultimately the WOD is very meta. Science didn't destroy magick, but it did weaken magical fantasy stories. You don't see a lot of fiction these days things are as wild as they were in the oldest folklore. Even Harry Potter type stories are nowhere near as crazy as the oldest folklore myths.

                                    That's the thing about the WOD's design: on some level it never forgets that these are fantasy stories, and that it's telling a story about how fantasy stories have become more mundane over time, and barely pretending that this is about a universe where reality has become more mundane over time. Much of the WOD only works in this very meta/allegorical way.

                                    So effectively, those old wizards were weakened by something akin to The Nothing from the Neverending Story, because those wizards were just fantasy characters, and changing culture in the real world made their particular set of fantasy tropes change.

                                                        Originally posted by Prometheas View Post

                                                        That's literally what Earthly Foundations (M20 pp.612–613) are for: they define certain things that are Vulgar “no matter who's doing them, where they're doing them, or how they're being done. In game terms, acts that violate these Earthly Foundations contradict the baseline reality for Earth.” What I've been saying on the matter has involved coming up with some better ideas as to just what the Earthly Foundations are, and to provide in-game metaphysical means for softening them and bringing some dynamism to something that, at least as presented in the core book, is very, very static.

                                                        In the book, Earthly Foundations are what they are; and there's no indication that they can ever change. I've been suggesting that they can change; but in order to be Earthly Foundations, the things that can change them cannot be the things that shift Reality Zones: again, M20 indicates that you can shift RZs and provides guidelines on how to do it; but it implies that EFs are an exception: if you could shift an EF simply by altering the Consensus, they wouldn't be defining things as Vulgar regardless of who, where, or how.

                                                        It's quite possible that even saying that Terranorming can shift EFs is going too far, and that the only things that can shift EFs are archmagi and other godlike entities. Even the Technocracy's massive success in spreading Technocratic Reality around the globe isn't officially presented as them affecting the EFs in any way; at most, it can be read as them aligning their Enlightened Science with the EFs. I'm not personally a fan of being that strict; but it is a valid reading of M20; and to the extent that there is a “correct” reading of it, I think that that's it.

                                                        It's because I'm not a fan of being that strict that I've been thinking about options such as there being ways to soften or harden them, with the hardness “setting” determining how much of a given RZ's nature is defined by the EFs vs how much is defined by the Consensus. If you don't buy that the Consensus can give rise to Paradox (because there isn't sufficient opposition to magic in the Consensus to justify that), then you'll want the hardness turned up so that the EFs can be the source of Paradox instead.

                                                        Originally posted by Prometheas View Post

                                                        No. The only thing that can make Awakened magic impossible is deliberate interference by other supernatural powers (usually Awakened Magick; but anyone who can perform countermagic will do), or some aspects of Earthly Foundations — though that's very rare, and tends to be more about such things as “the process of how magic works”*. Sleeper Avatars are weak enough that even when aggregated, they're not powerful enough to block an Arete 1 mage; they can only attach consequences to his actions.

                                                        There are some things that even the combined might of billions of Sleepers worldwide can't do, because it's not just a matter of sheer force. Awakened Magick isn't just a matter of Will; it's a matter of Enlightened Will. That Enlightenment (or Arete, in the case of non-technos) isn't about raw power alone. For raw power, I'd rather look at the Avatar Background. Rather, Arete/Enlightenment is more like “finesse”: more like Dexterity than Strength, to give the physical attributes as an analogy.
