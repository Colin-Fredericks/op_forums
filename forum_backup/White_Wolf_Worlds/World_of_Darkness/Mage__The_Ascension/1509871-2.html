    Originally posted by Ragged Robin View Post

    The verbena have also been rather vaguely defined as the nature/pagany mages the trouble us they're genrally quite vaguely written in terms of what they believe and typically quire disconnected with pagan traditions and concepts

    Order of hermes revised has a more positive spin on.thr order. For some reason previous editions is obsessed with them being complete bastards. If your wizard classic order is all saruman the white then this is going to leave you frustrated if you're trying to play gandalf or Harry Potter.
    During an interview with the writers for Lore of the Traditions, it was mentioned that one of the instructions they were given was “if there's anything about your Tradition that doesn't make sense, go ahead and blame the Order of Hermes for it.” Tellingly, I think this was mentioned in conjunction with the writer of the Verbena chapter; so I get to address two of your points at once.

    My own preference is to just downplay the Traditions so that it doesn't matter as much if their portrayals are good or bad; because the main focus would be on the personal struggles of individual mages instead. And frankly, that's how I've tended to play the game from the get-go, even back in the days when the assumption was that the protagonists are all part of the Traditions: I took the decentralized nature of the Traditions as a basis for saying that the protagonists are, in practice, on their own even if they were nominally members of an organization.

    Rather than getting a 5e revision to the Verbena to make them more defined and to connect them more with pagan traditions and concepts, I'd much rather get something like the Paradigm Explored series of Storyteller's Vault products (currently at three books: Wyrd and Weft, Number and Shape, and Fairy-Faith) that focuses on giving some insight on just what those pagan traditions and concepts are, in a way that will enable me to make a believable witch. Similarly with the Order of Hermes: if you downplay the importance of the Traditions in general, and of Traditions politics in particular, them all you really need is a Paradigm Explored for Hermetic views on magick. You want to play Gandalf or Harry Potter? Go for it! All you have to do is not insist that they belong to the Order of Hermes or partake in whatever shenanigans it may or may not be involved in.

    That is, stop treating “Wizard” as a synonym for “Order of Hermes”, and “Witch” as a synonym for “Verbena”. Keep a few references in there for those who want to use the Traditions; but make it something optional. For instance, Paradigm Explored: Number and Shape has a section in it about what Tradition or Convention the sample character might belong to; and VA is near the top of the list. But so are NWO and ItX, as well as Order of Hermes and a few others (Celestial Chorus, Syndicate, and Hollow Ones). And there's nothing saying that the character has to belong to any of them.

                        I took a quick skim through the main nine Spheres writeups in M20 and noted every place that a Sphere suggests the need for a pattern Sphere. These seem to come in five flavors:

                        1) The spell is a transmutation or grafting so both spheres are primary

                        2) The pattern sphere is a locking sphere

                        3) The pattern sphere is a technically optional but functionally necessary modifier to the main spell.

                        4) The pattern sphere is actually the main effect which is being modified

                        5) The pattern sphere is being used to target
                        ​
                        1 and 2 are clearly part of the rules. 4 is as well, just from a different direction. 3 is not specifically covered, but is a logical extension.

                        The largest chunk of sphere addons, though, comes from arbitrary targeting requirements that aren't in the rules. Instead they seem to be based on the assumption of a rule that sounds like it would exist but doesn't and doesn't have any reason to. Targeting is an extremely iffy reason to add a sphere and I would drop virtually all examples of it.

                        I'd also take a careful look at all examples of 4. Two of the four of them are nonsense. If Telekinesis were listed in Forces it would be easier to see that Mind isn't doing anything in the spell. Because it is listed in Mind we can easily miss that.

                                        That was ended in M20, by downplaying to irrelevance the idea that there's any connection between Traditions and Spheres. They don't flat-out say that the are no Seats of Matter, Entropy, Forces, etc.; but they don't mention such things, either.

                                        As for the first part: as I said before, I don't have strong opinions about reworking the Traditions, because I'd prefer that M5 take what M20 started in removing the Traditions as the focus of the game and run with it. In M5 as I imagine it, you don't play a member of the Traditions by default; you do so because you have decided that you want your mage to partake of the culture that defines the Traditions. And that culture is one part “can't we all just get along?” and one part “the purpose of a mage is to grow beyond her limits and Ascend”.

                                        That is, rather than existing to provide a set of archetypal approaches to Magick, the M5 Traditions would exist solely to facilitate people who want to play Mage the way it was played in the first three editions; if you want to get away from that sort of thing, just don't have your mage align herself with the Traditions. More generally, make membership in an arcane Society an opt-in thing rather than an opt-out thing. You don't play a Hermetic; you play a wizard. And if you choose to have your wizard join the Order of Hermes, that's an option you can take. But you don't have to; and it's not assumed that you will.
