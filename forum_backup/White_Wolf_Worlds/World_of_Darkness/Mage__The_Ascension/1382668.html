    I don't understand the purpose of M20 nerfing acolyte contributions to rituals.

    04-19-2020, 11:27 PM
    Background on the issue: In 2nd and 3rd/Revised edition, the rules for "Acting in Concert" put forward that every 5 acolytes involved in an effect contribute an automatic success to the end result of the ritual. In M20, it's every 5 acolytes contributing a single die, and (here's the part I don't get) it's capped it at 5 additional dice.

    Let me say that there are plenty of rules in M20 that I disagree with; mostly the sort that, rather than clarifying a previously ambiguous/vague rule, or expanding upon a previously inadequately explained rule, instead just take a clear rule and change it for reasons. The two examples that immediately come to mind are the radical changes to how mind control magick works (what HDYDT calls "Uncanny Influence"); and the second that quickly comes to mind is the fact that potentially every supernatural in the game can now have some kind of countermagick.

    I don't play with these rules; some because I don't like or agree with them (not because they're objectively bad, so much as they're not to my taste), others because I really just can't--I've been running a game for the better part of a decade based mostly on Revised rules, so even if I wanted to incorporate every single rule, I couldn't. There are a handful of important things that have occurred in the plot that would become inexplicable if certain fundamental rules were to just change.

    Nevertheless, even where I disagree with the change, I at least understand the underlying reason for the change, most of which are obvious on their face. Regarding the two examples above, the reason it's very difficult to use Mind to control people without their consent is a product of Satyros Brucato's social politics; I think he subscribes to the school of thought that matters of personal consent and bodily autonomy have been disregarded by our society for far too long and that it would be deeply violating and wrong to invade someone's mind and force them to do things they don't want to do... and so if it's really difficult to do that in-game, players will discard it as a viable strategy, and he'll (Brucato) have the satisfaction of knowing that when people play the game he put his name on, using the rules he wrote, that it'll be nigh impossible for certain things to transpire. The whole 'giving nightfolk countermagick' is even simpler than that: they doesn't want mages pwning other supernatural with incomparable attacks that no one but another mage has an answer for. Again, I don't use these rules, but I understand the policy reason for why they were implemented in the first place.

    But this brings me to the rule at hand about Acolytes contributing to rituals. Switching it from automatic successes to dice doesn't make me feel one way or another--but capping it at a maximum benefit of five is inexplicable to me. I can't explain it with Brucato's politics, and I can't explain it with a balancing issue, because it's probably incredibly difficult to get even 100 or more people together so bought into and knowledgeable about your paradigm that they'd count as "Acolytes" for the purposes of a ritual. And if you've already accomplished that, then, the premise of whatever game you're playing probably relies on that kind of extraordinary occurrence having come to pass. But even from a metaphysical standpoint, the way magick is supposed to work in this world, it doesn't make sense to me that having 20 acolytes help on a ritual is numerically going to be the same as having 1,000 or 10,000. Like, what are we fixing here?

    This is one of those rules that I couldn't change even if I wanted to because there's Big-Time-Plot-Device-Setting-Stuff that happened in my game that's explained by the notion that the rulers of ancient empires lead by people holding themselves out as a gods (or high priests) would have been able to accomplish miraculous feats because of the scale of the fanatical following of their state religions. But I'd still like to understand the why here.

              Moving it to dice does two important things:

              First, the nerf is totally deserved as noted.

              Second, making it dice gives it a different bonus from apprentices and peers helping you, which is good for encouraging different approaches. Cult is no longer a must have for any seriously powerful mage (and as a late addition Background, it's something a lot of the NPCs don't have which makes them much less impressive if you have people that make full use of the team rules). The usefulness of dice also scales with the difficulty of the casting. If you're sitting pretty at Diff 3 for your casting, dice are almost as good as successes, as your cult can easily keep up with your magic. If you're up to a difficulty 9 threshold 2 spell... the magical intricacies and highly delicate working make it that much harder for the cult to help.

              point, though, they can help you only so much."

              are no other witnesses around to contradict that impression."
