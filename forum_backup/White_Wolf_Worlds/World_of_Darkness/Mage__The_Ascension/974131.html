    What is an Avatar?

    09-21-2016, 08:30 PM
    What exactly is a mages avatar? Mages(and to a lesser extent, mummies) themselves seem to believe that they're some fundamental part of the soul, but at the same time there are cases where avatars seem to be able to exist separate from the soul of the mage or even leave at will. For instance, if a mage becomes a vampire it is said their avatar moves on, but vampires themselves are shown to retain their souls. The winged deamon in dark ages is another example, it was apparently "thrown out" by her mage when he grew tired of her. Yet another is the flaw "Ban", in which one of the punishments for violating your character's ban was your avatar getting fed up and leaving your mage character(taking their true magic with them).

    Considering that avatars are also(supposedly) the source of a mages True Magick, does that mean that awakening isn't the mage seeing their own true potential, but instead being shown said potential by an outside entity that then "Moves In"? What about the consensus, is the reason for it because humans can't control their ability to manipulate reality without a helping hand?

    Also acknowledging that ascension is something that avatars are pushing their mage towards, is it truely the mage's ascention or the avatar's?

      All of these are excellent questions.

      Unfortunately (or Fortunately), this being Mage: The Ascension, not only are these questions mostly not answered, but the potential answers could all be true at the same time.

      Is the Avatar the same thing as the Soul? A different aspect of a multi-part Soul? A revered ancestor or Totem? A figment of one's subconscious? A fragment of the divine, seeking to push the Mage towards a reconciliation and merging with the Most High? Some sort of spirit that attaches itself to sapient beings, giving them the potential for True Magick in return for a vessel to hang out in? A guardian angel?

      Who knows? Mages of every stripe have certainly spent a considerable amount of time arguing the point.

          "Avatar" is essentially Traditions magical lingua franca for "that thing which lets us do magick". What else is ascribed to it may vary wildly between Traditions, but past-life memories are common, as are certain manifestations, hallucinatory or otherwise.

          Canonically, though, the closest we come to something resembling a definitive answer is "fragments or possibly self-replicating wholes of cosmic reality-warping superweapons forged by Celestines with the power to manipulate cause and effect on a level unsurpassed by any save those who forged them, invented and given to them by one of the titans who forged the world in an act of ill-thought-out vengeance against his siblings, and designed to empower specifically human souls with the capability to fight things for which death has no meaning and nonetheless kill them".

          Of course, various canonical bits of WoD in general and Mage in particular are of dubious truthfulness, so take that with a grain of Starmetal.

                      Actually, they popped back up in "Manifesto: Transmissions from the Rogue Council," but that's just a nitpick. It was close enough to "Ascension" anyway.

                      As to the question at hand, it's been pretty well addressed. There are not hard and fast definitions of what an Avatar is canonically, or even a soul for that matter. Nothing mechanically, at least. Different factions and cultures will assert different things. Some will even imply that the Avatar and the soul are one and the same. There was mention of Vampire and its relationship with souls, but we start wading into pretty murky waters when we view the metaphysics of one game through the lens of another, so I think it best to avoid. However, I do remember reading once that the Avatar is shredded upon the Embrace, and I'm inclined to use that instead of allowing the Avatar to merely detach and move on in its cosmic reincarnation cycle. It adds quite a bit of horror, I think. Although... it might be cool for a reincarnated Avatar to meet its former host who yet survives as a vampire... Even better if said vampire is a Tremere and recognizes the Avatar as formerly his and doggedly pursues it for his dark purposes... I'm going to file that idea away.

                      It is generally accepted that Avatars are immortal in some way, and that it carries memories with it, and even retains injury to itself. The reason that Gilgul is such a frightening thing for a mage is that it's so damn permanent, both the maiming of the Avatar or its complete destruction. Carry that forward to a Nephandi's inverted Avatar: it's inverted forever and will reincarnate in its perverted, inverted state to continue spreading its mad ambitions.

                              I've been advocating this interpretation for a while now, specifically because it creates more avenues for potential stories. If the Embrace just destroys the Avatar, then that's it. No more stories are possible. But if the Avatar just leaves with the Embrace - because the Mage in question dies - then it can create any number of stories thereafter. Up to and including how the newly undead former Mage deals with their subsequent incarnations.

                              And while there's certainly plenty of stories involving an adversarial relationship between the two, there's just as much that can be found in a non-adversarial role. The Vampire acting as a secret patron, guardian "angel", mentor, magical instructor (sure, the vampire can only use blood magic himself, but he remembers how he did True Magick, right?), even surrogate parent.

                              There are even mixes between the two extremes. The vampire may feel conflicting emotions of protectiveness and bitterness. A mentor who helps their successor, but in such a way as to also secretly use them as a pawn for political advantage. A teacher insisting that their "student" do magick exactly the way he used to do it, regardless of the different needs of the times. Even a patron who thinks that, if their successor doesn't meet their standards of competence, it might be better to just kill them and hope the next incarnation is better.

                                                  Originally posted by Frozenfishy View Post

                                                  I'm really of a couple minds on this. I do agree with you about the compelling story possibilities that can come about involving a vampire and his lost Avatar. On the other hand, I like to preserve a considerable amount of horror for mages who deal with vampires and some of the rather dire consequences involved. It's for that reason that I tend to like the rules for mage ghouldom in Blood Treachery because of simply how bad they hurt.
                                                  I respectfully disagree. The very fact that the Mage loses their Avatar AT ALL is plenty horrifying. Not just because you lose the phenomenal cosmic power, but because it's like being blinded. Simple perception magick is gone, and that sort of thing has a huge impact on how the user sees the world. If you can see into the future as easily as breathing, and then suddenly can't? That's horror. (You know, on top of the horror of being a vampire.)

                                                  Anything more than that takes it over the top, and turns the whole thing into melodrama.

                                                  Moreover, I have a logical problem with the very idea that vampire Vitae has Gilgul as an inherent property. Why would this be the case? What's the rational behind this? Is it because they are Static or Entropic? Well no, because there are plenty of Mages with a whole lot of both Resonances, yet still have their magick. Is it because it's a curse from God? But some Mages think that the Avatar is a shard of the divine. Why would the divine destroy the divine? Or else, we must conclude that the fragment of divinity theory is objectively false, which screws over those groups and the players who run them.

                                                  What I'm saying is that this idea - that vampiric blood is inherently toxic to Avatars - only really exists to prevent Mage-Vampire hybrids. It's a Doylist conceit, used to prevent OP characters like Dio Brando from falling into the player characters' hands. But of course, if the Avatar just moves on at the moment between exanguination and revival, the result is functionally the same.

                                                  Personally, I would make the idea of the Embrace destroying the Avatar an in-universe LIE, told by master Mages on both sides of the Ascension War to their students. Layering the already bad parts of pursuing vampirism with a more punitive result. It's a way of scaring the young Mages straight, so they don't even contemplate the vampire option. For all intents and purposes, the results are the same, so the elders see no danger. Except, of course, for when a former Mage meets their successor, and suddenly takes an intense interest in them.

                                                  Hell, the idea of ghouldom also destroying the Avatar could be a lie too. It's not like being a slave to an undead regnant, or else a desperate vampire-hunting blood junkie, isn't already a horrible fate. The Avatar itself doesn't need to become addicted to Vitae. How would that even work, when the Avatar is a wholly spiritual being (maybe)? I'm even willing to meet Blood Treachery halfway, and say that so long as a Mage is a Ghoul, they can't increase their Arete. The focus is more on stagnation, painting ghouldom as a spiritual dead end, rather than a trap.

                                                  Otherwise, the mechanical price of becoming a Ghoul Mage is far, far too severe for any Mage to seriously consider it. Which, under my "The Masters Are Lying" scenario, is exactly the point. But it doesn't prevent a Mage from becoming a Ghoul, nor cutting off potential stories.
