                        Okay. I'm pretty sure I get the dislike for the Technephandi plot notion. IMO, it's tied to dislike for the larger forces of evil behind the World of Darkness, but that's really a secondary factor. I don't think using it straight is inherently bad, but seeing how I wouldn't use the Technephandi out of the box myself, I think I understand.

                        I'm still going to argue for it being emphasized in M20. Here's why:

                        In previous editions, the Nephandi were never explicitly called out as the most successful major faction of the Ascension War. It was hinted at -- sometimes broadly -- and in some places there were very broad winks. However, it was never stated outright. The Ascension War was Traditions vs. Technocracy, with the other factions occasionally leaping in and being terrifying. This model, conveniently, works for both Trad and Techno campaigns. If a savvy ST wanted to spring a "mua-ha-ha-ha" twist into the war, it was still a twist, but the context was there -- a vital element to all twists. Now, though, the Nephandi have functionally been revealed. It's probably been too obvious for too long anyway, but it's nice to have the acknowledgement. Sure, in-universe it may not be clear, but now that subtext has become text, the Fallen are going to be on a lot of PCs' radar. So how do you keep the Ascension War's traditional bait-and-switch game in play?

                        You leave one of the big "Future Fate" options the Technephandi. Make it look like canon. Drop major hints that the Tradtions are in the same boat. Instead of having the Nephandi slithering in the background, make it look like they've gone big. All-in for the triumph of the Absolute. Only the heroes can save the day -- by doing what they've always wanted to do and bringing down "the enemy." Meanwhile, as ever, the Nephandi sit back and laugh.

                        The problem with corruption is that it's usually subtle. Insidious. Obvious corruption falls quickly. Only when monsters think their power is absolute do the masks come off (c.f. Nephandi 1945), and even at that, they're wrong more often than not (ibid). The Nephandi are corruption incarnate. Make their real plans too obvious in the text, and they don't really work. Sure, those of us who've been at this a really long time can guess what the Nephandi are really up to (and make it up as STs, which is very convenient), but to newer players, and in-universe Awakened who lack most of our knowledge, the Nephandi are largely a foul, terrible mystery. While I expect the coming Book of the Fallen to have some major revelations and nightmarishly fascinating possibilities, a certain amount of obscuring (especially in the players' introduction to the universe) is very much in order, IMO.

                        Heroes die. Hope is a phoenix.

                                Systemic Corruption - where the corruption is built into the system via the way it is set up, in the case of the Technocracy because of conflicting goals and lack of transparency. The Nephandi can exacerbate this one, but they don't really play into it at all.

                                Noble Cause Corruption - where unethical things are done in the pursuit of a higher cause. The Nephandi can facilitate this one quite a bit by providing the tough choices and the unethical opportunities, but they can also push it to the sidelines if they become so dominant that they've supplanted the original cause.

                                The Best System we Have - On a more personal level a Technocrat may know the system is crap, but still think they can do some good from within.
