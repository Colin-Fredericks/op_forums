            Getting back on topic thou, if "Enriched Uranium" was just a mythical hermetic "Hellstonium" material in middle ages but now is just a "simple component", meaning that it would be Matter 5 in Medieval Period but now is just a simple substance due to Consensus, that has very large ramifications for the setting at large. It implies heavy consequences to how the Spheres work and a direct connection to the power of Consensus. This would mean not only that, if tomorrow the Union decides to push some other new "Unobitanium" into Consensus, it automatically becomes "simple stuff". "Adamantium" (maybe rebranding it to "Super Steel", just like we could claim that "Enriched Uranium" is just rebranded "Hellstonium") could become exactly that.

            Take Kevlar for example. Is this a simple substance or is this some sort of "Unobitanium" that was pushed through Consensus?

            Would you have to use Matter 5 to create plastic in the medieval times? (And probably call it "Divine Leather").

            But those considerations don't cease for Matter alone. It could extend to many other Spheres. What happens in a distant future Star Trek style where teleportation becomes mundane?

            What's more, this also have severe implications for Reality Zones. What if I create Adamantium inside of my Sanctum, where the local reality zone accepts it as a mundane material? Can I then be creating Adamantium in my Lab with mere Matter 2?

                This a personal interpretation, but I consider this exact realization - that Spheres are limited only by convention and the way a Mage perceives reality - as step towards Ascension.
                In a way Marauders are very, very close to this, but they lack the consciousness and control of their own reality superseding conventional reality. It's one thing to read "transcend reality" in a book and be truly able to do it, which is the more difficult part. It requires the Mage to let go of some very fundamental concepts ingrained in their mind and personality.

                I think in-game, Spheres are crutches established by convention. Their architecture makes sense as something to learn and master, but the division and progression is ultimately arbitrary.

                So yes, I consider that scenario completely feasible. It becomes even clearer the longer you go back in time: consider a bronze age Mage vis-a-vis carbon steel. Meteor Swords. I don't think anyone would consider carbon steel a Matter 5 effect.

                    What properties does Kevlar have that would have ever qualified it as some sort of Unobtanium? Polymer sciences weren't nearly as advanced, but it's not like the concepts involved would be hard to explain to past humans. Kevlar is just a synthetic material instead of a naturally sourced material. "If you carefully mix this thing, and that thing just right, and then dry it, it turns into fluffy fibers you can then spin into threads instead of gathering wool, processing flax, or harvesting loose silk."

                    Seriously. Look at a flax plant. If people thousands of years ago were fine with the idea that you could turn that in to linen, which can be everything from a fine light handkerchief to body armor if used correctly, how is Kevlar supposed to do anything that qualifies as Matter 5?

                    Would you have to use Matter 5 to create plastic in the medieval times? (And probably call it "Divine Leather").
                    They had plastics in Medieval times. European and Asian culture learned how to form early natural plastics from purifying polymer chains derived from keratin via animal horns. Shellac was also something we know was in use earlier. The first recognized natural plastic processed by humans was early rubber over 3600 years ago. Humans are also very used to the discovery of a new material leading to a lot of experimentation in what uses it could have or better ways to make it. Resins of various sorts were used since pre-history for a huge number of applications we use synthetic plastics for now.

                    The massive revolution in plastics in the Industrial areas was in synthetic plastics and thus the ability to customize polymer chains for specific properties.

                    What happens in a distant future Star Trek style where teleportation becomes mundane?
                    See this is why Matter is written poorly. And theory-crafting in consensus based Sphere ranks for Matter effects doesn't actually make the game better.

                    The Spheres necessary to teleport don't change if a Sleeper usable teleporter is invented. Teleportation just becomes coincidental (or at least much easier to do coincidentally).

It's not like working Magick is a static thing that doesn't change over time.

                            The thing is, a Mage always believes what they do is possible. From the character standpoint, the distinction between Matter 2 and Matter 5 is the level of knowledge and skill necessary to synthesize the material, not if it is a real and naturally possible substance. It is, by definition.

                            And for medieval Mages? Nothing in the game says they can't use Matter 2 to obtain modern materials. And just as modern Mages can use Matter 2 to obtain substances that are very hard to synthesize by mundane means, so could medieval Mages use it to obtain substances that were too hard to get for any regular use back them.

                            Now, surely there could be times when a medieval Mage would have used Matter 5 to synthesize something we see as mundane now. But that would be less due to the Mage not trying it at Matter 2 than it not being possible, and it would be useful anyway, as Matter 5 would give him finer control over the result's properties.

                            The whole thing of distinguishing Matter between "real" and "fantastical" materials is bad writing as it actually makes the power of the Sphere very dependent on how much the player knows material sciences.

                                        Except, as I just pointed out, humans were producing plastics over 3500 years ago, and plastics were in regular production during Medieval times. There weren't as good as modern plastics, but lots of material sciences have advanced drastically based on a number of factors. You're putting making bouncy balls out of the sap of rubber plants into the same category as anti-gravity rocks.

                                        The problem is that no other Sphere really works this way. The fact that statistics and probability fields of math are far more advanced now than a thousand years ago might influence a bit of coincidental vs. not with Entropy, but there's nothing that changes what level a given manipulation of the odds of something happening is. Forces does not care if I'm manipulating a magnetic field generated by a lodestone or an electromagnet. Life does care if an species has gone extinct when it comes to what levels of Life are needed to manipulate or reproduce something.

                                        There's also a big difference between a game that encourages learning, and a game that encourages collecting factoids to manipulate the game in their favor. Having an engrossing game of Mage that inspires you to go study a given subject after the session is over is fun. Having a session drag on because one player keeps going, "Well actually..." is not fun. It's less fun when that turns into how to push the ST around by multiple people. And it's even less fun when your ST cancels the game because they're tired of people trying to game the letter of the rules instead of just enjoying the spirit of them.
