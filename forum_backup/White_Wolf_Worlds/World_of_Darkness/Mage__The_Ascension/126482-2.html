    Originally posted by theoutlander523 View Post
    So I'm playing a Sorcerer using Sorcerer Revised and I need some help. For you that don't know, let me state some things. Sorcerers are weak and need a lot of experience to be effective. They're super squishy, and are only able to cast good spells as long as they have temporary willpower. Firstly: This book is terribly written. I mean half the needed stuff for a Sorcerer isn't defined any where in the book and they have multiple words for a single concept, like number of successes. It took me multiple read throughs of the casting mechanics to understand them because they are so horribly written. Like holy crap, I'm still not sure if I'm throwing fireballs correctly. Secondly: This book is terribly written. I mean this as in, they literally took the Sorcerer's hand book, copy pasted it, spent a Saturday to update some stuff, and published it. There are things in this that don't even exist in the rest of the book. Like the Path of Cursing or Ephemera. Also you can do aggravated damage, but it doesn't say whether Path of Healing actually helps since it's for healing mortals, and mortals don't care about aggravated. Also some of the rolls don't make a lick of darn sense. Alchemy is Intelligence + Alchemy, but Advanced Chemistry is Intelligence + Science. It says you are normally using Attribute + Ability for a roll, yet I can't find an ability named Alchemy, but there is one for science, so do I use the Alchemy Path rating? Then there's Weathercontrol which uses Manipulation + Willpower for it, so you're getting a huge number of successes. So there's a bunch of Paths that ignore the go to rule already. But then there's the Path of Healing which uses Manipulation + Intuition. Intuition was removed in Revised IIRC, and is not even a choice on the sheet anymore. Ignoring why would you use Intuition over Medicine FOR A HEALING BASED POWER, why is this in here? Thirdly: Useless Paths are all over the place. Shadowcasting, Alchemy, and Enchanting are all terribly useless. Alchemy and Enchanting are ritual only paths, meaning you can't do them in combat really, which makes sense. However, they require a 3 experience point investment to learn a ritual, what this means is that you can have a single point spent into Enchanting, and can only make a single magical item that has super weak effects. Enchanting and Alchemy are suppose to be the Bread and Butter of a Sorcerer, yet he has to blow 30 experience to even bling himself out with basic stuff. Why would anyone ever touch this? Removing the ritual only part of this would make it so much better, yet it's effectively pointless to take at all since it's so costly. Finally: Spells are in general hard to cast. Want to throw a fireball? Enjoy your minimum 3 turns of casting time for that Aspect 3. If you don't get the 5 successes required to do your 1 lethal damage fireball, you need to spend another 3 turns casting. You could hang the spell and use it later but now you're doing 2 willpower per spell, so you can fire 5 spells that do super low damage. Hanging spells also makes any rolls involving social way harder because you're focusing, so you can be a prepared Sorcerer, or a social one. Well you could just spend mana to lower the difficulty, but that also makes the spell require something and you can only hold 5 mana at a time. Assuming you survive the 3 turns, you will actually be able to kill something by blowing a lot of your mana. You could avoid willpower by casting the spell at a lower level, but then you're not doing much of anything at all. Sure it's easier, but your spells aren't as good really compared to a shotgun and cast way slower still. You can also use willpower to cast faster but that raises the difficulty so you need more successes since you need 1 per aspect you want in your spell effectively doing nothing. So what's the point of being a Sorcerer if they're literally a 10 shot shotgun (assuming they maxed willpower). My question is how do you make a viable Sorcerer that can actually be useful, since a pistol seems better in all sense, and what houserules are basically required to make them actually some what good, because gods know they need it. I hope Sorcerer does get a new book after M20 because it's a lot of neat stuff like for psychics which are top tier amazing, but Sorcerers are way worse do to their super long cast times, and willpower limits. I'm playing a Sorcerer in an upcoming game so if anyone has any advice, please feel free to speak up.
    Who cares about throwing fire balls in combat when you can do so much amazing sorcery with the other paths. With summoning binding and warding you can turn demons into your pawns, with Fortune you can curse others and give yourself the luck needed to win a fortune of wealth, with divination you can know what the future holds and with shapehsifting you can look like anyone you wish. Think about what you would do in the world if you had this sort of power, think about how influential you could become. Sorcerer's are like gods among insects, though perhaps, not among demons. As a rule focused piece of advice there's a line somewhere which states that you don't need to buy rituals for ritual only paths like alchemy and enchantment which are actually *very* powerful. I would add that you can also be a psychic who learned sorcery as per the merrit in the revised edition and that Dream Speakers revised (while also showcasing new paths) supports the Path + Attribute system which is mentioned as a new standard and one worth converting to for a variety of sensible reasons.

                                            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                            There’s little chance of an M20 Sorcerer any time soon, unfortunately; but once M20 comes out, I intend to tackle this issue. A preliminary thought I’ve had is that M20’s new Focus rules assign common Abilities to the Style as well as common tools: for M20 Sorcerer, the Attribute + Ability pool for a given Path should be dependent on the sorcerer’s Style. As an extreme example, a sorcerer who’s Style involves bargaining with supernatural forces might use a social attribute such as Charisma rather than Intelligence when forming his dice pool, and/or an Ability such as Etiquette or Performance instead of Occult.

                                            In terms of house rules: consider dropping most of the Willpower costs; the casting times are more than adequate to prevent the sorcerer from spamming their rituals; and consider allowing the use of mana in place of Willpower in those cases that still have a cost. Consider removing the effects of Aspects from the Difficulty and Time determination; the fact that you must allocate successes already implies increased chance of failure and a longer casting time due to the need to accumulate more successes.

                                                            Originally posted by Muad'Dib View Post
                                                            Can there be an Appearance or Strength-based Sorcerer in your system? A Dexterity or Wits-based Sorcery sounds even more unlikely, but at the same time it makes one wonder...Certainly Charisma and Stamina do fit.
                                                            Absolutely. If you can justify a Strength+Skill pool, you can justify a Strength+Ability pool for a Path; you just need the right Focus. For instance, Strength+Athletics is used frequently; a Sorcerer whose Practice is Martial Arts could use that as a dice pool for, say, Hellfire: think Street Fighter. Appearance+Expression could use the Practice of Bardism (Book of Secrets) for Fascination.

                                                            Upon reflection, I wouldn't try to the an Attribute to the Paradigm after all; the choice of Attribute should be made in terms of how the Ability gets used, as normal.

                                                            Incidentally, I could also see the Art of Desire using a lot of Attributes, including Strength and Appearance.
