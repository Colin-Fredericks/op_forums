    Standard Fantasy Campaign using Mage?

    07-17-2017, 11:09 AM
    So two things I've been considering. One is mage in a fantasy setting a la dungeons and dragons.

    And second is completely wiping the spheres in favor of Player specific spheres.

    Basically, the idea is that I meet up with each player and talk about the character they want to make. Come up with spheres that work for that concept. And move on. For example a sphere of theivery. At rank 1 you can detect opportunity for thefts. At rank 2 you can hide these opportunities or give yourself bonuses to steal something. At rank 3 you could set a thieves lure or curse. Basically +/- successes to all rolls while trying to steal the item you place the spell on. At rank 4 you could create opportunity for theft where none before. And at rank 5 you could create opportunity for theft where it doesn't make sense. Like stealing the flame off a candle or some such similar feat.

    But essentially the idea is to go through and build the world partially out of the ideas they come up with for spheres. So each has a vested interest and connection to the world. If Joe wants to be Joe the chthulhu summoner. It means he wants chthulhus in the world.

    The one concern I have is the potential lack of balance between sphere concepts.

      First I recommend dark eras, it's good for inspiration.

      Second, unless you're just doing "MAGE IN FANTASY LAND" it might be easier to use hunter the vigil. Has less powers but it's more flexible especially the upcoming 2e which has 'build your own endowment' rules good for everything from paladins and alchemists to warlocks (in terms of D&D-esque classes). Might even be worth trotting out witchfinders and letting them use those magic rules (which suits your custom sphere idea). Other options are second sight and the influence based mages somewhere in these forums.

      Mage's problem is that it tends to dominate and consume games it's in, and here that would translate to mage dominating every other character aspect. No warriors who aren't adamantine arrow (or equivalent for your setting), no clerics who aren't obrimos, druids are just thysus, ect. custom spheres would be difficult to balance as basically your first half dozen sessions would be nothing but creative thaumaturgy and the players could (through no fault of their own) break the game by trying to find ways to use their custom powers for every situation.

      This doubles as the nature of a fantasy setting means everyone knows magic is real so it's likely that while paradox may exist it's unlikely that anyone would be considered a sleeper. So basically you'd be in a world about duels between sorcerer kings warring for power across a mystical land. Which can be awesome if that's what you want to play.

                          I once ran a SpellJammer D&D campaign using WoD rules. I had magic following Sorcerer path rules. The Sorcerers were the movers and shakers of all things magic. Mages were known in legends as being powerful and crazy. I treated all difficulties for casting as Vulgar without witnesses. Paradox was calculated according to subtlety. Coincidental magic was performed by following Hedge Path rules for casting times and rituals; or making it look like magic wasn't used at all. Showy magic that skipped from the paradigm was treated as Vulgar without witnesses. All paradox points only applied to Quiet though; hence the crazy. The one Mage player learned to keep things subtle real fast.
