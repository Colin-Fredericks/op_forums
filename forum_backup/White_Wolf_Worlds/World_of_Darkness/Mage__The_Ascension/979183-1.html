        Originally posted by Bogdan View Post


        Odd. I've seen pure magickal attacks being ruled the other way around.

        Now that begs for a question why would one want to throw a fireball if they can just set someone on fire without having to roll for hit.
        Starting character example:
        Arete 3 Forces3 Life3 Prime2 Dex+Ath 5

        Fireball (Arete against TN 7) we generously assume he gets the 3 successes needed for a fireball (m20 p. 503). The target (dex+ath 4) rolls for dodge and scores 2 successes.
        That means the target gets burned for 4 levels of agg (bonus success from forces).

        Now assume you need to roll to hit (all else being equal).
        The mage scores 3 successes to hit with his 5 dice.
        Subtract 2 for dodge, the fireball still hits.
        3(+1 for Forces) means 8 levels of agg.

        As long as you have more dices in the suitable attribute+ability than in Arete, you are better off rolling to hit.

        Pattern Attacks are a different story.

        Rip the man body with the above 3 successes are 6 levels agg. Only Countermagic can help in this case.

        So, this Zen Master walks up to a hot dog stand and says: "Make me one with everything!"

                Sorry for making a post then having life jump me in a dark ally and bend a trashcan around me (ok, maybe I have had a little time to catch a couple episodes of Luke Cage).

                I see this conversation has spun into a tangent and I see most questions have been answered.

                Concerning: Why would anyone make an effect that forces the player to have to roll to hit vs just rolling the magic (ie, bursting flames where a person is standing vs hitting them with a fireball. Or using a thousand other things people can think of using forces).

                I assume most mage players are reasonably intelligent people and they can figure out how to create a “curse effect” vs a “physical effect” (lack of better words) while staying inside of the characters paradigm.

                The above is a problem I am having with the game and part of the reason that spur this conversation. If I do Rip the Man Body, the only “dodge” the target has is with countermagick. Which is cool. I can deal with that and it makes sense. If I throw a magick something at the target, they can try and dodge out of the way. I think this is a cool idea and it also makes sense. But where is the mechanical benefit? I mean this is a game, if there is a yang then there should be a ying. Why would I give the target a dodge roll, when if I think for 5 more seconds, I can take away their dodge roll?

                My group has been trying to use the rules for Violence Enhances Magick for all physical attacks. So if Attacker rolls 5 successes and defender rolls 3 successes the effect has -2 difficulty to be that much better. It was a little TOO good, so we make one minor adjustment. We treat it like an attack roll. The first success is a hit, the second success reduces the difficulty. It has worked so far for us, but we have only been trying it out for a few get togethers.

                I notice people in the game now make a decision based on what makes a better scene vs what is more efficient. Mostly because we all agree that between these two attacks they are sorta balanced now. One way gets a dodge and a possible reduced difficulty and the other way rolls effect vs counter magick. There are benefits on both sides.

                Whereas before my players were like, “Well X seems like something my character would do, but she could also do Y and Y has a better chance of success right now so I am doing Y.”

                The only other suggestion I received was give the attacker an additional damage dice to be tacked on to the effect. But that means one attack could have a total of 4 rolls. (Attack, dodge, arête, and bonus dice). I already feel we roll a lot… well in combat that is.


                Which leads me back to my original post:
                Then we ran into this question. “What if I want to flip a car on someone or pull a bank safe out of the sky? What do I roll for attack?” This look like something from page 417 (openly magickal or Indirect assault) where it says you roll your Arete as your attack roll. That doesn’t make ANY sense to me. We used our characters to test this. I made my Arete roll and the other player rolled their Dex+Athletics to dodge. Then we tried this with mooks. We have starting characters Arete 3 (a schooled mage), but Dex+Athletics using a mook thug from the back of the book starts with 4 dice. You could probably tell, we were not hitting much.

                So that is when I reached out to you all. Maybe someone will say something that makes sense.

                What would be a good attack roll for someone making something fall out of the sky, flipping a car onto a target, making a rock fly out of the ground to hit someone, doing a gesture with their hand and a fireball flies out of the clouds?

                If there isn’t an attack roll, the target should get a chance to dodge, what do they roll against? Do I create a random successes needed? What kind of mechanic would work well here? Is there something already and I am missing it?

                All help is appreciated.

                                Your "yes, yes" sounds like you're tired of people recalling you that RPG is about playing a role... :'(
                                That is not what I mean by "Yes Yes". Every time I try to have a conversation about mechanics someone jumps out and wants to talk about the role part. RP isn't a problem. You can role play with any game. My friends and I dress up and RP our characters when we play board games. We do this often with zombiecide. I have played many Table Top board games and RPGs. I have attended a few different LARPS. RolePlay is pretty consistent across all these types of games. The difference between all of these games are mechanics.

                                In my opinion, RP enhances a game, but the mechanics are what make and break games. “What allows the player to best be the character they designed without rules?” That is the question I rate every TTRPG on. Obviously the character must be created within the theme of the game (ie a player wants to play a vampire in Star Wars, most SW TTRPGs are not designed for that). This is one reason why FATE is my favorite TTRPG.


                                So when I ask about these things, I am focusing on mechanics. If I want to build a mage that throws things around like a car, fridge, rocks from the ground. Mechanic wise I am at a disadvantage to someone who does the exact same thing, but with a gun, with their throwing arm, with a sling shot and more.

                                This, to me, is a problem with the system. A player should be able to play any kind of mage character they want and we should be able to look at the system on a fundamental level and say "You are doing a (sphere) effect. It is a physical attack; the target gets to dodge; effect is determined by Arête roll."

                                Non-dodge-able (curse) type actions all work like this. Doesn’t matter if you have to make soup, dance, throw a hissy fit, speak Latin, or poop to cast your spell. You roll Arete; target rolls counter magick; effect is determined by Arête.

                                So, if they are throwing a fireball, punching, tossing a car, spitting, or some dodge-able action. These should all fundamentally work the same. Roll some kind of Attribute+Skill combo to attack, Target rolls Attribute+Skill combo to defend and on hit effect is determined by Arete roll.

                                This is how I feel it should work. I am just trying to get there.
