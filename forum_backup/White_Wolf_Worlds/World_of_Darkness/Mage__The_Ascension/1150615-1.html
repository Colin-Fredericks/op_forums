        Because... and this bears repeating... the Technocratic Union (emphasis on Union) was willing to take the collective lumps of paradox until enough people believed in the utility and repeatability of science.

        The Order of Reason started out with a noble goal. The problem with the Verbena's special herbs and the Order's spells was that they were exclusively under the control of a mystical elite. Only the chosen few got the secrets of healing magic. The Order's goal with Science was to open up the masses. You don't need to be a seventh son of a seventh son with a sympathetically attuned rowen wand to throw fire at your enemies... you just need this thing the Order of Reason calls a "gun"

        This noble goal of democratizing magic is what gave the early technocrats the courage to take the hits of paradox until enough people believed in what they were doing that it became coincidental.

        They also took anyone willing to buy into their paradigm and tens, hundreds and then thousands of willworkers willing to take the paradox hits for the cause can effect great changes.

        Meanwhile the Traditions continued to horde their power and secrets and exclusivity and even when they did start to organize there was no central common paradigm to their efforts. The shamans, akashics, wizards, witches and priests of half-a-dozen faiths each promoted their own beliefs with the only true unity of purpose being Order of Reason = Bad.

        The Technocracy won the world over to their brand of magic by presenting something that could work for anyone and being willing to endure the pain it took to get there.

            Also, there are paradigm shifts in science as new data comes in. I wouldn't agree completely with Thomas Kuhn's The Structure of Scientific Revolutions, but in science, if you start to accumulate a mass of evidence which doesn't answer a question, alternative theories come into play. Scientists will perform experiments and debate those theories. In the Technocracy, because Control is key, there's a lot less debate than in pure research. For the Technocracy, science serves the main purpose of controlling the masses and making their lives more predictable. The Progenitors, for example, want to make everything in an organism's life cycle a matter of biology free of any supernatural origins, so they want to know how down to the molecule a drug would interact with someone. Iteration X is into time-motion studies because it wants efficiency and replicably--so that each factory worker does the same operation day in and day out for their whole working life.

            The Technocracy denies funding to, discredits and kills those who disagree with its view of reality. They are the man, but they are fighting for a world without the insanity of the Marauders or Nephandi and which they think would work better on science and reason than mysticism. Given how the High Age of Magic is portrayed, it probably was like we see the Medieval Age now: a bad time to not be one of the elite.

                                Not only is this correct, it goes further. I would maintain that, in Mage: The Ascension, everything is magic. The differences lie in how readily people believe in it.

                                Here's one example: automobiles. Back in the day, a horseless carriage was Vulgar magic, unless you lived in a particular Reality Zone where the local commoners believed that a wizard could make a carriage move by his magic alone. Then, in the last few centuries, technologists built - by hand - contraptions that could move under their own power. These technologists were, at first, Mages using particularly advanced Instruments to perform the Effect of moving them at greater speeds. As their forays into this budding science gained traction, they started making ones that could be used by the layman - Devices, the technomancer version of the Talisman (a type of Wonder that comes with its own Arete score). Each had to be built by hand, and required a great deal of work to repair and maintain.

                                As these Devices became more common, the idea became accepted enough that the Mages could teach non-Awakened how to build and use them. This transitioned it into the Consensus...as an Instrument for linear Sorcery. Specifically, the Path of Conveyance, a form of "hedge magic" used for transporting the user across long distances quickly. Then, with Gerald Ford, the car became mass produced, as the technology had become SO accepted that the ability to operate it left the realm of Sorcery, and entered the realm of Ability. Building cars started being covered by Technology, while operating one became the province of a new Skill: Drive. What once was so esoteric that only the most cutting edge of scientists could make and use them (and then, not always safely), now became so common that it's not considered "magical" in the sense that it requires any special understanding. Everyone agrees that cars work, and so anyone can learn to use them.


                                There's another example that pushes even further: Language.

                                Language is, from a certain point of view, a form of Telepathy. Using nothing but grunts, hisses, and inflections, I can plant an idea into your head. I'm using a variant of that same process right now, just substituting mouth-sounds for arcane symbols arranged in a set order. In this case, you can read my message and have it planted in your mind, while we may be thousands of miles and hours, days, or even years apart (depending on when you read this). Yet, despite how staggering a feat it is that I can share ideas from my mind to yours, in MtAs I wouldn't need Awakened magick, a special Path, or even an Ability to do so. In M20 at least, I only have to purchase a 1 point Merit to take a Language...and that's if I want a Language in addition to my native one. This amazing form of Telepathy is so ubiquitous, a character is assumed to have some ability in it by default.

                                Now imagine how long it took early Mages to get that concept introduced into the proto-Consensus of early man.

                                                            I have undergraduate degrees in biology and history and the Technocratic view in both is that experiments must be repeatable many times to be valid. For history, while you cannot obviously repeat an event, the father of modern history Leopold Van Ranke, emphasized archival history. If you have detailed documents kept for a purpose, these are seen as being more accurate and of higher value than oral history or the written history of ordinary people. While interpretations of historical documents differ, a good historian will base their conclusions off the evidence. However, unlike non-introspective natural scientists who see their work as not directly flowing from a test of a view of reality, historians who are introspective know that they are supplying researched and rational opinion.

                                                            The very question one asks in history determines the research methods and conclusions. In biology, the same is also true, but others can apply the technical procedures while asking different questions.

                                                            In Mage, magic works because someone with the power to alter reality believes, 'the universe works this way. If I use this model and these techniques, the universe will produce this outcome.' Accepted methods work because they work and if you think of Kuhn's work about paradigm shifts, a paradigm shift happens because a bunch of people make assumptions, the assumptions don't match the data and someone decides to play politics and say 'we were wrong about this, and the world operates this way.'

                                                            Science is an overarching paradigm about how the world works and in Mage 20, you can see it as the basis of several paradigms: A Mechanistic Cosmos, Everything Is Data, Tech Holds All the Answers (Mage 20 main book, pages 568-571) and Turning the Keys to Reality (Mage 20 Book of Secrets, 194-195).
