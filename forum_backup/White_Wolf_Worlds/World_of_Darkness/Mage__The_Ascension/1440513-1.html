        I'm going to try and address your questions about "Reality Zones" and "Earthly Foundations" because I think it will help a bit with your Edit at the top about Mage saying nothing is true/everything is equally true, because those two things pertain to that specific point.

        I think the specific concept of "Reality Zones" was introduced in M20, but reflected the earlier concepts of Mage regarding the mutability of Truth and its relation to Consensus. The idea of Reality Zones is that, if what is True and the Laws of Reality are determined by Belief, then what rules are actually in play are determined by the local Consensus. For the purpose of game rules, Reality Zones mostly refer to what types of True Magic might be seen as Vulgar versus Coincidental, IIRC. For example, imagine an isolated Fundamentalist Christian community that strongly believes in things like miracles, angels, etc. A Christian Mage with a Christian Focus (methodology/theory for casting magic) could perhaps perform blatantly supernatural acts in public and still be able to treat it as Coincidental rather than Vulgar, because the dominant Consensus in the area allows for it. Conversely, Technocratic tech and abilities might be hindered by Paradox in ways not normally expected if the local Consensus regards it as impossible, or blasphemous. It's a term for the way that Consensual Reality might apply to specific areas.

        To contrast, "Earthly Foundations" are rules that are presumably independent of Consensual Reality, to a significant extent at least. There are (perhaps) some things which are True no matter how much Mages or the Consensus might wish otherwise. In usual Mage fashion, actual Earthly Foundations are never discussed AFAIK, they're just a theoretical extrapolation of the belief that SOME PART of Reality has to be independent from the whims of the Consensus.

        PS To actually respond to the OP: as some comments above might indicate, I'm pretty open to the idea that truly immutable Earthly Foundations don't even exist on Earth, so the idea that such a thing holds up in the Near/Deep Umbra makes sense to me. When looking at things from a Mage perspective, I already tend to view different Umbral Realms as Reality Zones shaped by their spiritual denizens. I think the Deep Umbra might be a bit more complicated though, because you have the Deep Umbra that is, to use what I think is the Tychoidian term, "unformatted," versus Deep Umbra that has been "formatted" but by alien, Lovecraftian, etc., entities. "Unformatted" Deep Umbra could easily be "formatted" to be compatible with a particular Paradigm, whereas the Deep Umbra of Eldritch Abominations might be even more inhospitable to regular Mages but better for Nephandi (associated with the relevant Deep Umbral horror).

                    Let's take this from another angle: what is an Earthly Foundation?

                    I don't mean list off specific examples of possible Earthly Foundations, but rather to determine the actual criteria or nature. If an Earthly Foundation is an eternal metaphysical Truth, where does it come from? To apply it to Dataweaver's idea, why would one part of the Tellurian possess such traits, but others not?

                    Rather than viewing the Earthly Foundations as independent Truths, I instead prefer to frame them as a consequence of metaphorical inertia, or accretion. What are regarded as Earthly Foundations are not absolutely immutable, but rather practically so; there is so much "weight" behind those ideas, built up over millennia, that they have a stability that other facets of Reality do not, making them more difficult, or even impossible, for Mages to overcome in "typical" fashion.

                    To apply this back to Dataweaver's original point: in this framing, I would probably disagree that ("formatted") Umbra lacks Earthly Foundations, but rather that the Earthly Foundations of those Realms are fundamentally different from those on Earth. Part of where this theory definitely starts to tread on established Mage lore is that it requires entities other than humans to have some kind of effect on/contribution to the Consensus. But then, I tend to enjoy kicking all of the gamelines in some of their assumptions, and undermining the Humanity-As-Exceptional component seems like the best spot to smack Mage egotism in the 'nads.

                        Let me counter with this: historical inertia is certainly one possible explanation for Earthly Foundations (though it doesn't explain how the whole thing for started). Another possible explanation is that they exist because a powerful spiritual entity (such as God or Gaia) wants them to. A third possible explanation is that they're exactly what they're called: the foundations of Earthly reality, the natural “shape” that's inherent in the fabric of the Tapestry. Any of these might be true. The end result is the same: if there's a way to change them, it takes a lot more than it takes to change a Reality Zone. And that's saying something. In theory, they might be mutable; in practice, they rarely are: the most you can do is to temporarily override them with your Magick.

                        I can see Realms in the Umbra having something equivalent to Earthly Foundations: rules of the Realm that hold true no matter what the denizens might happen to believe. But I think it's just as likely that in the various Umbral Realms, you're not going to find any where the locals' beliefs are at odds with the local reality. That's what it would mean to not have anything like Earthly Foundations. They would still have a reality Zone that defines what is Coincidental and what is Vulgar, but there wouldn't be anything pertaining to that Realm that couldn't be changed through the mechanisms involved in changing a Reality Zone.
