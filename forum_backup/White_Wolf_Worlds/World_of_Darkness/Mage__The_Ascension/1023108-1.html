            You're missing the population bottleneck aspect. Humans are very genetically similar to each other. VERY similar. If the total gene pool of humanity 100,000 years ago were a standard deck of 52 cards, we ended up loosing like 30 cards worth of genes. Add in a few from crossbreeding with close relatives (Neanderthals, Denisovans, whatever else those other genes are) and one or two that sum up all the relevant genetic mutation, and you are talking about a small variety of genetic "cards" out there. It doesn't matter how big the deck is now, it's just lots and lots of copies of that smaller number of cards.

            So, yes, even in a small group, those effects come into play. In addition, the modern concept of restricted travel in the past is pretty much false. Villages weren't isolated. They were part of numerous long distance down the line exchange networks which included enough genetic material to keep those from being cut-off as you are depicting them. (More modern travel, since the Age of Exploration, isn't really all that much greater, it's just that it allows for more distant points to have contact with each other. Think of a tapestry where people have to travel across the surface until long distance ships come along, Those ships allow one to fold the fabric and allow different parts of the tapestry to touch). Remember, during the Lower Paleolithic, there was enough genetic "sloshing" occurring that Homo erectus didn't differentiate enough to cause speciation across the entire Eastern Hemisphere. And modern humans were even better at keeping in touch with the folks next door and across significant gaps (like between the then present Greater Indonesian Peninsula extending out from SE Asia and the large combo New Guinea/Australia).

            I agree with your Sphere choices. Though, perhaps, it would be more like using Time like Correspondence to "reach/go back" and Entropy to find the right places to effect any real change. And those HUGE associated Paradox costs and high numbers of successes needed are indicative of much of the above. The "Continuum of History" resists significant change on a large scale because individual events or people just don't typically don't matter when compared to the warp and weft of, well, everything that was.

            However, as a codicil... Those events, places and people that DO matter are probably going to be heavily guarded in some way by time savvy mages that have an interest in keeping history the way it is. After all, there are some points in time and some people (individual or group) that really matter in both the magical and mundane history, and messing with those would cause significant changes. The kind that can wipe out pretty much everything the meddlers came from and make them walking Paradoxes. (Say "Hello" to the nice man in the white suit that desperately needs some dry cleaning....)
