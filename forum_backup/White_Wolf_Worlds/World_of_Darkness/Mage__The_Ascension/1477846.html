    Dual Fellowships for Dark Ages mages

    04-01-2022, 09:29 AM
    Recent discussion on another topic got me thinking about the issue of Dark Ages mages having multiple Fellowships. Da:M rulebook and the Grimoire sourcebook allow it and provide Xp costs for additional Foundations and Pillars. M20 has an equivalent rule through the Dual Affiliation Merit, even if its very flexible Foci rules make it unnecessary in many case, except for flavor purposes and/or the benefit of having a second Affinity Sphere. This character development opportunity seems interesting, both in terms of the synergy benefits between the Foundations and Pillars of multiple Fellowships and the ability to bridge the paradigm and ideology differences between different groups. I wonder which the most fruitful Fellowship combinations would be in terms of practical benefits for magic use, likelihood of occurrence, and/or paradigm/ideological compatibility. I can think of a few good combos myself:

    OoH/Old Faith. One of my most preferred options, quite feasible for Hermetics that lean towards paganism or OF that have a scholarly bent, and with good synergy benefits. If you apply the setting hack of House Diedne surviving the Schism War and House Tremere being destroyed in its place, this may canon for an established subgroup, depending on whether the Diedne studied both sets of Foundation and Pillars or developed a synthesis.

    OoH/Messianic Voices. Not my cup of tea due to my dislike of CC/MV paradigm and ideology, but I acknowledge it is a great combo with good synergy and easy to set up.

    OoH/AiB. Not my cup of tea due to my dislike of the Batini way of doing things (magic should be flashy, dammit), but I acknowledge it is a great combo with good synergy and easy to set up.

    AiB/(non-Christian) MV. As above.

    Old Faith/Spirit Talkers. Good synergy, easy to justify in paradigm/ideology and organization terms.

    Old Faith/Valdaermen. Practically a prelude to future partial merger of the two groups in the Verbena. Just needs a paradigm/ideology balance between the zest for life of the OF and the grimness of the Val.

    Valdaermen/ST. Basically merging the two main mystic traditions of Norse culture into one.

    OoH/ST. Feasible if you got an Hermetic with a strong bent towards animism or a ST with a solid scholarly side.

    OoH/Valdaermen. Pursue power and suffer for it.

    Old Faith/(non-Abrahamic) MV. Feasible if you got a rather heterodox approach to religion.

    Valdaermen/(non-Abrahamic) MV. As above.

    Opinions? Suggestions?

    Yes, I know that getting adequate proficiency in two sets of Foundation and Pillars is going to take some serious effort and a sizable Xp bill, but obviously we are talking about an advanced character option here.

      One of the big advantage I think you're overlooking: group casting. That's the big reason to establish a metaphysical stance that embraces two Fellowships/Traditions/etc. While DAM doesn't play this up much, in M20 you can get +5 dice and +5 successes per roll with enough teamwork (though by the rules that's only for ritual magic, but it also takes over 100 people to get that kind of bonus). This should also be one of the big advantages for the more religious Fellowships: religions offer a ready source of a large number of believers that will gather in one place to perform rituals you can use to further boost your magic. Esp. in the DA, purposeful and deliberate applications of being flashy (also easier as a group) only helps reinforce religious communities.

      This also helps some of the combos above. It's important to remember that the idea of religions being "exclusive" is somewhat recent (and even more so in the DA setting). The idea that you would have more than one "religion" was actually pretty normal for a long time (still is in some places). The Romans were huge proponents of syncretism, and Christianity adopted a lot of that thinking in Europe. The whole reason why the PC Fellowships tend to be more standoffish is because they're the precursors to the Traditions; the mages that felt balancing Christian faith magic and Hermetic philosophy was completely logical and normal were the founders of the Order of Reason and that mentality helped shaped the much more internally cooperative Union (another point on the power of mages working as a group).
