    320) 'Til Your Fingers Bleed [Burn]: When a musical magician creates magick, it is done by pouring their Will into their literal Instrument. Where the fingers meet the strings, the keys, or whatever, is where the magus and their instrument become one. But such a point of union can be frayed if the magician puts too much force - too much damned intensity - into their performance. In moments of high passion - the kind of passion that would move Reality into a wildly different shape - the user unknowingly digs deep into their instrument, until their fingers bleed. Is this merely zealous commitment, or an act of Paradox? Who can say? All that matters is that the mage comes out the other end with sore, scarlet-slick digits. A variant of this backlash can occur in singers, where their magick strains the throat until they end up coughing up blood. Artists suffer for their Art.


    321) A Close Encounter [Realm]: Affecting certain UFO-obsessed Etherites and other technomancers, the mage's Vulgar activity draws the attention of beings from beyond the stars (or from a like Paradox realm). Usually occurring outside, in isolated locations, the mage is blinded by a flash of light, before being whisked away suddenly and without explanation. They reappear several hours or even days later, usually in the same location they were abducted from, with no ability to account for the missing time. They have, at best, hazy recollections of their abduction, unless they had active Mind effects running at the time to resist the mental manipulation. Most will vaguely recall alien beings hovering over them, and the use of bizarre tools. Those who are totally clear of mind may wish they weren't, as they would need to suffer through a protracted and unpleasant series of medical procedures, of dubious purpose. Of course, the aliens are equipped with Countermagic - befitting powerful Paradox spirits - making escape from the clinical violation of the mage's body...difficult. Eventually, the person is returned, mostly unharmed, though they may sport hidden implants or strange skin marks. The mage may develop other Paradox Flaws as a result of their contact with the visitors.

        322) A Shuffling of Tastes [Paradox Flaw ; Mind and Life] This Paradox Flaw often affects True Mages who try ad nauseam to prove that the processes of the mind and the soul stem primarily, or wholly, from functions of the body; but any True Mage who botches Spells or Procedures involving both the Sphere of Life and the Sphere of Mind - or the equivalent Pillars for Foundation and Pillars True Mages - can be affected by this Paradox Flaw.

        The sense of taste of the True Mage is altered - food items and/or dishes have their tastes swapped. A pear will taste like an apple, and vice versa. A vegetable soup is now an excellent substitute - somehow.. - for a chocolate cake, and vice versa. Sugar switches with salt. Sometimes the alteration is part of a triangle - oranges taste like apples, apples taste like pears, pears taste like oranges; but larger groups of tastes swapping have not appeared (yet?) .
        These taste alterations anchor themselves around wholesale food items and dishes. Thus a chocolate cake tasting like a vegetable soup does not mean that the taste of just sugar or chocolate bars is altered in any way towards vegetables.

        Depending on what occurs, the Mage might have some problems preparing and sharing food with others.

        This Paradox Flaw can be dealt with by developing one's interest in cooking and cooking skills.

            323) An Insidious Breakdown [Paradox Flaw ; Prime and Mind] True Mages who seek to impose on others their world views and viewpoints, while being unsure of them, risk being affected by this Paradox Flaw.
            While speaking to other persons the affected True Mage starts forgetting to mention main concepts, arguments, conclusions, elaborations et al. in regards to the outlook(s) that they tried to impose; missing and forgetting to say not only single words, but whole sentences and paragraphs. They also acquire a strong tendency - that can increase - to not notice this; perceiving and regarding their manner of speaking and credibility as impeccable.

            Someone else pointing out the errors and missing parts in the affected True Mage's words can possibly make the True Mage easily realize that they forgot to say some things, and help in dealing with this Paradox Flaw. But the person doing has to be wise, intelligent or/and considerate in order to fill the gaps in the affected True Mage's speaking; knowledge of the topic at hand also helps in this.

                324) Tracking Death In [Flaw]: The Dark Umbra is a place of elemental death - Entropy is strongest there, and paints the place in a coating of gloom, malaise, and decay. If a Mage travels there - through an Agama Sojourn, spending time in the low Penumbra, or even somehow physically traveling to the Shadowlands - they can find themselves carrying a bit of it with them when they return. They stink of the grave, and look pale, with a waxy complexion. They kill grass beneath their feet, and those footfalls can also decay wood flooring and lightly erode stone and other earthen ground. As if their footprints are decayed into the surfaces they walk on. Their fingers dirty glass, discolor plastic, rust metal, and chill flesh at the touch. Their clothes face in hue, and have a tendency to unravel on their backs. If they put a hand on a living tree, the place where their hand touched will lose its bark, and not grow for months or even years.

                Any location they spend a great deal of time in - if the Flaw persists or gains intensity - will begin to look more and more like its counterpart in the Shadowlands. A monument to decay, and the inevitable march towards ruin.

                        326) World Outside In [Quiet]: A master of Correspondence sees space, locations, and even the concepts of "inside" and "outside" different. For, when you can alter any of these at will, the concepts must be flexible indeed. With this Quiet, the mage's perceptions shift, until they believe that he is inside when he is outside, and vice versa. The exterior of buildings look like interior walls, complete with wallpaper or painted drywall, hanging framed pictures, etc. Interior surfaces of buildings, by contrast, look like exterior ones, with brick walls, ad signs, etc. The sky outside looks like a high ceiling, with the sun as a massive overhead light fixture; the ceiling inside looks like open sky, every light fixture a sun. Public benches outdoors look like couches, while couches indoors look like public benches. Grass is carpet and carpet grass, light posts are shaded indoor lamps and vice versa, etc.

                        The mage, altogether, comes to live in a world where the "outside" is a massive, interconnected building; where every open space is a room and every street is a hallway. And where the "inside" is a series of separate, disconnected atriums, surrounded by walls but exposed to the sky. Even living things are altered. A vagrant living on the streets is interpreted by the mage to be the "building's" actual residents, while people who live "indoors" are obviously homeless trash, deserving only contempt. And wild animals are domesticated pets, and vice versa.

                        Clearly, the only sensible course of action for a mage suffering this Quiet is to live "inside", taking bed where he pleases and making friends with the resident "pets". All those other people who keep insisting that he's outside are simply confused, and should be pitied and disregarded.

                            327) Syndicate's World [Quiet ; Conjunctional use of two or more of: Prime, Mind, Correspondence, Entropy] This Quiet affects True Mages who are either Technocrats or have reasons to think that the Syndicate has a positive attitude towards them – or those that hold hopes that the Syndicate might be friendly towards them. Naturally, members of the Syndicate are the most vulnerable to this Quiet.

                            This bus connection? Probably arranged by the Syndicate; for convenience, and as a favor. The bus driver, wearing a tie and listening happily to the radio? Surely a Provider of the Syndicate....perhaps even on the way to becoming an Associate. That hot dog stand? Quite likely it has been built by the Syndicate, as evidenced by the nice car parked nearby; the hot dog sauces might even have some of the Syndicate's expertise in them. The metro's elegant design, and the catchy phrases of the large adverts within? Certainly suggested by a Syndicate Associate well-placed within the city's council. This beautiful and fashionably dressed women, smoking a cigarette while she smiles at me as I exit the underground? Visibly a member of the Syndicate, performing a subtle Adjustment of encouraging me, and showing me that I'm in the Syndicate's good graces. The shopping mall in the center of the city? In one way or another property of the Syndicate. And in one of those skyscrapers? Surely a Syndicate Chair is sitting in her or his Construct, watching over countless Providers – current and future – going about their business in the world... Syndicate's World.

                            The affected True Mage has a tendency to see everything and everyone as part of the Syndicate. Considering that the Syndicate's agents and members, as well as their Adjustments and Procedures, are very subtle, this results in a dreamy outlook in which the whole web of interactions and events in the city (and beyond) are under the patronage and subtle – but firm – control of the Syndicate.

                            An integral pat of this Quiet is that the affected True Mage will not interact with the persons and the infrastructure they perceive as part of the Syndicate's World in any way other than how an average person on the streets would interact. After all, it would be disrespectful, and would somehow break the Coincidental charm of all of it. The Syndicate will surely - and rather sooner, than later - openly contact the True Mage, when the time comes.
                            Even if the True Mage – perhaps in moments of desperation, doubt, or in an attempt to initiate someone into the obvious secret of the Syndicate's World – tries to talk to one of the many “Syndicate members” or "agents" who they perceive, or to call upon the favors and the opportunities that are available in the “Syndicate businesses and locations”, and is met with a total lack of understanding and response, the Quiet can continue as the True Mage rationalizes away this one interaction, and returns to the behavior of not interacting with the Syndicate's World. At most they might wink at a cashier or a person wearing a business suit and carrying a business suitcase; or perhaps go on a shopping spree at a location they perceive as belonging to the Syndicate. Perhaps they will eat at the most expensive restaurants, trying to impress the Syndicate with their taste. Or maybe they will engage in small talk with many a stranger they see as a Provider; dropping subtle comments in regards to the medias' quality and/or the city's ongoing development.

                            This Quiet can place the affected True Mage in danger - for example they might perceive a location as totally safe, because they saw a patrol of two policemen who "Surely represent the Syndicate." ; while the location is not safe.

                            The Technocracy – and other Nightfolk, like the Kindred – have observed, to an extent, this Quiet; and some of them have tried to exploit it for good or ill. However, it never seems to work out; as the ones seeking to do so almost always end up underestimating the extent of the Syndicate's World perceived and felt by the affected True Mage.

                            Some Nephandi and Traditionalists have expressed opinions that nearly all the members of the Syndicate are living in this Quiet, in one way or another.

                                328) Bad Luck [Paradox Flaw ; Entropy] The affected True Mage will be suffering from a string of bad luck in regards to mundane details of her or his life. Keys get dropped and when they hit the floor they deform just enough to be useless, the only bus during the hour cruelly passes by the waiting Mage, the laptop overheats right before the saving of a file, the police pointlessly wastes the Mage's time by doing a detailed checkup...
                                The difficulty - or ease - of dealing with this Paradox Flaw depends on the amount and scale of the True Mage's use of the Sphere of Entropy.

                                        329) Credit Fraud [Flaw]: For the Syndicate, money IS magick. However, in the modern world, there's no need to actually touch cash. Everything is done through those miraculous plastic objects that fit inside the wallet: the credit or debit card. It is the Syndicate rep's magic wand...just, you know, yes Unmutual. So naturally, when a Syndicate rep does abuse the power of dosh one too many times - or uses it so unwisely as to bring down Market Correction - the law of probability steps in to punish them...by depriving of them their card. Somewhere along the line, their credit or debit card becomes lost, misplaced, dropped, or stolen, winding up in the hands of someone else. Someone who has no qualms about wracking up expenses to the operative's account. Even with the latest banking and credit integrations with technology, where a person can disable their card with just a phone app, this backlash ensures the Syndicate rep fails to notice their missing card until the damage has already been done.

                                        Such a Flaw is especially catastrophic if they happen to lose one of the Technocratic Union's special (read: Wonderous) expense cards. Because now the operative must not only deal with the financial loss, but with the Union's displeasure at losing a valuable resource.

                                            331) Suspicious Notes [Paradox Flaw ; Prime, Time, Mind, Entropy] This Paradox Flaw affects Technocrats of the New World Order most often. Any handwritten notes that the True Mage makes will, after being left out of sight, become rearranged into a reversed order - the last page being first, the second to last page being second, the first page being last, and so on. Printers used by the True Mage will print their output in this reversed order; and any computer files and documents that the True Mage opens will be opened on the last page (or, in files that have no pages, at the end) of the file.

                                                332) Seasonal Passage [Flaw]: Some Verbana theorize that when a witch travels from one location to another, they are briefly passing through the realm of Faerie, where space and distance are meaningless. While there is no concrete proof that Correspondence travel involves a shift into either Arcadia or some related Umbral realm, some witches who believe it can have the theory manifest in their Paradox. Opening a door between one place and another means traveling briefly through a fey realm, where the seasons reign supreme. And so, when that door opens, at the moment the witch travels, they leave evidence of their passage behind, at both ends. What that evidence is depends on the season they cast the Effect in.
                                                -Spring: Flower petals and fresh dewdrops
                                                -Summer: Vibrant green leaves and a sweltering heat
                                                -Autumn: Dried Fall leaves of reds, yellows, and oranges, and the smell of harvest feasts
                                                -Winter: Flurry of snowflakes and a bitter chill

                                                            335) Bendy Bod [Flaw]: Harking back to the golden age of animation, the mage's body bends, stretches, compresses, and otherwise moves in the exaggerated style of classic black and white cartoons. Often a backlash against general alterations to the mage's body. If the magician wished their form to be more flexible, Paradox will gladly acquiesce, and make the user as flexible and non-solid as can be.


                                                            336) Film Grain [Flaw]: Afflicting many an artist mage (especially those who use the medium of movies for their magick), the victim's vision distorts, as if they saw the world around them through the filter of old film stock. It can also jitter, manifest flickering spots of color at random, or seem to have inconsistent "framerate".
