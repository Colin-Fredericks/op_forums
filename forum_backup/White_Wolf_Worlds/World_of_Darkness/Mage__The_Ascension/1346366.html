    Finding Nodes, their Distribution, and Ownership

    11-06-2019, 01:04 AM
    Given this subjective question, how do you, or would you:


    1. Distribute Nodes (i.e. In exalted 1E Demesnes, the equivalent of Nodes, were distributed one per 25 square miles)

    2. Let players find a node (i.e. Massive Corr2/Prime2 Ritual?)

    3. Determine if the Node is owned, or at least known and monitored?

    4. What do you do for purposes of immersion in your game on this subject?

    5. Factor in that in the entire TU, there are many Enlightened Scientists who have Correspondence 2, Prime 2 who can cast huge workings over large areas to identify all non-hidden nodes/caerns?

    Obviously this is chronicle dependent, personal preferences and interpretations etc. What I'm curious to talk about is other people's thinking process on this.

      Well, like you said, this is naturally subjective but I'll take a pitch at it.

      1. So nodes are essentially pools of mana which are tapped into by Mages. They tend to be connected to chantries and sanctums but not always. We know that energy in the World of Darkness pools in a variety of ways and I'd say that rather then an exact ratio, I'd think more through the perspective of history and energy. Places that spirits congregate, places where faithful gathered for centuries, places of inspiration, places of evil, that sort of thing. Given enough time, Mages will gather and grow more wells of mana and fight over control of them, as well as rivaling the Changing Breeds & Fae who would claim them. So, naturally, create as many as needed for your chronicle. Aside from that though, consider how many places of true significance exist in the area and think about how this effects the politics of scarcity within the domain.

      2. I'd say that most Mages with any degree of Prime can feel the presence of a node and the properly attuned mortal can probably feel something as well. However to search for one and identify its location, after a mage has naturally found a method of leveraging their paradigm into it, I would say that yeah some sort of Prime/Correspondence ritual, perhaps P2/C2 extended to find one. I'm not a big crunch guy but I'm giving this my best guest.

      3. While the possibility of dormant, buried, and/or hidden nodes are certainly there, they are likely to be the minority anywhere that civilization gathers. Mage factions, not to mention other supernatural factions, no doubt have claimed the nodes of the area and attempt to actively occupy them. If the mages and their sorcerer allies don't have the juice to hold one, the fae and Changing Breeds will unleash a great deal of ferocity to claim and repurpose the nodes.

      4. Give each node a specific flavor and feeling, perhaps it's own history at well to some sort. Also consider who has claimed each node of energy and how they interact with it. How they may have altered it. Different factions will have different attitudes. However, perhaps the most important things for providing some natural drama is that there will almost certainly be fewer wells of energy then their will be interested parties and that sort of competition breeds all sorts of political, intrigue, and dramatic story fodder.

      Well, hope these were a help!

                          1. In the games I've run, most of the nodes I've made are either pre-built and part of the story or a fun magical interpretation of existing places. Most of these will have something in it, be it a mage owner, a supernatural guardian, or those pesky kids who hang out in the weird abandoned house. I tend to go with the idea that every village or town has something like at least a weak node in it and the only ones unoccupied are fairly new or really hidden and inaccessible. Places of consequences to the world like cities and big patches of wilderness or places with significant legend will have more numerous and powerful nodes, maybe each district will have something or more than one something.

                          2. In my games finding one isn't too hard with Prime 1 or even just guesswork and research. Claiming one is what tends to be a pain. Typically there would be some ghost, spirit, were-creature, mage, technocracy agent, or hedge mage cult in any node of significance or else some reason no one goes there and takes it as theirs (like it's an active volcano or lies on the mage politics equivalent of no man's land)

                          3. It typically is easy to learn if it's owned in my games. If the local Traditions know about it. They'll tell you. If you happen upon an owned one, there will be defenses or at least signs of habitation. If it's simply monitored though, and the local Traditions folk don't know of it, you probably wont know without good magical or investigative or magical investigative skills.

                          4. When traveling or reading or watching shows about interesting locations, I sometimes try to think, "if this were Mage, what sort of nodes would likely be here" and then make stuff up

                              First, if a Node is claimed, those with the means to do so will hide it from magical senses. Neither Technocrats nor Traditionalists have any interest advertising their places of power to the other side, and other mages will want to avoid having theirs claimed by either side in the Ascension War. Same with Werewolves, and probably Changelings and Wraiths, too. If a mage wants to go looking for Nodes with Prime, they'll need to exceed the number of successes rolled by the Effect hiding the Node. This is likely a high bar, since well-entrenched groups/individuals have doubtless taken the time to cast potent wards.

                              These defenses against magical sensing, moreover, likely persist long after the site was lost or abandoned. Effects meant to cloak the Node's presence are built to last. A Node might remain undiscovered for decades or even centuries after their last owners were driven off or died out. Only revealed by a chance encounter, or after the Effect wore down enough that Prime senses could pierce it.


                              Second, unless you have conjunctional Correspondence, you as a mage might not be able to sense a Node's presence with Prime unless you're right on top of it. Magical senses, without Correspondence, are limited in range to one's natural sense radius (line of sight, within earshot, etc). Dense forests, rock formations, deep caves, or crowded buildings could serve to provide barriers to senses, both physical and metaphysical. (For, indeed, Magic is Metaphor, and a physical barrier can be a magical one very easily).

                              For this reason, I like the idea that finding a Node is not as simple as the game mechanics make it out to be. Correspondence senses generally require Sympathetic connections, area knowledge (in the spatial and geographic sense), or lots of data (in the case of the Data alternate Sphere). Finding a Node may involve a great deal of surveying, referencing maps, studying local legends, and using good old occult detective work. Charting leylines and interviewing elders (or spirits). The more nuggets of "data" (whatever a mage considers data relevant to magical location), the easier it is to establish a viable Correspondence connection, and therefore the easier it is to find the Node. Even the unAwakened can find Nodes this way, though those without "Mana" tapping Paths or knowledge of employing Tass may have trouble tapping the resource fully.

                              More powerful Nodes leave a larger "footprint" in the magical landscape - the same mystical factors that allowed the Node to exist in the first place also point investigators toward it - making them easier to find. Doubtless the most potent Nodes lie in someone's hands already, both for the vibrancy of their auras and how obvious they are to locate through indirect means. Conversely, weaker Nodes are more easily overlooked, because their Quintessence output is weaker and because fewer features in the physical and occult landscape point to them. This, incidentally, is how relatively weak mages (or other groups) can get their hands on it, even a Cabal of street-level PCs.


                              Third, how many Nodes are around and how hard they are fought over is relative to how potent Nodes are in the Storyteller's game, and whether the chronicle is meant to be "High-Magic" or "Low-Magic".

                              A High-Magic game means Nodes give 5 to 10 Quintessence per dot in the Background, and there may be many Nodes around. While Nodes still factor into the Ascension War - because no amount of Quint is ever enough, when there's so many uses it can be turned to - it won't necessarily be the single driving force, because magic is relatively plentiful.

                              Whereas in a Low-Magic game, Nodes may give only 1 to 2 points per dot, and they might be few and far between. In that case, every single Node is an invaluable resource, that mages on all sides will fight tooth and nail to claim. That would also mean some mages would use Prime + Correspondence to search out Nodes, no matter how small, in order to get an edge.


                              The degree of scarcity for Quintessence will also have a knock-on effect for other factors in a chronicle's magical landscape. Fewer Quintessence points mean mages take fewer risks, as they have only so much Quint to smooth over spellcasting. Magical acts will be methodical and involve a great deal more high ritual, mages looking to secure as many advantages as they can before committing to an Effect. Lowering the difficulty of Effects in other ways, in lieu of "greasing the wheel" with Quint.

                              Magical items require Quintessence to craft, meaning the number of such things in circulation is directly proportional to how many Nodes are about and how powerful those Nodes can be per dot. In Low-Magic games, permanent Wonders are rare, and Charms even rarer (because every mage has an incentive to save their Tass for big projects). Entire storylines might revolve around tracking down a single Wonder of middling potency, simply because it's so rare. (Characters may even be willing to endure a Wonder with a lot of flaws or curses, simply because beggers can't be choosers). Conversely, an abundance of Nodes, and the potency thereof, would lead to many Wonders being present, relatively speaking.

                              Familiars and other Bygones depend on Quintessence to survive in the material world. Scrounging for Quint in drips and drabs can only do so much. Low-Magic games will, obviously, have fewer fantastic beasts or monsters, whereas High-Magic ones will have more. Whether those creatures are attached to mages or on their own, they take the presence and potency of Nodes very seriously. A Node not known to mages or other supernaturals may be claimed as the home of a Bygone, which leads to the classic "defeat the monster to claim the prize" type adventures.

                              Finally, if there are fewer and weaker Nodes, Junctures (times or events where Quintessence flows more freely) become more vital to mages. They have more incentive to observe times and moments of great mystical or emotional import, and may work hard to both engineer them and control them (so no one else can tap the Juncture for Quint). Celestial alignments, mass media tentpoles, flash mobs, music festivals, local or national tragedies, raucous parties, religious holidays, and shifts in the stock market can all be opportunities knowing mages can exploit.
