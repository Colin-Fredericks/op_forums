    How does your table handle the interactions between Magick and violence?

    08-19-2023, 05:09 AM
    Mage: the Ascension, and especially M20, has a number of options that combine in interesting/useful ways to produce different outcomes when using magick in combat. There’s quite a few interactions, though, and I thought it might be worth seeing what other groups have been doing, and what their choices about how to apply and/or interpret the rules have done for their games. Thinking this, I have devised a series of scenarios and related questions that I would be interested in hearing others’ answers to (and maybe others would too, especially if there are differences between groups). If you care to expand upon your answers, please do.

    I’m not answering myself at the moment, partly because it’s probably best for the questioner to immediately bias the responses by giving my own answers, but mostly because in at least some of these cases, I haven’t yet decided what my answer will be for my upcoming game.

    For all examples, suppose a mage uses a Martial Arts practice, and uses the Martial Arts ability (in conjunction with whatever other elements of Focus are considered necessary at this table) in order to produce effects that qualify as “magick is the attack and the attack provides the magick” per page 413 of M20 (and similar language on the following couple of pages), such that our example mage can deliver magically-empowered punches.

    So, question 1: M20 allows this type of mage to use an act of violence to cast a magick effect, all in one round as one action (but two rolls), but this is new, and similar in previous editions needed two actions (either split across multiple rounds, or using that edition’s rule for multiple actions). Given that, do you prefer to:
    • allow the attack and Arete rolls as a single action, as per M20; or,
    • stick with the requirement for separate actions, as per older editions?
    After that, I’m going to assume that Violence as a Focus, Magick Enhances Violence, and Violence Enhances Magick are all options for our example mage (and assuming the answer to Q1 is the first option - use the M20 rule - he could use Magick Enhances Violence to roll Arete and reduce the difficulty of his punch by 1 per success, as a fairly simple effect using any of various spheres).

    Now, further suppose that the mage has a simple rote using Prime 2 that allows spending a point of Quintessence to infuse his fist with Quintessence and allow his punch to inflict aggravated damage. Under this scenario…

    Question 2: How many successes would you require to achieve the basic effect of the rote?
    • 1 success, because by imbuing the ability to inflict aggravated damage into his own fist, only the mage is being directly affected; or,
    • 2 successes, because while his own fist is being enchanted, that enchantment is about to cause somebody else to receive aggravated damage?
    Question 3: If the mage rolls extra successes, what would you allow to be achieved with those extra successes?
    • Using Magick Enhances Violence, each extra success would reduce the attack roll by 1;
    • Nothing, as you would not let a rote producing another effect also use the Magick Enhances Violence option;
    • Something else?
    Question 4: If the mage fails the Arete roll but succeeds on the attack roll, would you have the mage roll ordinary bashing damage for the punch against his target?

    I will presume that Violence Enhances Magick is not an option with this rote, because if the successes from the attack are being used to decrease the difficulty of the Arete roll, there is nothing inflicting damage at all, so the rote’s effect is just wasted. I suppose someone might be able to come up with a way to split a dice pool in a way that slightly increases the odds of inflicting aggravated damage, but pool splitting is generally just going to produce a worse outcome here.

    And then switching up the scenario again, suppose the mage has a Prime 3 rote that allows him to spend a point of Quintessence to inflict aggravated damage directly (per the Base Damage or Duration table), delivered via a punch. This will certainly require at least 2 successes on the Arete roll as a base, as this is how many successes are required to inflict damage on a target.

    Question 5: Would you allow the use of Violence Enhances Magic when using this rote?
    • Yes, and each success on the Martial Arts attack roll would reduce the difficulty of the Arete roll by 1; or,
    • Yes, but since the punch must land to inflict the damage, 1 success goes to landing the hit (or 1 more successes than the target rolled in defence, if appropriate). Successes beyond that reduce the difficulty of the Arete roll by 1.
    • No; any extra successes on the attack roll achieve nothing;
    • No, but any extra successes on the attack roll will add an extra die of damage.
    • Something else?
    Question 6: Would you allow the use of Magick Enhances Violence when using this rote?
    • No, extra successes should all go to increasing damage;
    • Yes, I suppose, if the player really just wants to inflict some damage, and use extra successes to lower the difficulty of the attack roll to ensure the punch lands;
    • Hell yeah, all successes count towards both damage and reducing the difficulty of the attack roll;
    • Something else?
    Question 7: If just using the basic Violence As a Focus approach, do extra successes on the attack roll do anything?
    • No;
    • Yes, each extra success adds a die to the damage;
    • Something else?
    I can think of parallel scenarios when using Abilities Enhancing Magick and Magick Enhancing Abilities options where similar questions would apply, but I think the answers (for each table) could be largely extrapolated from answers to the above questions, as the mechanics are similar but with somewhat less twists.

              I agree with Lord Revan's answer on everything except #5; and even there, it may just be a misunderstanding. I don't think that this particular Rote (using Prime 2 to change your damage to aggravated) is compatible with Violence Enhancing Magick because there's nothing about the mundane action that makes the Magick more effective. Look at the examples in M20 (which, unfortunately, I can't right now, because I don't have the book handy); you'll see that there are three different Rotes being described, one for each example; and the one for “violence enhances Magick” doesn't involve an Effect that converts damage to aggravated, per se; IIRC, it operates by the mage arranging to surround his hands in something flammable which he then ignites, taking advantage of the fact that vampires take aggravated damage from fire.

              And as Lord Revan points out, you can't double-dip or triple-dip: if you're using the violence as Focus, you don't get to lower the difficulty of either roll based on the result of the other roll; and if you use either Violence Enhances Magick or Magick Enhances Violence, you don't get to use the violence as Focus — meaning that you're performing two actions (one for the violence and another for the Effect's Focus) and will most likely have to perform those actions on separate turns

                      Well, that’s the question, isn’t it? Because the book says this:
                      Originally posted by M20, page 414, third paragraph under Violence Enhances Magick
                      Unless the magick and the act involve a single activity (as in firing a gun or throwing a punch) an attack used to focus and enhance magick needs to be performed before that spell is cast. If the player wants to use both the attack and the magick in the same turn – say, an Infernalist who wants to open a gate at the moment of sacrifice – then she needs to either divide up her dice pool between them (as detailed under Multiple Actions in Chapter Eight, p. 388), or else use Time 3 to get extra actions within the same turn.
                      So, certainly there are cases where the rules are explicit about several actions being needed (one to use an act of violence, and a second to cast the magick effect), but it also carves out an exception for cases where both are performed as a single activity. Throwing a punch is even one of the two examples of such a case in the text, and the mystical martial artist is the pre-eminent archetype for this.

                      However, if we do permit Violence Enhances Magick to be used with a single action, I think it’s legitimate to wonder whether Violence as a Focus would ever be used. Violence Enhances Magick is simply Violence as a Focus with a difficulty reduction in the Arete roll, mechanically.

                      And this is why I have asked about this: to find out what others are doing in practice, and perhaps learn about the effects that that has had in their games.

                          Violence as a Focus lets you make a separate Magick roll the activate the Effect and then a full Attack roll to hit the target in a single turn. If you want to make both a Magick roll and an Attack roll in a single turn using either Magick Enhances Violence or Violence Enhances Magick, you need to use the Multiple Actions rule (pp.388–389): you get a single dice pool to split between the actions, and it's the smaller of the two pools that you'd normally get if you weren't trying to squeeze them into a single turn. This will most likely be your Arete pool; and if it isn't, it's probably because your Attribute+Ability pool sucks. This pretty much cements Violence as a Focus as a viable alternative to the others, with a handful of exceptions:

                          Using Bullet Time to get around that is nice in theory; in practice, though, not so much: first, you need to have Time 3, which is in no way guaranteed. Second, you still need to take an action to focus the Bullet Time Effect; only then do you get to make an Arete roll to get additional actions on a subsequent turn: one for every two successes you roll. The Difficulty starts at 6, if you can justify it as Coincidental; and there really aren't that many options available for lowering the Difficulty. That means that you'll need Arete 4 or 5 before you can reliably achieve two successes for a single extra action.

                          A third possibility would be to allow the crafting of an Effect to be done using a Reflexive Action under limited circumstances. Obviously, the Practice and Instruments would need to be appropriate; and I usually wouldn't allow it for anything more advanced than a Simple Feat (one success); and even there, I'd be inclined to restrict its use. Mainly, I'd use this for sensory or defensive Effects, as those tend to be rendered moot if you can't perform them reflexively; that is, Effects that are responses to time-critical events should generally be available through a Reflexive Action.

                          I'd also allow Bullet Time as a Reflexive Action, as it too gets rendered moot if you have to spend even a Simple Action casting it; see above.

                          If you allow any casting of magick that enhances violence as a Reflexive Action, then Magick Enhances Violence becomes the superior option to Violence as a Focus as one turn's worth of activity. As this tends to render Violence as a Focus moot, my inclination is not to allow it.

                          The one other time that I'd allow a casting to be done as a Reflexive Action would be if the mage chooses to Work Without Focus or to Surpass his Instruments. In this case, the mage isn't doing anything to craft the Effect; he's not even meditating. And as such, he should be able to achieve any Effect that he can get enough successes for on a single Arete roll, as a Reflexive Action.

                          ​​​​​​​

                              To be fair, all three modes can have the focus being the action. Actually, all spells require the time necessary to employ their focus, the Arete roll is not an action. So yeah, you can just punch someone and choose which one of the 3 options (or none) you will use:

                              - Violence as a focus: The focus of the spell is an act of violence and you roll attack and Arete. A failed Arete still deals the regular attack damage. A failed attack deals no damage on the target. Both Arete and attack successes deals the Arete x2 regular dmg according to damage table.

                              -Violence Enhances Magick: The mage makes an act of violence to enhance the magick, just like Abilities Enhancing Magick, and again, if the ability is the focus of the spell, it's only one turn: roll your ability, in this example, the ability is an attack. So you roll it and each success reduce the Arete diff by 1 (max 3). So, failed ability means no reduction in diff (a botch might mess with the spell at ST call) and failed Arete means no dmg.

                              -Magick Enhances Violence: It's just like the regular spell to reduce some ability difficulty. You roll Arete and each success reduce the ability diff by 1 (max. 3). In the example, the ability is an attack. So, a failed Arete means no reduction in diff. A failed attack means no damage.
