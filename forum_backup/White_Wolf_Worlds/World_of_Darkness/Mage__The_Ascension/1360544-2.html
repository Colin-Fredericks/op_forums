    To me, the line that makes me think “hopeless setting” is “past the point of no return”: the Traditions and Technocracy are so far gone in their respective corruption that there's no saving them. The Nephandi aren't just winning; they've already won — it's just that the final steps to ultimate destruction haven't yet finished playing out. The world has already crossed a line from which there's no coming back, and the end result is going to be the same no matter what. That's about as bleak and hopeless as things get.

    There's a difference between “the Traditions and Technocracy have been compromised, and can't be trusted or relied upon as they are now” and “they're so far gone that there's no saving them”. The former is desperate but has a ray of hope; the latter is just bleak.

        House Canon/Setting Themes
        Generally, we follow DA:M and the Revised supplements in terms of setting history. The Craftmasons and Cabal of Pure Thought split off from their respective Fellowships around the 13th century, etc.

        When we have threads like this, I tend to think that I "agree with Dataweaver from the opposite direction." I think of paradigms as paradigms (which is to say, as models). The Union's paradigm isn't delusional - there's a lot of ways to talk about your Avatar, and all mages think reality has rules. Rather, the Union's unusual position comes from its doctrinal rigidity ("everyone must conform to our model") and the inner circle's desire to suppress any discovery (scientific or sorcerous) which threatens its power - neither of these are necessary parts of a Unionist's paradigm. But I very specifically don't draw any meaningful or mechanical distinction between science and magic.

        We have two long-running "continuities" in which our chronicles and one-shots are run. They have slightly different thematic elements, with the metaphysical trinity being more important in one (and the moral compass being more concerned with balance than niceness) while the other is more Manichean and Christian in tone (primarily used for one-shots in the Colonial Americas and other historical eras.) In the latter, we also use the old proposed Victorian Mage idea about the Trads/Conventions being deep in the background, so the various characters aren't really privy to the Ascension War or Horizon Realms, etc.

        We tend to run mono-Trad oneshots, often in historical eras, so the "splats" we use for those tend to be subfactions. I've done some work on these to broaden their scope or make them more interesting, so that you can have a diverse cast within the bounds of a single Trad.

        There are some historical groups we've added, early fellowships which didn't directly reform into a Trad or Convention. Some of these are envisioned as being fairly influential even though they never achieved political power - basically, these groups live on through their discoveries, iconography, and rotes, instead of as a political unit.

        Rules Stuff
        We've been adopting the translation guides and nWoD rules over time, and now basically use them full-time. I have a nauseatingly large "playbook" for running Ascension using a version of Awakening 2e.

                    Since my chantries are all earth based, I had to fiddle with a few of the famous ones, and ended up inventing a few to serve as central chantries for some of the Traditions.
                    The Akashic Brotherhood's Akashic Library remains mostly unchanged; it's a construct of the mind - and of Mind magic - that exists within the minds of all members of the Tradition. And because that includes both past and future members, it is entirely possible for those with enough understanding of Time magic to meet and converse with Brothers from other eras. (Their more normal chantries include the Temple of Hidden Truth in Tibet, the Eight Dragons Trading Company in Singapore, and the Golden Drum School in Sao Louis Brazil.)

                    The Celestial Chorus's Curia and Pontifex Maximus operate out of the Temple of the Sun, originally founded by Mentu-hetep in ancient Egypt, and now co-located in sites in Rome, Iran, Ethiopia, Peru, Japan, Arizona, and Australia. (Others include the Temple of Brahma's Footprint in India, the Hidden Catacombs beneath Jerusalem, and the Theban Club in Philadelphia.)

                    Balador is a combination French Salon, "Oriental" pleasure garden, and modern nightclub that exists outside the flow of time and space, serving as the home of the Shakti Divya, Marianna, the Cult of Ecstasy's spiritual heart, and a popular gathering place for those Awakened who know how to get on the guest list. (Others include the Hidden Dream Village of Nepal, the Temple of Sapho in Greece, the Rose Luck Club in Seoul, South Korea, and the nomadic Coyote Caravan of the American Southwest.)

                    During the Grand Convocation, the Dreamspeakers established the House of the Clan Mothers (named in honor of Naioba), a grand meeting lodge that exists simultaneously in thirteen locations around the globe. These locations are determined by the ritual placement of thirteen sacred artifacts, each one entrusted to a different Dreamspeaker Master or Archmage. (Other important sites include Boolarung, an Australian songline that takes those who follow it outside of mundane space and time, the hidden West African village of Olodumare, the Great Turtle Lodge in Quebec, and the Temple of the Eternal Hearth in Greece.)

                    Vajra Coumatha, the Eternal Crossroads, is both a construct of the mind and a physical place outside of mundane time and space that can only be reached by those who are fated to reach it. It has served as a sacred meeting place for Thanatoic Masters for countless centuries. (More conventional chantries include the Great Wheel Marabout in the foothills of the Himalayas, Southern Africa's Ghost Citadel, Iceland's Well of Mimir, and London's Locksmiths' Club.)

                    For centuries, the ancient fortress chantry of Doissetep was the Order of Hermes's pride, the heart of the Order, and meeting place of the Grand Council. Its recent destruction1 has left the Order in something of an upheaval. (Surviving strongholds include Milan's Harco Piazza, the secret library of Seshat in Alexandria, Egypt, the Red Griffin Inn in England, and the Pyrander Club in Washington, DC.)

                    Established in the 19th century, Victoria Station is a secret hidden part of London's Underground that uses EtherTube transportation systems to connect to locations around the world. It is host to the head offices and publishing center for Paradigma, and meeting place for the Tradition's ruling Council of Deans. (Other places of note include the converted New York City speak easy that now serves as the exclusive Gibson Club, the Kimota Observatory in Australia's Outback, California's Time-Stop Laboratories, and the Hidden Island of Doctor X in the Pacific Ocean.)

                    Arcadian Grove is the most sacred of sacred groves, believed to date back to the time of the Wyck and only accessible by those initiated into the ways of the Wyck and familiar with the secret hidden paths that lead to it. Leaders of the Verbena have met there since prehistory. (Other places of power include Woodwich Downs, the family estate of Lady Charlotte Quay in England, the Temple of Demeter in Greece, Tara Plantation in Georgia, and the Cromby Farm in Washington State.)

                    The Crystal Palace is one of the oldest and most secure fortresses in the Digital Web, and a cornerstone of the Virtual Adepts since it was established in the 1940s. It is the center of the Adepts' and even the Traditions' efforts in the Web, and open to any Virtual Adept once they've been initiated. Many areas of the Palace, however, require additional security codes and clearances to access. (Other Digital sites include the youth hipster hangout known as the Crashpad, the mysterious safe-house Dark Side, and the techno-geek paradise of Vulcan. A rare meat-world chantry of note is The Bunker, a hidden doomsday shelter at an unidentified location in the Nevada desert.)

                    The Hollow Ones lack any real central chantry, but a number of locals have gained fame - or infamy - among those in the know. These include the Waydown, the wandering outlaw rave of the greater San Fransisco Bay Area, the haunted Chicago mansion known as the Sepulcher, and the underground London sex shop and nightclub Guilty Pleasures. But the most famous is Grimme's Occult Specialty Shoppe, a small and colorful curiosity that seems to exist simultaneously in dozens of cities around the western world.

                    With the recent destruction of Doissetep, there was (in my games) a need to establish a new meeting place for the Grand Council and create a symbol of hope for Mages going forward in the Ascension War. That place is Horizon, the brain child of Archmage Porthos Fitz-Empress. My default setting for it is outside of Seattle, but I can easily move it to wherever I feel like setting my games. This isn't the Horizon of the sourcebook by that name, but a recent construction that takes full advantage of Correspondence and other magics to fit a massive amount of material into a small space.

                                    I enjoy the Sons of Ether quite a bit, however my take on them differs slightly from canon.

                                    They are still scientists and explorers seeking to understand the true nature of reality while rebelling against the tyrannical conformity of the Technocracy, inspired by the ancient works of Aretus and his writings about lost Atlantis (ie, the world that existed before the "shattering" that led to the current state of reality), and based on the philosophy that Reality is stranger than one can imagine, as it is infused with Ether, the very material of the Tapestry itself, which can be manipulated through proper use of Science and ingenuity. However, they've never been part of the Technocracy, not even in the Victorian period. (Though certain strains of their history do tie in with the medieval Craftmasons.) In my games, when the Technocracy was formed in 1851, a number of those enlightened scientists invited rejected the idea that the masses - and with it their science and technology - needed to be controlled and shepherded by their elite betters. To these dissenters, this mindset flew in the very face of open and honest scientific inquire and the advancement of mankind's understanding of the universe and their place in it. They formed their own Scientific society, The Electrodyne Engineers. After The Great War and the colossal failure of alleged Technocratic expertise in managing human politics and conflict (as well as the noble but doomed quixotic effort by Czar Vargo to avert the war through force), the Engineers, through one of their leaders and heroes Doc Eon, form an alliance with the Nine Mystical Traditions, formally joining in the 1920s. (This brings there number back to nine, as I don't have the Ahl I Batin leaving until WW2.)

                                    The name is a matter of some contention within the Tradition. Traditionalists demand on preserving the "Sons of" name that the group created to mark its formal alliance with the Traditions, however, since the rise of second wave feminism in the 1960s, there have been those advocating (and demanding) a change in the name. Many younger members in the 21st centuries refer to themselves as the Society of Ether, but even this isn't enough to satisfy some. (One reason I keep the "Sons" part of the name has to do with one of the factions I mention below.)

                                    Their key Sphere is still Matter, and one of the reasons all of them have Matter 1 to start with is that part of their initiation is to build their first set of Ether Goggles, allowing them to better observe Ether and its effects on the material world. These Ether Goggles become one of their first foci. Other common foci tend to involve scientific meters and tools, specialized alloys and chemicals, experimental weapons systems and vehicles, strange mental disciplines and exercise programs, bizarre mathematical and chemical formula, and mind boggling engineering schematics and blue prints. Almost all of the above tend to be self-designed and built. Their sanctums are mostly private labs and workshops, but also include exclusive social clubs and secret hidden lairs. Their acolytes tend to be extremely openminded and courageous lab assistants, personal assistants who can take care of all the boring stuff that distracts one from Science!, would be sidekicks to science adventurers, and engineered robots and bio-forms. The weakness of the Tradition lies in their excessive enthusiasm and eccentricity. (Basic concepts: Daredevil test pilot, Eugenic superman, Obsessive cryptozoological conservationist, Steampunk adventuress, Time-traveling doctor.)

                                    My Etherites have four main factions, each with a number of subsections.

                                    L'Grand Collegium serves as the primary organizational body of the Sons of Ether. Organized along the lines of a college or academy, they are responsible for overseeing and enforcing the Protocols of Science (the rules and guidelines that govern the Tradition), recruiting and training new Scientists, and conducting diplomacy with the other Traditions and other outsiders.
                                    • L'Academie Ether - devoted to the sacred and often thankless task of recruiting and teaching the next generation of young Etherites, members embed themselves in various schools, universities, research labs, and technical facilities to try and find promising minds before the Technocracy can corrupt them; They teach their charges the basics of Etheric Science, ethics, history, and Ascension War survival skills.
                                    • Paradigma Publications - the publication and propaganda arm of the Collegium and the Tradition as a whole, maintaining quasi-hidden websites and running a print on demand book service; Members work as reporters, correspondents, and investigators around the world and beyond, filing news reports, public interest pieces, and editorials.
                                    • Council for the Enforcement of Scientific Ethics - policing the Tradition for the taint and infiltration of the Nephandi, the Technocracy, and other malign influences, as well as more mundane cases of insanity, criminal behavior, and violation of the Protocols of Science; Members are expected to be skilled Scientists as well as having a working understanding of philosophy, law, forensics, and self-defense.
                                    • Right and Honourable Keepers of Etheric Tradition - a gentlemen's club and political society for arch-traditionalists devoted to preserving Victorian and pre-War mores of etiquette, ethics and values; This includes the ideals of honour, courage, and chivalry, as well as traditions of formal wear, high tea, and dueling; Members firmly believe in such outmoded concepts as duty, patriotism, and good manners while insisting on suitably elegant stylings - Steampunk, Art Deco, and Silver Age Science Fiction - to one's Scientific creations. (Though as younger members join, optimistic 1980s styles are becoming acceptable as well.) The current leader of the society is the bi-centenarian engineer, explorer, and metallurgical alchemist Professor Lord Primium.
                                    • Society for a Better Tomorrow - utopian activists working to use Science! for the betterment of all humankind, eliminating need and want, disease, war, poverty, and misery; Members want to create a world of social justice and equality, but are prone to a form of passive-aggressive pushiness and often ignoring human nature.
                                    (Concepts: Globe-trotting Paradigma reporter; Utopian idealists trying to design the City of Tomorrow; Victorian throwback obsessed with decorum and manners; Rookie agent of the Science Police; Smooth-talking academic recruiter; Over-worked teacher's aide; Well-respected traditionalists carrying on a secret romance with a progressive anarchist.)

                                    The Ethernautical Society are daring explorers eager to push forward the boundaries of knowledge and possibility into new dimensions, lost lands, and strange frontiers. These undiscovered countries must be protected from exploitation and harm by the Technocracy and other threats. Members are constantly seeking new and exiting avenues of exploration and adventures into the furthest secret corners of Reality.
                                    • The Cryptozoological Conservation Committee - a collective of naturalists, folklorist, and veterinarians dedicated to studying and preserving Bygones and their habitats; They are ready, willing, and able to trek to the most remote and dangerous corners of the globe for their charges.
                                    • The Etherjammers - taking their name from the umbral ship of the legendary Pulp Era explorer and adventurer Captain Tiberius, they work in teams (Cabals) to create and man Etherships to cross the Gauntlet and explore the hidden corners and outermost reaches of the Otherworlds; Members specialize in cosmic and cross dimensional navigation, xenobiology and xenopsychology, Ethernautical engineering, and defensive combat. (These Etherships and their crews are more akin to something like Firefly's Serenity or Star Wars's Millennium Falcon rather Star Trek's various ships; small ships with small, tightly knit Cabal crews rather than massive battleships.)
                                    • The Metaphysical Research Society - investigating the hidden mysteries and untapped potentials of the human mind; Members specialize in psychobiology, metaphysics, psychogenesis, psychometrics, and psychochemistry, experimenting with mind-altering chemicals, meditation techniques, and methods of mental projection; Some of their research also involves trying to help those Etherites who have fallen into mental instability, as well as trying to gain a better understanding of Marauders and Changelings.
                                    • The College of Chronoarchaeology - as much a part of the Collegium as the Society, they delve into the mysteries of the past and the future, with a special focus on secret history, conspiracy theories, and the true nature of the universe's creation (and possible end), as well as the study of future technology and events, and unlocking the secrets of time travel. Their headquarters is the Time-Stop Laboratories in Southern California.
                                    • Cyber-Liberte - cybernaut explorers of the Digital Web and of the mundane internet; Strongly allied with the Virtual Adepts, they are staunch advocates of free speech and freedom of information; The group has always had a strange affection for both pirate and French Revolution motifs, frequently styling their Web avatars with buccaneer or pre-Jacobian themes, and seeing themselves as adventurers on the high seas and back alleys of the Digital Web, preying on the data of the Technocracy; Members tend to design and build their own computers and VR rigs, some of which confound even the Virtual Adepts.
                                    (Concepts: Professional Bigfoot hunter; Time-lost anachronism trying to find his way home; Swashbuckling pirate queen of the space lanes; Self-proclaimed Master of Mental Mysteries; Conspiratorial historian determined to expose The Truth; Radical ecological terrorist defending Bygone habitats; Mentally unstable necro-naut.)

                                    The Adventurers' Club are a social organization for those staunchly dedicated to the use of Science! in the active and resolute battle against evil and injustice. Its members act as detectives, science heroes, and vigilantes, battling the foes with high tech wizardry and a firm punch to the jaw. Their headquarters and central meeting place is the Gibson Club, a former New York City speakeasy that was converted into a private club in the 1930s.
                                    • The Crimebusters - focused on waging a war against mundane crime and corruption, as well as that resulting from supernatural influences, primarily at a local street level, but some target white collar crime and government corruption; Members favor a mix of applied psychological warfare and custom made high-tech weaponry and gadgets; While many of the original members were willing to take the lives of criminals with little hesitation, there has been.a divide on the issue of lethal justice for the past several decades.
                                    • The Guardians of Tomorrow - futurists, prognosticators, and predictive scientists working to stop crises before they develop, sometimes with mixed results; They also work to foster a more optimistic and positive view of the future in mundane society, through media, technological development, and inspirational actions.
                                    • The Exemplars - a fringe faction of self-made supermen and women, touting their mental, physical, and moral superiority as worthy of emulation by the rest of humanity; While most are well meaning and genuine, their perceived arrogance tends to rub others the wrong way.
                                    • The Ladies' Science Circle & Adventure Society - a long standing social club founded in the Victorian era for female Science! adventuresses, including detectives, reporters, aviators, explorers, swashbucklers of the stars, computer hackers, and other thrill seekers; They tirelessly work to maintain and publicize the historical achievements of female Etherites and Adventurers to help inspire future generations.
                                    (Concepts: Annoyingly optimistic science fiction writer; Faceless urban vigilante trying to stay true to a code against killing; Square-jawed throw-back to the thrilling days of Shere Damn Manly Science Adventure!; Jet-setting detective with an endless bag of high-tech tricks; Obnoxiously paternalistic ubermensch; Mentally enhanced psychic seer; Complete fraud who made up all of his exploits.)

                                    The Progressivist Alliance is a loose collection of radicals, rebels, and revolutionaries united in their opposition to the dictates and limitations imposed by the Grand Collegium. They want to take their Science! and the world into strange, disturbing, and even dangerous directions.
                                    • The League of Scientific Anarchism - the core of the Alliance, radicals demanding a lessoning - if not outright abolishment - of the Protocols governing Scientific research and experimentation; They include sincere libertarians and anarchists, as well as mad scientists wishing to carry out their morally questionable research in peace.
                                    • The Vargo Collective - true believers in the vision of Czar Vargo, advocating the overthrow of all mundane governments and their replacement with an enlightened Etherite meritocracy (some also advocate the inclusion of select members of other Traditions), total global disarmament, the end of war, and the pursuit of Science! for purely peaceful and benevolent purposes; Since the 1960s, they've been divided over their adherence to Vargo's code against taking human life, with more radical members arguing for violent action against the Technocracy and other enemies.
                                    • The Electrodyne Divas - all womyn radical femynist movement firmly united in their opposition to the Tradition's outmoded and sexist phallocratic patriarchal society, beginning with the Tradition's name and moving down a very long list of grievances both real and imagined. Their outspoken and impressively intimidating leader is Dr. Amazonia, also a member of the Examplars and frequent publisher of scathing Paradigma editorials.
                                    • The Monster Makers - a small circle of fanatics obsessed with the creation of biological, mechanical, and spiritual monstrosities to unleash against the Technocracy, headless of collateral damage; Most of them have very short but interesting careers.
                                    • The Galateans - advocates for the rights and human treatment of artificially created lifeforms. including clones, androids, Prometheans, artificial intelligences, uplifted animals, and others (including mystical creations such as homunculi); While this movement tends to be socio-political and non-violent, some radicals take a much more direct approach in their activities.
                                    (Concepts: Radical female-supremest Science Amazon; Radical anarchist with a death-ray; Fanatical Vargoite willing to kill for world peace; Seemingly normal Scientist with a secret basement full of monsters; Forthright political advocate for freedom and discovery; Modern-day Frankenstein trying to raise his creation properly; Mentally unstable delver into Things Man Was Not Meant To Know.)

                                                The Kitab al-Alacir lays out the general philosophy of Ether Science. Everything exists, everything originates in The Ether. Humanity filters out most of the cosmos so that it makes sense; but limitless potential is always battering at the gates of the Consensus. Science draws from the well of the infinite to fashion a more complete model. Eventually, Sleepers will be able to understand the entire majesty of Creation; the Wall of Troy will encompass it all.

                                                Sons of Ether Tradition Book, p.55
                                                This is the essence of the Etherites; not Ether. That said:
                                                The Eternal Ether
                                                The only common root is the Ether, the basic medium of the universe. Ether takes an infinite number of forms and occupies all space and time, but it is defined by what it is not. It isn't a mental construction, a substance or a force. It is the place from which everything — even Quintessence — rises into existence, and where everything goes when it departs. All matter and energy is conserved.

                                                Aside from the fundamental Ether (what Scientists often call the “Ideal Ether”), other “Ethers” encompass partial states of material and metaphysical reality. Quintessence is held to be the “purest” of these. It is wholly metaphysical, only existing when perceived and acting when acted upon. Contiguous Ether is more base, since it encompasses the physical properties of distance and the relationship between objects. Etherspace is a series of dimensions that lie outside of the Consensus's “Wall of Troy”. It has physical features, but they lie outside normal human experience. Quantum Ether Theory holds that they are alternate universes that have developed due to humanity's rejection of certain cosmological principles.

                                                Sons of Ether Tradition Book, p.55
                                                This last point in particular ties into why the Sons of Ether call themselves that. They defected from the Union when the Void Engineers introduced their Tychoidean Cosmology model, which encouraged humanity's rejection of certain cosmological principles: it made it Union policy to embrace the divide between Conventional Space and Etherspace and to keep the Masses ignorant of the latter. Ultimately, the goal was to strengthen the barrier between the two to the point that Etherspace would be completely cut off from Conventional Space, and humanity could expand throughout Conventional Space without ever having to worry about intrusions from beyond. It was this dismissal of Etherspace and the underlying embrace of reductionism, not the Luminiferous Ether, that the Electrodyne Engineers took offense to and named themselves after when they left the Technocracy.
                                                True Ether
                                                If there is a “10th Sphere” for the Sons of Ether, it is their namesake. Ether is the fundament of the cosmos, and to discover its true nature underneath the phenomena it creates would surely raise a Scientist to the pinnacle of her calling. True Ether is more basic then even the Tellurian's Quintessential roots because it must include Paradox (which is not Quintessential, but reacts to Ether Science) and the methods of other Traditions. Ether holds the key to these alien secrets, and the Sons are eager to find it.

                                                It is said that to the real goal of all experiments is to touch this True Ether. The Sons' holy grail is a theory that explains all of the Ether's manifestations according to a single set of laws. This would describe the Ether fully — and with it, the true nature of the universe.

                                                Sons of Ether Tradition Book, p.60
                                                So yes, Ether is central to the Etherites — but not in the way most think. It's the ideal that they pursue, a goal of a grand unified theory of everything, not a substance or force that they manipulate. One could say that Matter and Forces can be thought of in terms of exploring the True Ether's manifestation through substances and forces, just as Correspondence is Contiguous Ether and Prime is Metaphysical Ether (and Entropy is arguably Quantum Ether, and Spirit is arguably fundamentally about Etherspace). Etheric Biology (the Life Sphere) has a tendency to incorporate notions such as Elan Vital, the idea that there is a “Life Force”. Instead of Etheric Biology, one might call this notion “Biological Ether”. (Likewise, Mind is generally envisioned in terms of “mental energy fields”, akin to Trinity Continuum's notion of Noetic Science.) And Time, of course, is Causal Ether (dealing with the fundamental nature of cause and effect) in the same way that Correspondence is Contiguous Ether. So, the Nine Spheres as seen by the Sons of Ether:

                                                Biological Ether (life energy)
                                                Causal Ether (a web of cause and effect)
                                                Contiguous Ether (connections that transcend distance)
                                                Energetic Ether (Luminiferous, Gravitic, etc.)
                                                Etherspace
                                                Material Ether
                                                Metaphysical Ether
                                                Noetic Ether (mental energy)
                                                Quantum Ether

                                                True Ether

                                                    As far as style goes, my take on the Sons of Ether is that for a bunch of scientists, they also tend to have a strong romantic streak to them. Besides the ethical devotion to honest scientific inquiry and discovery, and to expanding human potential, this also leads them to being more than a little flamboyant, or at least extremely enthusiastic about their pursuits and very attached to certain time periods that they either came of age in (the older ones) or have an attraction to as a symbol of the creativity and possibility of science that the Technocracy has stamped out (the younger retro ones). While some go full retro, I suspect most just favor incorporating certain elements into their designs or fashions. Those common styles likely being some of the following.
                                                    • Gothic-Punk: Probably the most common, almost to the default, and those who appear "normal". They dress like the average club crawler or young iconoclast, complete with piercings and tattoos, their gear looking like it started in a science lab but was finished in their garage workshop.
                                                    • New Wave Science: Those in love with the 80s and its strange mix of bright optimism and internal anxieties. Taking a lot of cues from Buckaroo Banzai, Back to the Future, and even Devo, as well as some of the aspects of Cyberpunk.
                                                    • Analog Punk: Grim and gritty do-it-yourself engineering of the pre-digital era, using vacuum tubes, transistors, and other older technology, largely as a backlash against how easily the Technocracy and its agents have been able to co-opt and abuse modern digital technology.
                                                    • Silver Age Science Fiction: A favorite of utopians looking for a nice "lean and clean" look to their work, it also frequently tends to involve the use of Atomic Energy (or ATOMICS!) in some fashion. Flying cars and phasers are also popular.
                                                    • Diesel Punk: Inspired by early German Expressionism film, WW1 and WW2 era engineering, and Film Noir, you can see some examples of it in parts of the Hellboy comics, Dark City, Sky Captain & The World of Tomorrow, and Crimson Skies. Etherites who adopt this style will sometimes be mistaken for Hollow Ones.
                                                    • Art Deco Pulp: A frequent favorite of the Adventurers, usually reflecting a devotion to the more humanistic styles of 20s, 30s and 40s architecture and design that was supplanted by the somewhat dehumanizing and oppressive styles like Brutalism and Functionalism.
                                                    • Retro-Victorian Steampunk: One of the popular styles/fads for the past decade or so, possibly owing to a number of Etherites having been part of the subculture prior to Awakening and simply carrying it over.
                                                    There's also the possibility of "Geek Chic", the current trend of pop culture to be full to the point of bloating with things that used to be part of "nerd culture" - comic book superheroes, video game merchandise, sci fi and fantasy franchises, anime, etc. - that are currently popular and hip because of big budget films, streaming series, and the like. However, this sort of thing may be rejected by some Etherites out of the belief that this is the Technocracy co-opting these things for their own purposes.
