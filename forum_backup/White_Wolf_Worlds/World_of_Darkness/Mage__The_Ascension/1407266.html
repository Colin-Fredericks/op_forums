          Ars Magica 3rd edition (and possibly the end of 2nd and beginning of 4th) was produced by White Wolf. It was all about the Order of Hermes, and was then the origin of some of the Mage line. When Atlas Games took over production (with the 4th Ed core book), the timeline link was removed, but some of the IP was still used in both - especially around names, locations and some of the history.

                          I've never played it, so someone else is probably better fit to explain. As far as I understand, the biggest difference is that House Tremere still exists as a House, and the vampires have been removed from play. I think there are other, smaller things too.

                          I suppose it doesn't necessarily mean later edition Ars Magica CAN'T be a prequel. It predates the fall of Mistridge after all, and one could perhaps go with the idea that the Mages of Tremere only THOUGHT they got rid of the vampires only to lose the long game. But it's no longer considered directly linked the way 3rd Edition specifically was, and 1st and 2nd were heavily implied to be.

                              Originally posted by Penelope View Post
                              What is the historical event that causes Fourth Edition Ars Magica to split from the Dark Ages WoD timeline? (Did I say that right?)
                              Hard to say. Ars Magica's version of mystic Europe completely lacks things like Cainite vampires or Garou werewolves, as well as any of the groups that would form the foundations for the Technocracy, Verbena, Celestial Chorus or so forth. So anything that would've involved any of those groups and their influences never happen. And the founder of House Tremere has apparently been dead for a number of years.
                              Also, metaphysics seems to be different, with four distinct realms of power (Magic, Divine, Infernal and Faeire, IIRC) that all the mystical stuff derives from. And, again IIRC, the further one gets from Europe, the weirder things get as the world apparently tends to conform to what medieval Europeans thought the rest of the world was like rather than what it actually was. (There's a 2nd ed Ars Magica book on sub-Saharan Africa called South of the Sun which, in addition to the Kingdom of Prestor John, contains a multitude of period weirdness.)

                              I've mostly used the various sourcebooks as idea fodder for the Order of Hermes (the various books on the Houses help fill in details about the original ones, especially the ones that got stuck into Ex Misc) and for period details about medieval Europe (the book on Mythic Judaism is one of my personal favorites, but the 5th edition regional books all have really good maps that I've found insanely useful).

                                          Originally posted by Penelope View Post

                                          That’s great! Thanks 😊. I’m gonna buy a bunch of old Ars Magica Tribunal books and see if I can scavenge some useful stuff out of them.
                                          IIRC, the list of Tribunal books are as follows,

                                          From 5th edition:

                                          Guardians of the Forests: The Rhine Tribunal - A large part of Germany, as well as the Low Countries.
                                          The Lion & The Lily: The Normandy Tribunal - Northern and central France.
                                          Faith & Flame: The Provencal Tribunal - Southern France.
                                          Against the Dark: The Transylvanian Tribunal - Most of what is now Romania, Hungary, the Balkans, Slovakia and Bulgaria. (It's dominated by the Tremere, and can help flesh out some of the real world regional stuff from the early Transylvania Chronicles and House of Tremere.)
                                          The Contested Isle: The Hibernian Tribunal - Ireland
                                          The Sundered Eagle: The Theban Tribunal - Greece and Asia Minor

                                          And a trio of books that are set up like Tribunal books but technically aren't, covering the Middle East.

                                          Cradle and The Crescent - About Mesopotamia, Arabia and Persia. (I don't have this one, but it's on my list of ones to acquire, as it apparently includes a rival magical group, The Order of Solomon, and a description of Mythic Zoroastrianism.)
                                          Lands of the Nile - About Egypt, Nubia and Ethiopia.
                                          Between Sand & Sea: Mythic Africa - About North Africa west of Egypt.

                                          From 4th edition:

                                          Heirs to Merlin: The Stonehenge Tribunal - England and Wales. (Obviously, this would be mostly superseded by the stuff in Dark Ages: Britain, but there's a number of interesting ideas to mine, and the maps are pretty good.)
                                          Sanctuary of Ice: The Greater Alpine Tribunal - The Alps of central Europe and Bohemia.
                                          The Dragon and the Bear: The Novgorod Tribunal - Poland and Russia.
                                          Blood and Sand: The Levant Tribunal - The Holy Land.

                                          And again, a pair of books about an area that doesn't actually have tribunals - Ultima Thule: Mythic Scandinavia and Land of Fire and Ice: Mythic Iceland - which, if you want to do stuff with the Valdaermen, are probably pretty useful.

                                          And then Third Edition, which was the one White Wolf did a big chunk of. I should note that these seem to have a weird fixation on Infernalism, which I guess was just a thing with the company back in the early 90s, as its very prevalent in a lot of 1st ed WoD stuff as well. However, it moved to Atlas near the end of this edition, so the final Tribunal book doesn't seem to share this infatuation. And some of the stuff in this apparently isn't canon anymore, but if you're just using them as idea fodder for Dark Ages, that doesn't really matter.

                                          Tribunals of Hermes: Iberia
                                          Tribunals of Hermes: Rome - which is the majority of Italy.
                                          Lion of the North: The Loch Leglean Tribunal - Scotland.

                                          And as I mentioned, the 2nd edition book, South of the Sun, which is a deeply weird take on Sub-Saharan Africa.


                                          In addition, fifth edition has a trio of Houses of Hermes books (True Lineages, Societates, and Mystery Cults) and a four book set about medieval society (Lords of Men, The Church, City & Guild, and Art & Academe).

                                          Forth edition's two standouts, for my uses at least, are Kabbalah: Mythic Judaism and the Medieval Bestiary Revised.

                                              The various books on the Houses of Hermes can be useful for Mage if you have Order of Hermes character concepts for the houses in Ars Magica that still exist in the Mage universe. I have the Guardians of the Forest book in PDF and just scored a copy of Land of Fire and Ice in paper (it's hardcover) for my Verbena player through my local Halfprice Books.

                                              I have owned in paper and in PDF the version with Jonathan Tweet as the author and published by Wizards of the Coast. Yep, the big Magic card makers got an Ars Magica book in there right between 3rd and 4th edition. Speaking of the 4th edition of Ars Magica, it's free on the Warehouse 23 store, probably because Steve Jackson Games has carried PDFs for Atlas Games products for a while and never removed the 4e book that was being given out for free during the transition to Ars Magica 5e.

                                              While there's not a ton of detail on all the locations and houses and the rules will differ a little, the free 4e PDF should give you an idea of where Ars Magica is going. If you like more and like PDF, Steve Jackson Games sells several editions worth of Ars Magica through Warehouse 23. . .

                                              http://www.warehouse23.com/products?utf8=%E2%9C%93&search%5Bprice_range_any%5D%5B%5D=Free_&keywords=ars+magica
