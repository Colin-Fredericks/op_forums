            Originally posted by Octavo View Post
            If you do this, I recommend following the example of Dark Ages: Mage and make damage levels equal to number of successes on an Arete + Sphere roll. In M20, each success inflicts roughly 2 levels of damage.
            I'm doing a similar model, but it's less potent than you think. Consider you need to roll 2 successes before even counting the damage.
            Here's my houserules. Some are old and independent of which wod splat we're playing, made more to speed things up than anything else.
            1) Everybody important soaks lethal like bashing. NO ONE soaks Agg without equipment and specific powers for it.
            2) Dealing Agg damage is the exclusive realm of quintessence-infused attacks (and non-chargen powers for other splats). Generally any source of agg damage is halved compared to canon.
            3) Evading or Parrying attacks is reflexive, not a separate action. Reply attacks no longer exist. If everybody's feeling like it, Clash may be used (when two people attack each other on the same initiative...instead of two attack-defense pairs, it's just one attack against the other, winner hits by the success threshold)
            4) Spellcasting roll is Arete+Avatar
            5) Everybody starts with Avatar 1, and raising Avatar during chargen is done with almost the same cost and restrictions as Arete. Avatar may not be raised with XP, though every character will have a fair chance of gaining it over time. (expect 2s and 3s by the end of a few stories)
            6) Standard spellcasting difficulty is 6(coincidental), 7(unwitnessed vulgar) or 8(vulgar with witnesses).
            a) The final difficulty modifier is still +-3, but only the final modifier. If something gives you -8 and events also bring a +7, you get a -1.

            Check my Exalted homebrew!

                    My house rules involve inverting the difficulties between Coincidental and Vulgar Magick, and having the universe discourage Vulgar Magic with Paradox and Unbelief. It should be more difficult to make your Paradigm blend in with Consensual Reality, not less. Vulgar w/o Witnesses is an annoying sub-category which can go away.

                    Also, dice for casting equals your Arete + your Avatar rating. Your Avatar rating equals 1/2 your Willpower rating, round down.

                    Also, I'd like to come up with a way to allow mages to cast over their Sphere Limits, at the cost of things going horribly wrong. "The Sorcerer's Apprentice Effect", if you will.

                    Something has to be done about the Ability bloat.

                    Also, I'd like to simplify how Arete is done in regards to xp. You can buy up Arete with bonus points at character creation to 3 as usual. But from then on, your XP purchases should focus on Spheres, If you attempt to buy up a Sphere over your Arete, you go on a Seeking. At the end, you roll your current Arete versus current Arete +1, and if you win, your Arete goes up. If you lose, you have to wait until your next Sphere hits your Arete rating. Once all Spheres are at your Arete rating, you go up automatically. This changes once you hit Arete 5, but I'm working out the details on that. I don't want Arete to be an xp sink; I want the players out there getting into trouble and making things go BOOM! around them.

                        Mage: the Awakening introduced the concept of “Arcane Experience”, a second type of experience that you earn by investigating supernatural phenomena but limited in terms of what they can be spent on (primarily Gnosis, that game's counterpart to Arete — which explains why “investigating the supernatural” is the primary way to earn it). Arcane Experience is earned in addition to regular experience, so your regular xp rewards are freed up for other uses.

                        For Mage: the Ascension, I'd change the means of earning it: Gnosis is all about attaining a deeper understanding of the world's mysteries; Arete is about being excellent. Select the earning conditions properly, and you'll get a double whammy: not only will regular xp be freed up for other uses, but Arcane Experience will be most effectively earned by going out there, getting into trouble, and making things go BOOM! around them. IIRC, the Mage Translation Guide has some advice on this.

                            Certification is definitely gone. Sinking four starting background points just for a medical license is ridiculously overcosted.

                            I was hoping we might see some sort of standardization among basic Ability sets between 20th edition games, with Hobby Talent/Professional Skill/Expert Knowledge being used to cut out the bloat, and just focusing on the handful of important secondary skills in each game, but no such luck. Technology really made more sense as a Knowledge. >.< Back to a knowledge it is.

                            I'd say I'd start with just using the Abilities from Vampire 20th and then working from there.

                            Martial Arts is gone; we don't need Brawl+. Why the Hell take Brawl?

                            Animal Kinship as distinct from Animal Ken is fine.
                            Blatancy isn't happening.
                            Carousing is a drop.
                            Do I'll keep.
                            Flying is really just an Athletics subset.
                            High Ritual seems more like a focus of Etiquette, Occult, or proper Academics: Sociology specialty.
                            Lucid Dreaming is fine.
                            Search is going away.
                            Seduction I'd drop; you can get the effect from Empathy and Subterfuge.
                            Acrobatics is good.
                            Archery is good; it's never really been close to Firearms or Athletics for my tastes.
                            Biotech is really a Science specialty.
                            Energy Weapons is best done as a Firearms specialty.
                            Hyper Tech and Jet Pack are both fine.
                            Riding I might keep as a skill.
                            Pilot skill seems to be dropped from the book.
                            Torture is something I've thought best role-played.
                            Area Knowledge I don't like and rarely use even in games that feature it.
                            Belief Systems is a whole slew of different skills, but could mostly be covered purely by Academics.
                            Cryptography is fine.
                            Demolitions is fine.
                            Finance is fine.
                            Lore/R&D data means I'm really not sure what you are trying to accomplish with Occult any more. If Esoterica covers occult practices, and Lore/R&D covers knowledge of other supernaturals, what is left, and why is Occult even useful any more?
                            Media is fine.
                            Pharmacopeia/Poisons is just Medicine skill, flat-out. Redundant Redundancy Department of Redundant Redundancy.

                                                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                                I think it helps to start by just treating the whole Secondary section as Hobby/Professional/Expert examples and never require them for play.
                                                To an extent, yes. That said, Hobby Talents, Professional Skills, and Expert Knowledges are subject to the Well-Skilled Craftsman option, allowing you to make a jack-of-all-trades (Professional Skill + Well-Skilled Craftsman option) or a polymath (Expert Knowledge + Well-Skilled Craftsman option). With that in mind, I think my preferred approach here would be to introduce a counterpoint to Well-Skilled Craftsman, namely an option that explicitly lets you single out a Specialty of another Ability and take it as an Ability in its own right; then I'd go through the various Secondary Abilities and recast as many of them as possible as Specialties of the core Abilities. For instance, Flying would become a Specialty of Athletics, and Hypertech would become a Specialty of Esoterica.

                                                The ones I'd keep separate would be ones that cut across several core Abilities (e.g., High Ritual), ones that don't seem to fit as Specialties of any core Abilities, and ones that are essentially reskins of existing Abilities but applied to something else (Archery vs. Firearms; Riding or Pilot vs. Drive).

                                                Originally posted by Heavy Arms
                                                Though I've been thinking of combining Hypertech and Esoterica to basically be the same with with a different name depending on your focus. The point of both is how well you understand mystic/hypertech principles and can apply them to actions even if they aren't casting an effect.
                                                This. We've got a number of Abilities with dual names depending on whether you're dealing with A mystic mindset or a technological one; to me, it looks like Esoterica and Hypertech is the same sort of thing. Indeed, the only reason I can think of for keeping them separate is that, as written, Hypertech is capped by Technology while Esoterica has no comparable restriction.
