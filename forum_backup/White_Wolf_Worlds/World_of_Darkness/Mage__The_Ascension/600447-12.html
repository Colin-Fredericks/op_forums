                The Name of the Rose by Umberto Eco. Yes, Umberto "Ur-Fascism" Eco. His career was mostly as that of a novelist, and many of his works have to do with conspiracies. Which is, obviously, relevant to our purposes.

                The Name of the Rose is a medieval detective story, although it plays with many of its assumptions and subverts its norms. And not just because it's medieval, and brings a lot of End Times fervor and ecclesiastical politics into it. As such, it can be a useful tool for Dark Ages Mage stories, or to an extent Sorcerer's Crusade.

                The novel has been adapted a number of times, including an 80s film starring Sean Connery.

                                                            Regarding fiction inspirations for Mage: the Ascension, I'd add the t.v. series of Travelers. (2016-2018)

                                                            Travelers does a great job of depicting its Technocracy as the good guys, both at the civilization-level and the personal-level. Its premise is that volunteer time-travelers from a dystopian future come back to stop civilization from falling apart. The catch is that the team members know beforehand that their time-travel trip is one way only. The protagonists and the audience have no way to know whether each episode's mission truly succeeded or failed, except if additional travelers come back with new news. For such a gloomy dystopian storyline, its tone is one of calm, reasonable hope. It's also one of the few sci-fi shows I've seen where "the greater good" isn't automatically a sinister cliche.
