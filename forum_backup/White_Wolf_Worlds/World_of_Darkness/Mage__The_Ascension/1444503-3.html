    Originally posted by Aleph View Post
    Where is the focus?

    A focus would be nedded to cast the effect, wouldn't it?. Technocrats can't cast w/o Instruments. A password, gesture, ID or something has to be included in the list of Instruments to make this "casting" feasible.

    If I had to put it into words I would call it Analytics.

    In the other scenario we've seen Technocrats cast without realizing it they essentially analyze their surroundings and "realize" that a structural collapse or some other convenient event is about to happen. "The Reality Deviant doesn't realize it but his reckless actions have damaged the support beam and the roof is about to fall on him..." That kind of thing.

    I would say something similar is happening with the "conveniently" seeded technology. The Agent sees a couple of reality deviants conspiring near a fuse box. He "scans" the box and realizes it happens to have a seeded microphone he can use to listen in.

    This is one of those cases where the Mage's own sense of believability plays an important role. Since the Technocrat doesn't realize he is casting, what he thinks he could reasonably find will limit what he can find. It's not really a Vulgar vs Coincidental thing, so much as what he would even consider looking for.

                    If you have Mage Made Easy, check out the Tycoon Approach (pp.44–45). In particular:
                    For details about what this sorry of magick looks like in game terms, see Mage 20's entries “Resources” (pp.322–323), “Fashion” (p.591), “Mass Media”, “Management and Human Resources”, “Money and Wealth” (pp.594–596), and “Social Domination” (p.598), plus the practice entries noted below and the character Malcolm Jamal Leonard (pp.254–259).

                    And
                    Associated Practices: Art of Desire/Hypereconomics, dominion, hypertech, invigoration, and reality-hacking.

                    It also suggests that “tycoon mages specialize in working with energy and influence (as described in the How Do You DO That? entries of those names, pp.42–51 and 114–136).”

                    I would add that the “working with energy” stuff is phrased in terms of “working with value”. Resonance plays into this in that “value” isn't easily quantized; different Resonances represent different kinds of value.

                    Conjuring material forms (HDYDT p.45) isn't done often by tycoons; when it is done, I'd have him practice hypertech through something like a 3d printer: the Syndicate Convention Book talks about them sometimes using hidden features of mundane technology; in this case, a 3d printer can make things that weren't there before, but a tycoon can use Primal Utility to enhance that beyond the strictly realistic limits of what a normal 3d printer can do. If he's willing to go Vulgar, he can enhance it far beyond its normal capabilities. In general, that's the Syndicate approach to hypertech: seemingly ordinary tech that has hidden features that make it inordinately effective. It generally doesn't add new capabilities, it just enhances existing features; and when it does add new features, they need to be logical extensions of the item's normal functions. The tycoon isn't going to find a gun hidden in a laptop, because guns aren't typically hidden in laptops.

                    Another possibility is to use something akin to Dramatic Editing from Adventure!: if it's reasonable that a gun was just lying around and you simply haven't noticed it before now, then the tycoon could use expertise as an instrument to notice it.

                    Conjuring immaterial forms (mind or spirit) pretty much requires hypertech; and since mundane tech that does something vaguely like this isn't exactly common (we don't exactly have holographic projectors yet), this is something that you're not going to see a tycoon doing; it falls the “of you can't explain it within your Focus, you can't do it” clause.

                    My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                Maybe this is just me, and maybe the canon has lots of examples of Technocrats subconsciously reality-warping, but I see the Technocrats as probably being the least capable of getting away with these kind of post-hoc magical justifications; that secret door was "always" there, "every" new model of technology conveniently contains whatever hidden feature a nearby Technocrat needs. To the extent that their Paradigm/Focus strongly emphasizes rigidity and consistency, I think it's reasonable that the Technocracy needs to have more tangible limitations on what they can pull out of their @$$. Being able to fit a laser-gun inside of a laptop is Vulgar enough, I don't think they should be able to "conveniently" find one in any laptop laying around; they *should* have to find Technocrat brands that they know contain the appropriate features. I'm on the side of agreeing that sort of thing should be worked out with the ST when figuring out Focus during char-gen.

                                The Technocratic paradigm comes with advantages; I lean somewhat towards the argument that the cheap mass-production of Devices and Wonders (lack of WP cost) that the Technocracy is capable of is an advantage that most Tradition Mages simply cannot replicate due to radical paradigm differences, for example.

                                If a Technocrat is casting spontaneous Vulgar magic to the extent that they are just pulling junk out of nowhere without a strong rational justification in-paradigm, I'd imagine they'd be put on watch for signs of Reality Deviancy.

                                PS It would probably require a lot of figuring-out between player and ST, but the name for such an Instrument would probably be "Hidden Features," "Executive Privilege," "Developer Mode," etc.

                                            Technocrats aren't primarily pattern either, they have all four of pattern, dynamic, primordial, and questing in their ranks.

                                            As well, they're not making something that wasn't there before suddenly appear. They're taking a situation that's ambiguous and removing the ambiguity. There might have been a secret panel there, or there might have been a bug. As long as it's plausible that those things might be, the Technocrat gets to say that they are.

                                            If you want some technobabble justification for this, I refer you to the wave function collapse in quantum mechanics. Prior to the collapse of the wave function, the state of reality is indeterminate. When the wave function is observed, it collapses to a definite state.

                                            But that's just one take on it. Most technocrats don't get into that sort of thing, and just describe it as “I got lucky!”

                                                            The thing about the Technocracy is they are not really hung up on science as the correct way because it is science.
