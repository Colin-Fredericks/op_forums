    How do you reward style ?

    04-09-2018, 04:50 PM
    Hello, all.
    I'm reading M20 rules, and i realized that i used to play Mage with a very different kind of system. I want to try the official rules, but something feels odd. Looks at the following scenario:


    A verbena wants to feed energy to a guardian jaguar spirit for her land, near a small node. She handles prime with blood, and spirits with celtic runes. She has to prepare a ritual combining both.

    Roleplay 1
    She will hunt animals related to the spirit food tastes ( vivid animals, the bigger the better ) and keep them alive until it's sacrifice time. This will be governed by survival skills.
    Then, she will draw the most respectful rune(s) she can think of about the spirit. Drawing a huge rune is not a easy task, and it is governed by many steps if you want to get that right. Stamina, dexterity, intelligence (INT is used to draw something larger than your field of view and not distort anything), and of course, the knowledge of the rune itself.

    Then, goes the sacrifice preparation, where she will use a special knife crafted for those times, probably based upon a jaguar tooth. and she will spill the blood of her offerings, with the goal to fill the rune with the blood. Again, not an easy task. You dont want to pierce a stomach and spoil your blood with acid.

    Roleplay 2
    She pierces her finger using a tack and draws a rune with her bloody finger on whatever the guardian spirit is anchored


    How do you do it ?
    While the RP2 is correct, i hope you all find it disappointing.
    In the official rules, i cannot find how the first roleplay variant can be rewarded. Except for the extended rolls part.

    With my friends, we used to add "style dices" to arete rolls to show that the greater your hand on your paradigm is, the greater your mastery is.
    But this method as many drawbacks:
    - it takes a lot of various skills
    - methodic mages becomes more powerful than instinct ones
    - after being rewarded, a player may lack creativity and reproduce the same methods with small adaptations (even if it is not what the character would have done)
    - Modifiers become too much powerful (we used to put magic at difficulty 7 even low ranks, modifiers were only for reality hardening/softening like node and antimagic)

    So, how do you do it ?
    I'm eager to read your methods.

      Well, you have in the experience section of the characters chapter an extra experience point as a reward for playing your style well (page 335). This is called 'focus.' You could also make the more evocatively described magick using styles coincidental more often in the right zones than than quick 'I do my ritual magick.' So, for the verbena in the jungle summoning jaguar spirits, making it easier to do the effect would be a way to explore that theme.

      Also, some of the point of style is to enjoy exploring ideas, which is a metagame. If you don't want to use extra dice or modifiers, just consider that a game with more of the right flavor is more interesting than one which is bland. I mean, if you're playing Dungeons and Dragons and the GM hears you describe how your thief is going to disarm the boulder trap, if the description is interesting, that is a lot more fun than saying, 'I roll my Disarm Trap skill. . .here's my total. I disarm the boulder trap.'

      There's always a balance between creativity in the game and keeping the action going when you have to make a lot of rolls. I do find the second scenario disappointing, so I tend to reward players with more experience and more knowledge (study points which are targeted experience points), if they go into paradigms.

              All of the extra preparation she is doing will give Difficulty Modifiers under the M20 rules.

              The survival rolls, the careful drawing of runes, this is Abilities reducing the difficulty of Magick.

              Furthermore, such preparation can pull on Mythic Threads, and taking longer to cast than you actually need reduces difficulty also. So in purely mechanical terms RP 1 is rewarded over RP 2.

              And to top it all off, the person who does RP 1 will be able to justify raising their Arete to the ST at the end of the Story, the person doing RP 2 may take longer.
