                        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                        So it is the opposite to what you're doing: in the books the Union says it does something completely different from what the RTs do, and since it isn't true in the game, they're delusional. You proposes that this isn't the case because they don't need to say such a thing (they have better things to say for their point). But the OP proposes this isn't the case because it is not actually a delusion, but the truth.
                        To further the point, Mages have a lot of different reasons for how subjective reality works. Coming up with one for the Technocracy isn't the problem. The bigger question is why, knowing that, they would restrict themselves to a narrow range of doing things? Why would they be so certain that they are doing things the right way that they can't even drop instruments? Why would they spend so much time and energy working on the Consensus? It seems like they are trying to have it both ways: they are both scientists and technology is a paradigm they invented and are trying to convince the world of.

                        Of course that only seems ridiculous if we assume reality is simply understood.

                        A lot of people assume that Mage reality is obviously and simply subjective, so the Traditions are right and the Technocracy is engaged in ludicrous self deception. Others assume Mage reality is mostly based on modern physics and the consensus just creates the possibility of magic, so the Technocracy is right and the Traditions are in a futile and pointless struggle. Neither of these makes sense and neither fits with Mage as depicted. Indeed if the former were true, the Traditions would likely be a hodgepodge of magical ideas rather than the continuation of mostly faded cultural practices, and if the latter were true the Technocracy wouldn't be using the magic system at all.

                        Instead what we see is a world that clearly has a consensus that affects reality itself, but we also see that most Mages (not just the Technocracy) do think that certain things are more true (or at least better). The implication is, the world is complicated and not fully understood and both the Traditions and the Technocracy are in some way right, no matter how much they seem to contradict each other.

                                        *One of the key differences between Mages magic(k) and Sorcerers magic, it's that the first *impose* their Will on reality to "create" magic (which does, still, employ and obey certain rules - those of uppercase Reality), and the latter employ existing undercurrents of magic (not Reality - per se - but also magics that are part of reality, like that which Mages can create).

                                        *Ironically, the faction that believes in "science" has been the one that was able to impose their Will uppon reality more effectively, and actually changed what's real and what's not. Not only by making stuff like planes real (allegedly against at least one Foundation) ... but also by making "un-real" a lot of *stuff that was real*, like dragons.

                                        *Philosophical scientist Francis Bacon said scientists needed to "put lead to the wings of reason", to be like Dedalus and not like Icaro: Bacon proposed to submit research to a scientific community, to judge it, rather than to trust in the individual genius. This could be slower, but would make more solid conclusions that would make the building blocks of better science (Bacon was an alchemist, and this was a criticism against the way alchemists opperated: Bacon disliked how alchemists investigated alone, and then encrypted their discoveries)

                                                            Science is done in the dark in our world too. There are various assumptions in the method itself that we don't know are true and we press on with it anyways. If you look at the history of scientific theories, it is a hash of odd ideas, strange exceptions, and special pleading, and that's without looking at all the times scientists have unintentionally (or even intentionally) disregarded an element of the method and carried on as if nothing was amiss.

                                                            The method was developed to deal with a world that we didn't (and honestly still don't) know would play along. It's going to have some utility even in a subjective reality. Even knowing that expectations and biases will affect the results doesn't make it completely invalid as that's something scientists already have to deal with (admittedly for psychological rather than metaphysical reasons) As long as there is at least some consistency and at least the appearance of underlying rules the scientific method or something like it will have a use.

                                                            The only scenario that the Technocracy would be completely stupid (or evil) is if they knew for a fact that there was no underlying structure to reality and that complete nonsense would work as well as reasoned belief and yet they keep applying the method anyways. Even most marauders don't act like they believe that.


                                                            Also, there is a hidden bias that's messing us up on this argument. Traditions members have just as much internal consistency in their paradigms as the Technocracy has in theirs and if we knew what they really believed we'd probably think it was just as incompatible with subjective reality as the scientific method. We don't, though, so it looks like the Techies are the only ones hit by that issue.
