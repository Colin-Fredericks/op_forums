        Indeed, the idea of knowledge it's always subordinate to the idea of defining an orderly reality with the Technocracy.

        Every and each mention of something akin to investigation within the Technocracy it's always envelopped by the idea of Control. They may invest resources to discover what makes Deviants tick, but that's strictly in order of eradicating them. As it's clearly stated in their Mission Statement, that we can read in Lian's post.

        Which doesn't mean that they don't care about investigation or exploration, but merely than these are secondary goals instrumental to their actual mission. As an example, let's analize the fourth:

        4. Define the nature of the universe. Knowledge must be absolute or chaos will envelop all. The elemental forces of the universe must not be left to the caprices of the unknown.
        To "define" something it's not the same as to discover or investigate it.

        Then they say, Knowledge must be absolute. But that's to prevent chaos from enveloping all. Again, knowledge here seems to be subordinate to the idea of order.

        And what does imply to science the idea of preventing "the caprices of the unknown": In real science one studies the unknown and if our definitions don't accomodate reality we change the definitions. This mission statement doesn't sound like that, what it says it's that the unknown shouldn't be allowed to have power (over the forces of the universe). A rather odd argument for a scientist: Usually the unknown isn't something to be feared.

        It rather sounds to me like it's more important to prevent the unknown to interfere with the statical model of the universe (our definitions that allow absolute knowledge) than to discover new stuff that could challenge such definitions. The goal of discovering stuff it's subordinate to a "model" that's pre-defined (the fabled Technocratic Paradigm), and the stuff that doesn't accomodate to that model has to be eradicated, as latter shown in the Principle that comes just after it:

        5. Destroy Reality Deviants. Their recklessness threatens our security and our progress toward Unity.
        It trully can't be clearer than that.

        Or that's how I interpret all of this. Surely other interpretations are possible.

        Anyhow, the fact that in each an every principle the idea of knowledge appears tied to the idea of averting chaos or fostering stasis really shouldn't be ignored in any interpretation

                    Originally posted by Muad'Dib View Post
                    Why did you change from a possibly heroic Technocracy all the way to a merciless and dogmatic Technocracy ?
                    One of the reasons why the extremely ruthless Technocracy doesn't work for me is that such a portrayal doesn't go in line with the Technocracy's mission statement of exploring and investigating reality. If Mystic Mages are capable of the feats that they are, doesn't this merit investigation, and respect ? Also, I think that it might very well be that the objectives which involve guiding and influencing mundane humans can be accomplished more easily by Technocrats if cooperation with Mystic Mages is undertaken.
                    I'm sorry? The Technocracy doesn't have a mission statement of exploring and investigating reality. The precepts of Damian are the closest thing to a Technocratic Mission Statement, and they're all about redefining a chaotic reality as something predictable, and walling off the pieces the Technocracy doesn't like past the gauntlet.

                    Precept 1.) "Bring Stasis and Order to the universe. Predictability brings safety.once all is discovered and all is known unity will be won.

                    Precept one is the closest the precepts get to a mission statement of exploring and investigating reality. However it's not a mission statement so much as a statement of the Unions win condition. And that condition is when all is discovered and all is known. When there is no mystery and no room for chaos in their perfect new order.

                    Precept 2.) "Convince the masses of the benevolence of science, commerce, and politics. Conflict and Suffering will be eliminated in our utopia."

                    Included this largely because I felt like including all the precepts, but the wording here is clearly construction focused, rather than investigation focused. Their goal is not to investigate and make available the fruits of that investigation, but to convince the masses of the benevolence of their brand of magic by way of good works.

                    Precept 3.) "Preserve the Gauntlet and the Horizon. Chaotic Individuals who open gateways with impunity threaten the stability of our world. Uncontrolled portals also allow outside forces, such as the Nephandi, access to our world. This must never happen."

                    Again, largely included because I wanted to include all of them. But this demonstrates the incompatibility of the Umbra, in which anything is possible and as a realm of chaos, with the perfect order the Union aspires to create.

                    Precept 4.) "Define the nature of the universe. Knowledge must be absolute or chaos will envelop all. The elemental forces of the universe must not be left to the Caprices of the Unknown."

                    And here we get to the brunt of it. The laws of the universe must be defined, and created whole cloth by the union, because the building blocks of their world cannot be left to the 'caprices of the unknown' They can't simply make do with what they discover to be the laws. They must break down the world that was there before and rebuild it to their liking. This is the primary conflict with the Sons of Ether, who are totally willing to apply the scientific method to a universe that makes no sense and accept the answer it gives back, maddening and contradictory as those answers will be. The union however, demands order. And if the world won't provide it, then they will kill the world and build order atop it's corpse.

                    Precept 5.) "Destroy Reality Deviants. Their recklessness threatens our security and our progress towards unity."

                    Precept 6.) "Shepherd the masses. Protect them from themselves and others."

                    These last two are fairly self explanatory. However the term 'reality deviant' is telling. They don't deny that mystic mages have power. The issue is the source of that power and the means by which they use it. Magic is chaos. It's potential is infinite and varied. Though they make use of Magic, the Union's ultimate goal is a world without it. A world where the laws are redefined to eliminate magic and any need for magic.

                    EDIT: And it seems several people beat me to the punch.

                                                        One of the bigger influences on how I came to view and present the Void Engineers came from a Philip K. Dick story "Souvenir", a synopsis and review of which can be found HERE. In my games, they have been, since the 1920s (deliberately coinciding with HP Lovecraft's writing career), increasingly paranoid about what exists beyond the Gauntlet, and are devoted to keeping Earth and humanity safe and sanitized from "alien" influence. They're effectively Ghostbusters and Men in Black without the humor, in a world where things like The Astronaut's Wife, Stephen King's "I am the Doorway", Lovecraft's "The Whisperer in Darkness" and John Carpenter's The Thing are all too real. They are the truth behind the stories of Majestic 12, Area 51, and Project Serpo. Weak points in the Gauntlet must be shorn up; People under the influence of extra dimensional beings must be rounded up and deprogramed; Non-human entities must be captured, studied and then eliminated. While some vestiges of 19th century ideas of exploitation of the beyond still remain, Sanitation is the goal, and any effort at exploration is only to further that end.

                                                        And yes, I realize this is in stark contrast to the way most people have always viewed the Void Engineers. But it works for the sort of Mage games I usually prefer to run. Except for the times I feel like running Aliens space marines/Stargate/Star Trek mash ups. Then they are heroic explorers with lasers and space ships and ItX androids assistants.
