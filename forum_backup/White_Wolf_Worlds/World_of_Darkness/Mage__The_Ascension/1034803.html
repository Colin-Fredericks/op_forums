    Entropy: Disambiguation (Sphere) ALSO: broken nomenclature, and the Ahl i Batin

    01-19-2017, 04:00 PM
    I see that this Sphere is the least understood of all by people in general, so I thought maybe I could share some insights about it (and to gain insights too).

    Quick notes: (I'll expand later)
    1- Entropy, as one of the Triad forces (Wyrm), has no relation with the Entropy Sphere, AT ALL
    2- Entropy deals with Fate. Destiny. Not chance per se, and certanly, not destruction.
    3- Levels 3-5 doesn't give direct control of their areas of action (matter, life, mind). It is inderect control.
    4- The name of the sphere makes no sense, considering Mage Lore. First of all, the system of Spheres, althouth it is the way we deal with the game mechanics, is NOT consensus amongst the paradigms of the Traditions (in terms of interpretation, most Mages don't think about their powers in terms of Spheres, even if we, as players, must, for rules purposes). Instead, it's a system that were created by the Hermetic Order. Problem is: the word "Entropy" is a scientific physical denomination. It fits the Technocracy, perhaps even the Etherite or VA, but not the hermetics. It would be much more logical if it were named "Fate Sphere" or "Destiny Sphere".
    5- The ban on Entropy by the Batini makes no sense (I'll expand later). It would make more sense for the Celestial Chorus to ban it than the Batini (I'll also expand later)

          1. Not entirely correct. according to the Book of Madness, the Nephandi often emphasise use of the sphere of Entropy, and all members are encouraged to gain at least the first rank, and there is one group of nephandi, the Malfeans, who are effectively servants of the Wyrm (regardless of whether or not they think the Wyrm exists literally or just conceptually).

          2. Again, not entierly. Fate, Chance, and Decay are all part of what the Entropy sphere encompases, and a lot of how these things manifest depends very heavily on paradigm.
          If you consider your life to be planed out from the start, then Entropy is the sphere of fate, shifting outcomes around and moving your path around.
          If you think there is no plan, that it's all just luck, then Entropy is the sphere of chance, being able to fix the odds in your favour, providing Fortune.

          It should be noted that there are whole sects of Euthanatos who devote themselves entirely to Chance based magic, rolling dice, flipping coins, shuffling cards, throwing rings and seeing where they land. For them, Entropy is all about the manipulation of chance, circumstance, and happenings.

          In addition, you may have the view that the only certainty in life is that all things fall apart. Real life physics has demonstrated that all things tend towards higher entropy. This means that all ordered systems will become increasingly disordered over time, until there is not distinguishing features of their being. Walls crumble to dust, Plants wilt and rot. People age and forget. Ideas evolve and become loss.
          The Entropy sphere is able to sense how far along things are towards their "final fate" as they were, and thus be used to determine how far from Order, or close to Chaos they are. Thus higher levels will give the mage the ability to affect order or chaos within a given system.

          3. I'd agree with this. Expanding on it, I'd say this Sphere allows you to dictate the outcome, but the methods of achieving the outcome are out of your control without the use of other spheres.

          4. If it makes you feel better, the Hermetics actually calls it "Ars Fati" and originally "Ars Fortunae". But regardless of that, how do you know it wasn't the other way around? That the term Entropy originally came from a mystic source, and the Technocracy appropriated it, changed its definition to one which suited their own paradigm, and then revised history to make it seem as though that's how it always was?

          The Problem with calling it the "Fate" sphere is that not all mages believe in Fate. Similarly not calling it the "Chance" sphere is not all mages believe in Chance. Entropy, on the other hand, is a concept that most mages from the traditions should be able to understand and internalise in whatever way they see fit. Some as luck, some as fate, some as decay. It's a word which encompasses all these meanings, and is therefor much more useful for cross tradition discussion, which is the whole point of codifying the spheres this way in the first place.

          5. I'm not all that knowledgable of the Batini, but if you're basing this on the assumptions you've made above (That Entropy is exclusively about fate, rather than chance or decay), then I think you may be coming to false conclusions.

              Originally posted by Saikou View Post
              In addition, you may have the view that the only certainty in life is that all things fall apart. Real life physics has demonstrated that all things tend towards higher entropy. This means that all ordered systems will become increasingly disordered over time, until there is not distinguishing features of their being. Walls crumble to dust, Plants wilt and rot. People age and forget. Ideas evolve and become loss.
              Hold on cowboy, this is not quite true. The second law of thermodynamics states that a total entropy of an isolated system always either remains constant or goes up. What this means is that natural processes are not reversible. When you put a piece of cold bacon on a hot pan the heat will always go from the pan to the bacon, and never the other way around. Entropy doesn't concern itself with a destruction of a system, but rather with its change and use of energy. When a plant grows - that's increase in entropy as the plant takes energy from the system of plant and sun. And of course entropy doesn't apply at all to things like "ideas", which are not physical objects with energy.

              There is also a concept of entropy in Informatics, but it's largely unrelated to physical entropy. Entropy in informatics is a measure of uncertainty about the contents of any given message.
