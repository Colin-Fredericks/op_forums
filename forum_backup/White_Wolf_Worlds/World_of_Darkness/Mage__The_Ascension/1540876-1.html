    Magus Maximus, feasible is different from certain, that's the distinction you're failing to make. It's certainly feasible, and I think you're mostly right about their method. We do need to consider the challenges and obstacles, though.

    One of the big first obstacle is the need to survive without the Union. Regardless of how similar this world is to theirs, they are indeed stranded in an alien environment. That may force their hand, and they may be got by some native org because they do start in a weakened position.

    The environment, specially the supernatural one, is dynamic, which means that their expertise in hiding doesn't automatically means they're out of danger. And it is made worse by this world being even more dynamic in this regard. The supernatural beings of this world also have resources they're not used to, even if compared with their apparent counterparts.

    Union agents are exceptional at hiding, but local Mages are also exceptional at finding stuff, and do so through means the Union never heard about. In general CoD splats are better at finding out things. This is one of many examples of risks the Union is completely unprepared to deal with at first, and this first is crucial.

    About the distinction on Mages, how the Technocrats think they work is irrelevant. The distinction is a fact. I'm not saying they can't use technological props, I'm saying they're a distinct splat altogether, whose powers have zero to do with Ascension's magic. Local Mages don't work on belief, and their Paradox doesn't come from Reality lashing out. A local Mage that stop believing in the Supernal simply stops doing magic until they stop their deluded nonsense.

    It's a matter of similar results achieved through completely unrelated methods. And we have to add that those people do achieve similar results, so they're not strangers to mind control. The Union never got to brainwash RDs without being noticed, that won't start now.

    Now, can they recruit people to their cause? Absolutely. It just isn't as straightforward as indoctrinating fellow Enlightened (which already isn't all that straightforward), and the distinction does add to the complications.

                                                First, to contextualize the matter, since a technocratic building appeared in CofD, I'm going to assume that it was magic. In this case, this would put the building in its place, but it wouldn't attract the attention of ordinary people. However, it would attract the attention of the creatures of darkness, after all, a new player in the city. The Technocrats would try to do two things: first, get information about where they are, what happened and what has changed; second, get resources, after all, they are now in a new environment, deprived of contacts and far from any source of income they had before. Soon, they would be called to the attention of the other creatures of darkness who would knock on their door. Some of them might send a scout first, but eventually they would make a direct intervention. I believe that 4 species would be the main interested parties: vampires, mages, arisen (mummies) and beasts, the latter being less likely, It would all depend on how many and how much influence they had within the city.

                                                Depending on the group or intentions of these creatures, they could sabotage the technocrats or even exterminate the group as a last resort, perhaps even joining forces to do so, but I'll assume that the factions present are not of the shoot first, ask questions later type. The kindred would be interested in their abilities so they would be start an partnership and the technocrats wouldn't be in a position to refuse; the mages I think would start with something similar to a non-aggression pact, both sides don't know what to expect from the other yet, considering that they still don't know how the other's magic works; the arisen I don't see then wanting to have peaceful relations with the technocrats unless in the case where he abandoned his judge, but whatever the case I believe they would avoid direct combat against an unknown enemy, so they seek information first and then take action, either to destroy them or use them, in the last case they may judge that they are more trouble than it would be worth; The beasts I think would ignore it, unless a particularly powerful one had influence in the city, in which case he could offer an alliance or a deal like the one offered be the kindred. As for the other creatures, I believe that the existence of the Technocracy would interfere little in their lives. The lost would continue to flee, the created would remain on their pilgrimage, although a curious individual could seek them out for study. The divergent ones would only interfere at the behest of another organization. The hunters would observe them, but I don't think they could try anything. The same goes for the Uratha. Finally, the Sin-Eaters have no reason to get involved, unleast I can think in one.

                                                Now, regarding the Technocrats' stance on these new, not so new creatures.

                                                As for the Kindred, they would have a business relationship as I said before, but in general they will take an attitude similar to that of their original universe, that of non-intervention until necessary;

                                                I would say that the werewolves would not change much, they will of course gather information on how to fight them, but keeping their distance;

                                                I believe that the mages would try to maintain an armed peace while exchanging information on how magic works in this world and on the different factions that exist, only later deciding to ally or intervene;

                                                The beasts would be an interesting species, since there is nothing similar to them in their original world, so they could choose to interact with them for research purposes and perhaps cooperation, after all, beasts are useful adaptable;

                                                Arisen are a case study for them, after all, a creature that does not die can be very useful and the Arisen are usually very powerful, so they would try to capture one;

                                                The Lost and the Sin-eaters, I believe they would study the subject, but in general, just ignore them, because they have nothing that interests them at first sight, unless one develops an interest in souls, but a mage would be more viable;

                                                The demons... I think they would see them as objects of study and they would approach them believing that this would be a plan of the God-Machine, they would also be interested in the cryptids as guinea pigs;

                                                In the case of the Prometheans and the Divergents, they would have a very practical interest in them, because it is a way for them to accumulate more power within their faction, it might not work, but they would at least try and in the case of the Divergents, I believe it would work, in a way.​
