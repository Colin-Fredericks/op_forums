                How many from among the Traditions would know about this school ? Would this school staff and students strive to make it known, or the opposite of this ?
                - I'm planning to have quite a few Hermetics and Choristers (the school was only for Hermetics until the 1500s, and only for Hermetics and Choristers until the 1850s), and there will be fewer representatives of the other 7 Traditions yes. Some teachers will only visit the school a few times a year, and some students will have to rely on books written by members of their Traditions. However there should be at least one representative of each Tradition at the school.

                When you leave the school you are not finished learning as a Mage. You could leave with a completely average or stereotypical Etherite understanding and then develop a more complex or esoteric understanding later. The stated intention of the school is not to train perfect or ultimate mages, it's to keep vulnerable children safe. The school exists because of fear of the Technocracy. It could be argued that the true purpose of the school is to keep the world safe from the students however.

                Some students will have to rely on their own research and experimentation, and on other students, to get a robust understanding of their paradigm. It's far from perfect. It's very likely that any shamanic mage who leaves the school will immediately return to their homeland to reconnect with their roots and discover the lore of their people, and their view on spirits and shamanism.

                So yeah the school is not all things to all students, and would never claim to be. It's a safe environment where they can learn loads of useful stuff (a good chunk of which will just be improving Abilities). When an Etherite leaves with a solid scientific understanding and a very vanilla scientific paradigm they may later develop a completely different approach based off of that foundation.

                Some Mages whose relatives are studying at the school will be unhappy with the way their education goes since students are free to dabble as they like, and a Hermetic might be annoyed to find that their niece leaves the school with an understanding of magick that they don't approve of. However the Traditions have benefited from having this population of cooperative Mages who have broad understandings.

                - With regards to Nephandi, since there's no way of sensing them they will be a problem if they attend. However Mages tend to be sent to the school after they've attracted some attention, and if we're looking at a Widderslainte the most likely result is them being recruited by the Nephandi first. Furthermore Widderslaintes are very rare compared to normal Mages and like most Mages will awaken in adulthood, not childhood.

                But in spite of those slim odds a Widderslainte will have ended up at the school at some point. The lore of the school can include the one case where they've had to use the Gilgul rite on a student, but this has been buried within the school's history. Furthermore I might insert a Widderslainte into the PCs' midst, and let the events play out around them.

                - The school's existence is only known explicitly by those who have attended and those who have worked there. Otherwise the Council of Traditions simply has this understanding about that if you are dealing with a precocious Mage the safest thing to do is pass that information up the chain of command and let the Council take care of it. There are rumours about some sort of school and it's generally understood that child mages are better off there compared with the alternatives, which are death, recruitment by the Technocracy, recruitment by Nephandi, becoming a Marauder, or causing trouble among sleepers and drawing too much attention to Mages.

                Simply put the school wouldn't function at all if Mages weren't terrified of the Technocracy, and if there weren't horror stories floating around about Mages coming home to find their families killed or abducted by agents during a pogrom. Furthermore there are horror stories floating around about child mages losing control of their powers and they aren't pretty either.

                The Technocracy doesn't know that Anstruther exists because of its powerful Arcane rating. However it is aware that something like Anstruther *probably* exists since it has education facilities for precocious citizens. The same is true in reverse.

                            The School has 5 houses. There were originally 4, one for each of the foundations of Hermetic theory.

                            Anima (command over life)
                            Vires (command over elemental forces)
                            Corona (command over the mind)
                            Primus (command over quintessence)

                            When the Choristers were allowed to enter the school a 5th house was established, Saint Benedict's.

                            In modern times there are students from across all the different Traditions, but the tradition of the school is very old and students are simply divided among these five houses. Hermetics always go into the first four and Choristers always go into the fifth, but otherwise students are divided between them. Each house has a motto, notable alumni, associated traditions (from songs to pranks)...

                                PC concepts are starting to roll in, and I have one question...

                                One of the players wants to play a mage who is an artistic soul, and they imagine them doing magick using art and music. The kid is currently inept in these things but that's where room for growth comes in. This seems to fit best with the Cult of Ecstasy since they aren't religious. However they also love the stuff about lucid dreaming, the Dream background and the Demesne background. I'm keeping Lucid Dreaming at 0, Demesne at 1 and Dream at 1 since they're freshly awakened, BUT I'm wondering which Tradition is best associated with magick relating to Dreams, lucid dreaming and the high Umbra in general? The cult of ecstasy? The akashics? The verbenna?

                                The other concept is "Chosen One gone wrong". When he was a baby a counterspell sealed by his dying mother caused the death of a Nephandus who tried to murder him. This fulfilled a prophecy about him defeating the said Nephandus, who was widely feared and reviled. However this is his actual span as a chosen one done. Destiny had that role in store for him and it was completed when he was 1. Now he's 11 and he is certain that things can't be so closed and finished. He finds that he's mediocre compared to his peers, that he is far from exceptional in his studies. This all comes after a lot of "we are expecting a lot from you young man". He just can't accept that he's normal, so he starts to do that thing some kids do where he lies to himself and others to inflate his value. The trouble is that he's a Mage and his neurosis is only getting worse with time. The player imagines that he may even go Marauder over it, with his own reality being one where he is the destined chosen on, and his reality sink being an effect which drags in bystanders into agreeing with him.

                                I've decided to possibly take it a step further; his Manifest Avatar has taken on the form of this dead Nephandus. The Nephandus is 100% dead, no question, but his Avatar is mimicking him. This means that it can drive him on as the antagonist he is wishing for, the dark figure who will make him special, a figure he can seek vengeance against. The prophecy says he's special because this dark figure exists and he is destined to kill him; if the figure is still around then he's still special. If he ever does become a Marauder this figure will kill another student and start actually being a real threat. It's like with an arachnophobic Marauder. If they fear spider attacks people might get bitten by spiders (assuming they have the necessary spheres to create spiders, or dangerous illusions of spiders, or call upon existing spiders, or pull spider spirits into our world).

                                So he acts as a subversion of chosen one plots essentially. Normal guy ruins his own life and everyone around him by trying to adhere to a prophecy that makes him important.

                                                    Yes, Hobgoblins can do both of those things. Hobgoblin is basically a catch all term for manifestations created by a Quiet that can affect reality.                                                             Had our session 0. It was very illuminating and interesting.

                                                            Oliver; a bit of an anti-chosen one as I mentioned earlier. Devotional (Destined) and Static (Prophetic). Hermetic, with standard tools and practice. Wants so badly to be the chosen one, to the point where he has manifested his own personal antagonist as an Avatar. He is going to steal credit for things and use his Manipulation and Leadership to get people to believe in him as a chosen one. Furthermore the player has invested bonus points into Resonance and Synergy so he REALLY seems like a chosen one superficially.

                                                            Rachael; a cyber-bully who runs the line between Virtual Adept and Euthanatos. Performs curses and blackmail using forums, social media, chaos magick... drawing runes with a graphic pad, digging through facebook profiles to find dirt... Her Avatar is a forum account which only she can interact with, her Sanctum is an abandoned computer room in the basement full of rot and broken equipment, her Familiar is a Virus spirit. She has a very very powerful Entropic Synergy. She's fond of fanfiction and cosplay, and while she is a nasty critic she does protect her friends up to a point. Think "no one gets to bully my friends but me".

                                                            Finn; a budding herbalist who grew up on a farm. He has an Elemental (Verdant) and Dynamic (Growing) Res/Syn. His practice mostly involves preparing plant ingredients to make concoctions, and gardening, working with plants directly. He's a spiritualist, and has Willow as a Totem. He's just a lanky friendly kid who is probably going to be bullied at first. He has a Greenhouse as his sanctum, with a beehive in the roof as his Familiar.

                                                            Derek; a dreaming clumsy stocky kid who wants to be dainty and artistic. When he dreams he can fly and dance and sing beautifully. Unbeknownst to him (and the player) his mother was a Fae Queen who died when he was young, making him Fae Blooded. I'm representing this with the Nephilim merit. He has a goldcrest bird as his Familiar (created by his mother in her dying moments to protect him; it is in fact some sort of fae), and has a Demesne. Temperamental and Devotional, with a focus on artistic flair and mood. The concept (art and dreams) is neatly tied together by the background (fae influence). He's painfully dyspraxic so his dreams of dancing and performing may be misguided, although music and art are still options. Performs magick through meditation, dancing, art, music... He's very strong and has a talent for fighting, but he really hates brawling and doesn't want people to be afraid of him.

                                                            Ivan; a child from an island not found on any map. His tribe are descendants of Mimir himself, and their island has slipped away from our realm (midgard) with time, just as lands like Lemuria and Atlantis have, like Bygones. His tribe is full of mystics, and he has been sent away to form closer bonds with the Traditions, particularly the Verbena. Unbeknownst to him his family's greatest seers have also predicted doom befalling their isles, so sending away a few children seems like a safe move. He performs magick through god-bonding, and a sort of pagan spiritualism. He's a fish out of water in midgard, and will be a tad arrogant and out of place. Mimir acts as a "Totem". He's very soft for his culture, preferring using a bow to melee weapons, and having little skill in combat.

                                                            Since the characters are just starting they only have 3 dots in spheres and I limited the freebie point totals along with the base Ability totals (11/7/5 not 13/9/5). I also capped Abilities at 2, allowing only one level 3 Ability (whatever they're especially good at).

                                                            I'm placing a lot of emphasis on Synergy and Resonance since the school is such a clusterfuck of mystical and technological paradigms, with an incredibly permissive (if volatile) consensus. Paradox might not get you, but magick still has consequences. Mess with the weather? Rain for a week. Too many people teleporting and conjuring? You step out of bed and into the lake. Ward your dorm against spirits? The Gauntlet across the school thickens, disrupting work everywhere.
