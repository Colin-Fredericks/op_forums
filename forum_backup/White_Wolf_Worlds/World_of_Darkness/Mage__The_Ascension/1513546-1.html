                Originally posted by voidshaper View Post
                Stranger in a strange land is a classic trope,
                While this is true, if four out of five PCs are travellers from an external culture, you really don’t wind up doing stranger in a strange land any more. The stranger character is a good narrative device because it provides the point of view for an audience who is not of the featured culture to learn about that culture, and gives the other characters a reason to show and explain aspects of their culture. When most main characters are each of a different culture, you wind up focussing a lot more on the differences between PC cultures.

                It seems to me that, rather than touching lightly on four or five different cultures, a Dark Ages game is a great opportunity to do a deep dive into one culture. Instead of hunting for excuses to pull in multiple other Traditions, expand the Tradition of the featured culture so that there is a variety of interesting niches for players to choose. When you don’t have Verbena hoovering up all the Life mages, the Order of Hermes doing the same for Forces mages, and so on, the single dominant Tradition of the culture should naturally expand - not necessarily to the point of becoming a monocultural Nine Traditions Council, but certainly such that there are multiple options for Affinity Sphere within the one Tradition. And even within Sphere, there can be groups with different Sphere specialties and rotes, different practices.

                Also, if you choose to set your game in a time and place of turmoil, you can easily have two clashing cultures. There may be war between them, or it may be more of a slow burn - new settlers, intermarriage, trade. But there’s always fringes where cultures meet.

                And then, there’s still the option of an out-of-place character, serving as your stranger in a strange land.

                So if you’ve got 5 players, you might have 5 from one culture (and expanded options to provide niches); or 4 plus a stranger; a mix from two local culture; or 2 and 2 from competing local cultures plus a stranger.

                    All Player Characters are, by definition, exceptional. They are Main Characters, made special by dint of having a player controlling them and being the center of a Storyteller's campaign. Even if they weren't also Mages, PCs would have license to be whatever exception you wanted. If a player wanted their PC to come from a different locale, that's their right. They're "allowed" to break normal rules.

                    Not, as we've established, that it's unusual for a person to be well traveled in the middle ages. They do it all the time. The idea of homogenous populations that never interact with one another is a myth created by our own modern, reductive view of history, informed by equally reductive historical narratives. People moved around. Traders traded, mercenaries sought work, people got exiled, folks deserted from armies marching to foreign shores. People went on vacation. Even peasants might travel from village to village as part of pilgrimage. (Heck, you could do a lot with the idea of characters rushing off to the Crusades and returning having been turned to the ways of the Ahl-i-Batin.)

                    In a game all about Magick and pushing against what's possible, taking issue with "person in one location using magick style associated with another location" seems to go against the spirit of the game. You're already playing people who can turn flower petals into pegasi. You can suspend your precious disbelief about a Viking adventuring in central France.

                        Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
                        If a player wanted their PC to come from a different locale, that's their right.
                        No, it’s not. It’s a game played by the mutual consent of the participants. There’s no rights beyond what the group agrees to.

                        There’s not necessarily any problem with playing MDA using characters from all over the world, but it’s also perfectly okay to put together a game which features exploration of one culture, or a small set of cultures. If there’s one player who doesn’t want to do that, nobody is going to force them to participate.

                        Also, it’s pretty common for GMs to propose a game in which characters should fit certain requirements. It’s not outlandish to ask for only Mystics, for example. (It was once the default mode of play for M:tA.) Asking for characters from particular cultures isn’t much different.
