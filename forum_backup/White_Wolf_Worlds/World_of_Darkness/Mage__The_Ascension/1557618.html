    Rapid-fire questions about some merits (and other specific things)

    01-14-2026, 05:18 PM
    As the title says, this isn't a coherent post, but a mix of questions that bug me and I can't see how each of them merits a dedicated singular post. The majority is about some merits in The Book of Secrets, while others are completely unrelated:

    1) The Merit Dual Affiliation (7pt - p. 79) gives examples of characters capable of joining enemy factions or joining friendly factions in the same group (Ex.: Dreamspeaker/Ecstatic). Does it make sense to apply this merit for Technocratic characters, say a Progenitor that ends up joining the NWO to fight scientific negationism hands-on? Or will the rigid hierachical nature of the Technocracy prohibit such an affiliation? Maybe, it's the case that since for the higher-ups it doesn't matter from which convention you are, every member of the Techocracy is a Technocrat first and a member of a Convention second, makes this merit useless in this context?

    2) Since the spheres and techocratic spheres don't have expressive mechanical differences between them, but the book ilustrates how their perceived difference is satiric (i.e. everyone is doing essentially the same thing but still fight tooth and nail for capital R "Reality"), can a character have the Sphere Inept Flaw (6 pt - p. 94) in a sphere but no problem learning the same sphere from a opposed faction paradigm? For example, a materialist Technocrat can't fathom the notion of a "Spirit World" and has great difficulty learning the Spirit sphere, but if a fellow Void Enginner approaches them explaining that what the RDs call "Spirit" is just "Dimensional Science", can he surpass the limitation of the Flaw?

    3) If a character shares the Merit Twin Souls (4pt - p. 75) and their twin ascends or inverts their Avatar, does the character feels a similar backlash to this event as the twin dying (Things Man Was Not Meant to Know)?

    4) I can visualize a Master from each faction as it pertains to their apresentation, be it a Hermetic that can summon a meteor with one word, a Verbena that can move the world spilling her blood in the soil, a VA that can cross countries entering a computer and leaving at the other side, but how a Hollower Master presents himself? Being more specific: what an Archmage from the Hollow Ones looks like?

    5) Given that the Uplift Ideal is central to the Technocratic mission and the limitations around instruments and Arete for Technocrats, is there space for Personal Ascension? Or is this kind of transcendence seen as supertitionist nonsense?

    And... I think this is it (for now). Feel free to respond to any of these questions in a direct or more roundabout way.

      1. Yes, it can make sense for a Technocrats to be a member of two different Conventions, given an appropriate explanation.

      2. IMHO, Sphere Inept ought to affect all variations of the Sphere. If you have Sphere Inept (Correspondence), it will apply to Data and Void Correspondence as well.

      5. It's generally viewed as a Superstitionist thing; but the thing about Personal Ascension is that it's personal, and anyone can pursue it and ultimately achieve it. More generally, I view it as being unique to the Traditions as an institutional thing: if the Traditions share any goals besides opposition to the Technocracy, it is to promote Personal Ascension; as mentioned above, the Technocratic Union views Personal Ascension as nonsense, and Union members seeking it will have to fight the system to Ascend; and the a Crafts as a whole are agnostic on Personal Ascension, with some being supportive, others antagonistic, and others neutral.

              Summoning a meteor with a word depends a great deal on what we mean by summon a meteor.

              At the lower end, a mage could look up into the night sky at a meteor that is already falling and use Correspondence to move it somewhere. This is line of sight Correspondence. It the meteor is small enough the mage could do it with Correspondence 2 and some really good gloves. Otherwise Correspondence 3 or 4.

              If the meteor isn't already falling, the mage will have less of a connection to the rock (its not in sensory range, for one), so that would increase the difficulty, though not the Correspondence level.

              If the meteor isn't already falling (but is close enough to pull down in any reasonable time) and the mage wants to make it fall, rather than teleporting it to himself, then we are into Forces/Correspondence territory. A meteor the size of a Buick would still only be Forces 3/Correspondence 2*, though depending on its trajectory the number of successes might be quite high. Entropy could also work if the meteor was already in a rapidly decaying orbit and the mage is manipulating where and when its inevitable fall occurs.

              *The spell is Forces 3/Correspondence 2, but a mage could only do it if he had Correspondence 3.

              If it isn't close enough, the mage is back to teleporting one close enough to fall in a reasonable amount of time, which raises the Correspondence needed. If the asteroid is closer in size to a mile, that is Forces 4 instead of 3. One many miles in size is Forces 5.

              If it doesn't exist at all, the mage will have to make it. Making it in front of himself would be Matter 2/Prime 2 for a meteor of simple composition, and add Forces 2 if it appears moving at speed. Successes required are based on size and speed, but meteors tend to only be going a few hundred miles per hour by the time they hit the ground.

              Making it in space adds Correspondence and making it in orbit mandates Forces, since by definition something in orbit is moving at high speeds (the ISS is moving at 17,500 mph). Matter doesn't scale with the size of the asteroid, but Forces does. Correspondence skill necessary will scale with Forces. The number of successes required will scale with both size and speed.

              Repeating the Chicxulub impact is definitely a feat of mastery (regardless of how) and would require an unsettling number of successes, but there's a lot that can be done at lower sphere levels.

                      Hey there, I wanted to revive this post for a second, since I have another question about the Flaw Sphere Inept. Just like the Merit Sphere Natural, The Book of Secrets suggests to the player that the Sphere in which the character is a Natural/Inept should influence other aspects of said character. For example, in the entry below the XP charts for the Sphere Inept:

                      Again, this Flaw can apply to only one Sphere (fortunately!),
                      and comes through in your practice and backstory. Someone
                      who’s inept at understanding, say, Time will have a very wonky
                      relationship with schedules and timing.
                      Besides the aforementioned example of the Time sphere, I imagined that a Mage inept in Spirit would be fervently materialistic in his approach to magick. An inept in Life could be someone who gets alergies from a simple breeze, an inept in Correspondence could have an horrible sense of direction, an inept in Forces could have a specially static view of reality. These are some examples that I came up with without thinking too much about it, but my point is that these spheres that I mentioned seems easy enough to imagine how a Mage would be affected by being a Natural/Inept in them.

                      But an Inept for Entropy? What, does the Mage in question have a serious bad case of bad luck? Would someone Inept in Matter be dressed in rags? Is the Inept in Mind an extremely guillible person? Worst of all is Prime, would a Mage inept in it be a magnet for Paradox?

                      These questions of mine is less about the RAW of the Merit/Flaw, but rather how to best connect the Merit/Flaw with the narrative aspects of the character.

                          Originally posted by Agrat View Post
                          But an Inept for Entropy? What, does the Mage in question have a serious bad case of bad luck? Would someone Inept in Matter be dressed in rags? Is the Inept in Mind an extremely guillible person? Worst of all is Prime, would a Mage inept in it be a magnet for Paradox?

                          These questions of mine is less about the RAW of the Merit/Flaw, but rather how to best connect the Merit/Flaw with the narrative aspects of the character.
                          A lot of this depends on how much you want this to affect their lives. You could have something with more narrative weight or something that inflicts difficulty penalties in specific circumstances. It’s also worth noting that it doesn’t have to always be a negative effect, but it shouldn’t be predictable.

                          The Time example could be any of the three. A wonky relationship with timing and schedules could be something the ST can just weave into the strory. Or it could be a +1 difficulty to any roll where exact timing matters. Or it could be that the character never manages to show up when he wants to but always shows up when he needs to.

                          For Entropy a good narrative effect would be that the character’s actions often have unintended consequences, usually minor but not always. Bad luck is a good mechanical penalty, but another one is difficulty telling when someone is lying (+1 difficulty to see through deception).

                          Mind Inepts might have a similar mechanical problem with seeing someone’s motivations, or they might frequently read wrong to other people, sometimes seeming to have the wrong emotional state for the situation (which could be mechanical or narrative).

                          For Prime a wonky aura or resonance might be appropriate, perhaps the problem sometimes flares attracting specific kinds of notice or setting people on edge. Or alternatively they read as an almost blank resonance (since Prime mages tend to pick up resonance easily). Or perhaps they are naturally drawn to places and events with great resonance (translation: the ST has justification to place places and events of great resonance in their paths without warning).

                          Matter Inepts could, like Dr Grant in Jurassic Park, have a tendency to break mechanical things in inconvenient ways or be slightly more likely to knock things over. These are yet more things that could be a mechanical effect (+1 difficulty) or a narrative one (in this case, failed rolls and especially botches are more likely to involve the previously mentioned effects).

                          Does that help?
