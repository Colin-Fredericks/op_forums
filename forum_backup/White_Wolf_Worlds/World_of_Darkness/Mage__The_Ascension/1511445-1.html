        Woad would count as an object, technically. Also, it should be possible to do this to a person as well. This is one area where the writeup of Prime gets very iffy.

        Prime 2 describes infusing weapons and attacks with quintessence in order to hurt spirits and inflict or protect against aggravated damage. The very next line it says: "On a related note, she may also – with Life 2 for simple organisms, or Life 3 for complex ones – consecrate a living thing at this Rank, as if that life-form were an object described above."

        If you read that paragraph in isolation, as you are likely to do if you are quickly referencing the section, it is very clearly saying you can enchant living creatures to take and resist aggravated damage. The sentence immediately follows the description of enchanting weapons. It is in the same paragraph with the description of enchanting weapons. Enchanting objects is the only other thing in that paragraph, and the sentence itself starts with "On a related note," It's not just a reasonable interpretation of what is written, it is the most reasonable interpretation of those words in that order. Prime 2 even lists Enchant Patterns in its header, so it tracks with what the book says that Rank can do. If it wanted to limit the effect to objects, the header should say Enchant Objects.

        On the other hand, Prime 3's header lists Enchant Life and its writeup specifically mentions (with the same nonsensical Life 3 requirement) enchanting living things to inflict or endure aggravated damage. The wording here is more direct. Also, Prime 1 has a definition for consecration

        So we have two alternatives. Either the same effect has duplicate writeups in both Prime 2 and Prime 3, which is quite possible given some of the other editing issues in the spheres writeups, or we completely ignore the words "On a related note" and the proximity, and we interpret the sentence as applying to the consecration effect described in Prime 1. Given the context of Prime 1 and Prime 3 I think the latter was the intention, but you'd never even consider that if you were just grabbing a quick reference.

        And don't get me started on the ridiculous way extra Life requirements are thrown into both sections with no regards for the capabilities of Life or Prime.

                    On an unrelated note, there's a method I've been using to figure out sphere requirements: I break down the spell into Main Effect (X) on Subjects (Y) with conditions (Z).

                    X is just the primary effect stated as simply as possible, with no consideration for complications.

                    Y is what it takes to get and keep the spell working on what you want it working on. By default a spell can be cast on any complete pattern in normal sensory range, but it won't move with the target. This step covers what you need to extend beyond that default, so elements like Range Extension (Correspondence and Time), Locking Effects (Pattern Spheres), affecting parts of patterns, etc.

                    Z covers any other modifications you need to make to the spell to get it to behave in specific ways, most commonly triggers and exceptions.

                    So for instance, a spell to teleport any contestant who crosses out of bounds back to the starting line might break down like:

                    Main effect: A gateway spell where the opening is the out of bounds line and the exit is the starting line. We don't tend to think of gateways having different sized entrances and exits but there's not really a reason they have to. Correspondence 4.

                    Subject: The subject is the locations themselves. If it is a long course the mage might need range extenders to cast it over the area, however the spell is already at Corr 4 so that doesn't matter.

                    Conditions: When I picture this spell I picture everything looking normal until the line is crossed, so light and other forces need to pass through the region normally. M20 doesn't give much explanation of how to make exceptions, but the few examples we have (Wards and Bans) points to needing the same rank you would need to affect a pattern directly. Forces 2 would be the easiest way, though you could also handle this by making the gate only work for Life and Matter.

                    Overall: Correspondence 4/Forces 2 for this method.

                    By breaking things down this way I get an idea of what each sphere is doing and how the requirements might change if we consider a different primary effect (e.g. if instead of a gateway, the primary effect is a direct teleportation of the contestant (Corr 3)).

If the mage wants to absorb that Quintessence - even if the matter was conjured from Quintessence in the first place - Prime 3 would be required. (Quite possibly another mage with Prime 3 could channel that Quintessence as it is released, probably without even needing Matter.)
