    Note: Overpopulation can be real. It just isn't what they used to worry about. When the Medieval Climate Anomaly suddenly went away, the Inuit suffered brutally, but the Norse Greenlanders suddenly found that they could no longer feed themselves or their cattle. They experienced overpopulation relative to their resources. Many cultures have experienced environmental changes that collapsed their ability to feed themselves. The higher the population, the more brutal the collapse. Imperialism has several times caused this type of overpopulation by forcing improper farming methods on the people (many late 19th century Indian famines,) or by warping the local society and economy ( Ireland in the 1840s and after).

            Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post
            If you wanted to play upper class English high society, you already wanted to play members of a evil genocidal institution, the Union reflecting that is just being honest about this era of world history.
            Then why are they a playable option at all?

            Frankly if this is the stance the book is going to take about the Order of Reason, and if the developers were that concerned about OoR being used by bad faith players...

            They shouldn't be a playable option. Period.

            Remove their chapter and stick it in the Antagonists section, remove that choice period from players completely, and make it a Traditions and Crafts only time period.

            Otherwise as is, it's sending mixed messages on what the intent of playable OoR are. And at face value saying that "only play OoR if your table agrees to play Evil Mages" from OOC point of view restricts the kind of chronicles and stories that could be told at individual tables, which frankly is bad for everyone.

            (going to put a pin on OoR for now, since I'm not done reading this chapter)
            --------------------------------------------------
            I HAVE, however, finished reading the Traditions chapter and....it's a mixed bag.

            -Ahl-i-Batin: So right off the bat...the write-up mentions that Batini are heavily involved with the Ottoman Empire...and considering how much they shit talk Western Imperialism that's kind of an issue since Ottomans were engaged in their own atrocities before and during this time period (nvm the ones that they're going to do after the Victorian era...) Some of these atrocities that should be the kind to get Choristors and Greek Euthanatoi to stick the Batini on their Shit List.

            So either this is inconsistent writing, or the Batini are in denial that the Ottomans are failing and refuse to acknowledge their crimes.

            ...but apart from that, the rest of the write up is actually pretty solid. Particularly the sub-faction listed that sounds like a proto-version of the Paradise Garden. That the way how they describe interacting with Ottoman politics, using Mind Effects to either influence particular politicians or flush out corrupt ones, describing their use of Corr Effects by comparing it to telegrams, and even saying that they're leaning to learning technomancy...it makes them sound like mystical-NWO. Which is actually a far more interesting take then just M20 core's "wandering ascetics that believe in Unity".

            It's honestly to the point that it makes me consider playing one, clicking with me in a way that the M20 write-up didn't.

            (and more justification for me on the "sub-factions should have been mentioned in M20 core")

            -Akashics: Very amused at painting them as folk heroes which in the context of this era...yeah, sure I'd be up for some Drunken Master 2 and Ip Man shenanigans (yes I'm aware the Ip Man movies take place decades after the Victorian Era, hush now. It's still about a folk hero taking on imperialist forces, just a different one). Also, am glad for at least the mention of Akashics operating in Asian immigrant communities.

            Although I am weirded out how the Boxer Rebellion Future Fates sidebar...seems to be trying to downplay it which is odd.

            -Celestial Chorus: As I said further upthread, yeah I'm digging how their actual splat write-up gives them more nuance and doesn't say that ALL of the Choristors are up for converting the """savages""" to a more civilized way of belief. And even then, it is at least grounded in the Choristor's fear of their Paradigm being eroded away, and doing whatever it takes to preserve it.

            -Dreamspeakers: I'm...mixed on the Dreamspeakers.On the one hand, their write up does highlight other animist cultures and takes more step to distance itself as "the Native American group". But that not only have all Dreamspeakers across the world have been in communication with each other, but all of them mutually agree to be united against colonialism seems...way too clean.

            Especially since DSs have always been depicted as having the loosest/non-existent organization of all the Traditions, but they all agreed to the same thing.

            I get what the write-up is going for: that out of all the Traditions the DSs would be impacted the most by colonialism. And that they would try to make a unified front to combat it...I think it's just stretching credibility way too much that they all unanimously agree on it, and with the Tradition known for being the least organized. And at worse it dips into problematic race color-blinding.

            -Ecstatics/Sahajiya: A bit whelmed. Feels about the same as they are in modern day Mage. Not much to say, honestly. I get the angle of wanting people to break free from stuffy and constricting Victorian social norms, it's just that context isn't drastically different enough from how they are in a present day game.

            -Euthanatoi/Chakravanti: This actually makes a bigger effort to clearly thread the needle on the relationship between the Death and Fate aspects of the Entropy sphere in how the Euthanatoi view it and it's great. The bit about them taking on the role of anti-heroes and vigilantes...like the classic Western gunslinger. It's not bad by any stretch, just surprising. Like, it's definitely a strange association to imagine an Euthanatoi as The Man with No Name, Doc Holliday, or Sanjuro

            -Order of Hermes: ...and I'm disappointed that this went almost too far into "all Hermetics are complicit in colonialism and think Western Imperialism is Great-for-Everyone". To the point that if a group takes this depiction of them as is, and for whatever reason they don't have access to other depictions of the Hermetics, there'd be the sense of "ok, so you're playing the Villain of the game". Or at worse, with the other Traditions being so against...why would any of them want to work with the Hermetics?

            Only time I could see this as an exception is if you do a game set in the heart of the British Empire and having everyone in the cabal be attached/hiding among the occultist and spiritualist movement and circles (so being with an Ecstatic, a Verbena), but with the whole conceit of the book's setting being about looking at people of groups and communities that aren't white Europeans or any other group who benefits directly from colonialism, that's going to severely limit the kind of games to have a Hermetic in. Not without Golden Ruling all over them.

            (also, counter to all the other Traditions listed, incredibly disappointed that it lists of the names of Hermetic Houses but...doesn't actually say anything about them)

            -Verbena: Same as Ecstatics: for the most part being pretty similar too their modern day counterparts and nothing too exciting and different to go over.

                    Just finished the Order of Reason and it was about what I expected. To echo what others have said, this is the Order at it's morally darkest, perhaps the darkest of the entire combined history of the Order and the Union. This is not the halcyon days of the Renaissance and their noble beginnings, this is the Order drunk off Victory and Power and Corruption. While without doubt there are noble groups and individuals within every Bloc and Convention, the Order they all work for has become a monster, driving the engines of Industrialization and Imperialism across the globe, with all the good and bad that entails.

                    All and all, I love how the Order was handled in this chapter. We see how their ideals have become twisted and corrupted, driving them to backing some very awful ideas in this time. We can see where the future Technocracy came from and it's not a pretty sight. Even the Void Seekers were involved with the darker side of their explorations, something their modern counterparts would deny which for me adds more character to both the Seekers and their successors.

                    And yet, the noble ideas that birthed the Order of Reason still remain beneath the blood and dirt that soaks the Order during this era. Just as with the era itself, there is more going on with the Order than blood-soaked Imperialism and the remorseless grinding engine of Industrialization. New ideas and technologies are making the lives of the Masses better. Advances in medicine are treating and curing diseases that plagued people in earlier times. Developments in communications and travel technology is bringing the world closer together. Political and social ideas born of the previous century are being developed and refined. The Middle Class is growing as old Feudal ways of thinking are fading away.

                    It's changing, complicated time for the World and the Order. Good and Bad walk hand in hand here. The Good of what's being done does not absolve the Bad being done but at the same time, the Bad does not erase the Good.

                    The Chapter is not painting the Victorian Order of Reason as evil monolith of greedy Imperialists and blackhearted villains but simply as people. People that are products of their time and are as flawed as anyone else.

                    Anyway on to less depressing talk...

                    The Blocs within the Order are an interesting idea. I wasn't entirely sure if I was going to like them or not but I ended up liking them. They add an interesting layer to the politics of the Order and could be intriguing to explore within chronicles and fiction. The Conventions themselves are... well the Conventions of the Order. We had long known most of them from previous books even if some of them have become the Blocs of this era instead. The Golden Guild being the one exception as we had know the Syndicate predecessor as just the Invisible Exchequers. Though they did turn the Skeleton Keys into Awakened investigators prior to joining the Order which I'm fairly certain was a retcon. But that's not a big sticking point for me as now we'd be potentially playing them and having them all or mostly being unAwakened would be potentially limiting and annoying. Though I will admit it does undermine the original awesomeness of the Keys being just ordinary men and women investigating and facing down the evils of the WoD.

                    When I first saw that High Guild had been split into the Golden Guild and the Invisible Exchequers, I didn't get why we needed them split other than their Bloc needed another group within it. Then I got to their sections and better understood. This is the age that Wall Street and other modern financial institutions were born and would be a different beast from the businesses that the older High/Golden Guild leaders would be more familiar with, leading to the rise of younger minds that do understand these new concepts.

                    Though I will admit that the Electrodyne Engineers using the symbol of the Craftmasons is a bit on the nose for my liking. It feels more like something the modern SoE would claim, that they are true successors of the Craftmasons and their ideals, than something that would have actually happened. Plus it did kind of come across like they were saying that the Engineers are the lone 'good guys' of the Luminaries of this era which I don't think should be the case.

                    Homo sapiens. What an inventive, invincible species. It's only a few million years since they crawled up out of the mud and learned to walk. Puny, defenceless bipeds. They've survived flood, famine and plague. They've survived cosmic wars and holocausts. And now, here they are, out among the stars, waiting to begin a new life. Ready to outsit eternity. They're indomitable. Indomitable.
