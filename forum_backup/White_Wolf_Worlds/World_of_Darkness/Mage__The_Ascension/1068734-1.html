        Originally posted by Firanai View Post
        I assume that sorcerers exist because as far as I'm concerned any major magical group, tradition or technocracy, it doesn't matter, wouldn't be able to exist without them. They're the middle managers that keep the organization running, keeping an eye on the unawakened members and allowing mages to focus on the big decisions.
        Sorcerers are also important for the purpose of passing knowledge down through the generations, especially if Awakened recruits are sparse. Game mechanics aside, the Focus of a given group is largely the same, regardless of whether the members of that group are Awakened or not. Hermetic Sorcerers draw the same magic circles, Choristers sing the same hymns, Akashics all learn Do as a martial art/lifestyle, etc. It's just that Mages can take the same methods as their Sorcerer compatriots, and take them farther.

        The Verbana in particular makes extensive use of Sorcery, as family lines pass knowledge of the Old Ways down the generations, from parent to child. As it's unlikely for each generation to produce an individual that will Awaken, Sorcery becomes the default assumption among these families. Any family member who does Awaken is considered particularly talented or gifted, and is provided with all the old spell books and items that only they could properly use. The fundamental framework of witchcraft, however, is taught as a matter of course. Same with the Order of Hermes, where High Sorcery is considered standard curriculum.

                    Originally posted by Enginseer-42 View Post
                    See, my problem with that is primers exist. Meaning you can teach Awakening.
                    It's possible to induce Awakening. That doesn't mean it's guaranteed, even with a Primer. Not everyone who reads a Primer will "get it", and not every reader will try. Some are diametrically opposed to the world view espoused by a given Primer, and actively resist internalizing its lessons (assuming they bother to finish reading it). Some Primers are just so dense, abstract, and old that it takes real effort and additional scholarship to grasp the meaning.

                    When I say "old", I mean the Primers were written centuries or even millennia ago, and in contexts that modern readers may have trouble grasping. Even if we assume a Primer has been translated (which takes a great deal of effort to do, while allowing the text to retain its enchantment), words may change dramatically in accepted meaning between one generation, let alone the historical time scales we're working with. The Kitab Al-Alacir is one of the most widely used Primers, not because it's any more effective than other Primers in Awakening people, but because it's been the beneficiary of numerous successful translations. If a translation hasn't been "updated" in a century, though, the meaning behind word choices could be lost to anyone who isn't familiar with the old contexts with which those words were used. Or, more simply, dedicated scholars of obscure linguistics.

                    Indeed, many groups don't translate their Primers into more accessible languages at all. Partly because it's difficult to do so while not "breaking" the enchantment on the text. And partly because they may decide that only the truly dedicated and educated should be trusted with Awakening, if it can be helped. It's the same reason why Hermetic education involves learning several languages - only someone with the force of Will needed to jump through their hoops should be allowed access to their magic.

                    The various mage groups also likely keep careful control about how their Primers are distributed. Sure, it's the Text and not the medium it's written on that makes a Primer, but there are dangers to letting that Text proliferate too widely. If anyone on the street could get their hands on the Kitab Al-Alacir, for instance, it would mean that anyone on the street could potentially Awaken...in a setting where the Traditions or other groups would not know about it. That person could end up being picked up by the Technocracy - or the Nephandi - bolstering the ranks of the Traditions' enemies. At least when folks like Society of Ether hand a copy to hopeful recruits, there's the understanding that if it doesn't work out, the text can be retrieved or destroyed later. To release a copy of a Primer out into the world, with no controls in place, is a risk. And if they're going to do it, it must be a calculated risk.

                    Magestuck: The Ascension
