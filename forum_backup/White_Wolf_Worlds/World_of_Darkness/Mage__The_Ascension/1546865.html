    Damage over time

    12-11-2024, 10:23 AM
    Are there any rules for magical damege over time?
    Personally i homebrew the durations of spells but im interested how in RAW does a spell that lights someone on fire (dealing dmg) constantly for lets say a day work mechanically. Cant find it in the book.

      It's on page 538 in M20. But it's not written for turn by turn ongoing damage. Instead it is for longer term damage over time spells, and in addition to duration you have to pay successes for each interval within that duration. Keep in mind, though that the first level of duration takes it to one scene, which automatically makes it longer than a spell that lasts for a few turns.

      For setting someone on fire, I'd just default to the actual fire rules for as long as the spell keeps them on fire.

          Certain sorts of damaging effects will also impose damage over a longer period of time, just because of what sort of damage it is. This is mostly in the realm of secondary effects, such as using higher level Forces or Matter effects to create poisons or radiation, or Life effects which influence the spread of disease or simulate particular conditions.

          Being able to do this sort of thing depends heavily on understanding what you're trying to create or impose well enough to produce it with the appropriate effects, and that's reflected by one sort of skill or another. If you want to give someone radiation poisoning, you do need to have some good understanding of what radiation is, as knowledge of Forces and Prime by itself doesn't impart an understanding of such phenomena. (If a character has directly observed radiation of some sort using Forces perception, they might well be able to simply copy that pattern to produce it, but they won't necessarily fully understand the practical effects of what they're playing with.)

                      I could be wrong, but I'd assume the OP (given references in other threads) is somewhat inspired by the way Awakening 2e handles this, as Awakening has a concrete mechanical answer to the question where Ascension does not.

                      IME though, offensively it tends to be overkill in either game; save a small niche of when you're effectively trying to nullify another supernatural's healing abilities by causing them enough damage over time that you follow up attacks don't get healed and thus you don't get stuck being ineffectual in net damage. Defense tends to be a better use case for characters without the oomph to give themselves werewolf-esque levels of regeneration, but can give themselves significantly better healing than humans would have coupled with not having to consciously activate it each time.

                      What happens in Ascension if you heal yourself for 4 damage but extend the duration to a day has no real answer, and there isn't something equivalent like blast a vampire with sunlight magically where you can just point to the VtM rules for sunlight exposure to dictate what happens next.

                      As it stands, Ascension doesn't have any sample rules that really offers any suggestions on how to handle an effect that's between "single instant casting that heals X damage and that's that (maybe a conditional hung spell so it happens when you need it later)," and, "high level Life magic for full super-regeneration."

                                              I know a lot of people don't like "How do You Do That?", mysellf included (although it does have some good, very usable rules), but it does have rules for damage over time

                                              P.37
                                              If, for example, Chopper wants to set up a super-cold wall of ice that lasts one day and inflicts six levels of damage to anyone who touches it, her player would need to roll a total of six successes: three for the Duration, three for the Damage. Remember that the Forces Sphere automatically adds one success to the Damage. If Chopper decides to make that wall of plasma-energy (Forces) rather than ice (Matter), she needs only five successes because one success is already taken care of by the Forces Sphere.
                                              The examples are for ice and plasma walls, but the logic would apply in your examples as well, I guess.

                                              Also, it seems the damage would apply everytime someone touches it, so it would deal that X damage each round.
                                              It seems a bit excessive, but that is Mage…
