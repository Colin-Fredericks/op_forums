    That would be really dumb if it was the case.

    I think it's better explained in Revised (pg 193): "By pulling time back into a loop, the mage causes a small area to suffer a local "rewind" of time. The mage herself remains immunized against this Effect due to her command of Time magic (otherwise he wouldn't know that he'd done anything and the looping would be almost pointless). From there, the mage can change her actions and responses to a given situation, already knowing how it would turn out otherwise. By combining life Life and Mind with the Effect, the mage can actually rewind herself physically and undo the effects of physical trauma, while still retaining her memory of the events that never happened."

                                                    Cynic01
                                                    So the way you rule it is:
                                                    Time 3: Everyone is aware that time has reversed, new actions can be done however they want.
                                                    Time 3, Mind 3, Life 2: Only the time traveller is aware that time has reversed, and everyone will act as they had done unless changed

                                                    I don't like that. It takes everything that's special about being a time traveller, being the only guy to know about what will happen and using your secret knowledge to aid in pinpoint ways, and throws it away.

                                                    The way I saw it saw like this:

                                                    Time 3: Only the time traveller is aware that time has reversed, and everyone will act as they had done unless changed.
                                                    Extra successes can be used to "extend" the awareness of the time loop to other people (and if you really want, you can have a Mind requirement to target other mages minds)

                                                    Time 3, Mind 3, Life 2: You can reverse time for your body, essentially healing yourself or reversing disease, without reversing your mind.
                                                    To do this to other people, you may need Mind 4, Life 3.

                                                    Similarly, other spheres can be used to reverse time for specific things rather than general things. You want to undo the draining of a node? Prime with Time 3. You want to return a damaged object to its original form? Mater with Time 3. You want to reverse the decaying process? Entropy with Time 3. You want see exactly where a bullet was shot from? Forces and Time 3.

                                                    Regarding what range Time 3 effects, that depends on paradigm. If your mage sees time as a constant everywhere, then when they loop time, time is looped everywhere (though only sensed at the sight of casting). If your mage sees time as flexible and relative, then when they loop time it may only be in a localised area, in which case they'll need Corr 3 in order to reverse time for specific things.
                                                    This is more of an optional rule that I like to use, where paradigm defines what the mage believes their spheres are capable off, and whether they need extra spheres or different levels to do the job. It makes things complicated, but I find it makes magic a bit more personalised, and drives players away from the Purple Paradigm.

                                                        Saikou

                                                        Time 3: Not everyone is aware time has reversed unless they have time magic but they might end up taking different actions; part of my logic comes from an earlier section in How do you do that pg 107 to 108

                                                        The Unpredictable Nature of Time
                                                        To start with, treat time as an imprecise series of events whose results cannot be accurately understood. Every change to an event results in other changes to related events. And so, when a player uses Time magick to look backward, rewind time, or otherwise alter the timestream, the Storyteller can also use those events to retcon events in unpredictable ways. Sure, that Ecstatic weirdo thinks she can simply sidestep time to prevent a bullet from hitting her… and she can even succeed. When the events replay themselves, however, her would-be shooter picks another target instead, with results that she had not expected when she rewound time. Although the Storyteller should play fair with such variations, there’s no reason that time should hop to the tune of a single character. Events and people, after all, are unpredictable, and not even a full Master of Time can accurately control what will happen, only assume what might happen.
                                                        Time 3 Life 3 Mind 1: Not only do they remember exactly what everyone else was doing they remember exactly what they were doing. It means they wont cause the timeline to change ever so slightly because their arm was off by 45 degrees or their feet were in a slightly different spot or they turned their head to left instead of the right. They are exactly as they were in the first go through up moving exactly as they did their body in the same state until the exact moment they choose to change it. The section also provides a limit to the Reboot effect How do you do that pg 111
                                                        The Storyteller may declare that it’s just not possible to reboot a character more than five turns into the past. Alternately, he could simply say, “Sure, you think you remember something about things having gone differently before, but everybody else remembers them going the way they’re going now… so perhaps you DID go back in time, but this present is the result of your actions back then.”
                                                        Five turns would be the max before that temporal drift from earlier seeps through even the mystical memory such is the nature of messing with time.
