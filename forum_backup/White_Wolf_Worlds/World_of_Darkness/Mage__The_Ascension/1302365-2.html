    Originally posted by Bluecho View Post

    All this assumes that the Mage starts on a good or neutral footing, in respect to the law. That the Mage doesn't already have a target painted on their back, as far as the powers-that-be are concerned.

    A not insignificant number of Mages have been institutionalized, at one point or another. Think the Dreamspeaker that got sent to a mental asylum for the "voices in their head". The Children of Knowledge alchemist who got locked up in a previous life for cooking meth or other illicit substances. The Euthanatos killer who is wanted in eight states. The Orphan who lost their parents young (in an Awakening-induced disaster), and spent years in the foster system. An Ecstatic who had a stint in rehab. Even characters who were never IN an institution might have trouble with the law. Like any number of Mages who happen to control a Cult, which is or could be under the watchful eye of the authorities.

    Not to mention the clustercrap that arises from systemic prejudice. All because a Mage committed the "crime" of not being white, male, straight, or conforming to other social, behavioral, or religious norms. Minorities in our world get killed all the time by police, despite doing nothing illegal.

    None of these things make it any easier for the Mage, if she's caught carrying a gun. Someone who was a former mental patient or ex-con might have been stripped of their right to own guns. Under this or other conditions, the Mage can expect much worse treatment from authorities.
    Yes, most people are (almost unavoidably) on the grid. That's why at my table, it's common practice in the traditions to try and "obfuscate" your past as you enter into your life as a mage. Take steps to cover your tracks where possible, take a new name, change your appearance. This is why mages tend to have enigmatic and cool names; a James Smith can become a Darius Doyle if they want to. Members of houses sometimes take their House as a new surname; Shaea Hermetics call eachother sister/brother/mother/father/etc. and have Shaea as their surname (since they know so well the power a name can hold).

    Virtual Adepts are, well, adept at destroying and changing records. The "schizophrenic" native boy (a Dreamspeaker who was institutionalized when he Awakened) who is in mental health records can be changed digitally so that the truth is covered up (hopefully) forever. This means that it will be more difficult for the NWO to find details of Traditions Mages.

    Anyways yeah the overall sense at my table is that the Traditions have a strong idea of what sort of things the Union can exploit, and Mages take steps to protect themselves where possible. And this is why the NWO still values physical records! Harder to alter.

            Oh, I very much agree with a lot of this But I think it highly depends on the Mage in question, and also their age; That is, how long they have been awake.

            The state - heck, the actual moment of the event - of awakening fundamentally changes a person, at least when it comes to their very perceptions of the world.
            It can (and probably will) change more than just those over time, but the Mage also doesn't immediately drop all their morals, all their ingrained behaviors, social desires or relationships - the human psyche, even awakened, doesn't completely flip in an instant.

            In a way, where Vampire has the struggle between the vampire and their beast, where Werewolf has the struggle between a human or animal upbringing versus the strange new state of utter rage and gaian duty, Mage has its struggle between a simple human self, the awakened senses that are bound to change your mind, and the Avatar trying to steer and influence the Mage mentally and physically towards...well. The ways of the Avatar can be enigmatic.
            But strong Avatars certainy have a much bigger impact thank weak ones (Background-dot-wise, not actual Avatar-power-wise, given that the background even states that Avatar 1 can also mean a very powerful and ancient Avatar that is simply not very invested in you).
            It's why I see taking the Avatar background at 4-5 as quite a big deal for a character's RP.

            So yeah, every Mage turns at least a bit more alien compared to sleepers of course, but the degree varies on how much the Mage clings to what they have, on the influence of the Avatar, and the general mindset of the person in question.
            There are some that (mostly) manage to live a somewhat 'normal' life...heck, Destiny's Price is pretty much "Struggles of Mages vs. Everyday Life - The Book".
            But many Mages depart from it, I agree; Both the Order Of Hermes as well as the Technocracy in general have their formal departure from their "Oldlife" for a reason - it makes this step easier in the way of making it mandatory, and then leaves it to the Mage to decide how much they want to interact with the Everyday.

            The other Traditions however do not really have such a formal departure, and here's where, I think, a lot of the Mages can be found that try to find a balance between the new and the old, or actually struggle more and more over time with keeping their old friends and assets - last but not least because they might lose the *desire* to do so.

            In the end the most frightening thing about Mages is, as you said - that they can look perfectly human, but mentally can be completely alien. And vice versa...you can have a Mage with an almost childlike joy of being able to change their body in downright bizarre ways, but fundamentally have a very relatable mind.
            With a Mage, you never know what you are looking at. You don't know how understandable or inhuman their logic or thoughts are, in their awakened state of dementia.

            Also, it's why https://www.drivethrurpg.com/images/4261/162241.jpg is one of my favorite depictions of a Mage. It's a wonderful example of one that is...very..eerie.

                                Hell, in Mage, the Rule of Cool is a literal active force in-universe. That's what Mythic Threads are. At least in M20, a Mage adhering to what's expected of their magickal style can get reductions in difficulty, or even make Vulgar Effects Coincidental under the right circumstances.

                                A Hermetic wizard in the modern day could wear sunglasses and a leather jacket, wielding a gun etched with Enochian script. There's even plenty of practical reasons to do so. But if the wizard stays true to their heritage, going Full Wizard - flowing robes, pointed hat, ornate staff, ritual dagger, etc - the Hermetic could get a boost to casting. While the Consensus in general prefers the modern and technological, it remembers the Old Ways. It responds to the Old Ways, even if it still treats them as Vulgar. The same general effect that allows unAwakened Sorcerers to cast spells in the old manner at all, also aids the Mage, to some degree.

                                From that angle, wielding melee weapons in the modern day is a valid (albeit risky) choice, when it comes to channeling their classic Arts. It's not subtle, and guns beat all comers for a reason. But it's valid, if the Mage has the courage to embrace it.

                                            Guns aren't that good in the system (or reality). You want a minimum of 18 damage dice to average one-shot kills.... which requires some combination of high stats, advantageous situational modifiers, and high powered guns.                                                     There's certainly a possibility of having to cut ties as a mage awakens and needs training away from people that don't understand.

                                                    The Chorus, Dreamspeakers, and Verbena philosophies would all balk at the idea in general. All three are highly invested on a paradigm level of community being part of magic.

                                                    The Cult and Chakravanti are more willing to cut ties as necessary, but both highly value maintaining ties as much as possible. The Euthanatos esp. have this as one of their rules because it helps keep their mages grounded and free of Jhor.

                                                    The Virtual Adepts and Society of Ether are mixed. They don't have a specific philosophy around it, but it's highly likely for them to pursue their studies away from traditional community ties, and focus on their specialist communities instead.

                                                    The Order and the Akashayana are the two that actively encourage mages to seek detachment.
