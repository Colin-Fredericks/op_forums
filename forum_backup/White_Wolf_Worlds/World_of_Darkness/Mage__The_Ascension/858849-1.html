    Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
    Yes, because it's really that easy for someone - anyone - to Ascend, let alone their entire membership.

    The Traditions also have more than just personal Ascension as their goal. If that was it, they'd all retreat to Horizon realms and let the Technocracy have the world. What the Traditions want is to make their brand(s) of magick work on Earth again. They don't want to live in hiding, too afraid of Paradox and the watchful eyes of the Technocratic Union to perform anything but the most coincidental spells. Mages have the potential to do and be anything, yet are held back. They can't accept defeat, or surrender, or obscurity. They want to take back the world.

    Moreover, individual Traditions have moral and philosophical reasons for wanting to spread their Paradigm. The Celestial Chorus has its various religions, and want to combat the godlessness and sin present in the world. The Dreamspeakers have their various tribes, etc, that must be defended from an imperialistic mono-culture, one that would happily either destroy the ethnic groups or destroy their cultural identity. The Euthanatos see the Wheel in imbalance, and desire to serve its proper turning; healing or reforming those who can be saved, and giving the Good Death to those who cannot. Etc, etc.

    Not to mention bloody-minded pride and deep-seated animosity. The Traditions have been fighting too hard, for too long, to let the battle end with anything but victory.

    What this means is that the Traditions don't have the option for such a pat, easy Win Condition. They want many things, and the Technocracy is directly in competition for those things. Power in many forms, resources, newly Awakened Mages to mold, the hearts and minds of the common man, the ability to dictate what is right and wrong, and many more things besides. These are battle grounds on which the Traditions and the Technocracy (and the Disparates) compete. They have Win Conditions that are incompatible, but only because they are finite.

    The Nephandi do not share those as Win Conditions. They don't want to control anything...they just want to break everything. And it's far easier to break something than it is to build. Building is what the other sects want, while Breaking is what the Nephandi want. In this, they have the flexibility to choose their battles and battlefields. While the Traditions must react if the Technocracy tries to claim ground in the former's sphere of influence (and vice versa), the Nephandi can attack in ways and at times no one is expecting, for purposes that no other rational actors would consider. It is for this reason that Nephandi rarely engage in "open" conflict with other sects: why fight the enemy on the field when you can undermine them from within? Why fight over a well when you can poison it instead?
    Saracasm aside. Yes. It is. MUCH easier to Ascend than to destroy the universe by poking a hole in it (a beautiful, magnificent, wonderfully decadent hole of decay and destruction... because, ya know, the Cauls...) How do we know that? Because people HAVE Ascended (unless they haven't, but the general trend is towards that and away from the "Big Lie" concept). And the Universe hasn't been destroyed. Ergo, one is easier than the other. All other objectives of the Traditions aside, if they can serve as Ascension incubators (and they do) for individual mages to go from Awakened caterpillars to Ascended butterflies, then they refute the "Nephandi have the easiest road" hypothesis. In fact, It seems like the Nephandi's goal is more of a chore than the Union's Mass Ascension, due to the previously described lack of any semblance of internal organization and goal-oriented cooperation, which the Technocracy has in theory (completely) and functionally (not as much, but it's definitely there). (Unless of course, the whole Union is a front for some aberrant Nephandi plot that has held together for centuries despite the whole tendency to tear stuff apart, the Nephandi raise d'etre after all.)

    In fact, you have a 0th premise in that hypothesis as is. That destroying is easier than creating. It's definitely something we, in our culture, have internalized (up to and including how the zeitgeist interprets thermodynamic entropy), but that's not necessarily the view of others, particularly where magic and worldview is concerned. Evil can just as easily be seen as HARD WORK, because you are consciously going against the natural grain of creation.

        Even if destruction is easy, you still need a lot of coordination. For instance, if you want to bring down a particular mage... you can assault them, make them go paranoid, spread rumours they are going nephandi, actually turn them nephandi, and so on. Except those plans undermine each other, and without strong coordination, you'd have each nephandi following their own plans and getting in each others way. Same goes for plans targeting organisations; sowing internal distrust and assaulting them from outside are two things you don't want to be doing simultaneously.

        Above all else, it's this incredibly efficient coordination that makes no sense for the nephandi. They should be much more backstabby and treacherous than the traditions, for instance, not the perfect cogs of a well-oiled corruption machine.

                        Originally posted by Cheesefondue View Post
                        Even if destruction is easy, you still need a lot of coordination. For instance, if you want to bring down a particular mage... you can assault them, make them go paranoid, spread rumours they are going nephandi, actually turn them nephandi, and so on. Except those plans undermine each other, and without strong coordination, you'd have each nephandi following their own plans and getting in each others way. Same goes for plans targeting organisations; sowing internal distrust and assaulting them from outside are two things you don't want to be doing simultaneously.
                        Do you though? How much do those plans really undermine each other?

                        If four different Nephandi, an assaulter, a manipulator, a rumor monger, and a corrupter, all decide that a mage (let's call him Harvey) is in the way of their plans and get to work, what happens? The threat of the assaulter is the perfect fodder for the manipulator and corrupter to get close and go to work. Their plans might not interfere with each other at all, but even if they do one can out the other to gain Harvey's trust. They can even rat each other out, and their plans still go forward. The manipulator revealed gets Harvey jumping at the shadows, just as he wants. The corrupter revealed loses some ground, true, but she's far from the worst threat in Harvey's life, so she's not out of the picture yet. Meanwhile, a Harvey on the run has left himself open for the assaulter to do her work, and the rumor monger is starting to whisper "who is this Harvey that the Nephandi are fighting over him, must be a widderslainte..." And with his friends dwindling, the corrupter's offer is starting to look really appealing.

                        Harvey is screwed in any number of ways, likely can't interfere in whatever plans they needed him out of the way for, and the Nephandi didn't even have to talk to each other to do it. In fact they used each other as disposable pawns, which is the opposite of cooperation. At the worst they wasted resources.

                        Now, if the various Nephandi needed him for specific reasons, if the assaulter needed to sacrifice him brutally on a specific night and the corrupter needed to turn him Barrabi to have an inside man, then there might be a conflict. One Nephandus will have to go home disappointed. Or brutally murder his rival, I suppose. But how often do you think that happens, comparatively?

                        It's the same way for your organizational struggle. The enemy at the gates is the perfect source of tension. The infiltrators can capitalize on that to spread discord and frame potential reformers. Meanwhile the internal struggle ruins coordination and weakens defenses. Both groups benefit, even without any intentional cooperation, possibly without even knowing about each other (as Nephandi I mean, there's no way the infiltrators will miss the enemies at the gate).

                        Even if you accept the Nephandi chapter at face value, even if you accept that they are dominating the world, there's no need to presume any kind of incredibly efficient cooperation. The Nephandi have so many irons in the fire that every defeat is the grounds for another victory.

                                            There are two workable models for the Nephandi, IMHO:

                                            1) the Nephandi are a dangerous, organized force because their masters organize them.
                                            2) the Nephandi aren't a dangerous, organized force; individual Nephandi are dangerous to individual mages, but they lack the cohesiveness to be dangerous as a faction.

                                            Personally, I go with the second one. I don't need the Nephandi to be anything more than a rot that's eating away at the innards of the Traditions, Technocracy, and Disparates. No grand conspiracy of corruptors, plotting and coordinating in concert; just the disconcertingly common tendency of mages to succumb to their wiles.
