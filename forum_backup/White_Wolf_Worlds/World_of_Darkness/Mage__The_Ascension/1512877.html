    Multiple actions, especially in M20

    05-26-2023, 11:39 AM
    I’ve got a game in another ruies set shutting down soon, and the players have requested M:tA for our next game (out of the options I was prepared to run). So I’m in the process of figuring out the quirks of the M20 rules.

    And something that struck me was that the M20 rules for multiple actions are really harsh, especially in combat situations. Hear me out, as I examine some rules and history here…

    First, the various multiple-actions rules:
    • 1st Edition, when taking multiple actions, use the lowest die pool from among those actions, and split the dice between the actions.
      • If one if your dice pools are very different from each other, this is going to be harsh. 2 Arete, 4 Dex, 4 Dodge means if you want to cast a spell while dodging an attack to keep your hide intact, you’ll have only 1 die for each, and this will be no better than if you had 2 Arete, 1 Dex, 1 Dodge.
      • If both pools are low, everything will be very low.
      • If both pools are high, you may have a decent chance at success at both actions.
    • 2nd Edition… I don’t know, I didn’t bother to check. It’s the same as either 1st or Revised, and I don’t care exactly when it changed.
    • By Revised Edition, to take multiple actions, use the die pool for each action, but for the first action lose dice equal to the total number of actions. For the second and subsequent actions, subtract an additional die for each previous action. (So for two actions, -2 dice on the furst, -3 dice on the second. For three, -3/-4/-5.)
      • If one of your dice pools is low, you may not be able to combine it with even a high pool. 2 Arete, 4 Dex, 4 Athletics means no chance of casting while dodging.
      • If both pools are high, you can get a lot of dice out of this. But am I bothered by a highly skilled combatant who can throw a 7 dice dodge pool and an 8 dice attack together? Not necessarily.
    • M20 reverts to the 1st Edition version, with no sign of the Revised rule.
    Now, when I look at basic combat, I realise that any time your enemy has a weapon, things get difficult. It doesn’t take a very highly skilled attacker to be throwing 3 or even 4 dice, the odds of 0 successes are low once you have that pool. Lethal damage bypasses innate soak, so that hit is probabiy going to mess you up a fair bit. (Sure, magick can fix all things, but I like a reasonable baseline to work from before magick gets involved.) The way to avoid that injury is to take an action to dodge, parry or block.

    The way I see it, having a player declare, “I want to strike at my enemy while trying to avoid injury myself,” is a pretty reasonable thing. If a player wants to declare an all-out attack with no thought of self-preservation, or if they want to avoid injury at all cost, these are reasonable to declare too, but I would like these to be choices. Having a sensible middle-of-the-road option seems like a really good thing to have, but not well-supported when default defences are virtually non-existent.

    So that suggests a reasonable way of taking multiple actions at once, at least for suitable combinations of actions - like attack and defend at the same time.

    But both of the rules that have appeared historically are really harsh for some characters - 1st is really harsh with a low pool, Rev is harsh with a high pool.

    So then it occurs to me, why not just allow both rules? Just let my players choose whichever multiple actions rule benefits them at the time.

    If one character is a highly trained martial artist with attack and defence pools both at 10 dice, let them roll an 8 dice attack and 7 dice defence, or vice versa. This is an awesome warrior, let them be awesome. And if somebody else has 3 dice in each, let them split 2 attack and 1 defence, or vice versa; it’s not very likely to be effective, but at least the player has a reasonable option to represent what they want to attempt.

    Does anybody foresee a problem with that?

              A follow-up thought…

              Something I do not want is players using these multi-action rules to attack a single enemy multiple times in the same round. (Apart from the use of a Time effect, of course, because that’s what Time is for.) I do not want players doing calculations during play to find the most effective mechanic for attacks. I want them to be able to declare “I attack that enemy”, and have the system supply an easy and effective set of mechanics for that, without that standard mechanic being overshadowed by another mechanic. Modifications to the mechanics should represent some aspect of the fiction.

              If a character wields a weapon that does Strength+1/L damage, with Dexterity+Melee totalling 5, the most effective way to do damage against an unarmoured enemy is to divide the pool 5 ways. Each success would then inflict Strength+1/L with no soak, for (Strength+1)*successes/L damage, much better than the Strength+successes/L damage from a single attack using multiple dice. (All assuming we aren’t bothered by botches.)

              Or with Dexterity+Melee totalling 10 (admitted awesome, as per above), the other splitting rule could give 4 attacks at 6, 5, 4, and 3 dice with minimal botch probabilities, but much higher damage than just a single roll.

              So I need to simply say no to attacking the one target multiple times in one split action, and consider how I would want to handle attacking multiple different targets all at once.

                  I would make it a rule to handle multiple actions with the fewest number of rolls possible. If two actions can reasonably be treated as the same action, they get treated as the same action. Thus it is one attack roll whether the player describes stabbing his opponent once, twice, or five times. If nothing else, this cuts down on math at the table.

                  For whatever is left remember that the multiple attack rules, for the most part, cover simultaneous actions, not multiple consecutive actions sped up to fit in a single turn. There's obviously some play for dramatic purposes, but these rules don't make someone supernaturally fast. They need magic for that.

                              Originally posted by Lord Revan View Post

                              Your calculations consider the target wont try to dodge/parry/etc, which won't be true a lot of the times, since you are changing this rules. So, if your 5 dots player tries 5 attacks and the target dodges, he have a great chance of not hitting at all.
                              it’s a simplification for the purpose of evaluating the rules. I’m aware that it isn’t taking into account all possible factors.

                              However, if we do look at dodging… A simple dodge would easily negate the first attack, but that leaves the target defenceless against the remaining 4. If the attacker was to just use one attack action, each success on the dodge would negate only 1 die of damage, unless all attack successes are negated.

                              Unless the target starts splitting dodge actions. But once you start splitting defence that way, there’s a much higher probability that a success on a dodge will be matched against no successes on the attack, thus wasting dodge successes. We still wind up with the heavily-split attack being much more effective, usually.

                              Or if the target uses desperate defence, which will give much more than 1 die on each dodge roll, but also prevents the target from doing anything else. So then a hugely-split attack pool becomes a great way to lock down the defender.

                              Originally posted by Lord Revan View Post
                              Also, as Ramnesis said, multiple actions aren't limited to attacks. The players can do a lot of other actions *and* attack.
                              Yes, this is what I wish to encourage. At least when the several actions can plausibly be undertaken together. (Like attack and dodge - a very plausible combination. But doing, say, field surgery and dodging at the same time might not be so plausible.)

                              Attacking multiple targets at once is also a question. It definitely makes sense to spray down a group of targets with a machine gun, but how to balance that against concentrating fire on one target?

                              Originally posted by Lord Revan View Post
                              Also, why you don't want player to do more than one attack per turn? If the player has 4 dex and 3 brawl, he is a very good fighter and should attacks faster...
                              Because I want the system to support the fiction and the intentions of the players, without the players having to put a lot of effort into manipulating the mechanics (without a corresponding in-character decision) to be effective. The system should support a declaration of “I attack the target, with the objective to disable it regardless of what harm it might do me in return” (that is, attack and not split dice for defence) and provide a simple sequence of rolls to represent that. Intuitively, we expect that declaration to be represented by rolling our full attack pool.

                              And I don’t want the guy sitting beside that player to greatly overshadow them by saying, “I also want to attack the target, but if I split my attack 5 ways I will get to apply my base damage 2.5 times over, on the average…”

                                  Originally posted by Lord Revan View Post

                                  Also, why you don't want player to do more than one attack per turn? If the player has 4 dex and 3 brawl, he is a very good fighter and should attacks faster...
                                  That question revolves around what are we trying to simulate with the rules and what is the best way of simulating it? Is each punch an attack roll (and each attack roll a punch) or is the attack roll for the overall attempt to hurt the opponent? For my part I prefer the latter. It lets the players be descriptive with their attacks without adding extra paperwork. It means I don't have to argue over whether multiple quick jabs are superior to one strong attack. It also gives me more freedom in interpreting the results. There are only so many ways I can say someone fails to connect with a punch, but if we can choreograph the engagement after seeing the dice we can play around with how the fight looks.

                                  Also, I want to avoid situations where the players are making choices solely because of the math. The math is not bad in and of itself, and certainly some players get a lot of enjoyment out of it, but it is not the game itself. If the players are changing their character choices because of quirks of the system, that's a bug not a feature.
