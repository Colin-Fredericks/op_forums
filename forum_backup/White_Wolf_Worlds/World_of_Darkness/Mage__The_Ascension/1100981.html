                              Originally posted by Muad'Dib View Post
                              It shouldn't be Correspondence I'd say - just Mind if the pilot needs some convincing or courage boost. I think it's also a downgrade as Correspondence 3 because other Mages could Unweave your flight, perhaps. Even if we consider helicopters as a Magickal vehicle, then I'd say that the Correspondence use would be on part of the pilot, not the Technocrat summoning a helicopter. But helicopters are not Magickal anymore (unless they are in a particular case, of course).
                              You need Correspondence (and moreover at a level equal to Mind) because you're using the Effect over a distance. If the pilot was present with the helicopter, you wouldn't need to summon it.

                              If you were using the helicopter as an Instrument for a Correspondence 3 effect, that's another thing entirely...as well as something with precedent in MtAs. As I said in a previous post, the Order of Reason often used their Enlightened Science to expedite otherwise mundane travel. Infiltrators entered buildings using climbing shoes, lockpicks, and grappling hooks. And explorers would use maps and their knowledge of the environment to make over-land journeys much faster, which often involved taking "shortcuts" that covered more distance than should be realistic. It's entirely possible that modern Technocrats only further refined their methods in this regard.

                              No, this isn't exactly teleporting (except when it is). But the Order of Reason book made me consider that Correspondence 3 isn't explicitly about "Teleportation", but rather "Making intervening space and obstacles not a factor in one's movement". (Which, really, would also justify using Correspondence 3 to walk through walls. It's not like there's an appreciable practical difference between walking through a wall and teleporting to the other side). But I digress.

                              Yes, this is all less efficient than just teleporting. But that deliberate limitation is taken in trade-off for something potentially far more important: being Coincidental. There are ways to make teleportation coincidental, yes, but these ways are not always feasible, nor does a character always believe it can be done (or that the character themselves can do it, even if they believe it theoretically possible). Syndicate reps don't usually study the mind-bending physics needed to fold space, nor can they always afford to rely on gear provided by other Conventions. Nor, again, can they afford to employ such Vulgar methods all the time. For everyday purposes, they are more inclined to leverage their connections, interpersonal skills, and knowledge of geography to call in a helicopter, and then make that helicopter transport them to their location faster than it normally could. An Effect that's completely Coincidental, and which wouldn't cause them to fall from the sky if someone used Countermagic on them (like you said, the helicopter itself is established in the Consensus; the Enlightened Management is just there to make it go faster).

                              I find that, when it comes to Paradigm-generated limitations, one must remember that these are features of the Sphere system, not bugs. The Sphere descriptions tell us the upper limit of a mage's capabilities at any given level. It's the Focus - Paradigm, Practice, and Instruments - that tells us how much of that a particular mage can use. Whether "Can Use" is determined by what they believe possible, what they are equipped to pull off, or what they dare do in light of Paradox.

                                          Enh. I honestly, find that response not particularly germane to what I was talking about.

                                          I am not saying that it makes the instrument too inconvenient for the mage so much as it adds overhead to the player. I am saying that it introduces an unneeded layer of prescripting, one not present in the SyndRev, and one that SyndRev did not (to my mind, at least) suffer for lacking.It requires users of HR&M to instantiate into the fictional world in much more definite terms the elements of their paradigm, not as a check on "power" (because there is almost no consideration for "balance" between instruments, as your own example displays) but as a method of preserving M20's axis of coincidence.

                                          In SyndRev, it is possible for a Matter-using syndic's player to, as part of the terms of their effect, state that a given automobile has an untapped hidden feature that improves its gas mileage. This does not require that the player first establish with the ST that the car is of such-and-such a make, and to have a Background to represent their influence in the manufacturer's factories. Rather, the conspiratorial nature of the world exists in an unrealized state, one that resolves into definite effects at player input. M20's concern about PBD seems to mean that it doesn't have such an unrealized state: we have to know if the feature "really exists" - which meansthe conspiracy has to really exist - because PBD won't permit us to elide these steps, any more than it will allow us to "conjure" a cab.

                                          We know that the WoD is haunted by conspiracies as much as it is haunted by the High Ritualist's choirs of angels - but M20 only requires one of these players to buy Backgrounds to make their host of specters "real."

                                              I agree with Bluecho, this is a feature not a bug. And it doesn't matter if adds overhead to the player, as, in essence, that's exactly what Practice and Instruments DO. They make the game less one of players (particularly bad players), just handwave away the magic and just say "I use Correspondence 3 to get there."

                                              Should Syndicate members be teleporting? Does that make sense for them? Can they touch a pile of leaves and turn it into money? Which leads to my digression, which isn't about the different ways that Correspondence can be used to walk through walls, exccept that it is, because there is still something over and above Focus that used to be called "paradigm" but has been lost in M20 - that iconic thing that held together one Sect and differentiated it from another. Meaning some mages can use Correspodence 3teleport from one side of the wall to the other, some mages can walk through the wall and some mages do other things entirely - like arrange to have someone get through the wall for them or have whatever they want to have happen on the other side of the wall get done by proxy. Some groups may be able to use more than one way. Some groups cannot use some of the ways Some ways to handle the situation require adding in other Spheres, but adding in those other Spheres does increase the functionality of what they are able to accomplish.

                                              Sure, Intstruments can also be Backgrounds. A Syndicate mage might have Money as an Instrument and have multiple dots in Resources But that adds to what can be accomplished and leads to several different ways to accomplish it. When the Resources are used without magic, there's no chance of a Paradox blowout but there are definitely some issues that can be cleared away by using the Spheres with the Instrument of Money (or finances or whatever). E.g. when the mage uses Resources alone, it's the same as hiring a helicopter pilot like a normal rich person would. Unless they are really rich and have thought ahead to have one standing by, it's going to take finagling, which takes time. When a Syndicate mage uses Spheres and the Instrument-Money, perhaps he is connecting to the underlying hegemony that Syndicate holds over all capitalistic systems. Maybe the Syndicate has a way to co-opt helicopters (or taxis) that are getting ready to go or are ready, but sitting idle. So there's someone who thought they were going to use that helicopter but they just got booted. (Which seems like it makes sense... The helicopter pilot or company gets a deal that can't refuse and boom, here comes your helicopter, Mr. Anderson....

                                              In some ways I don't see a problem with the character getting really creative and saying things like "The Syndicate has arranged for all cars to have X installed as a secret feature and I am activating it". So long as X is a coincidental effect that makes sense within the parameters of what cars can do on pretty much every level. In fact, I think that's GREAT, as it can be used again and again in so many ways. It even can illustrate how the Conventions are working together routinely (which is something that gets short shrift... pretty much the game seems to indicate they all hate and despise and undercut each other). To get such a feature installed on all cars, it would take integrating it into the production process in a way that wouldn't tweak the Masses, which implicates Iteration-X (for the technical specs), N.W.O. (because legally requiring something more mundance, like emission control, allows for a system can piggy back onto makes a ton of sense and expedites things) and the Syndicate (for their control of the production process as a whole). Meaning, members of any of those three Conventions could know about and be able to access that feature (depesning on possessing the relevant Spheres and Instruments). The coolness factor of the Technocracy as a whole just went up a notch due to player creativity.

                                                          Foci does not necessarily mean 'a gadget I built myself.' a Foci is a fairly flexible concept. The NWO are a fairly good example of this. Psychology is their foci for Mind. They talk to you, and things happen. With a convincing argument, a NWO agent, or similarly trained technocrat, can whip a crowd into a frenzy or disburse it. There's no mental thought control laser they have to use. They just use their words. Knowledge can be a focus. Actions can be a focus. A martial arts training camp, is theoretically a focus. Just like the Traditions have their ephemeral foci, so too does the technocracy. In the case of the 'secret car button' the focus is the car. IF there was no car, they couldn't push a secret button on it. If they couldn't push the secret button then no magic would happen.

                                                          And as far as vulgarity goes, several conventions very much so DO use vulgar magic. The difference is, they don't often need to. Why go for the vulgar and flashy plasma cannon if the Thunderhead BFG is just as deadly, and entirely coincidental to the point where it's actually treated as a normal gun unless someone who's not a mage picks it up?
