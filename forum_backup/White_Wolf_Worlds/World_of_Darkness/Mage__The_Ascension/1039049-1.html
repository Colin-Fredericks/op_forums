                                            Ok, so from what I can gather here there are about 4 ways to create a "delayed effect".

                                            1. Good ol' Time 4 with a dash of Entropy 1 for the famous Programmed Event rote
                                            2. Prime 2/3/4 for Charms/Artefacts/talismans that are capable of doing the effect all on their own (which Prime level it is depends on which rules you read).
                                            3. The Hermetic "Everything but the last step" stuff, where all the prep work is done ahead of time to build the effect, only to be set of later.
                                            4. Entropy 1 with various Life, Matter, and Forces patterns under the Technocratic Paradigm of Rates of Decay.

                                            This is how I see these four methods differ descriptively as well as mechanically

                                            1.
                                            D: The Mage has cast an effect and froze it in time, only to go off if certain events come to pass. Whether it be by asking Angels to keep the event on hold, causing a flux around the time stream, or simply zapping an effect forward in time, the effect has been cast and will happen later.

                                            M: Mage needs Time 4 as a minimum, Any other sphere can be used to determine when the effect will break (if not at a specific time) such as "Break when the wind changes" with Forces 1, or "Break when a spirit walks by" with Spirit 1. But Entropy 1 is usually the go to sphere for this, since it can be a lot more versatile and can encompass things like "break on utterance of the password, break upon my death, break when Jenkins falls asleep" and so forth.

                                            Arete is rolled at the point of casting, with all parameters determined before (or during) the ritual, subject to successes. This is probably the most versatile of these methods described, as well as the simplest to operate, not requiring the mage witness the results for them to happen. But it comes at the steep price of requiring Time 4.

                                            2.
                                            D: The mage crafts a tool capable of either doing a specific kind of spell, be it only for a limited number of charges, or permanently. While the full scope of Wonders are too vast to state here, the Mage is essentially crafting a piece of their paradigm into a static object or dynamic life pattern (assuming other spheres aren't being used). The Wonder crafted is often an extension of the mage's beliefs, and thus may work independently of the mage themselves. The magic is now a (perhaps) permanent feature of the object imbued.

                                            M: Prime of various kinds is required. Which Prime is required for what types of Wonder differs depending on whether you go with Revised of M20 rules. The technique of crafting them also differs, but suffice to say this cannot be done "on a whim". You will need a substantial amount of quintessence (possibly in the form of Tass) as well as a innate knowledge of what you are trying to craft (want an enchanted sword? Better call a Blacksmith). Once crafted, the Wonder will function reliably for as long as it is required to, and possibly indefinitely. Providing the material components are met, and the great number of successes is achieved, sometimes higher than 20 or 30, then the wonder is good to go.

                                            Arete is rolled at the point of crafting, and can be one of the most convoluted Ritual Magic Rolls an earthbound player will ever do in their life. The upside is you've made a magic item that doesn't your supervision to function. The type of Wonder made will determine the limitations, but for the most part the magic is done once the wonder is made. This has the added benefit over the Programmed Event rote of your mage not needing to keep the effect in place, which may otherwise hamper their ability to do magic along the way. The downside is that anyone with the correct paradigm will be able to use these Wonders for whatever they want, and having Wonders stolen is a staple of so many campaigns.

                                            Also, Resonance. But I'm not going to get into that here.

                                            3.
                                            D: In theory, this can be done with any sphere at any level, all it requires is a good high ritual and knowing what you'll be doing for it. The Mage may set up all their equipment and start drawing Seals of Solomon but leave out one last line. They may start praying to the spirits to imbue a certain word with powers only released once the word is uttered with intent, they may begin an elaborate song and leave all but the last note. They may be charging up their Tesla coils within their Lightning Blasters, and waiting for the right time to strike. The point is the ritual is started and built, but not concluded, so the effect is ready, but waiting to be triggered.

                                            M: Unlike the techniques above, the spheres aren't so much the concern as the timing of it all, and this technique may not be compatible with all paradigms. An Ecstatic who believes you have to be "in the moment" for magic to work, for example, might not think it's possible to enter and exit "the moment" so easily. Either you do it while you're high, or you're not really doing it at all.

                                            The rules on how this works are not fully stated, so everything here is up to interpretation, but this is how I imagine it working:
                                            - At the point of Ritual, the Mage will make a number of Mundane style rolls.
                                            - The difficulty will be determined by how complicated the magic effect they want to create is. Arete is rolled once the "final step" is put in place.
                                            - Successes during the Mundane rolls will determine how many Arete rolls you are allowed at the "final step" part, and will be proportional with the highest sphere rating within the effect.

                                            So, if you were doing a Forces 3 effect to cause light to turn into fire. Every 3 successes in the mundane Ritual roll would equal 1 Arete roll at the time of the Final Step.

                                            I imagine there will be a few caveats.

                                            - The Ritual will take a lot of time to perform, and the mage will need their full concentration on the ritual for the entire duration. If they are distracted at any point, then the entire thing fails. Also, they would only be able to create one hanging effect per ritual. i.e. if you wanted to Charge up your blaster's tesla coil, you would have to wait until it's "fully charged" before you can then go and "charge" your Gravity Gun.
                                            - Regardless of how you perform your ritual, it will be exhausting. The longer you try to go at it, the more Stamina rolls you will need to succeed to keep going.
                                            - The effect is fixed when the ritual is complete, this includes which spheres are what levels are involved, as well as what is being manipulated. Things like the location of your teleportation, the form of the animal you will turn into, Whether you're chucking Thunderbolts or Lightning, all of that must be decided now and cannot be changed.
                                            - Other things, such as how many targets, duration of the effect once cast, and damage produced, can be decided after the Arete roll as normal.
                                            - You have to roll every Arete roll you accumulate at the point of the Final Step with the difficulty determined as though you were doing an extended ritual (because, you did). Rules for Difficulty and Paradox are the same there.

                                            This means if you somehow Ritual yourself 10 Arete rolls, you have to roll ALL TEN ARETE ROLLS, and suffer the consequences as they happen.

                                            - These effects cannot hang indefinitely without the aid of Time 4 (by which point you might as well just make it a Programmed Effect). For every day the after the initial ritual the final step is not implemented, 1 arete roll is lost. The potency of the ritual decays over time. (perhaps you could strengthen this with Entropy 5 and Prime something, or maybe Time 3 and Prime something).

                                            The bonuses of this is that a lot of the work of a ritual has already been done ahead of time. So all you need do is roll your Arete at the point of the Final Step and Wizz Bang, Magick! Another benefit is, depending on how the mage's paradigm works, you don't necessarily have to be the on to do the Final Step. After charging your gun, someone else can pull the trigger, after enchanting a specific word, someone else can call it (with intent). It is still the Mage's Arete that gets rolled, and the Mage in question has to be in the room and aware of what's just happened. Also they need to believe that what's just happened has happened. (so if you don't say the magic word with enough Gusto in your voice, the casting Mage may not think it would be enough.) What you gain in time, however, you lose in adaptability. The effect is set at the point of ritual and cannot be changed without dismissing the effect entirely.

                                            4.
                                            D: In many ways, this is not unlike the 3rd technique, but with a little added twist, the technocrat does not need to observe the effect occur in order for it to occur. This technique only works because the Technocrat is able to rely on the consensus to observe it for them. In areas where the Technocracy's Science does not work, this too will be ineffectual. But it will work incredibly accurately in "lab conditions". The Technocrat need only set up their apparatus to, say, observe the rate of decay of a radioactive substance, and have the procedure set itself off once the radiation has dipped bellow a specific level.

                                            M: Requires Entropy 1 to sense and measure Rate of Decay, and one of the other Pattern Spheres to know what is being sensed, be it Enzymes (Life) Radiation (Forces) or Chemical Clock Reactions (Matter). Mechanically speaking, this allows the Technocrat to not require Time 4 to do their delayed procedures. Such is one of the benefits of working with the Technocratic Consensus. In theory, other mages may be able to create similar effects if they can find a piece of reality that is open to their paradigm. But such places are usually pretty difficult to come by. Even the Etherites may not be able to rely on these techniques since their brand of Science! tends to fall apart the moment they leave the room.


                                            So here's how a Potion would be brewed with the above techniques:

                                            1. Your Potions are simply bottled effects. The very effect itself is stored in time, held within the liquid, only to be unleashed at the point of drinking.
                                            2. Your Potions contain magical liquids imbued with powerful magics. They will function for as long as there is still juice in the bottle. (I imagine these will be Charm type temporary Wonders)
                                            3. Your Potions are how you do your magic. You brew them and once you drink them, or throw them to the ground, they come into effect.
                                            4. Potions? What are you talking about? This is a carefully constructed Solution which will have corrosive properties the moment the reaction is left to settle. I added a substrate which will turn the liquid blue to let us know once the solution is ready to administer.

                                                    The distinction is simple.

                                                    A Wonder is capable of casting the spell at the command of its wielder. If you were to simply enchanted a Sword with Forces 3 Matter 3 alone then yes, the metal would heat and its thrust will burn. But this would be an ongoing effect held up by the Mage who cast it. They would need to put successes into duration to keep it lasting, and then would need to maintain the effect indefinitely until it runs out or they ultimately dismiss it. Each time the Mage created their effect, they would have to roll arete.

                                                    To compose a Wonder out of the sword, however, you would be able to have the sword heat up on command, perhaps if one was to hold it up to the sky and recite a Latin phrase, it would then burst into flames, shutting off again when the user was finished. This would be possible to do multiple times without need of further Arete rolls. Furthermore, anyone who believed in the power of God and the use of Latin to invoke their favour, as well as the power the Wonder held, would be able to have the sword reveal its flame, even if they were a Sleeper.
