                            To put it another way, the way sensory spells impart information is highly related to paradigm. If an Akashic, a Chorister, and a Technocrat are diagnosing a TB victim, they will all make the correct diagnosis, but they will do so in different ways. The Akashic will recognize the specific energy imbalances related to the disease, the Chorister might be told by a messenger, and the Technocrat will find the presence of the appropriate bacteria. All three will see the fundamental information (that the victim is suffering from a wasting lung disease) but it will come clad in whatever way is appropriate to the spell (chi imbalance, messenger, or bacteria through a microscope).

                            The microscope is so embedded in the modern world that most mages of this time can use one and see bacteria, and those versed in the sciences or medicine (even those without scientific paradigms) will be used to seeing results that are consistent with the results of their own magics. For a mage like that (say an Akashic with an MD) it is arguable that their familiarity with the modern paradigm is strong enough that they could use it as their actual paradigm if they went back in time. In that case they would see something consistent to what they were already used to, because the effect would impart information in a way consistent to its paradigm.

                            If, instead, the time travelling mage was not well versed enough in the sciences or medicine they would be unable to use the microscope properly. They might find it hard to even focus the microscope, or to differentiate the blobs they were seeing through it. But they'd only get failure or something vaguely similar to what they would see in modern times, because people of the 1100s don't have any kind of opinion on what a microscope should show.

                            The only mages who would see something drastically different through a microscope are those who already do. A very small subset of mages (mostly Etherites I think) might use modern equipment via very different scientific theories. That's an edge case though.

                                Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post

                                The question is whether Miasma ever was a real thing. Back when most people thought "foul vapours" spread disease... did they spread disease?
                                Sorry for being obvious or/and pedant, but:

                                Is it me or is this post really a discussion about B. Latour's book on Pasteur ?

                                If a nutshell, if you say, twig , that miasma theory is absolutely wrong, and germ theory correct, you can presumably and logically only say that because you have a correspondence theory of truth - that is, you believe there is an objective reality out there. So your question isn't really a question, because you've already presumed the answer - by refusing to grant the possibility of a truthness (at least relative) to miasma theory. Now this is certainly something Latour would go great length arguing against you, because people who believed in miasma theory certainly thought they could "see" miasmas (and this belief, of course, had very real effects).

                                So, to take your example, I assume they would probably see miasmas (but the time-traveller would probably see whatever he believes in). Today as yesterday, people usually see just what they want to see (I'm not sure you needn't enter heavy metaphysical-historical-sociological debates here...).

                                Sidenote: the microscope probably isn't the best case for your point. In the Middle Ages, people would probably see "animalcules" (that is, tiny animals), just as they did in "real history" (Leeuwenhoek's term, for instance, for spermatozoids). Microscope was invented several centuries before Pasteur conceived the germ theory... (or, what is Microsoft?)
