    Originally posted by Czernobog View Post

    Isn't this the direction M20 is going in already?
    I think that's true, and in terms of the humanising of the Technocracy, you could see it from one of the very earliest supplements for 1st edition - Book of Shadows anyway. Mage 1st edition really had the Technocracy as a boogeyman to regulate and threaten the characters - taking most of their cues from The Terminator and cold-blooded Men in Black. It was only in Book Of Shadows that they started to characterise them in a more human way - and it was also where they generally got into the depth of motivations behind certain other groups too.

    The problem now I think comes when there is too many factions and groups detailed. Currently, M20 has about 25 groups - 9 Traditions, 5 Conventions, and 10 or 11 Disparate groups, depending on whether you count Orphans.

    Now, some people love the diversity, so I'm not talking with a Word of God here, but I think it's quite a lot to get a handle on. I don't think there should be any distinction between Traditions and Disparate groups, and I could actually see alliances between Technocracy conventions and other groups too. By extension, I also think that some 'groups' are really just game contrivances rather than entitites that would actually be able to organise themselves in any global way.

    My thoughts for M5, amongst other things, is that there should be less group affiliations over all. I actually think you could remove the concept of the 'Traditions' altogether, essentially saying that all player Mages are Disparates, in effect.

                                Originally posted by Czernobog View Post

                                I would put the Traditions in the core and have the other two factions under "Guide to the Technocracy" and "Guide to the Disparates" supplements.
                                It's basically what they did with Mage revised, but it met with some hostility at the time. I'm going to give a speculative crack at how they could condense down the groups a bit - obviously YVMD, but it's just a few thoughts:

                                I could see the Progenitors and Void Engineers moving away as Technocracy groups, and forming a new big collective Science! group along with the Sons of Ether and possibly even the Virtual Adepts. They'd need a new collective name, but maybe something like 'The New Order of Reason' would work? - and would represent all the more 'experimental' scientific practitioners out there. Techno-magic, could be used by all groups of course, so there should be scope for plenty of character choice still.

                                The New World Order could be condensed into The Syndicate, insofar that the Syndicate could be the upper echelons of the NWO, employing Men in Black, but more fundamentally driven by capitalism and economic control. Iteration X could be explanded as a group to incorporate the more hardline scientists that defect from the the old Progenitors and Void Engineers. So, fundamentally, The Technocatic Union (primarily as antagonists, but still playable in the same way the Sabbat are in Vampire), would be comprised of two groups: The Syndicate and Iteration X.

                                I'd keep all the various religious-orientated Traditions in tact: Akashic Brotherhood (or Record?), Celestial Chorus, Euthanatos (change the name to Chakravanti), reitroduce the Ahl-i-Batin to the 'Traditions' and also have The Order of Hermes as a 'broad church' group seeking to reassert magical and mystical paradigms of all sorts. The Solificati/Children of Knowledge would probably be reintegrated into a Hermetic House again, along with a House Ngoma (along with ten other Houses!). The Verbena might incorporate Sisters of Hippolyta (which actually, I think is a cooler name), and the Knights Templar could be a wing of the Celestial Chorus. I'd disband the notion of collective 'Dreamspeaker' and 'Cult of Ecstacy' groups, and instead just refer to a plethora of independent 'Disparate' shaman/ecstatic groups. Simlarly, the Hollow Ones would be part of a collective group just refered to as Orphans. 'Paradigms' would be chosen independently from group affiliation anyway, and so players could still play these kinds of characters, just without being shot-horned into a contrived group.

                                So…a M5 'Tradition' list could be:
                                • The Ahl-i-Batin - fully reintegrated into a formal Tradition (again).
                                • The Akashic Record - Largely as before, with a more inclusive name. More emphasis on the actual records, and connections to internet..
                                • The Celestial Chorus - Incorporating Knights Templar and generally Christian or monotheic groups
                                • The Chakravarti - As with the old Euthanatos, but more influenced by Sanskrit based traditions. Reincarnation and life/death cycle beliefs.
                                • The Disparates - incorporating any shamanic or ecstatic (or any other) Mage from any culture. Represents lots and lots of dicverse and disparate groups.
                                • The New Order of Reason - Incorporating Sons of Ether, Virtual Adepts, Progenitors, Void Engineers or any other 'free thinking' science-based 'magic'.
                                • The Order of Hermes - 12 Houses, incorporating House Ngoma and House Solificati and obviously having a diversity of other types of 'Western Occult' mages.
                                • The Orphans - being any Mage rejecting societal grouping; independents. Again, represents all sorts of individual mages.
                                • The Verbena - incorporating Sisters of Hippolyta, and possibly also a few Ecstatic mages.
                                Standing slightly apart, mainly as antagonists:
                                • The Syndicate - Incorporating the New World Order as an agenda, and largely interested in dominating economics, politics and social sciences.
                                • Iteration X - Incorporating aspects of the Progenitors and Void Engineers, and largely interested in creating a single mechanistic paradigm.
                                • The Nephandi - Possibly alien entities looking to corrupt and destroy human beliefs aspiration - an Anti-Ascension group - who may or may not have infiltrated the other two groups and possibly a number of other groups too. And this would be the fundamental conflict in the game.
                                • The Marauders - Less of an organised group and more of a phenomenon, that can potentially afflict any Mage.
                                Which adds up to a 'mystical' 13 groups, which could all be called "Traditions" if we'd like, and would be the equivalent to Vampires 13 Clans and Werewolves 13 Tribes. Oh, and I would refrain from trying to officially match up each group to a particular Sphere (let players come up with their own interpretations, and choose their own specialities). Characters would be able to choose their Paradigm as a separate thing, so you could have a Hermetic Mage that studies Voudun, a New Order of Reason Mage researching spiritualism (or 'parapsychology'), a Gnostic based Celestial Chorus practicising ecstatic magic, a Kung-Fu fighting Orphan, or any other sort of combination of beliefs.

                                How'd ya do all this? Well, have a metaplot suggest that the Ascension War intensified in the 21st century leading to a break up of lots of the existing groups and a fundamental re-organising of others into a new political situation.
