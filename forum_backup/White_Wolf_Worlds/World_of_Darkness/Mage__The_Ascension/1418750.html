    Cosmological Contractors, long-gone Mages whose Magick is in effect at a location.

    10-17-2020, 10:01 PM
    Edit: COSMIC Contractors. Question at bottom.

    There are two things I want in a location (Node, Construct, Chanty) important to my Mage setting.
    1. Adversaries whose Sphere abilities are reasonably balanced with those of the players' staring characters.
    2. Powerful lasting effects that define the localized reality of the place, beyond the ability of the Mages who currently occupy the location.

    Curious players investigating the adventure zone are going to ask questions about how the place came to be as it is. Who made that gate? Why is there a unique feeling experienced while I'm here? Why do I feel heavier? Who made this place this way?

    The Convention books present clearly defined roles for Mages whose services would be in demand all over the world.

    Syndicate -Disbursements: Making acquired Nodes available to other Technocrats and adjusting resonance with Primal Utility.
    Iteration X - Macrotechnicians: Building or improving the physical architecture of the facility. Installing security.
    Void Engineers - Pan dimensional Corps: Linking multiple facilities or via correspondence and Dimensional Science. Creating secret passages inside a single structure.

    The Traditions - not so much. Although, we can imagine Hermetic architects, Etherite engineers and Dreamspeaker portal-crafters.

    How do you imagine a global or cosmic contracting agency for either faction? Where do they return to between projects?

          I am trying to answer that question myself. The question has a lot to do with Sphere levels and the comparative rarity of master mages to newbies. Imagine an on-the-ground engineering firm. Add NASA and International Space Agency. Then add Magick and portals.

          Originally posted by Aleph View Post
          At face value, I would think that's what most of the Syndicate (and perhaps some of the "bureaucracy" aspects of the NWO) are all about.
          The Syndicate or ItX's Time Motion Managers might show up with the cranes and other non-Magickal things to do the coincidental part. Mages sent by one or two conventions would not have very much coverage of the nine Spheres, particularly at higher levels.

          Originally posted by Aleph View Post
          And that the Traditions would rather eschew such bureaucratic resources because they're very individualistic, and would rather do stuff by themselves or ask a trusted specialist if they need something particular they can't do (the same way you might ask a carpenter friend to make you a table).
          Except that it's not a table. It is a fortified structure with many specialized services used to make it durable, scrying-opaque, livable and condusive to the activities happening within.

          Originally posted by Aleph View Post
          Usually "powerful lasting effects that define the localized reality of the place, beyond the ability of the Mages who currently occupy the location" among the Traditions are either "natural" (or, rather, supernatural but not man made)
          The Traditions on Earth don't have to organize and specialize - "because Gaia". That is certainly how the books were originally written. With the ecocide that is currently underway, it would seem plausible that fewer of these phenomena would continue turning up. The existing ones would be disappearing at a medium pace. M20 books describe the Digital Web as a trash-fire, with "Heralds of Basilisk" trying to scare everyone into serving an evil AI god. This leaves few naturally occurring Magick-zones that reflect the Traditions' paradigms. I never came across an ability in the Umbrood Charms list that creates high level multi-Sphere Effects.

          Originally posted by Aleph View Post
          or the product of ancient Masters that lived there and then died or left.
          So, the services of Ancient Masters are no longer available to beneficially alter a suburban buildings for the Traditions. I think specialization among the Traditions for survival's sake will be the subject of another post.

          To better explain builder-to-occupant-ratio, there are American military bases all over the world. The contractors who built the facilities don't occupy those bases, because their specialized skills are in demand. The infantry men who occupy and guard such bases are more numerous. If every group of tradespeople who could build homes and offices simply built their own housing complex, plunked down and retired, the rest of the population would be at the mercy of the elements. Most buildings are occupied by people who had nothing to do with their construction. The security booth of a shopping mall is occupied by security guards trained to monitor video feed, but the electrician who set this up is gone.

          Suppose a Node with a rating of 2 has been discovered. It is an ideal location for a Symposium of starting Mages to use as a base of operations. They are inexperienced (assume starting Mage characters). Their lives are of value to the Technocracy, at least as human resources. Let's say I am a powerful Technomancer. I have some Spheres at 4 and 5. With Matter 5, I can make the walls of a facility as hard as pure titanium to protect the people inside from attack. With Mind, I can surround the place with a particular psi-resonance that reinforces the ethics of the place and protects staff from Mind based espionage. With Data, I can spatially link the facility to somewhere else to give the occupants an escape route or a means for backup to arrive quickly. For me to plunk down and occupy this Node would be a waste of the power I have, which is in demand at other locations. I stay for as long as it takes to establish the Effects, then leave to work my Magick at another location. I would likely travel with other Mages who have expertise in other Spheres to cover all angles.

          Either version of your Metaplot-preference makes such Mages rare. They were in the Horizon when the Avatar Storm wiped them out or they vastly prefer to live in the habitable Horizon. There are not many of these powerful Mages living primarily on Earth. I don't use Avatar Storm, so an exchange of services continues. Lowly starting Mages on Earth need safe places to work from (titanium-like walls, escape routes, protective Mind-fields). Earthly Mages extend the Technocratic paradigm out into the Sleeper community through the Magick they perform over the long term, ensuring that the accomplishments of higherups are not erased.

          Chronicle-wise (this is for anyone), what would happen to the Technocracy if the Mages who go around fortifying new constructs were killed by a Euthanatos-led raid? If you are running a Traditions chronicle for players, how would they plan that raid, given the power difference? Do they rely on the Void Engineers' many vengeful enemies in the Umbra? What if they are not going after such a powerful target, but are using dimensional Spheres (Time, Correspondence and Spirit) to look at the history of an Earthly Construct? This last question is what I'm exploring to make my own stories better. Time 4 allows activities to be warded from Time-sight, but what if the PCs accumulate enough successes to see through the ward?

          Some of these questions are applicable to a chronicle where the people who enhance the construct are the ones who plunk down there. They can't afford the immense power that the cosmic agency sells. "Why should we fork over quint from our Node to Earth-absentees when we can create some of our own effects?", reasons the team leader. There are level three Sphere effects that would be in effect that, once mixed together through conjunctional Magick (assume RAW version), would be harder for hostile scouts to discern. The fusion of Magick of many Spheres makes that effect almost unreadable to a Mage with fewer Spheres than the total Effect being studied. Using Time 2, a scout could look at what Foci were used to build Hypertech into a construct and who was involved. If someone has enough Mage Lore, they could figure out what Spheres were used by individual founding Mages, simply by reference to the Foci used. In the present, it is harder to determine what Magick lingers in the place, assuming it has a Sanctum rating and the effects don't need to be recast.

          Still, it makes a lot more sense that in-demand, powerful and rare Mages would go from place to place giving newbie NPCs a better chance of survival, in return for long term payoffs, like taxing a portion of a Node's quint to feed the Horizon Realm, space station, generation-ship etc. For a newcomer to the Masters' realm, does it take three successes on an athletics roll to walk ten feet? Enigmas to open a door? What are the favorite social activities of people with Mind 5? How much personal space does a Master of Matter need? What kind of traps could a group of Masters set with most or all of the Spheres at 5, assuming captured enemies are worth more than dead ones? How different is their paradigm from the Earthlings they work for? Is their base actually somewhere on Earth? Does it even need an entrance?

              Keep in mind that Masters are the rulers of the Factions. They aren't so much people you can pay to do work as they are your boss and guild master. They are the ones that set the agenda.

              Because Masters tend to be power hungry individuals set to rule the world (these "electricians" are also presidents and CEO's), they likely do have things they want to be done in the world and will provide their "minions" with whatever means they think they will need to do the task.

              If they (as likely happens) don't have Mastery of ALL the needed Spheres, they have the chanells to call uppon other Masters. But this isn't so much like calling an office as it's like the deals happen in expensive resorts between powerful businessmen and politicians.
              They will likely met in Horizon Realms and will trade favors, setting the policy that will define how the WoD will turn on.

              The "pay" for their services migth not be evident in the short run. For the mages on earth, it migth look like "the corpo" providing them with all they need to do their job. For free. After all, it's not like employees need to pay for the building where they work on (or, ather, they do by doing a good job for their bossess. That likely paid very generously). Albeit it migth also end in some sort of "Tass tax" too - especially if the Chantry it's part of the net of nodes of a Horizon Realm.

              Low level mages migth never interact with them in a direct fashion, but rather with their networks. Asking Masters to do something from the perspective of low level mages it's like asking the state to do something from the perspective of a normal person. As a Technocrat, you submit your need to your supervisors, who in turn check with the symposium, which calls for the services of a Master if the request it's seen as sufficently important, and then it's done or courteously rejected.
              There's very little low level mages can offer these guys, that they can't get from being the bosses of whole factions. They will act IF the requet it's seen as important for their agendas, or if the low level mages have a sufficently influential patron or ally.

              Many Technocrats will never even see or know these Masters. Most likely they don't "travel" to the place unless there's an unexpected need - they will rather automate the whole process within machines that will be then employed by lesser mages. Much like how HitMark production (that needs several Spheres at 5) it's done in factories. While the Masters dedicate their valuable time to rule the world and/or discover the mysteries of the universe.

              Traditionalist Masters tend to be (curiously) less obscure and more approachable than technocracy Masters, but also have a higher propencity to let their differences (Paradigmatical or in agenda) ruin any kind of cooperation.
              But ultimately I imagine will work in a similar fashion. A Chantry of low level mages isn't going to ask Porthos to reinforce their walls. But Porthos might as well do it, if Doissetep it's planning to get a new Node. And other Archmasters migth exchange favors with Porthos to get one of his angels to do the job, if they can't do it themselves. Same will happen with less influential Masters.

                              Players can rack up xp quickly over a campaign. Per session, there is a potential to get up to five xp. Automatic, Learning Curve, Roleplaying, Focus, Heroism. For inexperienced players, the second usually counts. Roleplay has to be given to people who are not natural at it, if they make reasonable effort. Focus, sometimes. Danger is the one I award the least frequently, because I put the danger in the sleeper community the players are meant to care about, not always on the front door or behind them. Mages risking their avatar-gifted existence to save sleepers from the other faction's "evil" plans is worth that point.

                              Between three characters, we are looking at some Time 4, Forces 4 and Prime 4. They have more than enough xp to buy these. It is just a question of finishing seekings and securing study time. It is not inconceivable for players to have some Spheres at 5 after a year of play.

                              The trick I use is de-positioning, sort of like forcing a player's king to move before castling in chess. That player may have a lot of material, but their position is one where threats come from many directions. Zero point mentors have agendas that can be in conflict with the seeking the player's avatar has sent them on (sometimes).

                              A single-point paradox flaw, a toxic smell, had one player calling the fire department, drawing attention to themselves and creating a written report with their name on it. It's these sort of things that accumulate along the way that balances out the immense power RAW gives them down the road. They may be able to stop time, conjure storms and pull quint from wherever. It's just that, by this time, their lives are incredibly complicated. As their power and awareness grow, their concerns about the whole world become ever more pressing. Paradox from a level five effect is an adventure unto itself. I'm actually planning some original paradox micro-realms in anticipation of those 1s turning up.
