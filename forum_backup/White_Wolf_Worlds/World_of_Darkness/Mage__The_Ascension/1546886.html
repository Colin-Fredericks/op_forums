    "Outdated" Technocracy Hypertech

    12-11-2024, 10:07 PM
    I'm trying to decide what happens to magickal hypertech when consensus "advances" past the point where what it does is no longer considered magickal.

    As an example, suppose an Electrodyne Engineer had put together an electric toaster a handful of years before consensus had embraced that use of electricity. Does it cease to be a Wonder and become a mundane antique toaster, or does it continue to be a Wonder with an entirely mundane effect and the very rare possibility it might do something bizarre when used?

    In one of the games I'm running, I'm anticipating the possibility that characters may gain access to a long-abandoned Order of Reason laboratory which was not properly decommissioned. (There is a reason why they sealed the place up without clearing it out first.) Aside from what they'd be after there, I'm trying to think of what other things they might find inside the place, and most of it would reasonably be information and projects which are rather dated.

      It's going to depend on how much it fits with modern Consensus. Much like plenty of Etherite Wonders, they might have a scientific flair, but they're still doing Science! not technology.

      And Order of Reason lab especially is likely going to still be Wonders because it'll be full of not just obsolete tech but pseudoscience that shouldn't work tech. The Order of Reason reorganized into the Technocracy in 1851. For example, this was a period where bloodletting was still a mainstream medical practice, and people were making "better" bloodletting machines. But in modern Consensus bloodletting is BS medical nonsense, so a Wonder that cures people by precisely targeting the proper blood to remove is going to be a Wonder to do anything.

          Here's the progression I see:

          1. A Wonder is created that counts as Vulgar. Think of it as an early prototype, highly prone to failure but still demonstrating a principle that the Technocracy's wants to incorporate into the Consensus.

          2. The Device gets refined, and new models shift from embodying a (Vulgar) Procedure to a (Coincidental) Adjustment as there's growing acceptance of the underlying principle by the Consensus. They become more reliable as a result of this shift.

          3. Once the Device is Coincidental, techno-sorcerers ("talented engineers") start using the Path of Enchantment to produce a new generation of the Device that isn't even Coincidental; its operation is fully integrated into the Tapestry, not just the Consensus, even if its manufacturing process isn't.

          4. Finally, the principles behind the Device become so ingrained in the Tapestry that it becomes possible to craft new devices without the need for the Path of Enchantment; you simply need the appropriate Attribute+Ability dice pool. The device is now mundane. This takes repetition over time, and cannot be rushed.

          Note that I describe that as four generations of devices. I would allow an early prototype to be rebuilt as a later generation of the technology; but barring that, the prototype would remain a prototype even as the underlying science becomes more firmly established. To answer Steve's question, I'd have the Order of Reason's lab contain stage 1, 2, and/or 3 technology as outlined above. Even though the scientific principles embodied in the technology have long since become mundane, these would be relics of a bygone age when those principles weren't as well understood as they are now, where the early designs were still flawed to some extent. They can be rebuilt to more modern specs; but until they're rebuilt, they remain what they were.

          Does that make sense?

          My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

Mainly notes and prototypes involving things which hadn't quite been invented or accepted yet, according to the consensus of the time. Nothing terribly useful, but fascinating in a historical sense.

                  One of the characters is an Etherite who'll likely have a field day going over it all, as he's the sort who tends to work within or very close to consensus a lot of the time.                           I would imagine that the Technocracy makes a point of not keeping these sorts of things around, and that even those examples on display inside Constructs are probably mock-ups or just the shell of the original. Still, there's probably the odd Technocrat here and there who has secretly kept their old Vacuum Cleaner of the Future prototype out of nostalgia, or some higher up who stubbornly refuses to trade in their groovy looking desk videophone (and has enough clout to shoo off underlings who insist that it needs to be replaced.)

                          I suppose I could consider these things to still be Wonders of one sort or another, but Consensus doesn't react very strongly at all when they "malfunction" during use, and any resulting Paradox just instantly manifests as harmlessly mundane (though possibly humorous) technical or mechanical hiccups. The reason why the Technocracy doesn't likely want these sorts of things laying around is that a Sleeper engineer might find little things which don't quite make sense, if they were to start examining them very closely.

                                  One thing to consider is that Wonders that were Vulgar once upon a time may remain Vulgar, simply because there's a Consensus about what was technologically possible at a given time. If a Hypothetical Average Observer sees a 1940s smartphone, even if smartphones are currently incorporated into the Tapestry, they're going to think "that's impossible, that couldn't exist then". (Mostly; we're all becoming increasingly aware of how more people in Real Life are starting to believe conspiratorial nonsense, including insisting ancient wonders were evidence of technology from an advanced and/or alien civilization).

                                  A Wonder can remain Vulgar simply because nothing that old should be that advanced. Or at least that's how the lay public feels about it, mostly.
