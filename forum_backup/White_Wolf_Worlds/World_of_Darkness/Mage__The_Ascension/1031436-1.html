                                Well first, we'll need to fill in some details. Because he's under an assumed identity and was part of organized crime, Fatty probably had plastic surgery done to change his face. Any number of plot points could arise from this, from the question of where his plastic surgeon is, to whether the government has his fingerprints or DNA on file. But these are small potatoes, and don't need to come into play if you don't want them.

                                Next, we need to establish what, exactly, Fatty knows. Sure, he might have an idea about his boss's true nature, but I doubt it goes much beyond "an idea". Mages are under no obligation to reveal themselves to the ones working for them, and followers of dark powers are most secretive. Fatty could be aware that his boss has power - supernatural power - but not know the full extent of that power, nor its source. It depends on what his boss chooses to share, and I suspect he'd share little. From Fatty's perspective, this is a double-edged sword. He knows his boss has resources above and beyond what is normally possible (and thus can be tapped for a little help). But he also knows that said boss can turn said resources on him, if Fatty is uncooperative or incompetent. And if Fatty thinks his boss is more powerful than he really is, the CFO may commit to plans of action that assume access to that power, leaving them all in a tight position. The classic risk of underlings overestimating one's abilities.

                                If Fatty is smart, he'll realize that his ignorance of his boss's true strength could bite him if the CFO isn't careful. He'd also realize that asking said boss for help over every little problem can make him seem weak or incompetent. As such, if he's clever (and a mob veteran who lasted for any length of time should be at least a little clever), he'll try to use as much of his own resources to solve his problems, relying on his boss as little as possible. Those resources, of course, are money, underlings, and connections. How he uses those depends on his personality. An aggressive person might try to attack his problems directly. A more subtle person might use indirect means. It's the difference between sending thugs to attack the coven, and sending spies to dig up intel and dirt on them. One that tries to remain above board on problems might attempt to use the legal system and press to work for him, playing the victim in any conflicts (while trying to make sure none of the company's incriminating activities get out).

                                How much he knows about his boss will also inform how much he can guess as to the capabilities of the PCs. What he thinks they can do informs his defenses and strategies. If he knows that Mages can go invisible, he might equip his people with infrared goggles. If he knows they can influence men's minds, he might work to grab strong-willed people, or else have everyone work in pairs (lowering the chances the PCs have to isolate and manipulate). Try to come up with a list of specific powers Fatty knows or suspects Mages to be capable of, and update it as the PCs tackle his operations.

                                            Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
                                            Another, more subtle use, would be to make sure that all the clones are partially related to one individual. This, therefore, would mean they all share a Sympathetic (and therefore Correspondence) connection to a person in the Nephandus' control, if it isn't the Nephandus himself (though I doubt the Fallen would risk that same bond of Contagion being used against them). Suddenly, he has a legion of individuals that are predisposed towards being controlled by him magickally. Agents positioned in many places throughout society, susceptible to mind control or other Effects.
                                            If you make the Nephandi a K'llasshaa, you could make it so the Nephandus sees himself as the head of octopus (or some other lifeform), and the clones, or people with artificially grown organs, as tentacles or pseudopods. Or he could see himself as a tentacle of an Outer Lord, and the people as his sensory organs, suckers etc. This is accomplished with some technepandic (or outright mystic) ritual; for example by putting something like the Yellow Sign on the clones or organs. This is his Correspondence, and wherever the tentacles are, he can scry, co-locate, create portals and use other Spheres. So if a tentacle touches someone he can use Prime (including vampirism); if a tentacle speaks to someone, this channels Mind; contorting into weird and unnatural shapes enables Entropy; self-mutilation, tattoos and scarring channel Correspondence (portals would be major body horror). Level 5 effects could be used if there are several tentacles in an area.

                                            The people used for this may or might not be aware of what is happening. They might have a fugue state (for a few seconds or whole hours) when they are speaking lovecraftian phrases and contorting in weird shapes so that effects can be cast through them; or they might have an urge to touch someone while being aware of it, but not knowing why. This might necessitate Mind used along with Correspondence. Perhaps they could slowly be made into (un)willing Acolytes; and then Mind wouldn't be needed.

                                            Trying to use this against the Nephandus would be risky, as the Nephandus can sense any tentacle which is somehow tampered with, and can channel effects at those attempting to use tentacles against him.
