                    In Re: White Wolf dying/dead/what-have-you...

                    White Wolf isn't dead/a failed business -- it is merely suffering the same problems that *all* of the table RPG industry has been suffering for a decade+ now: declining sales/interest in the hobby as more people gravitate to "instant gratification" video game RPGs, and generally aren't as into the table games as previous generations were/have been. This isn't unique to WW, at all, as even the venerable granddaddy of RPGs, DnD, has been having issues itself for some years. WW is merely adapting, via merger, with the times and trying to survive an environment that isn't RPG-friendly anymore. (The business projection, btw, for table RPGs, as of 2012, was a likelihood of extinction at about 15 years, or by about 2027, as an industry-hobby. In a similar though better boat is the comic book industry: titles that would have been cancelled just a couple decades ago due to low sales numbers are considered top sellers by today's market standards. The only reason they will hold out longer is because of the movie/marketing link-ins that retro-sustain the business as profitable. In that context, WW's merger is not functionally any different than Disney buying Marvel, only taking place at a different level/scale and acting as a *merger* as opposed to an *acquisition*, which is an important distinction... just saying...)

                    Originally posted by theoutlander523 View Post
                    What's any of this have to do with Sorcerer?
                    Absolutely nothing. It's a by-product of the natural discussion process: questioning the quality of "x" book, which leads to overall questioning of broader products/producer, which in turn leads to "Isn't WW dead...?" or "Who Shot WW, and when will Dallas:The Drilling be published?", or similar such. It's a nature progression. #GottaLuvGamerz

                    But anywho...

                    As I posted previously, Sorcerer is actually pretty good as a supplement, as long as you tailor it to your game/ST-created setting ("low-balling" it, as presented, if appropriate to the setting, or "up-grading" it, as indicated in alternate rules suggestions in the book itself, if the ST is running a more high-end game; even then, Sorcerers can be more powerful w/o eclipsing their hard-cover "superiors" -- it all just depends on how the ST wants things to go...).

                    The previously mentioned bit about (paraphrasing) "How can my Sorcerer be more useful/whatever to "x" Demon?" fails to note the *reason* WHY demons want sorcerers/whomever in the first place: the demon, itself, may be vastly more powerful than the sorcerer/human, but it's ability to act/function in the material world is often greatly curtailed/limited (as indicated in D:tF), and having sorcerers/humans/whatever to act through/for them, or provide them with Faith, is critical. Even a sorcerer with just a few dots spread among a couple Paths/Numina can provide the demon with a conduit/means of working itself into the broader world (especially if the other rules for demons, pre-D:tF, are used, wherein a demon can tempt/sway a sorcerer with the promise of greater power if said sorcerer serves it/enters into pacts with it). Devil's Due -- which, granted, is a Dark Ages book -- provides a *wonderful* alternative for how demons might bestow "gifts" to mortals -- sorcerers/mages included -- in exchange for service/pacts. Even if the ST wants to tweak the mechanics involved a bit, it still gives excellent insight into the underlying relationship/reasoning behind the demon/mortal interaction.
