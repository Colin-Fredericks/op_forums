    An exploration of the Traditions and the Enightened in America in the Victorian Era would probably require its own sourcebook along the lines of Castle Falkenstein's Six-Guns And Sorcery, but I'm hoping to explore a few items that would involve them while not getting lost in the weeds. Called "The Gilded Age" in America (from the title of the Mark Twain book of the same name), this era took America from being an agricultural former colony of the British Empire to being something much closer to the industrial and economic powerhouse we see in the 20th century. Even focusing specifically on the latter half of the century, we see enormous technological, social, and cultural changes, ones that simultaneously grow the economy and fund the drive to find and exploit even more resources. A number of issues and events from the time are likely enormously influenced by the Conventions and Traditions, and have equal or greater influence upon them. I'll explore one defining concept here, and probably some more later.

    Manifest Destiny

    The idea that the United States had not just a duty to expand its territory and culture to include all of North America, but was destined to do so, is so inextricably intertwined with Technocratic interests at the time that it's impossible to tell if the Union came up with the concept in the first place, or if they simply took advantage of a popular creed among the unenlightened masses. It is almost certain that if the belief did not originate with them, then they would have come up with something similar. The fortunes of the Technocrats, as well as the expansion of their hold on the consensus, goes hand-in-hand with American expansion and the removal of native tribes from their lands. Mines for coal, silver, and gold are dug on the site of ancient nodes. Oil wells draw Quintessence from sites sacred to the original inhabitants. For an ambitious young Technocrat, the risks of meeting's one demise at the hands of a Dreamspeaker Reality Deviant or a crazed Lycanthrope could be far outweighed by the enormous rewards. It should be remembered, though, that this concept was not universally approved of among the populace, even if it was a prime factor in the growth of the nation.

    The 1800s are arguably the most consequential period for the Conventions since the Renaissance. The Explorators may have outlined the continent itself, but there are still endless miles of untamed (though hardly uninhabited) wilderness yet to be explored by members of the Convention, ones that may contain creatures and beliefs not in line with the Consensus. As they expand the Union's grip, they leave behind flags, forts, and barbed wire to claim and to establish territory gained for the Union. With them or close behind come missionaries and teachers of the Lightkeepers, setting up missions and schools designed to "civilize" the indigenous people into accepting the Technocratic paradigm while back East their newspapers trumpet "Go West, young man!" to implore even further settlement of the conquered regions. When the Indigenous tribes prove resistant to these methods, or are found to be in possession of potentially powerful nodes, the Lightkeepers are willing to employ the most ruthless of methods to remove them from their lands, or even to erase the existence of the population if deemed necessary. Difference Engineers string telegraph and later telephone wires across the continent, and it's only a matter of time before some bright young thing wonders what will happen if you use this technology to connect a Babbage Machine in London to one in San Francisco, and then link them to a third one in Bangladesh. Individual members of the International Brotherhood of Mechanicians, the Electrodyne Engineers, and the Aesculpian Order often provide support to the first waves of Technocratic Explorers with Enlightened weaponry, defenses, and medical technology, then use the now-uninhabited regions to set up laboratories far from the unenlightened masses where they can experiment with less risk of paradoxical backlash. The railroad eventually links one coast to the other due largely to their efforts, but at the sacrifice of countless Asian and Black railroad workers who suffered endless abuse, exploitation, and death, possibly at the design of Syndicate operatives looking to gain maximum labor for minimum investment. The drive West has been entirely funded through their largesse, and they expect a return upon their investment. This pays off beyond all belief in with the gold and silver rushes of the era, though it also leads to economic calamity for many due to inflation and price-gouging.

    Among the Traditions, one thing is constant through the entirety of this century and beyond: The Dreamspeakers are justifiably pissed. Their nodes are being co-opted and destroyed, the spirits no longer speak to them as readily as they once did, their hold on the consensus is disappearing from the few cultures where it still held strong, and their people are being rapidly eliminated through slaughter, disease, and the poisoning of their lands. I can't see the Council as providing any sort of unified front against the expansion, either, as each Tradition is dealing with their own issues right now, or (in one or two cases) may even see this as an opportunity for further power. A number of Akashics probably make it over with the railroad workers, but perhaps can also be found among the devotees of popular exercise regimens and dietary fads such as those of John Harvey Kellogg. Later on, they probably establish a significant presence in San Francisco, along with their rivals in the Wu Lung. The Batini are fighting a hopeless war over their most sacred places, and even sympathetic ones are probably not in a position to offer significant assistance, though there's nothing to prevent them from sending representatives to find common cause with the natives. Euthanatoi ride the plains as dispensers of justice in a lawless land, and make a living at the poker tables of western saloons. A few likely serve as soldiers, Sheriffs, U.S. Marshals, and bounty hunters. Ecstatics run religious "free love" communes in areas far enough from the cities to not attract the attention of the law, work as entertainers and/or prostitutes in taverns from St. Louis to San Francisco, and fulfill the societal role of the town drunk in more than one settler community. The Celestial Chorus is split among its membership: While they deplore the violence against the indigenous people (and the resultant tragedy befalling their colleagues on the Council), could this not also be seen by some as an opportunity to spread the word of the One, and to bring those currently suffering to His Divine Presence? Most of the modern generation is likely horrified by this perspective, but I can see it as having a strong enough hold among the leadership for them to take a hands-off approach while still allowing individual Choristers to follow their conscience. Of course, long before the land was settled by Europeans, there were Native Choristers whose beliefs in a Great Mystery (or "Great Spirit," as it's popularly known) would cause many in the Tradition to see this as a shared struggle. Choristers serve as missionaries and traveling preachers (such as John Chapman, better known as "Johnny Appleseed"), but also as temperance activists, poets, and traveling musicians and singers, Among the Verbena, even those who are not among the Native tribes would probably be instinctively repulsed by the wholesale slaughter of the indigenous people, the decimation of the wildlife and the toxic exploitation of the land's natural resources. Popular professions and concepts would likely include medicine show peddlers, midwives, cooks, local wise women, and guides through the wilderness. Within the Order of Hermes, an entirely different perspective holds sway: As the Order does much of its American recruitment through the mystic lodges and occult orders that attract many who have extensive financial stakes in the expansion, they may see this as an opportunity to be exploited. After all, their reasoning goes, if the Dreamspeakers can't use their talents and primitive hedge magic to defend what is theirs, then what good is it? If they're doing nothing with their nodes to advance their knowledge or their power, then why should they have them in the first place? The only problem is that the power is falling into the hands of the Technocracy, but if the order moves fast enough, they may be able to keep a large number of places and items of power far from their reach. it's a shame that so many Natives have to die, but such is ever the price of knowledge. Naturally, not all Order members share this inhuman view, but not nearly enough of them to make a difference in the Tradition. Order of Hermes members are usually found among the more educated settlers, such as schoolteachers, professors, doctors, ministers, anthropologists, naturalists, and military officers.

    Forgive any unclear passages, disjointed ideas, or awkward verbiage. I only had about half of this in mind when I started writing, and it became more unwieldly as I thought of new ideas while I was in the process of writing down the original thoughts. I also have some ideas on Gilded Age New York and the Civil War, but this is more than enough for a single post.

    EDIT: Forgot to mention: The history of Hawaii during this time is also a result of this belief in Manifest Destiny, as mentioned upthread, but as my only familiarity with the topic is through my having read Sarah Vowell's Unfamiliar Fishes shortly after it was first published over a decade ago, I'll leave that story to those with greater familiarity with it than myself.

            I may be a bit late but...

            Russia is a perfect spot. It had it all. It was a setting where almost every tradition would find its place. A place of many religions, which would form a base of strength of Celestial Chorus and even Dream Speakers (shamanism was, and to this day is, common in Siberian tribes) and a melting pot of nations, which resulted in political turmoil. There was an advancing civilization with modern (well - more or less) cities, booming industry centres like Łódź, feats of engineering like Trans-Siberian Railway but also a very backward province, where the things have been the same for centuries (as were the old spells and miracles). There were the newly conquered territories of Central Asia where the the nomads roamed like their forefathers while the new cities rose. There were the secret cabals and occultism in aristocratic groups and there were yurodivys - sacred madmen typical to Rus (which is a very interesting concept for a chorister or maybe an ecstatic). There was a frontier in Far East with the mix of various races and religions (so even the Akashic could fit in). Reality Zones would change from place to place much more than in the West and the Reason is likely to keep battling against the Faith (or maybe Faiths) and Magic. That battle is going to be harder than in other places in Europe.
