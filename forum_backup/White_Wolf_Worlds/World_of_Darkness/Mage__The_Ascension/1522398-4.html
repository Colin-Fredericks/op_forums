    I find this discussion very interesting.

    Like many, I got into Mage and WoD when I was 12-14 years old, in this period that spans the late 90s and 2000s, and I agreee that there is a clear gen X mark on Mage, and this aspect got stale a long time ago.

How silly this paradigm looks to us nowadays, specially when the crowd who plays tabletop RPGs became increasingly more homogenic, uniform and thin.     It's not by chance that the conversion of the technocracy to a more ambiguous or even positive faction happened together with the transition from gen X to millennials.

    What change will the transition to zoomers cause? Probably nephandi will be seen in a positive light and people will laugh about a game centered around reality consensus, where one of the factions is described as objectively and unambiguously evil not only by others but by itself.

                Pegging the evolution of the setting to social generations continues to be a questionable exercise even from the beginning of this thread.

                A bunch of young teenagers didn't knock on White Wolf's HQ's door (it had one back then!) to demand a more nuanced Technocracy. The people that made the games realized the overly simplistic "good" vs. "evil" setup in many 1e books doesn't suit the WoD the same way that complexity and a lack of simple answers does. VtM making the Sabbat playable being a success was far more influential to the Technocracy getting the treatment than Millennials getting old enough to play the games (since, you know, the first Sabbat PG came out in 1992, when the elder Millennials were still 11 or younger).

                I'd also love to see some support for the idea that TTRPG players have broadly become more homogenic instead of more diverse over time. A coalition of diverse groups - including ones that might normally consider themselves 'enemies' in chiller times - standing up to an even bigger threat is pretty resonant with the current cultural zeitgeist. A bunch of weirdos ganging together to resist the rise of an authoritarian regime is basically the ideal power fantasy for younger players right now; especially not anything like trying to rehabilitate the Nephandi.

                                            The original take on the Technocracy was flawed for three reasons.

                                            First, the oversimplification was bad for the setting, period. WoD in general and Mage in particular, the game hurts with such a childish take on the many themes the Union represents.

                                            Second, and actually more important, both the Technocracy and the Traditions represent a lot of things, a huge lot of dualities and faux-dualities, and some of those were quite problematic to be in the box they were in.

                                            Yes, the Union is Imperialism, Control, Consumerism. They're also Science, Vaccines, the Common Good. The Traditions are Individualism, Self-Identity, but they're both the good and the bad of those things. They're strong proponents of the idea that some people are inherently better than others. Heck, most of the Nazi ideas would fit more in the Traditions than in the Union, specially their propaganda.

                                            Mashing all those things together and labeling them Good or Evil is very problematic in so many levels. And then we get the third problem: both are, at the end of the day, made up by people, and specially by Mages. One thing is to have the Traditions represent diversity and the Union represent uniformity. Another thing is to think this is effectively the only thing they have in their ranks. To think only the Traditions have enough diversity for individuals to improve above its flaws, while the Union is somehow as uniform as their facade shows.

                                            As is said in the GttT and in M20, at the end of the day they're Mages. The very definition of being an Awakened requires the agents of the Technocracy to be more than just cogs, regardless of how much they try to be (when they try to be). The idea that individuals within the Technocratic Union have different opinions about everything that's happening and everything they do, and try to improve, to do good from within a flawed system they think is good despite those flaws, they think can grow beyond those flaws with time, this idea is itself center to Mage, because it defines what being a Mage is about in the first place, regardless if you call yourself a Mage or not.

                                            Now, to say that players nowadays are more uniform is to just ignore how many people currently play RPGs and how diverse a background they have nowadays. Not only are more people playing, they're from more countries, more walks in life, more ethnic groups. And more of them get to participate in discussions through the internet.

                                                It depends on what Technocracy you mean. Because I'm of the group that believes that there's really no way to reconcile the Technocracy of each edition and they're fundamentally reboots.

                                                Because it boils down to the question:

                                                "Are the Technocrats fascists or socialists?"

                                                Because they've been presented as both.

                                                Mage 1st Edition: The Pre-Victorian Technocrats were The Good Guy Order of Reason and became Fascists with the Technocracy. Genocide and Capitalism hoo rah!

                                                Mage 2nd Edition: The Guide to the Union made them well intentioned extremists.

                                                Mage 3rd Edition: The Technocracy has lost its leadership and are now collectivist socialists who for some reason don't respect human rights occasionally.

                                                Mage 20: They're actively good guys vs, a collectivist Umbral Technocracy.

                                                For me, I've come around to the fact that the Technocracy being fascists wasn't misrepresenting the people in power and the more punk I am, the more I think "The Man" should be pure evil and not ambiguous.

                                                Which is ironic because as a teenager, I used to think the Union was much more morally ambiguous but fit the World of Darkness better. Because the secret masters of the government is not a group that cares about humanity in this world or the next. If the Union actually did, it wouldn't be nearly as bad a world as it is in this world. You can't have the people in charge of a punk world be a bunch of caring idealists even if they are ruthless. They have to be scumbags because its not punk otherwise.

                                                It reminds me a bit of a RL conversation:

                                                "I hate conspiracy theorists, it's all over the 90s World of Darkness. It's like 9/11 Truthers were right."

                                                "I mean, we also just lived through the President of the United States secretly arranging a coup attempt and that was after the Iraq War was under false pretenses."

                                                "Yes, but those aren't conspiracy theories. They're fact. It's just journalism to expose them."

                                                "I feel like we're talking past one another."

                                                In the end, pick any one of them because it's your table.

                                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                                    Yes; but no more so than the Traditions, who also have been reinvented in each edition. Which is part of the premise of this thread: times change, and so does the game.

                                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                    Because it boils down to the question:

                                                    "Are the Technocrats fascists or socialists?"

                                                    Because they've been presented as both.
                                                    The two aren't mutually exclusive. And don't forget that the Union is composed of five Conventions which don't always see eye to eye.

                                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                    Mage 1st Edition: The Pre-Victorian Technocrats were The Good Guy Order of Reason and became Fascists with the Technocracy. Genocide and Capitalism hoo rah!
                                                    Mage 1st Edition didn't have much to say about the history of the Technocracy. Remember that the 1st Edition was only around for about a year before the 2nd edition came out; most of the game's development took place under the 2nd edition. And the Victorian Age didn't actually get explored until just a few years ago.

                                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                    Mage 2nd Edition: The Guide to the Union made them well intentioned extremists.
                                                    Ironically, the Guide to the Technocracy was one of the last books to come out under the 2nd edition, and the "well intentioned extremists" bit had its roots all the way back in the 1st edition: the Book of Shadows featured the notion of Technocrats as potential good guys, and it came out under the 1st edition of the game. If anything, the Guide to the Technocracy was a proto-Revised Edition book.

                                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                    Mage 3rd Edition: The Technocracy has lost its leadership and are now collectivist socialists who for some reason don't respect human rights occasionally.
                                                    I'm hoping that you're not going by the Convention Books when you say this; because like Guide to the Technocracy came out just before Revised Edition, most of the Convention Books (all but Iteration X) came out just before M20. Check their copyright dates: they were published in 2012, just before development of M20 started. I wouldn't call them proto-M20 books, because the development team that created the Convention Books wasn't involved in creating M20; but they're also not MRev books in that they didn't come out until nearly eight years after the Revised edition had closed up shop.

                                                    The developers of MRev saw the Technocracy as a cautionary tale for people who think that if you're an intellectual, you're immune to falling for toxic ideologies. Or at least highly resistant. Rather, the MRev Technocracy represents a trap into which intellectuals are particularly prone to falling. I got this directly from one of the writers for the Revised Edition, during the years when Mage: the Ascension was considered to be a dead game, before V20 came along and relaunched the original World of Darkness.

                                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                    Mage 20: They're actively good guys vs, a collectivist Umbral Technocracy.
                                                    That's not M20's Technocracy; that's Void Engineers Convention Book. Again, recall that it's a Union of five Conventions. The Convention Books highlighted those differences, with the Void Engineers being actively good guys (albeit militant ones) fighting to protect their Earthbound brothers from a collectivist Umbral Technocracy; the Syndicate was the capitalists; the NWO was the socialists; and the Progenitors were self-proclaimed healers trying to mend the rifts in the Union. But again, they're not really ​​​M20; although some ideas from there did find their way into M20. We didn't get a fleshed-out take on just what M20's Technocracy is like until Technocracy Reloaded; and even there, what we found out was that the Technocracy is whatever the Storyteller wants it to be. Which is a very M20 thing to say, as the edition's mission statement was essentially "it's your game; so with it as you will."

                                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                    For me, I've come around to the fact that the Technocracy being fascists wasn't misrepresenting the people in power and the more punk I am, the more I think "The Man" should be pure evil and not ambiguous.
                                                    But that's not Mage. With the sole exception of the Nephandi, Mage doesn't ​​do unambiguous.

                                                    Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
                                                    Which is ironic because as a teenager, I used to think the Union was much more morally ambiguous but fit the World of Darkness better. Because the secret masters of the government is not a group that cares about humanity in this world or the next. If the Union actually did, it wouldn't be nearly as bad a world as it is in this world. You can't have the people in charge of a punk world be a bunch of caring idealists even if they are ruthless. They have to be scumbags because its not punk otherwise.
                                                    But should Mage be punk? That's part of what it was in the 90s, true. But it's been over thirty years since then, and times have changed.

                                                    At this stage, what I'd like to see for the Technocracy is an organization that's in the painful throes of reformation. Success isn't guaranteed by any means; but while Conditioning and Room 101 are still a thing, the Technocrats supporting it don't have the monopoly on power that they thought they had; the Friends of Courage can operate more or less openly now (whatever that means in the context of a secret society), and Technocrats who used to simply accept Room 101 as "just how it is" are starting to come around to the idea that the practice of brainwashing Technocrats into conformity is bad and ought to stop, regardless of the cost. Many may even be coming around to the idea that brainwashing anyone is problematic at best.

                                                    That is, there are rotten Secret Masters running the Union; but the solution isn't to "rage against the Man" (the punk outlook); it's to fix the Union. And it's doable, albeit a tall order, because it's turning out that the seeming omnipotence of those Secret Masters was an illusion.

I think Mage actually kind of suffers and benefits from the fact that it is a game where the nature of virtually every faction has been rewritten and done ambiguously enough that the biggest struggle of any ST is picking a lane as to what is true or not.

                                                            But is Mage punk?

                                                            I'd argue Mage is actually the MOST punk of the "Big Three" gamelines and that Revised was correct in retrospect that there needed to be a serious course correction from the guys who were die-hard Traditionalist and die-hard Unionist apologists. Because Vampire: The Masquerade and Werewolf: The Apocalypse kind of got themselves caught in the weeds.                                                             Mage by contrast has always been the only game where they kept the central premise of organizing your protagonists and disparate viewpoint about fixing the world or standing in opposition to the fucked up nature of the world.                                                             I kind of think back to the Mage's inspirations whenever I'm thinking of this. Grant Morrison's INVISIBLES for example. At some point, the ideal Mage character went from being a Hollow One and his cabal of disparate friends versus the Union and became ideologically committed Order of Hermes or Unionists.
