                                        Ok, so here's what I'm getting so far.

                                        The main differences between WTA and MTA regarding umbra is that of perspective.

                                        Mage suggests that Spirit world is flexible, bending around the expectations of the observer. The answer changes based on the question.
                                        Werewolf demands that the spirit world be more rigid, that it changes only by the will of the spirits, not the observer. There is a definite single answer, but you must ask the right question.

                                        For the most part this isn't really a problem since WTA rarely deals with the Lower umbra, Higher umbra or the Dreaming (ignoring Chimera for a moment). Meaning such a rigid view of a spirit world, void of any need of subjectivity isn't really a problem.
                                        But when it comes to the Middle Umbra or the Deep Umbra, this is where things get confusing because both Werewolves and Mages can end up in space.

                                        Saur Ops Specialist I can understand your reasoning that any attempt to amalgamate the two is already taking the assumption that Mage is right, because Werewolf explicitly states that the cosmology acts this way independent of any other factors, there need no observer for Gaia to do what she does and Luna to keep that moon going or the Weaver to have the world encased in a web. To say "Well, that's just was Werewolves think is going on" already assumes a subjective pretext to reality.

                                        So let's look at this the other way. what if both sources are right, but also wrong?
                                        What if Gaia is real, the umbra is part of her domain, and all forms of magic are the shadows of power offered through the trinity of Wyrm, Weaver, and Wyld?
                                        What if the Mage's avatar is but a wanton shard of the Umbra itself, one that has fallen free from the confines of its making, and is attracted to human minds, for whom they follow.

                                        Let's say that Mages are similar to Drones, Fomori, Gorgons, and Kami in that they are humans that have been possessed by a spiritual shard of the umbra. But unlike these four, the spirit is disembodied from Gaia, the Wyrm, Weaver, or Wyld. Perhaps the spirit has even lost all sense of self and purpose, floating raggedly along the umbral storms, flung out into the world of the mundane, forever to flail lost from its intended purpose. This time flailing about changes the spirit into something more subtle, its powers weakening, and its influence breaking, until it returns to its base mode. Gaia spirits to the Questing archetype, Wyld to Dynamic, Weaver to Static, and Wyrm to Primordial. These spirits, if they can even be considered spirits anymore, have been battered and beaten like rocks in a rock tumbler, losing their original form and function, any memories of the war, any affiliation to a side, they are weak and useless lumps of lost potential. And so they clings to humanity in order to survive.

                                        This spirit now stays attached to this human, feeding of its emotions, its views, its hopes and desires, on its thoughts, dreams, and even its beliefs. Throughout the human's childhood the spirit becomes as much a part of them as anything else the mage thinks. This process shapes them, transforms them, morphs them into aspects of their subconcious, becoming so ingrained in the child that they inhabit that they become more mundane than spirit, they become the avatar, and thus construct a completely new identity built of the imagination and experience of the children they inhabit.

                                        This transformation, however, leads to quite miraculous things for the avatar, through repeated exposure to the will of humankind, they are able to embody parts of human will to regain a power long lost to them, a power to manipulate the world around them, to change it, to break it, to fix it. This power however, is only accessible via their human's will, meaning they can only act on this power if their human wills it to be. But as more and more humans gain these avatars, the world starts to fluctuate more and more. Consensus starts to emerge, if only a slight amount. The rules of reality become self perpetuating. Unbeknown to these avatar, they are creating a new umbral realm, one of will. For a long time, these avatars work along their human's will to extract change around them based on the desires and thought consumed since infancy. They have only their base essence to motivate them, as well as the memories of previous humans they may have clung on to. It's not until a dramatic event, an awakening, that the human becomes aware of their avatar.

                                        The awakening gives the avatar much greater access to the human's will, and as such is able to create much more potent effects with the aid of their humans ability to focus their will upon the world. The communication between the avatar and their human is as unique as every human mind, These are the first Mage. These early Mage were much more in tune with their avatar spirits than those that came after them, as this was a time when the consensus was still young and easily mutable, but over time, tribes of humans started to give way to pockets of shared belief, and thus pockets of this umbral realm which obeyed different rules. In order for the avatar to continue acting in accordance with its base essence, it had to find a way to cope with this, and so allowed their mage to incorporate aspects of these beliefs into their practices, keeping their mages will strong and focused so the avatar may continue to function and shape the world as it is want to do.

                                        Over time, the consensus continued to diversify, and this new umbral realm expanded to encompass the entire world. These avatars became so adhered to their mages, that they ceased being spirits in the traditional sense all together. The possessed no Airte Sense, no ability to communicate via a shared language of the spirits, they had become the very embodiment of human belief, and their only driving force was the base essence they carried, a far cry from the beings they had once been so very long ago.

                                        Over the centuries many things came and went. The Wyrm, Weaver, and Wyld did their best to court these bizarre new "spirits" and in so doing created the seeds for the four factions of the Ascension War. In time the Gauntlet was erected, which created a definitive point of separation between the mundane and the umbral. Perhaps this was a way of controlling this consensual umbral realm lest it infected the rest of Gaia's domain, perhaps it was a direct result of consensual reality when enough humans agreed there was only one world. More likely it was a little of both.

                                        Centuries pass, and consensual reality has become the mundane world good and proper, with layer upon layer of belief pressed upon them from the humans and their little "spirit" avatars which feed on such belief to enact change. The avatar is no longer a part of the world of the umbra anymore than a Wraith is a part of the skinlands. They are both but fragments of the entities they were before, and have since fallen away.

                                        When a Mage enters the Spiritual Realm, their avatar acts just as it did before upon its Mage's will, but now it is unhampered by the stifling consensus it had been stuck in prior. Like training in +10G gravity, their avatar found even greater freedom enact their Mage's will, and thus wherever the mage stood created a tiny pocket of consensual reality, adhering to exactly what they expect to see, but as with everything their avatar created, it was no less real.

                                        The more mages that enter the Spiritual Realm, the more they change it a shape it in their own image, creating lasting changes that may lead to irreversible damage.

                                        When a human dies, it leaves behind three things (aside from a body). First is its avatar, which will simply tumble around and either find its way to another human, or break down into the building blocks of spiritual energy. Second is the spirit of the human, the compilation of its hopes, dreams, thoughts, and such, which make their home in a shared world of the Astral Umbra. A place where mental energy just feels drawn to. Lastly, they leave behind their Will. The concentrated essence of their desires, passions, regrets, and fears. If the will is strong and filled with such energies, then from this come the Wraith.

                                        There you go. Mages existing in the WTA universe complete with Consensus defining Avatars. The Paradigms of the various mages are all vastly different descriptions of the functions of the Umbra, the Spirit world, and the trinity of Wyrm, Weaver, and Wyld. As the Avatar shifts its role based on the mage it inhabits, such differences are never disprovable. And indeed, it is the effect of their Avatars to shift will around them, that were they to ever come face to face with a Celestine, there is a chance that their sheer belief would transform them into something more palatable for the mage.

                                        If Gaia were ever to disappear, and the apocalypse were to happen. Then it would happen, but the humans would only see it through their filtered eyes.

                                        So in a weird way. Mages are to Werewolves what Marauders are to Mages. But in a world were consensus is king, does knowing the "real" truth really mean anything? What good is knowing true colour in the valley of the blind?

                                        This is reality now. The Umbral shadow of the spirit world.


                                        So, there you have it. An interpretation of Mage and Werewolf cosmology that contradicts things from both Mage and Werewolf. The idea is that both the Mage interpretation of reality and the Werewolf interpretation are correct, but at the same time have huge gaps and simplified areas of aspects of what is going on so each side can understand it better. No one actually knows the whole truth, but neither side could have the insight required to understand any of this.

                                        I probably just created a bit of lore that both WTA and MTA players can hate. So at least they'll agree on something now.

                                        But yeah. In closing. In the umbra, Everything is Werewolf until a Mage shows up.

                                                What if the Mage's avatar is but a wanton shard of the Umbra itself, one that has fallen free from the confines of its making, and is attracted to human minds, for whom they follow.

                                                Let's say that Mages are similar to Drones, Fomori, Gorgons, and Kami in that they are humans that have been possessed by a spiritual shard of the umbra. But unlike these four, the spirit is disembodied from Gaia, the Wyrm, Weaver, or Wyld. Perhaps the spirit has even lost all sense of self and purpose, floating raggedly along the umbral storms, flung out into the world of the mundane, forever to flail lost from its intended purpose. This time flailing about changes the spirit into something more subtle, its powers weakening, and its influence breaking, until it returns to its base mode. Gaia spirits to the Questing archetype, Wyld to Dynamic, Weaver to Static, and Wyrm to Primordial. These spirits, if they can even be considered spirits anymore, have been battered and beaten like rocks in a rock tumbler, losing their original form and function, any memories of the war, any affiliation to a side, they are weak and useless lumps of lost potential. And so they clings to humanity in order to survive.

                                                This spirit now stays attached to this human, feeding of its emotions, its views, its hopes and desires, on its thoughts, dreams, and even its beliefs. Throughout the human's childhood the spirit becomes as much a part of them as anything else the mage thinks. This process shapes them, transforms them, morphs them into aspects of their subconcious, becoming so ingrained in the child that they inhabit that they become more mundane than spirit, they become the avatar, and thus construct a completely new identity built of the imagination and experience of the children they inhabit.

                                                This transformation, however, leads to quite miraculous things for the avatar, through repeated exposure to the will of humankind, they are able to embody parts of human will to regain a power long lost to them, a power to manipulate the world around them, to change it, to break it, to fix it. This power however, is only accessible via their human's will, meaning they can only act on this power if their human wills it to be. But as more and more humans gain these avatars, the world starts to fluctuate more and more. Consensus starts to emerge, if only a slight amount. The rules of reality become self perpetuating. Unbeknown to these avatar, they are creating a new umbral realm, one of will. For a long time, these avatars work along their human's will to extract change around them based on the desires and thought consumed since infancy. They have only their base essence to motivate them, as well as the memories of previous humans they may have clung on to. It's not until a dramatic event, an awakening, that the human becomes aware of their avatar.

                                                The awakening gives the avatar much greater access to the human's will, and as such is able to create much more potent effects with the aid of their humans ability to focus their will upon the world. The communication between the avatar and their human is as unique as every human mind, These are the first Mage. These early Mage were much more in tune with their avatar spirits than those that came after them, as this was a time when the consensus was still young and easily mutable, but over time, tribes of humans started to give way to pockets of shared belief, and thus pockets of this umbral realm which obeyed different rules. In order for the avatar to continue acting in accordance with its base essence, it had to find a way to cope with this, and so allowed their mage to incorporate aspects of these beliefs into their practices, keeping their mages will strong and focused so the avatar may continue to function and shape the world as it is want to do.

                                                Over time, the consensus continued to diversify, and this new umbral realm expanded to encompass the entire world. These avatars became so adhered to their mages, that they ceased being spirits in the traditional sense all together. The possessed no Airte Sense, no ability to communicate via a shared language of the spirits, they had become the very embodiment of human belief, and their only driving force was the base essence they carried, a far cry from the beings they had once been so very long ago.

                                                Over the centuries many things came and went. The Wyrm, Weaver, and Wyld did their best to court these bizarre new &quot;spirits&quot; and in so doing created the seeds for the four factions of the Ascension War. In time the Gauntlet was erected, which created a definitive point of separation between the mundane and the umbral. Perhaps this was a way of controlling this consensual umbral realm lest it infected the rest of Gaia's domain, perhaps it was a direct result of consensual reality when enough humans agreed there was only one world. More likely it was a little of both.

                                                Centuries pass, and consensual reality has become the mundane world good and proper, with layer upon layer of belief pressed upon them from the humans and their little &quot;spirit&quot; avatars which feed on such belief to enact change. The avatar is no longer a part of the world of the umbra anymore than a Wraith is a part of the skinlands. They are both but fragments of the entities they were before, and have since fallen away.

                                                When a Mage enters the Spiritual Realm, their avatar acts just as it did before upon its Mage's will, but now it is unhampered by the stifling consensus it had been stuck in prior. Like training in +10G gravity, their avatar found even greater freedom enact their Mage's will, and thus wherever the mage stood created a tiny pocket of consensual reality, adhering to exactly what they expect to see, but as with everything their avatar created, it was no less real.

                                                The more mages that enter the Spiritual Realm, the more they change it a shape it in their own image, creating lasting changes that may lead to irreversible damage.

                                                When a human dies, it leaves behind three things (aside from a body). First is its avatar, which will simply tumble around and either find its way to another human, or break down into the building blocks of spiritual energy. Second is the spirit of the human, the compilation of its hopes, dreams, thoughts, and such, which make their home in a shared world of the Astral Umbra. A place where mental energy just feels drawn to. Lastly, they leave behind their Will. The concentrated essence of their desires, passions, regrets, and fears. If the will is strong and filled with such energies, then from this come the Wraith.

                                                There you go. Mages existing in the WTA universe complete with Consensus defining Avatars. The Paradigms of the various mages are all vastly different descriptions of the functions of the Umbra, the Spirit world, and the trinity of Wyrm, Weaver, and Wyld. As the Avatar shifts its role based on the mage it inhabits, such differences are never disprovable. And indeed, it is the effect of their Avatars to shift will around them, that were they to ever come face to face with a Celestine, there is a chance that their sheer belief would transform them into something more palatable for the mage.

                                                If Gaia were ever to disappear, and the apocalypse were to happen. Then it would happen, but the humans would only see it through their filtered eyes.

                                                So in a weird way. Mages are to Werewolves what Marauders are to Mages. But in a world were consensus is king, does knowing the &quot;real&quot; truth really mean anything? What good is knowing true colour in the valley of the blind?

                                                This is reality now. The Umbral shadow of the spirit world.


                                                So, there you have it. An interpretation of Mage and Werewolf cosmology that contradicts things from both Mage and Werewolf. The idea is that both the Mage interpretation of reality and the Werewolf interpretation are correct, but at the same time have huge gaps and simplified areas of aspects of what is going on so each side can understand it better. No one actually knows the whole truth, but neither side could have the insight required to understand any of this.

                                                I probably just created a bit of lore that both WTA and MTA players can hate. So at least they'll agree on something now.

                                                But yeah. In closing. In the umbra, Everything is Werewolf until a Mage shows up.
