        Originally posted by Just John, Forever... View Post
        (Citing this quote, but addressing your other post, as well...)

        Sorcerers' powers work because they are accessing the remaining power/belief ("The Mythic Threads") that still exist in the world; they don't have to have Avatars to do that, as True Magic(k) -- even if a given Mage uses similar tools/methodologies as a given Sorcerer -- operates on a completely different metaphysical principle from Sorcery/Static Numina. It's also the reason why Sorcery doesn't normally create Paradox: whereas True Magic(k) alters Reality(tm) in order to make things happen, Sorcery works with the remaining threads of mysticism that are "accepted" by Reality(tm), and therefore don't disrupt it as greatly. (On the flip, however, as cited in Sorcerer, that is also why if in a situation where a Sorcerer's magic directly and incontrovertibly conflicts with Reality(tm) -- he casts a blatant fireball in a packed mall, surrounded by people, with the local news cameras on him, and it clearly is "I'm casting a Fireball, bitches -- make your Reflex Saving Throws(tm)!" -- then the spell will simply fail, as static magic *cannot*, by inherent definition, defy Reality(tm) on its face; a Mage, on the other hand, *could* accomplish the same feat -- though likely with Quintessence and Willpower to back it -- *BUT* would accrue Paradox for doing so... racking it up quite quickly with repeated castings, and absolutely having said Paradox come crashing down in short-order, Willpower or no Willpower, Teflon Avatar Merit or not...). Sidenote: the difference in metaphysical "oomph" also becomes clearer when the "juice-stats" are compared: Sorcerers generally can't have more than 5pts of Mana at any given time (assuming they have the appropriate Background), while even the lowliest Mage (unless using the DA:M alternative for Avatar/Fount, Quintessence scaling, which I strongly recommend as it better reflects the idea of the "strength of the soul", and scales better relative to certain other splats) can have up to 20pts of Quintessence, due to his Awakened god-self.

        *****

        It's a 7pt merit in WW20 -- it allows (usually Kitsune or Bastet, though it could be anybody, theoretically) for the changer to be able to develop/practice static/hedge magic. (That puts in on a "rarity scale" similar to having True Faith, which is also 7pts.) I remember saying that, exactly, back in the 90's, as an appropriate Merit-cost for, say, the Uktena, or similar changers who might practice hedge magic (including the Ananasi, or of course the Kitsune). I remember getting the brush off by other posters, too. My response: White Wolf will figure it out one of these days...

        ... and it looks like my patience paid off... eventually...

        Careful my friend, whitewolf may go back on it's words sooner than later, you may be eating your words if they decide to change the rules.

                                    That'd be news to me. The magic of Changelings/The Fae is almost (not quite, but almost) diametrically opposite to how most sorcerer paths work/are structured, at least metaphysically speaking. Changelings' magic is much more dynamic, with certain metaphysical structures shifting even from one moment to the next; Sorcerers' magic is much more hardlined and patterned, relatively speaking, even if it does have *some* qualities of flexibility/versatility. The only thing more opposite to Fae magic is Vampiric Thaumaturgy (and their Disciplines, in general). I am not a complete expert on Changeling, but I've at least skimmed through almost all the books, and if I had ever read *anything* that said that, I'd have been all over it back in the day, because it's not metaphysically sound, at all. (Now, some fae-blooded individuals can certainly be Sorcerers -- I'm running a DA game for such a character right now -- and maybe that's what you're thinking of. Mages can be fae-blooded, too, though it's rare to the point of being unheard of in the Modern Era, and even rare during the Sorcerers Crusade and Dark Ages, though the Merit is available for mages back then. >shrugs< )

Sorcerers aren't sleepers: their dedication and/or natural bend towards the mystical eventually manifests via their acquired mystical skills/powers (which can include Psychic Numina in some rare individuals, too). Sleepers *can't* work Path Magic without becoming Sorcerers, and very few humans/mortals have what it takes to become such (that's addressed in the books, as well).
