                      I'd just like to mention that while M20: Book of Secrets says that Primers cannot be copied, Forged By Dragon's Fire has no such restriction. Whether it's Grimoires or Primers, they can be copied, and even translated (though the latter takes a great deal of work, since the translation needs to convey the same ideas). The M20 team probably meant for the newer edition to have caps on it for balance reasons, but in the Revised era no such limitations existed. It wasn't the physical (or oral) vessel of the Grimoire/Primer that was enchanted, but the Text itself. Information can be propagated without limit, because it transcends the merely physical. This is partially why books, scrolls, and oral histories are considered magical. It allows for one's worldview to be transferred to another, even when thousands of miles away, centuries in the future, and long after one's death.

                      Grimoires and Primers aren't rare because mages can't copy them freely, but because they won't. For the Awakened especially, knowledge is power, and they have reasons for wanting to keep that power from being shared.

                      Partly is personal pragmatism (what others might call "selfish"), as magicians of all stripes have much to gain from keeping a lid on their power and trading it tactically. A mage could profit greatly if they keep their copy of a Grimoire close, and only allowing someone to copy it in return for money, resources, or favors. Mage institutions - like Traditions or the factions therein - can use the promise of knowledge as a way of motivating their initiates, so they buy into the system and benefit it from their labors and loyalty. Even groups that would like to have their worldview propagated can still reserve specific parts of their knowledge base, if it means keeping the trade of information going.

                      The other reason for wanting to control the propagation of knowledge is safety. This is the less obviously self-serving part, since it has to do with what mages argue is the Greater Good. Knowledge is power, and not everyone should or can be trusted with that power. Any Hermetic will tell you that their arduous and labyrinthine initiation system exists both to perfect the student, and to weed out any who lack the discipline, intelligence, and commitment to become Magus Rex. To them, giving the keys to the universe to someone who is lazy, stupid, or unstable is akin to giving a loaded gun to a child. While other mage groups don't have the same extreme paranoia as the Order of Hermes, they still see the wisdom in this idea to one degree or another. Hence, keeping sources of great knowledge - of great power - in reserve, to be disseminated when and to whom the owners feel is prudent.

                      Primers are the exception, to an extent, because their function, first and foremost, is to Awaken Sleepers. All mage groups need as much fresh blood as they can get. Hence why Primers are so much more common than Grimoires, despite being harder to make. Nonetheless, the previous paragraph still holds true, and mages have a vested interest (if not a duty) to be careful where they throw their Primers around. An emotionally unstable, stupid, or lazy person who finds a Primer in an attic and goes on to rampage around or summon demons is a real problem. Even the Etherites - who use their Kitab Al-Alacir quite liberally - will still observe a potential recruit, before just handing a copy to them.

                      Unless, of course, the Etherite is a Mad Scientist, who thinks the Society of Ether's ethics board is too much like the Technocracy, and thus needs to be opposed out of spite. There's a reason that faction is considered radical, even among the Etherites.

                          Oh, and I forgot to mention: every copy of a Grimoire or Primer is a potential tie to the one who copied it.

                          Not in the magical sense, where the Rule of Contagion in involved. I mean that when a mage makes a copy of these wonders and gives it to someone else, there's the potential that investigation could trace that book back to its source. If a Sleeper was given a Primer and Awakens, their antics could draw attention to both themselves AND the mage who gave them the Primer. The original mage might be very careful about discovery, but the new mage might not be, or could end up making a mistake the first mage would have seen coming. The Technocracy is constantly vigilant for such activity, and are interested in not just junior mages.

                          Further, there are internal pressures to treat Grimoires and Primers carefully. If a mage gives another mage a Grimoire, and the second mage goes on to abuse the power learned, superiors in the Tradition/Convention/etc may punish the first mage for imprudent dissemination of knowledge.

                          EDIT: This is also why even the Virtual Adepts - many of whom believe Information Wants to be Free - are going to exercise some restraint. If a VA mage just dumps a Primer or Grimoire onto a server in PDF form, and lets anyone access it, "anyone" can also include the Technocracy. The Union has a vested interest in keeping magickal knowledge under wraps - especially the Reality Deviant variety - and so will do everything in their power to track the server back to its owner. This sort of thing causes Hacking Battles and Encryption Wars, which doesn't always end favorably for Virtual Adepts. So the web address on such a server must, by necessity, be ever changing, making it difficult for Technocrats and normal people (the VA's "target audience") alike to find. More often, I'd see VAs doing as Etherites do, and sending limited use download codes to potential initiates the VA have vetted beforehand. Sometimes, they may even insist on only transferring the data through physical means, in old days through floppy drives, and now through flash drives or external hard drives.

                              A Grimoire it's essentially something you do for other people (more so than other Wonders). And it should be regarded like that. It's something a mildly famous mage would do to raise his status on the Tradition and gain some favor, but even if the mage hasn't a strictly utilitarian motive it's something that will influence others.
                              Moreover, all of this assumes the creator of a Grimoire is simply aiming for a tangible benefit. There are any number of more nebulous reasons for writing a Grimoire. First and foremost, we must establish that the creator is writing a book. So we need to ask: why do people write things?

                              It could be because the mage has an idea stuck in their mind, that they need to get out. A current of thought, likely winding around their head for a great deal of time, that needs to be expressed. In much the same way that Victor Frankenstein needed to make his monster, the mage needed to get the fire of the idea out. To exorcise the mental demon and transfer it to the text. Except the gravity of their need could not be satisfied through any pedestrian act of word-smithing. The idea needed expressing in the most perfect manner possible. Hence, why an enormous amount of work, both intellectual and magical, is invested into the making of the text. Nothing less would do, or the mage would simply pen a treatise and move on.

                              The mage could, alternatively, have loved learning. Many mages are scholars, for whom the act of learning and understanding is, itself, both enjoyable and virtuous. Such a mage might write a Grimoire for no other reason than because they want to pass on this love to others, and to enrich the body of occult lore of all mankind. If you asked them why they made a Grimoire, they would not understand the question, because it ISN'T in question. Making the Grimoire is its own good.

                              Similarly, the message inherent in a Grimoire isn't just "how to do X Sphere better". It's usually bundled up with the nature of what the mage's Tradition values. A Celestial Chorus mage may write a Grimoire that conveys the sublime nature of The One. It may simply take the form of a series of psalms, meant to uplift the reader's spirit and bring them closer to god. For them, a higher Arete rating or a dot in Spheres is a happy byproduct that comes from communion with and appreciation of the divine. Bringing readers closer to god is its own reward. Indeed, the writing process may be a method of prayer and communion unto itself, for the writer.

                              Preservation is a worthy goal, specifically of knowledge as it IS during the time of the text's writing. Sure, a mage can teach a dozen students and have them teach their own. But each one of those students will internalize the lessons differently, come to different conclusions, and create divergent streams of thought. While this is sometimes good, as it allows ideas and ethos to change and evolve with the times, it also creates issues where the original meaning is lost. Or creates a situation where some or all mutations are worse, not better or just different. The students or their students could go on to turn the belief system into something ugly, simplistic, and/or harmful. Every mage fears that what they cherish could be corrupted or lost. So creating a Grimoire can be as much an attempt to set the mage's thoughts into stone, as to pass them on. To say that no matter what new thoughts are had about the subject in the future, that a person can point to a magically empowered source and know what the mage said and meant. That Grimoires and Primers both need to be copied verbatim (or close enough to retain the intended meaning), in order to keep their magic, is proof against the ravages of misquotation or misunderstanding. You CAN'T get the benefits of these texts if you don't grasp what the writer meant.

                              There is even satisfaction in making a Grimoire to empower one's own Tradition. Every Grimoire and Primer is a resource that future generations can look to and draw strength from. Especially if old masters are lost to the war or happenstance, and only their libraries remain. Any stack of notes or published treatise can serve this purpose, but the textual Wonders go a step beyond. For some, their Tradition or whatever is important enough to them that they'd be motivated to provide for its future.

                                  The posters above covered excellently benefits related to Grimoires, including ones not pertaining directly to transferring knowledge. Other possible benefits of learning from a Grimoire instead of personal interaction with a teacher :
                                  It is a better choice in regard to some students ; whether because of their personal qualities that they have and will have, or because of transitory circumstances. Some students might prefer working with a text at their own pace, for example. Conversly, sometimes the author of a Grimoire might because of some reasons prefer to teach through methods other than personal interaction.
                                  The writer might want to approach a particular person at a later time, after she ( or he ) has read a Grimoire ; or observe a person after she has read a Grimoire, to ascertain and decide whether to get involved with this person.
                                  The writer might to avoid taking personal credit for her ( or his ) teachings.

                                          With a Grimoire:

                                          • You can learn appropriate Abilities at half cost.
                                          • You can improve Spheres up to the Grimoire's level at a cost of Sphere Rank × 6 or 7 minus the Grimoire level — although the text is ambiguous as to whether that means “Sphere Rank × (6 or 7 minus the Grimoire level)” or “(Sphere Rank × 6 or 7) minus the Grimoire level”. Either way, it can provide a substantial price break.
                                          • You can improve your Arete by 1 or 2 to a maximum of your Grimoire level without undergoing a Seeking; but if you don't spend the experience, this increase will only last for a week or two.

                                          (BoS p.143)

                                          As far as I know, Library does not provide any of these benefits; all it does is grant more dice on the study rolls that need to be made to justify raising a Sphere.



                                          I also have some Spellbook house rules that basically extend the utility of Grimoires by (a) letting them kind of duplicate the utility of Artifacts by having the reader follow the instructions in the book on how to cast a particular rote that he doesn't have the proper Spheres to perform on his own, and (b) letting the student learn a rote that he doesn't have the Spheres for by expending experience points on it in a manner similar to how one can create a Relic using Prime, Life, and Quintessence: the Spheres needed for incorporating the Effect into the student's Pattern are provided by the writer when he creates the Spellbook, and the Quintessence requirement is replaced by the student's xp expenditure. If you still think that by-the-book Grimoire benefits are insufficient, consider adding something like this to them as well.

                                          My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
