        In short, we already have two varieties of “Awakened”: one that involves mere awareness of the supernatural, and another that involves being able to actively influence it. I'll come back to this in a bit.

        The “punching through” notion that I mentioned actually has nothing to do with babies, by the way; it's something I came up with a long time ago to explain why Wild Magick seems to coincide with Awakening, and rarely happens apart from that. When you asked about Wild Magick and Awakened fetuses, what immediately went through my mind was “if the natural human state is Awakened, then there wouldn't be a moment of Awakening; and without a moment of Awakening, there wouldn't be that Wild Magick spike that accompanies it”. Again, nothing specific being invented for the unborn.

        Now, I'm not picturing an unborn child as having Spheres, any more than it would have a Focus; its complete lack of any experiences precludes both. And that same lack of experiences means that it's not in a position to contribute to the Consensus; it doesn't have any belief system to contribute. All it gets is a couple of dots of Arete, which mostly do nothing except the rare one-dot “Wild Magick” Effect — and nearly all one-dot Effects are sensory in nature, making this functionally equivalent to the “aware of the supernatural” kind of Awakened.


        The other thing I'm thinking of here is how the Child Mage section talks about fresh Avatars coming with the functional equivalent of True Faith, and then rapidly losing it. I see a direct parallel between this and the idea that Avatars start out Awakened, but quickly go to Sleep. And in both cases, a similar thing is missing: a belief system to contextualize the Arete/Faith. Without a Paradigm (or a similar belief in a particular higher power, in the case of Faith), the Arete or Faith is unusually fragile and tends to disintegrate when brought into contact with harsh reality. In line with Heavy Arms' latest post, an infant raised in a Chantry or otherwise sheltered from harsh reality wouldn't face that same disintegration, at least not as fast, and would be better able to develop a Focus before ending up a Sleeper.

        (I also recall a section in the Revised Celestial Chorus TB that examined the relationship between Arete and True Faith; one of the possibilities it suggested is that they're the same thing; that Faith is “merely” Arete coupled with a belief in a higher power. But I'm getting off track here.)

        Although the unborn children and infants would have Arete, I would not call them mages, because they lack nearly everything else that makes someone a mage; and their quick descent into Sleep illustrates exactly how important that missing Focus is.

        And it also suggests a possible avenues of research for the NWO when dealing with Superstitionists: is they could develop a technique, similar to Room 101 (or possibly as an application of Room 101) to shatter a mage's Focus — call all of her beliefs into question — could they maybe put her back to Sleep, and put the genie back in the bottle?

            Basically, I'm seeing four categories:

            • Sleepers have neither Arete nor Focus.
            • Mages have both.
            • Extraordinary Citizens and their mystical counterparts have Focus, but not Arete.
            • Arete without Focus is possible, but unstable: you either develop a Focus and become a mage, or you lose the Arete and become a Sleeper.

                                                    It should also be noted that the rules for making child mages explicitly give them: Arete 2 and 6 dots of Spheres, and an Affinity Sphere (which they're free to pick any rather than being bound by a faction's set).

                                                    This applies to any child mages, as the rules do specify other things that only apply to some child mages (including things like developmental stages, not just supernatural factors).

                                                    I would also say that I'd be very cautious about getting into neural development as it relates to the ability to form a Focus. There is a large difference between a Focus that's been refined to be functional on the level of what's assumed to be base-line for normal mages, and what an unrefined Focus that works well enough for some magic to be performed look like, and what level of development would be seen as necessary to form such thoughts.

                                                    I would posit that the core basis of the whole concept of Paradigms/Focuses is the human brain's pattern recognition system, because its the foundation of how we learn about the world, and build our understanding of the world. It's a part of our brain that develops in utero, because as a matter of survival we need to start sorting stimuli into patterns, and start to correlate patterns to each other. A case against very young child mages having enough of a Focus to perform magic has to be more nuanced than just "they can't possibly have one yet," because we know that it's possible to have an "immature" Focus that is less practical that the one's presented for PCs by default, that can grow into something closer to what's a normal Focus with time.

In this case, when I say “no Focus”, I don't mean literally none; I mean that any Focus they have is too “immature” to have any use as a way to focus Magick. While I'm no expert on child development, I'm pretty sure that most infants haven't gotten to the point where they can assemble what Mage considers to be a Focus: a working theory about how the world works (the Paradigm; “Everything is Data”, “Technology Hold All the Answers”, and so on) and some sort of approach to leveraging that into practical results (the Practice; Alchemy, Medicine Work, etc.). I could maybe see something like Everything is Chaos as the default Paradigm (though I tend to associate that more with an active rejection of the alternatives than as a default belief structure); but every Practice I can think of requires more experience than an infant has.

                                                            That's why I'm arguing that Awakened infants and fetuses wouldn't have a working Focus. And without that, they would have no way to use any Spheres that they might happen to have, barring only Wild Talent. The point where they develop a sophisticated enough Focus to Willwork would almost certainly come after the point when they'd normally start retaining memories. (It's entirely possible that Heavy Arms is right with his theory that being Awakened causes you to retain memories, perhaps in the form of the Avatar retaining them for you; but I wouldn't take that as a given.)

                                                            Initiates of the Art starts a newly-Awakened mage with Arete 1 and two Spheres, the idea being that the core book's starting mage has already had some time to acclimate. The fact that a child mage starts with six dots in Spheres makes me think that a similar premise is in play there: the assumption isn't that the child mage has just Awakened; is that he's been Awake for a while now, and doing things that have helped develop his Magickal comprehension of the world. Indeed, the core of thrusts debate is centered around this that section suggests that may have always been Awakened. An Awakened infant would be more like the Apprentice, if even that far developed: at most two Spheres, maybe one or even none. And again: until a functional Focus is developed, having Spheres doesn't mean much. Likewise with Willpower: starting mages have 5; but newly-Awakened mages only have 3, the same as Sleepers. I could see arguments for sharing that infants might be even weaker than that, on the average; possibly as low as 1 or 2.

                                                            The Arete 2 bothers me a bit, as I like to think of Arete as how refined your Focus is; and Child Mages having a higher Arete than adults contradicts that. But that's what the section says, so I'm more inclined to go with it than to toss it out. Perhaps a better way to think of it would be as a sort of Magickal finesse; more like an Attribute than an Ability; and how refined the Focus is might be better represented by an appropriate Ability (e.g., Esoterica or Science).

                                                            Backgrounds are also most likely to be almost entirely nil for the car majority of infants, let alone fetuses; only a handful, like Avatar or Past Lives, stand a chance of having any sort of rating; so the vast majority of Awakened infants aren't likely to have any Backgrounds. Merits and Flaws that you either have or don't will be there all along; but there aren't that many of those that actually matter in terms of the distinction between Sleeper and Awakened.

                                                            Abilities are right out; even Talents are something you develop with practice, and infants are still at the point where they're struggling to grasp language and basic mobility. And while the Child Mage section says that you must take the “Child” and “Short” Flaws, I think that even they aren't up to the task of representing the difficulties that an infant faces.

                                                            In short (no pun intended), an infant is too underdeveloped to be represented on a standard character sheet. They're missing so much of what it takes to be a playable character that there's really no point. I bring this up only because of the argument that they somehow ought to have Backgrounds, Merits, and so on, should they happen to be Awakened. I can see the argument that they shouldn't even be able to be Awakened at that stage of development; but the Book of Secrets suggests otherwise, so that's what I'm going with.

                                                            Finally, one more issue I have with saying that “they're Awakened by the time they're born” refers to the other kind of Awakened: it still suggests that the Child Mage transitioned to the Mage-style Awakened state from some other state, whereas the Book of Secrets implies that those who have always been Awakened have always been Mage-style Awakened. That's why I'm not inclined to explain away the “already Awakened when born” notion with a different kind of Awakened, and why I'm instead leaning heavily into the “Mage-style Awakened, but not yet developed enough to do anything with it” approach.
