    Esoterica

    09-24-2019, 09:55 AM
    What's the general ST take on the Esoterica Skill and its application of counting 10s twice for Arete Rolls?

    The description seems to imply it is granted for an entire practice which seems much to broad to me. I was thinking of refining it to a specific focus and the mysteries of such.

    Thoughs?

      As written, Esoterica gives you double 10s on Skill rolls, not Arete rolls (unless I missed something in HDYDT). So Esoterica basically has a side bonus of providing you a floating specialty you can apply as long as the esoteric practice would apply. You can get double 10s on a Medicine roll when using herbal medicines if your have Esoterica in Herbology, etc.

      This interacts with Arete as per the standard rules for Abilities doing so.

      The description also references Instruments not Practices when it refers back to Focus. But there really isn't a big deal here. Some Practices are going to be able to cluster Instruments around a small set of Abilities naturally, so Esoterica boosting those Abilities isn't that big of a deal.

                  Gah, why is that burried in that paragraph.

                  Though it's important to quote the whole sentence here, "Say, for example, your character has four dots in Esoterica, with a specialty in Herbalism; if he gets some time to study yoga, he can spend four experience points and then add a specialty in Yoga to that Knowledge… after which time he can use yoga as a focus tool (assuming that it fits his magickal paradigm), and count 10s as two successes on mundane or magick-casting rolls that employ that practice."

                  But yes, this would mean that you'd get to double 10s if Esoterica would apply to your magic in this fashion. I would probably be more likely to insist on some of the optional stuff to tamp down on abilities and magic working in synergy if you're worried about this getting too potent.

                          My PCs tend to have a dot or more in Esoterica. Tarot, Symbolism, Runes, Herbalism etc.

                          If they're prepared to make a ritual of what they're doing, and it's all justified, they can reduce the difficulty of an Arete roll as per the M20 rules. So Jayden could use his Tarot ability (rolling Perception + Esoterica perhaps) before trying to read someone's mind with Mind.

                          I haven't run into any problems with this, but it does occur to me that if the situation isn't time-pressured it's ALWAYS better to do an ability preparation roll if you can. If you're going to sing to do magick, then do a Charisma + Art (Singing) roll first. If you're going to rig up a hypertech device to blow something up, it's better to do that Intelligence + Hypertech roll. I suppose I could just make this sort of standard, to represent how the actual skill is the foundation of someone's practice.

                                      Yeah, I think the Esoterica entry is just weirdly worded; The way I read the 'magick-casting rolls' part, it essentially refers to Esoterica (given it's a candidate of the Well-Skilled-Craftsman rule) being a good way to get the double-10's when using the M20 rule of using an ability to reduce the Arete difficulty. That's the 'magick-casting' part of it. After all, depending on the instrument in question, there are even some practices where you *have* to roll the ability in order to do the Arete roll.

                                      You can already get double-10's on Arete rolls themselves starting with Sphere Ratings of 4+ (an often overlooked thing in M20) by courtesy of the Specialty rules. Granted, those only apply to a specific kind of effect more than a specific practice, but that's also what makes more sense for Arete rolls themselves.
