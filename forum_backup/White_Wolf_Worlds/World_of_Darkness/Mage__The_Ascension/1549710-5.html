                For my part, I think Entropy has to be able to affect forces and even moreso it has to be able to affect weather. Frankly, I find it unbelievable the Entropy couldn't be used to enhance or degrade a storm, or influence where rain lands or lighting strikes.

                Now I do think Entropy would be subject to the same restrictions as influencing material and living things: It can't create and it can't transmute, it can only push these patterns in ways they could already behave. A mage could intensify or degrade a blizzard, possibly turn it from snow to freezing rain, but he can't turn it into a hurricane or a tidal wave.

                What I am less sure of is if weather should be Entropy 3 or 4. I'm leaning 4 because Forces 4 is when that Sphere gets weather manipulation, and I think that was a game balance decision.


                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                M20 does treat ghosts and spirits as separate categories in the common effects chart. Like living things, Entropy can bless/curse ghosts and spirits at Entropy 3+, but harming ghosts actually takes Entropy 3/Prime 2 (sigh), while harming spirits directly is only listed as a Spirit effect.
                Take that for what you will.
                I missed this when you first brought it up. This is one I've been wrangling with for a while now (at least since I started the Sphere Oddities thread. I initially dismissed it as just a bad sphere combination or an artifact from earlier drafts (and it may still be) but here is what I think is going on now:

                Prime 2 (and a point of quintessence) can upgrade an attack to aggravated damage. I think M20 sometimes uses this to instead upgrade a spell into an attack that causes Aggravated Damage. Looking at the Magical Damage chart, it can make any Sphere do aggravated damage, which would include Correspondence and Time, which cannot inflict damage at all without help. That's what I think Prime 2 is doing with Entropy 3 here, escalating a touch spell to a direct attack.

                For what it's worth, the idea is first introduced on page 418, and that page also lets Spirit 2/Prime 2 affect spirits like Life magic.

                If this is the case, Entropy 3 would essentially function like Touch Ghosts, while Entropy 4 would be the Harm Ghosts level.

                    Entropy 5

                    ••••• Affect Thought/ Shape Memes/ Binding Oath

                    The most esoteric applications of Entropy allow the mage to alter ideas, strengthening or breaking down concepts. Although he does not affect the actual workings of consciousness, he can cause synapses to misfire (thus confusing perceptions and mental processing, inflicting penalties on an enemy’s dice rolls), bind someone to an oath, or degrade the patterns of thought. By doing so, that Entropic Master can craft, perpetrate, reinforce, and undermine arguments, beliefs, and even memories.

                    To do such things, the Master merely speaks to, or glances at, the subject of his attention. Chaos Masters can scramble someone’s perceptions with a few weird utterances, and Masters of Order can present arguments with apparently perfect logic. By offering compelling statements, the Master can create or destroy memes (see p. 594), thus influencing whole patterns of belief. An oath, meanwhile, ties the subject’s fate to her loyalty; if she breaks the oath, then her luck goes really, really bad.

                    In game terms, such feats demand a certain number of successes. Inspiring a whim requires only one or two successes, sparking a fancy takes three, setting or undermining a conviction takes four or five successes, and setting up obsessions demands five successes or more. Such activities are usually coincidental and are typically rolled against a difficulty of the subject’s Willpower, with a minimum difficulty of 4.
                    A much more meaty entry.

                    Entropy 5 can:


                    Manipulate Thought Processes (Affect Thought)

                    A Correspondence master gains the ability to mess with the process of thinking, manipulating synapses and perceptions. This may sound like Entropy 4’s Affect Living Things, but this rank has the power and deftness to create specific and intentional changes.


                    S.E.P. Field

                    The classic from Douglas Adams, this makes a person ignore something as Someone Else’s Problem. This is most easily accomplished with Mind, but should be possible by the level of manipulation shown in Entropy 5.

                    Manipulate Thoughts (Affect Thought)

                    I’m not sure if this is meant to be a different effect than Manipulate Thought Processes, but some of the listed effects seem to target the actual thought rather than the brain that is thinking it. A mage can plant impulses, ideas, and even beliefs in a subject with the use of Entropy. This is so much like mind manipulation that difficulty is calculated like mind effects.
                    It is worth noting, though, that Entropy 5 is explicitly stated to leave consciousness alone. This can implant motivations, memories, and impulses, but it can’t manipulate actual choices.


                    Sudden Revulsion

                    By imparting a sudden feeling of disgust or distrust, a mage can taint impressions of a person or thing.


                    Placebo

                    The mage makes the target trust that a substance or treatment will have an effect (good or bad). This leverages the well known Placebo Effect to create physiological effects in the target in response to a stimuli or treatment, even if that thing shouldn’t have any effect.


                    Placebo Domingo

                    Like the above but specifically giving the target a love of opera music and a certainty that it will make him feel better.

                    Alter Ideas (Shape Memes)

                    An Entropy Master can also skip the mucking about with brains and brain matter and skip straight to manipulating ideas themselves. What this means is a little vague and so utility will depend on the ST. At the very least, though, a mage can make an idea easier or harder to believe or understand.
                    This is one I’d like to see other people’s takes on. How do you let this work?


                    Ear Worm

                    While I named this after the song idea, the effect is an idea that keeps bringing itself to the forefront of someone’s mind. This could be a distracting annoyance, or it could manifest as oddly compelling. Whatever the case, the idea, word, song, etc is unforgettable for a period of time. This is particularly useful paired with an effect like Placebo above.

                    Bind an Oath (Binding Oath)

                    The mage can take a specific type of idea or thought, a promise, and attach a curse to it such that if the promise is violated the curse activates. In a more general sense, the mage can tie someone’s fate to an idea, thought, or belief.


                    Bind a Geas

                    The condition doesn’t have to be breaking your word. Folklore is filled with other conditions like refusing hospitality or eating dog meat.

                    This may require other spheres. For binding an oath, both the trigger (breaking an oath) and the consequence (bad luck) are in the purview of Entropy. Not every trigger will be. In the aforementioned ‘eating dog meat’ geas, Entropy 5 dooms can trigger if someone knowingly eats dog meat as that’s a mental state, but a mage might need to add Life if the doom needs to kick in when they unknowingly do so.

                    I usually add an Everything Else entry, but Entropy is hard to limit at the edges so I'm not going to do that here.

So we would have to go a little sideways of strict raw anyhow to do that, even with Forces (I think there was a Correspondence Rote somewhere that does that and we could copy, but the point stands).
                        Probability, as a concept, always deals with the undefined. As was said, if you know every element of a system there's no probability, everything works as determined.

                        Now, Entropy can't create wind, so if you want to create a wind to carry an arrow somewhere, that's something Entropy alone can't do. But in practice the ST can't and will never describe everything that's in the scene. Ofc, different ST describe things differently, but there's always things left out. At this point, should we be more concerned about the impending drought than about the monster army? :P

(regardless of the level being unclear, because Entropy doesn't affect Forces alone, albeit it should be able to because they're still part of a system, you can make statistic models about weather and such, etc).

                        I think Entropy plays a lot with that element of uncertainty, and expect players and ST to play along. To not ask for anything too outlandish, nor to deny everything that it's plausible.

                        That does make the Sphere very "ST dependant", though perhaps a better expression would be that it makes the Sphere dependant on the dialogue between players and st. But, then, I think that "too ST dependant" could be a criticism valid about all of Mage. Entropy it's just a little worse on that end.

                        I have a doubt of my own: Can I use Entropy 3-4 to create something like a protective effect on the PC that makes weapons that are used to attack them to break, regardless of the type of weapon?

                        I could argue that I don't need to know exactly what patterns I'm affecting, because Entropy ain't a pattern sphere, I'm creating a "protective aura of decay" of sorts (in a similar way as I can affect the probability of attacks to miss, regardless of the type of attack) - a counter argument could be that I *DO* need to target these systems individually to cause them to break (because it's not the same as affecting probability, patterns are mentioned as targets)
