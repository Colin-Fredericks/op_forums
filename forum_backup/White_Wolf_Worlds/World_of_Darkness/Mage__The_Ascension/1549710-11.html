    I disagree with them, yes. Heavy Arms made a good explanation that I agree with, but let me try to be more concise.

    Manipulation and Charisma are ultimately behavioral in nature. It's not about the body you have, but about how you use what you have. A big part of them is knowing how to read the room to know what's appropriate and what's not.

    Forget about pheromones or similar stuff, humans aren't much driven by "instinct", we're driven by memory. Sensory cues affect us through memory and experience, especially (but not only) social experience.

    So there's nothing you can do to your body that'll have the same effect on everyone. Charismatic people behave in a way that capitalizes trends and common experiences. Manipulative people read the target and adapt their behavior accordingly. And any "personal gift" for those things has more to do with personality than smell or appearance.

    There are more complications and nuance to that irl, and the very idea of Charisma and Manipulation as distinct Attributes is weird. But then again, that's the game as we have it.

            - What do you think Charisma and Manipulation represents in real life and how do you think someone could buff them? (Not by rules, but with in-game explanations)
            I don't think any of the Attributes coherently represent any real world traits. They are, as written, nothing more than narrative archetypes in dot form. They exist to help actualize character concepts in the mechanics, not simulate real life. These archetypes of course stem from the imaginations of real life people, so they correlate to real life traits; just not in ways that make sense on any level of inspection.

            Consider, for a moment, vile figures like serial killers, personality cult leaders, and dictators. They illustrate quite clearly that there is no real line between "charisma" and "manipulation." Ted Bundy charmed jury members sitting in judgement of him for murder. Is that insane Charisma? Insane Manipulation? Both?

            The closest thing these Attributes have to something we see is studying human interactions is the difference between pathos and ethos in the concept of classical Greek rhetorical classification. Pathos is rhetoric that appeals to the emotional desires of the audience; coinciding with Charisma. Ethos is rhetoric that appeals to a positive social position of the speaker (authority, expertise, reputation, etc.); coinciding with Manipulation. The third type in this is logos, rhetoric that relies on fully reasoned statements (which you can say is Intelligence in the few examples in the books that touch on it, but it never really comes up in terms of social influence). You notice that none of this is about what's true or not true, or the motivations of the speaker. It's just about how the strategy to appeal to the audience.

            If one wants to improve their "Charisma" and "Manipulation," like anything else, you are either addressing flaws in your current skill set, or seeking to improve on your existing strengths. In either case, you're seeking out formal or informal ways to influence other people where you can get effective feedback. Taking part in community theater or modern dance classes are easily accessible ways to work on presentation and evoking emotional responses in your audience, and you'll get feedback on your efforts without too much pressure compared to professional oriented classes. Finding a course on extemporaneous speaking is another avenue for building up your social skills where you'll receive feedback that doesn't have the same complexities of doing things in public where it would be kinda weird to ask people to rate your style.

            Other options are to expand your horizons. Immersion in other subcultures or traveling to places with significantly different cultural norms helps you reflect on your own ways of behaving when it's taken out of the normal you've adjusted it too. A simple example is "personal space." Most people don't really think about what size the bubble of space around them is that dictates how close they are comfortable with other people getting to them, and how much gradients it might have based on personal social dynamics. One of the best ways to start thinking about this (and then applying it to social interactions purposefully) is to spend time in a community where your sense of personal space is wildly different from everyone else's. People that living densely populated areas rarely intuitively understand that part of what people from more rural areas find them rude, is because urban living shrinks the sense of personal space. You can't walk down busy streets, use mass transit, and so on if you can't be very close to other people. It's not uncommon for urban living people to get "too close" and upset people used to having a wider personal space. This also goes in the other direction where rural people don't realize they're coming off as unsociable in urban situations by trying to claim too much personal space when people expect to be physically closer.

            For those less inclined to being careful about ethical or moral considerations, you can also just study con artistry. South Park did a great primer on this by taking down John Edwards (a formerly prominent TV "medium") by showing how basic the tactics of influencing people can be if you pick the right targets that Stan accidentally convinces Edwards's audience that Stan is the real medium despite him openly saying he's just manipulating them (it is South Park's kind of humor). Cold reading training coupled with mastering how to use the Forer effect and subjective validation is going to make you very good at getting people to do things they wouldn't think they'd do for a complete stranger.

            And lastly, inducing hormonal and moods reaction is on the area of Life, so, why couldn't Life create a scent that triggers oxycotin (or any other hormone or bodily function that relates to pleasure, relaxarion and trust) and makes the mage more trustworthy?
            Because there isn't a universal scent that triggers any such responses; nor are the specific reactions to such activated responses predictable. Now, a mage could certainly do that on a single target and wouldn't even need a scent trigger in the first place. The problem is that the more people you target the less effective one chemical pathway activation can possibly be. So it can never be good enough as a single chemical output to make everyone more likely to trust you (aka increasing your Attribute).

            Consider that many antidepressants have warnings of suicide risks (esp. in younger patients). Even if the risk is actually extremely low, it's not zero. So if you're a walking antidepressant chemical emitter, most people are going to feel nothing special (they're not depressed or not in a fashion that responds to medication), a good number of people are going to feel mildly better than usual around you (hey, a place for a bonus to your roll), and some people are going to hurt themselves just because they're around you too much.

            This is why I go back to science mattering if you're going to use science to justify things. This is why direct emotional manipulation is generally a Mind effect even if Life can do some emotional effects as well. Mind is the Sphere of, "aura of trust." Life isn't.

Unfortunately, all three use different terms for the limitations. Rank 2 uses ‘state of being’ for the limits on Simple Life, rank 3 uses ‘essentially human’ for Alter self, rank 4 here uses ‘intrinsic nature.’ While the words differ, though, all three seem to indicate that the mage can make a vast array of changes so long as the target is still the same kind of creature it started as.

                                        Changed: What Good is a Phone Call if You Are Unable to Speak? (Heavy Arms)

                                        The continuation, but oddly not culmination of the Alter Self track. The examples list changing from one life form into another, not turning inside out or pulling a Tetsuo (from Akira), and the transformation is limited to similar sizes and masses. One line in particular stands out:

                                        Contradiction Resolution - Special Abilities

                                        Special abilities of that form (flight, water breathing, and the like) do not carry over, however, unless he builds them into that new form with additional Life Effects

I also suspect that the additional effect line isn’t supposed to mean that the ability must be part of a separate spell, but is there to cover cases where the extra ability requires Life 5 or another sphere to achieve.
