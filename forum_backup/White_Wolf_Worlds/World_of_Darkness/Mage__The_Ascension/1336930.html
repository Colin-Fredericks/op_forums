    Understanding "Sphere Logic" in Mage: the Ascension

    09-19-2019, 04:17 PM
    I have gone through Mage a few times, and several aspects of the Sphere system are still hard to grok.

    I get some of the basic functions of how casting and effects work, and have a sort of cheatsheet down:

    * One Sphere = control over that domain, starting with perception and moving to "godlike feats."
    * You can maintain multiple ongoing spells, but a spell can only have one Effect on it.
    * An Effect exists in a "fixed" point of spacetime, unless you use another Sphere to lock the Effect onto a certain Pattern.
    * You can only cast the effect in your localized spacetime without the use of Correspondence or Time Spheres.
    * You need Prime in order to "create" patterns ex nihilo; you can only "alter/control/link" patterns otherwise.

    This said and done, there are several things I am unsure about how to actually work, and I would rather not have to look up "How Do You Do That," as I hear there is a lot of skub involved with it. The big one is this:

    Can you create a spell with a "parameterized"/"wildcard" effect? For example, suppose you want to create a spell so that, the next time a person steps through a door, you mentally project"Hello, {that person's name}" to {that person's} mind.

    From strict RAW, this looks like it would need two seperate: One to determine the target's name, and one to broadcast a message to the person who just stepped in. The problem is, I don't see any way for the "output" of the first effect (determining the target's name) to be supplied as an "input" to the message broadcast to the target's mind.

    Am I missing something here?

      Originally posted by MagicJuggler View Post
      * You can maintain multiple ongoing spells, but a spell can only have one Effect on it.
      Just something that seems worth amending: You can maintain a limited number of ongoing spells.

      Can you create a spell with a "parameterized"/"wildcard" effect? For example, suppose you want to create a spell so that, the next time a person steps through a door, you mentally project"Hello, {that person's name}" to {that person's} mind.
      Yes.

      This specific example takes Correspondence and Mind (and maybe more depending on how paranoid your mage is). Correspondence is used to create the spatial parameter of the spell (when X walks through or into a specific point in space), as well as linking the spell to the space itself (rather than, say, using Matter to link it to the doorway, which you could do.... but why bother). Mind both detects the specific mind of the person once they meet the parameter if this is pre-programmed for an individual or scans their mind for a name (this is were paranoid mages would add more to such a spell to help make sure they're targeting the right individual and not a faker), and projects the mental thought into the target's mind.

      It is, in essence, a very nice variation on using a Ward/Ban effect as a greeting instead of a warning or actual barrier.

      I think what's hanging you up is, "...but a spell can only have one Effect on it"

      This is true, but not as hard as you're making it to be. You're viewing this as two Effects because it's two basic/common Effects. Effects are allowed to be far more complicated than most of the sample ones. They key is (and this is not a simple hard line to draw sometimes) are you adding conditions to one Effect to create a more complex Effect, or stacking multiple spells together.

      Some of this is going to also come down to the mage and how they perceive things/want things to be done.

      The way to keep this a single Effect is to, at least mechanically, treat the core effect as, "project the target's name as they perceive it into their minds as part of a message," this is a single coherent effect. The addition parameters such as giving it the power to read their minds, and locking the spell to a specific point in space are adding complexity to this concept, rather than adding new things.

      The one Effect rule is meant to stop things like trying to case a healing spell on your friend, and a fire ball at your enemy in one spell. It's not one cohesive if complex Effect concept, it's two different ones that would be convenient to do at the same time.

      In practice, don't sweat this one too much. Doing it as multiple spells has some pluses and minuses, as does doing it as a single spell. Esp. for something a mage has plenty of time to set up, a single spell is generally better for them, but most of the time it's not going to be the difference between the characters doing what they're trying to do or not, just how much work it takes to get there.

          Originally posted by MagicJuggler View Post
          Can you create a spell with a "parameterized"/"wildcard" effect? For example, suppose you want to create a spell so that, the next time a person steps through a door, you mentally project"Hello, {that person's name}" to {that person's} mind.
          Yes. That is what the Time 4 triggers are for. In this case you would use Time 4 to make the trigger/hanging effect, you'd use Matter 2 to tie the effect to the doorway, Life 1 to associate it with any lifeform moving through the doorway (life 3 might be specific enough to make it work on people only), and Mind 3 for the mental message. You can get by with Forces instead of Mind if you want to make it an audible message. No need for Prime to create the sound ex nihilo, since you can use another force nearby to convert and use depending on level.

          The only really tricky part would be the Name...in that case, it might be wiser to stick with Mind instead of Forces. Mental message that also tries to discern the name from the subject.

          All this, by the way, pretty much counts as a single effect. The effect is 'Personally greet people as they step through the doorway'.

          Don't be too restrictive in what counts as a single effect..it's very blurry borders when it comes to those, and very much ST decision. If it serves a single purpose, it usually can be called a single effect.

          EDIT:
          To say, Heavy Arms' solution is more elegant, as it replaces a lot of the Sphere necessities with Correspondence. I still say it requires TIme 4, though.
          But, It just shows you a thing many people experienced with Mage will tell you: There are always multiple ways to skin a cat.
          Heck, we had a thread where we tried to figure out all possible ways to literally skin a cat with magick.

                          Originally posted by MagicJuggler View Post
                          * An Effect exists in a "fixed" point of spacetime, unless you use another Sphere to lock the Effect onto a certain Pattern.
                          * You can only cast the effect in your localized spacetime without the use of Correspondence or Time Spheres.
                          Bit of a minor quibble here but it is important. You're using the word 'spacetime' twice here which kind of implies that you're assuming a scientific/real world physics approach to the spell effects. When you take a 'real world' approach to magic you're ignoring the various other paradigms that mage allows for. It's actually quite hard to 'think like a mage' when working out spells and their rules but it is an essential exercise to understand that the same effect can be attained through different paradigms (and that some paradigms are innately 'easier' to work with certain effects).

                          Take the classic fire(ball/bolt/etc) spell - to a technocrat it's a high tech flamethrower/plasma projector, to a traditionalist it's tapping into hermetic forces, to an alchemist it's combining potent ingredients, and to an infernalist it's channelling demonic power.

                          Each of those paradigms has a different sort of 'physics' and flipping viewpoints between them is an important skill to master for Mage players and storytellers. It's all to easy to fall back into thinking about effects with the modern scientific approach we have been taught at school, but mage is multi-angled and works really well when we put aside our modern thinking and open up to the paradigms in the game.

                          Edit: Forgot to say, because I'm not calling you out for having wrongfun, that if you're happy with the modern approach then go for it, I like the way Mage allows you to use different mindsets to solve a problem but it's not the 'one true way'

                              FWIW, I also think one should consider the significance of the effect when setting sphere requirements, rather than trying to parse the exact physical mechanics. It's magic, afterall. You can always add more specificity and talk yourself into more extreme requirements, but I don't find that to be in the spirit of things.

                              To me, the key elements of the proposed spell are that it's a telepathic message, and that it waits. As such, I'd say Mind 2 (maybe 3 to add in the "name" blank to the message, but I'd say that's within scope for the Mind sphere alone if you just make it "whatever the mind calls itself"), plus Time 2 or 3 to delay it until the condition is met. Mind 1 can detect the presence of a mind so that's good enough for the trigger. I wouldn't call for Correspondence or Matter unless you wanted to, say, embed the message specifically in the doorframe itself (so that it will still work if, say, someone moved it to another building), and I wouldn't call for Life unless there was some biological property (however specific) in the trigger that Mind alone couldn't handle. E.g., a woman, a pregnant woman, a pregnant woman with a cold, etc. Ironically, being maximally broad ("any conscious mind") or very specific ("my friend Dave") would fit comfortably in Mind, you'd only need other spheres to come down in the middle, or to get even MORE specific (Dave, but only if he's bleeding from a cut on his left hand)

                                          The thing is, Time and Correspondence work very differently in the way they treat their influence.

                                          Time 2 doesn't send anything anywhere. It allows to see stuff, but not to talk or otherwise influence the future/past you're seeing. Time 2 would be WAY more powerful than described if it allowed to interact with other times the same way Corr 2 interacts with other places

                                          To send the message *later* but *right here* you would need Time 5. If you're literally sending it to the future, that's time travel.

                                          Time 4 it's used to keep a message (or, in this case, an Effect) *frozen* to be released at a latter time. Time triggers it's an odd way to refer to the act of freezing an effect with Time 4 so it doesn't take place the turn it's casted (like it's normal for most effects). The nature of the *actual* trigger seems to matter little to this (I think it's mentioned somewhere to add Entropy if you want the effect to be triggered by an event, but Time 4 it's pretty constant in Effects that are to take place at a later time).

                                          So, I have to agree with Heavy Arms and Ambrosia here. Time 4 would be needed to place the message in suspension until something happens, unless you have other way to make the effect *hang* there. And that's the cheapest way using Time alone to define the "delivery".

                                          That being said, a houserule to make Time more in line with Correspondence (to allow these sort of "late deliveries" that we call time triggers) wouldn't be a bad idea. I don't think it's op, if we add also the Correspondence precaution of allowing to hang only effects of the same Sphere level as the level of Time used.
