                As I said, I like the names because they show momentum within the Traditions, which reflects the fact that, due to the metaplot, the oldest and most powerful mages are gone.                             The Euthanatos's Wheel concept has had its origin in South Asian philosophy since the First Edition. The entire backstory with the Akashayana is based around the fact that they're both Asian Traditions that incorporate the concept of Reincarnation. Kali and the Thuggies have been used as thematic examples for a very long time, and Voormas is both East Asian, himself, and his art depictions are clearly inspired by Hindu religious art. The spokes of the wheel are called Chakra-Dharma in the first Tradition book. "Jhor" is not a Greek word.

                            And like Akashayana, the Chakravanti name appears as early as the first Tradition book, so it's not actually a new idea.

                            The Euthanatos name works as a subset for the specific Tradition members who use Greek or Celtic religious elements in their practice, but it makes much more sense, to me, for the very heavily South Asian inspired Tradition to have a name that reflects that.

                            I'm certainly not saying anyone has to use the new name. But I think it makes sense. Especially when we consider how, in the game's metaplot, all the stuff with House Helekar and Voormas would have happened relatively recently, and perhaps would have inspired the Tradition to embrace a change of external image.

                                On the Euthanatoi/Chakravanti thing: the names for the Traditions first appeared in M1e, before they got fleshed out in their respective Tradition Books. When they did get fleshed out, they occasionally ended up being significantly different than the stereotypes of them that were embodied in their original descriptions. The Euthanatoi are a prime example of this: originally portrayed as death-mages and assassins, their expanded treatment has them as much more than that: death magic and assassination is what they're best known for because they're the only Tradition that's willing to do the dirty work that needs to be done; but it's only a small part of what they do. The shift from Euthanatos to Chakravanti is about recognizing that the Tradition is more about the wheel of fate than about death and killing; it's not about “cleaning them up” as it is about being more accurate. Of the three “reversion” name changes, it's the one that bothers me the least.

                                And I'm fine with “Virtual Adepts”; I don't think they need a rebranding.

                                    So with the Chakravanti thing.

                                    Fundamentally, on the most visible level of things, You should keep as much as you can in the language you speak. For the uninitiated, the least amount of loanwords you use, the better. "we're the Chakravanti" doesn't sell as well as, say, "we're the Wheel" or the Tapestry or the cycle, especially when you're the whitest Canadian the world has ever seen. Once people have taken the hook you can really get into Dharmas, but if you try too hard too soon you come off as a wanker who's trying to sound fancy and cultured and "Culturally appropriating". You need a shiny, easily digestible name to attract people into the deep end of your philosophy. I really love the Samsara path of Paradox for Ravnos, and all it's Dharma stuff, but the draw of the name there is the Path of Paradox, which gives me some idea of what the path might be about. The other stuff just looks like noise until you invest in it. When you're trying to sell all the different traditions to players, you need them to have clean and easy names.
