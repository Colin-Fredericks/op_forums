    Would the Tradition mages be anti-vaxxers?

    09-23-2026, 07:46 PM
    Would the Tradition mages use the waves of fake news to try to corrode the Technocracy paradigm?

    For example, would they defend the idea that the Earth is flat, that vaccines don't work, that evolution is not real and etc?

    In one hand, it would make sense (at least for the Traditions that don't rely on science), since in theory it would help drive the consensus against the Technocracy.

    In other hand... Yuck?

      Some would.

      The Traditions' old guard in particular has a lot of folks who are anti-science and opposed to modernity in all forms. They're not as powerful as they've been in the past, but are still there.

      But they're not unified behind this, and crucially they wouldn't sacrifice their own Paradigms for aimless jabs against the Union. A Mage doesn't easily promotes a worldview they don't believe in, themselves.

      Most Tradition Mages, especially after the turn of the century with the Avatar Storm, believe in modern science and technology and that they are ok things. Not central to their beliefs, but ok things. They're not going to promote or accept this kind of complete bullshit.

      Yet some definitely believe, and being Mages they can actually find proof for their beliefs. So yes, there are anti-vaxxers in the Traditions and they're the violent kind who think this is a life or death matter.

      Worse, because the world of Mage is what it is, they may have a point: just because vaccines are conceptually real doesn't mean vaccination programs are all real. Plenty of entities in WoD are capable and willing to mess with vaccines, including the Technocratic Union. Some think it abhorrent as it would foment distrust if found out, but others wouldn't think twice before they put something in or even change the content to something else entirely. Reality Zones are even more complicated as they can change how the vaccines work within.

      Now, what kind of misinformation exactly will be spread depends. They are not equal, even if frequently supported by the same people. A lot more people doubt evolution than Earth being round, and vaccine deniers have a spectrum of rationales and positions that matter, especially in Mage. This game is about belief, the reason behind an opinion is as important or more than the position itself.

      Flat Earth in particular isn't a new phenomenon as we actually have known the Earth is round for a long time. Contrary to popular belief, the Church scholars of the Middle Ages were well acquainted with the fact the Earth is round and with several ways to demonstrate it, and also had to worry about idiots defending theories of anti-biblical conspiracies hiding the truth of the Earth being actually flat. We've known the form of the planet for millennia already, and even the exact circumference is known since the 3rd century BCE.

                  One thing to remember is that most Tradition Paradigms don't really "break" if they believe in things like a round Earth or vaccines. The Earth is Divine and Alive, whether it's round or flat.

                  The main pushback you get in the Traditions is from the Old Guard (which is to say, those 100+ years old) who oppose the Technocratic worldview on principle. Most Mages born within the last century carry a lot of cognitive baggage from their pre-Awakening days that never gets abandoned. Because why would they? Vaccines save mankind a vast amount of grief.

                  Traditionalists likely debate this kind of thing all the time internally. Is it useful, or even moral, to dismantle the parts of the Technocratic paradigm that genuinely help people? And if Sleepers go around spreading pseudoscience and such, is it really okay to co-opt their movements to spread Traditionalist beliefs?

                  Moreover, there's a massive slippery slope here, when we consider how often pseudoscience and science skepticism are intertwined with Fascist movements. The Flat Earth movement notably lost steam after a while...because a ton of their adherents moved to QAnon.

                  (I'm of belief that any MtAs game set in Current Year has to reckon with resurgent Fascism in Real Life. And the way that, much like the 1930s/40s, a political philosophy that is both Anti-Rational, and built on reinforcing unjust power structures, is not a "Technocracy vs Traditions" divide. It's going to play out ACROSS sect lines. With members of both being forced to face others "on their side" that have embraced Fascism, for various reasons. Which, at the very least, creates interesting seeds for mixed Tradition/Technocracy cabals, or at least team-ups.)

                                                  You misunderstood me. Falsify is not about lie.

                                                  Falsifying is a methodology, one of the cores of modern Scientific Method. Falsify is proposing an experiment to test a hypothesis with focus on its failure points. Instead of pointing to existing evidence of it working, you stress test it (or at least propose a future stress test)

                                                  Gods are deemed outside scientific scrutiny because they are non falsifiable: whenever you predict a divine intervention will happen and have X effect, X not happening may have a lot of perfectly reasonable explanations, from the god being offended, knowing things you don't, to other gods intervening.

                                                  Einstein's theory that light had mass was falsifiable, although it took years for an opportunity to test it to appear. If light has mass, it is subject to gravity, if it is subject to gravity, then any sufficient mass should work as a lens, and if all those are true, then it should be possible to watch a star, calculate its motion until it is behind a known massive body, and predict a point where it would be seen that is not its actual position, due to lensing. If the theory is wrong, the prediction will fail and will fail hard.

                                                  That's the non-falsifiability problem in Mage: a Mage alters reality by nature to conform to the Paradigm, and so they produce the response in accordance to their Paradigm. Any failure is a mere fluke if execution or lack of skill, not the theory failing, and this is objectively true every time with them.

                                                  No Mage can devise a stress test where their Paradigm is proven wrong, because any stress test exposes Reality to their power and change the outcome. No success or failure can be reliably attributed to the theory, only weakly so.

                                                  And I agree that Mages have a tendency to be more open-minded. But not always. And not in a predictable manner. We see that clearly on many Crafts. The clash of diverse experiences with unshakable confidence in one's own view can and does produce truly weird outcomes.

                                                  So, is any Mage a dumb follower of QAnon? It's unlikely. Are there Mages that include a lot of QAnon stuff in their worldview, even if the whole picture is more picturesque? Probably a bunch of them and with the type of confidence that foments action and justify wars.
