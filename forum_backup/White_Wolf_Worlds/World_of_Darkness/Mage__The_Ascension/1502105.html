                      It's worth noting that while Entropy frequently gets associated with or portrayed as evil in the WoD, it's important to note that it's not the only source of ill in the world. From the perspective of the Traditions, the Technocracy-as-Stasis is arguably a bigger problem from day to day. And many Garou oppose the Weaver just as much as the Wyrm.

                      Other gamelines don't directly associate Entropy with evil, but they do often come with some notion that there is something wrong with the world, or that it is heading into a low point in the cosmic cycle. And the forces of Entropy in the world are some of the best suited for manifesting that corruption, especially when your game embraces horror themes and aesthetics, as mentioned above.

                                          Evil basically boils down to what humans deem harmful to us. We fear pain of both physical and emotional origin, we fear death and the lack of understanding of what follows.
                                          We don't like it when the things that make us happy stop making us happy or start upsetting us. Be that because we lost that thing through destruction or death, because our personality has changed, we fall out of love or whatever.

                                          Point being; death and change typically scare us and make us experience other negative emotions, and so they are bad. Fictional personifications of those things that bring us misery are thus evil.
                                          (On an objective level, WoD's Entropy is antagonistic because it's out of balance due to Stasis overwhelming it due to that hatred/fear of change and endings.)​

                                              The idea of a metaphisical Triad comes directly from hinduism - the WoD one, not the Christian kind.

                                              In Hinduism, you have Brahma the Creator, Vishnu the Keeper and Shiva the Destroyer. In some traditions hinduism is even monotheistic-like, with the 3 being aspects of the same one being, and every other Gods being aspects of them - sorta like Avatars of those.

                                              "Avatar" by the way also comes from hinduism - it's a demi-god encarnation of a God, like Krishna for instance, who is the 9th Avatar of Brahma, and probably the most well know of all hinduist Avatars, with Krishna being one of the most beloved of all mythic figures in hinduism.

                                              Some gurus even compare Krishna with Christ (to the horror of Christians), and in a sense, Jesus could be called as an "Avatar" of the God of Israel - what Jesus is for Christians is exactly what avatars in hinduism are.

                                              Anyway, despite the literally MILLIONS of Gods in hinduism - and the reason that they have so many is that there are minor Gods for everything, including Gods for individual families, somewhat akin to the idea of personal "guardian angels" for christians (mostly for catholics thou), however, the most worshipped God of all in hinduism is Shiva, the Destroyer.

                                              Shiva is the yoga dancer, a serene God and basically the one that invented meditation. Hmmm... A God of Destruction... That meditates? Seems... Off. Right?

                                              Well, Shiva is considered the ultimate benevolence. Shiva is supposed to "turn the lights off" at the very end of the Universe, "undoing" even all the other Gods at the end of the cicle.

                                              AKA, Shiva is the one who will be responsible to destroy EVERYTHING by the end of the cicle.

                                              Remember all the Nefandi and Spectres and Wyrm that wish to destroy the Tellurian? Well, that's Shiva's job.

                                              His wife is Kali, who is the Goddess of DEATH (quite appropriate for "The Destroyer"), and Kali on her own end, is an aspect of Parvati who, surprise surprise, is the Goddess of LIFE.

                                              (In fact, in some traditions, Kali and Parvati are the two coins of the same entity... Hindu Gods are weird for us to understand and it's hard to explain, their theological concepts are completely different from our christian cultural base)

                                              Shiva is venerated even by DEMONS (Rakshasa or something like it, Im not sure I spelled it right, cut me some slack) - but not because he is evil, quite the contrary, it's because Shiva is the ultimate good and ultimate justice. There are even stances upon which Shiva judged on the side of the demons against the Gods, when those were in the right. ALL are equal for lord Shiva.

                                              And his wife Kali is an exceptional demon hunter by the way.

                                              You see, I think that this idea that Entropy is bad comes from a western view of eastern ideas. The Triad is unquestionably hinduist in origin.

                                              But, death and destruction is undeniably viewed as "evil" in western culture. When you die, you go to paradise, were everything is ETERNAL. Death is a punishment due to the original sin, and we live in a world of death and decay because of it.

                                              Even an atheist like myself have that christian cultural heritage.

                                              But in hinduism, life, creation, death and destruction are weaves of the same fabric, and are all part of an endless cicle of reencarnation.

                                              Therefore, my take is that the reason why Entropy is considered bad is simply that it was an eastern idea that was misunderstood under a western viewpoint
