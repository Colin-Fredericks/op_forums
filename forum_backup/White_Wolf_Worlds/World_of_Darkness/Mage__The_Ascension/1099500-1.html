                Originally posted by Faradn View Post
                As Queen Nihil mentioned, according to The Book of Madness (Revised) they're described as permanently damaging reality in a way that Sphere magic typically does not. Like for Qlipspondence, the space in between the objects affected is partly erased, forever. Anyone subjected to a Qulipmind Effect will experience a permanent decrease in sanity--etc. That seems like a big difference to me. It seems to be kind of a fundamental feature actually, that goes beyond Resonance.
                And that is something that is perfectly 100% totally and completely that any normal Sphere could do effectively. And if you REALLY want to hammer it home, there are Prime and Entropy effects that specifically deal with that. So, once again, there is nothing other than shear flavor text to make the distinction. Would you, as a ST, tell a player she or he could not eliminate the space or utterly wipe out the memory if their Focus and way of doing magic created a framework in which that makes total sense (of which I could reel off half a dozen with no problem)? Would you, as a player whose character was so configured feel that the ST was diminishing your character when you tried to do such a thing (which, once again, makes PERFECT SENSE according to how your character does magic) and gives you, as an excuse, that, no, you can't do that because that's something only Nephandi can do? When there is nothing out there but some stuff over here (a few statements on "What Nephandi Magic Is" which is directly in contradiction to all that huge pile of stuff over there (e.g. Mage magic and it's inherent flexibility as a whole)?

                Which, once again, is why the Marauders come across as genuinely different and the Nephandi are just "bad mages" that really aren't different from any other "bad mages". There is a mechanical difference for the Marauders built entirely on one salient feature - the way Marauders handle Paradox, keying in on how they experience Quiet.

                Whereas, since Ascension per se is pretty flavorless (Another Arete = another die and that's pretty much it... compare to the way Foundations work in DA), so Decension is equally flavorless. And there is nothing beyond ST fiat or some really difficult overreaching/mental gymnastics on the fluff to make Qlippothic Spheres anything other that "woo-woo bad" Spheres that work just like their normal counterparts. Pretty much, the Nephandi are missing that key THING that makes them categorically different from the other mages. Which is 90%+ of the problem when any thread comes up discussing them. There is nothing that really keeps Gilles de Rais from being set up as a Hermetic or Chorister who is also just a very very very bad man who uses Maleficia as a Practice and all sorts of naughty things as Instruments as well as courting some absolutely awful Resonance willingly. There is nothing (besides the agreement of the players at the table) that keeps any PC from being just as bad as Gilles de Rais and still being a perfectly acceptable mage as far as being able to do magic, including "magic of ultimate negation". Of course, there are usually some laws, ethics and social mores that are being shattered and there are social consequences, but there is nothing that is stopping them from doing that mechanically. As further proof of concept on that, just replace Gille de Rais with Voormas (or anyone from the Consanguinity of Eternal Joy) and the equivalencies are nakedly there. Which leads to all of these various sorts of discussions about how bad are the Nephandi, can one toy with being one and what does that mean, etc. And with the fact that those discussions inevitably fall apart in some fashion.

                So... as I said after Dataweaver's post... Simplest is best. The rules side can be kept VERY simple and the whole "Dark Road She's Going Down" is more of a story thing than it is a mechanical thing, because there just aren't any mechanics that essentially aren't just fluff behind a thin scrim of kinda-sorta-but not really rules.

                    I think I have a different problem than Ajax above. I don't really see a problem with tweaking the mechanics for your Qlippothic spheres to emphasize the destructive elements. I think that fits the vague flavor text of the Book of Madness and Infernalism.

                    My problem is that I think the concept is seriously diluted when you try to make it something for player characters. I think the flavor text makes it clear that Qlippothic spheres, like the Nephandi themselves, go beyond just standard "I am evil" bad guys. The concepts of the Nephandi, descension, and bringing the world back to Oblivion are very compelling when used well. But it's supposed to be about Oblivion. Scorched Earth or the Heat Death of the Universe. Actively pursuing the end of all things, and so on and so forth.

                    If normal spheres are about manipulating the Tapestry, then Qlippothic spheres are about actively trying to unravel it. You've said that your character is amoral, not evil, that she believes in the cycle. Then there's no way that I could rationalize such a character actively using Qlippothic spheres. Because Qlippothic spheres are not intended to reflect natural empathy or facilitate the turning of the wheel. Qlippothic spheres are meant to kick the fulcrum out form under it. The cycle gets destroyed with everything else.

                    That's why the books emphasize the idea of very little mechanical difference between them. Because it isn't about getting a slight advantage. It's because you're literally not supposed to be able to access these spheres unless you have the specific intention to not just destroy an object, but to unravel the fabric of reality.

                    I'd recommend Infernalism, if you haven't read it, Queen Nihil, because the short section on Qlippothic spheres has a very quick look at each individual sphere and how the Qlippothic mage approaches them. And honestly, to me, there's no real way to rationalize using them unless the character is genuinely evil.

                    But that said, your setting sounds like it has a number of unique elements, so if it works for you guys that's what matters. :-)

                                Sphere variants aren't just about adding or focusing on certain aspects of a Sphere (e.g., play up the destructive aspects). If that's all they did, they would all be overpowered. No, when you enhance certain aspects of a Sphere to better represent a given theme, you should also de-emphasize other aspects of that Sphere that run counter to that theme. If a Qlipphothic Sphere is inherently better at tearing things apart, it should also be worse at putting things together. If it's about 0 rather than ∞, identify what sorts of Effects correlate to 0 and what sorts correlate to ∞, strengthen the former, and weaken the latter.

                                Look at Dimensional Science. It can do everything Spirit can do; but it's better when it comes to manipulating the Gauntlet and traveling to other Realms and it's not as good when it comes to manipulating EDEs.

                                Sometimes, this shift of focus can be accomplished by trading levels: move shapeshifting effects down a dot in a Life variant while moving healing effects up a dot, and you have a variant that focuses more on change and less on stability. And so on.

                                My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
