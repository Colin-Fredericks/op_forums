        So, another game mechanic question that I am having trouble untangling... And I realize that this will sound like min-maxing, but I can't see any reason not to do the following.

        Any mystic character concept you like; Arete 3; paradigm, practice, 7 instruments of your choice; and the instrument you choose to discard for gaining the third dot of Arete is as versatile as your practice can allow (language, thought forms, etc)

        Is there any GAME-MECHANIC reason why that mage wouldn't use that specific instrument for all of their effects, getting the -1 TN for using an instrument you don't need?

                        Originally posted by Saikou View Post

                        This is one of the reasons why I disregard the "build-a-paradigm" approach M20 goes for in favour of a more personalised "how does the universe work in your mage's mind" philosophical musings approach.
                        How does that work out in actual game play? Does the game become even more of an extended philosophical sparring match between a particular player and the ST over pretty much every casting? (With the play effect of sidelining every other player....) How does that even manifest?

                        That's one reason finally having some real concrete paradigm in play utility rules (aka Focus) were, IMHO, one of the major contributions of M20. Now we don't have to stumble over the whole player part of describing their part of the casting as it was previously. Now they know they have to give an explanation of what their character is DOING in-game to make the magic happen.

                        E.g. We have two characters in my current Mage group that have Mind 3 & the Correspondence to use it beyond of line of sight. One is NWO CSI and the other is a Cultist transcendentalist rustic poet. In the most recent session, the player of the NWO said, "I want to set up a telepathic network with the other members of the group." So there was a brief navel-gazing discussion that was focused on the practical mechanics of how that worked vis-a-vis his Focus, which ventured into how that would, or even could, work as a possible effect for his character. Since he is usually reading people's minds by making deductions from kinesics and proximics, often prompted by eliciting responses by his statements, tone, postures, etc. He posited he could do it using the ear buds created by another PC. The question was still "How does that work, if he needs to not just hear, but see and also interact with the target?" The answer was that he couldn't really do that as things were set up. (Which led to a later discussion between the NWO character and the tech support character about making the "ear buds" do more than just transmit/receive noise and maybe transmit a neural map to the NWO's new tablet to allow him greater insight combined with his understanding of tone, word choice, etc. that would be the telepathic-reciever part of the "mass mind link" effectand he could just use his typical super-innuedo voice to get his thoughts across to the others.... These neural mapping ear buds are on the tech-guy's To-do list.)

                        He ended up just asking the Cultist to do it. Which was much closer to effortless. Previously, the Cultist had drawn pictures of each character in his journal and had them sign them. He had made 5 pics of himself, signed them, tore the page out and up into 5 pieces and gave one to each of them. When asked to set up the telepathic link, he opened up his journal to the page and made up a poem about them, listening to the whispering wind, mutual understanding, etc. (No, the player didn't make up the poem, but, sometimes she does create a few lines on the spot.... typically gets a -1 Diff for that effort). Wham! Telepathic links all around.

                        Note: Most of the discussion (which took less time to have than it has taken me to type it) wasn't about how the character's perceive magic, particularly as a philosophical construct. There was some of that for the NWO PC, but much of it was implicit rather than explicit. The vast majority of the discussion was about how doing all that looked in the world, what the characters put into doing their magic, etc. That's one of the benefits of the M20 Focus system. Sure, we did that to one degree or another beforehand (sometimes, often it was just wishcrafting reality, particularly back in 1st Ed days), but now we have an actual structure to inform those discussions and make them material and meaningful when actually playing the game.

                            That is helpful, and once gameplay has started, it makes sense, but it leaves the question(s) that come up from the create-you-own-paradigm/practice (CYOPP) at character creation about which Instruments to choose and when the benefit of working without instruments from higher Arete applies.

                            The house rule that I have been working on goes like this.

                            Every practice has X number of instruments (default 7, but I'm considering 9). Each instrument covers one from a set list of categories and is the instrument that counts when the primary aspect of that effect falls into that category. All Instruments must align the mage's chosen practice.

                            (See below for category idea)

                            At least X/3, round down (so 2 or 3 for 7 or 9 respectively) need to be things the mage DOES, represented by an Ability.

                            At least X/3, round down, need to be things that the mage HAS, being a physical item (or items) the mage has.

                            The remaining can be either Ability or Item.

                            For Ability Instruments, the rank in that Ability must be equal to or greater than the rank of the highest sphere used) and the Ability must be able to be used at the time of casting. If the Ability is a secondary Ability (meaning the Ability itself won't see much dice rolling), it counts as a Unique Instrument (standard -1 to TN).

                            For Item Instruments, the item has to be unique, complicated or delicate enough that replacing it or repairing it takes a week of effort and the item(s) have to be present and in the possession of the mage at the time of casting. If the item is unique, complicated or delicate enough that replacing or repairing it takes a month or more, or its own side quest, it counts as Unique.

                            Each instrument (Ability or Item) has its own recommended amount of time needed to be used appropriately as well as whether or not they are suitable for extended rolls or ritual work. Using instruments too quickly or inappropriately causes the appropriate penalties to the TN.

                            Examples

                            Computer: 1 minute, Ritual: Yes
                            Drive: 1 combat turn, Ritual: No
                            Firearms: 1 combat turn, Ritual: No
                            Intimidation: 1 minute: Ritual: No
                            Meditation: 1 minute, Ritual: Yes
                            Technology: 1 hour, Ritual: Yes

                            Art (drawing, music, quick sketch): 1 minute, Ritual: Yes
                            Art (other): 1 hour, Ritual: Yes
                            Media: 1 hour, Ritual: Yes
                            Social Dominion: 1 minute, Ritual: Yes
                            Melee Weapons: 1 turn (combat, 6 seconds), Ritual: Yes
                            Vocalization: 1 turn (combat, 6 seconds)


                            The category issue...
                            In 1st Ed, the categories were obvious. 9 categories, 1 for each sphere. The highest sphere or the one primarily responsible for the effect would be the one that counts for this idea. But not all groups use the sphere model (Kopa Loei for example).

                            Another category list could just be the general purposes of the effect, like

                            Offensive
                            Defensive
                            Restorative
                            Transportation
                            Alteration
                            Perception
                            Communication
                            Control
                            Creation

                            This works a little better, but I am concerned it is not an exhaustive list.

                            The end goal is that the benefit of higher Arete goes toward a specific subset of effects, that subset grows as the Arete increases until Arete 10 covers all effects and that the Instruments chosen have a clear impact on time requirements.
