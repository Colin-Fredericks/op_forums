            That's not nihilism. Nihilism is the belief that moral meaning does not have the same objective reality as thermodynamics and 2+2 and so forth. Nihilism is essentially just a logical extrapolation on atheism. Nihilists are no more likely to want to destroy the world than anyone else. Confusing nihilism with what the Nephandi espouse is the virtual equivalent of confusing atheism with satanism.

                            Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post

                            Because the Marauders don't have any real organization due to their nature. Yeah, you may get a band of Marauders running around causing mayhem but they don't have anything resembling anything close to real organization.

                            The Nephandi on the other hand can and do. While they may not be as organized as the Traditions and Technocracy, there is a degree of organization and communication among the larger Nephandi groups. And there has been at least one instance of them gathering together in large numbers and wrecking havoc in a unified force, World War Two.

                            Long story short, it got so bad that the Traditions and Technocracy had to officially unite to deal with the Nephandi threat. To date, that is the only time the groups have officially united, not the temporary team of a Cabal and Construct but a straight up partnership between the two organizations to deal with the threat.

                            Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                            My relationship with World of Darkness is conflicted. I love the general ideas it has, but I also find myself troubled by the concept that these supernatural and magical beings have declined to the point of being forced to hide from humanity just to survive. Dreaming is the game that resonates the strongest with me, and I find it to be the most depressing setting in the entire World of Darkness cosmology.
                            Unsurprising. CtD is the most depressing game of the bunch. While it has the facade of being bright and cheerful, it is actually fairly depressing and bleak.

                            That said, it's a mistake to consider the supernatural in decline and why they are hiding from humanity. Certainly the times have changed and the world's far cry from the halcyon days of legend but it's not all grim and bleak.

                            To begin with, x20 has been showing the various splats better adapting to the modern world and moving away from this idea of a decline because how different the x20 Ed is from all the previous editions.

                            Keep in mind that the Rev Ed was a countdown to the Time of Judgment and the end of the World of Darkness. As a result, the books of that line are all flavored by that. And all of that came from the fact that one of the things that went into the WoD when they were first designing it was the Millennium Scare of the 90's. But when you jump to the x20 Ed of the lines, much of that has been pushed aside, moved to the optional box because the world didn't end in 2004.

                            In fact, the first MtAs book Onyx Path did were the four Rev Ed Convention Books that were never done before the WoD was originally ended. And in the Introduction Chapter of the first of those books, the NWO CB, they had this closing section talking about that and the setting of these new CBs.

                            This book is first and foremost a love letter to the Mage: The Ascension fans who carried a torch for this wondrous world these many years. This book, for the most part, leaves off from the Iteration X book in 2001.

                            In another reality, the Time of Judgment came, a Red Star its herald. The world ended, either through mass Ascension, war, or catastrophe. Yet, here we are in 2012, our world for the moment still spinning. For us, the Red Star was not a harbinger of doom, but a passing moment of curiosity, instilling dread in those most paranoid. Our destinies certainly changed, and we can only hope for the better.

                            The world didn’t get off scot-free, though. The Avatar Storm continues to savage the Horizon realms, cutting the Traditions and Technocrats alike off from their leadership. Some of those possible fates from that other reality hover over ours like the Sword of Damocles, particularly a Technocratic civil war. As for the rest of the details in the Time of Judgment, such as the Rogue Council and the Panopticon, maybe they’re in your chronicle’s future. According to one
                            school of thought, “Judgment Day is inevitable.” But another says there is no fate but what we make.

                            We’ll leave to you Storytellers the task of merging those timelines. This book is about our reality, today, and all that’s transpired to bring us to this glorious moment.
                            And that really is the x20 Ed thoughts on the Time of Judgement and the end of the world that everything seemed to heading towards. In one world, it did happen but that world isn't the x20 world. The signs and portents of doom, if they happened at all, were but a passing curiosities rather than warnings of what was to come.

                            Hell, W20's opening turned the Prophecy of the Phoenix, the big dreaded prophecy of the Apocalypse from a warning of doom into a call to arms. That this is not the time to give up and swallowed up by despair, that by standing firm and fighting they can, they will be victorious.

                            As others have mentioned, you have to keep a degree of separation between Editions, especially with the x20 Ed and the previous Editions.

                            Beyond that however, most of the Splats have reasons for keeping themselves hidden from humanity. Vampires have always been outnumbered by humans and especially in this modern age, with all the weapons we have, they fear the outcome of the Masquerade falling and humanity going to war with them because despite all their powers man can do a LOT of damage. Hell, the Inquisition back in it's day gave them quite a scare with the damaged they caused hundreds of years ago.

                            Werewolves are held back by the Delirium and the laws of the Litany. Even if they disregard the Litany, they can't go walking around in public in any form other than wolf or human because if ordinary people see them, the vast majority will go into a state of shock or temporary insanity at the sight of them.

                            Mages have the whole Paradox issue restricting them from fully utilizing their Magic safely, both for themselves and others.

                            Wraiths largely exist in a separate world from the living, with limited means of engaging with the living.
