                                                        Originally posted by Saikou View Post

                                                        Or perhaps the Umbra is much more fluid than this.
                                                        In Mage, with Mage alone as the game, it is. It's the only game to actually state that this situation is the order of the day, the odd duck out among the WoD, so if you do say that the Umbra is really fluid and shows up differently to different observers, you're only going by the way that Mage works. Even if you try to say that the Umbra works differently for other monsters, it's still the way that Mage does things above the way that other games figure things.

                                                        It could be the Revised era Umbra is how it is one week, then the next week it shifts. It could be that the VE interpretation of the umbra is completely true so long as you are in a ship travleing around. It could be that the Aetherian Realm only works if you travel across it from the start.

                                                        So why not have WTA be consistent and MTA fluctuate depending on who happens to be in the room at the time? Why create an Either/Or scenario for something which literally defies conventional understanding? Why deny a holistic approach?
                                                        Because it's not the way that the cosmologies of other games work. The Dark Umbra is different from the Middle and the Astral and the Deep, all of which admit entry in very different ways and occupy different spiritual spaces, often with denizens that observe wholly different mechanical systems. That's why The Thousand Hells, which blend together the lands of living and dead spirits, are unique. That's how the other games gain focus and don't step on others' toes. That's how the other games gain particular flavors of their own that make them feel distinct from each other.

                                                        There's no defiance of conventional understanding. While all the games have the makings of occupying parallel universes with only a few vague similarities and loosely shared events, Mage's parallel universe line is just further away from the others than all of the others are from each other, and there's far less blurring together with it and other lines. Moving it closer together would be a rather trying, if not impossible, effort, provided you wanted to keep Mage as-is.
