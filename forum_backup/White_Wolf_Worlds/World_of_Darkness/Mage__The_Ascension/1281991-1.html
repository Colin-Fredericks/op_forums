    Those are all very good arguments for why the Traditions' view of a fundamentally subjective universe, where everything is created by human observation, is suspect. Again, the Technocratic view on the Higgs Boson (and similar phenomena, like the virus and bacteria of germ theory) is that it has always existed, and has only recently been discovered.

    That said, the Traditions' view is that Patterns are real, even if things like the Higgs Field and germs are merely how they manifest in one's chosen Focus. A Progenitor who examines a patient's Life Pattern will find cells at work, including an immune system that fights off various germs (which are their Focus' manifestation of maladies in the Life Pattern); a Hermetic examining the same patient's Life Pattern will encounter an imbalance of Humours causing the malady. Same Pattern, different interpretations.

            Returning to the conflict between "We are exploring and exploiting the fundamental rules of reality" and "We are defining the rules by affecting the consensus:"

            Part of the issue here is that we assume that the former is just a detailed and concrete explanation of the Technocrats' belief in science and the latter is a high level set of goals. Of course those two seem to be in conflict then.

            Instead it is more likely that the Technocrats are exploring and exploiting much more fundamental, complicated, and counterintuitive set of rules behind reality (much like Quantum Mechanics) that changes its behavior based on observations (much like Quantum Mechanics in popular science fiction) and can exhibit multiple different forms of emergent behaviors depending on circumstances. They don't fully understand these Quantum Metamechanics, but believe they are understandable. Furthermore they do think they can change the way they manifest in the world by changing how they are observed (that is, what the consensus expects).

                The Syndicate, first and foremost, believe in the power of Dosh. More specifically, in the idea that "you get what you pay for". Hypertech designed and built by someone else works just fine for the Syndic, so long as they paid top dollar for it and have confidence that the money was well spent. And the Syndicate bankrolls the whole Technocratic Union, so they KNOW the tech is legit. As such, many of the tools available to other Technocrats can be placed in the Syndic's Enlightened portfolio, provided they take the time to learn how to operate them. (When you pay as much as the Syndicate does for tech, you don't walk into it blind).

                I mean, it's not like every NWO operative is an expert in science, either. Yet their agents can use gadgets just fine. Only part of the New World Order is a member of Q Division. It's presumed the agents just pay close attention when said Q Division personnel explain how the gadgets are used. Their confidence in the efficacy of Union technology bridges the gap from there. Why should the Syndicate be any different?
                Every Technocrat in the New World Order is an expert in regard to psychology and/or sociology, whether it is in practice, theory, or both. The NWO is a Convention to a large extent relying on their almost wholly Coincidental Mind Procedures. These are researched and developed with techniques and in conditions that could be considered to be equivalent to Hypertech ; certainly it is Enlightened Science.

                By contrast, the Syndicate has this Order of Reason – esque expertise as Foci, as well as borrowing technology and theories from other Conventions. Other than Hypereconomics, they lack Foci that could be called their own.
                I think the direction and presentation of the Enlightened Science Effects for the Syndicate is decidedly worse than this of the other Conventions.

                    I'll certainly admit that it is often harder to think up what the Syndicate are up to than any other convention, however I would hesitate before I'd turn all of the Mind effects over to the NWO. The Syndicate is the master of human systems. Not necessary psychology (though certainly some of that) or power structures, but of how the complex interactions of people work together in unexpected ways. I would also throw Time effects like Asimov's Psychohistory (mathematically predicting the general course of future history) and Entropy effects that cascade through chains of people firmly in their wheelhouse. For related reasons manipulation of chaotic systems (any system where small changes to the initial conditions can have dramatic changes on the outcome) like weather and traffic also fits with them.

                    For the more physical systems, they are very infrastructure based. Much like the VAs their tech often piggybacks onto mundane tech and they are the faction I think of for effects like sending objects through the phone lines (well they were in the 90s anyways, now it's cell networks). They are also the faction I think of for seeding mundane technology with extras that can be activated by those who know how. That not only gives them more opportunity for using their subtle hypertech, it also gives them more opportunities for unconscious casting.
