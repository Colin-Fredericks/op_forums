    Four levels of Paradigm

    03-29-2019, 07:03 PM
    The following may be obvious, but I don't think it gets explicitly talked about this way very often. Too much emphasis tends to be placed on Tradition Paradigm or Tradition subgroup paradigm.

    It helps to talk about Paradigm as kind of step pyramid with layers of increasing specificity. A lot of the following only applies to Tradition mages.

    At the base level is Purple Paradigm. Some sense that magic is partly based on belief is necessary for Tradition Mages to cooperate without being shaken to their core every time they see their comrades do magic.

    Next is Tradition Paradigm. Traditions are broader than they tend to be depicted; they're not single cultures, but agglomerations of different cultures and practices with similar philosophy who are allied out of convenience because of the Technocratic threat. You can see this most easily with the Hermetics and Dreamspeakers, but it is true for all of them. Yet, there tends to be unifying themes and stylistic influences that most of their members will follow.

    Within Traditions are the subgroups and individual chantries. A Mage will probably identify more with their House or tribe or coven or secret fraternal organization than with their Tradition. The sub-group will probably inform their Paradigm far more as well.

    Finally, all magic is ultimately individual. In modern individualistic societies, no two mages will do magic alike. This is very important to explain to new players.

                      That... depends entirely on a lot of other factors, depending on edition and which books and items you allow into play.

                      For example, with the Resonance system (from Revised and M20, but pretty backwards compatible, IMO), your ability to work certain magics will change as you gain Resonance. And one way to gain Resonance is through a Seeking getting intense - in other words, going up in Arete can change how your magic is flavored. Now, that itself doesn't directly change your worldview, but it can subtly affect it, especially when using sensory powers - if you suddenly gain a trait of "Electric" resonance, you are going to find it easier to sense things that could fit as "electric" - from actual electricity with Forces to an "electric" emotional connection between two lovers or potential lovers with Mind, Spirit, or Time (in the case of potential lovers, seeing the future) - and less easy to sense things that could be considered the opposite of electric - resistant materials such as rubber, stagnant romances, and so on. Depending on how far you and your ST choose to take the effects, that can slowly change your world-view in certain ways. That may not change your full, fundamental beliefs, but it can change little beliefs, which in turn can shift bigger ones as they filter your perception.

                      Also, there's a merit in "Masters of the Art" that presents an alternative view. "Ascension Message" is a five dot merit that says that you'll never become an Archmage. Ever. You can have Arete 10 and will never raise any sphere above 5. Why is this a merit? Because the view of Archmastery presented in the book is one of shortcomings - becoming and Archmage can hamper your ability to interact with the mortal world, and can hold back actual understanding that leads to Ascension. You become so focused on your power that you hamper your ability to actually go beyond it - you are trapped in the limits of your paradigm, cannot interact with the Consensus without risking Paradox by existing, and thus have a harder time leading the world to Ascension. However, an Archmage who realizes this can program their Avatar to avoid learning those higher levels of power. Instead, as your Arete increases beyond five, you find yourself able to incorporate alternative paradigms into your magic. The exact mechanical effects aren't defined, but this likely is similar to merits like Techgnosi (Revised Chorus TradBook), Dual Tradition, and similar sources, where you develop additional tools that you can use, break out of the limits your paradigm might have on certain types of magic more easily, and possibly develop other benefits that might come from having the ability to reconcile multiple worldviews (if nothing else, it makes you a killer diplomat).

                      Of course, you may not include that merit in your game or may not have even been aware of it, or you may just never have liked it and leave it out, and that will change your view on magic and paradigms and how they work, but it is an option, and as such someone who is familiar with it and especially has played with it in the past may use that as the basis for their own paradigm understanding - that enlightenment and skill can and should change your view of how the world works and thus change your magical viewpoints and abilities. Your game may run as you wish it to, but it is important to realize that deeper enlightenment CAN lead to a change in paradigm per mechanics that exist in the game as a whole (if not the modern version).

                      I also disagree that M20 changed paradigm from "an all-encompassing worldview to a few convenient soundbites" - or rather, they did, but they didn't. They changed the order of terms, turning Paradigms into the conceptual bases upon which your Focus is now built, and using the term Focus where Paradigm was used before. In my read, the game changed which words were used for which things rather than changing the system, and added in the added element of "pick a few of these phrases that help define your worldview" along with tools and practices and tradition. The same concept still exists, it's just that different words refer to different things. Again, YMMV and I may have missed what you specifically were criticizing, but the change in terms did come up elsewhere in another thread and I do feel it's important to lay that out and make sure everyone's on the same page so we don't miss each other's points.

                      -

                      I will disagree on the exact order of the pyramid myself in my mind. I do think Purple Paradigm (never heard that before but I like it) is a sort of base of the philosophy of Mage magick, but I think that your character's circumstances may be an important factor. A character who is taken in quickly by an Tradition or (more likely) Convention may find their own viewpoint warped or conditioned to match or jive with that group - the Traditions may be more willing to set you loose and put you with someone else if they can if your existing view is too alien to their practices, but if you are isolated or brought in by less scrupulous traditionalists, you may not get as much freedom and may find yourself forced into their view. Likewise, the Technocrats are likely to try and pair you with a Convention that fits your existing world view most closely, but there will most likely be some conditioning and shaping of beliefs. That said, I do think personal paradigm does need more time to shine.

                              I always see the Paradigm <> Arete relation being able to go both ways.
                              It highly depends on the characer in question, but the way a Mage can grow in Arete to me is either what Enginseer mentioned - namely the Mage more and more letting go of of the rigidity of their paradigm as they learn more about reality, becoming more flexible and loose and also dropping instruments that way...gaining a more universal flexiblity and understanding. A Mage is though never without paradigm - they always believe in *something*.

                              OR the Mage goes the opposite way:
                              They become more and more involved in the intricacies of their very Paradigm, and instead of letting go of parts of it they make it more elaborate, more intricate, more involved. Essentially instead of relaxing their Paradigm to encompass the new things they learn, they go the opposite direction and build up more and more paradigmic structures to make more and more things work in the confines of their paradigm.

                              Whatever form their paradigm takes shape, they are such an icon of it, they need the handholding less and less.

                              Two directly opposing paths, two completely different direction or mindsets, but both can be a mirror of raising Arete and dropping the tools. The only Mages that don't grow in Arete and flexibility are those that stand still and don't change in any direction. That is the key. A lack of evolution in any way. A lack of growth, no matter what shape that growth takes.

                                      What I like about M20 is that it allows for both approaches. Sure, it favors the “embrace your truth!” approach that is in many ways antithetical to the earlier editions' “Let it go!” approach; but with the inclusion of the “We Are Gods In Disguise” Paradigm in Book of Secrets, you get to nestle the old approach inside the newer approach: “We Are Gods In Disguise” is the Purple Paradigm with a different name; and as you embrace that Paradigm, everything else falls by the wayside.

                                      All that said, my personal head canon is that “We Are Gods In Disguise” was an innovation of the Traditions, is the glue that holds the Traditions together, and outside of the Traditions is most often found among Orphans, the Hollow Ones (who until recently were affiliated with the Traditions), and the Batini (who helped develop it back when they helped found the Traditions): other Disparates have a tendency to view their own faction's philosophy as paramount, and the Technocracy treats “We Are Gods In Disguise” as antithetical to their own theories. Put another way, Faradn's proposed pyramid only really exists for the Traditions and their allies/expatriates; the Disparates in general top out their pyramids at the faction level, and the Technocracy has its own alternative to the Purple Paradigm capping their pyramid.

                                      And that's my other problem with the earlier editions' approach: by making the Purple Paradigm a fundamental truth of the setting, they made it impossible to play nearly anyone who wasn't affiliated with the Traditions in some way without them either being liars, ignorant, or both. This especially applied to the Union, but wasn't unique to them. Which is fine if you're going with “Traditions good; Technocracy bad; and everyone else is dumb” in your campaign. But if you want anything more nuanced than that, you can't have the Purple Paradigm as an incontrovertible truth of the setting.

                                      Which is why I like the fact that it's “just another Paradigm” in M20 (though given its significance to the Traditions, I really wish it had been featured in the core book rather than the companion book). It goes along with M20's dethroning the Traditions as the protagonists of the game, and allows for other factions, or even independent mages, to be put front and center. Ironically, the game is more flexible this way.

                                          Also, one of my hacks is to take the technomancer/mystic divide and reformulate it as an external/internal divide, depending on whether the mage believes that what he can do comes from within or without. “We Are Gods in Disguise” pretty much requires an Internal Focus, and the Technocracy’s counterpart similarly requires an External Focus, which is why the division is generally conceived in terms of mysticism vs. technomancy (as the majority of the Traditions have mystical paradigms and nearly all of the Union has technomantic paradigms). But for the purpose of this thread, the important part of that hack is how it addresses Instruments: if you have an Internal Focus, you’re able to discard your Instruments as your Arete increases; if you have an External Focus, you instead come to master your Instruments: you can never work your will without them (as they’re what connects you to whatever the source of your power is), but you get especially adept at wielding them as your Arete improves, letting you get more out of them than an Internalist could ever hope to achieve.

                                          Being a hack, it’s not Rules as Written; but it’s very much in keeping with the view that I outlined in my last post, and I do think it’s largely in keeping with M20’s overall philosophy of Magick.

The underworld? A hell that traps souls that are still bound to our world? The gateway to oblivion? A shadow of our world, full of post-life-echoes of living people? What's important in Mage is that the strict scientist who discovers the Umbra for the first time can, once they get over the denial, incorporate it into their own ideas and come up with an argument that no one can falsify, and works for them. "Well these look like ghosts, but that's absurd. They're just psionic imprints left by particularly distraught and emotional people before their death."
