                                                        That last caveat is key. Also key is the question of who gets to vote. Mage posits the notion that beings with Avatars have the ability to Willwork, and that beings without Avatars don't. And with the possible exception of the Celestial Chorus' Pure Ones and the Taftâni's Djinn, Mage further posits that only humans have Avatars. That means that vampires, werewolves, spirits, and ghosts don't get a vote in the Consensus. Sure, you could argue that there are entities with Avatar-like reality-warping abilities who serve as “sponsors” for these beings otherwise have no say in the Consensus; but as soon as you say that, you're redefining what the Consensus is generally understood to mean: it's longer the collective Willworking of humanity's Sleeper population, but rather it's that plus the X-factor of “the Will of God” or whatever term you wish to apply to the inhuman entities who cause reality to frequently deviate from what the collective beliefs of humanity say it is. And since this X-factor is essentially unknowable, “the Will of God” is largely indistinguishable from “Cosmological Constants”. Do spirits exist as they do because the Triat believes in them, or is it because they're an intrinsic part of reality that can't be expunged by human disbelief (as opposed to the Bygones)? Who knows? What we do know is that spirits aren't subject to the “I don't believe in fairies” phenomenon the way that dragons and unicorns are.

                                                        Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
                                                        Now there are two things I put outside the reach of Consensual Reality. The first, is Consensual Reality itself. People don't believe they can alter reality with belief, so if they could take that power away from themselves, they would have by now. They also can't change who affects the Consensus, or write them out of existence. The second is morality. Not in the sense that morality is objective, but in the sense that mages should never know if it is or not. The question of whether might makes right is more powerful if you can't answer it for certain.

                                                        The door is wide open for other constants, of course, but it is surprising how far you can get with just those two. The revisionist history angle, for instance, doesn't come up. The Consensus can no more make the holocaust disappear than it can make, say, Mother Theresa into an axe murderer. Changing the past would involve the Consensus writing people out of the Consensus or overriding their will.
                                                        Given that the current population of the world dwarfs the entire accumulated population of the world from the dawn of time to, what, the 19th century? you again need something more involved than “majority rules” to keep the present Consensus from overriding the past. Admittedly, the Holocaust is more recent than that; but then, it wasn't until the end of WWII that it became public knowledge; so the population that you need to override to get rid of it is limited to a little more than the past seven decades of humanity. A tough hurdle, but not necessarily an insurmountable one if you can extend the population explosion (say, by colonizing Space). The point is that you need something more involved than “reality is subjective” to keep the promotion of Holocaust Denial from being a viable tactic for retroactively eliminating the Holocaust.

                                                        Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
                                                        And yes, there will still be some oddities. Sometimes a consensual reality works in counterintuitive or unpleasant ways, but that doesn't mean something has gone wrong. Mage is a game where quack remedies like blood letting and mercury used to work; where we can root for the crazy wizard over the modern scientist. Things we would decry in this world can be positive courses of action in game. There are ways of handling Climate change so that it resists change, it might be a widespread paradox or a hypernarrative. But if convincing the populace that Climate Change isn't happening makes it stops, that also works. Mage isn't our world, it's supposed to be stranger.
                                                        Paradox is another interesting factor. If reality is defined by the Consensus, then Paradox is, too. So in a world that's purely subjective, nothing accepted by the Consensus can cause Paradox. OTOH, if it's possible for the Consensus to be wrong, then it's entirely possible for something that's part of the Consensus to cause Paradox.

                                                        I'll grant the Hypernarratives thing, with the caveat that both Hypernarratives and Mythic Threads can only exist in a Consensus where minority views can also potentially influence reality. Which, again, means that the Consensus can be wrong (in the sense that it can produce inconsistent results such that what the majority believes doesn't always match what actually happens).
