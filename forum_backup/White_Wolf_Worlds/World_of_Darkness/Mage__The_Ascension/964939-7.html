                                    Some of the problems I'd foresee coming up that I haven't really seen mentioned also heavily depend on which splat would be favored. Outside of directly Fire and Sunlight related effects, are most other magiks going to be very effective against Cainites? From a Mage perspective, "Lightningbolts" might deal Aggravated Damage, but from the Vampire perspective they probably deal very minimal Lethal damage. Just as an example. Even dropping a building on them supercharged with Magik is probably going to have a very different effect on a Cainite depending on if you favor Mage or Vampire for how to handle it.

                                    I'd also argue, that if Mages are immune to Paradox, lets also just go ahead and assume that all Cainites are immune to Sunlight. That's pretty close to fair. Just saying.
                                    It's also one of those very odd things, where it really depends on which game you favor, as in the VtM perspective, most Vampires DO actually cause Paradox backlash for Mages, but there are a few notable exceptions, like Malkavians due to their insane and chaotic nature, and the Tremere who have an understanding of true Magik. Anyway, from this point onwards, we are all going to assume that either Mages do have to deal with Paradox as normal or Vampires are just outright immune to Sunlight, and we will leave the question as to if Vampires can cause Paradox for Mages up in the air for if you favor Mage or Vampire.

                                    There seems to be a few odd assumptions made that I'm not sure are accurate. For example that Vampires can't access space, other realms, or much outside of the planet earth or the physical world. That's not very true. It's less important to VtM to go into much detail about the Astral Plane, the Umbra(s), etc. . . just because to most VtM games, it's not that important. Vampire doesn't revolve around that much. However, Auspex, Necromancy, Protean all allow for access to the other realms, which are generally speaking, all connected, and are not terribly uncommon powers. Thaumaturgy as well.

                                    We also seem to assume that Mages somehow get free access to things like Nukes, but Vampires do not, which doesn't seem to fly. For example, Marcus Vitel basically infiltrated Washing DC for the exact purpose of getting access and control of the US's Nukes. The Mages might have a nasty surprise coming their way when they try to pull that card, only to find out that much of their arsenal isn't actually at their disposal after al, but is instead going to be used against them, or if we assume that both Mages and Vampires have influence and "control" of the key world leaders, that many of those weapons are suddenly, unforeseeably, taken off the table completely for BOTH sides.

                                    On the idea of creating Rituals/Paths for AntiMage things, I'd argue that both sides are partially correct, and at best it really does come down to splat favoritism. It is possible to create a new Path and Rituals for basically anything. However, doing so is NEVER an on the spot thing. Even in the new Anarchs Unleashed book, it talks about how Punk Magic, (sort of an improvised variety aimed at specifically trying to do on the spot effects) is very difficult, limited, and a time consuming process up front. The fact that the Tremere did so in a lot of their backstory is just one of those things that doesn't make any sense and we just have to handwave logic under the rung because the devs wanted to have the Tremere in the game no matter what.. However, all that being said, we can probably also reasonably assume that the Tremere specifically went out of their way to develop some, at least basic, AnitMage Thaumaturgy as their first real battle was against other Mages as they tried to establish themselves, and as cunning right bastards, it makes no sense whatsoever that they would have left themselves o vulnerable against probably the single on threat they actually knew about in detail.

                                    Ravnos' death was literally a plot device. He (she) had just woken up, and fighting during the daytime, SHOULD have been extremely limited in Dice Pools, but still manages to nearly survive going head to head with a Werewolf Pack, Wraiths, KotE elders, and Mages, at the same time or one after another. At best, we have an example of some rules being ignored in favor of telling a story and making a point, but the issue(s) is that the different points it makes say some very different things. Ravnos did have Fortitude as a Clan Discipline, and (if we assume) that means it was maxed out to 10 dots (or more???), it makes it, at least on the surface feasible that it took all of that to BARELY put him down. Except that well, he shouldn't have had access to his full power for multiple reasons, (fighting during the day handicaps Vampires to the Path Rating, which was unlikely at 10 and probably closer to 1-3, he was already weak from not feeding and just waking up, and confused and disorientated). End result, the Story overrules EVERYTHING else, but since Antis and Oracles are not in this, it really doesn't matter.

                                    Then we come to the issue of if Counter Magic even works on most Disciplines, and secondarily if Mage's other intrinsic ability to alter reality off screen affects Vampires at all. This can also lead to the overarching issue if Mage's, ANY MAGE, can override "God". In Vampire unquestionably no. In Mage, arguably the entire point. Just for the sake of argument here, by "God" I'm including both the J-C and Noddist version as well as the basic fundamental principles of the universe that Mages have not universally altered such as gravity. Once again, it's entirerly based upon which game line the ST favors over the other.

                                    There also seems to be a strong implication, or even taken as fact, that Mages know all, and are fully prepared, while Cainites are not. I'm not sure that that is very accurate. While in general, Cainites are probably going to be less specifically designed to fight a war against Mages than the reverse, that would probably only matter very significantly if one group was attacking the other from surprise. Since the basic assumption seems to be that both sides are pretty aware of the theoretical war, that Vampires would also start pulling out all the stop, which leads to a bit of a problem for Mages, even if we assume that both groups have exerted a great deal of influence across the mortal/mundane world, politics, technology, etc. . . and that is that Vampires probably have a hell of a lot more personal experience with fighting/surviving Mages and other creatures than Mages do Vampires. As immortals, they just have a lot more experience they can call upon, even if it's less directly applicable, it should have a lot of crossover potential. There is also the fact that things like the Inquisition didn't just target Vampires, and at least according to VtM was actually much, much more of a threat towards Mages and Sorcerers (and innocents) that it was against Vampires. There is also the fact that even the Technocracy really has minimal "true" information about Vampires (and any other Supernatural being), more studying the vampire from the Mage's Antagonist perspective (a watered down and inaccurate version) rather than actual Cainites. This is also true for Vampires, though, but I'd think that it's much less of a hindrance for VtM than it is for MtA.

                                    And finally, one last assumption I'm not sure is very good, is that it's not Mages vs the Tremere, but rather that there are also a lot of other Clans out there too who might have all sorts of nifty powers and tactics that might screw with Mages. An earthmelded Gangrel shouldn't be detectable by Mages at all (or at least not by most methods they would think to use), and many Mages might have an issue doing anything to a cloud of fog. Fortitude is also a pretty common Discipline, but is also one that is 1.) always on and 2.) pretty all inclusive. Technology, even boosted by Magick probably doesn't do a thing against Obfuscate, which doesn't turn you invisible, and isn't an illusion, but rather directly affects the minds of everyone, even after the fact to simply not accept that the user was even there. Mage's probably have little defense against a small group of Nosferatu that just walk in undetected, call on a small swarm of crows, and then unleash their Beast on a Mage who should then loose all access to magick and rational thought, and probably go freaking crazy and attack anything near it that moves.

                                    So, with all that in mind, I'm more inclined to say more likely it would go to Mages, but I wouldn't count Cainites out so quickly unless you continue to just favor Mage's rules, themes, and truths over Vampire's and everyone else's (seeing a trend here on that account), and then it's really not even a question. If, on the other hand, it is more of a unbiased thought exercise, Vampires might surprise you with the long game, that thing they excel at. There is also the little fact that you literally have to exterminate every single Vampire in existence for Mages to truly win, because even one Vampire can repopulate the earth, and if we also assume that Caine and the 3rd Gen stay out, that does mean right off the bat Mages automatically fail at achieving overall victory and just a stalemate.
