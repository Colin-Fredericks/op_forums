                        Going through Imperial Mysteries in a little more detail:

                        First, becoming an Archmaster isn't as simple as buying a sixth dot in an Arcanum. While you must have achieved Mastery in at least one Arcanum first and must have enough Gnosis to technically be allowed to purchase the sixth dot, actually gaining the sixth dot requires what's called a Threshold Seeking, which is compared to a Second Awakening — only this time, it's something the mage initiates rather than being something that happens to him. That is, you need the Storyteller's permission to transition from Mage to Archmaster; but the player must ask for it.

                        In Awakening terminology, the new Archmaster replaces the Watchtower he Awakened to with a personal one of his own devising. A Watchtower in Awakening serves a similar role to an Avatar in Ascension; and I personally would liken this Threshold Seeking to replacing the Avatar's Essence with something unique to the Archmaster that has the potential to be expanded into the Infinite Essence. This, in fact, would be the goal of Archmastery, something that ordinary mages only whisper about and don't even have the slightest clue as to where to begin.

                        The Threshold Seeking doubles as the first act of Imperial Magic performed by the new Archmaster; and as an act of Imperial Magic, it requires a Quintessence to complete. Once we've hammered out what the Ascension equivalent to what Imperial Mysteries calls “a Quintessence” is, we'll know how this part of Imperial Mysteries would best be adapted to Ascension, assuming we want to adapt it.

                        Upon completing the Threshold Seeking, the mage gains a sixth dot in an Arcanum and replaces his Watchtower with a Cintimani. Translating to Ascension terminology, the mage gains a sixth dot in a Sphere, and replaces his Avatar Essence with a custom Essence, which he will subsequently flesh out as his Arete increases until he Ascends and it becomes the Infinite Essence.


                        The first application of the power of Archmastery has to do with “spell factors” — a system that Awakening developed out of MRev's “dividing successes” mechanic, and which resembles the Aspects used in SRev and M20 Sorcerer. It suggests paying an Arcane Experience in order to enhance the effectiveness of the spell. In an effort to Keep Things Simple, I would suggest a simple mapping of how much successes achieve: if an Archmaster pays whatever cost we choose to replace Arcane Experience, one success becomes a Standard Feat instead of a Simple Feat; two successes become a Mighty Feat (normally requiring 5 successes); and every success after that kicks it up the equivalent of another five successes, to Overwhelming Feats for three or four successes, and Godlike Feats for five or more successes. This is extremely powerful, which is why it makes sense for the cost involved to be comparable to spending experience points — a character resource that's very precious to the player and which the player will think twice about spending frivolously. It might be something like the way the Talisman creation rules require a mage to sacrifice a dot of Willpower to complete the act — a dot which then needs to be repurchased with Experience Points, making it an indirect XP sink.

                        Note that there's no mention of Quintessences here: spending Arcane Experiences to enhance Feats doesn't require that additional hoop to be jumped through.


                        The second application of Archmastery is the Imperial Practices: namely, what the sixth through ninth dot in an Arcanum lets you do that the fifth dot doesn't. I briefly outlined these before. Here, I'll lean more into what the ground rules are. First, using an Imperial Practice is never an instant action; it must be done as a Ritual. This is where “the Quintessence” comes in, in that Ritual casting in Awakening normally requires a sacrament; for Imperial Practices, this gets replaced by a Quintessence:

                        [quote]A Quintessence can be an object, place, person, organization, event or phenomenon. Quintessences do not obey any strict patterns, but archmasters have divined some loose guidelines:
                        • The Quintessence for a spell symbolizes how the spell feels to the archmaster casting it. It is the Mystery that allows the Road to be used, not a simple “spell component.”
                        • Quintessence for a spell usually serves as a metaphor or symbolic reference to the effect or target of the spell, especially if the spell is cast at sympathetic range.
                        • Quintessences often “balance” the effect sought, not in moral terms but in the symbolic language of the Supernal. Returning someone to life must be paid for with death. Forging a wonder often requires the destruction of another.
                        • A Quintessence is a unique Mystery — anything easily come by to an archmage’s resources is unsuitable. Impossible situations and items are often used.
                        • A Quintessence may be manufactured or caused by magic. In fact, much of an archmaster’s time in the Fallen World is spent arranging for future needs.
                        • The Quintessence is not usually destroyed when the spell is cast, though many are temporary in nature.
                        • The Quintessence is often colored by the archmage’s own attitudes, especially her entente loyalties and Noumenon.​
                        The developer has described the Quintessence as a McGuffin to be used as a story seed and to give the player something to do while the Storyteller works out the implications of what the Archmaster is trying to achieve and how to address them as the game moves forward.

                        Second, an Imperial Practice can never be turned into a Rote: every invocation of an Imperial Practice is a unique action, an improv that can never be repeated. If you use any sort of rules about formalizing particular applications of Magick into something that can be taught or learned, those rules don't apply to what dots 6+ let you do.

                        Third, an Imperial Practice, like all Imperial Workings, costs Arcane Experience. And not just one, as with the aforementioned Imperial Spell Factors: as the number of dots in the Arcanum goes up, so does the Experience Cost This is justified in part because the things that Imperial Magic does aren't temporary; and performing an Imperial Practice is akin to buying a Merit or Background.

                        My initial thought is to say that the mechanics of performing the Ascension equivalent of an Imperial Practice should be modeled on its rules for crafting a Talisman. Only, the thing you're crafting is considerably more extensive:
                        • I would move the ability to craft a Hedge Path to the sixth dot of Prime, on the basis that once a Path exists, it exists globally: it isn't limited to a particular person, place, or thing the way a Wonder would be.
                        • The Imperial Practice of Dynamics is an exception to the permanence of Imperial Practices; instead, you craft an Effect that takes on a life of its own: “This Practice allows archmages to create phenomena that change according to conditions — chain reactions that follow basic principles in a fractal manner without the caster’s
                          conscious direction or complex “programmed” effects. … Dynamics can create spells that vary in effect depending on the person encountering them, spells that can repair their potency when degraded, spells that react to conditions, expanding viral effects that change Imago as they spread, and spells that would be beyond the archmaster’s ability to conceptualize the Imago for but use Dynamic properties to include all the permutations of a single cause or effect. Dynamic spells are often only loosely controlled by their casters, if at all. … An archmaster casting a Dynamic spell can determine general principles and leave the spell to itself by relinquishing spell control.”                           An example given is using a Time Effect to alter the past; but instead of arranging for a particular change and then guessing what the Butterfly Effect will do to it in the present, the Archmaster specifies what he wants the change to the present to look like, and leaves it to the Time Effect to work out how best to get there.
                        • ​The Imperial Practice of Entities lets you target a person or creature and alter its fundamental nature. This goes beyond granting the Enhancement Background to the subject; it lets you transform the subject into, say, one of the Kindred. Not “something that resembles the Kindred”; an actual vampire who operates exactly like a character from Vampire would. Or you could create a Werewolf, a Mage, a Changeling, or anything else, even a custom supernatural type of your own making. Its counterpart Practice, Excision, would let you remove any of these, curing the vampiric curse and reverting the subject back to a normal human.
                        • The Imperial Practice of Dominions lets you create Otherworlds of your own design, or alter existing ones.
                        • The Imperial Practice of Transfiguration is also an exception to the permanent role of thumb I mentioned: it's basically a fancy way of saying "and anything else you can think of.” If you come up with an idea for an Effect that doesn't fit into what any of the lower levels allow, Transfiguration allows it.
                        The equivalent to acquiring “a Quintessence” would be how Talisman creation requires you to incorporate “Primal Energy” into the Talisman in a Paradigm-appropriate way. Especially at Archmaster levels. You can't just use any Quintessence; it needs to be Quintessence in a form that's appropriate for whatever you're building. I might also require an XP cost or something equivalent (e.g., sacrificing character traits such as Willpower).

                        This is getting rather lengthy, so I'll address the Appendix (“Imperium”) in a later post. I'll note only that the Appendix is the story generator of the book.

                        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                            The Appendix, in a nutshell: define a Noumenon, a.k.a. the vision of Ascension that you seek, and define three Omens for it, establishing goals that need to be achieved: a Personal Omen (representing a change in either your life or the life of someone you care deeply about); and Earthly Omen (representing a change to life on Earth); and a Celestial Omen (representing a change to the Otherworlds), plus the possibility of one or more Profane Omens (additional goals unrelated to Ascension which must be completed first).

                            Each Omen is rated on a five dot scale based on how hard they are to achieve, from Masterful to Epic to Legendary to Godlike to Supreme. For each, design a story lasting at least one chapter and culminating in a challenge requiring you to earn a large number of successes, from 6 (for Masterful challenges) to 10 (for Supreme challenges), with intermediate levels being Epic, Legendary, and God-like. You can reduce the number of successes needed by employing a number of strategies such as splitting the challenges into multiple simpler challenges, employing non-Archmaster agents to complete the stories for you, or employing mortals using non-supernatural means to achieve your goals. It also suggests the Storyteller Adventure System (a tool that Onyx Path developed to help provide structure to story creation) as a way to aid in creating these story arcs.

                            If the culminating challenge fails, the Archmaster can search for a replacement challenge; but the replacement has its challenge rating bumped up by one. On a success, the corresponding Omen is sealed, and the Archmaster begins a bidding war in the Supernal World to Exalt the changes, granting them “eternal, universal truth”. For instance, an Omen of “destroy all vampires” might involve a story that might involve destroying every vampire in a particular region; but once that's accomplished, the Archmaster can Exalt it, and suddenly the world as a whole changes so that vampires do not exist, and never have. (“Destroy all Witches, in this world and every other, past, present, and future.”) Exalting an Omen is retroactive and universal.

                            Exalting an Omen is a bidding war between the Archmaster and his allies on the one hand, and those who oppose the Omen on the other hand. The “bargaining chips” in this bidding war are earned through the performance of Imperial Practices, after which careful application of the accumulated resources determines, without dice, whether or not the Omen is Exalted. Part of the challenge here is that you can come up short, but you can also overshoot your goal. Both are bad, leading to unintended consequences. If you're getting too close to succeeding, an enemy can disrupt your effort by pushing you to go too far instead of merely trying to restrain you.

                            Once every Omen has been Sealed and Exalted, the Archmaster Ascends.

                                In the Ascension version of this, the stories for Earthly Omens should frequently revolve around Reality Zone Shifts/the Terranorming process.                                     2. A character accomplished a Great Ambition (in line with a Seeking) (1);
                                    3. An important Flaw from a character or in the plan seriously risked the entire mission, but was overcome through sacrifice or growth (1);
                                    4. The group recorded a Key Knowledge about Magic, learning something about the cosmos, the nature of True Magic or the Effect (1);
                                    5. The mission was a partial/full success (1/2);
                                    6. The whole group survived (1).

                                    So at the end of a story, the ST judges how it went and awards [Arcane XP] accordingly. It's tallied against the goal (25) until it's met. This way what's being measured as cost isn't a fixed quantity of resources, but the quality of a story told to gather those resources.

                                    Our player may decide (throwing here arbitrary numbers just as example) the Effect will cost 5 Nodes (1 Arch Point), 50 Quintessence in Tass (2 Arch Points), 25 Quint from Avatars (2 Arch Points), 100 fully Nightfolk [Backers] (3 Arch Points), 10 Sleeper Witnesses (2 Arch Points), on top of 25 [Arcane XP] (5 Arch Points). All of those are story drivers in that the Archmage must plan something to gather such resources, but the Arcane XP is freeform as they plan a number of missions related to the Archspell that are directly judged by story beats.

                                    We can have Witnesses still as in "witnesses to Vulgar Magic aligned to Archspell Resonance" as one possible element in the currency chart, which would be part of the Paradox of the final Archspell being broken into several obligatory Paradox Events. Or maybe have a similar chart to the above for the Archmage to decide how they'll manage the obscene brunt of Paradox of an Archspell.

                                    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                    Going through Imperial Mysteries in a little more detail:
                                    That's really useful, I'll parse my observations on it, and how I think we could translate or not into Ascension.

                                    I'll cover my take on Quintessence later, but the Threshold Seeking may need a more specific guidance on how to run it than regular Seekings (which are already a confusing mess).

                                    About the Avatar, I wouldn't go for the Infinite Essence. Instead, I'd ditch Essences altogether for an Arch-Avatar Archetype, which's basically any layer of abstract meaning the player wants to put in it. It can be a Major Arcana, a Jungian Archetype, a TV Trope, anything.

                                    Drawing on the idea from Ascension that not only Archmastery isn't Ascension, but it may actually run counter it, I'm thinking about this Threshold Seeking being kind of blasphemous: it's using Magic to impose your Will over your very Avatar. It's not literally and or anything, but it's something that fundamentally removes the original role of the Avatar as a guide to Ascend and turns it into an expression of your Flawed self, for whatever that means.

                                    Going for Archetypes makes it both personal and customizable and easier to grasp, as Mage is already excessively abstract. Archetypes are ready-to-digest abstractions players can plug and play right away yet can elaborate infinitely later. We can then take a page from Spirit Rank in CoD and have the Avatar accumulate a new Archetype for each Arete dot above 5, turning more complex.

                                    Calling this Seeking/Magic would come either with buying a dot of Arete above 5 (triggering a mixed Seeking) or buying a Sphere dot above 5 (triggering only this Seeking). Unless we decide Spheres above 5 don't follow usual cost, which isn't something I'm against).

                                    I used Arcane XP as a placeholder for a reason: what it accomplishes in Awakening that I find interesting is it taking a similar kind of effort as regular XP, but is tracked in parallel and gained through additional opportunities rather than using your regular XP.

                                    For this system, that means spending XP won't put you behind the others as cost to get things done, which is an old pet peeve of mine. I never liked dot costs, by the way. Won't oppose if you insist but the above explanation on [Arcane XP] is my vote for how to systemize this.

                                    What I'd add: [Arcane XP] accrued beforehand is halved for use. I'm not particularly strong on this limitation, I just think adventures made as part of the quest to get an Archspell done are more interesting than those undertaken just to fill stocks.

                                    I'd simplify Spell Factors further: spend [Arcane XP] to multiply successes. It can be Quadratic (Success × 2^N), Geometric (Success × 1+N) or Geometric With a Toll (Success × N). N, of course, is the amount of [Arcane XP] used. We can put a limit equal Arete, Highest Sphere or Lowest Sphere. I'd use Quadratic with limit per Lowest Sphere, but require the use of Dividing Successes when doing so.

                                    Quintessence in Imperial Mysteries terms isn't a hard concept because Ascension already has the language to describe the idea: Resonance. It's basically every aspect of what the game always tried to explain Resonance as being, minus Synergy.

                                    My current definition for it: [Core Components] for [Archspells] are Unique Tass or other Quintessence repository that must fit the casting and Paradigm perfectly. Both the Effect and the Tass must share Resonance, and this must fit or bridge somehow the Resonances of the Mage, the target and the goal in some way. Player and ST are free to explore how it works, as long as the result seems logical and add to the themes of the chronicle.

                                    Tass already can take the other forms cited in the description provided, the only difference is that in Ascension it must carry extra Quint, but I think that's a fitting element for translation, it isn't by itself as inherently strange as it is in Awakening with Mana, and it's doable in many ways. We can work the wording of all options better, but the gist of it is the same.

                                    Imperial Practices become [Archspells], which are the core of our goal here. I think most of the components and assumptions work well:

                                    1. Always ritual, thoroughly agree. You already have [Success Multiplier] as an option that works with improvised casting, [Archspells] not.

                                    2. Always require a [Core Component], we may need a system to define how much Quint this Tass must have, just to have a reference of how hard it is to make. Quintessence = Target Successes is probably a good starting point.

                                    3. No Rote I agree with a caveat: I think one possible application of an [Archspell] could be the creation of a unique Rote that allows other Mages "merely" at Master level to perform that specific [Archspell], at least while they have the support of the Archmage or some setup they prepared.

                                    My model for this is Mummy and the Spell of Life. Isis performed the original [Archspell], but the result can be taught to lesser Mages, being one of the few recurring effects in WoD's lore that really requires the Mage to learn a rote. It's improved after the 6th Maelstrom, but we can place Osiris here as an effective Archmage for our purposes. That way Archmages can have lasting impact through Effects that break your normal rules, but aren't individually world shaking if not replicable.

                                    4. Agree with [Arcane XP] costs always being required, though I think the best approach is specifically the system I envisioned above in this post. We can set some elements at base levels, you always have to include at least that much of them, and for [Arcane XP] that could be Highest Sphere level minus 5, or just Highest Sphere level if we want to be stinger.

                                    One aspect to consider, though, is that it's likely that [Arcane XP] will also be used to multiply successes because of Difficulty. Unless we also change the basic spellcasting rules, those spells will have very high Difficulty to begin with, making accumulating successes a heavy task, and many will need a lot of them for [Spell Factors].

                                    5. I wouldn't move the creation of Hedge Paths to [Archspells] to avoid messing with what the rules already allow, but I think we can have the imparting of a Path as such an effect.

                                    The thing with Hedge Paths is that the result is not something that exists, it's something that can be learned by someone else with XP. Who's actually empowering the Path isn't the Mage, it is the hedge magician: they're working hard to master it, spending XP, applying their own will and paradigm. And the Mage has no control on this knowledge, it will spread on its own and be used, abused, adapted and modified however others are fit.

                                    At heart, even if created by a Mage the Path is entirely a thing if the Sleeper world that follows its regular rules, the Mage was little more than an inspiration. But what about empowering the Hedge Magician?

                                    With a 6th dot Effect you turn the Path into a middle state between [Dynamics] and [Entities]. A target acquires the Numen at the level defined in the [Archspell], no personal expense (or need for willingness), and the Mage retains some control, or at least an influence as defined by [Dynamics], over the resulting powers and how they'll be used or passed along.

                                    Creating [Archspell Rotes] could fall here, too, but with the added burden of executing your original spell anyway.

                                    6. For the rest, I think the [Practices] are ok. The [Dynamics] is maybe a tad redundant, Ascension has enough space to work something like that at lower levels, but as a more solidly stated effect + clear successes + [Path Imposition] and [Archspell Rotes] it's a solid 6th dot. The rest is good and the whole covers almost everything we could ask for.

                                    Should we assume ignoring Disbelief and such is inherent to those levels, or add a minor extra rule, maybe a [Spell Factor] for this?

                                    We could describe those [Practices] in terms of [Constants] to ground into Ascension parlance. 6th dot is [Engraving a Constant in the Effect], 7th dot is [Engraving a Constant in the target], 8th dot is [Engraving a Constant in the Zone] and 9th is [Engraving a Constant in the World].

                                    I think the Appendix is too much rooted in the Awakening cosmology, breaking some of the themes and assumptions of Ascension that even Archmages shouldn't. Namely:

                                    1. Reality is defined by Consensus. Mages fight over the minds of the masses already because they're the key to it, and causing lasting change at a global scale is the focus of an ongoing war between factions, being simultaneously easier but also vulnerable. No single Mage should be able to overrule the war itself.

                                    2. Ascension is not Platonic Gnosticism, the main conflict is open and within reality rather than above it. The thematic thread of its great works is not personal enlightenment changing reality from the supernal, but creating means for others to carry on with the fight for ideals.

                                    3. Ascension embraces ambiguity. Mysteries in it are unknowable by definition, never resolving in a definitive answer. Even Archmastery should leave in the air the question: "do I really know how I did that, or is my belief so powerful I can't know the reality behind it?"

                                    4. As its main mystery, Ascension can't be given a rule. There may be a Mage that'll ascend exactly as detailed in the Omens system. Most won't, and we'll never know why.

                                    I'm not against writing down possibilities for Ascension, I actually think its past the time for us to have an entire book on options about how to define Ascension in a chronicle, but the place for this is not the Archmage rules.
