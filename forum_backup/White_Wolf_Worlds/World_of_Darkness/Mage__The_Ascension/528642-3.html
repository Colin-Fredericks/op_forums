            I've never had a problem with Werewolf, possibly because I like a good vs evil storyline, but more likely because the Ultimate Corrupter is itself a corruption of something more pure. Likewise I don't have a problem with the Nephandi being evil. For at least 15 years now I've considered them to be the closest thing to pure evil in the cWoD, because unlike all the others they had to choose it. What I do have a problem is with them somehow being much less fallible than anyone else; better at hiding themselves, better at manipulation, better at either planning or recruitment, and just better.

            Now, don't get me wrong, they should seem to be that dark force that (seems to have) realized the secret Truth (that there is no Truth, just Lies) and is stronger because of it. That's the most terrifying thing about them, that they could be right. They play well as an implacable, overwhelming, incorruptible evil that is winning despite its obvious flaws. It's a great story, one that clearly fits in Mage.

            They just can't be objectively right.

            For starters, there's nothing about the Nephandi, mechanically, that makes them particularly special. They get two advantages no one else does. Because of the Cauls, they (by default) can't be infiltrated effectively and they can't be redeemed. But note, that doesn't actually make them any better at infiltrating or manipulating people. Sure, there are no double agents among the Nephandi who might be wrongly trusted with the NOC list (I just watched the first Mission Impossible movie, sue me), but they can still be spied on and they can still turn on each other. And at least an Etherite among the Technocrats doesn't have to hide a corrupted Avatar. So the idea that they are the force secretly manipulating everyone and not being manipulated in return, doesn't really make sense. They can be, but its not fait accompli.

            Also, the history of the Technocracy and the Traditions has always been problematic with me. It is riddled with bad choices and misunderstandings, which is fine and appropriate, but too often their history falls into the pattern of a Parade of Failures which relies too much on people being blinded by arrogance and flaws at inappropriate times. Yeah, its in keeping with the cWoD and magely hubris, and its often necessary to get everyone into the position they are in today, but that doesn't keep it from being clunky (and sometimes a little stupid). But if it has to happen, it should be happening to the Nephandi too, they are as prone to hubris and self delusion as any Mage. If they are one of the Big Boys and Girls they should be fucking up like them too.

            Because then, if an ST wants to have the Nephandi be that "implacable, overwhelming, incorruptible evil that is winning despite its obvious flaws," they have the obvious flaws to win despite.

                        The crossover aspect is largely irrelevant to the idea of the Nephandi. While the books have long since had Wyrm/Nephandus crossover, there are plenty of options with the Nephandi that could care less about that. They stand as their own thing.

                        I also continue to not get the whole excluded middle thing of, "the Nephandi infiltrating the Union removes all human faults and all Union guilty."

                        1) People with power being corrupted by bad people is a human fault. It happens all the time in the real world. No Technocrats ever getting corrupted would demonstrate a lack of human failings, and corrupting elements generally target the most powerful members of an organization they can sink their teeth into. If the mob wants to bribe a new anti-crime bill out of existence in their state, it's a lot easier to sabotage by getting specific powerful individuals (committee chairs, speakers/etc. in the general assembly, the presiding executive with veto power) than trying to actually corrupt 51+% of the legislature. If humans in the real world perpetuate both sides of that, why should mages (with the goal of having human faults matter to mages) be immune to the same pressures?

                        2) Why does being corrupted by an evil group absolve you of guilt for their actions? If the mob can only smuggle in a shipment of guns because a politician signed some paperwork for them, that politician is culpable for every person harmed with those guns (and probably whatever bad things the mob does with the money from selling the guns if they're doing that too). "I just signed some papers for a bribe!" doesn't make you morally clean in all this. Likewise, if a Technocrat makes a deal with a Nephandus to sabotage their rival Technocrat's research in exchange for access to a Technocratic database, that Technocrat is still morally culpable for when the Nephandus uses that intel to blackmail a someone into a much more dubious action.

                        I simply don't get this incredibly black & white view of morality that follows the idea that being corrupted makes you squeaky clean in the end.

                                        Okay, I'm going to try to sum up: most of the complaints about the Nephandi winning seem to boil down to: "can't the failings of the Technocracy/Traditions/whoever be just human failings?"

                                        Yep. They are. And that's exactly why the Nephandi are winning.

                                        They're not creating any of those problems. They're just exacerbating them. Nephandi nudge, they prod, they make the floor just that bit more slick for other mages to stumble and fall, but all they do is change the TN. The Path is Fragile, and the metaphysical gravity of hubris, terror and fanaticism combine to make Descent so much easier than Ascension. Transcendence is tricky and rare; atrocity is never more than one weakness away. Revenge is simpler than forgiveness. And, frankly, there are plenty of folks who don't need Nephandi to be unspeakably evil. Look at Voormas. He's explicitly not a Nephandus, but a lot of Nephandi probably wish they were half as terrifying as he is. The world is already an awful, tangled mess. Add Nephandi, and how hard do they even need to work? They just cut off the occasional genuine reformer. A saint becomes a martyr here, a hero becomes a dead legend there, and then you make sure the other side takes the blame.

                                        Most importantly, the Nephandi aren't infallible. If they were, it wouldn't be much of a campaign. They're really good at what they do, but after centuries of their foul efforts, the world is still here. The Technocracy and Traditions did get over their mutual hatred for long enough to stop the Aswadim back in the 40s. If the right group were to make the right efforts, who knows? The Awakened of the world will never stop arguing, but maybe a shared enemy and a few moments of clarity can get them to stop killing each other. It'd be a lot of work, and the Nephandi resist nothing more than peace. Now give a group of PCs the right motive to try it, and it could make one Hell of a campaign, couldn't it?

                                        Heroes die. Hope is a phoenix.
