                                    Originally posted by mark View Post

                                    I actually thought about this before. The detail with which Al-Aswad, and the other Aswadim, are fleshed out in the "Hell on Earth" scenario would, at first glance, suggest that it's not gameplay and story segregation (that is, that the Aswadim indeed follow the Sphere Paradigm).
                                    However, maybe it is, in a way, gameplay and story segregation. As in, the authors made the Aswadim use the Sphere Paradigm within their background, because there wasn't enough word count to flesh out their unique approaches and Paradigms.


                                    The Pillars: Desire | Curiosity | Excess | Avarice


                                    Excess is keyed to the Sphere of Time primarily, through Desire and Avarice. It is also linked to Spirit through Avarice, Prime through Curiosity, Entropy through Curiosity, and Matter through Desire.

                                    Curiosity is keyed to the Sphere of Prime primarily, through Desire and Excess. It is also linked to Mind through Avarice, Entropy through Excess, Life through Avarice, and Correspondence through Desire.

                                    Avarice is keyed to the Sphere of Mind primarily, through Desire and Curiosity. It is also linked to Time through Excess, Spirit through Excess, Life through Curiosity, and Correspondence through Desire.

                                    Desire is keyed to the Sphere of Correspondence primarily, through Curiosity and Avarice. It is also linked to Time through Excess, Matter through Excess, Prime through Curiosity, and Mind through Avarice.

                                    Alternatively, in order to make a point to the Traditions, a True Mage of this Paradigm would say:
                                    “The Sphere of Correspondence is keyed to Desire, the Sphere of Prime to Curiosity, the Sphere of Time to Excess and the Sphere of Mind – keyed to Mind.”

                                    ( I think those pairs work very well on a conceptual and practical level.
                                    You can conjure gifts to someone using Desire, and Avarice Desire: Correspondence and Matterindeed is ideal for putting persons, and spirits, to sleep :-) )


                                    How does this work in practice?
                                    For example, followers of this Paradigm can Ward spirits off through use of Excess and Avarice. This results in Wards that work on the principle that “Why would you bother me in my sanctum, inlaid with gold and jewels, while I'm going through 20 grams of amphetamine?." Even the most stalwart demonic pursuers pause and apologize, awed by the Excess and the the sheer nonchalance of a Mage's Avarice. Depending how the Mages handles the interaction (remember the demons did intrude upon a Sanctum, so they are in a way, at a disadvantage :-) ) the demons might even promise some way of making up for disrupting the Mage. Do think carefully before offering a line of coke to a demon - it might cause him to become psychotic or paranoid...if the dosage is wrong. :-)
                                    This can work without any spells cast beforehand, or it can involve some kind of ritual can to establish the Wards

                                    Time effects based on the Excess-Avarice link involve dedication and, sooner or later, sacrifice of precious items and works of art. The Excess-Desire line of Time effects work by trances – whether reached through tantra or through the use of stimulating substances. (of course, both of those can be used at once in a trance)

                                    The Avarice-Desire link (which has Correspondence and Mind as it's Spheres) involves astral projection, lucid dreaming (can be used to establish various Mind 1 effects, including Mind Shields) and manipulating other person's dreams - personally, or at a distance.

                                    Another property of such gifts is that, if someone throws them away...they have a tendency to reappear...Take a long walk up to a river, throw it and run away without turning your back...you come back to your apartment, and the pendant you've accepted is right next to your bed, shining on a table :- ) You could destroy it maybe, but how could you do it, if you didn't do it in the first place?^^ This reappearing can be guided dynamically by the Mage, of course.
                                    So when this person who tried to throw away the pendant hears "...but how could you do it, if you didn't do it in the first place?^^" it could very well mean that the pendant is now immune to getting damaged by force...in a way, it's now a better pendant :-) So think carefully, if you'll be planning to melt it using fire. )

                                    Avarice 3, Desire 2, Curiosity 1 could make someone daydream about the Mage or someone or something else. This daydreaming can make someone vulnerable for the Desire 4 Avarice 2 gift-giving.
                                    ( Curiosity is needed to see someone's current state to fullest extent, and direct Desire and Avarice to lull someone into daydreaming state. )

                                    This Paradigm, as per the dualistic (or contrasting) Foundation, would focus heavily on interactions between two Pillars to achieve it's effects.

My idea was to basically add in 3 sub-Sphere level traits per Sphere (Ok it was bigger than that, but it's the part that matters to this) that allowed a bit more flavor/customization. The point of this was to keep the general Sphere structure and the cross-compatibility stuff, but have more Tradition/Splat flavor around. So a Verbena and an Etherite might have the same dots in Life, but the Verbena might have "Healing, Shapeshifting, and Plant Mastery," to reflect how they relate to Life and what kind of spells they're good at, while the Etherite might have, "Body Modification, Genetics, and Homeostatic Control."


                                                            I guess some of this is rather than looking at "limiting" mages into their Tradition/Splat paradigms, doing more of a carrot to encourage them to play to the strengths of their paradigm.
                                                            Perhaps each player should have 2 or 3 'specialties' that a player needs to choose that get reduced difficulties. This would entice players to cast those spells in times of difficulty to get the best chance of success. If I were a wizard in stress, a situation where things were getting very difficult, I would naturally fall back on my strengths and fundamentals without even thinking about it.

                                                            Lower difficulties for certain sub-spheres or specialties would give players incentive to cast along those lines. Thus, creating a flavor of a character that differs from another.
