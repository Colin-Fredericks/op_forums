    "Do-While" and "Do-Until" Spell Contingencies

    12-07-2024, 10:15 AM
    A Virtual Adept character, of the reality hacker variety, has started looking into effect contingencies not involving hanging effects. Players in the games I run are considerate enough to let me work out the details of novel awakened magickal applications between sessions, by letting me know that their character is "working on" some approach which the written materials don't provide clear immediate examples of. This has worked out well.

    What he is wanting to do is use Entropy conjunctionally to provide a condition under which an active effect will cancel itself, similar to a hanging effect which goes off at some future point based on certain conditions. As an example, the mage works an effect which provides power to a lightbulb, with the condition that the effect expires if someone walks through the doorway of the room it's lighting, provided that the duration of the effect doesn't expire naturally first.

    So far as I can tell based on the available M20 material, this sort of thing would simply require a conjunctional Entropy effect of an appropriate level relative to the contingency. Time wouldn't seem to be necessary, as the effect isn't a hanging one being "cast into the future." (I'm basing this mostly on what's presented about this in How Do You DO That.)

    Does anyone know of a written example of this, which I might have missed somewhere? We tend NOT to do house rules unless the existing ones outright don't cover some potential situation.

                                      What Time 4 does, specifically, is put a spell in suspended animation until a trigger point is reached. I would argue that seems like overkill for "Do While" or Do Until" although I suppose it could work if only the cancel clause is suspended.

                                      M20, unfortunately, is missing clear rules for making spells able to detect changing conditions, so it gives a different answer every time it comes up. Time triggers don't need extra spheres, wards use associated spheres at the highest level they can be inflated to. The former acts like any spell can detect changing conditions without extra spheres. The latter acts like detection takes the same skill as transmutation.

                                      I don't think either makes sense, though if I had to choose between the two I would go with no additional spheres on the grounds of usability. My recommendation, though, is that any time a spell needs to detect something (or touch something without making changes) use [Appropriate Sphere] 2. Entropy 2 would work for most "event" triggers.

                                          Yes, officially the requirement has always been Time 4 Entropy 2, which I fully agree is absolutely overkill, and I also say that it doesn't make sense given what it is supposed to do. I didn't include Time 4 because I thought that was already simplicity, but I houserule it out and make it Entropy 2 alone.

                                          The reason for the usage of Time 4, as explained in official sources from 1st ed to rev is that it is used to "hold the spell", as if you need to keep the spell frozen in time.

                                          That... Makes no sense thou. Because it is Entropy 2 that do all the job. Entropy 2 is used to cause the spell to "selective fail". For instance, the ward spell will let any redheads go through; this means that the ward will always be working, except when a redhead goes through, upon which Entropy 2 will cause it to selective fail.

                                          Time 4 doesn't do anything really, and this is yet another instance upon which the writers "go with their feelings", as they so often do, instead of what the levels of the Spheres were supposed to do. In fact, adding Time 4 is less than useless, because it means that Entropy 2 will be causing the Time 4 effect of "holding the spell paralyzed in Time" to selectively fail or not, instead of the main spell itself, which is stupid.

                                          I also disagree on the usage of other Spheres. Entropy 2 is all that you need. You don't need Matter to identify someone carrying a firearm, or Life to identify a redhead, which would be the targets of your ward for example. All you need is Entropy because you're identifying DESTINY. "This ward is DESTINED to ignite upon redheads". It doesn't matter if the effect can make a chemical analysis of the person's body hairs.

                                          It's just like using Entropy to shoot 2 arrows and to hit the 2 people at the end in a line of 3 but not the person at front. You don't need Life to do that, because you're dealing with Fate, Destiny, Probability. Likewise, conditional spells need Entropy 2 (and according to RAW Time 4, but I hope Ive already made a good case to why this is stupid and should be removed), because you don't need the spell to analyze the subject, you just need the Fate or Destiny conditions to be set. Therefore, no other Spheres are required - except for whatever Spheres are required for the underlined effect itself.

                                          For example: Bullet Time - Time 3, Entropy 2. Whenever someone shoots at you, you gain increased speed.

                                          Time 3 accelerates time (as per the rules, one extra action per turn for every 2 successes), while Entropy 2 is the "trigger".

                                          Now, the thing about Triggers is that you have to spend successes on duration. Now, for the above Bullet Time Routine above for instance, I would say that keeping that for less than a day would be useless. Now, you can of course just simply constantly walk around with a bunch of triggers spells permanently attached; just remember to enforce the increased difficulty rules for simultaneous spells being held on but hey, on many occasions I would trade the capability to launch new spells (due to prohibitive dif) for having a ton of cool powers "always on" on me at all times (like for instance instant regeneration all the time, mental shields, the "All Seeing Eye" effect which I laid in another topic that has all the lvl 1 and some lvl 2 Spheres to basically give you Spider Man's "spider senses" on steroids, "Perfect Escape" which I'm just inventing right now - Corr 3 Entropy 2, which causes you to teleport to a safe location whenever you're in mortal danger and a few others. Walking around with a bunch of permanent effects may be better than being able to cast spells at will - and remember, you can truly have a ton of them)

                                              To make sure I understand you correctly:

                                              There are two ways I could see constructing a Bullet Time spell that turns on when someone shoots at you. The obvious one from looking at the rules is a Bullet Time spell put in suspended animation with an event trigger. This would be Time 4 for Trigger Effect (plus Entropy 2 if it needs a trigger, though the text is vague about that). In this case the spell is not active at all until the trigger event, at which point the spell runs its course like usual.

                                              The second is an ongoing Bullet Time spell that only turns its effects on when certain conditions are met and turns them off at other times. This would be a standard Time 3 Bullet Time spell with an Entropy 2 trigger. The spell is not suspended at all, it is active but it isn't doing anything until the conditions are met. Similar to how a mage might give himself flight but not use it for every second of the spell.

                                                          Well, it is in M20.

                                                          Originally posted by M20, pg 529, "How Many Effects Can I Use at Once?"
                                                          As an overall note, an Effect that has a Time-based trigger, one which has been locked into another Pattern, or one that has been cast but whose duration has not yet expired, does not count toward that total.
