                Originally posted by Penelope View Post
                Starcomet that makes no sense. Why is alchemy special?

                I think this is because Alchemy is more than just a path of magic. It is also a philosophical/spiritual discipline as well with the ultimate goal of enlightenment and spiritual perfection. Alchemy is not just creating potions and making perfect metals, objects, etc. but also the spiritual transformation of the individual alchemist. At 6 dots, you have achieved the knowledge to not only make material things perfect, but also bring out spiritual perfection of yourself and others. This is why only a sorcerer with 6 dots in alchemy can create concoctions capable of awakening any mortal individual who consumes it. This is also why Alchemy 6 is strong enough to create vampirism (as what happened to the Tremere inner circle), werewolves, mummies, etc...in theory!

                This is in contrast to all other paths which are just different methods of working magic with no overarching goal or philosophy. The end goal (6 dots), of Alchemy is the spiritual transformation and perfection of the Alchemist.

                                                    In the X20 line, Hedge Magic isn't a single thing. The Hedge Paths found in Hunters Hunted II are different from the Hedge Ways found in Kinfolk — not just in terms of which ones are presented, but also in terms of what their mechanics are. Sometimes drastically different. And the upcoming Ghost Hunters will be introducing its own Hedge Magic and Psychic Phenomena, with its own mechanics.

                                                    I say this to point out that the idea that there's a single unified Sorcery system in the X20 lines is an illusion. Take the Path of Divination: in HHII, the levels go something like: immediate signs and warnings; compare possible outcomes in advance; determine what potential follow-up decisions may arise; divine specific future events; see the future clearly. By contrast, Kinfolk defines the levels as Fortune-telling, Dowsing, Augury, Premonitions, and Vision Quest. Finally, at least in the KS draft, Ghost Hunters' Divination isn't even Hedge Magic; it's been redefined as a Psychic Phenomenon, with a Tarot-based progression: Coins, then Cups, Wands, Swords, and finally Kings and Queens.

                                                    So when you ask about Night Folk using Sorcery, I think of “Sorcery” as “Mage's flavor of Hedge Magic”. I don't have a problem with HHII-style Hedge Paths appearing in a Vampire game, or Kinfolk-style Hedge Ways appearing in a Werewolf game; those aren't crossover issues, as the respective versions of Hedge Magic have been tailored for the respective gamelines.

                                                    But including Sorcery in either is a crossover issue, every bit as much as throwing a mage into a Vampire or Werewolf game would be; because Sorcerer was written with Mage in mind. Even the first edition of Sorcerer, which was touted as presenting a way to include magicians in other gamelines without having to deal with Mage's metaphysical elements, is still heavy on Mage-isms, from incorporating its system of foci (called magical tools in Sorcerer) to the fact that sorcerers are professional magicians, not dabblers the way their V20 and W20 counterparts are presented.

                                                    My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
