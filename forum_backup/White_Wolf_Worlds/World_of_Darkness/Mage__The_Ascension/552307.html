    Fleshing out the Mirainohmen

    06-12-2015, 07:10 PM
    Of the new Crafts mentioned in passing by Mage Twentieth Anniversary Edition, the Mirainohmen were my favorites- they have an interesting concept that's either a direct reference to the Shin Megami Tensei series or a lucky coincidence. I'd love to flesh out them out with a full writeup, and the SMT connection provides an easy way to do it, since there are a lot of fun concepts to incorporate. For example, the idea of a line of demon summoners from the Raidou Kuzunoha series would be an interesting way to provide a background for the organization. I'd also love to add a subfaction based on the Persona games- Mages funded by the mysterious Kirijo Group, who use Evokers to force their Avatars to manifest physically.

    And of course there's a ton more to work with- the various demon-summoning devices, the Midnight Channel...

      Mirainohmen strike me as being uncanny similar to one of cabals in my headcanon. I have not played Shin Megami Tensei series, so cannot get reference, but hope that may be useful.

      The cabal is named LTJP. Initially it name probably was nonsense, or maybe some Japanese pun, but has since beed backronymed as "Living, Thinking Jigsaw Puzzle" and as "these 'Leet Japanese" by Virtual Adepts. It was created in the latter half of 1990s by Hollow Ones, more technically-minded Cultists of Ecstasy (some suspect connection with Dissonance Society), and maybe Children of Knowledge and/or Li-Hai - primarily in Japan, but quickly became international. From the early on, cabal has ties with Virtual Adepts' SIGMEM (Special Interest Group: Memetics. SIGMEM is primarily Cypherpunk's creation and dedicated to the study and application of memetics, including information warfare with Technocratic Union. Cypherpunks being cypherpunks, the Special Interest Group is secretive and intriguing; there are rumors abound on them, including creating memetic-based AI daemons and having a strong hand in Reality 2.0 project). LTJP also have interest in memetics and secrecy, but use more intuitive approach. Using combination of music, drugs and visual arts, LTJP members are able to synchronize their minds over the Internet, allowing them to create gestalt minds and use weird kind of "mutual possession". Some accused LTJP of possessing and otherwise influencing Sleepers through memetic bait pages and other digital artifacts, scattered through mundane parts of the Web. There is a strong suspection what both SIGMEM and LTJP have their parts in the Anomymous movement. Some even attribute creation of Non memophore (see M20 core, p. 639) on either of these groups.

                                  A while ago I tried developing a write-up for a SMT inspired Virtual Adept Faction, aptly called the Demon Summoners (in this case using the term "Demon" loosely, just as SMT does). It draws much more from the main series than it does from Persona. It's actually funny how much the Virtual Adepts sync stylistically with the hackers from the original SMT. I mean, have you see recent redrawings of the protagonists from SMT 1 and 2? You'd swear they were VAs, complete with bulky headsets and arm-mounted computers. Google image search it, and tell me that's not VA.

                                  Anyway, the Demon Summoners would have been a loose collection of hacker mages who employ demon summoning programs to do Spirit magic. You could easily pattern the more Spirit-minded VAs after this franchise, and it would work surprisingly well.

                                  I've also toyed with the idea of writing up stuff for a group of Persona users, but as this thread has illustrated, Mage doesn't really lend itself well to directly summoning one's avatar to fight for them. So I shelved that idea.

                                      Back on the matter of SMT, I did draft an idea for Manikins like in SMT3: Nocturne. Rather than being some kind of Gaia Possessed with special powers - W20 had that thoroughly covered with Kami - I made them out to be Gaia-created artificial humans. Basically a Construct from the soil. The idea being that, because the element of Earth gives life most readily, lost or shattered souls (or Wraiths) sometimes coalesce in spots beneath the ground, and form humanoids. Since I always had a soft spot for the perpetually suffering Manikins in SMT 3, I designed these ones to be just artificial humans (as opposed to obviously inhuman golems or something), just with an incurable twitch.

                                      I also gave them a requirement that they had to be targeted with both Life and Matter in order to heal or harm them, but I always considered that enough of a blessing and a curse to be neutral (and thus worth no Merit or Flaw points). And, of course, I made it so that they could Awaken, but they couldn't breed naturally, and were sometimes more vulnerable to possession by spirits, since they usually started with more of an amalgam of spirits inside of them instead of one whole soul.

                                      This was back before M20 came out, so I envisioned the classic WoD Manikin as being a very rare phenomenon in times past. At most one would claw its way out of the ground every half- or full century, and often halfway across the world from the last one. The Shadowlands were just too efficient in times past to let broken fragments of soul and pathos collect in one spot long enough to produce Manikins, at least not in large numbers. So they were rare enough that most supernaturals were ignorant of their existence, and those that knew of them saw them as an idle curiosity or inspiration for their own work in making artificial humans (I can see an alternate creation method being formed by a Mage, probably with Life and Matter and Prime as a base, used either as a shell for spirits or astrally disconnected people, or given minds and souls of their own, for use as servants).

                                      Then the Sixth Great Maelstrom happened, and Wraiths were being torn apart and blown every which way. Massive amounts of emotional energy were released by the Maelstrom, by the Ravnos Antedeluvian's rampage and death, and by the loss of the Umbra for Awakened beings. Suddenly, a lot more Manikins start crawling out of the ground and wandering about, completely ignorant of who or what they are. And with that many of them running around, they are bound to find each other and form communities. While everyone else is busy with their respective crises - the Traditions and Technocracy have their hands full with the Avatar Storm and the fallout from that - a legion of twitchy, confused Manikins get together and start thinking about what they want to do with their lives.

                                      Now this was before M20, but it's not like this idea is out of the question. Sure, the Avatar Storm has receded (assuming you go with that metaplot option), but the "damage" has already been done as far as Manikins are concerned. By this time they'd probably be having the first of their few Awakened becoming Disciple level or greater, and of course they'd rise to the fore of their communities. They might even be developing their own culture and - gasp - Paradigms. I can easily see these as player characters.

                                                  Yes, it's the necro-thread, again. Please accept my apologies.
                                                  I did some research on the Shin Tegami Tensen games referenced in older posts, but it didn't seem to quite cover enough ground with respect to "rearrang[ing] identities and social preconceptions" to my thinking.
                                                  For my part, I can imagine at least one young Orphan taking cues from the "Noppera-boh", a legendary Japanese ghost that looks perfectly human except for the absence of a face and notable for doing little more than what we modern Americans would call "jump-scares". Using Sphere Magick (at the first dot level) for this kind of thing might be a useful excuse to practice, even if such things amount to using a top-of-the-line desktop computer to play "Space Invaders". But what about paradigms, practices and instruments?
                                                  "Rearranging identities and social preconceptions" sounds very interesting, especially in societies where social roles are almost upheld as holy writ; "Contrarianism", "Crazy Wisdom" and "Cybernetics" would seem tailored to the group. Beyond that... well... What kinds of things would Orphan techmomancer-tricksters wind up doing?
