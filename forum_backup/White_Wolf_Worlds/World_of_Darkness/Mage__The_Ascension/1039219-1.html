    Originally posted by Saikou View Post
    Here's the thing. If mage was just Traditions and Technocracy, you have yourself a great number of cultures which are ignored or simply absorbed into the others. The Islamic batini would just be a sect of the chorus, losing their unique Arabian focus. Similarly kopa Loa would just be another example of "everything third world's a dreamspeaker".
    What's good about the crafts is it demonstrates that not every magic user has to be a tradition, and indeed new crafts can spring up in areas where belief is strong.
    Counter argument: this waters down the setting and diminishes the importance of every group. Once you've added the Crafts, the Traditions have gone down from a major threat the Technocracy cares about to one of the many terrorist organisations.
    Second counter argument: the magical traditions Crafts represent are too minor to warrant a separate faction. Kopa Loei represent hawaian shamans and according to the wiki have 15-30 members. Why does this school club need to be special snowflakes who stand their ground alone in the fight against the forces of reason?

    Saying that every shaman is a dreamspeaker is okay. In fact - that's the entire point. Various tribal shamans united under a broad umbrella of dreamspeakers. Various monotheists united under a broad umbrella of Celestial Chorus. It's an anti-technocratic alliance. So long as you have a freedom to say "my character is a Tuvan shaman who interacts with spirits by throat singing" - you don't need an entire faction of Tuvan throat singers. You just say that they are members of a dreamspeaker faction of the Traditions and call it a day.

Various monotheists united under a broad umbrella of Celestial Chorus. It's an anti-technocratic alliance. You just say that they are members of a dreamspeaker faction of the Traditions and call it a day.
                    The main difference between the Technocracy and the Mages are that the mages do not share a consistent ideology on how magic should work. They may argue a hell of a lot, and indeed may become outright tyrannical in the way they press their practices. But unlike the Technocracy, for the most part they recognise that enforcing one overriding consensus does more harm to their cause than good. And while the Technocracy has unity on their side, they lack in versatility.

                    Your insistence on Tradition conformity is, therefore, a very technocratic argument. No doubt there are several mages that have put forth similar viewpoints. But let us not forget, the Traditions are a Hermetic idea, and it's all very well and good touting the benefits of conformity and speaking in a united voice when it's your own language that become the shared language. The Dreamspeakers were grouped together because the Hermetics had a superficial view of them. Lumping them together hundreds of different tribes with views ranging in compatibility from similar enough to outright contradictory. Why did they do this? Because partially because the Hermetics didn't recognise their differences, "Oh, another Shaman, take them to the Dreamspeaker table", others considered their tribes so small and secular that their individuality didn't really matter all that much in the grand scheme of things.

                    For the dreamspeakers, this was an insult, and is the main reason why they remain the most aloof of the Council of Nine. However, as the Ascension war raged on, there are some shamans that saw a benefit in their superficial union. Now many of them band together for survival, not our of shared belief, but so the Council will take their requests more seriously. As such, unlike the Hermetics or the Choristors, there is no central governance for the Dreamspeakers, many of them act alone or in small tribes, but will emphasise a shared comoradere, and a willingness to aid one another in the face of greater threats, including the Hubris of the Council.

                    However, there are groups that rejected the Hermetic's attempts at amalgamation outright. For them, their pride was so great that to consider others on equal footing was tantamount to betrayal of their own ways. These are the conservative few who cling to their ideas and consider the Tradition's fight as not their own.
                    Why do they exist? Because this attitude is all too human. Pride creates such rifts, and that is where the crafts are forged.

                    So you can say every Shaman is a Dreamspeaker, but then you'd be simplifying what is a very big problem with humanity. Pride and an stubborn adherence to what we are already used to.


                    But that is the main thing, the Traditions are more coordinated than the crafts. For the most part the crafts are pretty isolated, hidden within the far corners of the globe keeping mostly to themselves. I doubt the technocracy even acknowledges them at all, let alone considers them worthy of discussion.
                    My guess is that most Technocrats don't eve know there's a difference. All mages are part of this Tradition alliance. But that's of course assuming these technocrats in particular are briefed on how Mages operate.
                    Some Technocrats won't even consider such distinctions outside of "categorise their ways to make it easier to fight them."

                    Also, Mage isn't a story, it's a setting. It's a play set filled with toys. It's up to us to make games with them. You don't need to use every toy in the box, but they are there if you have the interest.

                                How about this:

                                Each sorceror MUST have an occultism-like knowledge... That's a skill that represents their grasp of their personal paradigm.

                                So, for example:

                                Personal Science (Etherite)
                                Consensual Science (Technocratic)
                                Virtuality (VA)
                                Witchcraft (Verbena)
                                High Arts (Hermetic)
                                Do (Akashic)
                                Celestial (CC)
                                Balance (Batini)

                                And so on; it's a variation of Occultism (so the char won't have both), but with a paradigm lenses.

                                That skill will determine the maximum level of the effects the sorceror can have, and to have access to higher level effects, he must have at least one effect of the previous level. If you wanna, you can make it so that whatnever the sorceror increases one point in that skill, he gains a new effect of the same level.

                                And yes, one could learn more than one Paradigm, if they spend the XP for it (and role play it)

                                How does he buy the effects: any effect is a separate fixed "power". The level of the effect is determined by the highest level of Sphere on it, so for example:

                                Steel Skin (Life 4, Matter 2)

                                Is a level 4 effect.

                                Mind Scan (Corr 1, Mind 1)

                                Is a level 1 effect.

                                The costs would be, level of the effect x2 in XP. So:

                                Level 1 - 2 XP
                                Level 2 - 4 XP
                                Level 3 - 6 XP
                                Level 4 - 8 XP
                                Level 5 - 10 XP.

                                Those prices are adjustable as you see fit. If you wanna more powerful sorcerors, make costs be 1 XP/lvl. If wanna them with very few powers, make 3XP/lvl or more.

                                Casting: effects have two categories: spells (fast and dirty) and rituals (low and powerful). Some spells also have a ritual version (and vice versa), but they count as two different effects, and must be learned apart.

                                For Spells: Wits+Paradigm Skill
                                For Rituals: Inteligence+Paradigm Skill.

                                Number of successes: double the requirements for mages. For damage, each success does only one damage, +1 if it is Forces, and so on.

                                If that's not for your liking, roll just their Paradigm Skill and deal the same way as Arete rolls.

                                Paradox: if anyone care to help me here I would appreciate. I'm thinking that instead of Paradox, they could receive "Desbelief", and that thing would cause effects to malfunction or not working at all from time to time.

                                How's that now?

                                PS: mind you, if you like the sorcerors as they are, it's fine. It's just that for me, is not to my liking, so I really would like to make a new way that would be functional, any idea would be helpful
