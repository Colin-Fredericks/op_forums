            The First Edition Corebook gives some examples of results that are not within the capabilities of Magick Effects with particular Spheres at Levels 5. In the case of the Matter and Entropy examples, it is stated that a particular result might be within the capabilities of Oracles.
            Matter – Creation of Matter-based entities.
            Entropy – Affecting ideas on level of societies. Making a person forget who she ( or he ) is.
            Mind – Pure mental existence. There is an allusion to “higher powers” that Oracles might have.

            The levels 6-9 of Archspheres in the “Masters of the Art” book should give some ideas in regard to limits and boundaries of Magickal Effects. Also, I think a that careful and throughout reading and consideration of the capabilities of the Spheres should reveal some limits.

                They can't create EXP.

                For me, Archspheres should be the following:

                - Archmastery should be clearly described a trap out-of-universe, but seen as the pinnacle of achievement in-universe. Archmastery is losing sight of ascension and instead doubling down on More Power.

                - Archmastery should be represented by a level 6 power which simply fills in a bunch of gaps left by the previous levels. With level 6 spirit you are creating Avatars, splitting them into pieces, redeeming lost souls, healing a "dead" soul that it can truly return to a normal life with the right host... none of this is easy. It demands powerful tools and the right input. An Archmage NPC should be saying "I need you to bring me X" rather than "I'll have that done in 5 minutes". That way even if they're your unconditional ally the game keeps some sense of balance. Yeah your friend can remove Permanent Paradox but to do it he needs to cut away a bit of your Avatar. And your friend can increase your Avatar's power, but you need to provide him with an absurd amount of Quintessence of a spare Avatar he can work with, and there are loads of practical and moral implications associated with all of this.

                - Archmages should only realize their mistake (if they ever do) when it's too late, and become partly consumed by their own craft. They stop being active agents in the world and become more like powerful spirits. They're not the protagonist by any means, they're a part of other people's stories. Their work consumes so much of their attention that they rarely leave the place where they work, if ever. They're a potential source of paradox free time travel, but they're opening the door for you and letting you get on with your adventure. They would never come along and make sure you get home safe.
