    Help me play a technocrat

    01-21-2019, 03:13 PM
    I've not actually got a game to play in atm but I like creating characters, working out how/why their magick works and doing some background, rotes, etc.

    But Technocrats have me foxed. I can't get a handle on them.

    I've read a fair few of the sourcebooks, old and new. I know Satyros has talked about some of the writing being inconsistent on certain points across the editions and books so maybe thats it but I have the following issues and require some discussion to help me work through them.

    1) Do technocrats understand that they are a special class of people because of their enlightenment and do they understand that this is the same as being awakened? If not, how do they understand a) the tradition mages that were once technocrats such as virtual adepts and SoE, b) the tradition mages they re-condition as union members and c) the similarity between the existence of mages and enlightened agents (i.e. they both have unique perspectives that allow them to do things other can't)? Why would the technocracy even kidnap and re-condition mages if they don't understand that enlightenment and awakening are the same thing?

    2) Do technocrats realise that they are altering reality through a property of themselves or do they see it ALL as training and tech. If the latter, how to they rationalise the same tech doing diddly squat in the hands of non-enlightened agents? Especially something simple to operate. For instance, weapons are simple to operate, they need to be because you can't fuss around with them in the middle of a fire fight. How does a technocratic agent understand their plasma rifle or rail gun or whatever not working, ever, in the hands of a non-enlightened agent, even if they very carefully explained how to use it somewhere entirely safe like a shooting range. Doesn't work for the non-enlightened guy, works for the enlightened guy. And he can see that the non-enlightened guy isn't doing anything wrong. And this also applies to EVERY hypertech device that he has been issued with.

    3) When we are designing technocrats, how advanced should we make the tech? Guide to the technocracy says they are about a century more advanced than mundane reality. Assume slightly less none since they lost so many labs in the avatar storm. But 50 to 90 years is a certainty. Should we be looking at plasma rifles and laser guns or is this too crude. Are we talking Iron man mk2 suits (the suit used for the majority of the first Ironman film) or the more advanced nanotech version from Avengers Infinity War? Certainly the higher the tech, the more cool technomagick we can do, but it also leaves a gap of tech too advanced for reality, like exo suits, but not close to the cutting edge. Is this still hyper tech?

    4) What about the cool stuff the technocracy should have thats only 10 years ahead of the rest of us. This would be much more likely to be issued to field agents and much less likely to cause paradox. Its entirely believable to deploy such tech as mundane prototypes and be almost entirely co-incidental. Is there any mention of this kind of tech in the books?

    5) Reality zones. Guide to the Technocracy explained this as removing the variables that makes tech do weird stuff, such as gravity for space stations. But this makes little sense since a space station can be easily given gravity by making it spin (and I'm sure they have artificial gravity too). Do technocrats understand that their coolest and most advanced tech works offworld because these places are in areas with more malleable laws of nature? Do they get why offworld labs work? Since they construct their own horizon realms, one must assume that they have some understanding, or is this limited only to the senior masters?

    6) Slightly off topic, but some of these questions might apply to technomancers from other magical groups such as the VAs and SoE, but I think they are far less problematic there.



    As far as I can tell I have to play a technocrat on one of two ways. Either they do understand that they are will workers and that they have chosen to work their abilities through a shared paradigm that is readily codifiable and explainable and will eventually be introduced to the masses. OR, they don't understand any of the meta-physicks and really believe its all just really great tech and advanced training and simply can't understand why that gizmo won't work for that other guy but works everytime for you.

                          A word of warning, don't get too lost in the weeds when it comes to hypertech, dimsci, and the other high-end, far-out applications of Technocratic methods and practices. That stuff is cool to talk about, sure, but it's the minority of Technocratic goodies...and a pretty small one to boot. The most advanced piece of tech most Technocrats -- even Enlightened ones -- work with on a day-to-day basis is a regular-ass smartphone, maybe a bit more powerful when it comes to memory, processing power, wireless bandwidth, or battery life, but a regular-ass smartphone nevertheless.

                          Put a Technocrat of each of the five Conventions in a room, and ask them individually to define "power suit". You're going to get the Iron Man stuff out of two of them, the Iterator and the Void Engineer. The other three are going to start talking about formal wear -- colors, cuts, styles, patterns, fabrics, brand, favorite tailors, and fashion trends in general. The conversation might drift to ideal cuts for concealing firearms, or concealed yet fashionable armored inserts, if either the NWO or Syndicate person is a field operative of some sort, but that leaves the Progenitor high and dry since their definition of "suit up for field work" is more often than not a biohazard suit. That's what they know, and what they work with on a daily basis; for at least two of the Conventions (NWO and Syndicate) a good business suit is one of the most fundamental Tools (capital-T, as in the basis for Technocratic Procedures) of their Convention.

                                              Another thing to consider: as Aleph mentioned, Technocrats generally see what they do as exploiting hidden principles of reality. Put another way, their relationship with Instruments is inverted from the traditional mage: where a regular mage sees Instruments as a way of channeling an innate ability to willwork (as represented by the Arete trait), a Technocrat sees the ability to “change the world” as residing in the Instruments, and being coaxed out and directed by the Scientist’s Enlightenment. In both cases, you need both the Instruments and the Arete/Enlightenment; but the perceived relationship between the two is very different.

                                              This has a couple of game system effects: first, Technocrats don’t ever get to surpass their Instruments. Since they view their Science as being fundamentally rooted in their Instruments, being able to discard them and perform Science through force of will is sheer nonsense. Conversely, the fact that Science is not intensely personal and drawn from within means that Technocrats have an edge when it comes to making Wonders: in particular, mages who craft Talismans or Grimoires have to invest Willpower and personal Quintessence into the very act of doing so, above and beyond whatever Quintessence needs to be incorporated into the resulting Wonder; Technocrats who create Devices or Principiae (the techno counterparts to Talismans and Grimoires) can get around that requirement, as long as they have the proper facilities.

                                              And remember that “Instrument” does not necessarily mean “a physical object”: Instruments also include appropriate activities. And even those Instruments that are physical objects need to be activated in an appropriate manner. To a Technocrat, those activities are what make up their Practices, which are conceptual frameworks for how activities influence the world — as opposed to the mystics, for whom a Practice is all about how to focus one’s Arete.

After all, in the big picture of all human history and prehistory, secularized will-work that in turn is the basis for concepts such as Forces itself*, is honestly a pretty new concept, dating back maybe as far as classical antiquity.

                                                          [By this I mean discrete, identifiable phenomena that are the result or cause of interaction between objects, signifying or being themselves instruments of change in the observable world over time. These phenomena being innate properties of the world itself, which can in turn be observed to determine how objects interact and how said phenomena effect change, as opposed to derivative of higher beings such as gods or spirits which would be in turn the source of all causation in the universe.]

                                                          Hell, that brings us to the biggest chicken-or-the-egg question relevant to WoD metaphysics of all time: what came first, the thunder or the thunder god?

                                                          Or, changing tact, did layers of the Umbra such as the Astral plane always exist, or did early mages exploit the concept of dreams to birth the Astral plane? Qaf is actually a cleaner example, considering it actually disappeared from the Tellurian to later be reconstructed as the Digital Web, but are we to take from that it was always an artificial space, was it originally a physical space to which supernatural properties related to the practice of Connection and Correspondence were later attributed by proto-Batini, or was it always a place of metaphysical import long before mages learned to exploit it?

                                                          And, if Qaf is the Correspondence Point, why and how the actual fuck is the Shade Realm of Correspondence not just connected to another entirely different planet (that was discovered to be a planet two thousand years ago), but these two locations are sufficiently unconnected the Batini didn't even find the fuckin' place until the 16th Century? I don't intend to go reductio ad absurdum on this, but I bring this up specifically to point out and remind everyone how straight-up weird MtAs metaphysics really gets.

                                                          All the same, ultimately my point is the Technocracy doesn't hold a monopoly on using their capacity for Will-work to create, in essence, self-fulfilling prophecies born of otherwise unfalsifiable notions about the world. They're just the latest, biggest, group to do it.
