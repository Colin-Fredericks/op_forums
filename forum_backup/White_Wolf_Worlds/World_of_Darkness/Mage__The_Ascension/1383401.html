    Mage Manila, just bouncing ideas

    04-23-2020, 02:01 PM
    I'm back. I was kept away from this site by reality. Anyway, I'm currently making some material for Mage games set in the Philippines (Because I love Mage and live in the Philippines). Though I don't know when I'll be done, I was hoping to bounce some ideas off you people.

    Currently, I've made 9 NPC tradition Mages who are supposed to be establishing a major tradition chantry in Manila. Sort of a multi-tradition school, library, and designated survivor bunker for a just in case leadership gets wiped out again. I'm making a counterpart set of 10 Technocracy NPCs who work in Manila recruiting awakened mages and other people who might be of help to the technocracy. Later on, Implant to make a Tradition mage adventure involving the creation of the chantry, a Technocracy adventure and some epic Adventure involving Bakunawa, the moon eating dragon. Im hoping to eventually make more stuff. Not even sure how to show this stuff to people. Thanks in advance for any input.

      For the polished work, the Storyteller's Vault would be a good place to put your work.

      The Traditions chantry could be one short book. With 9 NPCs and a write-up of the chantry and a little on Manilla, it could be something for other Storytellers to use directly, or to take pieces of for use in their game (like a chantry description, map of the chantry or NPCs).

      The Technocracy Construct and its 10 NPCs could be another book.

      Then, your adventures could each be another book or a three-pack connecting all adventures.

      I'm not sure where you are at in the writing, but with the Technocracy: Reloaded book scheduled to hit Kickstarter on April 28, or 5 days from now, you might want to have the Technocracy material ready soon to coincide with the wave of interest in the Technocracy that Kickstarter will generate.

      There are several ways to share your work before it is final
      1. Forum threads
      2. Google Drive (set editing permissions careful)
      3. Dropbox
      4. In a Discord group or some other chat service with document sharing.

      I'd recommend you be clear on what type of feedback you're looking for and what are the areas which need the most feedback. Fans can be great playtesters, but I have found in helping with playtests and doing rules work for other games that you can spend a lot of time on corner cases and on trying to develop very specific phrasings and language instead of letting the game move on.

                                  Random thoughts.

                                  Tradition-wise, I'm guessing that what would eventually become part of the Dreamspeakers would be the oldest established faction in the islands, dating back to the pre-iron age peoples who've been there since at least around 2000BC. My (admitted limited) understanding of this period is that these peoples had linguistic and genetic connections with various Pacific Island peoples, including Polynesians, so it's possible there may be Kopa Loei groups in the Philippines going back that far as well. The modern Dreamspeakers are undoubtably as diverse here as they are everywhere else, ranging from ultra-traditionalist shaman and Catholic syncretist faith healers to modern storytellers using Pinoy manga as a medium to keep old stories alive and techno-shaman oceanographers studying sea-dwelling spirits and bygones.

                                  Maritime trade with the various kingdoms of Southeast Asia and China very likely brought Thanatoics and Akashics to the islands. I'm not knowledgable enough to guess what Thanatoics of that period would've been like, but there's probably been two other influxes of them during the American colonial period and the wave of immigrants who came from Sindhi after the 1947 partition of India and Pakistan. My understanding is that most of them are middle class merchants and financiers, so I could easily see the Lhaksmists being one of the main factions. The Akashics are probably a mix of various native ethnic groups (including their cultures and traditions as gateway arts to Do), Chinese, and later Japanese cultures, as well as all of the other groups more modernist Akashics associate with. Chinese immigration, especially in the past century or so, has probably also brought with it members of the Wu Lung and the Wu Keng.

                                  Muslim traders and the influx of both missionaries and refugees from the Portuguese and Dutch conquest of Malaysia and Indonesia brought Islam, especially to the south, and very likely the Ahl I Batin with it. Just as my personal head canon, I have placed the Philippines as the eastern most edge of the Web of Faith network prior to it getting badly damaged in the 20th century. I suspect there are still Batini covertly lurking around the country.

                                  The Spanish conquest and colonization certainly brought members of the Celestial Chorus. They've probably been as active with various syncretist groups as they are with more traditional Catholics. Another source of Chorus history in the Philippines could be the Japanese kirishitan, the Catholic Japanese who sought sanctuary in the islands during the 17th century Tokugawa purges. It's a culture that mostly died out, but makes an interesting bit of Tradition history, IMO.
                                  The Spanish also probably brought the Order of Hermes with them. Given their traditional ties with Iberia, I can easily see a number of House Flambeau as the forefront of their arrival. Probably also the more tactful members of House Jerbiton as well. They've probably got at least one chantry dating back to the early 17th century, thought it's entirely possible it could've been destroyed during WW2 or some other point.

                                  And, obviously, the modern world has certainly seen Sons of Ether and Virtual Adepts in the nation as well. Unfortunately, I don't know enough about modern Filipino culture to even guess if the Hollow Ones would have a niche there.

                                      Originally posted by Spacecat View Post
                                      As for what feedback I'm looking for right now, I'm wondering what other sorts of things people might look for when running or playing Mage in the Philippines setting. Like what would most interest them. Like, more Adventures? More NPCs? Wonders? Specific location stuff?
                                      Off the top of my head:
                                      1. Anything about local mystical traditions (both old and new), including what sort of tools/foci they'd use and Rotes.
                                      2. Talismans/Wonders, especially anything unique to the region, such as objects of local mythology and folklore (including modern urban legends).
                                      3. Native Bygones.
                                      4. Local folk beliefs, urban legends, superstitions, historical mysteries, social taboos and the sort of things you never really see in guide books or travel documentaries. I find these make good plot hooks.
                                      5. Places that might make for cool or interesting nodes or other places of power.
                                      6. Local subcultures that PCs might be a part of. (Again, this is one of those things that is sometimes hard for outsiders to find.)

Things like medical incubators made of bamboo, cheap aquaponic urban vertical gardens with plastic "fish tanks" as a water source, mango yield tripling chemicals, coconut cellulose burn patches, and artificial wood made from agricultural waste.
