    Saikou The rules outright state that Sorcerers and other Night-Folk aren't Sleepers. They don't clarify (to the best of my knowledge) whether Night-Folk can use Wonders. So the real question is "can Night-Folk use Wonders?" I'd say yes for Sorcerers and other living creatures, but only if the Sorcerer's paradigm matches that of the Wonder within a certain degree of latency. Sorcerers are a special exception and can't be considered the same as humans when figuring out whether they're sleepers or not; like I say they are very very very rarely Sleepers, as confirmed by both Mage 20th and Sorcerer Revised, so you need to come up with a Wonders system that works with that by yourself. I've given one which works and makes sense, even if there isn't text backing it up.

    As for Vampires and the like... I'm less sure. Saying yes would be simplest, but there's also some poetic justice to Dead Creatures being unable to use Wonders.

        Originally posted by 11twiggins View Post
        Do you allow Flaws? Why do you feel a need to balance characters who intentionally choose to play someone with a limp or mental illness?
        .
        I don't consider choosing sorcerer over mage to be a flaw. Everyone else is fundamentally a mage in a mage game, no matter their difference in flaws, paradigm, etc. A limping mage and a non-limping mage are still both mages. It'd be like if everyone else is driving mint 2016 Ferrari Aperta's in a race and the one guy wants to drive a 2000 Buick LeSabre with 200,000 miles, I don't feel the need to put extra effort to make sure that Buick is equally effective just because that's what he wants. Even more specifically for this example, you can take flaws to be lesser generation in Vampire. But you are still fundamentally a Vampire. Whereas this is a case of choosing to play something (a sorcerer) that is fundamentally different and worse than what the Mages are, even with it being an existing thing within the splat.

        The implication that wanting mechanical recompense for playing as a mini-Splat somehow goes against RP or organic characters is The Stormwind Fallacy, however.
        Not the intended implication. Merely that 'I'm playing an almost objectively worse mini-splat, I deserve mechanical benefits to make me equivalent to something better than me' is asking for unnecessary special treatment. There has to be a limit. If a player said, 'I want to play a normal mortal human' in a mage game, I don't feel the need to give them special treatment for doing something different. I don't fault them for wanting to play the character they want and be the equivalent of other characters/effective. I also don't think ever player desire deserves to be indulged. Wanting to play something special and wanting special treatment is too much imo.

        I agree that Ghouls and Kinfolk can be fun with no balancing whatsoever, and I've seen them both work well. However, those are differences of degrees, not kind. The Kinfolk have weaker gifts and a single form, the Ghouls have weaker Disciplines and a lesser status. Mages, on the other hand, are not just Sorcerers who start with higher levels in Paths, they don't use Paths at all.
        Sure, but you'll still need to give a fair amount of extra benefits to the person to playing the ghoul of kinfolk to make them the equivalent in effectiveness as to a vampire or werewolf. I think the point remains that if you are playing something fundamentally different in game terms, you don't necessarily deserve special treatment. But as usual, it centers back to my wish that there was an X20 sorcerer book that inherently made Paths more competitive.

        Now playing a Sorcerer with no balancing whatsoever certainly could be fun, however if an ST decided that they did want to balance it, what would you recommend? That was basically my initial request, ideas on balancing the two types of Starting Character at least a little bit.
        I was mostly addressing the questions of 'would you allow it and how would you run it.' But if I was allowing it, and if I felt the need to give compensation, I'd go with XP compensation. There really is no easy way that I see to match paths with the sheer versatility of spheres without just giving them more to work with. But the easiest way would be that. I'd have to fiddle with the numbers and expected Chronicle length to settle on a number.

            Monalfie RE Ghouls, it can be done. Just make the Ghoul antique and play the strangeness of the Prince's (or Bishop's) 150 year Ghoul. Obviously the Prince is very keen to make sure that this Coterie of misfits doesn't screw up, so he sends along his trusted servant to keep an eye on these Neonates. The Ghoul has 1 dot in a few Disciplines and great Attributes/Abilities, but little scope for growth or development in the Chronicle.

            Weirdly, with Kinfolk, the best solution is to make them a Sorcerer. It's an established bit of lore that Kinfolk produce Sorcerers, and that Garou (and Fera) are very protective of Sorcerous Kinfolk. This is assuming your ST is capable of keeping two systems in their head (most experienced STs can handle multiple systems from what I've seen).

            And I think it's a bit harsh to describe this as "asking for special treatment to make me equivalent to something better than me". In reality it's going to be "I really love the Sorcery system and have a great idea for a Sorcerer who chooses not to Awaken for personal reasons, however I don't want to be a ragdoll or dead weight in the chronicle, or rely on the Spheres of others to buff me, so can I start with a few more Path/Ritual dots and more EXP please?" I feel like there's a bad feeling here, where you're assuming that this Sorcerer PC has dark intentions and is trying to trick something out of you. Like everyone else, they're just here to have fun. If they were trying to game the system or power-game, they'd have a faithful paradigm with True Faith in some entity whose rules/teachings line up with "what I wanted to do anyways", gaining a paradigm to do whatever they want with "I pray for X" as a catch-all, and all of the (many) benefits of True Faith. That's the sort of thing STs should be skeptical of and give negative responses to, not "am Sorcerer, plz buff so I can haz fun?".

                                        Monalfie I think there is something to be said for the specific dynamic of being a Sorcerer in a Mage game. M20 decided to drop the Sorcerers are common in the Traditions etc. thing because they didn't have enough space to do it justice (there's a sidebar on it) however I do personally love the interactions between Sorcery and Magick and think that it should be a bigger theme in Mage. So it isn't as random as turning up to a Mage game and asking to play a Vampire, or turning up and saying "can we play Sorcerer", it's a person asking if they can play a Sorcerer in a Mage game, because they want the very specific dynamic that engenders. And since the systems are difficult to compare, I think it's fair for there to be some form of balancing factor, and it doesn't even need to be a complex one.

                                        So if we can agree that there is value in playing a Sorcerer among Mages, which is an interesting concept (at least to some people) and could be fun to explore, it might be worth talking about ways to make them mechanically relevant. Party Balance is a big deal, and that's why you have a Session 0. If everyone turns up with Prime, Life and Matter, and no one has Spirit or Correspondence, your Party has hit a brick wall already. Character Generation must have a collaborative element to it if you're going to get a group of Characters who can all take turns in the spotlight and come from different walks of life. It's just more interesting if they're different and are good at different things. So if there is to be any collaboration (in Vampire this is less of a problem since collaboration isn't the default) you need to consider this party balance, where everyone brings something "to the table" so-to-speak. If Julien and Clara are both Life experts who are faithful and focused on Healing, perhaps nudge one of them away from that concept to pick something else they'd like to play, being sensitive and avoiding pissing either of them off.

                                        So, how does our Sorcerer factor into this? Say they run a normal character gen and have Conveyance 5 and nothing else. They're now a Car, Teleporting Device, Horse or whatever you want to call them. Okay, this time they've got Hellfire 3 and Conjuration 2. They're now something akin to Forces 2 and Correspondence 2, except they don't have 2 extra Path dots left, and they can't do the trillion other things you can do with those two spheres. Say you gave them 1 dot in Alchemy, Hellfire, Mana Manipulation, Summoning/Binding/Warding and Healing. They can now mix up Aspirin, light cigarettes and zap bugs, sense "supernaturals" with no real idea of what they are or how powerful they are, summon animals and make them obey and get rid of a nasty cold. No matter how you mix it, they will always be... kind of superfluous to the Party after character generation. And if no one is, say, a Healer, you'll end up with a considerably weaker Healer than if you had a Mage do it, since Life 3 and Entropy 3 is a far better Healer than Path of Healing 5. So now the question is just: "why did The Higher-ups give us THIS GUY instead of another Mage?" 'Wants to be useful to the Party' shouldn't be contraversial, and I run a very inclusive table, so I personally feel the best solution is to give them more dots. Make it 10 dots. Okay, now we can start with Conveyance 5 and Hellfire 5. Now we have a guy who is VERY mobile and can carry the party places, and is also some safe paradox-free fire support. Conjuration 5 and Alchemy 5. Now we have a guy who has a mixture for every occasion sitting in the fridge back home, ready to be pulled out of his coat pocket to help when they're needed. As a result, we now have a good reason for why they are there, how they are going to contribute to 'the team', and that allows you to carry out the unique RP opportunity of being a Sorcerer assisting a group of Mages on some quest.

                                            I generally think that two Sorcerers are worth one Mage (if you are using 'Equality for All' rules). Sorcerers are fine beginning characters, but they get left behind by Mages in mixed games pretty quickly (strangely enough, they retain their utility longer in non-Mage games much longer because they bring capabilities that are difficult for other supernatural creatures to duplicate). Of course, Sorcerers retain their defensive superiority over Mages forever, so Sorcerers are useful as counterspell specialists against enemy Mages (each counterspell optimized Sorcerer can shut down two enemy Mages by splitting their counterspell dice pools).

                                            Sorcerers are probably best as NPCs in Mage games, and they make interesting threats against PC Mages when they are created under 'Equality for All' rules. The Union probably makes good use of its Extraordinary Citizens because they can be sent to deal with Reality Deviants without requiring much Enlightened Member support. Its Enlightened Members can be used for greater concerns while its Extraordinary Citizens deal with lesser concerns.

                                            For example, a ten-person squad that consisted of Extraordinary Citizens working for the Union would be a problem for five Tradition Mages because five of the Extraordinary Citizens could shut down the magic of the five Tradition Mages with counterspells while the other five would be able to capture the five Tradition Mages. If they are prepared for trouble, the five extraordinary Citizens who are not tasked to counterspell duty could have hung spells that could do massive amounts of damage (Conjuration 5 could be prepared ahead of time to bring twenty HMMVVs with four soldiers each from within 5 miles of the target, Fascination 5 could be prepared ahead of time to force a group of Mages to surrender, Fortune 5 could be prepared ahead of time to death curse a group of Mages, Hellfire 5 could be prepared ahead of time to summon lightning that deals 8 dice of aggravated damage to everyone within a 20 square foot area, Shapeshifting 5 could be prepared ahead of time to turn two enemy Mages into sloths for a scene, etc).
