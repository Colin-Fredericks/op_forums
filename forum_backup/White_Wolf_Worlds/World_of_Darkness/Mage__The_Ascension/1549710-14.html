            Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
            There is one major power for Matter 2, but it has a lot of utility and it comes with some strong limitations. Between those two, this is going to be a long section. Matter 2 has:
            There is one glaring problem with the limitations of Matter 2: "...are possible if the player rolls plenty of successes." IIRC this is the only time the core book allows you to do this explicitly. The Spheres are full of poorly explained and arbitrary lines the separate what rank you need to target specific categories of patterns or perform specific alterations, but they're solid lines. The ST gets the shitty job of picking what side of a line the situation is, but then you go from there.

            Punting that to a mid-ground of "add some extra successes" causes a lot of oddities in play (esp. since there's no guideline of how many to add).

            But then again, this is a section that labels milk as a homogeneous simple combination... which is kinda baffling when most people reading this text are people that buy milk that has been homogenized during processing. Milk, naturally, isn't homogeneous, we process it to be so because we like it better that way (and generally buy milk in large enough quantities, and formerly sold it in glass that shaking the bottles all the time got messy frequently). There's a whole rabbit hole this example opens up if the homogeneous requirement is contextual. While the idea of being able to use Matter 2 on oil and water that were just given a strong mixing before they separate back isn't that impressive, there's a lot of potential chemistry insanity this opens up that seems entirely out of keeping with the rest of the description of Matter 2.

            Despite the many restrictions, this can be quite powerful depending on what does and does not count as simple.
            Further, there's two important kinds of simple/complex that this level of material workings: ingredients and structure. And the write-up doesn't address this at all. Chocolate for example, has six different crystal structures it can form when cooling from a liquid to a solid, all of which have different properties based on those crystal structures despite the ingredients staying the same (and it gets even more complicated when you start adjusting the proportions of the base ingredients of chocolate). If someone does chocolate art (fun YouTube stuff to watch IMO), they're generally mastering chocolate mass and cocoa butter, which readily homogenize, and then tempering (how to get the crystals they want for what they're making). So you could argue that this is a simple combination of two ingredients that are only two ingredients because you separated them out from the same starting ingredient (fermented cocoa beans) in the first place. And you can argue that chocolate is very complex because it can take on so many crystal structures with so many different properties.

            A different culinary trip would be trying to decide if spices are simple or complex. Most spices are simple in the sense that they're just a refined part of a plant. But what makes spices valuable and desirable are the intensely complicated array of aromatic compounds they contain (some spices have over 200 individual aromatics that make up the flavor experience you get when eating them).

            Quick Conduction

            By changing insulation and wall materials to something conductive like copper a mage can quickly change the flow of power. The simplest use of this is to pop breakers and drop a house into darkness. With more finesse a mage could also electrocute a target or turn on a device that is not plugged in.
            This gets even worse with a laser pointer (or Forces if you don't have one handy, but then Forces could just zap someone), as lasers produce an thin homogeneous zone in air around the beam that is mildly conductive. Mage the Awakening 1e had a conductivity spell that was very heavily debated because as written you basically could buy a laser pointer and a cattle prod and zap people from a pretty substantial distance without all of the normal considerations for trying to do that (and being able to completely skip Forces).


            Non Newtonian Fluids (aka Make That Vein On Your STs Face Pop)

            There are things that are neither solid nor liquid but behave like both depending on circumstances. Turning a wall into oobleck is a quick way of melting it. Turning a pool into oobleck is a quick way of running across it.

            Of course, an ST is well within his rights to say if it isn’t a solid or a liquid you can’t turn a solid or liquid into it. That said, most of the same effect could be accomplished by transforming a solid into something that will rapidly melt, or a liquid into something that will rapidly solidify.
            I think this is a bit of a misconceptions about non-Newtonian fluids. "Fluid" in physics is not a state of matter. Matter in any state can act as a fluid, even if most people think fluid = liquid. Solids rarely act as fluids until acted upon by external forces, while liquids, gases, and plasmas usually act as fluids unless an external force acts on them.

            Sorry to get a bit gruesome, but it's a simple explanation: When a building gets blown up, the solids that get flung around act as a fluid. Thus, experts in investigating these things can actually analyze the debris fields using the same basic science as crime scene investigators analyzing the liquid blood splatters from a stabbing or shooter. In both cases the rules of fluid dynamics don't change.

            When we talk about non-Newtonian fluids, we're talking about materials that don't follow the normal rules for fluid dynamics. This can mean things like inducing a state-change instead of normal fluid motions (rapid compression of obleeck results in the liquid solidifying instead of flow out of the way of the kinetic impact). At rest all non-Newtonian fluids are whatever state of matter they normally occur at under those conditions. Obleeck is a liquid (if a very thick one). Since you can't change states with Matter 2, only liquids can be turned into obleeck (at least at rest, see my rant about milk).

            So I'd say turning a wall into obleeck really shouldn't work because walls are solids and obleeck is a liquid (but I'd also be against temporary suspensions of water and oil counting as a homogeneous liquid); but there are non-Newtonian fluids that are solids at rest a wall could be turned into. Turning a pool of water into obleeck is fine by me (hard enough to pull off just to run across something you could run around, and with Matter and Prime you could just summon up a lot of starch over the pool and make the obleeck that way instead).

            Build Your Own Archaeological Fraud

            Since radioactive material is specifically off limits, anything a mage conjures will show up as older than 50,000 years old if it can be carbon dated, or 4.5 Billion years old if it can be uranium dated. This was probably not the intention behind the radiation restriction, but it is fun nonetheless.
            I think this is a bit of a poor application of the radioactive material stipulation. If you take it to this extreme Matter 2 is useless because everything is radioactive in the sense that all matter is constantly undergoing radioactive decay. I firmly standby interpreting any reference to radioactivity in this context as meaning ionizing radioactivity or matter irradiated by it.

            That said, I don't see any reason why Matter wouldn't let you create specific mixtures of isotopes to fool decay-based dating systems. If I can summon carbon out of nothing with Prime, and I have an appropriate Focus, why can't I control how much is C13 and how much is C14?

            Explosive Decompression

            Interestingly enough a mage is only restricted to the current temperature, not the current pressure. That means a mage can create a liquid under pressure that would (and does) rapidly expand into gas when that pressure is removed. That is an ability with an explosive potential.
            You can even time delay this without Time. Think Mentos and Diet Pepsi, but you turn the Mentos into something inert to the Diet Pepsi with a duration to expire when you want the reaction to go off.

            Convert Matter to Spirit or Back Again (Basic Transmutation + Step Sideways?) Matter 2 / Spirit 3

            Contradiction Resolution - Awakening Spirits

            This is a contradiction I almost missed. Spirit 3 can already turn things to ephemera and vice versa without Pattern Spheres. That’s what it does in gauntlet crossings. Possibly the intention was it can only do this on its own during gauntlet crossings, but if so that is not a stated limitation of Spirit.

            I don’t have a good application for Spirit 3/Matter 2 in this context.
            "He may carry a few material possessions, although transmuting them as well raises both the difficulty of the roll and the number of successes required for the trip. (Normal clothing and items raise both factors by +1; bulky clothing and items raise them by +2.) That traveler must step sideways on his own; bringing large items or other people across demands a higher Spirit Rank."

            I would say that the application of Spirit 3/Matter 2 is for when you don't have higher Spirit magic. I think Spirits limitations are pretty clearly only when crossing, and then are quite limited to a few items on your person. Spirit 3/Matter 2 would override the limitations of it being part of Step Sideways and the difficult increases on the Step Sideways roll.
