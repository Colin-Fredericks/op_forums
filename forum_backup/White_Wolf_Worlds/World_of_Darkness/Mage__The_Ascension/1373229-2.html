        Personally, I don't feel a lot of need to add Sphere levels just to add "extra strenght" to magick.

        In general, the number of successes determines the strength of the magick, and that it's a function of Arete. I can see why some would want ArchMages to do the same with less effort (as MotA Forces 6 trully adds nothing to the concept of Forces, it's just a "multiplier"), but I just can't call that a "Sphere level".

        Spheres are "spheres of influence". And while it's not entirely correct to say levels don't add power, each level always change radically what you can do and what you can affect. The impossible becomes possible. MotA Forces 6 doesn't do this: I can do everything that Forces 6 can do with Forces 5, it will just take me twice the time.
        As far as ArchSpheres go, this is my opinion:

        One line that I loved of MotA was how they described Archspheres effects as "stories" (there are no Rotes for powers this grand). How could *that* work?

        Of course, MotA then goes and butchers this idea by providing some Sphere levels so closed in what they do, that they're practically Rotes: They even have the same "format" as Rotes (fluff and System, rather than only fluff like Spheres).

        I honestly don't think that having a spell that allows to cast with successes *2 or does *one* thing like Awakeening someone can be called a story. It can be used as mcguffing of a story, sure, but so can lvl 5 Sphere effects. Except that lvl 6+ can be put out of the reach of the players. But, ultimately, if they were intended as game-breaking mcguffin effects outside players, why limit them with rules?, why limit STs when WoD always assigns narrative(AKA. undefined) powers to their mcguffing characters?. Better say 6 it's narrative in that case: In a scale of 1 to 5, Porthos has a 6 - of course he can do that.

        The concept of "replace effect with story" it's, I think, very well represented by one crossover module: The Red Sign. It deals with reversing the Curse of Caine. But rather than giving you a Rote with a System and telling you: "you need to be this tall powerful to cast this effect", and then ask you to build successes in a generic ritual, the story explores various different paths that could be taken in order to perform the feat.

        Abeit this module barely involves Archspheres (only *implied* to be a thing by the Yellow Sign Grimoire), it's still interesting on the grounds the whole story it's basically the casting of a big effect, and thus it shows that it can be done.

        Now, the module involves a lot of "mckguffing hunt" and ST fiat, and that's not very Spherical...but I think that focusing on that would be missing the forest for the trees. Ultimately, all this setup needs it's for ArchMagic(k) 1º to exist (as to justify that magick can, indeed, do the impossible - if only at some nebulous, ordinarily unreachable, level) and 2º to be something more involved than the ordinary Rote.

        Ultimately, I think that magic(k) that *does the impossible* can be modeled this way.

        Now, as much as I criticize ArchSpheres, MotA introduces some very neat concepts. To mention some:
        *Metapatters it's a whole new thing, much closer in concept to "platonic ideas" than ordinary patters...that these *exist* has very interesting ramifications (what can be learnt by studying, say, the MetaPattern of Vampires in a city?), that, of couse, MotA reduces to a *bonus* w/o exploring it further.
        *Entropy 8 it's the only time in all of Mage where resurrection it's adressed in a way that's 100% compatible with Wraith. Everyone uses Life (obvious) + Spirit to resurrect by forcing the spirit back to the body: but what if there's *no* spirit left?, what if the person, like most people as per Wraith, has been devoured by Oblivion or hasn't left anything like a ghost?. The ideas of having to "fish" and stich together the fragments of the soul lost in the Tempest and to "bring life from Oblivion" adresses this (and dealing directly with these supernatural powers it's also a "new concept" rather than a mere bonus, I think).
        *Time 6+ it's mostly "time travel", but it does allow some interesting shenanigans connecting timelines. And I think the idea that there's a point in time that's like the Correspondence point it's interesting, albeit the applications seem nonexistent - the very "spell" states it's useless for the mage who casts it.
        *Mind 6+ brings the idea (that was already on Horizon) of messsing with the "collective subconscious". That adds a whole new dimension to mind (one level before that either didn't existed, or was just a fancy name for the Astral Umbra).It's also not very explored. Also opens the memories and " mind" of the Avatar. Albeit, again, nothing of this it's very explored

        And so on...

        I can even see some of these involving stories in and of themselves: Going to fish the pieces of a soul can be a story, for instance.

        The main reason to have Spheres above 5 it's to have a level of power that *can't* be reached ordinarily, but that could be used as a road to permit powers outside the normal rules. As Dataweaver says, lvl 6 as a mckguffin level that PCs can't reach while being PC seems the way to go to me.

        In a similar vein, I could see this Arete x 2 successes as the result of a mage actually *studying the universe* and discovering some power or entity that can be abused to increase her own "magical potential".
        Like, perhaps if you transform yourself into a living nexus of power with Forces 5/Corr 5/Spirit 5/Prime 5 to connect your Avatar to the pulsatic core of the Shade Realm of Forces, then you can do that "metamagick effect" of Forces 6 (with whatever consequences the ST deems appropiate to such an esoteric transformation, including maybe painful death if your body it's merely human). And the travel to "the pulsating core of the Shade Realm" would be a story...

            This.

            Personally, I'd adapt heavily from Mage: the Awakening's supplement Imperial Mysteries: not so much the 6+ dot levels (though there's stuff in there that might be worth considering) or the stuff that's specific to Awakening's cosmology (e.g., Golden Roads and the Supernal Realms); but things like Imperial Spell Factors (i.e., a way to amp up the effectiveness of the successes that an Archmaster achieves) and Acts of Imperium (detailed in the Appendix, and basically built around the notion of “archmagic as story” that Aleph just mentioned), and possibly even the notion that arch-magic costs the equivalent of experience points to use, making it something that even Archmasters use sparingly. (This isn't that different from how the creation of certain Wonders requires an expenditure of Willpower dots, which then require experience points to recover).

            It even has the notion that arch-mastery isn't merely a result of spending enough experience points for a sixth dot but is a sort of “second Awakening”, although the implementation found in Imperial Mysteries ties heavily into Awakening's metaphysics and would require substantial reworking before it could be applied to Ascension.

                            Originally posted by Aleph View Post
                            Snip
                            I just want to say that I think lots of the ideas here as presented are quite interesting. I'd also so they're not mutually exclusive with the mystic senses of forces 6 and doubling successes. When it comes down to it, the usage of an ability even in regular MtA is still a story, even disbarring results. You have to come up with the idea, make it work within your paradigm, set aside a space to do it, set up a few precautions in doing it. Then you're subjected to a roll where for better or worse you could still fail (even with doubled successes). And then you deal with the potential consequences of even a successful casting of that ritual (What did your rivals think of that? What does Unbelief think of that? How does that change your approach?). You might then say that you could do this with Forces 5 for example, it's just slightly harder. Maybe. But your character is an Archmage now, and your inclination / aptitude at bringing these new mythical effects into being is in it of itself a complication that a normal master might not experience.

                            Another sticking point for me personally, and this probably is just a me-and-three-people-I-know-thing, but I kind of hate it when ability is left explicitly as being a plot device, especially if a player wants to use it. Spheres, as freeform as they are, still have a basis and system you can use at each rank. It sort of ruins the flow of the story if you're trying to get in there and then you immediately recognise something as a plot device. Not my cup of tea.

                            You're totally right about them introducing new concepts but then deciding not to...well...explore them, which is something a hypothetical archmage supplement should do. I want to know more about this 'sub realm' of time that it's sometimes mentioned in the Time 1 Sense. I want to know what is it about Life Patterns that makes their flow of raw quintessence so interesting? MoTA did a rather excellent job at talking about what potential stories / complications come with being an Archmage, and I feel it could go a step further.


                            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                            Snip
                            I'm not too familiar with Awakening or it's cosmology, but I kind of feel this suggestion does a dis-service to both what an Archmage is and what an Oracle is. Archmages are ultimately still attainable / real figures that if you had the know how and connections could walk up to them and talk (albeit perhaps dealing with their wonderfully designated Quiet or hundreds of years of grumpy). They lead political factions and they're still, most of the time, human beings with a very honed perspective. They're attained great power and sometimes great wisdom, but ultimately that does not an Ascension make.

                            A second awakening type thing for Archmages doesn't really make sense, considering if anything most editions post 1e posit that attaining power to this level draws one away from Ascension. A second awakening should be the province of the Oracles, totally mystical and often apocryphal figures that try to encourage others to attaining actual Ascension. And they know what Ascension is or at least how to 'do' it. They have even greater political power of Archmages if they wished to use it, but they don't.

                            And again, perhaps it's just a me thing, but I find using XP as an actual resource for things like casting a spell feels kind of bad in play, especially since most of the rules are rather lax on XP (unlike in Exalted). The consequences of trying to cast, failing to cast or succeeding to cast should be the main focal point, not something so gameified as XP.


                            Just to reiterate, these are just personal musings of mine, and I think both your ideas are rather interesting. I also think it's a good exercise to hone our ideas in an open forum like this, either from critique or articulating our own thoughts :P

                                        Originally posted by Eldagusto View Post
                                        I get the feeling its not a typo, It just seems some wished it was a typo but it doesn't appear to be a typo.
                                        The OP is correct that the original, 1st edition book contains hints that Stewart Wieck was thinking in terms of there being ten levels to the Spheres, with the idea that the details of those Spheres would be worked out later.

                                        It was the only book in the entire line until Initiates of the Art that Brucato had nothing to do with. For the next five years, Brucato shaped Mage. He started by taking the glorious mess that was 1e and beating it into some semblance of a viable game. That frequently entailed throwing out or reinterpreting things that didn't work; and the idea that there were more than 5 levels to the Spheres was something he eventually came to the conclusion was a bad idea — but not before a book made it into print that had six dots on the character sheet under one of the Spheres. Brucato has referred to it as a typo; I think “oversight” or “mistake” might have been a better description of what he was trying to say.

                                        1e doesn't trump literally everything that came out over the next five years; and what the original writer was thinking doesn't matter as much as what the subsequent line editor did with the game.
