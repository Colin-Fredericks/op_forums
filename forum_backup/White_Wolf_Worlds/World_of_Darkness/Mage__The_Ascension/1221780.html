    Found a cheeky Nephandi detector

    05-24-2018, 02:44 PM
    Mages can get Spirits to do stuff for them. They can ask nicely, or force them, depending on their personality and traits. Regardless of the means, it is possible for a Mage with the Spirit Sphere to acquire the assistance of a Pattern Spider. If I had to describe Pattern Spiders simply, I'd say low-level zero imagination static drones which fortify order in the Umbra, culminating around Technocracy labs and cities and skyscrapers.

    There is a Charm Pattern Spiders may use, called Calcify. This Charm binds a mortal in webs which harden, draining physical Attributes of the Spider's choice. If you hit 0 in any Attribute you're stuck, completely immobile until someone frees you.

    This Charm never works on a Mage who has 4+ in the Entropy Sphere, or is a Marauder, or is a Nephandi, because their dynamism or entropy are too great for such a static power to hold them. Presumably this would also extend to Mages who had fallen into Jhor or a Dynamic Quiet, but I digress.

    In any case, detecting Marauders with outward (that is, ones that go beyond simple perception) Quiets, especially those of 2 or higher, isn't that difficult. And Mages who have a strong understanding of Entropy will give off a certain resonance. So if you apply this Charm to a Mage, the likelihood of a false positive isn't that high. The odds of a Mage having Entropy 4 or greater, or being a Marauder, without that already being obvious, are quite slim.

    The main problem here are false negatives. If the Mage is a Euthanatos Mage of considerable experience, you will immediately conclude that the test won't be worth much there, since they have likely developed an entropic magickal style.

    Regardless, I think it's interesting that this is technically mechanically possible, even if hogtying a potential Nephandus and dragging them into the Umbra so that a Pattern Spider you've tricked into helping you can determine whether they're a Nephandus or not is pretty inconvenient.

      Well, there's yet another thing Nephandi must contend with to be good infiltrators, one wonders how they manage...However, in my personal opinion, I don't think Nephandi should have a lot of problem with this one.
      Among other things, it's not very nice to order spirits to attack fellow mages. Mages don't trust mages that much, and thus defending violently against such a "test" should be fairly safe - it's not the kind of things courts would accept as part of the proceedings...especially when it isn't a foolproof method.

      Also, I think Nephandi may already have a counter:

      Ressonance detection it's fairly basic magick. You can do that with Spirit 1 or Mind 1. A Nephandi that doesn't know how to hide his Ressonance it's raising a red flag - Entropy 4 it's OK but Jhor is not, and Jhor it's usually detected by sensing the bad vives the mage constantly emanates.

      There are spells that can hidde Ressonance, but what has this to do with "being too entropic for calcification"?

      Being "too entropic" is covered in M20 by the concept of Synergy. Now, the "Entropic Synergy" it's little more than Ressonance that comes from the outside, formed by the way you interact with the cosmic principle of Entropy. Synergy was what Revised called Ressoance, and the two are still very close (while Resonance it's the world's answer to your manipulations, Synergy it's a response from the Triad).

      It stands to reason that you can hide your Synergy the same way you can hide your Ressonance (after all, Revised had at least one rote to hide Ressonance, and Ressonance in Revised it's M20's Synergy), and any acomplished Nephandi should have one.
      This would be a useful trick even without spiders involved, just to be able to invoque the lords of hell in your room that's next to the Chorister's room ...if the supression it's enough to fool magick senses, I don't see why such a feat can't erase or diminish the weakness of Calcification, that seems to be more of a broad weakness to certain Synergy/Ressonance than a specific immunity of Nephandi.

              Just another thing to point out, pattern spiders are probably one of the more difficult spirits to command, especially at the lower levels, because they do t really understand the concept of individuality. To a pattern spider all mages are one and the same, part of a whole that acts to undermine the work of the weaver.

              It might be possible to gain the favour of one, but using that favour will be limited to what the spider is capable of, and knowing one mage from another might be a bit beyond it. Jaglings might be a bit easier to instruct, but then harder to command or strike a deal with, and anything greater than that is probably asking for trouble.

              Generally speaking, pattern spiders make good spy drones, help with access to gadgets, making wards and fighting on the defence. But beyond that they tend to be rather limited, especially when on their own.

                      Don't forget that Pattern Spiders are part of a hive mind. Whatever you tell one is carried back to the rest, while attempting to control one will alert the others immediately. It's very dangerous to force a pattern spider into anything lest the whole cavalry will start hunting for you.

                      As for favour, pattern spiders are not as easily placated as you make them out to be. They do not want or desire except to carry out the will of the Weaver, to preserve the web and grow its influence. Even if you do something which aids them in their goal, their inability to recognise an individual makes it difficult for them to acknowledge you as responsible. To have a proper attempt at chiminage with a pattern spider, it will need to be with a jagling at least.
