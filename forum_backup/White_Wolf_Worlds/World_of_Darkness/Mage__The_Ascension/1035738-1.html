    Originally posted by AnubisXy View Post

    You have a lot of options, but at the same time you're still going to have a hard time battling something like a werewolf or a vampire. In general, dealing with a supernatural threat is not easy even for many mages. For example, to directly affect a werewolf with magic you generally need to both Life and Spirit (and possibly Prime too). A Matter mage or Life Mage or Forces mage could inflict some damage on a werewolf (possibly aggravated if the mage is a Matter mage who conjures something silver) but werewolves are pretty damn good at healing damage.

    Meanwhile a very powerful mage would simply rip the werewolf's soul of his body and call it a day. When you're looking at the capabilities of high end Mages and low end Mages you're dealing with two very, very different power levels.
    A Matter mage can burry the werewolf neck deep into asphalt and walk away, or turn air into white phosphorus, or walk away through the nearest wall. A Forces mage can fly away and there's fuck all a werewolf can do about it. Unless of course you define victory exclusively as murderizing the opponent.

        Originally posted by Kammerer View Post
        I have to disagree with this. A mage with one of the real spheres (i.e. Forces, Life or Matter) or Entropy has a virtually limitless toolbox even at level 3. Forces and Matter (and Life) cover literally everything that actually exists. The possibilities are endless.
        The "problem" about mages isn't really that the mechanics restricts them too much. Actually, it's the other way around... And sometimes the ST will just have to say "that's too much dude". A Mage's power is only limited by the player creativity and/or ST desired level of his campagn.

        So, if you wanna make Mages as enemies against your Vampires or Garou PC, just keep a Basic understand of what Mages can do, and use that as guidelines for narrativa purposes - unless you really want the players to face a guy that can conjure an Ion Cannon pulse from his satelity-talisman on space that can do 1.000 aggr damage in an area of a house.

        So, some ideas for balancing things up:

        Max ritual success: I never saw that rule before except here in the fórum (is that some new feature o M20?), but I like it (it doesn't work when making magikal items thou, just ignore it when making them and use Forged on Dragon's Fire instead for the CREATION of the item only, but keep it for the level of successes the item can have) Your maximum success equals Arete + Willpower.
        That's good, no "200 successes to open the Abyss and release all demons) stuff.
        Still, that means that initial chars will have a Max of 8... Which is A LOT, but not borderline insane.

        Narrativa: don't just say "ok, how much tass do you have? 50? Ressonance? Entropic? Ok, roll the dice... Fail, too bad, try again... Do you have another 50 Tass.. Oh, you do have? Ok, one more go... Fine, 7 rounds to make the item... Ok, your spectral scythe that steal corpus from wraiths and store as quintessence with entropic ressonance is done"... No. Go away from mechanics a little... The more unusual the thing, the harder to come buy... Make the PC having to find an Eutanathoi to teach him how to go to the Underworld, make him having to gather corpus from Specters (now, that's a challenge), make him have to WASTE a good deal of that just so he can study the substance enough to be able to deal with it...
        There is a reason after all that some effects are know only by a few... Spirit 3 allows a Mage to enter the Umbra... That also includes crossing the Shroud to the lands of the dead. So, why only the Eutanathoi know how to get there amongst the Traditions? Why only the Technocracy and the Sons of Ether know how to make Primium? Why only the Akashics know the Dim Mak?

        Now, even with all that, I'll just give two simple effects that make it a lot harder to make mages "easy prey", even if they are initial chars. Both simple level 1 effects, and anyone could make them 2 "always on" at a rather easy cost

        Danger Sense (Entropy 1) - Exactly as the name says; if there is immediate Danger, you'll get that "spider sense". You may not know what it is, but you know that IT IS.
        With a HUGE pool of 8 (!!!) successes on this staying " always on", that's a great thing for a begginer. Spend some 3 successes for duration and you're fine with a always on 5 hints of danger... Note that that sense, althouth not allowing you to see past ilusions or invisibilities like obfuscation, will still bell anyway

        Mind Shield (Mind 1) - 8 successes, minus 3 for duration, gives you an always on mental defense of 5 dots against ilusions, obfuscation, mind readings, domination, mental attacks, etc. Any mental intrusion will need at least 6 successes to bypass that, at which point the Mage can resist with his Willpower (that is never less than 5) if the power allows it. Also note that, as soon as anyone tries to get into the Mages head, he will notice the intrusion

        Now, those are two MIGTHY defenses for initial chars

It also causes necrosis of bone tissue in the jaw due to toxic interactions with phosphate metabolism. Even if you just spontaneously make it with magick, it will still be very noticeable, and anyone fleeing the site (which the hypothetical mage won't be able to do coincidentally without having some kind of residue on them, given that Garou and other monsters will be on you very up close and very quickly and combustion will violently send the products of the reaction everywhere) will probably be wanted for questioning, if seen. Afterwards, the area that the mage doused in WP will be contaminated with it, just so that you could inflict a paltry amount of bashing damage on a werewolf (Master of Fire changes fire to bashing for the scene; it is also a first rank homid Gift, so at least 2/3 of all Garou on the planet will at least have the option of picking it up whenever they have a spare moment to talk to an appropriate spirit).

                                                Nerve gas is even worse of an idea because it's even more likely to catch attention after the fact, on account of how it will diffuse even faster through the air and is even more immediately lethal... but less so for werewolves, who can use Resist Toxin to knock it down and easily reduce the damage down to survivable levels even without it. If a mage is just blithely creating VX or sarin without a second thought, they'll probably end up apprehended by magickally-inclined authorities in no time flat, because there are few threats to the sort of safe and orderly existence that the Technocracy wants to produce than nerve gas anywhere near a populated, wealthy area. Bear mace would be far more sensible choice, but keep in mind that Resist Toxin can easily deal with capsaicins. As can being a non-mammalian shapeshifter (only mammals are affected by capsaicins, so Ananasi, Corax, Mokole, Nagah, and Rokea would shrug off the effects in non-human forms).

                                                Quite frankly, I'm shaking my head that the alleged boundless creativity of Mage is being sent straight for the spontaneous appearance of uncommon and highly controlled nerve agents and Willie Peter when they could just locate a grain silo and tweak it a bit within coincidental paramters so that it creates a thermobaric explosion, or even just buying some sacks of flour and spreading them around to the same effect. Of course, the fire would still be much less effective against werewolves than it would be against vampires. Post-Revised is a bitch for pyromaniacs who want to pursue Garou.
