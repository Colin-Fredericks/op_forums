    Essences, Avatars and The Portrayal of Characters

    12-01-2025, 09:18 AM
    Lately, I have been reading Mage: The Ascension books for the first time and I wanted to start making some characters for a campaign, but I had some questions about how Essences really work and how to properly diferentiate a Dynamic Mage from a Questing Mage in their portrayal. I got confused while reading the examples of possible Avatars in Gods and Monsters and past sourcebooks didn't clear my doubts in the topic.

    In the M20 Corebook, p.266-267, there's a quick comparison before the book develops each essence:
    A Dynamic Essence Mage feels restless, driven, impassioned, and hyperactive; his Pattern companion might be more settled, reliable, one of those good head on her shoulders types with a solid approach toward life. A Questing vagabond rarely sticks around one place for long, and the enigmatic Primordial soul gazes at her surroudings with eerie calm and an agenda no one else can fathom.
    Now, here's my first question: a Questing Mage could also be restless in pursuit of the next mountain to climb; a Pattern Mage could be in his precise demeanor as eerie as Primordial Mage; and a Dynamic Mage could be passionate about mysteries as described in the paragraph of the Primordial Essence in p.267, so what key differences is there in the Essences to better contrast them with each other?

    Besides that, there's the metaphysical aspect of Mage (and Werewolf) to account for. Dynamism related to the Marauders and the Wyld, Pattern to the Technocracy and the Weaver, Primordial to the Nephandi and the Wyrm, and Questing to the Traditions and Balance. Admitedly, the M20 casts that the aforementioned connection is broad, but still, can a Garou, for example, identify the "Taint of the Wyrm" in a Primordial Mage? Morever, isn't there different aspects to each of those metaphysical concepts, like the distinction between a Defiler Wyrm and a Balance Wyrm. How those differences affect the narratives of Mage, if at all?

    The Avatars presented in Gods and Monsters, p.124-127, don't really help with answering my questions. The Manic Pixie (Questing Avatar) seems to me to be an Avatar intent on pushing the mage to see this "dynamic" aspect of life, while Mask and Stalks-The-Master (both Primordial Avatars) are both Avatars that seem to push the mage beyond the boundaries of timidity and fear respectively, which makes me think that the mage that would have those Avatars is not "eerie" per se, but someone whom their lack of confidence keeps them from their path to Ascension.

    Lastly, sourcebooks from previous editions seem to hold quite a distinct view of Essences and how they define Avatars. In Guide to The Technocracy, p.136-137, the essence of a Genius relates more to the position of a given character has with how Enlighntened Science must be applied in the world, with a Dynamic Genius being proactive, Pattern being a slow foundation, Primordial seeking to see beyond the limitations of that scientific foundation, and Questing seeking to be the vanguard of knowledge. Guide to The Traditions takes a different approach and associate paradigm, Essence and a given sphere to shape the worldview of a character, but the post is already verbose so I will not develop that.

    My main question in the end is: what really distinguishes the Essences of characters narratively, more so than mechanically or lore-wise? Examples would be really helpful.

      Don't worry about grammar, the worst posters in this regard are all native English speakers, and something between a third and a half of the forum are not.

      There's nothing to your question, really. Essences are just a late-2nd/Revised attempt to rewrite the previous Resonance trait in a way that conforms more to Werewolf's cosmology for reasons, but don't really deal with any core assumption of the game. Which is a problem Resonance already had for the entirety of the game's existence.

      Mostly they exist to flavor Magic and Quintessence, nothing else, and even that is in a rather loose manner. They have exactly the impact you want them to have in the game, nothing more or less.

      Avatar classifications are likewise arbitrary. The point of an Avatar is that they nudge the Mage towards Ascension in some manner, for whatever interpretation of "nudging", "Ascension" and "manner" you want. Any classification misses the point more than helps, and Essences are no exception.

      Garou don't usually identify triatic taint from a Mage because of Essence, unless thats the effect you want for a given character or your entire chronicle. Even their link to the "factions", or to Quiet, are later revisions and you don't really need 'em.

      So, at the end of the day the answer to your question is "whatever you want", because there's no canon answer as this is a concept thrown in in the books with very little context or explanation and are no more than rough ideas.

          Essence exists since 1rst Edition, it wasn't "thrown in in the books" any more than most of the core concepts of the game, such as "Ascencion".

          It relates a bit to Ressonance, maybe, but this really isn't a thing until Revised. And I think it just added a layer of complexity that wasn't needed to explain Essence.

          It HAS a lot to do with Werewolf because Mage since the 1rst edition had a lot to do with Werewolf and Vampire, and it has tried to sepparate since then, but the roots of the game are what they are. And it has not been consistent about this.

          Originally posted by Agrat
          Mask and Stalks-The-Master (both Primordial Avatars) are both Avatars that seem to push the mage beyond the boundaries of timidity and fear respectively, which makes me think that the mage that would have those Avatars is not "eerie" per se, but someone whom their lack of confidence keeps them from their path to Ascension.
          I think there may be a core misconception here:

          The Essence isn't describing the personality or identity of the character - that would be Nature and Demeanor. The Essence it's more like Nature & Demeanor of the Avatar

          Mages are influenced by their Avatar in certain ways - they're being "pushed", as you say. This does influence them and the way they act. It also can colour their magick, arguably, albeit this was never seriously tackled until Revised, that tied Ressonance, Essence and the Trinity. And then Synergy. But that's a load of stuff that really it's diverting from the question at hand:

          Essence it's the "Nature" of the Avatar

          On 1rst edition it was said "Nature and Demeanor defines your character in human terms, but Essence defines that aspect of your character that makes her a mage"

          So, Essence its a bit like a "magical nature", that represents your Avatar "pushing" the mage towards Ascencion.

          *Mechanically, an Avatar whose Essence it's ignored may impose a penalty on the Mage when he casts magick (there's a penalty about angering your Avatar on the list of bonuses/penalties)

          *Narratively, it sort of informs your "path" as a Mage. On the Character it would manifest as a sort of "second Demeanor"

          A "timid mage" (with a "timid" Nature & Demeanor) may be pushed towards looking "eery", by an "eery" Avatar. Even when he wasn't, and may not even like it. It also makes sense for the Scientists to pursue avenues of research that would "accomodate" his Genious.


          Relation to Werewolf Cosmology

          This is always going to be iffy, because the cosmologies never meshed.

          But, in essence, , Mage contemplates the "Metaphisical Trinity" of Entropy, Dynamism and Stasis. These are fundamental forces or principles, not spirits - The Wyrm, Wyld and Weaver are the spirits that represent these forces or principles.

          Each of the "extreme" Essences it's linked/related to one of the Trinity (Not the Triad! , Avatars aren't triatic spirits! - well, not usually at least). All things on Creation are influenced by the Trinity/Triad to some extent, but not all things are triatic spirits, nor "tainted". Essences being related to the forces doesn't mean they're an extension of the spirits that represent the forces.

          Now, the link exists (even if it's very small), and it may influence mages on Werewolf crossovers to some extent. For instance, Stasis mages may be more prone to "hearing the One Song" and becoming a Drone than Dynamic ones. But it's just a very minor tendency. Technocracy has the largest amount of Drones according to the book of the Weaver, yet, according to the same book, the Technocracy isn't "of the Weaver", and the Weaver doesn't like Mages in general because they warp reality - she's more related to Paradox than to any mage of any essence.

          This is not enough to start smelling like anything. Not more than a sandwich with too many additives, or a regular dude that works at a "droning" office job, anyway.

          Nephandi and other people that are so unbalanced to one extreme may start "smelling", depending on the ST and the book. But I don't they should, really. This should be a function of having a super loaded Ressonance

          Relation to Ressonance/Synergy

          Essence supposedly describes also how you do magick. I would say this is mostly trough the Avatar imposing penalties if you act against it's "Nature", and suggesting courses of action that are according to it.

          When Mages act uppon the world with Magick, their actions cause Ressonance. A sort of small effect magick has over you. Mages that soothe and heal people may start having a calming influence over people. Mages that kill a lot with magick start smelling like death has come. That's Ressonance

          If we had to link Ressonance narratively to any other game, I would say that it's the closest Mage has to a Humanity rating. The way you use magick reflects on how your mage, and their action, looks to the outside world. It doesn't ever require you to retire your character (except for Jhor, maybe) but it sort of acts as a consequence for your actions

          It makes sense to think that a strongly Triatic Avatar it's going to suggest Triatic-aligned actions. That being said, most Avatars aren't that triatic to begin with. Nephandi have very special Avatars, and even then not all serve the Wyrm.

          On Revised it was determined to honor the link each Essence has to one member of the Trinity by forcing each type of Ressonance. This never really worked, but was salvaged on M20 as Synergy. And, as monteparnas said, there's not much more to say about it.

          Anyway, if anything it's going to make a Mage "smell like the Wyrm", it would be having a Ressonance by doing lots of Wyrm-related things with magick (such as summoning Banes, for instance).

          And, no, having a Primordial ressonance isn't enough. Not even "enacting" it. At most you could say a Primordial Avatar it's sligthly more likely to show their mage courses of action that would end smelling (or at least wouldn't actively discourage them). But I would say having a cruel Nature/Demeanor combo (such as Monster or another cynical nature) it's going to be more conducent to end with your magick "smelling the wyrm", than having an "eery" Avatar. And in any case, it should always be uppon the character's "actions".

              About the Questing / Dynamic thing.

              This one took me back, I remember to ask the same question when I started Mage long ago.

              As I had it explained to me, the difference it's small, but substantial. It's the difference between a guy that always starts a new project before finishing the last, and a guy that's always seeking for new experiences, but commits to them.

              The Questing Avatar it's going to push you to the next mountain top, but after you've reached the last one. The Dynamic Avatar it's always going to show you a new course of action even if you're not done with the current one.

              But, remember, this is the Avatar. This is going to colour the Ascencion path, the seekings, other interactions with the Avatar, maybe the Ressonance if you chose to play with Synergy.
              But the mage has to choose how to deal with it as a human being, and because of that they may not always reflect this trait. It can be fun to play with a mage whose character it's conflictive with essence, and has to work on some "Magic(k)/Life balance" of sorts. Most mages do reflect their Essence, it's the path of least resistance after all

                              Funnily enough, Mage has had the most consistent authorial lead of the original five WoD games, with Brucato writing a bunch of the 1e supplements before being the lead developer for all of 2e (including Sorcerer's Crusade) and M20.

                              So, part of the confusion about Avatars is a purposeful in-universe thing that happened as the setting moved from the mythical ages, to the SC era, and then into the modern times. The earliest mages - and thus the founders of the philosophic groups that would lead to the different Fellowships, Traditions, Crafts, Disparates, and Conventions - were in-unity with their Avatars by default to the point where they didn't even know that Avatars were a "thing," because Avatars couldn't manifest or express themselves as anything more than stray thoughts in the mages' minds. So for thousands of years, mages this inner power was interpreted by the local beliefs that mages grew up with. Reincarnations, divine sparks, attunement with other planes of existence, etc. were what mages were taught by older mages for generations. Then something happened, and the matching of Avatars and mages, starting getting imperfect. As time passed, mages became more and more aware of their Avatars as not just part of their spiritual natures as humans awakened to magical reality, and tried to mesh this with their traditional teaches from before this. And of course, being mages that can warp reality, Avatars tried to adapt to their mages' belief systems; taking on the attributes that older mages gave them before this divide started. As time passed though, the divide continued to grow and the disparity between Avatar and mage grew to the point where Avatars became fully independent entities filtered by their mages' perspectives loosely.

                              That doesn't really explain "what" an Avatar truly is in Mage, but it can help put context to why mages themselves are confused on the matter and why modern Avatars can vary so wildly between ones that still have some form of their older nature and others have fully adapted to modern times.

Attributing the Trinity to the "factions" is a Tradition thought, as is a lot of explanations for rules big and small. Literally, theories that have identifiable origins through the setting's history and are debated by the characters in the fiction.​

                                              Personally, I prefer to downplay or even outright disregard the Essence/Triat connection. I view Primordial as being more along the overall lines of Werewolf in general: primitive, wild, and untamed; and rather than Questing being specifically about "balancing the Triat," I think of it as someone who is fundamentally goal-oriented, always working to further some cause or higher ideal.

                                              I do connect the Essences to the Resonance flavors, but indirectly: an Avatar with a Pattern Essence will provide guidance that, when followed, results in the mage taking actions that tend to generate Stabilizing Resonance; a Questing Avatar doesn't cause Devotional Resonance, but a mage guided by one tends to take actions that generate Devotional Resonance; Dynamic Avatars similarly promote, but do not cause, Temperamental Resonance; and Primordial Avatars guide their mages in ways that lead to Elemental Resonance. But ultimately, the Resonance that a mage generates derives from how the mage uses Magick, not from what the mage's Avatar Essence is.

                                              I also use the Resonances to flavor Quiet; and I don't use the model of there being three distinct types of Quiet. Rather, any Quiet will manifest in three ways, often all at the same time: delusions (seeing things that aren't there), denials (not seeing things that are there), and exaggerations (overemphasizing aspects of what's there). Some tend to lean into one of these aspects more than the others; for instance, the Negative Men (the closest thing Mage has to Changeling's Autumn People) have a powerful Stabilizing Resonance that manifests almost exclusively as denial of anything wondrous. What M20 calls Morbidity is a perfectly ordinary Quiet that's heavily influenced by the Elemental Resonance of Death, causing the Quiet to exaggerate decay and sickly pallor in everything around it. And so on.

                                              My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
