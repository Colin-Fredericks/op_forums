    06-20-2023, 04:15 PM

    The main reason the Technocracy use to control the world and the Consensus, is safety. Supposedly, they have changed the world from hell on earth, where femons and dragons flew around killing ppl and spirits were everywhere eating children, into WoD modern times, where you can be shot, exploded or even mauled by a freaking wolf-man, but hey, at least there are no umbrood flying around.
    Now, in DA, there were no paradox, there were no strict control over consensus, but there was already a Gauntlet (as always did). Here is the rating of the Gauntlet in DA



    A private home has a rating of 6. And a marketplace has 8.
    Now, this is the Gauntlet in a time where Paradox is a thing and the Tecnocracy has control over the Consensus:



    Downtown has a rating of 8. Which is the same as a busy market place in DA.
    So, after all the work of the Technocracy, all the deviants being hunted and killed, all the effort to remove anything mystical from Reality, all that changed was +1 in rating in Gauntlet?

    So, is this safety thing only Technocratic propaganda, since the Gauntlet is not much stronger than it was in DA, or are the Gauntlet rating just the authors screw up?

    Also, regarding this freedom vs. safety topic between Trads and Crats. How much of freedom should the Traditions aim in a possible new golden age future?

          Safety is a bit short. Stability, progress on many tracks, and access are more the premises that the Technocracy keep their appeal on.

          As a point of comparison, the notion of a gun is certainly a way of ensuring safety, but part of that is because a gun will work for you no matter who you are, what your mindset, destiny, disposition, morality, etc. While the technologies that the Technocracy chases can get arcane and require specialization, the Technocracy's inevitable aim is that the principles that all the technologies work at are technically accessible to the lay person. The Time Table is also a general principle that keeps the Technocracy rolling out new technologies that better address standing problems in it's more unified world, and with that a new norm that sees the masses keeping pace with that roll out. This isn't a strictly "for the people" take, simply that it's easier to have advanced technologies when your baselines are easy to use and grasp. For as totalitarian and imperialistic as the Technocracy is, that rooting of the power in the paradigm into what the unAwakened can personally handle did them a lot of wonders for controlling the board.

                          The Traditions aren't necessarily angling for their Practices to become widely accessable to the Sleepers. I mean, there are the occasional Traditionists who are interested in pursuing that goal; but by and large, the Traditions are about the Awakened: they don't wasn't to spread their beliefs among Sleepers so that the Sleepers can use their Practices; they want to spread their beliefs among the Sleepers so that their own Magick won't be Vulgar. And there's no guarantee that, say, getting the Chorister beliefs firmly ingrained into the Consensus will lead to Sleepers being able to get results by praying; that may be, and by default is, something that's reserved for the priesthood and/or “holy men”. Magick is not necessarily a trickle-down affair.

                          By contrast: for all their flaws, the Technocracy does strive for their innovations to end up in the hands of the Masses; it is perhaps their single most redeeming quality. That's what ArcaneArts was talking about in terms of the Union having a Timetable for introducing their innovations to the Masses.

                          As well, M20 mentions that “the Technocratic vision of reality holds the coincidental edge… because science-based technology is rooted in following the laws of Earthly physics.” (M20 p.613). It's not much of a leap from there to saying that Technocratic innovations are more compatible for use by the Masses than the Traditions' counterparts. That is, in a manner analogous to how the Technocracy has discovered a way to produce Devices with less effort than other mages creating Talismans (i.e., no need for personal Quintessence and Willpower to push the process along), it's entirely possible that their innovations become part of mundane reality more readily than the products of the Traditions do.

                          This is speculative, mind you, as nothing in any Mage book anywhere tells you the process by which something transitions from being Coincidental Magick to being non-magical, assuming that's even possible. The closest we get is a box in M20 Sorcerer about how a mage can use something akin to the Wonder creation rules to produce a new sorcerous Path; and while that Path isn't Awakened Magick, it's still magic. Not even the Terranorming rules in the Operative's Dossier lets you redraw the line between Coincidental Magick and Not Magic. For all we know, nothing has ever transitioned from magic to mundane, and everything that's not magical was produced entirely in accordance to the laws of Earthly physics and without any involvement of magic beyond suggesting to the Sleeper inventors what might be possible. And even if it is possible to make the transition, saying that the Traditions are just as capable as the Technocracy of doing so is a preference, not a fact. And there's some reason to think that the Technocracy does have an edge over the Traditions in this regard.

                          Note that the last two paragraphs haven't been about the Technocracy, per se; they've been about Enlightened Science, separate from the Technocratic policies. One of my own pet peeves concerning Mage is that it's far too easy to conflate the two. But in the context of M20, it ought to be entirely possible to be an Enlightened Scientist, complete with all of the benefits and drawbacks that come with it, without partaking of the toxic culture of the Technocracy. That said: the Union has been the main proponents of Enlightened Science in the setting as written; and they should get some credit for that, rather than snide remarks about how their supposed contributions to the betterment of humanity don't amount to anything important. ​​​​​​

                              I think we tend to blur two different distinctions: Awakened/Non-Awakened and Inducted/Non-inducted. Most Traditions mages would very much like it if their practices weren't locked behind the Awakened wall, but far fewer are interested in opening their practices to the uninitiated.

                              Even the Technocracy makes this distinction, really. They're not trying to make it so everyone understands the complex laws of the universe (which may or may not have been invented by the Technocracy, who knows), they're trying to make it so anyone can use those inventions that carefully exploit them. Anyone can use a carefully engineered light switch or drive a carefully engineered car, but they have very little interest in a kindergarten education unlocking the secrets of engineering (to use a hyperbolic example). They are quite comfortable with a world where not everyone is inducted but everyone uses the practice.

                              The Traditions are the other way around. They would be quite comfortable with a world where everyone is inducted, but they have problems bringing about a world where the uninducted can use their magic. This isn't (usually) for elitist reasons, either. Instead it is because for most Traditions mages the mage is part of the magic. The uninitiated can't use it because initiation is an inseparable part of using magic.

                                  First of all, while the merits and flaws of their approach can be debated, I think it is fair to say that the Technocracy does generally make an effort to protect mundane society from supernatural interference and threats (beyond their own, anyways), and they do make an effort to share the benefits of their power as widely as possible, even if they retain strict control over that power, and are probably spreading its benefits at least in part as a utilitarian move to influence the Consensus in their favour. I think the best interpretation of the Technocracy is of noble intentions corrupted by the struggle they have been fighting and the power they have acquired along the way to fight for those intentions.

                                  I think looking at how Gauntlet Ratings have changed over around 800 years or so is a bit of a narrow measure of the effects that the Technocracy has had on the lives of Sleepers. Whatever arguments you can make about what the Traditions *could* or *could have* done, the fact is that they did *not* introduce things like public education, civic sanitation, and other mundane aspects of modern life that have drastically increased our lifespans and reduced our vulnerability to the countless cruelties that life can inflict on us. I don't love the Technocracy, but I was won over to this attitude when I read a somewhat-sarcastic argument about how later editions softened their views on Technocrats because the people who gave us mass literacy and toilets probably can't be all bad. That doesn't even enter into things like how Vampires generally treat influencing the top figures of countries like the USA as off-limits in our modern world, because the world the Technocracy has created makes it *too dangerous* for all but the most powerful of Kindred.

                                  The Technocracy as an institution is absolutely interested in expanding and maintaining its own power and influence, regardless of whatever noble goals or intentions it may otherwise profess, or even authentically pursue. That's one of those punk threads that just runs through almost every World of Darkness interpretation of systems, institutions, and power dynamics.

                                  Also, just a random thought that I had on the Gauntlet Ratings, but it may be worth considering that changes in the Gauntlet have less to do with the Technocracy and more to do with the larger divide between material and spirit worlds that are a common feature of the various WoD cosmologies. Those splits are generally depicted as starting thousands of years ago, in prehistoric times, so less than a thousand years is maybe not enough time for much of a huge shift there.

                                                  There are two things that are important to consider in any discussion of Technocracy vs Traditions and what they did or did not do for humanity.

                                                  The first is what they actually did. The Technocracy did not invent public sanitation or public works. Aqueducts, sewers, mass irrigation systems, etc. all predate the forming of the Order of Reason. Many of them are even attributable to proto-Traditions. Irrigation and calendar systems are deeply intertwined and go back to cultures we would associate with the Verbena. Hospitals are at least partly an invention of the Church (they started out as free lodging, places of hospitality, hence the name. Since they didn't turn away the sick they rapidly became places that priests and doctors would go to tend to the sick). The Order of Hermes has at least as much claim on Roman civic architecture as the Order of Reason.

                                                  When the proto-Traditions had the ability to make people’s lives better, many of them clearly did. Moreover, they did so without the knowledge that doing so would change the consensus. That's not something you can say about the Technocracy as they knew from their inception about the consensus.

                                                  The second is what successes are recorded in the history books. Remember that in Mage the history we are familiar with is a sanitized one convenient for the Technocracy. In the same way that they’ve taken credit for ideas that weren’t theirs, they’ve also edited out effectiveness of other philosophies. Whether the Traditions methods were effective or not, the sanitized history will say the same thing: they weren’t.

                                                  None of this is to diminish what the Technocracy has done for humanity in Mage. They’ve had a profound impact; much of it even good. However, when the question is how effective the Technocracy is compared to the Traditions, remember that that is a fundamentally biased question and the answer is not based on what happened in our world but on what happened in the world you explore at your gaming table.
