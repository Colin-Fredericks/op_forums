    Storytelling Nephandi and Marauders

    11-17-2016, 08:07 PM
    I have the original Book of Madness, the revised Book of Madness and Mage 20. But, I'm still not sure how to run either group. Barabbi I more or less get, they subvert the organization they used to belong to. What do you do with Widderslainte?

      Widderslainte are treated as the "mean kids in your neighbourhood that caused all kinds of stuff, sometimes quite disturbing too" and those little maniacs grew up with much bigger ambitions and intentions, and to make it worse they were also Awakened.

      Some Widderslainte, however, instead struggle with that corrupted nature that stems from their Avatar. It is usually up to Storyteller what exact kind of Widderslainte is encountered - whether the one that wants to make your day a bad one, or the one seeking help and redemption - but as a rule of thumb, those are the people that /not neccessary/ have fallen into that situation by their own will. Barabbi choose to become what they are, Widderslainte are cursed with it.

      Though, the reason why there's so little Widderslainte that ever find redemption or remain on that path for long is because of how others are untolerant to them, and for a reason. Nephandi are notorious for all kinds of stuff that will make you have shivers going down your spine, and the fact that there's a guy claiming something like "hey, I need help, I think I'm related to Nephandus" would not really get them help but rather make others cautious, if not aggressive and violent towards the person.

      What else Widderslainte have to do? It is rare to find Widderslainte that tries to struggle with that inner seed of corruption, and even rarer to find someone who will understand them and try to help. They have no other choice but to join the ranks of Nephandi, else they'll most likely end up killed "so that they can be cleansed in the next life" by other Mages. Widderslainte are victims in this, often forced into fate they never chose on their own.

      As for Marauders, there's one big advise - do not treat Marauder's insanity as excuse for that Mage to be some sort of a comedian or clown. Marauder's insanity is a tragedy, often triggered by desperate events that occured in Mage's life. It's a burden they carry in lack of awareness about situation, and due to fact how often the "silent Quiet" (insanity that is hard to distingush) manifests, it is often too late to help Marauder once the insanity is apparent.

      There's a brief story about Mage who lost his wife and children, but his sorrow and despair was so intense to the point that he has fallen into Denial Quiet, simply refusing to believe they're gone. In his own world, his wife and children were still alive and well, but for others that insanity was not obvious. It was quite a heartbreaking thing to find out for other Mages once they realised what's going on - and could you even blame that man for falling into Quiet? His own consciousness refused to believe that his family is no more and he slipped into madness where he was actually happy, reunited with them once again.

      When storytelling Marauders and Nephandi, you have to keep in mind that it is in fact rare to encounter "True Evil". Everyone is a hero and a victim in their own tale, and in the end a lot of Nephandi do not even think of themselves as something evil, just pragmatic enough to see that the world is going to hell anyway. Marauders are often the victim of their own gift after they Awakened, or something pretty bad happened to them during life which triggered the state of Quiet.

      Storytelling Marauders and Nephandi as some "soulless monsters that just try to kill you every damn time" gets boring too quick and repetitive, moreover there's far more depth to them that most assume.

      Hope this helped. If anything else, feel free to ask more.

          For me, the act of turning Nephandi and getting your Avatar inverted in the Cauls doesn't make you Evil per se - it makes the feeling of fulfillment and satisfaction from higher things (helping a friend, seeing someone you love) instead come from lower impulses. So you would get the same feeling a normal person derives from comforting a loved one by instead harming or violating them. Nephandi aren't mind-controlled into evil, they are following a twisted superego.

          So for widderslainte, you can imagine someone who has these "wrong" impulses and feelings but doesn't know where they came from and didn't ask for them. They aren't forced to follow them, but denying it is like being asked to abandon someone you love. They'll never know true happiness because the only way their soul will give it to them is if they engage in destructive behavior.

              I really like the concept of Widderslainte. When done right these characters can be the most sympathetic yet diabolical NPCs you'll come across.

              When it comes to Nephandi, the central theme is that of descent. Some are in it for the cathartic pleasure of destruction, some are in it because they hate the world and want to exact terminal revenge, and some are in it because they think reality is broken and only a hard reboot will do.

              When it comes to Barabbi, the big factor for them is that of choice, why did they choose to turn, and who they were influenced by?
              Did they go down the Joker route of having one bad day?
              Was it a gradual accumulation of events, with each good things gradually stripped away from them until they were left with nothing but hatred?
              Were they betrayed by someone who created their world view, their moral code, and now question whether morality even exists?
              Were they seduced by the power offered by the Nephandi, and have a desire for subversive control?
              Have they been tricked into helping the Nephandi, and now no one will trust them, leaving only the Nephandi as their source of companionship?
              Or do they simply think the Traditions are as bad as the Technocrats, that humanity is fundamentally evil and no one truly deserves ascension?

              Widderlsainte, on the other hand, don't get a choice. This is them from the start, trapped with a tainted avatar who seeks only destruction.

              The first question for them is how well they agree with their avatar?
              Are they aware of the evil intent of their sou, or are they blissfully ignorant of the destruction their seemingly innocuous actions cause?
              Once made aware, do they try to fight it, or do they recognise it as a losing battle early on and give in?
              Do they seek help or do others try to help them before they are indoctrinated into the Nephandi properly?
              Do they like themselves? Do they act only as a puppet for their powerful avatars or are they the ones in control and merely use their avatars to fuel their instinctual desire for destruction?
              Are they afraid of what they are capable off?
              Is their avatar the only source of comfort they have from a cruel and uncaring world, one filled with those who think their death makes it a better place?

              Another thing worth consideration is who their avatar belonged to before them. Are they a long line of widderslainte or was this avatar corrupted merely a generation ago?
              How does this affect the relationship between Widderslainte and Avatar?


              Regarding Marauders, I like to split them up into a few categories.

              The first is Humans with Neurological/Mental illnesses who happen to be magic.
              If you've read "The Man Who Mistook his Wife for a Hat", you'll get a great deal of real life neurological disease stricken patients, any number of whom could be good inspiration for a marauder. The titular Man was a music teacher who steadily lost the ability to associate visual data with archetypal meaning. He'd see a glove, for example, and would recognise the colour, the shape, the fact that it had five protruding lumps, but wouldn't realise it was a glove until he accidentally put in on, at first he thought it might have been a coin purse designed to keep certain coins separate. Taken externally, a Marauder like this would live in a world of constant flux, doors would suddenly disappear, replaced with a light bulb sticking on the side of a shelf. Paintings become windows and landscapes could resemble paintings made by Magritte. So to mages that approach this man would turn into objects, or objects into animals. To the Marauder all of this is normal.
              The underlying theme of these Marauders is one of disconnection and isolation. Through no fault of their own, the world has become vastly different from others, preventing them from being able to interact with others for any meaningful length of time. They may be considered dangerous, or at the very least too unstable to rely on, and thus are socially cut off from the rest of mage society, doomed to be confined to their own bubbles of reality. Though perhaps not. There could be many Marauders who's mental illness is subtle enough that it is hard to distinguish from normal magical effects, and thus must fight against the world trying to maintain the facade of health as they constantly question whether what they are experiencing is real or their, and if even such a difference holds any merit. The fear of ostracism is ever present, as is the desire to either "cure" themselves, or find some environment where they can just be themselves free from persecution.

              The second are Stuck in Fiction.
              These guys are either fans of a specific series of books or mythos. They are so struck by these worlds that they literally see they are convinced that they are living in these worlds and may even be characters within this story. Not unlike Don Quixote, these mages will assume the role of hero in their story and will transform events around them in order to match key narrative points. They may see themselves as Peter Pan, and thus your group of mages may transform into a horde of pirates, they may see themselves as Batman, and the streets around them become Gotham, with every Mage transformed into some character, perhaps the Verbena witch become Poison Ivy, or your Virtual Adept friend into one of the many iterations of The Riddler. Explosions become frequent, and the tone can shift drastically from silver age camp to modern day horror depending on the mood of the Marauder in question.
              They aren't limited to hero's however, they could be other characters, perhaps they think themselves as Sir Lancelot, desperately searching for King Arthur. You could also make them villains from these stories, but this is much harder to do while keeping them sympathetic.
              These kinds of Marauders play on the idea of self actualisation, they want so badly to be the hero of these stories, that they lose themselves to it and forget their previous lives. To do that with an outright villain archetype will be much more difficult to sympathise with.

              Alternatively, they may see themselves as an average Joe trapped in the world they've conjured around themselves, trying to escape but unable to comprehend that it is them that creates their trap. The chronicle I'm running right now deals with such an unfortunate, trapped in the world described by H.P. Lovecraft.

              The Third are Stuck in Time.
              Like the Stuck in Fiction group, except rather than a specific narrative, these guys are convinced that they are within a specific time period, be it Victorian England, Ancient Rome, The Sengoku Jidai, or even the 80s. While it's not impossible to find such Marauders who believe themselves to be historic characters, it's more likely that they'd consider their surroundings to be analogous to the time period they find themselves in. It may not necessarily be a historically accurate period, but rather one fuelled by the beliefs and prejudices of the day. Perhaps in this Marauders Ancient Greece, gods are literally walking amongst men as they were thought to do at the time. Perhaps they see themselves in the South Africa at the height of the apartheid, and can't help but seeing anyone non-white as literally animal like, forcing such a transformation upon them, while the white people look in some way more human (sensitive topic, I know. Be aware of what's appropriate in your group).
              The theme for these Marauders is one of displacement. They live in a reality which once was, but has since been discarded. The world has moved on but the mage is trapped, either left behind or simply unable to accept the world they are presented with in favour of their current beliefs. Their madness can take the form of Denial, where anything that does not fit into their time frame simply doesn't exist or is a much older model. Theirs is an attitude of revisionism. They will most likely justify their own beliefs by virtue of their own experience, and will be entirely incapable of accepting what they don't already believe to be true. Even events they have performed themselves may have simple "not happened" if it goes against what they're used to. Not unlike those with the Sleepwalker flaw.

              The Fourth are Trauma Survivors.
              These poor Mages are those who have endured a great deal of stress from a series of traumatic events. Perhaps they were kidnapped and tortured, perhaps they were abused as children, perhaps sadistic forces kept throwing everything they could until they eventually snapped. These are mages that may have become Barabbi had they not snapped first, or instead chose specifically not to join the nephandi and thus were left no alternative than to withdraw into their own minds for safety.
              One version of these Marauders are those who have retreated entirely into a self made safe space, maybe a warm bed in their room, a cool meadow by a river, or their mother's loving embrace, anything that does not fit in with the feelings of safety are banned from entry or transformed in some way. The avatar is so consumed with healing the Mage that nothing else matters. However, their safe little world may start to fray at the edges, slowly unravelling after enough external influence forces the Mage to confront the reality they've been pushing away.
              They may alternatively see themselves as children lost in an uncaring world, and become a child in turn. They may see themselves as the one to blame for their victimhood, and thus manifest their hatred and fear as external forces, perhaps as demons, perhaps as snakes, perhaps simply as angry men with chains. These marauders are often unable to stay stable for very long and may be easily triggered into reliving the moments of their attack of seeing the people involved. Perhaps they are done being scared and hopeless and will attempt to fight their inner demons.
              The themes of these Marauders is one of being broken and dealing with the shattered pieces, and whether or not it's possible to mend completely if at all, and how much damage one may inadvertently cause to their surroundings in the process. A question of self vs those around them, and the struggle against the memories that pull at them.

              The Fifth are Sufferers of Loss.
              This is example given by Bogdan above, a Marauder who fell to madness as a way of coping with a heavy loss. It could be, as described above, that they refuse to believe their family had been lost and so may project the roles of their family members on to other people, or simply still "live" with their lost family at home. In the reverse, their family may have left them for one reason or another, and to deal with this feeling of abandonment, they invented the narrative of their death by the hands of another, perhaps creating a Revenge archetype character not unlike that seen in Memento. Another angle on this is a Mage who is convinced that they themselves are dead and are in fact a ghost. They may exhibit many facets of being a Wraith, but are not actually in the Dark umbra, and spirit magic has no effect. They may believe that they have died and their family and friends moved on without them when in reality their morbid musings drove them away. They may believe that they had been killed by someone innocent and now seek to exact misplaced revenge. They may in fact think they are dead but trapped in their own body. Perhaps their body will start to decay as they become more engrossed in this theory, perhaps they may even try to "escape" their body, but find every attempt of there's fails because they are in some way indestructible, yet at no point will their body feel likes "theirs" anymore, it's is simply a lump of dead flesh they can't seem to shake off.
              The major theme here is that of denial and an inability to accept things as they are. They have lost something, something they can't bare to be without, and so their mind and avatar invents something to make sense of this loss. Unlike the Trauma Survivors who would do anything to gain back their normalcy, these Marauders will go through any measure to keep their illusions alive. Facing the world they have rejected is not an option anymore.

              The Sixth are Trapped by Hubris
              These are very high level Mages, perhaps even Archmages who have attained a great level of understanding in how the universe operates. Maybe they've been the mastermind behind incredibly ambitious projects of Council level interest, maybe they've even spent time in a couple of Horizon realms or Deep Umbra Worlds, or maybe they are reclusive individuals obsessed with their particular area of study. The point is that they've reached a level of understanding beyond what most mages achieve, and have become so absorbed with their paradigm that literally no other form of magic or science makes sense to them. They know how the world works, they are certain about it down to the smallest drop, and that's how things simply are. These Marauders are like mini Horizon Realms in and of themselves, creating new paradox rules and Reality Zones just by their very presence. They have a "logical" explanation or reason for anything that flies in the face of their reality, and for anything that doesn't, it simply morphs into something that does, or just doesn't exist.
              These are the Marauders that all Mages are susceptible to becoming, and some may argue that the vast majority of Masters are already half way there. Their theme is that of the dangers of extremist ideology, of creating echo chambers around one's self, and of considering one's own reasoning to be infallible. It is the fate of all those who do not wish to examine their own ideas with scrutiny, and consider that perhaps they have been wrong all this time.

              That's about all I can think up right now. All of these Marauders can be down with any variety of Quiet, and nothing says you can't have a Marauder that fits into multiple categories. The big thing to remember when making a Marauder is:
              A) They are human at their core.
              B) They believe in their Quiet very strongly.
              C) They are most likely unaware of what they are, and even if they are, they can't know the full extent of their madness. None of us can.

                      I think this was addressed in the Book of Madness. Not all Infernalists are Nephandi. Those who have sold their soul to the devil for some Faustian agreement are not Nephandic, they are simply really foolish Mages/whatever who lust for power and sacrifice the long term in favour of short term gain. Infernalist Nephandi, on the other hand, are the guys who are helping such agreements take place. They don't assist their demon god because of pure personal gain, but because they believe their demon god has the right idea. If the world's going to hell anyway, might as well get a good seat.

                      In order to have your avatar inverted, you have to actively believe the nephandic ideal of destruction, it's not enough to rely on doing it for personal gain. Inverting your Avatar is a one way street to irredeemable levels, you cut off ascension from yourself, and devote your existence to cutting it away from everyone else.
                      If you want to have Power Lusting Infernalists in your game, go ahead, but they aren't Nephandi.

                      As for the K'llashaa, they may not be worshippers of Azathoth style "Destroy Everything" gods, but they are certainly for the idea of getting rid of this sorry excuse for a planet. Either that or they know a "hidden" truth that they bring upon themselves to make real to the rest of humanity, whether they want it or not.
                      Point is that they may not be for the destruction of all things, but they are certainly for bringing down humanities ideas of how they think the universe works, because "they know better", they know the words of the elder gods, the things that man was not meant to know, ideas only seen in dreams from distant stars, and once the stars are right, their glory and power will be brought back unto this world where they can reshape reality in their own image.

                          The one time I really used Widderslainte, I definitely went in for the "tragic" angle. A central conceit of the whole game was that the PCs were a group of companion souls that had been "reincarnated" (for whatever value of "soul" and "Avatar" you buy into) over a vast swath of time, and had, in fact, been critically important personages at pivotal points in Mage history. (E.g. one character had been both Baldric La Salle and Tytalus in previous lives.... 'Cause, why not? It was a "Go Big or Go Home" kinda chronicle). The game started off with the characters starting to cross lives pre-Awakening. The story that was set in the midst of all their Awakenings involved a mass homicide of a family and the kidnapping of the one survivor, an adolescent girl. They rescued her. Took care of her since she was all alone in the world. Became her ersatz parents. And.... she was one the "travelling souls". The one who had Fallen. So they got to know her when she was definitely struggling and tortured, but they were also there when she Awakened and realizes who and what she was. It was wrenching and awful (and lots of wonderful role-playing). One PC willingly gave his life to try to keep it from happening. It didn't stop it. She killed him She KNEW she loved these peoples and had loved them across many lives, but it didn't change who or what she was. She wept as she set off (flew off, actually) to start wrecking ruin on the world...

                          Overall, it went pretty well, as far as I could tell.

                          Another character in another game had some suppressed memories of a childhood where his cousin was a Bad Seed who, at age 10, did all those things attributed to budding serial killers and was able to intimidate the younger version of the character into silence. The kind was, obviously, a Widderslainte. When the two met in the course of the game, the once Bad Seed kid, now accomplished Nephandus, there was definitely some very interesting interaction. First, with the "Surely, you must remember.." Followed by the remembering. Followed by near-vendetta levels of attempted thwarting of Nephandi plans. It definitely made the whole things personal.

                          I think that's the only time I really used Widderslainte. The other Nephandi have been Barrabi. Either the "hidden disease from within" type or out there accomplishing their evil ends after having left their old groups behind. Plus a Nephandus or two that was converted so soon after Awakening that they were never really anything other than a Nephandus.

                          I really haven't used Marauders as much. I have had some ideas for future chronicles that definitely could correct that. The one I recall offhand was a lonely neglected boy who Awakened and started seeing the whole world as a computer game, quests and all. He didn't come across as different from a really focused Orphan at the start. The cabal even tried to hook him up with their VA allies to get him "trained up". Eventually his Quiet deepened and became a significant problem as he started reformatting the world around him to fit with his "game". (People do pretty destructive and antisocial things as the heroes of those kind of games...) They eventually put him into a coma and left him there while they desultorily tried to find a cure. They hadn't done so by the end of the chronicle. Later on, there was a spin-off Vampire mini-chronicle where the same players with new vampire characters in the same setting slightly later met the character again. A local Malkavian Elder who was cozy with some wraiths recognized the issues the dreaming Marauder was beginning to have on reality in his area. Said elder tried his own version of a "cure" by embracing him...

                              There's a subtle aspect to Marauders I don't see used very often. Every other Mage fights against the wall that is reality. Even the most hubristic of Mages will run into the consequences of their actions, the reactions of other people, or the misbehavior of other people. They may try to turn a blind eye, but there are thousands of little cues that draw mages back towards coherency and consistency.

                              Most Marauders have none of that. They are permanently insulated from dealing with the fallout they (and their Avatars) don't want to feel. Nothing stops them from embracing nonsensical and contradictory ideas. Nothing stops them from spiraling further and further from any semblance of an understandable world view. Many people try to make their madness relateable, either by drawing on known mental conditions or simple tragedies, but even if their Quiets start that way, there's no reason they will stay like that. They are adrift.

                              Like the sailors of old, they lost sight of land and there are no stars to guide them back.

                                      To be honest, having your avatar inverted sounds a lot like taking the red pill in Matrix:
