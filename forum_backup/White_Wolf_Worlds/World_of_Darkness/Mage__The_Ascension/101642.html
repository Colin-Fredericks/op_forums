          Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
          The Mage's Avatar dies and the Mage becomes pretty much an ordinary vampire. I think there are optional rules where he can convert the XP he spent on spheres into Disciplines, but that is it. There are no vampires with awakened magic.
          Important metaphysical distinction: the Avatar most emphatically does *NOT* die -- it merely leaves the mage's soul, much as with what occurs when the mage dies an ordinary death. Avatars can only be killed, relative to vampires, by an Awakened mage slowly gilguling himself via being a ghoul to a vampire, losing a dot of Avatar every year he is a ghoul, with the "0" year being his last chance to salvage what is left of his god-self; after that, the Avatar dies, and the mage starts losing dots of Arete until he becomes powerless.

          Tangent Topic: People who ask "Why can't vampires have Awakened Magic?" totally and utterly miss the whole metaphysical problem that comes hand-in-hand with being undead -- that the vampire loses his connection to his "soul" (in this context representing his connection to a greater, divine state). He may well develop some supernatural powers (Disciplines, in the WoD setting), but he is utterly bereft of his Avatar/god-self. (Even in the vampire mythology, Caine didn't have any particular supernatural power *until* Lilith used *her* magic to "awaken" the darker aspects of his spiritual identity that remained, and then he developed the static, conceptual powers later called Disciplines. Without Lilith having done her part, Caine would have merely been a cursed *thing* wandering the world, and vampirism would never have arose -- at least in the form recognized in WoD. Note, also, that Lilith didn't merely have powerful magic, but carried within her the very seeds of both Knowledge and Creation, allowing for such a powerful thing as vampirism to arise in the first place, particularly as this all took place in an era when magic was still abundant and reality was more fluid/flexible.)

          Just saying...

                              Far as I understand it (and I am a complete novice at Ascension stuff) when a Mage gets Embraced he stops being a Mage.

                              He dies to become a vampire. Under normal circumstances (and the embrace does not count as abnormal in this situation) when a Mage dies his Avatar detached from his soul and will attach itself to another person later on down the line (sorta reincarnation...sorta). The former Mage wakes up a vampire.

                              There is an optional rule in the Mage the Ascension Storytellers Handbook Revised that lets a Mage embraced into Vampirehood exchange his Sphere dots for dots in his in-Clan disciplines or under rare circumstances another discipline that has the most thematic link to the Sphere he is loosing.

                              From what I have been reading in those two books and another Vampire/Mage Crossover book (Blood Treachery) Vampire's have no direct way of interfering with a Mage's Avatar. Some of the older fluff (Vampire the Masquerade Storytellers Handbook Revised (1999) had a Mage's Avatar be destroyed by the Embrace. However, Blood Treachery (2000) and the Mage the Ascension Storytellers Handbook Revised (2000) both retcon that an embraced Mage just "looses" his Avatar instead of having it be destroyed.

                              The only way for Vampires to interact with an Avatar is through the Blood Bond. A blood bonded Mage (a Ghoul Mage) is slowly killing her Avatar, and it will be corrupted beyond repair over the course of years. Luckily for Mages, vampires are kinda blind to this side effect and if they have Mage servants likely don't want them loosing all those useful magic powers. Likewise, Mages who have previous experienced the Blood Bond or have high Occult and Prime 1, Entropy 3, Mind 3 and Life 4 can break blood bonds in other Mages.

                              I do believe that Mages can also stop themselves from being Embraced (and therefor loosing their Avatars) with the right precast conjunctional castings. A few different example were brought up on another thread I can't find now. Most involved the Spirit Sphere if I remember. Possibly Life, Time, Prime and Entropy were all involved in various "anti-embrace" or "counter-embrace" example people gave as well. Actually if people could post a few of those example again I would appreciate it.

                              So in short Vampire Mages are not a thing, and Ghoulled Mages are screwed and grow weaker and weaker, pretty straight forward right....

                              Except when you look at the True Black Hand/Tal'Mae'Ra stuff.

                              As its cannon that the True Black Hand have Undead Mages (Litches) that live in Enoch and do magic. This is even talked about in the Rites of Blood V20 book that coming out soon (or at least the drafts posted online by Eddy).

                              Now, it never says these guys were embraced, just that they are undead. So I've taken it as meaning they used some powerful necromantic (Entropy?) spell to become Undead but not actually die in the process and therefore keep their Avatars and Sphere magic.

                              However, I suspect the reason they never leave Enoch is that they would Paradox explode if they came back to the material world.

                                  The setting has some noticeable Vampires that where Ex-Mages. Some of the Antediluvians were were mystics and oracles, and may or may not have been mages, but were at least magical in some way, such as supposedly the Elder or Malkav. Cappadocius seemed to be a Mage, and Giovanni was a Necromancer (But Giovanni may have been a Sorcerer as his family magic seemed to be something passed along the family, though Dark Age Mage may have been appropriate for this). Tremere was thought to be about a thousand years old maybe when he died, along with the councile of 7 and most of the early Tremere. Baba Yaga was the most Notable Mage turned Vampire Witch. Al Ashrad was also in a similar situation as Baba Yaga and Tremere, he was a legendary Mage (Self Proclaimed greatest Wizard in the World, who even slew Haqium's favored childe).

                                  I am a proponent of Mages converting their power into disciplines when they die. Al Ashrad seemed to learn Mastery Dur-An-ki but we don't really get much info into this process, he was under particular circumstances where he had access to world class tutelage. Baba Yaga however instinctually converted mortal magic to potent and unique Kindred Magic. I would give her maybe Koldunic Sorcery to represent her style. Her power was said to be undiminished only changed. Meanwhile Al-Ashrad seemed to act humbled when becoming a vampire. When a mortal man he viewed himself as the Greatest Wizard in the World and slew Ur-Shulgi's elder brother. When made into Ur-Shulgi's Childe he acknowledge Ur-shulgi as his superior in magic. Part of this humbleness may be due to his undeniably lower generation.

                                  The Tremere in my opinion had instinctual development of Blood magic when changed, but worked together to cobble it into something coherent, so when converting mages their powers would convert along a familiar paradigm. They also likely increased in Dominance and Auspex to a greater degree then general Thaumaturgy because many of house tremere were manipulators and masters of the mind over other arcane secrets.

                                  I would translate a Mage's powers to the disciplines that make sense along their power/paradigm. For instance an Etherite with a hypno crytal paradigm may just develop Presence, Auspex, Obfuscate, and Dominate as a conversion to his spheres rather then just his Clan Disciplines, even if he was embraced by say a Gangrel. I also give them a greater chance of being their own offshoot bloodline.

                                                          I don't know how germane it is to talk about about which Vampire was a Mage or not. What "being a mage" means is such a porous term. Modern mages have a lot in common with mages during the Renaissance. A lot alike, but not quite. Both are quite different from Dark Ages Mage. If "being a mage" means using Sphere magic(k), I'm not sure there can be an answer to that question when there were no Spheres (which are both a game construct and a reality in the actual World of Darkness).

                                                          Were these guys from the Dark Ages mages, like Tremere and his buddies? In the modern sense, probably not. Were they sorcerers, again, in the modern sense, maybe. Were they something that was both and neither? That probably hits closer to the target. Same for the bronze magic types all twisted up in the Assamite history? Probably even less likely to fit into the modern paradigm. And Baba Yaga, back in the Neolthic? Who knows?

                                                          I'm guessing that the term Mage vis-a-vis modern magery probably MOST applies back in the whatever-never-never-long-ago of early Vampire history. Back in those days reality was probably pretty malleable. So many, if not most of the original 3rd could be considered mages in the modern sense. And not just Tzimisce and Cappadocius, who seem to have a sorta "casting spells" rep, but also Saulot (because being a seer is definitely something pretty magelike, Time Sphere?). How about Ventrue? If NWO's social engineering is "magic" how about one of the first guys to ever exercise his will to become a leader of humans and creator of cities? (Didn't they eventually give us all these guys actual names at some point?).
