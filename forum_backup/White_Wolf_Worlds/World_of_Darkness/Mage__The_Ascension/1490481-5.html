                Mage is ultimately a game of Titans in the body of mortals. And every single one of their strugles, is an existential fight. For the Technocracy, it the Union falls, the Things-Men-Was-Not-Meant-To-Know will devour everything. For the Traditions, if the Technocracy wins, humanity will be utterly enslaved and all the flame of the potential within each person will be snuffled forever. Adding to this, each one of this individuals are idealists who are convinced they are right. There's just too much at stake for half measures here, it's an all or nothing proposition where the winner takes all, and the barbarians are constantly at the gates.

                In this it truly is important the notion of humility - particularly for those who are essentially Gods in human guise. And for that same reason, it's hard not to be caught up in their own greatness - but it is essential to do so. Truly heroic Mages must strive NOT to advocate for their own worldviews, but to respect the choices that others, even the "Sleepers" which they deem as lessers, do, even when such choice means that said Mages will be at the losing side of this game.

                This is a hard balance to achieve thou, trying to accept that the Sleepers have ultimately chosen the Technocratic Paradigm and rejected the alternatives presented by the Traditions or Crafts, and still deciding to "fight the good fight" against the Union nevertheless, but not to oppose the decision of the masses, but rather to make sure that the Union will also respect the "people's decision" too. And that's something that the Traditions (and the Crafts even more so) still need to learn and adapt to

                                                The technocracy is several things. One of those things is a stand-in for the idea of a global government. Another thing is a stand in for economic elitism. Yet another is a stand-in for the transhumanist philosophy. That's part of what I was trying to get at with my earlier post about how each convention would approach the notion of transgenderism. The NWO would do what governments do, and would try to regulate the behavior of the people to resolve the difficulty; the progenitors would do what transhumanist biologists would do, and would try to adjust the biology to match the individuals desires; iteration X would do what the more mechanistic transhumanists would do, and try to upload the person's mind into a machine to perfect them. And the syndicates would see it as a revenue generator. Only the NWO would approach it from the perspective of a governing body.

                                                    My argument however is that the setting of MtA is just simply outright WRONG. The problem is that the setting is modeled in the real world; the problem with this is that in the real world, scientific knowledge is a FACT rather than a weird conspiracy of miraculous men and women to create a smoke screen to hide the truth. My smartphone works because electrons ARE indeed being exchanged inside the circuits, and the Wi Fi actually DO send radio waves to satelites in space.

                                                    In the setting of MtA, that's not true - smartphones work because everybody collectively believe that if you plug them into the grid, the "magic happens", and they have correspondence links between one another. All the talk about "radio waves" is just technobabble.

                                                    This creates a problem, because in the real world, technological advancement comes by scientific advancement, and that happens regardless of what people may believe. Relativity, Quantum Physics, all that would sound as superscience or even pseudoscience for a 19th century classical scientist, yet those were proven, regardless of personal belief. And today our GPS relies on relativity.

                                                    That would not be the case for the world of MtA, where belief is what determine possibility, rather than possibility shaping belief.

                                                    The problem is, this idea that scientificism - a blind faith on science that the Techies would want people to have - just simply aint a majority anywhere. Not even in the US or Europe. Not in the real world at least.

                                                    Im a good example of it. I am an atheist. My wife is Umbandist, which is a brazilian religion with african origin - very similar to the caribbean Vodoo or Santeria, which probably are more known for you guys in the Northern Hemisphere. She believes in ancestral guiding spirits. In the West, Christianism is most prevalent, and as any religion, it has beliefs that are a matter of faith and go beyond the scope of scientific scrutiny, as well as belief in the supernatural and in miracles. Even discounting the christian majority, there are other minor religions, all with some sense of belief into the supernatural, and loose movements, like Wicca or New Age and other kinds of esoterism.

                                                    Even people that declare themselves to be non religious or agnostic, there are still a huge plethora of non scientific beliefs, like astrology, numerology, homeopathy and some such that people adhere to.

                                                    The fact is that only a tiny minority truly adhere to atheistic skeptic materialism, which is what it could be called the Technocratic Paradigm. The truth is that the setting of MtA gets it's core assumptions wrong.
