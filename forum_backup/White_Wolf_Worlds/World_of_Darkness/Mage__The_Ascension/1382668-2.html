        Uh. Wow. Long thread.

        But, I wanna actually respond to the very first post, because I think Captain Otter is...overlooking something very, very, very important in regards to the Mage 20th rule:

        Originally posted by CaptOtter View Post
        Background on the issue: In 2nd and 3rd/Revised edition, the rules for "Acting in Concert" put forward that every 5 acolytes involved in an effect contribute an automatic success to the end result of the ritual. In M20, it's every 5 acolytes contributing a single die, and (here's the part I don't get) it's capped it at 5 additional dice.
        ...
        But this brings me to the rule at hand about Acolytes contributing to rituals. Switching it from automatic successes to dice doesn't make me feel one way or another--but capping it at a maximum benefit of five is inexplicable to me.
        In 2nd/Revised, the Cult background / Acolytes added an automatic success for every 5 Acolytes. It also did those only up to 5 successes. It added those successes to the effect. To the END RESULT of the ritual. The Cult background mentioned in Guide To The Traditions specifically mentions that it represents the Acolytes helping in said ritual:

        As listed on p. 154 of Mage:
        The Ascension, a group of believing acolytes can
        add successes to the results of an Effect, if they
        participate and truly believe in your mage’s power.
        Add five successes to any
        rituals cast when the entire cult is gathered.
        So it technically always was capped at 5 before as well, given the Cult writeup, and it added to the end result.
        Even if you ruled that you could get more than 5, it also only added to the end result.


        Now, here is the super important part about the 20th Cult background writeup and the change from automatgic success of the ritual, to dice:

        In Mage 20, the Cult background adds up to 5 dice to the CASTERS DICE POOL. That is, you get to roll up to 5 additional dice *per ritual roll*. Not the end result. Per Ritual roll.

        As detailed under Allies, Assistants, and Cults and Acting
        in Concert (Chapter Ten, p. 532 and pp. 542-543), a group
        of assistants who share a common belief add to the caster’s
        dice pool in a ritual cast with their help
        The Cult Background adds one die to the caster’s dice
        pool, up to a maximum of five dice.

        This isn't a Nerf. It's a huge *boost*.
        Double so with M20 going back to the pre-revised rules for using total successes of a roll for the aspects fo an effect, by default. In 20th, getting a group of acolytes together is more powerful than before.

                Originally posted by Ambrosia View Post
                So it technically always was capped at 5 before as well, given the Cult writeup, and it added to the end result.
                Even if you ruled that you could get more than 5, it also only added to the end result.
                All of this was actually covered already.

                The thing is that while Cult was always +5, the acting in concert rules weren't. If a five mage cabal that all follow the same magical principles and all had Cult 5 individually, they could lend their Cults to each other for +25 successes (and vulgar w/o witnesses effects would become coincidental) to any ritual rolls (and also less strict rules on ritual rolls back in earlier editions).

                As was explicitly noted in previous editions, you could have as many people helping as you could get together.

                When you can get to that level, the "at the end" aspect isn't that important. Being able to hit 26 successes minimum one on ritual roll is as many successes as most PC mages will even actually need for a spell. With PC level stuff, you're rarely going to be high difficulty, so getting at least one success is extremely likely.

                This isn't a Nerf. It's a huge *boost*.
                Double so with M20 going back to the pre-revised rules for using total successes of a roll for the aspects fo an effect, by default. In 20th, getting a group of acolytes together is more powerful than before.
                It's not bad, but the maximum potential is generally lower (esp. for PCs instead of powerhouse NPCs).

                The issue with calling it a boost is the risk of rolling as many times as you'd need to hit the same level of high successes. An mage rolling ~8 dice over 10 times has a significantly higher chance that once of the rolls fails or botches than a mage with ~3 dice rolling once or twice (and thus having the WP to spare for each roll just in case). This is especially risky with the "more dice = more of your failed rolls are botches" quirk of the system.

                Turning it into dice means one average an individual mage not pooling acolytes with others and so on has the potential to roll even better than just adding +5 successes at the end, but also a significant increase on the risks of the ritual exploding in their face (and their cult's faces); esp. again, with M20's more substantial ritual rules.

                Once you have multiple mages pooling their resources together, the math moves towards what got nerfed: the ability to gather lots and lots and lots of people together for huge automatic successes at the end.
