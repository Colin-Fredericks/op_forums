    Correspondence 4


    •••• Rend Space/ Ward/ Co-locate Self

    Creating larger holes in space, the mage can now open permanent gates between locations (10 or more successes); isolate forces, spaces, objects, or people into their own tiny realms (by combining Correspondence 4 with Forces, Life, Mind, or Matter); and ward certain locations against specific Patterns (again, combining Forces, Life, Mind, or Matter with Correspondence) or Resonance energies (combining this Sphere with Prime 4). This warding effect can impede or even block the forbidden elements from crossing into, or out of, the protected space. (For extensive details about wards, see the sourcebook How Do You DO That?)

    Using the Co-location Effect, that mage can also appear in several different places at once. To function, however, she must add Mind 1 to that Effect. Each self mirrors the original’s actions unless she also adds Life 2 to that Effect, granting independence to every self.
    Corr 4 has the following abilities:


    Lasting Gates (Rend Space)

    At Correspondence 4 mages can now create gates with duration which can be used by more than one person and can accommodate larger things. Strictly speaking the text only indicates permanent gates, but if Rank 4 can go all the way to permanent gates, it can do less too. There is no given limit on gates or the sizes of objects they can handle.


    Speedy Thing Goes In, Speedy Thing Comes Out.

    A portal that opens above itself could create a situation where someone falls forever. Add a vacuum and it can accelerate indefinitely too. Pseudo perpetual motion machines and impromptu rail guns are possible applications. The exit of a portal doesn't have to be in the same direction as the entrance, so once the thing has reached appropriate velocity another portal can place and direct the object anywhere and any direction.

    Pocket Dimension (Rend Space or Ward)

    A Mage can isolate things in spaces removed from the rest of reality. Whether this involves creating a pocket dimension and moving a space there or just separating out an existing space from everything it normally connects to is unclear and could probably work either way.

    Contradiction Resolution - Pattern Spheres

    Once again, Correspondence doesn’t need Pattern Spheres to affect whole patterns. That said, one common use of this power would be shunting a location into a pocket dimension, and that could easily involve separating buildings from foundations and other pattern splits. So there are situations that would call for pattern spheres and they come up frequently enough that it is worth pointing that out.


    Rabid Weasel, I Choose You!

    The most pop culture application is holding an animal in a pocket dimension. A wise mage will make sure the pocket dimension also contains food and air, or include Life or Time to keep the animal alive or suspended until it is needed. This is another way that supplemental spheres would be useful.

    Greater Ward (Ward)

    Greater Ward can outright bar things entering (or leaving) a space. No rolls, no difficulty to overcome, just failure. It is a qualitatively more powerful effect than Minor Ward.

    Contradiction and Ambiguity Resolution

    Now Corr 4 directly mentions warding against specific patterns and adds additional spheres in that context, but again Correspondence doesn’t need additional spheres to affect full patterns. The resolution I see is that Wards like this are not cast on their subjects. A ward against badgers isn’t cast on all badgers everywhere, instead, it needs to respond to badgers if and when they get close. Corr 4 can ward against badgers on its own, but it can’t ward against only badgers on its own. The extra spheres are used to tailor the scope so that it is more nuanced than ‘everything’ or ‘nothing.’

    Unfortunately, there’s not much guidance on what levels are needed. STs are going to have to figure that out for themselves. I gave my take on that back in Correspondence 2, [Relevant Spheres] 2. YMMV.


    Automatic Sorting

    One unconventional use of Wards is as a sorting mechanism. A mage could set a series of wards up in certain ways to separate out one substance/creature/type of person from another.

    Co-Locate Self (Co-locate Self)

    A mage can actually be in multiple places at once, which is quite useful. Well it’s actually of limited use on its own since he is still limited to the same mind and can only make the same motion in each location. He needs Mind to process multiple locations and Life to act independently in each location. Needing these spheres is not a contradiction as Correspondence has no claim on those abilities.

    Possible Contradiction

    On the other hand Life 2 doesn’t have any listed abilities that would allow multiple motions. I’m not sure what ability it is actually using here. Possibly the idea is that since Correspondence is giving the mage multiple bodies, it only takes a small alteration to make those bodies operate independently of each other, but Life 3 is Alter Self.


    Alibi

    One use of being in multiple places is a mage can create an alibi for himself. My very next thought was that if one alibi is helpful, imagine what a mage could do with nine. Mwa ha ha ha.

    Amusingly, that only seems stupid. While having multiple contradictory alibis might be super suspicious, it is also really difficult for a prosecutor to explain away.

    From elsewhere in M20:

    Teleport Anything (Rend Space) (from Corr 3)

    Correspondence 3 puts a cap on how large an item (and presumably creature) can be teleported. It states that Corr 4 is needed for larger things. So using the teleport or brief gate of rank 3 for larger things is possible at this rank.

                            Correspondence 5


                            ••••• Spatial Mutation / Co-Location
                            Distance and dimensions become child’s play to a mage at this Rank. She can distort space; alter sizes and stretch or compress objects (Matter), bodies (Life), or forces (Forces); connect different Patterns to one another across intervening space; or even superimpose places or objects on top of one another (extremely vulgar).

                            Combining this Rank with Life 3, the mage may also expand her senses to perceive many different places at once.


                            Contradiction Resolution?


                            I pulled the last sentence out because I have no idea what to do with it. A straight reading of it is that it is a complete duplicate of Corr 3’s Co-Locality Perceptions (and Corr 3 doesn’t need the Life 3 help). I don’t think it is meant to be about seeing all of the superimposed locations mentioned in the previous sentence; if someone needed magic to perceive them the spell probably wouldn’t be “extremely vulgar.”

                            Do any of you have ideas?


                            The rest of Corr 5 is sparse on description. There are multiple abilities, but not much explanation of them:


                            Distort Space (Spatial Mutation)

                            Distort space is a very general description. The most direct interpretation is that a Correspondence master can twist and alter space in just about any way. That’s reasonable, Correspondence Mastery should be capable of that, but there’s not much suggestion of what to do with this. Here are some possibilities:


                            Change the Distance of a Road or Journey

                            The mage could alter space such that a road so that a journey of a thousand miles begins and ends with a single step. Conversely, he could make a short walk down the hall nigh infinitely long. He could also connect two points or paths that are not ordinarily connected, so that a hiking path in New York leads to a trail in Germany, or a closet door in Rio opens into a small shack in Nepal. The mage could also turn space back on itself so that a road or path perpetually loops, or randomize doors so that they never lead to the same place twice.


                            Non-Linear Space

                            The mage could mess with directions so that up is left and inward is down. It is, in fairness, very difficult to visualize these. The mage could also change reality to use non-euclidian geometry. I’m hard pressed to see an advantage to doing this, but there are computer programs that simulate this kind of thing if you want to see what it is like. More classically, MC Escher and Inception style worlds are possible.


                            Added: Pluck an Airplane From the Sky (AKA I’m Crushing Your Head Redux) (quelsec)

                            At this level a mage really can act as if forced perspective were real and grab an airplane from the sky or crush someone’s head as if it could fit between thumb and forefinger. Since earlier levels of Correspondence already allow position to be manipulated, the airplane trick might not even need other spheres, though the head crush would. Correspondence cannot directly attack by itself.

                            If this ends up with the mage holding a toy sized plane in his hands, it also involves Alter Size aspect of Correspondence 5, below.


                            Added: Space Warping Shield (quelsec)

                            While Correspondence 3 could let a mage manipulate the position of a bullet to direct it around him, Correspondence 5 lets him warp space so that bullets and other projectiles pass harmlessly around him. If he wants some things to pass around him and others not, he will likely need to set conditions much like a Ward spell.


                            Separate Objects and Perceptions

                            The mage could change space so that everything sounds 10x further away than it looks, or everything appears to be 2 feet to the left of where it actually is.

                            Ambiguity Resolution - Two Separate Effects

                            Because of the formatting here, it looks like Alter Size and Stretch/Compress are two different effects. Not only that, but it looks like the pattern sphere requirements are only applied to stretching and compressing things, but not to altering sizes. Those are the issues I am trying to cover.

                            The resolution I see is that while these are quite similar powers in that both reshape space, they are different in how they treat the things occupying that space. If it appears from the outside that gross changes must be happening, but on the inside of the effect the patterns are unaltered it is Alter Size. If someone on the inside would feel themselves warping or otherwise impacted, it is Stretch or Compress Patterns. Both of these effects are things Correspondence 5 should be able to do, after all, though the latter would need help.

                            Alter Size (Spatial Mutation)

                            This one is a little more straightforward. The mage can alter the size of something by altering the space that it occupies. This would be something akin to the shrinking in Fantastic Voyage or Ant Man, or Honey, I Shrunk the Kids. This would also work for creating spaces that are bigger on the inside.



                            Stretch or Compress Patterns (Spatial Mutation)

                            Like with Alter Size, the mage can change the space a pattern is occupying, but this time in a way that changes the pattern itself. Because the pattern is altered by this, this ability would require pattern spheres. Examples of this include compressing the space so that there is no room for the pattern in question, or stretching it in a way that stretches the actual pattern. The Master’s Tissue Compression Eliminator works this way… sometimes. I also remember an episode of Voyager where someone was injured by the shifting of space around them, though I can’t remember the episode.

                            The mage could also change part of the space the pattern occupies and not the rest.



                            Connect Patterns Ignoring Distance (Spatial Mutation and/or Co-Location)

                            Normally connecting two patterns (e.g. installing a bionic implant) requires that they actually be in same place. At Correspondence 5 a mage doesn’t need to concern himself with that triviality. There are many ways this could manifest. A mage could perform a life-saving heart transplant while leaving the heart and the recipient 1000 miles from each other. He could connect one side of a door to another door on the other side of the earth (another way of doing the previously mentioned door trick). He could potentially merge multiple people into one while leaving their bodies apparently intact.
                            Note that Correspondence doesn’t have pattern altering abilities, so actually fusing two patterns still requires the appropriate spheres. This just gets rid of the requirement they be in the same place.


                            Disembodied Teleportation AKA Teleport the Head Off an Elephant


                            Because two parts of a pattern no longer need to be in the same place to be the same pattern, a Correspondence 5 mage no longer needs Life to teleport someone’s head without teleporting the rest of him. Unlike the Corr 3/Life 3 version, this doesn’t kill or even injure the target as their pattern is still whole, it is just in two different places.


                            Superimpose Places (Co-Location)

                            One of the great extremes of Corr 5, anything can be made to occupy the same space as anything else. It is quite difficult to conceptualize how this would actually work, which I suspect is one reason it is described as highly vulgar. I can think of several, but they are neither exclusive or exhaustive


                            Everything All At Once

                            The simplest I can see is that anyone in the effect interacts with every location at once. From their point of view every location would appear to be fused into one, even if that technically wasn’t true. If one location had a wall at one point and another had a passageway, the wall would still block a travellers way.


                            A Change in Perspective

                            Here each location exists slightly out of phase. People inside can see them, but only interact with one or some of them at a time. They may be able to change which ones they interact with by changing perspective or taking specific actions.

                            Added: This is a good explanation for defensive effects which make the mage seem intangible. He can be seen but attacks don’t interact with him. Aleph


                            Added: A Step Diagon-ally (Aleph)

                            The mage can take a space and place it within another space but keep the two from interacting except at specific access points. This allows for the classic hidden pockets of reality: buildings hidden in the gap between other buildings, farms hidden in closets, entire train platforms hidden within a pillar, etc.
                            The mage could also take a pocket dimension and give it specific entrances which overlap with the world at specific points, creating something bigger on the inside (the TARDIS).
                            ​


                            If you give me more suggestions I'll add them to this list.

                            Co-Locate Anything (Co-Location)

                            The reverse is also true, a mage can make a single object or place exist in multiple locations at once. He could already do this for himself at Corr 4, now he can do it for anything.

                            Everything Else (???)

                            As with all Rank 5s, Corr 5 should be able to do just about everything possible within the Sphere’s purview. The existence of Archspheres could change this, but they aren’t listed in M20.

                                                Entropy 1


                                                • Sense Flaws, Fate & Fortune / Ring of Truth
                                                Basic Entropic understanding allows a mage to see the currents of probability, spot flaws in Patterns, and note the subtle yet telling details in a person’s speech and behavior that suggest whether or not she’s telling what she believes to be the truth. Although he cannot yet control such phenomena, the mage can predict dice throws, card draws, and other apparently random events; spot weak spots in objects, people, or arguments; and use those imperfect yet profound insights to his advantage
                                                Entropy is considerably clearer in description and it is fairly easy. We are given three basic abilities at the start: Sense Flaws, Sense Fate and Fortune, and Ring of Truth. It is possible Sense Fate and Sense Fortune are two different things, but there isn’t a clear distinction.

                                                Entropy 1 can:


                                                See Probabilities (Sense Fate and Fortune)

                                                See the currents of probability is somewhat vague but at the very least that should give the mage the ability to know likely outcomes of random situations. Knowing the exact outcome of random events is listed as a possibility.

                                                There’s no detail on how specific the information is or what counts as a random event, so this power could range from mildly useful to almost game-breaking depending on ST call. For my own part, I would limit knowing the exact outcome to purely random systems and have the information gleaned get more vague as the situation gets more open ended.


                                                Predict Results

                                                The mage can predict the outcome of games of chance and other purely random systems. This could also be used to guess random numbers or pixels in television static. In the event of a particularly unhinged mad scientist the mage could even know if the cat will be alive or dead.


                                                Minor Prophecy

                                                Prophecy is more of a time thing, and even that is uncertain. Nevertheless, where the outcome is in question the mage can see the currents of fate (or likely outcomes) and act accordingly.


                                                Never Tell Me the Odds

                                                A mage should be able to quickly determine the exact likelihood of a random event, such as the odds of getting a royal flush. Added: This would also include knowing how random a system is, knowing if there is a disparity between expected odds and actual odds, and if something unknown is affecting the odds. (Heavy Arms)


                                                Unconventional Uses:

                                                Detect Cheating

                                                Because a mage can predict likely outcomes of random situations, he can also detect when the outcome wasn’t actually random. Even without knowing what was supposed to happen, he should be able to tell that it didn’t. Other aspects of Entropy 1 may even be able to add who had their fingers on the scale and how.

                                                Sense Flaws (Sense Flaws)

                                                While the initial description is limited to sensing flaws in patterns, the examples make it clear that this is a considerably more far-reaching power that can spot flaws in anything be it physical (like objects and people) or abstract (like arguments). A clever mage could use this in surprising ways, like figuring out exploits in the wording of a ward or curse, or the rules of a bureaucracy.

                                                Contradiction Resolution (Sort of):

                                                The biggest weakness of this ability is how often the book forgets to have it have an effect. Finding a weak point or a way in is often a good course of action so Ent 1 gets added to a lot of examples, but those examples frequently forget to add any benefit for incorporating the Sphere.


                                                Fine Print, CliffsNotes Edition

                                                A mage can quickly scan the EULA and figure out what is relevant to him, what is not, what is a trap, and what is a flaw he can use.


                                                Always Prepared at the DMV

                                                A mage can navigate a bureaucracy with inhuman efficiency. He can easily find what forms he needs and who he needs to speak with, and is rarely if ever caught out by needing a previously unknown form.

                                                It is quite likely that the Technocracy constantly uses this on their own processes, giving them a skewed perspective of the efficiency of Bureacracy.

                                                Detect Lies (Sense Flaws)

                                                A peculiar application of Entropy, this power specifically detects whether the target believes they are lying and specifically relies on observation. This can be quite powerful but it is more effective at detecting attempts to deceive than actual truth or falsehood.

                                                There are some decisions STs will have to make for this one that will determine how powerful it is. The example specifies a lying human, but does this power also work on animals? On robots? Does it work on writings?

                                                Missing Abilities:

                                                Detect Entropy Manipulation (Sense Fate and Fortune)

                                                Most spheres can detect the use of that sphere by other mages anyways, so this doesn’t take much justification. That said, if Entropy can sense probabilities or fate, it should be able to sense when those are manipulated. It should be able to tell if someone is blessed or cursed through detecting fate, flaws, or both.
