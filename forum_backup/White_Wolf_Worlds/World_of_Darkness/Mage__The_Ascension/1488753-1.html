                                        On closer examination:

                                        I'm doing a 3-way comparison between SRev, S:PoP, and S20. First thing I notice is the page counts: SRev has 133 pages; S:PoP has 167 pages; S20 has 113 pages. Yep; S20 has fewer pages than SRev.

                                        S20's Aspects seem a little… problematic: as I read it, the more Aspects a Path has the more successes you need to achieve in order to do anything: “When the hedge magician casts a spell, they must choose what level of each Aspect they want to include, with the default being zero — and therefore, no effect — in each.” … “When the spell is cast, it requires one success for each rank in each Aspect, plus a single success for the spell as a whole.”

                                        To illustrate my concern, take a look at Conjuration:

                                        “Aspects: Conjuration uses Aspects of Distance, Number of Targets, and Weight.” So in order to cast the simplest Conjuration spell, you need four successes: one for the spell, one for a Distance of only a few feet, one for a single target, and one for a Weight of less than an ounce.

                                        By contrast, SRev has a single success give you one level in every relevant Aspect, with additional successes only needed for additional levels after the first. So a single success in Conjuration lets you Conjure one item weighing less than an ounce over no more than a couple of feet. Same effect; but one success vs. four successes. Oops…

                                        S:PoP is similar to S20 in this regard: you need one success per level of Aspect per Aspect.

                                        Unless I'm misreading something?

                                        The simplest solution in S20 is to say that the single success needed for the spell itself gives you the first level in all relevant Aspects automatically; additional successes are used to raise Aspects to higher levels.

                                        How do I submit errata?

                                            SRev handles Witnesses in a binary fashion: if there are any Witnesses, the spell fails. In S:PoP, Witnesses impose a difficulty increase, up to +3. In S20, Witnesses remove successes, up to 5 for large crowds.

                                            SRev and S:PoP assume that magicians have an optional fuel (“Mana”) that's different from Quintessence; S20 flat-out makes sorcerers use the same Quintessence that mages use: it even renames the Path of Mana Manipulation to Quintessence Manipulation. Speaking of Mana/Quintessence, SRev has a Background called Mana; S:PoP has an equivalent Background called Technê. S20 doesn't have either; but I'm assuming you can achieve the same result by taking the Avatar Background (with the caveat that a magician's Avatar is still Sleeping, and isn't going to be giving him any guidance). That said, that's speculation on my part; S20 simply doesn't address the issue.

                                            And on the subject of Backgrounds: SRev has Cholé, Guide, Mana, and Status; S:PoP has Guide, Spirit Ridden, and Technê; S20 has Artifact. As well, S20 says “Backgrounds presented in Mage 20 p. 301-328 can work for most sorcerers, but several only apply to mages.” It doesn't go into any more detail than that.

                                                Paths: both S:PoP and S20 have all of the Paths found in SRev (though the latter renames Mana Manipulation as Quintessence Manipulation and Shadowcasting as Shadows); so I'll focus on what new Paths each introduces.

                                                S:PoP gives us Chronomancy, Mediumship, Saturnal Anima, Saturnal Manes, Scrying, Spirit Awakening, and Spirit Chasing. Saturnal Anima/Manes effectively come packaged with the Enlightened Society of the Weeping Moon, a sorcerous society lifted from W20's Wyld West supplement. Spirit Awakening and Spirit Chasing originated in… I want to say the Revised Dreamspeakers Tradition Book? Mediumship is a new name for Ephemera, a Path that made appearances in the Year of the Hunter books but somehow didn't make it into SRev.

                                                S20 gives us Ephemera, Illusion, Maelstroms, Mortal Necromancy, Necronics, and Starlight. The last four of these originated in Ghost Hunters, I think. I definitely applaud the inclusion of Ephemera and Illusion; but I think I would have preferred Chronomancy and Scrying rather than the Ghost Hunters Paths.

                                                SRev presents specific Attribute+Ability pools for each Path; S:PoP requires you to decide on a Focus for your sorcerer; for each Path, the Attribute comes from the Path and the Ability is to be chosen from one of those associated with your Practice. In S20, the Attribute comes from you Affiliation and the Ability comes from your Practice.

                                                The upside of this is that S:PoP and S20 have much more flexibility in establishing your own dice pool for your Paths; but they lose some of the ease of use that SRev's established pools provide.

                                                    Psychic Phenomena: S:PoP adds Empathic Healing, but it's otherwise the same as SRev. S20 adds Shadow, but it's otherwise the same as SRev. The big difference is how you use them: SRev gives you Attribute+Ability pools for the Psychic Phenomena; S:PoP and S20 make you spend a point of Willpower and roll your Willpower pool. I'm off two minds about this: on the one hand, Willpower can be very hard to come by; forcing psychics to spend it to use their powers severely limits their powers. On the other hand, SRev's Psychic Phenomena didn't have any drawbacks compared to its Paths, which required the magician to use what M20 calls Practices and Instruments. A possible compromise might be to say that Psychic Phenomena require an expenditure of 1 Willpower per scene: that is, spend one Willpower, and you can use any of your Psychic Phenomena for the rest of the scene.

                                                    SRev and S20 cover Paths and Psychic Phenomena; S:PoP also covers True Faith and Investments and Dark Paths. S20 presumably skips the former because M20 already had it covered, and the latter because Book of the Fallen covers it. By contrast, S:PoP builds on both: it adds a few features to True Faith, and it adds some Dark Paths for infernalism.

                                                    One final thing that S:PoP and S20 have in common: both allow mages to create Paths, adapting the rules for creating Talismans from Book of Secrets to do so. S20 calls this out as something the Technocracy specializes in: by creating a Path out of what would normally be a Vulgar Effect and teaching it to Extraordinary Citizens, they can push an Effect to be Coincidental, and then Mundane.

                                                    While I like this in principle, I have concerns about putting it into practice. In particular, I don't think that Talisman creation is the most appropriate way of modeling this. I'd prefer that the Terranorming rules from Operative's Dossier be used instead, or perhaps in conjunction.

                                                        And my final word on the subject: S20 is basically a stripped down version of S:PoP. If you have the latter, you probably don't need the former. There are a few minor differences between the two (most notably the way Witnesses are handled, and how S20 bases a Path's Attribute off of your Affiliation); but that's about it. The only reason I'd recommend S20 over S:PoP is that the former is officially endorsed, whereas S:PoP contains some material that (while nice) isn't officially sanctioned.
