    Build a Mage to fight an Antediluvian

    10-06-2021, 02:10 PM
    I am in a long running WOD campaign that is a mix of mage and vampire PCs. Everyone is powerful. (IE: 4th gen vampires, archmages) - this has been running for years and we started as humans. I'm a mage. We have encountered two antediluvians in our travels and the coterie/cabal/whatever has a pretty good relationship with one and an escalating relationship with the other (Set, who is... currently not able to take direct actions). I think we are about to tangle with others or see changes as we are approaching the time of the Camarilla / Tremere / etc. It's in the 1100s.

    Anyway, forgetting for a moment whether you think this is insane or not (it's fun, we've played together for 20 years, so we're ok with this game structure which would obviously be OP/nuts for others)...

    How would you build a mage to fight an antediluvian?

    The following special advantages exist:
    * Paradox is extremely low in this time period / environment.
    * Fast casting / on the fly casting is largely possible. (This is a pretty loose interpretation of the mage rules..)
    * Maybe assume 1 6 dot sphere, 2-3 5 dot spheres, 1-2 4 dot spheres. And Arete, Avatar 7+.

    I have Arete 8. There will likely be several vampires with 1-2 disciplines in the 8-9 range in the fight. If we are forced to fight an antediluvian it will likely be Set, although Tremere and Tzimisce are also possible.

    I am not sure we can win, so the strategy might also be a fight to escape / stalemate / imprison.

    Looking forward to your thoughts!

          So....

          1) The biggest issue is that we don't have stats for Antes to build against. The Antes are walking plot devices that are as potent as the writers/ST deem. As such it's basically impossible to mechanically suggest ways to beat them because it's hard to know what your ST will consider them capable of.

          2) That said, preparation is always a mage's biggest advantage. Esp. when it comes to archmages existing in a time of little Paradox concerns, you should probably have a suit of prepped effects where single reflexive action (even one your PC isn't consciously aware of) actives an potent effect relevant to the sudden threat. Hulking out in physical combat is obvious, but things like having a whole Demense dream fortress prepped that any magical mental intrusions cause you and the offender to be sent to your own private dreamscape where you control the rules for a mind-to-mind fight is also something for an example to be ready for; esp. with vampires.

          3) Your second biggest advantage is going to be group ritual castings. Not sure what rules you're using exactly, but if your PC group have enough of a shared style, or you've amassed enough apprentices and/or cult member in service to you, you can get a huge boost to your magical casting. Having five apprentices that can't cast what you can, but have the base Spheres you do, means +5 successes on each of your ritual rolls. So this means your prepped magic get real strong real fast.

          4) The only real example in the books of what this looks like is Voormas in Ascension (the End Times book for Revised). The stats for fighting Voormas are insane, and they're for him when he's distracted doing his big world ending ritual. Because fighting him any other time is insta-death for anyone not on his level (and he outclasses the presumed stats you posted, he can beat archmages like Senex if a fair fight... which is why Senex has never let that happen).

              I would want Time 4 or higher so that I could pause time and avoid the antediluvian having any chance to act. If you are facing an adversary with such power, any opportunity they have to use one of their high level disciplines could be instant death for the whole party.

              To actually Find the enemy, who may have incredibly high Obfuscate, I would want Correspondence, Mind and Entropy to break through the illusion - Memory's Fading Glimpse, for example. Since there is already a story established in which you know some antediluvians and have met at least one, you are already considered to be above the effect that purges the target from memory.

              Prime 5 so that you have as much Quintessence as you need to lower the difficulty of high level multi-sphere spells.

              Combine your Prime 5 with whatever level of Forces is needed to deliver effects that inflict Aggravated damage (It might have to take the form of fire, for example).

              I would say Correspondence should be one of your level 4s to cover every variation of delivering an effect from a distance.

              Cast multiple effects with Time 4 Contingency that all go off all at once, ensuring he has absolutely no way to react. He is incinerated.

              Other ideas:

              Correspondence 5, Matter 3, Forces 5 to blend his location with that of an active volcano. It's not targeted at him, so any Disciplines that would resist can be bypassed by targeting the location.

              Another idea would not be to destroy but to capture the antediluvian and gain control of him. If he's sleeping, he will have to feed when he wakes up and probably has some plan in place so that he has a supply of blood when he wakes up. With Life, Matter, Prime and Mind, you could enchant blood so that it functions like a drug. Keep him addicted to a drug that facilitates your troupe influencing him. If he's awake, use the aforementioned Time 4 and Correspondence to target the blood he's drinking when he feeds, from a safe distance.

              You could try to use the Vampire's own high level Auspex against him. Compare it too blasting sonic waves at a highly sensitive receiver. With Mind, Spirit, Correspondence and Entropy, force him to peer into something like a Nephandi Labyrinth (or equally complex thing in the universe that avoids turning the antediluvian into an infernalist who is more dangerous).

              Good luck.

                  The only thing about this I find problematic it's that the capabilities of an Ante are entirely STfiat, and thus there's no real answer outside of what your ST wants to happen.

                  But I think it's worth while to speculate what could be cool or interesting ways to approach such a feat, and what has been done in the Lore to do it.

                  One could speculate about the amount of firepower that took to kill an Ante in the canon (nukes + sunligth) to estimate the amount you need to bring (in a Mage book it was suggested the OoH could replicate the nuking of Ravnos by summoning a meteorite to stop him, for instance)

                  Or about the Lore of that specific vampire:
                  *Set may or may not be fused with Apophis, making him more spirit than vampire (if that entity can be said to be a spirit) - because permanently killing such a being may well be impossible even for your regular Arch-dude, imprisonment could work better.
                  *Set may or may not be in a cold war with his own Bane Mummies, who may or may not know his True Name (could be relevant for some forms of magick) - so you may have insider help if you're willing to make some nasty deals.
                  *Seek the aid of Horus/Osiris (that fought him in the past)
                  *I don't know much about egyptian mitology, but if you study the way Set was killed/injured there (I think Thoth made a special spear for Horus), that may provide a Bonus

                  But, ultimately, it really depends on how your table does things.

                  Personaly, I would approach this not as a Rote but as a Story - a certain amount of steps would need to be taken to bring the beast coser to it's demise, more than a single spell. Magick that vampires *normaly* don't have defense against, like Time/Space manipulation, might not be so unilaterally effective. In that sense, no single "build" would do the trick (albeit some could certainly help).

                  But maybe your table it's different, and you need to be able to do X amount of Aggravated Damage, have "soul extintion" powers to erase it's spirit, prepare a spell to teleport the ante to the sun, or some other combination of combat mechanics that I can only speculate on. Maybe the ST already finished the Ante's charsheet, and you just need to beat/sidestep whatever's in there.

                  If you have to flee, I would recommend fleeing to an "Heaven-like" place where the Ante wouldn't be welcomed. Like, in the case of Set, the court of Ra comes to mind

                  But, again, if any of this works would depend heavily on your ST's intentions. If you're not sure you can win, much less I. Soo ... try to bring in as much power as you can (have defeated Methuselah before? - well, bring in more power than when you did that), taste the waters before jumpíng, and be prepared to flee.

                                          Fight an Antediluvian? Not try to combat Antediluvian's influence, plots, or their agents, not try to stalk an Antediluvian in their place of rest (also a folly, I'll come back to this later), but try to fight one directly? Oh dear, I just think this cannot work. The way I see it, the original 3rd Generation Founders just have this privilege that they cannot be simply harmed directly, except by each other. Their physical bodies are too resistant, their mysticism too potent, and their combat stats (including regeneration ability) are too high. Even the battle forms of Mages (be it the mystical ones Traditions have listed or the Hard Suits of the Technocracy) are simply not powerful enough.

                                          There is also another factor - did you know that many Disciplines have higher level Powers that allow for instant disabling or kill? Dominate has Still the Mortal Flesh, Auspex has Psychic Assault, Obtenebration has the Darkness Within, and Dementation has Personal Scourge. Just on the possibility of an Antediluvian having one of them or a similar one, attacking one directly is a folly. Even if such a Power is not known, then simply using Dread Gaze, Majesty, Mesmerize, or Celerity might end the fight before it has really began. Remember that those Powers are used with full weight of an Antediluvian's potency (so possibility of traits of 7-10, in terms of mechanics).

                                          In regard to using Spells, I'm thinking that even a fully loaded destruction Spell wouldn't be enough to disable or destroy a being like an Antediluvian. Also consider that in setting, actions do not happen in a single instance like in the rules for casting Magic. There is gonna be a need to assume a posture, speak some words, channel power through Foci etc. While this is going on an Antediluvian can move in for the kill or to disable. Also, considering who they are, I'm thinking at least some of them could Unweave Spells and Rituals used against them. Or use some item or speak an ancient secret, that disables spellcasting around them.

                                          Just to go through some specific Antediluvians...

                                          The most suited to fighting Mages seems to be Arikel - who would think so, considering she is the Toreador Founder? But it makes sense, she was known as Ishtar, goddess of love and war, after all. With mastery of Celerity, Presence, and Auspex, she seems the most powerful in regard to taking out vulnerable and flighty targets like Mages, including multiple ones. The scariest part is that she could probably Entrance some of the attackers mid-combat. And even if some would survive, then due to meeting Arikel they would be vulnerable to being Summoned by her in the future...

                                          Ennoia - I just do not see the point of challenging her at all. With mastery of Fortitude and Protean she probably can resist damage the best out of the Antediluvian's. Also Animalism can affect mortals at higher levels, and she could have animal servants for additional defense and distraction.

                                          Ventru - Dominate and Presence, while protected by Fortitude? It is not about whether he will win, but how he wants to win.

                                          Malkav - this one is terrifying, as he has three mental Disciplines. Each of the Mages could be subjected to a separate kind of torment for challenging him.

                                          Set - considering his occult and mystical inclinations, Mages should be wary of challenging him. He might have particularly strong mystical protections or be guarded by dark Spirits, for example. And his Disciplines all taken into account provide very strong protections.

                                          (I think that he is not an Antediluvian, but still interesting to consider)
                                          Tremere - considering he was the foremost Certamen duelist in the Order of Hermes, founded the House Tremere, is the leader of Clan Tremere, and as a Kindred has access to Auspex and Dominate...it also seems a folly to challenge him.

                                          Why do your poor Mages consider fighting an Antediluvian? Can't it be done otherwise? Combating their influence, trying to dismantle their plots? If anything, maybe attacking one while she or he is resting has some sense? Though this is probably asking to be killed or captured, as well - a resting place of Antediluvian likely has protections and guardians. And she or he just might wake up ; they are several ways for a Kindred to overcome the lessened ability to act during the day. And even with lesser capabilities, an Antediluvian might just have enough potency to fight off an attack by Mages.
                                          Various quests and undertakings to gather means, items, knowledge, and power to challenge an Antediluvian directly could be attempted...but considering who they are, would any Mage want to risk it? An Antediluvian could come to know about it, after all...

                                          Maybe it is that Antediluvians are not so personally potent, but this is how I see them. And even under the assumption that they are not that mystically and physically powerful, they still have their superb mastery of Disciplines to rely on for protection.

                                                          I definitely agree that the party seems to be at the 'level' for the task; they probably don't individually add up to Porthos or Voormas, but collectively they seem capable in theory. But that's why a lot of what I mentioned had to do with their awareness and escape plans. If the Antediluvian in question has reason to suspect that they might be a threat, then they will probably make themselves as scarce as possible while engaging in a proxy war through their minions. I think that monteparnas is right that information-gathering and counter-intelligence are as important a part of this as the raw firepower, because an Antediluvian isn't going to get in a fair fight with someone that has the raw power to bring them down, so the party needs the means of setting the confrontation on their terms, whatever those may be.

                                                          The fact that we know we're dealing with Set tells us a bit more about the situation. As noted above, one of the masters of Obfuscate is going to be extremely difficult to target. Set is also an extremely powerful sorcerer, which means he's likely to be better defended against more esoteric forms of attack than some of the other Antediluvians. One possible upside is that he's not necessarily going to have the highest levels of Auspex, assuming all of them focus most of all on their Clan Disciplines. And they are at a bit of a disadvantage in that it sounds like there has been an ongoing antagonism in their relationship with Set, which puts them "on his radar" and justifies the Storyteller having them under observation. And they might be the target of a pre-emptive strike if Set suspects them of plotting against him.

                                                          Going back to the discussion of how rules are on the table, one of the big questions is how the Storyteller will interpret the power of Level 10 Disciplines. To a lesser extent, I don't know if they ever put down in writing what an Antediluvian's max Blood Pool is; the charts in the Core Rulebook always leave it a mystery IIRC. Depending on how the Storyteller chooses to rule Level 10 Disciplines and their costs could have a lot of influence on how a head-on conflict with an Antediluvian could go. In contrast, the rules for Archmages are a bit more concrete, though I'm not super familiar with them.
