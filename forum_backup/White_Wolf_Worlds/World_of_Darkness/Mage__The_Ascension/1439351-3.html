            Originally posted by AkatsukiLeader13 View Post
            Yeah in MtAs, Belief is EVERYTHING. We can say that Tradition/Technocracy Conflict is in the end pointless because we are privy to knowledge that very, very few Mages ever discover and does that do are seldom in a position to share that knowledge.
            At what scale is it pointless, though, and for whom? If a cabal/amalgam/whoever is minding their own business in their neighborhood, doing something innocuous and harmless like giving food to the homeless, and some other amalgam/cabal/whoever suddenly jumps them and blows up their chantry/construct/underground lair.... the attack was almost certainly pointless, but the guys being attacked have every reason to fight for their own lives. In the larger scheme, if it's entirely cosmically possible for multiple forms of magic and technology to all simultaneously exist and be believed in and work alongside one another, like in Starfinder, then there's no real reason for the Traditions and Technocrats to keep fighting each other. Except over all kinds of other things like democracy, or freedom of thought/thought crimes, or whether it's ethically justified to Gilgul Nephandi, or kill or enslave all the faeries everywhere. It's hard for me to believe the consensual reality is so fluid that ultimately nothing matters.

            What doesn't matter is how people achieve Ascension, right? But what if some Mages say... "Wait a minute, if all of humanity Ascends then the universe will dissolve into chaos and/or cosmic violence! We have to stop this!" and then you get people who are against the very idea of mass Ascension.

            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
            Right. But there's always the danger that you only think that someone is intolerant, possibly due to having fallen for propaganda yourself. To me, that's a big part of the horror of Mage: the Hubris. Everyone is so cock sure that they are in the right that it becomes far too easy to rationalize violence and oppression in the name of tolerance: “they're the intolerant ones, so let's squash them like bugs!” at the very least has built-in irony.
            Yeah, this is why they need diplomatic relations, so they can attempt to talk. If one side says "Stop killing us! Stop taking away our voting rights!" and the other guys say "No, convert or die!" or produce some dog-whistle* crap that amounts to the same, well then they still have a war no matter what they call it. If they respond with "Stop blowing up our XYZ factories!" then you can hope there's room for actual dialogue. Or at least try.

            Of course, nobody knows if the other guys' diplomats are lying or using illusions or who, precisely, they speak for within their larger group - nor if their faction will be unseated in some election/coup/bizarre magical accident next month. And everyone has to worry about their own diplomats being mind-controlled or possessed or replaced with clones or thrown in the Cauls on the way back home.

            The added difficulty is that if the leaders of both sides are still dead-set on war (even after the previous leaders all died), then their diplomats won't be reasonable, even if their underlings are generally less extreme.

            But the real underlying problem is if they actually don't really have any shared values, and both groups want to be the uncontested sole rulers of the world. The former seems fairly unlikely if both groups are large and diverse, but the latter... does seem to be true.


            *Dog-whistle isn't actually the word I'm looking for. I mean when someone tries to dissemble/hide/gaslight their hostile intent in some way, when talking to the person/group they want to hurt instead of to their own followers.

            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
            In terms of the Technocracy and the Pogrom, how would you know? We've been told that they stopped the Pogrom in MRev for logistical reasons, not out of ethical concerns; but if they had stopped out of purely ethical concerns (“the guys who were in favor of the Pogrom are gone! Now we can stop that nonsense!”), would the result have looked any different, in character? I don't think it would.
            You're right, they wouldn't know IC. But if Mystick Mages and people who believe in their religions are squashed anytime they try to do anything besides hide with their heads down, they know the war isn't over.

            I think the key question is if group A is fighting group B over what they are, or over what they do. And if the As don't have the resources to target every single B, no it isn't possible to tell. And this being the WoD, the underdogs will be terrified and paranoid and probably assume the worst - especially in this case, where nobody seems to have anything really driving them to make peace. It's very, very hard for someone who has spent years being persecuted and traumatized, especially with their life constantly threatened, to let down that fear even if the pressure seems to be letting up. Every instinct screams to remain hyper-vigilant for the next attack. There'll still be that fear that it's only temporary, or even a ruse. And once group A has created that utter terror in group B, they've created hatred and fear that they can't easily fix even if they do decide to make peace.

                                        As per general Realm defense. Realms have a few big advatages that make them very hard to invade by hostile forces, be them mages or spirits:

                                        *Reality it's defined by the mage who created the Realm, thus Paradox affects any invader that doesn't share the Paradigm.

                                        *It's not so easy to enter and leave Realms, for those that don't know how to do so.

                                        Any Realm it's surrounded by a Pericarp, that requires Spirit 5 to break by brute force, if I remember correctly. It's either that or entering trough a Portal built by the mages - Portals (and sometimes also the Pericarp, as was the case in Horizon) tend to be *very* well defended.

                                        So much so that Technocracy was unable to invade Horizon until a roge Hollow One ambassador betrayed the Traditions telling the Union of a back door (One of the many back doors built with less defenses that all Traditions had as a way to access and exit the Realm unofficially - Shady stuff, gotta love how mages so readily betray each other)

                                        If my understanding of the facts are correct (I didn't read Ascencion Warrior novels, only have second hand info from other books) Technocracy, with all their firepower, still were unable to just kick the front door. Too many deffenses built by too many Archmasters, I guess. Dirty betrayal doomed the Realm

                                        *Since the ppl who built the Realm define it's Reality and suffer no Paradox, there's no way to tell the powerful magicks they could have built inside it. In general mages would make the environment very hostile for those that don't know the rules of the Realm, and would have built quite a number of guardians and other defenses.
