    Trauma, Therapy and The Changing paradigm

    01-25-2017, 10:34 PM
    So, soon im going to run the Horizon War and im already considering the aftermath on the PC's.

    i was wondering if any book mentions how do mages deal with mental traumas, PTSD and such. i been thinking if its possible for a mage to recieve such a strong mental trauma that he whitdraws and even 'forgets'/fears to do any magical effects (in a way, shutting his own avatar down or put it in a 'coma').

    One of the pc's is going to have his mindshattered and will be find in earth rambling insanely and being taken into a sleeper mental facility to cope with his traumas.
    while other Pc is going to turn out to heavy drugs to try to escape the demons and horrors of the horizon war.

    also, its it possible that a mage's paradigm shifts due to heavy trauma or to cope with personal pain?

      Take a look at the rules of Quiet. If you wanted, you could have your mages get PTSD infused flashbacks which recreate themselves right infront of the mage themselves. They hear something that sounds like an explosion, suddenly it is an explosion. The see a shape which reminds them of a HIT mark, A HIT mark appears and chases your group for as long as the PTSD'd mage imagines them being around.
      But that's just for Dynamic style Quiets, you could also have Static style quiets where they are fixated on one particular version of reality, and anything that doesn't fit it simply does not exist.

      Another thing you could do is Mindscapes, where the mage is trapped within their own mental torture. Their body is effectively catatonic, and they may need a few psychonauts to help them out of it.

      These kinds of events can lead to things like Seekings, and indeed may result in a Paradigm shift if things get really twisted.

          If a Mage goes through a stressful, traumatic event, and they don't believe their existing Paradigm can or did help them get through it, they may very well shift to a Paradigm that could. It doesn't matter if the new Paradigm actually would be better for them, they just have to think it would at the time. People alter their worldviews all the time in response to changing circumstance. One of the examples cited in M20 was the fallout from the horrors of WWI, which caused mass abandonment of the previous era's optimism. It's difficult to believe in an inevitable societal progress towards Utopia - prevalent in the Victorian period - when you were made to cower in a trench, fearful of artillery shells and mustard gas, as a result of petty and short-sighted political decisions made by distant kings and diplomats.

          Magestuck: The Ascension

                              Originally posted by Saikou View Post

                              Even in Mage society, certain people slip through the cracks.
                              Slipping through the cracks is one thing. What happens next is entirely different.

                              A known mage with mental problems going missing, who may very well be in Quiet and/or gone Marauder, is invariably a multiple-alarm fire that needs to be put out RFN. Your best-case scenario is the mage stays low, snaps out of it eventually, and comes back to the fold, but that's really only going to happen if the mage retains control of their mental faculties enough to lay low in the first place. Even in the best conditions, that's about as far from a foregone conclusion as you can get, and no cabal in the right mind would ever bet on it.

                              The safe bet is always to work from the default assumption the missing mage is a walking time bomb, and a danger to themselves, nearby Sleepers, their cabal, and possibly their Tradition, if not from their own activities then definitely the Technocracy who'll be after them with a vengeance once that mage peeks their head up in the least.

                              There's a reason there's a whole lot of ink spilled about mages with mental problems, even if they're not in Quiet or Marauders, the dangers they pose to themselves and others, and the lengths other mages go to protect them and those around them, on both sides of the fence, starting from the VE Convention book on.

                                              Strangely enough, the one place where a mentally unstable Mage could go and be undetectable would be the Far Dreaming (Mystical Mage can see into the Near Dreaming without forgetting everything because of their low Banality).                                                   I think it comes down to two questions: 1) Does the cabal-mate make a habit of disappearing for extended periods of time, and 2) Does the cabal care about the particular Mage in question?

                                                  As a corallary to the latter, did the cabal have cause to believe the cabalmate is unwell? If the Mage was displaying signs of mental instability as a result of the war they just fought, that's all the cause they need to be suspicious if they suddenly disappear. Don't forget, also, that War is a known traumatic phenomenon, and one of the basic questions that might come up when they come back from it would be "Are you feeling okay?"

                                                  I'm not saying it's a sure thing, or that the cabal can't be distracted or willing to let a snap of "I'm fine!" go without pressing. But the act of showing interest in a cabalmates well-being makes a concerted search more likely if that cabalmate later goes missing. It depends on the cabal (and the players running them), and what they have going on.
