Human Resources is one way to do this; Wonders are another, as that convinces people that the Magick is in the Wonder, and that anyone who has the Wonder can do it.

        But in that regard, it only works if the Wonders are commonplace, which is hard to do without the Technocracy's edges in making Devices.
        In the modern WoD setting, the technocracy is engaged in Terra-Norming, a process of making individual Reality Zones internally uniform. This, they do in the hopes of later merging all the Earth's RZs into a single uniform Consensus without regional deviation. Even in a world with cell phones and the Internet, the dominant Mage faction is still having a hard time bringing it all together.

        In a Mythic Age type world, the geography would be divided up into many widely different Reality Zones. News travels slowly. A village can be a Reality Zone. Here, a Mage trying to make something coincidental and then commonplace isn't faced with the metaphysical atmospheric pressure of the whole world against his effect.

        Let's use an example. Classic fantasy. There are some craftsmen - and women - in the town who have skills that are vital and in demand. They make swords and only swordsmiths understand how to create a sword. Karthal the Hermetic alchemist wants to help the swordsmiths produce more swords so warriors can defend the village against goblins.

        Karthal has Mind 3, Prime 3, Matter 3. He inscribes runes on his metalworking tools to Enchant them. The Enchantment improves the tool's effectiveness, making it harder, sharper and also causing anyone who holds it to feel the power of the inscribed runes (Mind 3). He takes the tools to one of the swordsmiths while they are alone and offers the chance to use his special tools.

        Darina the Swordsmith tries out his tools. She feels the power of the runes giving the tools the improved usefulness for creating swords. "How do you inscribe these runes of power?", she asks Karthal. He replies, "Just wait until the full moon and speak these Magick words as you engrave the runes. I will go with you to the edge of the lake where the moonlight reflects its Magick. There, you can create enchanted tools of your own.". Darina agrees and meets Karthal at the time and place described.

        Now, Darina is using her own engraving instruments. As she goes through the ritual, Karthal casts the Magick silently, Enchanting her tools. He is using her actions as an Acolyte to focus his Magick. She now has tools that are enchanted for utility and have the same mind Effect that makes the user feel power coming from the runes. One by one, Karthal and Darina involve all of the swordsmiths in town in the moonlight ritual.

        Now, Karthal wants to build on the Mind enchantment while the smiths are not under its direct influence - so, not holding the engraved tools. He goes to the tavern where everyone goes to drink and socialize. He asks about how sword production is going. The smiths, who have put their tools away at home, thank Karthal for showing them the engraving ritual. Others in the vicinity overhear and inquire about these runes. The swordsmiths explain the new technique for enchanting. A bystander says, "Is that how we ended up with so many superior swords? Ha! Now we can deal with those goblins. If it weren't for your runes, we would be facing being eaten by the goblins. So glad this new method exists."

        A young man seeking a trade hears of the power of the rune-using smiths and asks to be Darina's apprentice. He already believes in the effectiveness of the runes, because everyone in the village is sharing the good news. Darina shows him the ritual. The enchantment takes effect as he performs the ritual. It is no longer a Magickal effect, but physical law of the local Reality Zone that can be used as needed.

            HorizonParty2021 That's the spirit.

            However, I think the process will take a little more effort and time. If everyone was able to teach magick with some little trickery on the local comunity, all mage acolites would be able to do it. However WoD shows us that actually being able to do Linear Magic takes some serious effort and willpower on part of the sleeper. And the mechanization of magick that the Technocracy made possible it's a step further in that direction.

            Darina the Swordsmith believing she can imbue the swords with magick because you did a trick and convincing the local people that she can do it are both steps on the rigth direction, but I think Darina's apprentice can't be just a random person. The apprentice will need to be actually compromised and have some serious willpower to be able to learn the enchantment, becoming a linear mage. As more people do that, the thing will become easier to teach, and eventually commonplace (or almost commonplace, in the way sleepers can learn medicine and use it, but they still need to study)

            That's the way Technocracy does it: First they have mages show that the experiment migth work to sleeper agents (altrough only in controled lab environments). They teach these "experts" to do it on their own (teaching linear magic). At the same time they advertise the method, and latter start to send their experts - first into places where people it's already very much convinced of the Technocratical Paradigm (academia, hig-tech corporations, and such - institutions that most likely are under supervision of the Technocracy), then into the public domain. After that, it becomes commonplace.

            I don't think the Hermetic approach would be so much trough Wonders as trough teaching (albeit I don't think that would be impossible, especially if House Verditus becomes more powerful).

            This process will likely take more time for Hermetics than it did for the Technocracy, because the latter rushed towards this revolutions as one of their foundational goals (to give power to the common man was deeply ingrained in Craftmason philosophy), but I think - I'm almost certain - that it would happen eventually. Because that's how humanity rolls, we like to live better lives within our understanding of what's better.

            Hermetics already teach people to use linear magic. If they had more people, I could see this education system branching into lesser lodges of linear mages that would be left in charge of improving the life of the comunities in little ways (building better houses, making lesser wards, helping with the goblins...you know).

            Since hermetics tend to be very absorbed in their own enligthenment, and treat linear magic as lesser, probably the next step to make things commonplace would have to be an initiative coming from these lesser lodges - these would need more people becuse the small and mundane problems are quite a lot, and Hermetics migth consent even uninitiated to be shown some lesser tricks. This could end cementing some sort of "technology" - runes, enchantments, and such - as something almost anyone can do and benefit from (like anyone can do maths).
            Because people likes stuff, who knows, maybe mystical Wonders start becoming common tech too - but then, maybe not: maybe society evolves to become less materialistic and more intelectual or mystical under Hermtic Paradigm

Place the runes on the sword and tell the warrior wielding it to invoke it's power through an activation word and force of will. Those who believe get flaming swords. Those who don't spend the WP are the goblins' preferred target. In this case, the goblins are an existing threat. A truly devious NPC could manufacture the danger to weed out non-believers.

                        As others have mentioned its about general welfare/standard of living improvements (not just food, but health and safety) generally to the point that you minimize mortality rates and maximize birthrates. That sort of approach (again as others note) seems best suited to the Order of Reason/Technocratic Union because you're talking 'mass production' of magical solutions and that's more or less what they've been trying to do. I don't think it means you have to make everyone an 'active' user of magic so much as a consumer - the key things is that they benefit from the effects on a large enough scale that human gains outstrip losses by some margin.) Consensus can help here, as can making use of Enlightened Citizens (that's the term for technocratic 'sorcerer-equivalents') to help production rates. Not everyone needs to know how to make a car, or a gun, or manufacture aspirin. But as long as they have some instruction in its use, they can enjoy its benefits.
