    Originally posted by Caitiff Primogen
    Its not actually accurate to say there was no paradox at that time, it just took a different form (the ravaging?). House Tremere had already been sustaining itself with immortality potions, but their effectiveness was waning due to the encroaching effects of paradox and the Circle of Seven feared that this heralded the end of Magick entirely. They didn't end up being correct, but based on the evidence at the time it wasn't an unreasonable supposition and as a result they're all still alive centuries later to know they made a mistake.
    This it's true, to an extent...Paradox in Dark Ages it's not just different, but also a non issue 90% of the time. You only get to suffer Paradox when you Botch, and it's believed that some long standing magical practices (like certain immortality potions) become more costly in Vis to mantain. That's why they started the investigation.

    How did the Tremere survive and multiply? Well embracing their House would actually have been pretty easy. Dominate is a bitch unless you're wearing active Mind defenses, and the flat fact is that most Mages aren't paranoid twinked out knife-in-teeth PCs. The blood bond is even nastier, especially if you don't know what vampiric vitae is or how it works.
    While all of this it's possible, I can't believe it so easily, ¿why?.
    Thake this things into consideration:
    1° Paranoia i't very common among mages, especially powerful ones. We are talking about the Mages who admit in their Trad Book to be constantly breaking the rule that forbids scrying on another mage (rule that exists for a reason, namely justified paranoia). Heck, the OoH couldn't have been created if Bonisagus hadn't invented the "Parma Magica" to keep a shield when talking with other mages, that's how paranoid mages are. Mind shield wasn't invented for no reason, it's actually pretty "standard" procedure.
    2° The Tremere didn't lived all in Ceoris, hidden from the sight of other mages
    3° Any mage with anything that resembles Prime can detect the "wrongness" in a vampire, even by accident, merely by looking at the flow of magick, same can be happen with Life. These two resources are within OoH grasp, and they're common.
    4° The Tremere were having problems with vampires from the point they entered in the lands of the Tzimisce uninvited. It didn't escalated into all out war until they did the ritual by capturing two Mathusalems, but it wasn't unheard off. Vampirism wasn't unknown.
    5° The Tremere were all Neonates without access to a lot of vampiric power. Thaumaturgy wasn't invented. Presumably one of these young vampires could have been subdued easily if found

    While I can buy that the Tremere quickly gained the control over whatever minnor dissidence there could be in Ceoris, it's too much to believe that a group of Neonates with low level Dominate managed to:
    A. Never find a Mind shield blocking their attempts
    B. Never find a mage that would notice anything "undead" in their magic (which would, at least, amplify the paranoia)
    C. Either call to Ceoris or convert every Tremere in the place without anyone noticing that the mages are either disappearing or being changed (see A and B)
    D. When one of the above happened, they never faced a resistence that could have ended the charade.

    There's many things that could have gone wrong. It's not impossible for this scheme to work, but it's hard to beliebe that it worked so well.

    They failed in the Massasa War for two main reasons. One, Thaumaturgy is linear but it doesn't invoke paradox.
    This it's wrong because, as I mentioned, in DA Paradox was a non issue most of the time. You only incur on it in a Botch, and botching Thaumaturgy it's bad too.

    Two, in an apocalyptic situation the trump card all vampires have is the ability to reproduce at a faster rate than any other supernatural. For every score of Tremere the Hermetics killed another two score could be sired before the sun even rose. The victory condition for the OoH was complete destruction of the Tremere, whereas all the Tremere had to do to win was keep existing. As vampires all they have is time.
    While ths it's more reasonable, there's some important difficulties too:

    1° Vampires may be easy to create, but Disciplines aren't easy to learn. Neonates that can barely control their blood aren't a chalenge for experienced mages that have been fighting vampires for a long time. (especially because Paradox was almost a non issue)

    2° The Tremere had vampiric enemies that should have been able to end a clan of young Neonates without Disciplines even after mages decided to stop loosing their time and numbers against them.

    3° How many towns in Transilvania can you "mass embrace" before some forces other than mages steps on your toes and trashes this little scheme of yours?

    4° This apocaliptic scenario didn't happened. The Tremere aren't a clan who got "recycled" after most of it's members being anihilated over and over. Ceoris never fell during the Massa War. Plenty of the most important Tremere elders were Tremere in life. Antediluvian magic needs to be called at some point to justify all of this.

    And think that, after this, the Tremere still had enough power to fight in the Omen War against three/four Clans for centuries.

        I think we underestimate hubris as a factor though. I mean, sure, most Tremere mages didn't live in Ceoris, so they wouldn't have been among the first turned. But that would just make them sitting ducks later.

        There are a lot of psychological factors to consider, but as paranoid as many Hermetic mages tend to be, they're still indoctrinated in a culture that is big on fostering an "us versus them" mentality. The Order of Hermes vs. other Magickal practitioners. The House vs. other Houses. They're not used to looking at themselves critically. And they're arrogant. They look at the fall of other Houses and think "well, obviously, that can't happen HERE." (And we've seen how the Order tends to continually end up blindsided by corruption in their own ranks even to today. One would think the fall of House Tremere would mean that they were watching closely, and things like House Janissary wouldn't happen...and yet...)

        Given that we're talking about a Dark Ages scenario, without the kind of mass information available, we have something else to consider: how many Mages would have any idea what a Vampire actually is? And certainly, they're not going to have the advantage of long term observation to detect the tell tale signs.

        You meet an elder from another Chantry. He reads very oddly to your life detection magic. He's pale, cold, and strange. But would you think Vampire? Or would you think "Haha, Octavius screwed up a magickal rite!"

        I can absolutely buy the idea that the average Hermetic Mage is too arrogant to think that some of his own people would ever resort to something like vampirism, assuming they even know what vampirism is, when they have all of creation at their disposal. So, if they do come in contact with a Ceoris vampire, they might not realize what they're seeing until it's too late. The Tremere vampires have the advantage of surprise and can target any internal skeptics first. And if anyone outside of the House starts saying "Hey, I think your elders are vampires," then, given the House politics, the likely response is going to be a kneejerk defensiveness/denial. That other House is just jealous of our power/trying to undermine us/making trouble so they can seize the advantage.

        Especially if we start thinking about the games that House Tremere played in the past against House Diedne and others. The Tremere would have been LESS likely to accept the idea of corruption in their own House if the idea comes from outside. (Heck, if I were Tremere, I'd be seriously tempted to try to use that.)

        It would have to be very gradual, but I think if Tremere was careful and patient, spreading Vampirism as a plague one Chantry at a time, especially if he can use other Houses as a distraction, it could work very well. At least until he has the numbers to do a full takeover by force. And once he has all of his own house, he might be able to work similar campaigns against the other Houses for some time before they figure out what's going on.

        And of course, while this is happening, Tremere and his cronies start figuring out the basics of Thaumaturgy. So that by the time it becomes an all-out war, they're by no means unarmed.

                                                            Hermetic paradigm isn't JUST angels and elementals. There is a lot of alchemy, geometry, and other stuff too. But that said, you're making a few assumptions here.

                                                            1. That the spirits consulted by the Hermetic mages are universal. If we operate under the idea that these angels are Astral creations, then there's nothing to say that Stephan of the Tremere and Marcus of Flambeau are talking to the same being when they call on the Archangel Raphael.

                                                            2. That Umbrood/Spirits/Angels have a comparable morality to the Hermetic mages themselves, and would see fit to report to other Hermetic mages. They may simply not care what the mortals that communicate with them get up to. Certainly, we've seen plenty of examples of corrupt and evil Hermetics, who aren't Vampires. And Hermetics aren't the only ones who call on Angels: Ksirafai, Choristers, and some Barabbi do as well. I haven't read every sourcebook, but I don't remember seeing references to tattling spirits.

                                                            And even if they have the same morality, they might well see this as a test. God did give humans free will after all.

                                                            3. That every use of Angels/Spirits/Umbrood as a focus actually calls down their attention/opens communication. If we are talking about a universal set of religious or astral figures, then they would likely have a LOT of people calling their attention.

                                                            4. That, even IF the Angels/Spirits/Umbrood warned a couple of people, those people would actually be listened to.
