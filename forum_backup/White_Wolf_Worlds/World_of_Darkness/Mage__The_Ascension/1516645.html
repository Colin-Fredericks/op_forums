    Mage Game Styles

    06-29-2023, 10:31 AM
    I had some thoughts about different styles of play for Mage that I thought I'd share. Feel free to add your own.



    Full Pogrom

    This is the classic brutal Ascension War. The Technocracy is in ascendancy and the Traditions in hiding. Those mages with public personas had better be very careful because a single misstep could mean a luxury vacation to Room 101.

    This is the style I think of for early Mage when the Technocracy was The Man and soulless modernity and an omnipresent conspiracy all rolled into one.


    Partial Pogrom

    The Technocracy is brutal, but projects much more power than it actually has. They do not have the resources to crush every Reality Deviant, so they crush the most visible and the most dangerous. They still largely control the world, but they do so by being smart and calculating.

    This is the base style I think of when I think of Mage and I suspect I am not alone in that.


    Cells at Work

    The Technocracy and Traditions, as deep conspiracies, are largely independent cells. They have their own goals and missions and they do not interact with each other. They are mostly constrained by local resources and will only occasionally receive outside help unless things really get out of hand. The Men in Black aren’t going to come to the rescue of the local Progenitor Lab unless things spill out into the open, but don’t expect the Council to send any backup either.

    In this kind of game cabals and cohorts are expected to take care of themselves without much outside help. There’s not a lot of backup to be had but there’s also not much backup for the antagonists. If you do manage to take down the local Progenitor Lab, its going to be some time before anyone even notices.


    The Pax Coincidentia (aka Cold Ascension War)

    The Ascension War burns at a low simmer rather than a boil. An uneasy détente exists and everyone is willing to overlook a lot to make sure it stays that way. The fight continues, but civilly and coincidentally. No bullets, bombs, or disappearances in the night (unless you are sure you can get away with it). Instead, the war is fought with influence, propaganda, red tape, and petty one-upsmanship.

    This is a game where mages are very visible, but magic is not. Whether for political or metaphysical reasons, Vulgarity is very dangerous. Coincidental is the name of the game. This scenario makes the most sense if, like the real Cold War, there is some existential threat like Mutually Assured Destruction that pushes everyone to keep the war cold. Things are stable, but one wrong step and they may not be for long.
    This one comes from an image I saw recently of two sides of an occult store with a Subway sandwich shop jammed (sandwiched, ) in between. It made me think of a game where that is the default move in the Ascension War.


    Where the Wild Things Are

    The Ascension War is only one of multiple problems the Traditions have to deal with. The Technocracy, by and large, controls the physical world but that’s only a small part of reality. Out in the rest of the Tellurian there are gods and monsters, aliens and rogue mages, apocalypses and existential threats. Someone has to deal with them and the Technocracy has too many blind spots to be relied on.

    This style turns the Ascension War on its head. Now it is the Traditions who have to split their time between fighting the Ascension War and facing other threats to Earth. Humanity and the consensus are important but they are only one important thing among several.

    This one works especially well if it is the Traditions are firmly rooted in various Mythic Threads.


    Occupied Earth

    A combination of the previous two scenarios. The Technocracy takes the role of a hostile yet passive peacekeeping or occupying force. They still are trying to impose their paradigm on the world, but they think the job is mostly done and they are mostly maintaining the status quo at this point. There’s a lot more magic left than they think, though, and it is up to the Traditions to help it grow while keeping it from spilling into the open.
    Reality on the Brink Factor: The Technocracy isn’t nearly as victorious as they think. Strangeness keeps seeping through and its often not clear how dangerous it really is.



    In the Shadow of Angry Titans

    The Ascension War is largely a fight between Masters and Archmasters; veritable gods who have lost sight of the humanity they claim to fight for. In the trenches things are much messier and the Traditions and Technocracy rarely get to choose who they share a foxhole with when reality is bombarded. For those on earth, the name of the game is surviving the fallout, protecting the sleepers, and patching things back together. The players are just regular mages trying to survive a world that occasionally goes mad and where affiliation isn’t as important as picking up the pieces.

    For a good idea of the feel of this one, think Mage the Awakening, but instead of the Tyranny of the Exarchs it is a raging Ascension War that oppresses Earth from the heavens.

      While "The Pax Coincidentia" is likely the ground state of Mage post-Avatar Storm, at least until whatever inciting incident threatens to push things into hot war "now", I think "Cells at Work" is also an interesting angle to explore.

      It wasn't just the Traditions that had their Masters and Horizon Realms cut off. The Technocracy literally lost Control. The administrative threads that bound the whole Union together were severed. In effect, when the Technocracy detonated a ghost nuke in the Shadowlands, they inadvertently decapitated their own command structure.

      Moreover, it's likely most of the Union didn't even know that's what happened. Why would they? It's not like Control shares its activities with every operative on the ground. As far as most of them knew, Control suddenly and seemingly permanently went completely radio silent.

      Especially in the early days following the Avatar Storm, a great number of Constructs were effectively on their own. All they had was what connections they possessed on an individual basis. Nor could they necessarily trust one another. As I said, they didn't know what happened to Control. For all they knew, there was some strike that disrupted Control or killed it outright. Either from without (Traditions? Nephandi? Marauders? Something else?)...or within (The Friends of Courage? The SPD? Project Invictus? The so-called "Panopticon"?).

      If you're a Construct, left to flap in the wind without direction, who do you trust? What if the other Constructs demanding your agents and resources is part of the plot to take over the Union? What if the agents insisting you all need to stay together are in on it? What if your remaining superiors left earth-side are?

      All this not even getting into various factions - both the Conventions and those within or across them - that would use the destabilization of the Union for their own ends. Even if there was no foul play intended with the Avatar Storm, that doesn't mean there wouldn't be legitimate attempts to seize power or form coalitions.

      Of course, between the Traditions (who are experienced working as independent cells) and other Reality Deviants, a Construct can't afford to start openly pointing fingers or declaring war on other Constructs. Even if you ignored the Conditioning that makes them WANT to maintain the Union. So while the paranoia is palpable in the extreme, it's kept under control. Delicate dances occur, where Constructs and individual agents act loyal enough to avoid being censured, but not committing too much. No one wants to be caught working with a faction that turns out to have orchestrated the severing of Control, in case Control ever reestablishes itself. (Nor, indeed, is anyone eager to agree too readily to anyone claiming to be Control).

      If nothing else, the Union's scattered "assets" all agree, more or less unanimously, that they cannot appear fractured in front of other Mage factions. So when some Constructs start insisting, loudly and vociferously, that the Technocratic Union has "won the Ascension War", no others will jump to refute that claim. They resent that words have been put in their mouths, but they don't deny it. They go along with the lie, to maintain appearances. Let the Reality Deviants think the Technocracy has pulled off some master stroke, and is now more powerful than ever.

      It's a bluff, and circumstances may conspire to call it. If Constructs are attacked - wiped out, even - can other Constructs afford to let it go unpunished? What if the Reality Deviants begin to see through the deceit, and see how precarious the whole Technocracy really is? Of course, committing too many resources in defense of other Constructs is itself a gamble. Without a centralized supply network, can a Construct afford to stretch itself thin? And what if they people asking for aid...are "in on it"? What if it's a ploy?

      Who can you trust?

      Some Constructs may even think working with the Traditions or Marauders (never the Nephandi, though, ofc), over their own colleagues is a more sound move. At least you can trust a Reality Deviant to be a deviant. They often wear their intent on their sleeves. A fellow Technocrat from outside your circle of personally vetted assets is much less easy to pin down. You would know, since you ARE one.

      All this in service of rebuilding what was lost. Investigating the truth. Sussing out other Technocrats, to see who can (nominally) be trusted. It takes a long time. A Construct can ill afford to pick unnecessary fights. Only the most overt instances of "Reality Terrorism" warrant attention, because that's all the Union can deal with. Not until and unless Control can be reestablished. One way or another.

          Necroing this post, as it has everything to do with my current chronicle.

          I'm actually going for another approach entirely: the Ascension War is over. Not one of the sides, but the war itself.

          For now it's just the beginning of it, but little by little both sides are noticing that the effort simply isn't feasible anymore, neither their old goals make sense in the current world.

          Part of it is that, in a sense, both factions lost. The modern world doesn't play by their books, most people believe in a plethora of things, and this essentially went out of control.

          This partially plays in favor of the Traditions, but without their masters and with limited influence, it's a "victory" with little actual benefit for them.

          Secretly the faction that's really winning are the Nefandi. The rise of the Far Right around the world is their biggest victory. They're finally in a position in which they can ignore how most people are better informed, and just be openly evil, hateful and destructive.

          Both the Traditions and the Union must find new purposes in this new reality, which is slowly drawing new political lines within and between them, while the Nefandi become an ever more pressing threat.

          Also, this Nefandus victory is slowly disintegrating the Consensus as a prequel to the disintegration of reality. So Paradox is back to 2nd Edition levels and improving, Reality Zones are more common, and overall the dangerous part of the Mythic Age is coming back.

                                          And even then, reality's stability isn't dependent on the stability of the Consensus. Or rather, it is and it isn't. It is in that if Consensus isn't stable you do get a lot of little reality zones and the rules aren't always consistent. But reality is still there. Its inhabitants thrive and are healthy.                                           You could run a game where the problem is Consensus itself, I suppose. Have the PCs find out that Consensus didn't use to exist.                                           The PCs have to figure this out, then decide if they're going to try and fight over Consensus or just try and abolish it entirely.

                                              It seems to me that the one possible impasse is monteparnas is attempting to portray in their chronicle two things happening simultaneously. Consensus is calcifying and growing stronger while "reality" is weakening. This has some parallels with the "Consensus of Apathy" introduced in M:Rev. At the time I had some reservations of that concept. It came with mechanical adjustments to casting and paradox I wasn't used to as well as a bevy of other mechanical or plot changes such as the Avatar Storm which, I feel, narrowed rather than expanded the setting.

                                              Let me set that distaste aside for a while. Decoupled from the mechanical changes the Consensus of Apathy is actually quite a good setting option. Its very on theme for the World of Darkness. Raging against apathy is very punk. So how do we integrate the Consensus of Apathy into their world to get the result they are going for?

                                              Im going to offer what I call the "Material Science" solution: Disclaimer- I am not a scientist just a fan of science. Hardness measures how resistant a material is to surface deformation. Although the correlation isn't universal hardness often comes at the price brittleness. As the technocratic consensus hardens it also becomes less able to bend and more brittle. On a local level maybe this means any successful and sufficiently vulgar magic could cause actual damage to the Technocratic reality zone, weakening or destroying it. Or maybe cracks and holes propagate both in geography and time. Maybe at one point all of New York was safely a Technocratic reality zone...but lately jewish mysticism is easier to perform in Midwood (mostly orthodox Jewish population) and vodou in Flatbush (large afro-Haitian population). Maybe for 20 hours a day downtown Wall Street is a technocratic paradise...but after hours in the clubs when the music is pumping and the drugs are flowing an Ecstatic can get away with a lot more.

                                              If I remember correctly something similar happens towards the end of the Ascension novel. Reality starts breaking down, local paradigms flourish, magic gets easier to perform, awakenings skyrocket, and eventually the gauntlet breaks down and the Tellurian as a whole rushes towards unity and oneness. Making this a story of Nephandic Descension would require some changes, of course. At the start, or even for most of the Chronicles the characters may view the new reality zones as an unmistakable good. But perhaps it is Nephandic magic (not vulgar magic) that disrupts Technocratic reality zones, making the flourishing of differing zones a poisoned chalice. This could all be happening because the TU is otherwise distracted with their own concerns (TU civil war, threat null) and it may require working with them (or some of their less murderhobo-y factions) to correct.

                                              At the risk of extending the metaphor too far - What is one of the best ways to strengthen a metallic material with a lot of hardness but too much brittleness? Its alloying. Mixing in a more flexible metal that is able to absorb and redirect energy and prevent immediate fracture. The TU allying (alloying) with the Traditions may be the only way to preserve the consensus but good luck convincing either side of that.

                                              I had another model in my head called the "Tectonic Plates" model. It involved reality zones shifting, moving, bumping up against one another and occasionally popping up unexpected places ("Why is my Apalachin woods witch magic so easy in downtown Shanghai?"). The origin of the problem is pretty much the same with slightly different results. I'd love to expand more on this but unfortunately I went to lunch in the middle of writing this and now desperately need to appear busy at work.

                                              Oh, and not that you need ME to tell you this monteparnas but keep politics in your game. Make your game about a bunch of unhoused AuDHD queerdos fighting a demonically possessed Steve Bannon if that's what you want. If you want to make the alt-right nephandic than make them nephandic. The fact that "things have been bad before" is in no way a useful response to the fact that things are bad NOW. In fact, that position is the Consensus of Apathy in action. The reason why the struggle matters here and now is because here and now is where we currently are. Of course the alt-right isn't powered by literal metaphysical evil in our world. But in your game, at your table, it can be so. Maybe because it gives you a sense of control and agency in the world just for length of one gaming session. Maybe because you think its funny. Doesn't matter. It's your table, your game, your world, your rules.

                                                      The thing with apathy is actually present in every WoD game line since the beginning, although MRev did got it to a whole new level. But it is a 90's thing, specially in the US but not only in the US. It was the End of History sentiment.

                                                      But as Phil Brucatto says in the Book of Secrets, the ideas that came with this sentiment were completely wrong. Magic was pretty much alive, and still is. Most people have some sort of magical thinking and almost everyone has at least some magical beliefs. In fact, even skeptics are mostly people that learn to set their beliefs aside, not people who don't believe in some weird shit.

                                                      And because of that, my first idea is that the Consensus of Apathy, and maybe the Consensus itself, is someone else's doing. But it is breaking. The current world is far different in this regard from the apathetic future the devs thought were coming, and so I think in Mage that means the Consensus is weakening.

                                                      On itself this is not Reality breaking, just the Consensus, as Citrakayah said. The Union thought it had power over the beliefs of the general population and they were making the Consensus, but while they got a lot of power and a lot of influence over its course, ultimately they were wrong, and today's world is proof of this, and it has nothing to do with the Nefandi (unless they're the ones to actually create the Consensus, maybe as a means to 'make Reality brittle').

                                                      Now, the thing about the far right isn't about Consensus, or about how numerous they are, but about more pressing matters. We still live in a world kind of on the brink of destruction. While millenarianism receded, the actual real problems that threat our future remain: climate change went from a looming threat in the 90's to actual reality and worsening, nations with WMDs are ever more belligerent, we just recently proved we can't unite against an epidemic even having all the tools to control it.

                                                      Here's where the far-right comes. They're openly positioning against everything we can do to make this mess a little less shitty. This is not by any stretch humans being worse than we've been through history, but it is the consequences of their evil becoming far greater than before. In this situation, the Nefandi don't need to work the Consensus, just work to keep bad people in a place of enough power to stop any meaningful change (and I definitely agree that bad people shouldn't be bad just because of supernatural influence, and shouldn't think themselves bad at all, quite the opposite).

                                                      Oh, and just to be clear: my whole Mage group is made up of left-wing people. Real left-wing, not the progressive right-wing of the US Democrats.

                                                          Thinking more on this, Consensus as an evil plot (Nephandic or otherwise) has a lot going for it.

                                                          1. If you believe that reality is entirely determined by Consensus, you don't really need to do the hard work of changing things if you can just convince people it's not necessarily. Why change the world to solve global warming if you can just deceive people into thinking global warming doesn't exist? That should get rid of the problem, right? You should be able to do the same for environmental problems generally; just have people stick their fingers in their ear and not listen and disbelieve it out of existence. Even many social problems should function the same way.
                                                          2. We know there are alternate routes to magic and expanding the human technological base. It's less versatile and less powerful, but sorcerers can create new paths without any involvement from mages or any Mythic Threads (see the Starlight path, which has no real world folkloric basis). Yet mage factions are almost exclusively focused on Consensus and Sphere magic. There's a lot of other things that don't seem to be affected by Consensus and some logical issues posed by its existence that would be neatly explained if it didn't always exist and wasn't always as strong.
                                                          3. From a meta perspective, Time of Judgment scenarios involving Nephandi have emphasized that there are no simple answers and anything that appears to be a simple answer is probably a trap. Changing people's minds is hard. That's politics for you. But "If we all agree, we can will utopia into existence" is still a lot simpler than the grunt work of actually building a better society from the ground up. And in this hypothetical, the Nephandi aren't making people do anything with Consensus. They just created it and let mages fight over it. This ties into the theme of hubris and rivalries dooming the world.
                                                          4. Consensus is really good at genocide. There's the cultural genocide of making entire culture's society no longer function--imagine being a mythical society that calls on the gods to bless your fields and then suddenly that starts failing--but it's taken parts of the globe away from Earth and into the Umbra. And it will outright kill Bygones. How many sapient species did we use to share the planet with who are all dead now because they were murdered by disbelief? Mage often posits Consensus as this egalitarian liberating thing that allows the human species to decide its own destiny... and maybe this is just me being a Werewolf main, but giving one species control over that always seemed like the least egalitarian thing imaginable. Even if we're just concerned with humans, Consensus is a perfect weapon against society's minorities.
