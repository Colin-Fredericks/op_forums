                I’m not a big fan of the house rule originally presented here (not because of any particular mechanical weakness; I just don’t think prepared effects really fits very well with the paradigm - seems more D&D wizard than mystic martial artist to me). There’s nothing actually wrong about it, though, as house ruies go.

                But if somebody is looking for something for Prime to add to some of these Feats of Focussed Chi, and doesn’t like the original suggestion here, I just stumbled across a possibility while reading up on something unrelated. From a rote in the Akashic Brotherhood tradition book: “Because of the use of Prime, the mage need not concentrate on the effect once cast.”

                Now, that was a completely different style of rote, involving a much more long-term effect on a target (who is not the caster). And it is Prime 3, not Prime 2. (From prior to M20, though, an effects can get shifted or dropped between editions.) This doesn’t use Quintessence, either.

                But you could steal the idea. Allow Prime 2 plus Quintessence expenditure to extend duration of an effect without requiring concentration. Prime because it’s temporarily modifying the martial artist’s pattern to sustain a magical ability on its own. And it seems to me that this fits reasonably well with the type of character being emulated, because if you watch the “flying” scenes in Hero, Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon, and similar movies, the characters seem very focussed on their fight, and not at all distracted by the flight that seems very natural to them.

                (Some of the feats are not duration-based, so this wouldn’t apply, but those tend to be strikes anyway, and on those Prime 2 + Quintessence allows for aggravated damage.)

                I don’t like this enough to want to do it, but it is an idea to consider for anyone who is still looking for a Prime 2 effect that they are happy with in this area.

                            I do like the idea of Prime allowing you to invest Quintessence in an ongoing Effect: the invested Quintessence becomes unavailable for however long the Effect lasts, and you get it back when the Effect ends.                                         I’ve avoided bringing it up before because it’s pretty edge-case, but technically I think Prime and the Quintessence spend are having a mechanical effect in Spirit Strike.

                                        First there’s the simple Spirit 2 + Prime 2 + 1 Quintessence case. As mentioned, this will inflict aggravated punch damage on a spirit (manifested or not). Sure, Spirit 2 should let you inflict bashing punch damage on a spirit by itself, but if you want to upgrade to aggravated, you need Prime 2 and 1 Quintessence. What I’ve been unsure about is whether aggravated damage makes any difference to spirits, but having been checking up on this, I think it should (in M20 - I haven’t going looking for the spirit combat rules in earlier editions, and I don’t intend to). Once the damage is taken by the spirit, it just reduces the spirit’s essence, and it doesn’t matter whether that is bashing, lethal, or aggravated (although possibly you could house rule longer recovery times for lethal or aggravated damage even for spirits). But I can’t see anything that says spirits soak lethal and aggravated damage. They soak using Willpower instead of Stamina, but I haven’t found anything about them soaking damage with Willpower when Stamina could not normally be used to soak. So, my inclination would be to say that a spirit can soak bashing damage with Willpower and the Armor charm (if it has that charm), can soak lethal damage only with the Armor charm, and cannot soak aggravated damage at all. And that makes Spirit Strike’s most basic form mechanically useful.

                                        Then, of course, you’ve got the Spirit 3 + Prime 2 + 1 Quintessence version. This inflicts damage as if using Life 3 on a regular living creature, which means reading the successes through the Base Damage table. And Life 3 can already inflict aggravated damage. However, Life 3 aggravated damage is always a vulgar effect. Nothing says that Prime 2 + 1 Quintessence aggravated damage is vulgar. (Punching someone in the soul is a lot more subtle than body horror-style gaping wounds opening.) So this combination allows reading successes through the Base Damage table as aggravated damage to a spirit as a coincidental effect. And a coincidental effect means a lower difficulty, and less paradox - a fair trade for 1 Quintessence, I think.

                                        And finally there’s the Entropy 3 + Prime 2 + 1 Quintessence version, but that works the same as the Spirit 3 version on ghosts.

                                        Now, it has occurred to me that by the time a mage is punching spirits and ghosts, the ST might want to rule that everything is pretty vulgar anyway. That’s a fair ruling that negates much of the above logic. If that’s your approach, Spirit strike becomes not mechanically useful again. But I figure the above could be worth considering.

                                        And I think that means that all three strikes in Feats of Focussed Chi have mechanical effects. Between that an Dataweaver’s interesting mechanic, that’s 7 out of 8 Feats of Focussed Chi having mechanical reasons to be built that way.

                                        And the eighth is Chi Healing, which works fine when healing aggravated damage.

                                        And admittedly that leaves a dodgy Prime 2 and spending 1 Quintessence to healing bashing and lethal damage, but it’s pretty good coverage overall. I think there’s value in partial solutions, for those who don’t like any of the suggested complete solutions.
