                                I'll separate this out a bit just to address certain things individually as (by your response) I can tell that there is a little bit of cross-communication going on (and thank you for actually addressing the points made in a thoughtful manner... unlike with some people...)
                                Originally posted by kalinara View Post
                                This isn't a problem specific to the Spheres. This is a problem that's built into every single system of magic in any game. Including Pillars/Foundation. I admit that it's something that is less obvious when you're dealing with the Pillars/Foundation system than it is with the Spheres, because it is a lot easier to make a very specific Paradigm built around the Pillars as presented, but there are still some odd sort of glitches that come up. Look at the pillar of Gavri-El. Healing and fire. What if I want to play a priest who ONLY heals, and has no interest in any fire related possibilities?

                                Or another example. I really want my Old Faith witch to be able to turn people into trees. According to the rote in the book, I need both Autumn and Spring for that. But why should I have to buy both Pillars just to explore my desire to build my own man-made forest?
                                It isn't about being opposed to when conjunctional effects are appropriate or necessary within a given paradigm/set of metaphysical expressions (sidenote: several people back when DA was originally published argued about a few of the exact details of the different Pillars just as some had about Sphere delineations, so that's not new, and debate about what should/shouldn't have been more complex magic(k) for "x" Fellowship is certainly a valid point); rather, it's about the mixing-and-matching aspect. I'll get more into that in a moment...

                                Originally posted by kalinara View Post
                                It's the same thing really. Aleph's necromancer doesn't have a Paradigm as expressed to us. He has a very weirdly applied aversion to one aspect of a relevant Sphere. But Aleph wants his to specifically have the ability to travel to the Underworld. Okay, well that does mean that unless he invests in one of the special merits or wonders, he needs the lower levels of Entropy. But as we've all mentioned, there are a LOT of ways that Entropy fits into that concept: awareness of when something would decay or die, triggered maledictions and curses, manipulating his own or someone else's success or failure.

                                Aleph has never shared his necromancer's paradigm, so I have no idea why his necromancer doesn't seem to incorporate anything involving fate or probability into his world view. To me, that feels like an artificial restriction placed by a man trying to win an argument, not a true character trait. But I'll take your word for it that it's a reasonable paradigm limitation. It's still a limitation imposed by a player.

                                And that's the thing, if you are creating a character using a particular system, with the deliberate intention of limiting that character from full use of that system, that's a problem with the player, not the system. It's like making a wizard in D&D who uses fire spells, and then complaining that I have the ability to learn mage armor or cloud kill as well.
                                The difference, though, is that in DnD, the metaphysical paradigms in place are very, very fixed. (That is, while there is plenty of "range" of ability, the exact how's, who's, what's, etc., are very fixed and mostly immutable, regardless of what anyone -- god or mortal -- believe, both on a strictly in-narrative, story level and on a meta-mechanical level.) That's not the case, however, in WoD, as demonstrated not only by the tightening/loosening of Reality(tm), but of the variance of "rules" not simply from edition to edition but setting to setting. Also, Aleph doesn't have to express his character's finer details of paradigm (as often glossed over in the fluff of many Traditions, Fellowships, Crafts, etc.) because *that* "level" isn't what is actually being addressed: it's about how the essence/resonance/line-item approach of the mage in question may be quite different than the metaphysics as-delineated in the Spheres. Examples will be forthcoming...

                                Originally posted by kalinara View Post
                                The thing about the sphere system is not whether or not it's "Paradigm-Neutral", because that's honestly a ridiculous, meaningless label whether you're arguing for or against. It's that the sphere system is specifically designed with Ascension as an end-game possibility. And part of Ascension is that our characters are meant to transcend their paradigms.
                                Now, *that* is very true within the context of Transcendence(tm), and that is also one of the strengths that I've mentioned for many years now about *why* the Sphere System(tm) is a good one: it *is* a very, very broad expression of metaphysical (P)aradigm. It's also why, comparatively, it is much harder to master both per the in-narrative story approach as well as very expensive from a game mechanic perspective (even when the more expensive version of Pillar expansion, when trying to learn a second set of Pillars w/a new Foundation, compared to standard costs, is taken into account). It doesn't, though, hold the monopoly on Ascending(tm). Per Brucato and Achilles -- both in books and over the years -- a mage can have Arete:10, every Sphere at 5+, and still be no closer to Ascension(tm) than he was the day he Awakened(tm). Arete, while an expansion of both Will and relative Understanding, can (as indicated) represent the internalizing of a mage's foci and a deepening rather than expanding of his (P)aradigm (as he becomes more powerful but not necessarily wiser in the conventional sense... as several Archmages -- Voormas, for example -- act as good examples of...).

                                Close -- *very close*. That is, just as the Spheres, as a whole, cover a wider range of possibilities as a group (and a deeper one in many cases), they also limit themselves relative to what a deeper/more focused metaphysical paradigm can do/create. (Again, examples on their way...)

                                Originally posted by kalinara View Post
                                The not-quite compatibility between Paradigm and Spheres isn't a bug. It really is a feature. That's the kind of thing Seekings are made of.

And, again, there's nothing "wrong" with that, in that context (And I hope nobody who has read my admittedly often long posts on this subject get the impression that I'm saying otherwise.) But, again, this is a thought process that cuts both ways. The metaphysical broadening of the Spheres, as-delineated, within the framework given, is a case in some instances of "throwing the baby out with the bathwater". Yes -- the broadness of the Spheres is a form of applied "Ascension(tm)", as it were (I've never said otherwise), but what about the applied wisdom/understanding found in the fact that many Pillars/Minor Spheres convey a superior expression of a given metaphysical (or several specific) applications compared to the Spheres...? (We're almost at those examples...)

                                Originally posted by kalinara View Post
                                Obviously, not every Mage game is going to be about Ascension. But well, it IS in the name.                                 Oh, well yeah -- the Minor Spheres/Pillars/whatever aren't "necessary" at all in the personal context. You (ubiquitous you) can work with the Spheres, as presented, and that's absolutely fine. (I use the Spheres when 'porting over new players from DnD and similar, because on several levels the Spheres, from a purely game mechanic level, are definitely easier to grok by themselves; Minor Spheres and Pillars, by their nature in terms of the game, require the players to understand the broader metaphysics involved with Mage(tm), as a game... and those thought processes are usually very, very different than those held when coming from DnD, et al...) The point Aleph is making isn't about the "fluff", as such, alone, but rather the disconnect between the applied meta-mechanics and the idea of how Paradigm(tm) affects magic(k) not only in terms of trappings but relative to the metaphysics, themselves.

                                That Entropy (for example), as written, includes the qualities that it does and is used conjunctionally to create a metaphysical effect that allows a given being to travel from the (understood) physical world to "the Low Umbra" is largely a matter of metaphysical conceit. It is no more "correct" than any other expression of metaphysical delineation. (It's also been one of the most questioned/contested of the Spheres since day one back in '94, as it is often misunderstood in terms of application, but also legitimately debated in terms of applied use... which was one of the reasons that later on in Awakening qualities of that Sphere were split into two Arcana, meta-game-wise.) That causality/probability is linked together with certain necrotic aspects in a given Sphere *is* in-and-of-itself an expression of metaphysical paradigm. That isn't to say that it doesn't "make sense" -- it does, and ironically enough I've spent the past two decades trying to get some people I know to understand why it *does* make sense -- but rather to say that those qualities being linked together are a matter of *conceit*, not *necessity*. Again, relative to the idea of conjunctional magic(k) and aspects thereof: it's not against the idea of requiring 2 or more "x's" to accomplish "y" -- it's about understanding the various "x's", as-presented, can be sliced, diced, Julienne-fried, and served with a garnish... and doesn't *have* to be done/presented in just one specific way.

                                Examples (from generic to more singular):
                                It's not that the OF witch cited earlier should grumble about needing two pillars to transform someone into something else (the slicing/dicing aspect of a given set of Pillars is open for alteration, per the guidelines for Making Your Own Pillars/Making Your Own Minor Spheres) -- some effects are powerful and often justified, even-and-sometimes-especially-in-paradigm, for having 2 or more "x's" involved. Rather, it's a case of which metaphysical aspects are represented in specific ways through the lens of the witch's worldview/instruction/natural affinities. (That is, that she has, for example, at Spring:3 the ability to control wind/blunt kinetic force (a piece of Forces:3, with a dash of Prime:2); the ability to heal others of serious wounds and herself of grievous ones (a piece of Life:2/3); the control and creation over the "gentler emotions" (a piece of a piece of Mind:3); manifesting supernatural grace (of both body and mien -- another piece of Life:3); and a few other contextually related effects that manifest conjunctionally with other Pillars.) It's not that there are aspects that require greater effort (either via a higher "level", or by having to combine more (C)omplex aspects within her practice) that is the a problem; it's that more "baggage" is required to fulfill her manifestation of her magic when her Paradigm(tm) isn't balanced (for most factors) by the metaphysics(game mechanics) in play.

                                With Aleph's necromancer, it doesn't matter whether his character has any accounting for probability or causality as defined by Entropy: it's more about how the given metaphysical aspect (stepping down or projecting into The Shadowlands, or further "down") that *should* be strongly in-paradigm for him is required by the Sphere System for him to master qualities/aspects that he would otherwise not only never use but -- by dint of worldview/understanding/natural affinity/what-have-you -- never even come to know about or even remotely comprehend. I've made more than 20 Foundation/Pillar sets (including reviewing/revising some that others made, initially, and I just helped "clean up" a bit) for other players/STs (including for Ophidimancer roughly a decade ago) and at least a couple of them had necromantic qualities that were more akin to the stepping "down" (vs. sideways) that was available to the MVs and OFs -- Uri-El:3 and Winter:3, respectively -- because their innate affinity/worldview simply made such magic "easier" (not less dangerous, mind you.. that's a separate conversation altogether) than per the "bias" of The Spheres (wherein simple dream travel and then astral projection can be had with one Sphere -- Mind:3-4(5) -- and stepping sideways only requires Spirit:3... but stepping "down" -- even into "just" the Low Penumbra (Shadowlands) requires Spirit:3, Life:2 and Entropy:4. That isn't to say that the last part "doesn't make sense" -- it totally does... from the POV of the Sphere System/Paradigm. But it isn't "that complicated/hard" for a number of (P)aradigms (particularly, for example, many Southern European strega-based mysticisms, wherein walking "out and about" has more to do with descending into the lands of the dead than with astral projection as more popularly defined in "modern metaphysics" as well as in the game we're discussing).

                                For Aleph, as player, it isn't a question of whether he's choosing to play a Wizard in DnD who specializes in fire magic and then complaining about the other spells available that he'll never use; he's effectively being told that in order to get to level 5 (and get his fireball spell), he has to take several levels in Cleric first... that is, take on magical practices that have nothing to do with his paradigm/worldview, and only "make sense" because of the Frankenstein metaphysics of DnD. *That's* the point being made.

                                It's not about throwing out the idea of conjunctional magic(k) -- in some cases, just the opposite, in fact, due to the inherent nature of Paradigm -- nor is it about saying the Spheres are "wrong"/"bad" (perhaps I shouldn't speak for Aleph on that part, so I'll just say that *I'm* not saying that) for having the metaphysical slant that they do. It's about saying that the idea that magic, especially in a game/setting like WoD vs. DnD, *has* to be this way or that way via the in-narrative vs. meta-mechanics is a concept that -- again, ironically enough -- is the hot button issue for Mage(tm) as a setting... even more so than Ascension(tm) is. It's not even that hard a thing to do -- the creation of Pillars/Minor Spheres -- once you've got the hang of the concepts put for by the Spheres/Arcana... it just doesn't *have* to be delineated the same way as they are, and any ST worth their salt should be able to recognize the concepts as-ported and positioned relative to weighing them for balance.

                                If the Spheres work for you/whomever, that's a good thing. Totally. But I can say from personal experience (and more gaming groups and conventions than I'd care to admit to) that I've come across a considerable number of people voicing "x" like how Aleph is. This isn't something I just decided to ramble on about starting over 20 years ago -- I literally run into this conversation all the time. For the first several years ('94-'98 or so), I was just a peripheral spectator in those conversations (our group(s) usually operated very rules-lite, and a number of us have a good grasp of metaphysical thought per our educational backgrounds and intuitions, so the rules in-and-of-themselves were always just so much window dressing). But as I started noting more and more people reacting like how Heavy Arms does -- never properly addressing the issues presented, and not noting the very valid metaphysical under-points being brought up -- it piqued my interest more and more... and especially once DA:M came out, and the STG:Rev offered the Minor Spheres along with much broader variations of how to use the Spheres and the Sphere System. It's almost like it's a study -- life reflecting art, in this case -- wherein, say, a Technocrat simply cannot see how The Spheres (what-have-you) can be interpreted/manifested in ways other than as-written and delivered by The Advisory Committee(tm). It's literally as if a number of people simply can't see the other side of the coin in terms of the points being made.

                                        I think my wishlist would include some focus on different terminology. I mean, ultimately, as it stands Dark Ages Mage does have a unified, or at least uniform, system of Magic. The foundation and pillars have the same structure in every Fellowship, even if they have different themes.

                                        I don't mean that as a critique of the system. It's a game, so I think it needs to have some kind of uniform system for any hope of balance. But it does seem odd to me. I'll use Old Faith as an example. I'm a literal sort of person and I keep seeing "pillars" and "foundation" and I feel like it's too structured. I picture a Greek library when I imagine it. Like House Diedne would use that terminology, but I'd like something that sounded a bit more free form for other Proto Verbena. Like "roots" and "branches". Or "hearth" and "channels" or even "home" and "roads".

                                        The Chakravanti might use "hub" and "spokes" perhaps? Maybe the Messianic Voices would use pillar/foundation, or something based on religion or music?

                                        It'd still be the same system, of course, but it'd feel more individual to me. And it makes it easier for me, at least, to see why a Hermetic can't just learn a Messianic Voice's Pillar. It emphasizes that it isn't just a matter of method, but a completely different way of conceiving of magic that would need to be learned from the ground up.

                                        I think Dark Ages already does this, to an extent, but I'd like to see it embraced a little more.

                                        (And in an in character sense, I think it showcases what is really important about the invention/re-discovery of the spheres, which IMO was never about the spheres themselves. ANY system of codification would have worked fine as long as it could be universally applied. The important part is arete. Basically the realization that all of these incredibly different foundations, at heart, represented the same thing: a deep and thorough understanding of the universe and their magic. Once they had that point of commonality, even if the understanding itself is very different for each person, then they could start communicating and learning from each other. If everyone's already using the same terminology, then it seems like an obvious discovery. A foundation is a foundation. But it wouldn't have been as obvious to the people at the time.
