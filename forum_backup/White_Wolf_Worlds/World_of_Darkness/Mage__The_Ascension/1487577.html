    Victorian Mage: if no Society of Ether, then who?

    07-23-2022, 09:17 AM
    In Victorian Mage, there's a Future Fate addressing the Ether. One of the alternate futures described is one where the nascent Technocracy accepts the Ether, and the Society of Ether never leaves the fold. It goes on to suggest that the Council of Magick would seek a ninth Tradition among the Crafts, and that they'd eventually settle on the Kopa Loei.

    Why? First, they've been a Council of Eight ever since the Solificati left at the end of the Sorcerers Crusade. Why the sudden urgency to take just anyone to fill the vacancy? And second, why the Kopa Loei? Why not, for instance, the Ngoma?

    I'm not just picking a name or of a hat here. The Ngoma are described in M20 as being borderline technomancers. As well, they're of the same cultural roots as the Wamukamwami, a Central/Southern African society that Victorian Mage suggests might have a better handle on Ether Science than the Etherites do. If we integrate the Wamukamwami into the Ngoma as one of its factions and play up the techno- side of the Craft, I submit that that would be a much better fit for the empty seat on the Council than the Kopa Loei.

    And besides, the Ngoma didn't reject the Traditions back during the Sorcerers Crusade when they were invited to join. They liked the idea of the Traditions, but refused to be lumped in with the Dreamspeakers, holding out for full Tradition status in their own right and leaving only after it was apparent that it wasn't going to happen. So if the Council suddenly, after four centuries, starts looking to fill the empty seat, I think the Ngoma would be at least open to the idea.

    And of course, that still leaves the question of why the Council would wait for centuries to ask. Though there, I could see the birth of the Technocracy being the trigger.

    Thoughts?

      Cultural changes over four centuries can help. Although we use the word "Orientalism" in an extremely negative way, the Orientalists were the real start of multiculturalism. Orientalists held that other cultures outside the West were not just worthy of study but were vital to understanding humanity. Although we now link Orientalism with colonialism and racism this is a complete reversal of history. Orientalists were among the first important voices in the West to challenge both imperialism and the "Scientific Racism" popular in the 19th century. Our present negative view of Orientalists comes from the cheap nihilism of the 1970s.

      Because of the Orientalists, and groups like the Order of Hermes researching their deep roots, I could see the Hermetics simply coming to realize they'd been wrong. After the disaster of the Verbena, the Dreamspeakers, and the Cult of Ecstasy's failed outreach to the Bata'as, the Hermetics, hat in hand, go to the Ngoma with apologies and an invitation.

      These factors would explain the timing.
