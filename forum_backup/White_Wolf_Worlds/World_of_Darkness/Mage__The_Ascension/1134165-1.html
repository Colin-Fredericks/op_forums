                            Ok, there are two scales here: one scale measures how far you've progressed in mastering a Sphere. Here, sorcerers by definition are rated at zero. Now, there may be a sorcerer who has fooled the local mages into thinking that he has Sphere magic, and maybe convince them to confer upon him a title that he did not earn. But similarly, it's possible for, say, an Initiate too pass for a Disciple, at least for a time — and conversely, a Master could pass himself off as an Initiate if he had reason to. But the Initiate is still an Initiate and the Master is still a Master, even if no one realizes it. Likewise, a sorcerer is always a sorcerer no matter what. Of course, a sorcerer can potentially do things that an Initiate or Disciple can't; and it's possible that sorcery deserves its own scale, one that doesn't necessarily align with the Spheres.

                            The other scale is social and/or political in nature, and varies from group to group. It's not so much about how far you've developed your magic as it is about how much authority and responsibilities you have. The Order of Hermes is the most likely to tie the two scales together; but even they have some flexibility that allows a disconnect between how capable you are with the Spheres and how much authority is bestowed on you.

                            Also, Consors are a thing in Mage. At least, they were back in Ascension's Right Hand. And yes, a Consor is "not a Mage, but supernaturally gifted". The Consors in the book included hedge wizards, psychics, faithful, vampires, Bastet, and so on. The book drew a line between them and ordinary companions.

                            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
