            It's also worth noting that Antediluvians+ are supposed to have powers and abilities that function similar to Awakened Magick, and themselves are intentionally beyond the rules. It's suggested that they have the ability to alter the way that the universe works, unintentionally direct the way people behave/believe/act, and alter aspects of reality simply by existing. Seemingly Caine laid down massive curses on at least 12 entire bloodlines on an angry whim, and there are plenty of cases where it is suggested that one Cainite or another made one place cursed or corrupted or another sacred, or whatever.

            Originally posted by idpersona View Post
            They didn't have that setup ready?
            We only have, OOC, some information on what happened, and because the story kind of comes from multiple splat's points of view, we can infer some things, but it's not really "factual". It seems that the Technocracy was caught completely by surprise by Ravnos waking up. This is and is not a little surprising, as Ravnos is typically seen as one of the weaker Anti's in the more physical sense. We don't know that, but generally speaking both he and his Clan have a reputation for being sneaky, deceptive, cowardly (in physical combat), and tricksters. So it makes perfect sense that he would be well hidden, but it's also a little odd that he chose to battle anyone at all, or even let himself be seen.


            Originally posted by idpersona View Post
            Simple and easy didn't work. I do completely agree it's plot device though. Their final solution shouldn't have worked either, given the mastery of Chimestry. But hey, Mage players got the feather in their collective cap that Mages were responsible for killing an Antediluvian.
            There are two sides to this. Cainites of his age are generally known to have very low Path ratings, and we are clearly told that the important segments of the battle take place during the day. Even if we assume that he has a Path Rating of 10, which is highly unlikely, but technically possible, he should have been capped at an absolute max of 10 Dice, and likewise 10 Dots in anything and everything. So, even if, for instance, he had 10 Str, 10 Potence, and Blood Buffed another 10 dots in Strength, he should have been limited to only a Str of 10.

            It's possible that as a 3rd Gen Cainite that's been alive since nearly the dawn of humanity, he might be beyond that limit, but by the rules, he should have been limited to his Path Rating on any rolls, also constantly making rolls to stay awake, and at a penalty. There is also the problem that he should have been very low of Blood, and also suffering from a general weakness/disorientation from just recently waking up from a long Torpor. That isn't really a mechanical rule as much as a "flavor" restriction.

            Another aspect to also consider is that the Technocracy was essentially fighting blind, and there where more than one threat in the immediate area, (it quotes something like multiple factor 8-10 beings), and all of them are different, (there are Mages, Werewolves, Spirits, Wraiths, a Vampire, KotE, if not more), and while their primary target was Ravnos, that would require a lot of Mastery of a lot of Spheres to be able to pick him out and target (affect) him. Hell, probably a mastery of all, in all honesty, but the Technocracy and Mages in general as still going to run into the issue where Cainite's powers such as Disciplines and Mage's magics such as Spheres only oppose each other if they are at an equal level, failing otherwise. That is, if you have a Vampire with Dominate 6 and a Mage with Mind 5, the Mage automatically fails to counter Dominate 6, because Dominate 6 is at a higher level. If it's Dominate 5 (or lower) and Mind 5, then the "defender" has a chance, but it is not automatic, (and vice versa). However, when we are talking about something Level 6+ vs something capped at 5, Mages really shouldn't have had a chance at a lot of stuff. Not everything, but a lot.

            One last thing to keep in mind is that the entire scenario, while it was Story Fiat, it was also there to purposefully give essentially every splat a massive wake up slap across the face, that no matter how much planning they had done, (like the section where Vitel is speaking to many of the Camarilla's higher leadership when his character has secretly been hoarding the worlds nukes as a plan to survive the Antediluvians from waking up), basically sees his secret plan not work. The KotE get destroyed, the Garou don't even make it to the true battle and die almost pointlessly.

                            Originally posted by Beckett View Post

                            I've actually answered your questions, (or called BS on your BS), multiple times, including the whole second paragraph you just quoted above. You may not like the answer, or perhaps didn't read it.
                            The fact of the matter is, that Mages and the Technocracy do not actually know a great deal about vampires, nor do they have a great deal of resources like nukes that the vampires do not also have, or more likely, are not actually more under the vampires control than they are the Mage's. They may think that they do, but that just means that it will be a weakness of overconfidence when it hits the fan.

                            Your whole plan is based upon vampires being stupid. They are not. So even if the Mages get the jump on the Cainites, (which is one of the ST fiat things), all that means is that there is going to be a lot of casualties right up front, (and most likely they are going to be the younger and weaker Cainites), while the more experienced, more powerful, and luckier ones start figuring out what just happened. And they will. Unlike Mages, the Cainites don't have to actually fully understand how what's happening actually works.

                            We have also already seen the Technocracy pull out all of the stops fighting 1 single Vampire (who was also fighting an entire pack of Werewolves, some of the strongest KotE, Wraiths, and other Mages, and in a weakened states), basically discover that their big plan almost failed. Not on it's own, but even with all of the other stuff going on at the same time. The Mages knew it was a strong vampire, but that's about it, because they don't own the Technical Manual Vampire the Masquerade Mage's Edition.

                            So, the Technocracy's big Hail Mary plan turned out to be a bust. 100%. It failed. And that was against just one Vampire. It happened to be an Antediluvian, but they don't know that, and they would be stupid as hell, (on par with what you seem to think all Vampires are) to continue to rely on that.

                            You also seem to think the Vampires actually need to know where the Mages are hiding. They don't, plain and simple. There are multiple higher level Disciplines that can affect targets without knowing where or who they are, and Mage's are going to have a particularly hard problem with that because Disciplines level 6+, by the Book, Mages do not have any particular defense against. Their Spheres only work to counter one for one. Vampires also have access to abilities that Mages are probably not going to know about, like the ability to become Astral, or read objects. It wouldn't take much for a survivor of a nuke to pick up a piece of metal, take a supernatural look at it, say "Hmm", take a little invisible trip to the Space Station/Satellite it came from, send a message back to earth telepathically right then and there, and another vampire used mass manipulation to Dominate the president and every single Project Twilight member in the room (who autofail, even with "Magick") to blast them and every satellite to dust.

                            All in all though, this is stupid. For every "But the _______ can do this" there is going to be a "But the _______ can counter it by, or are immune to, etc. . ."

                            Far, far too much of Mage also depends on the ST allowing it to work, which doesn't work in a Setting vs Setting battle royal, not unless there ST is partial. And if the ST is partial, than whatever side wins because the ST gave them that advantage. Nothing more.
                            Your entire argument is based on assumption that Mages are hapless like morons against 6+ Disciplines. And that is why we always substitute 6+ Disciplines with equivalent Spheres or allow the Mages counter it anyway. For instance, Eldar Vampire with Dominate 6+ has Mind 3, by the Book.
