            And this is why M20 redefined how paradigm interacts with things: to have something that ensures (non-Marauder) mages are operating on a consistent set of believes without the silliness that comes from the "straight jacket" approach many STs and Internet debaters developed over the years to resist the silliness of excesses of white-room creative approaches to paradigm.

            To paraphrase both 2e and Revised on this, paradigm is meant to inform how your role-play your mage, not turn into a philosophic argument or metaphysical treatise every time you cast a spell. This is a game meant to be played for fun. Paradigm is there to help players frame their role-playing to make for a consistent and fun magical praxis in a very loose and open system, not to be a cudgel for STs to smack players with. Yes, sometimes the ST needs to step in, but the books have been spending over 20 years encouraging us to resist that urge unless absolutely necessary.

            By the time you're actually playing the game, "how does this fit your paradigm?" should be a rare question. 99% of the time mages will use their tools and practices as informed by their paradigm which means that answer is front loaded in. If prayer is a significant part of a mage's technique as defined through there paradigm, then when they kneel in front of a shrine to pray to [insert devotional entity] they've already said how this fits into their paradigm.
