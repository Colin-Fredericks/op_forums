    Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
    I imagine most Etherites would rationalize it as a finicky invention they needed to keep in tune, so if its out of their sight for more than a minute or two it can't be relied upon. You want reliability, you make a Device (that's the term for a technological Talisman, right?), otherwise you keep pliers on hand.
    Yep. Device is Talisman, Invention is Artifact, Gadget is Charm, etc. The requirements for "make this actually work without my personal intervention/observation" are actually very very low - anyone with the barest meta-physical influence (Prime 2) can pull it off, even if that only gives it Arete x 2 "charges" per Forged by Dragon's Fire.

    It's also worth noting that this isn't even the first time we've seen a magic roll that that the player was aware of but the mage himself wasn't. I forget which book it is in but Technocrats have a technique where they can use statistical or structural analysis to "observe" that an event, like the ceiling collapsing on a reality deviant, is about to happen. In actuality they are causing the event, but they don't realize they are doing anything at all.
    As long as it's subtle enough, Passive-Aggressive Troll Casting is an amazing tool for "I'm just that good" paradigm-facets. Easy to explain away when it fails too, with "no, that was just not a correct observation".

    Originally posted by JoeDuncan View Post

    I'm pretty sure those rules are for when a mystic mage wants to cast a rote, but doesn't have their foci, or their foci have been destroyed etc...

    I could be wrong, but as far as I recall, while it is technically possible for a mystic mage to cast a rote without their normal foci, doing so *still* requires the mage to use some kind of improvised foci or physical expression of their Will. It's different than "surpassing" or "abandoning" your foci at arete 6; these are *penalties* for missing foci, not a shortcut to enlightenment. The foci are still required, and the mage is penalized for not having their pre-prepared ones.\
    You're wrong. Mysticks can just use wishcasting with that rule. Essentially, they''re borrowing from the Traditions meta-paradigm, which says "everything is just the enacting of pure Will on reality", which while true violates them actually having their own Paradigm. In my headcanon, they've been indoctrinated into it, but don't fully believe it except as dogma until Arete 6 (I use Revised's placement of everyone on equal footing), so it's ridiculously difficult for them to pull off.

    The Technocracy meta-paradigm, "the power is in a tool/people with Genius are more talented than the unEnlightened/etc." is... considerably easier to swallow for people in the organization. By design, I imagine.

    Of course, Technomancers can't use that rule, so its relevance to this topic is tenuous at best, and I'm not sure why Revan thought it would help his case. Rather than showing "wait, no, Etherites can't actually violate their Paradigm and make things they think are impossible happen".
