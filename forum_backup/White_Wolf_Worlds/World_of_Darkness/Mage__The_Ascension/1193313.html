    The Time Traveler's Microscope

    03-08-2018, 05:38 PM
    Through use of Time, a Mage travels back to the 1100s. They bring a single microscope. Taking some infected sample from a sick person, they make a slide and examine it under high magnification.

    Now, Mage STs; what do they see?

    - They see organic pathogens such as bacteria, since those have always existed and always been the vector of disease. This has serious ramifications; it means that Sleepers do not warp reality, and it means that the universe assumes a certain "objective shape" without outside influence. It means that Sleepers simply shape the Consensus, and all that does is affect whether Magick is vulgar or not, making it easier or harder to reshape things. So the Mage who thinks that "bad air" spreads illness is wrong, and his paradigm is loose and fluffy compared to the more "real" paradigm of a Technocrat bioterrorist who has an intimate understanding of what actually causes disease, up to the point where she could spread disease without Magick if she needed to.

    - They see no evidence of pathogenic microorganisms. Instead, they are able to detect a General Aura Of Badness. This "Stuff" is diseased stuff (imbalanced humours, miasma, malaise), and it will make people diseased. How? Good question! Now this wouldn't be unreasonable, since the books seem to flip-flop between "collective thought shapes reality" and "collective thought shapes the way that Magick shapes reality". If it's the former, than our Time Traveler will never see bacteria before the paradigm shift of the 1800s which embraced germ theory.

    The answer to this question is quite pertinent, and has ramifications. Do mobile phones only work because of the Technocracy shaping the consensus until phones were just part of reality? The Mage books often lean on this being true, and have reality zones where such devices are less reliable. Or are mobile phones just something that is an application of the basic nature of reality, fundamental physical behavior?

      Would the microscope even work? I'm not sure that it would.

      In mage, using a microscope would sort of be like a Life 1 rote under the technocratic paradigm, which even sleepers would be capable of using since it is a part of the consensus.

      If, however, we were back in a time where there was no such consensus, then the use of a microscope would be just as magical as hitting sticks together to drive away the spirits.
      If the mage had a paradigm which allowed for the microscope to work, i.e. they believed that light can be reflected and bent via ocular lenses so as to magnify an image, and thus can be used to allow us to see that which is smaller than can be seen by the eye alone, then they would be able to use the microscope for their Life 1 spell.

      But what they see down the microscope will also depend entirely on their paradigm. Perhaps they see little demons or spirits moving around the blood, Perhaps they see small kingdoms at war. Or perhaps instead they see shapes, pure mathematical shapes, or the forms that Plato described. Or even bacteria, so long as that is what they expect to see.
      Without consensus on how this tool works, there is also no consensus on what would be seen.

                  But such a suggestion completely ruins scientific history.

                  A person believes something that goes against the consensus of the learned world, as well as common wisdom. And yet they are able to demonstrate that they are correct, in a reproducible manner. They aren't some victor in the so-called "marketplace of ideas" (ugh), they win because no matter how stubborn other learned people are, they have some way of demonstrating that they are correct. John Snow was able to show others that germ theory was correct, while the absolute consensus in his reality zone was Miasma spreading disease. Was he insane? Was he warping reality to suit his own theories? Was the paradigm shift some grand application of Mind?

                  If we follow the answer that technology relies on belief, that our microscope works because of Consensus (fully correct within the Mage Lore, but frequently contradicted from what I can see), then John Snow was wrong. Disease was being spread by Miasma, and all of the methods being used to counter this spread were correct. But when we look at attempts to prevent the spread of disease through scented smoke, cleansing the city of dirt, things just got worse, in a way that doesn't match the paradigm.

                  Consensus: dirt in the city is releasing a miasma.
                  Idea: we can fix that by pushing all of the dirt into the river and letting the city wash itself of the impurity.
                  Consensual Result: people stop dying of cholera
                  Actual Result, which defies Consensus: it gets worse, because despite the consensus, cholera was spread by a germ, not by bad air.

                  See the problem?

                  And how about a modern example? Shills for the sugar industry convinced everyone that fat makes you fat. That's consensual, thanks to mass marketing. Ask people what kind of food makes you fat, and fatty foods are an obvious answer. The real answer is that we produce fat most efficiently when we have excess glucose in our system, and this can be proven metabolically and by looking at the dangerous results of people cutting fat out of their diets, eating "healthy" zero-fat alternatives which have been packed with sugar to fix the awful taste. If Consensus really shapes reality, then low-fat foods would make people more thin. The opposite is true, sadly. And how about the old consensus of cigarettes being good for you? Or radiation being good for you? If the consensus is actively shaping reality in such powerful ways as allowing mobile phones to work, then what's going on here?

                  My head-canon (which the Mage books do seem to occasionally use, as this isn't super well thought out) is that Consensus is only relevant when it comes to Magick. The Technocrats shape the Consensus to make enlightened procedures less paradoxical, and to make "Magick" more paradoxical. Sleepers might think that cigarettes help you get over chest infections, and this might make a disease-curing cigar Coincidental, but that's all it does. Sleepers might think that low-fat foods help you loose weight (ignoring sugar), and this might make a nutritious zero-fat broth an effective "potion" a Mage could create to bring about weight loss, but this doesn't mean that low-fat diets are good for you. Sleepers might think that a hit to the head can knock someone out for hours, reliably, Hollywood action movie style, and this might make it easier for a Mage to do this and make it Coincidental (a bit of Life and Time?), but it doesn't actually change the way a normal hit to the head works.

                  Does that make sense?
