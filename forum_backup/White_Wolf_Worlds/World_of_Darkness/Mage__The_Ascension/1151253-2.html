                                            As I understand it, the problem with the Sabbat has nothing to do with the chances that Technocrats have in a vs match.

                                            The problem it's that it doesn't jive well with a society of supposedly "well intended but flawed" people to prioritize the Traditions more than the Sabbat. This problem comes as a consequence of several facts:
                                            1. The traditions were "defeated" in revised (they tried to avoid angering the technocracy)
                                            2. The things that the Sabbat does, and the way they do it, it's right on the ballpark of the original purpose of the Technocracy. They're cults of monsters that prey on humans, they're no better than Mistridge or a dragon.
                                            3. The most cursory investigation on supernatural activity on Sabbat areas would lead to discover what these vampires do, and that it doesn't stop. This can be discovered by accident while investigating shady cults and suspicious murders. Even if you can't know this is part of a larguer conspiracy, as an issolated fact it's monstrous enough to require more atention than the hermetic selling self help books.
                                            4. The Technocrats supposedly care about humans in a less than abstract fashion. They're not "souless" anymore (at least not on average)

                                            Let's think about it with an example: Lets say a capable street agent (like a PC could easily be) it's investigating a shady cult that opperates within a cartel. It could easily be a Traditionalist waging for power in the criminal organization, but the lead takes the agent to a blood cult of Serpents of the Light related to the Sabbat...The Agent can't know right of the bat that these aren't Traditionalists or related to the Traditons (and even if he knew, why would that prevent further investigation?...oh, it's not Mage, best leave the setting). Further investigation leads the agent to discover that they are vampires...and the things they do to humans. He manages to flee with the info without being flayed alive...barely.

                                            And he goes back to the Construct and says "I've observed that the vampires never cause Paradox and always kill all human witnesses and thus the Consensus it's protected. Everything it's under control".

                                            ...See the problem.

                                            Those words, no matter how flawed the Agent is, are the words of the kind of "souless agent of conformity" that the Traditions say Technocrats are.
                                            If technocrats are supposed to care about humans, they can't have such a reaction with vampires. More so, it doesn't matter if the Technocrats can win or not, they should at least give their best try to stop it-prioritizing the defeated hippies over that sounds cheap

                                            The original Guide of the Technocracy had one possible scapegoat:

                                            The rift between the street agents that see how things are and the hidden masters that see the big picture. The agents may think vampires are very dangerous, but the leaders won't because they aren't interfering with the long term goals (which comprises a global chess game between the Masters of the Traditions and the Union that the Union it's currently winning). The masters are detached from things like "baths of blood" and the "gore" that agents regularly face. A few thouzands of lives don't matter to the ones that oversee millons.

                                            In the previous example, the Agent wouldn't be unfazled. He would grab his pen and writte a hundred of very serious memos to the Union asking for the eradication of the sect with extreme prejudice...and would find his petition to be rejected because it doesn't further the long therm goals of the local Symposium (that may be along the lines of expanding the drug business so the resources can go to build more starships for a conflict in Pluto or something like that-And you're planning to attack it, yeah, no, sorry, can't allow you to do that)
                                            Then the Construct may decide to take action (to the extent that they can do this without endangering the cartel itself), but they would be more or less on their own-without the impossible display of pure power that the Union sometimes show backing them. That's where the PC enter and save the day.

                                            There are other scapegoats:
                                            *Technephandi (or simply evil technocrats) that work with the Sabbat impeding the Union from taking action, deleting archives, and generally making noise.
                                            *In M20, the Traditions being very much undefeated and still a treath whereas the Civil War is still brewing. All of which keeps resources from do-gooder's hands
