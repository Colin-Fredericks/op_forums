    New gods?

    06-25-2017, 09:00 PM
    Ok folks, it seems to me (might be wrong, but it's seems so from the internet) that the new gods in American Gods built up a big fanbase. Is it right? Is it true for you? If so, could you explain your thought process on the thing?

    To be true, my puzzlement is quite great. I get the "tech and modernity is keeeewl and hip" angle, but honestly, I hated those guys in the book and hate them in the series. Not because the old gods (not those old gods) are good guys, but simply because the new ones and their vision of the world gave me the creep.

    Why am I posting this here? Because I think it resembles the ever-recurring "why people like the Technocracy/Technocracy vs. the Traditions" question we're all too familiar with, although I think the new gods have even less redeeming qualities than the Technocracy. On the other hand, they are a great inspiration for portraying Control, I think.

    If nothing worked, then let's think!

                                              Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post

                                              Well wouldn't recommend the show then. But sexual human sacrifice is pretty mythicaly on point for a primordial fertility goddess like Gaiman was getting at.
                                              She's not supposed to be Inanna or Astarte or Ishtar. She's supposed to be the goddess associated with the Sabaen period in what is now Yemen and pre-Aksumite Damot in modern Ethiopea, probably more explicitly the later than the former, as female primacy/equality was more of a "thing" there, but maybe not (the scenes depict her and her followers as more explicitly African than Arabian, but she is seen to be upset at the demolition of a temple in Yemen, so, <shrug>). Which in turn associates her with the legend/myth of biblical Sheba that he was using for the name. He's definitely taking liberties by inventing some mythology for a very vague figure, but, as you said, he was going for an archetype and there's no reason for it NOT to fit (and, considering his desire to avoid all things Graeco-Roman combined with a tacit Old World but not Eastern Old World focus, it's as good a card toss into the hat as any).
