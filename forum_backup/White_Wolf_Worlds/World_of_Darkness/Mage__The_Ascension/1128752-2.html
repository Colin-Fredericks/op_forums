                Or it isn't because that's not the narrative. I guess, I mean, it could be if you want it to be. If you have a group of players who enjoy that sort of game.... But to assert and aver that that's the game proper? Balderdash.

                The Spheres are a game mechanic that exists both above and on the table. So it has to be reflected in some way that makes sense to all the various groups across the board. Oh, and from fluff-land, it's a system that seems to be an accurate description in game of the fundamentals of the way reality actually works, so, when whoever sussed it out (I'm sure the Hermetics will take credit for it and/or the nabobs of the nascent Order of Reason) it spread like wildfire. Because it works (sotto voice "Because it's the rules").

                The Avatar is a game mechanic, so it's got to be explainable somehow in a way that makes sense across the board. Even if it doesn't really make sense, it has to make sense enough because pretty much every Mage has the Background. After that, it can be anything from a Platonic daimon to your pet cat to the Voice of God to the static on the TV to bumblebees (as a concept). That's not Roman nor Greek nor Romano-Greek (nor Platonic nor Aristotelian etc.)

                The Union is not the hegemonic power with all the resources and never has been. (But they are insanely delighted that you think they are.) There aren't enough of them to do more than nudge and instigate.

                The Pogrom hasn't been a constant. It has waxed and waned since the Union was founded (and the Order).

                The Traditions have been having internal conflict over the ideologies and culture and magical praxes of the world from the get-go. The small group that started the Traditions came from Western Europe (like, utlimately the people who wrote the game) and they recruited outsiders, but it was still largely their clubhouse and their rules. And that has been a categorical weakness from the get go (by excluding groups, driving groups away, mashing groups together that didn't fit well). But as much as they have been overshadowed by Western thought, there has been a counter-current of ecumenicism and inclusiveness that flows in the other direction too.

                Social Darwinism is inherently about position. In that one's position is proof of one's fitness. (It's really really bad misunderstanding of the fundamentals of the concept as part of Darwin's Theory of Evolution... but oh well). Since pretty much every mage starts out as an Orphan who erupts, there isn't as much of a possibility of "good breeding" and prior placement mattering. Sure it may pop up and there is definitely going to be some gladhanding and party favors passed out. But the recruitment pool is "All of Humanity" and there's no knowing where the next potential will come from. That's pretty much inherently the opposite of Social Darwinism... The "best" people don't become mages. Anyone can become a mage. And there's bugger-all way to pass down excellence.

                The highest probability is that the game is what it says it is and the game world, while rich with secrets and contradictions and unexplained nooks and crannies, is pretty much exactly what it presents itself as.

                                Though it's not fun to be reminded of this - Mage: the Ascension is simply a set of roleplaying books. The world it describes only exists as far as its writers wrote. If somebody asks "Hey, what was Porthos doing in the Deep Umbra after WW2?" there is no answer because nobody wrote it down and published it.

                                If you think it would be cool in your game to have a specific answer to that question ("He was punching the Wyrm in the nads!" "He made Grandmother a cake made out of Forces!"), that's great, but you have to get your players to agree to fork off a new game setting in that way. Rather than modifying the published setting, you are creating a derivative setting in your head and copying it into the heads of your friends.

                                So if you want to create your own version of Mage: the Ascension where Nephandi are somehow the good guys for eating babies and making grimoires out of their flayed skin, and the Technocracy runs the Traditions as controlled opposition, that's fine, and good luck convincing your friends to play your custom game with you. But you cannot change the published setting because you aren't writing those books. If the books say Avatars are guardian angels, that the Spheres are pulled right from Kabbalism, etc. - then that is the objective truth of the game.

                                And if the books say that Nephandi are irredeemably evil, that nobody comes back from Avatar inversion unless an archmaster takes a personal interest and maybe not even then - then that is the objective truth of the setting.

                                If you're going to claim the entirety of the books are in-character chatter from universally unreliable narrators - where do you draw the line? Is the character sheet also propaganda? What about the table of contents? Does Phil Brucato even exist?

                                                    My two cents on Nephandi motivations:

                                                    An inverted Avatar means that they feel satisfied, happy, accomplished, and all that when they hurt, break down, or mar something. The warm fuzzies other people get from helping, they instead get from hurting. Imagine if someone got a shock to the pleasure center of their brain - or more reasonably, an injection of heroin - when they harmed.

                                                    On top of that, for barrabi or widderslainte who have Awakened, that inverted Avatar speaks to them. Rather than being pushed to perfection, Ascension, and self-improvement, they are pushed to ruin, to mar, and to break down. They quite literally have a devil on their shoulder.

                                                    Therefore, it's nonsensical to propose rehabilitating a Nephandus. Could you tell a normal person, "You must never help another person. You must never love someone else. You must never build something or leave a legacy"? Of course not. They might be coerced into behaving that way temporarily but they will be inexorably pulled to the direction their heart truly lies. The Nephandus is the same way - he or she cannot deny that their heart is in it when they do evil, and it will always be so. They cannot deny who they are.
