        Let's remember which parts of the planet the Mage factions influence. The Batini and the Taftani are major influences in limited areas of the planet. The two crafts share influence in areas that are basically poor (the oil wealth is sometimes called the "resource curse"), war torn, mainly infertile, and culturally stagnant. The Technocracy and the Traditions struggle over the richest, most populous, dynamic, and influential, sections of the planet.

        If either the Technocracy or the Traditions hold these areas for another century, it won't be hard to slowly but steadily force paradigmic conformity on the rest. Prosperity and stability will require it. If Afghanistan is still violent, backwards, impoverished, and ruled by drug lords a hundred years from now, it won't really change the world at large.

                        Unfortunately, a huge part of the history of Mage involved the Traditions and Technocracy picking up the idiot ball whenever it is convenient. Indeed both factions are basically long periods of ineffectiveness or laziness punctuated by short bursts of supreme competence.

                        Possibly the better interpretation is a long and convoluted shadow war full of named offensives and gambits, with world shaking (or ending) consequences narrowly avoided several times a century. That is, we can presume both factions have been very effective and it only seems otherwise because we don’t see just how much energy they put into countering each other or what the world would be like if they ran unopposed.

                        Outside of a few key events, though, we don’t usually see that so it is easier to consider the former than the latter.

                                        It's more complicated than that. And I had a whole lengthy post about that but I seem to be cursed and keep losing it because I can post it.

                                        So I will keep this short because I am tried of writing it out again and again only to lose it.

                                        While the Technocracy has been the dominant force in MtAs, they haven't actually gotten closer to achieving their goals in over at least a hundred years. Not because of the Mages or other Supernaturals but because of the Masses. Yes, the Masses have embraced Technology but that's really all they have. Science-denying Flat-Earthers and Anti-vaxxers not only still exist but are thriving. Hate-mongering nationalists and religious fundamentalists endure and rally against the unified, scientific world they wish to create.

                                        In the end, the Union is now stuck dealing with the various Supernatural threats and mundane problems like climate change while also having to fight against the stupidity and willful ignorance of the Masses.

                                        Hell, the introduction of Technocracy Reloaded starts with this:

                                        We’re losing.

                                        Yes, I know what you’ve been told, kiddies: Our magnificent Union cornered the market on Consensus. Mankind’s curled up with their smart phones, everyone loves technology, and cybernetic monkeys fly out my ass on command, raining fire on the poor little sorcerers cowering in the shadows of our magnificent Technocracy.

                                        I ask you this: Does it look like we are winning?

                                        Irrationality rules the day. Our world unravels at the seams. If our sad-sack species has formed any kind of consensus, it’s that cutting off your nose to spite your face is much more fun when you use a chainsaw in public to do it.

                                        We’re partly to blame for this, you know. We got stupid, careless, so caught up in our own propaganda that we refused to see the infection eating us alive.

                                        Worst of all, we got complacent. We believed it would only take a few surgical strikes on the worst offenders, and a wealth of luxuries for the Masses, before humanity would come around and enter a grand age of civil stability.

                                        We were wrong.

                                        Man is fundamentally irrational. He wants ghosts and vampires and boy wizards with English accents. He wants to believe that genital configurations or melanin production define him as blessed in the eyes of nonexistent gods. He doesn’t fear Apocalypse — he craves it, so long as he doesn’t have to live personally with the results. He’s more fascinated with wanking off in the mirror than with looking toward the horizon of what he could be if he’d just get his hand off his cock.

                                        Man cannot be trusted with the future. If we are to have a future, that future must belong to us.

                                        Kids, we dropped the ball. Our enemies have it in play, and they’re sprinting toward the goalposts.

                                        If they score one more time, they win.

                                        Our enemies play for keeps.

                                        But, so do we.

                                        So, get back in the game, my friends, and remove your heads from your asses before we lose it all.​
                                        A far cry from the more triumphant introduction of it's predecessor, Guide to the Technocracy. And throughout the book we get other Technocrats just frustrated with the Masses over their willful apathy and ignorance. In one section, a veteran field operative describes humanity stupidity as the worst thing that any agent will encounter, as while the supernaturals can cause all manner of headaches, they haven't started world wars or endangered the planet with nuclear Armageddon.

                                        It's also a bit of a continuation of the collective apathy of the Masses from Rev Ed where no one really won the Ascension War and it ended up largely being a pointless waste because the Masses decided that they didn't care about all that Ascension business and would rather wallow in the mud of apathy and ignorance.

                                        I will also add that while the Ahl-I-Batin and Taftani haven't really held out all that well against the Technocracy, the Disparate Alliance might actually be a real success for them and more importantly, are targeting a much more serious threat to the world, the Nephandi. Who have kind of become a blind spot for the Traditions and Technocracy. Yes, both do fight them but not nearly as much as they do each other. And depending on your Metaplot setup, may have become the unwitting dupes of the Nephandi. So one could argue that while the Traditions and Technocracy have become fixated on their conflict and a goal that is perhaps impossible, even for Mages, the Disparates are focusing their attention to a much real threat.
