    The problem is that you can't really learn the M20 rules with Arete 1, because you never really get into most of the magic variations until 2 or 3. All you're going to learn is the core WoD rules, that the OP is already used to from running VtM.

    I don't think you're wrong in that Mage works well if a lot of time spent on backstory, however you want to get to it, but it's really hard to know what to do with a backstory without either (a) an experienced Mage player/ST to help out, or (b) playing the game a bit without it to see what you're missing.

    Sure, this is just my personal experience, but I have never seen anyone really enjoy Mage playing an Arete 1 character unless they'd already played a character with Arete 2-3 at start first. I have seen at least a dozen players say, "Oh, I'll just start with Arete 1 to ease into things so I can learn as I go," and it has never worked out because they all run into the same problem of never actually learning the system without enough Arete and Sphere levels to play with the system.

                    Originally posted by artuard View Post
                    Hi guys, as per topic

                    I have a group of 4 maybe 5 people who want to play mage. I have some experience with WOD, was a gm for vampire, played old mage for a while so I'm open to the idea.
                    However I had a few questions if that's ok :

                    1) two players have a tendency for power gaming, is there anything in particular that i should watch out ? To nip it in the bud so to speak.

                    2) Would you have any advice for a new game, and this system? General info of what to do and what to avoid? etc.

                    3) Finally, should I start with the awakening as preludium? And if so, should it be done solo or as part of the main game?

                    Cheers!
                    I would agree with much of what has already been suggested starting at Arete 2, allowing takebacks on rulings, reading the Focus section.

                    If you are using the M20 through PDF format I would print out the Combat System Charts (pgs 445 - 457) and the Casting Magickal Effects and Magickal Reference Charts (501 - 510). Both of those are immensely useful to have on hand, especially the Magickal charts considering the complexity and open-ended nature of True Magic (aka Awakened Magic, Magick).

                    One thing that keeps me thinking is how do I present traditions. I plan to run this game in NY, US. Not sure how to build up the mage ladder there yet, but I'm getting there.
                    • Dont worry about having stats for the mages of the region, rather just a descriptor of their relative level of mystical power Initiate (Arete 1-2) Adept (Arete 3-4) Master (Arete 5+) and their affiliation and sect.
                    • Worry more about presenting the traditions the characters follow than the others. Also unlike in a game like Vampire, there is not a central authority like a Prince among the Mages of a city. Rather a city (especially larger ones) may be home to one or more chantries and there may be multiple masters and cabals within those.
                    • Traditions are not straitjackets, but rather groups of similarly minded and trained Mages. This means two mages of the same Tradition while being similar might still have many differences.
                    • Dont think about a ladder in terms of a region, but rather a chantry. Figure out which mages hold authority within a chantry and why. Then think about what area/node(s) that chantry has influence over and whether they have had contact with any of the other mages/chantries in the area. If so what kind of contact, do they know about each other but not much else, do they have dealings with each other (trading services, training, etc), do they interact peacefully or are they in conflict. Larger chantries with more members and holdings are generally going to have more influence than smaller ones. If a small chantry does have a lot of 'pull' why? Does it have more powerful mages, are its members wealthier or more connected than others, do they have access to a powerful node, etc?
                    • Remember Mages are still in many ways normal humans, and until one reaches the echelons of the masters, they are going to need the contact and interactions with normal society. So think about how they do that, do they have family in the region, what sort of influence(s) do they have, what properties do they own? An Etherite with ties to high finance and regularly attends stockholder/board meetings is going to come off differently than another Etherite who volunteers at the local library and offers tutoring to college students; remember this when presenting them, Traditions are made up of individuals not faceless legions.
                    • What sort of connection do mages of the same Tradition have within the region. Figure out how likely it is for a mage of a particular Tradition to know another member. If they do know of other members, do they have their own separate hierarchy (Hermetics are especially likely to have this).
                    • Determine what sorts of interactions the chantries have with other mages in the region. Which mages/chantries does the Technocracy have knowledge of and how much? If there are Disparates do they have any interaction with either the Tradition mages or Technocratic agents?
                    • If there are any powerful spirits you want to have in the region, have they had any dealings with the mages of the area and if so how.
                    • If you are going to have any crossover (warning: if you are going to cross over make sure you are very familiar with the game you are crossing over with, Mage is hard enough dont overwhelm yourself) figure out how much the different supernaturals know of each other. Also what interactions if any do they have with the Mages of the region are they in conflict or have the interactions been peaceful.


                    In terms of the initial questions.

                    1) As mentioned before, but worth repeating given appropriate Spheres and Arete nearly anything is possible with Awakened Magic and a power gamer can quickly figure out how to do very powerful things with even only a few Spheres.

                    2) See below

                    3) Either way is fine, depends on available time and player/ST preferences.

                    Question 2 is broad so here is a whole pile of tips, hints, and advice for a new Mage ST/Player (in no particular order)

                    Read through all the Spheres carefully at least twice. Knowing what the different Sphere levels can do makes figuring out Spheres needed for Magickal effects much easier.

                    After character creation go through each character and have the group come up with a Magickal effect using the character's Spheres. This will give you all a chance to get familiar with building effects, assigning vulgarity, and seeing what difficulty reductions might be usable. Then have the player of that character try to describe how their character casts the effect within the Focus (Paradigm, Practices, and Instuments) of their Mage.

                    Mages are normal humans in terms of soaking damage, so combat can be very lethal. Be aware of this and dont be afraid to pull punches if you suddenly feel like the threat you've put them against is suddenly a lot worse than you originally intended (basically takebacks).

                    Read the Focus section. Thinking about and describing a Mage's magick through their focus is not only great for roleplay but can give you a better feel for how the character thinks.

                    The characters' Avatars are an easy way to guide the characters and offer advice or hints. Don't overuse it, but it can be a helpful for when a player/the group is lost and just needs a hand.

                    When designing a chantry and placing NPC mages, think about how those mages came to the chantry and why they remained. Think about how their Traditions and Focuses interact and shape the chantry, both physically and metaphysically.

                    I have a pseudo-house rule that I call "Are you sure?" Whenever I add a new player to a group I tell them that if I ever stop, look them in the eye, and slowly ask "Are you sure?" It means that they are about to do something monumentally foolish, and should they continue I absolve myself regarding the consequences of their actions. There will be no pulled punches, no fudged dice rolls, no takebacks, no NPC to the rescue, if the character dies so be it roll up a new one. This doesnt mean I do this whenever the player is going to do something risky, foolish, or dangerous, but rather something so stupid and boneheaded that I can see no outcome other than their death(s) or the destruction of the game and they should immediately rethink their choice of action. Its a nebulous thing and I've only had to use it a couple of times over the 16 years I've been running games, when I have used it the player being jarred out of their thoughts for a moment was able to see the folly in their chosen path. I see it as a 'please dont ruin this chronicle/campaign/game just because you are being monumentally stupid'.

                                And, speaking of seekings -

                                Coming up and running seekings appropriate for the character, making then easy or challenging enough to reflect the appropriate level of Arete, and finding the right moment in a character's life for a seeking is probably *the* hardest things to get done right when you are a Mage GM.

                                Both you and the player needs to be invested in the character's development, and you also should take notes - when the character makes good or bad experiences, has rivals, enemies or friends. What people, events and items affect them, or which ones they ignore when they shouldn't. How their paradigm hinders them at times, or brings them great success.

                                And then challenge the character in the form of a metaphysical journey or puzzle. It needn't be a mindscape the character steps into durign dreamtime, but that's how most modern seekings happen.

                                                    Something that I don't think that I've seen directly addressed (if maybe tangentially) about your powergamers: Paradigm/Focus needs to be enforced. I've seen plenty of people come to forums or the table wanting to build a character whose Paradigm roughly equates to "I see the truth of reality, and thus make my will manifest." Players who know how the Sphere system works, and who understand the Consensus, understand what we call the "Purple Paradigm," and sometimes will try to make that transfer into character knowledge. That's a big no no.

                                                    Instruments (or foci, whatever term you're using) that are more or less "I cast the magick with my mind" should be highly discouraged, or at least regulated. Skipping all the stumbling blocks and restrictions that is inherent Paradigm/Focus removes an enormous amount of depth from the game and is a clear path to powergaming.

                                                    That's not to say that things like telekinesis, telepathy, and other tele- words are out of bounds for mages. On the contrary, they're pretty proliferate in the Society of Ether, and it's been at least "studied" in the halls of the Technoccracy. However, one can't, or shouldn't, simply say "I do it with my mind." Explain it. How do you do it with your mind? What principles are you using? Why can't other people do it? Do you use a point of Willpower to represent using mind power to do it? Do you concentrate on/through a crystal that has a specific resonating property that magnifies your brain power? Do you see where I'm going with this?

                                                    Magick, inherently in the system, has a price, some manner of inconvenience standing between a mage and immediate, unrestrained access to her magick. Maybe it's a dance or a chant, a magick word or a specific thought form, a consumed point of Willpower or a raised difficulty (as per the rules for casting without instruments).

                                                        Some ideas to simplify things at the start:

                                                        Be clear with everyone that you are learning the rules along with them and may need to make decisions on the fly, and reserve the right to change your mind later.

                                                        Sit down with the players and pick one tradition, and run as a single tradition. You simply do not have the time or energy to teach five different people five different paradigms, plus whatever npcs you need. Some people just don't grok technomagic or are turned off by the sex/drugs/rock and roll stuff. Find one everyone is comfortable with and can play. Avoid orphans, as newbies often need more defined boundaries than that. Hermetics, Verbena and, possibly, Hollow Ones can be good choices because they are so prominent in pop culture and other tabletop rpgs. You will only need to really understand that one paradigm in depth, and no one will get over-whelmed. Plus, the group will come out of the chronicle with a solid, solid understanding of at least one tradition. This will serve them well in later chronicles.

                                                        Start with interesting characters as people, not mages. Make sure that all the characters have interesting and strong relationships with one another, whether friends or rivals. If they all have goals, fears, personal foibles and interesting back stories, just handing them some magick will lead to their producing a plot on their own. Just be ready to have the world around them react to their actions by having a good grasp of the characters and their environment. Maybe give them all a group mentor who initiates them into their Tradition to help move the plot forward, if necessary.

                                                        Whatever else you do, start them weak.

                                                        Begin first session with Arete 1 and three spheres at level one. The first play session will be learning the basic mechanics of the system, both magical and mundane. Make sure every player uses their magic at least once to try it out. That level of magic is more than enough to learn basic conjunctional effects and the concept of coincidentiality. Reward them with an additional dot of Arete and a second level in one sphere of their choice at the end of session one.

                                                        This will give them an immediate sense of growth and power in session two, while still keeping things manageable. It will also let both you and them know which spheres you need to study up on. At the end of session two, give them all another level two dot, and an additional level one dot.

                                                        This puts them at the book standard 5+1 sphere dots at the start of session three. Congratulations, the prelude is over! Use regular experience costs from there. Also, by now the plot should have advanced, on its own, to a point where it's self-sustaining. Someone will have used level one spheres to learn things about their families and co-workers they'd rather not know. Someone will have let power go to their head and deserve repercussions. Trust the process. The plot will happen if you let it. Just focus on believable characters and the mechanics for now.

                                                        Good luck, and have fun.
