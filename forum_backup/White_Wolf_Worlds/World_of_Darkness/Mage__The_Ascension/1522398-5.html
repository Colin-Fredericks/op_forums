    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
    It's also important to note that the Technocracy isn't exactly The Man.

    Sure, they're very much The Man from the POV of the Traditions, but that's in a context of war. And they're built as a parody of every conspiracy theory about a shadow government.

    But since very early it was made clear that the Union isn't really the shadow government. It tries to be, and it has a lot of influence, but there are other things out there, and in the end the Union is far from having absolute control over the big institutions.

    There is The Man in the setting, but it isn't exactly the Technocratic Union.
    Mage is, IMHO, a rabbit hole and a beautiful rabbit hole.

    I do think that the game invited its audience to question everything and that resulted in the humanization of the Union and their perspective. But I also feel like it's a gameline that requires an antagonist at the wheel of reality that is fundamentally wrong. Chronicles of Darkness made the metaphor literal by creating the Seers of the Throne as the Matrix's Machines. They're the Gnostic Demiurge's minions and the people keeping everyone else asleep.

    And the motive of the Seers is, "Fuck you, I've got mine."

    Morally indefensible.

    The Union isn't that because it wants to wake up humanity as well and has a point. Which you might argue is much better. However, we're kind of in a society today where there is in fact a huge chunk of society that aggressively wants to keep people ignorant, stupid, poor, and an underclass. They never were NOT there. As you say, there is absolutely The ManTM in Mage because the ManTM exists in RL. People who are basically Pentex, that much-maligned bunch of cartoon villains.

    A flaw of Mage is that unless you make the Nephandi a massive group, Mage suffers from being too idealistic for its own good. There's no group that fundamentally does hold the keys to humanity's ignorance and supports keeping them jailed because at their worst, the Union is idealistic just like the Traditions.

    And humanity has shown there is a massive chunk who are anti-idealists. There needs to be a Seers, Pentex, or even Camarilla motivated by keeping mankind under the thumb. The Union outgrew that rule or never fit it properly.

    Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post
        I kind of think back to the Mage's inspirations whenever I'm thinking of this. Grant Morrison's INVISIBLES for example. At some point, the ideal Mage character went from being a Hollow One and his cabal of disparate friends versus the Union and became ideologically committed Order of Hermes or Unionists.
        I think it's just the opposite, actually.

        One of the innovations of M20 that's often overlooked is that it broke the stranglehold that the Traditions, Conventions, and Crafts had on paradigm in earlier editions of the game. That is, it used to be that if you wanted to play a Martial Artist, you did so by picking the Akashic Brotherhood as your Tradition, and it supplied the Theory/ Philosophy and Practice. In M20, the new Focus rules makes that sort of thing a fundamentally personal and individualistic choice; and the choice of which group you subscribe to is now almost an afterthought, something that can be completely skipped without harm to the character. As such, the game is actually much more adept at facilitating the "Hollow One and his cabal of disparate friends" style of gaming, whereas the "ideologically committed Order of Hermes or Unionists" are largely holdovers from previous editions.

        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
