                Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
                This is what I meant by Subjective Reality has to be complicated. And I mean it's complicated in the Facebook Relationship sense.

                There has to be a way that the Traditions can think that they are all doing similar things while preferring their specific paradigms. There needs to be a way for the Technocracy to think that it can alter reality while still thinking it is operating off of consistent (or at least semi consistent) principles.

                That sounds difficult, but it is quite common in our world. There are competing views about physics and metaphysics and theology all the time. Sometimes it is obvious to reasonable onlookers which side is right, other times there are compelling arguments on both sides and it takes years to work through who is right. That's because reality is complicated and it is difficult to take it all in at once. A complicated Subjective Reality would provide enough justification for the competing points of view we see.

                That doesn't mean it needs to be complicated for the players. We don't have have to have in depth explanations of the Traditions metaphysical arguments or comprehensive charts of the data the Technocracy has gathered. We can simplify it to a short and quick explanation with and cover the rest with an "It's complicated" disclaimer.
                Having things be complicated is fine; as you said, it is realistic. I just think that having it be a complicated subjective reality is a step too far. All you really need is the basic spine of the Consensus for the magic system to function. Having it be a complicated reality is fine. Whether or not that reality is subjective or not should be up in the air.

                    I agree. For instance, I fully want "da Truth" of Mage to be fully 100% OBJECTIVE. Not that it IS, mind you, but rather that it is POSSIBLE TO BE. Maybe we are all "fragments of God" living in a fully Gnostic world... But maybe the world really is materialistic and fully mechanicist and the universe follows a simple Evolutionary path, and "Da Science" is actually "Da Truth". And "Consensus" is just some weird trans-dimensional stuff that we don't fully understand... Yet.

                    Or maybe not. But I don't want either way to be set on stone, and I want all options to be on the table. And this needs to be made clear, not just "things are like this, but the techies believe that...". No. No such thing. There must be one section stating "Cosmology under Divine Revelation", then another segment with "Cosmology under the Scientific Principle" and "Cosmology under Mystical Insight" and so on, each with their own defining "truths" that are absolutely plausible and coherent.

                    Oh, and the game mechanics MUST also reflect that. I see many people scoffing at game mechanics having an effect on how the setting and the cosmology of the setting is perceived by players, but the truth is that game mechanics is almost paramount towards how "da Truth" is perceived. That's one of the reasons why I ditch the old 9 spheres completely in favor of custom tailored "Realms" (custom Spheres) to each individual group. While the Hermetics remain pretty adamant on them, they are pretty much the only ones that adhere to that system in my games; that has a unique effect of turning Magik no longer "translatable" towards different groups - just as a small example of how game mechanics directly impact the setting. Also, if for example you have a set of game mechanics for "Divine" or "Shamanic" "Magic" and another for "Wizardry" (Mystical Insight), then they ABSOLUTELY are not mutually communicable - and this is GOOD.

                    This is good because, in effect, it would be much like a Mage nowadays trying to replicate a vampiric Discipline; a Hermetic watching a Chorister could replicate the end result, but it would be a completely new "Spell" that is absolutely unrelated to what the Chorister is doing.

                    In the end, it could all still be "all the same thing" and it could be the case that it is all just "willworking" - but the belief makes it so that it is impossible to "translate Magik". Or not, or it could really be the case that each has a different source for their powers (or perhaps the Hermetic thinks he is doing willworking, but he is actually just a vessel for the Divine, or he's a Psyonic, or maybe he's a weak point in the Quantum Fabric of Space and Time... Or any other reason. But he believes he's practicing willworking or instead he's hacking the universal code or something like it).

                    All possibilities fully on the table.

                        "People actually have different sources for their powers but for simplicity they're represented the same way mechanically" is what I like to think. You have to have Consensus work on it all regardless, but you can account for this by treating Consensus as a magical effect that interacts with magic but doesn't necessarily come from the same source of it. My Etherite PC does her magic by incorporating magical materials into mundane technology and explains away Paradox as the collective psychic field interfering with the magical components, but that doesn't mean she thinks the alloy invented by the superintelligent lemurs of Lemuria was brought into existence through belief.

                        Anyway, as far as how I interpret the Traditions, I like a more idealistic take on them. And I like to treat the Etherites (at least some Etherites) as more legitimately scientific than the Technocracy, which ironically means that they're more willing to accept the existence of the supernatural than the Technocracy because they're not willing to invent pseudoscientific technobabble to make something out to sound scientific. My Etherite doesn't truck with any of this dimensional science nonsense because she knows that's not what a dimension is; it is very clearly the spirit world and she may not understand it but she doesn't need to understand how materials harvested from there work so long as they work in somewhat predictable ways.
