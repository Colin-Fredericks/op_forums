    Dimensional Science remains little understood by the vast

    it, in fact.) Only the Void Engineers practice Dimensional Science

                        The problem of separating the mechanics from the in character views of magic is that it isn't possible. The rules system is going influence how you view magic can be done. The problem with treating the rules that way is that it invalidates the in game concept of how magic works. The spheres are very useful but they force you to think a specific way in regards to magic when other options might make more sense to a character in universe. Take sorcery for example. The rules for that might better represent how someone believes magic works. Example being, what if a character believes that it isn't different elements (spheres) that make up magic and what you can do but different schools of magic (elements, alchemy, necromancy, etc.) While you can do all the things in those schools with spheres you might need a diverse amount of sphere dots in order to fulfill something that a sorcerer can do with like 3 dots in a path. Spheres are a good system but they don't mesh as well with the fluff. Which is why I personally prefer the Pillar system because it better works with the fluff even though it does cause problems with keeping track of everything.

                                            This works as long as you're willing to overlook some quirks where the Spheres imposed some restrictions on what you can do that tend to conflict with certain paradigms. Which is usually fine, and I'm not trying to tell you that you're doing it wrong if you don't permit alternatives to the Nine Spheres in your game; you obviously can run while Chronicles using just the Nine Spheres and not feel anything lacking.

                                            The only thing I'm taking issue with is the implication that alternatives such as Sphere variants, composite Spheres, and the Foundation-and-Pillars system from DAMage were mistakes, or at best superfluous. As I said above, I understand that you get along just fine without them; and it I had to, so could I. But there are reasons why each of these got introduced. Dimension Science came into being because Spirit doesn't map well to the Technocracy's paradigm; and both Data and Primal Utility follow that same principle of tailoring a Sphere to better fit a particular paradigm. The Foundation-and-Pillars system was introduced in DAMage to highlight the fact that the Spheres hadn't been invented at the time of the setting question, and provided a reasonably functional alternative to the Nine Spheres for those who want it. Even Composite Spheres (called “Minor Spheres” in the Revised Storyteller's Handbook) exist for the purpose of allowing for more customization for those who want it.

                                            And yes, all of these are optional systems; with a few exceptions, there's nothing you can do with these alternatives that you can't do with the Nine Spheres. But I'll just note that in Mage, the faction that insists on there being One Way to do things is generally presented among the antagonists.
