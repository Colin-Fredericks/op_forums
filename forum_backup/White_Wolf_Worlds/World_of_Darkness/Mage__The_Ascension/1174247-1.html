                        Originally posted by Faradn View Post
                        Ok, I've given it more thought and I think I disagree with the book. Without restraints like this, duration Effects can be really OP. With enough successes in a ritual, you could ward bullets for the entire Chronicle, and have footsteps that make no sound.
                        The #1 deterrent against this kind of thing is quite simple:
                        Magick-wise, the more and the stronger effects you got running, the more you are a beacon for anyone and anything that can sense Magick in the first place. Awareness rolls, Prime 1, the other level 1 spheres, etc.
                        So while it's probably not even that uncommon for some mages to have a subtle effect running at any time, the more you pile on, the higher the chances for nightfolks and Mage factions antagonistic to you to spot you from afar. Especially certain Mage factions that oh so love keeping an eye on anything magickal and abnormal...
                        And there is always the option to introduce exotic unknowns (or should I say, stranger things?) that get attracted to that kind of thing like fireflies.

                        The #2 deterrent which is kind of optional is retroactive paradox.
                        While M20 states that paradox is usually not retroactive (and I personally like that fact), it's always an option for the ST if you want a more low-key magick campaign.
                        Have the mage with the silent footsteps suddenly be hit by non-witness paradox if they just keep on vainly strutting across things that, by all means and purposes should be rustling and clattering and crunching beneath their feet. If anything, that mage suddenly has to be careful where they thread, and wether they put on a sneaky act doing so or not, while the hail of stopped mid-air bullets becomes a quick trip to backlash town.


                        I think both those options, especially the first, are better and more clever to use than limiting the duration and/or simultaneous effects logic in M20, as it meshes well with the tropes of long-term curses and blessings, enchanted items, traps in Sanctums and olde mysterious ruins, etc..at least while the Mage is alive. Else you need Wonders.
