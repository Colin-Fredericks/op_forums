                    Kharnov: Yep. The issue is more of a mechanics thing than a concept thing, I think: specifically, The Syndicate convention book retooled Prime as Primal Utility because there were large chunks of it that could be reinterpreted in terms of value (e.g., sources of power and expenditures of Quintessence such as lowering difficulties or creating Wonders); but not everything could. This is one of those things that led me to develop a hack for the technocracy that rejiggers where the lines among the various Spheres lie. For example, I move a bunch of stuff that's in Primal Utility but can't be explained as value over to a slightly modified variant of Dimensional Science, which I call Void. The official products can't do that, though; data has to be able to do exactly the same things that correspondence can and vice versa, Primal utility has to be able to do exactly the same things that Prime can, and dimensional scientist has to be able to do exactly the same things that spirit can. You're allowed to make changes in terms of how well they can do things, such as how Primal utility gives easier access to the creation of Wonders but has more difficulty with physical manifestations; but changing which sphere gets used for a given effect would require tinkering with the entire set of spheres at once and can't be managed when you're addressing spheres one at a time.

                                            Speaking to the topic and premises behind the topic, if you're looking for the Syndicate to expressly do things in "mage X does Y thing, and due to a direct causal relationship between X and Y, Z occurs" terms from a Doylist perspective, you're not getting very far. You're not getting very far with that perspective with any mage, tradition or technocrat. Let alone disparates, nephandi, or god help us, marauders.

                                            This is implicitly understood with tradition mages, because how else do we get rationale for hermetics slinging lightning bolts from the phallic symbol of that hermetic's choice? What the conversation's really about, is how does the Syndicate mage rationalize what they're doing, and how does that impact vulgarity.

                                            Now that I've gotten the statement of the obvious out of the way, why pick nits? And, why apply strict, restrictive language and conceptions of how mages do what they do?

                                            Creation ex nihilo doesn't necessarily need a Syndicate mage goofing off with a 3D printer, or some other convention's toys like some stupid Void Engineer's Star Trek fanboy replicator. They're the fucking Syndicate, they all but control global commerce. Hell, they're generally the Technocracy's requisitions department in all cases but convention-specific gear and requisite NWO oversight.

                                            All they need to do is file a requisition; the Primal Energy cost would be a deposit for the item or maybe some palm-greasing, a few phone calls or requested/called in favors would be the ritual roll. Goody arrives via courier or arrives at dead drop or in deposit/post box. If there's any organization on the planet that would have some deep web equivalent of Amazon -- or hell for that matter, a deep web-only extension of Amazon -- for buying the kinds of shit Syndicate mages would need, it's the Syndicate.

                                            Where did the item come from? does it matter if it didn't actually exist before the Syndicate mage filed the requisition? who gives a shit! As long as the item doesn't just poof from existence in front of a sleeper, it isn't something truly ridiculous, or its inconvenient lack of existence can be reasonably explained away, it shouldn't be vulgar.

                                            How does this not get tracked by the NWO or Technocracy? Again, who gives a shit! NWO's smart but they're not omniscient. Who's to say some Syndicate associate or EC, or hell an algorithm for that matter, didn't receive the requisition and disappear it out of intra-convention loyalty or something. If there's any organization on the planet that would have some deeply-ingrained processes for off-record requisitions for agents, it's the Syndicate.

                                            Hell the Syndicate probably has a fake requisitions officer whose entire non-existent job is the fulfillment of orders that didn't actually get placed, so that adequately-skilled agents can be told if they need to "make a requisition" they can file a request with Associate Akefay Ouchebag-Day, whose office is on the 13th Floor at 1060 W Addison St.

                                            From a Doylist perspective, is the Syndicate mage filing paperwork that doesn't matter, sending it to a requisitions office that doesn't exist, and in return some silly thing is poofing into existence at some random predetermined location? Well, yeah. But it's Mage, Mage doesn't work from a Doylist perspective.
