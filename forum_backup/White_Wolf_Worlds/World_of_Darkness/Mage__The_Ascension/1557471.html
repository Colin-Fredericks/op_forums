    "Nephandi Are Not Playable!" Except They Are.

    01-11-2026, 12:50 PM
    So, apparently, the writers of the Nephandi book made a huge fuss about how they would never create a player's guide for the Nephandi, and they were supposed to be exclusively antagonists. However, I have discovered a flaw in their plan. Said flaw being the fact that magic in the Ascension setting is pretty much an open sandbox, where you're free to do anything you want, so long as you have a convincing way to fit it within consensus.


    With that in mind, it should actually be quite easy to make a Nephandus into a playable character. Whether that be a reflavored Hollow One using their magic for evil, or a corrupted Verbena who kidnaps children to steal their youth. There's all kinds of ideas you could draw from to make this work!

      I mean, there isn't really a flaw in their plan. The game doesn't support playable Nephandi.

      They are well aware that people "can" play Nephandi against the developers' wishes.

      In a way, you're kinda making their point (and most specifically Saytros Brucato's point, as he's the most adamantly against playable Nephandi): playing Nephandi isn't about just playing evil mages that do scummy thing. Nephandi aren't Hollowers on "evil" mode. They're not mages that kill to extend their own lives.

      Voormas, one of the big bads of the game, is not a Nephandi and he's trying to destroy the world by destroying death itself because ultimately he's too afraid of dying to let it happen to him. He's a mass murderer running a cult of mass murderers, and still isn't as evil as the Nephandi are (even if he's very evil and very immediately dangerous in a fashion that needs stopping ASAP).

      Playing Nephandi as if they were normal PCs generally means diluting them into something that removes their role in the setting, or means you're playing characters with far worse morals than Voormas; and while most posters here don't like being told they can't play such a monster, there is a justifiable question of why we would really want to, Yes, we're playing the WoD, but every WoD game has antagonists you're not supposed to play even if you can figure out how to do it within the mechanical rules.

          Agreed. Nephandi are evil mages, but evil mages don't need to be (and for the most part aren't) Nephandi

          A "corrupted Verbena who kidnaps children to steal their youth" isn't necessarily a Nephandus. It's just a terrible person that has the means to do something a lot of old horrible humans would wish to be able to do.

          Some depictions of Nephandi show them jumping to the fiery arms of the demons they summon as if they didn't cared about their lives or well being. Ofc not all Nephandi are like this, but I would wager the "Ideal Nephandi" doesn't care about their health other than as a medium to better fuck up the world. Voormass was opposed by Nephandi because of his horrific quest for immortality, after all.

          Nephandi are - at their conceptual core - inverted mages whose Avatars search for cosmic annihilation. How they as humans relate to this dark enligthenment it's going to colour their goals.

          So, yeah, "a corrupted Verbena who kidnaps children to steal their youth" could be a Nephandus. Or she could just be an evil witch that doesn't care about the children, and believes herself above retaliation. There's nothing particularly Nephandic in that, per se.

          I think that a Nephandi character should be developped thinking about being the "dark reflection of mages". But what does that mean? .

          It's not a normal expression of Evil. Mages are already horribly evil people as a collective w/o being Nephandi. The Technocracy, the protectors of humanity, are all stronlgly bassed arround SciFy distopies about control (they have the Room 101 - for crying out loud). The Traditions like to see themselves as the better version of all of their groups, all with lofty ideals, but in practice they can't enforce this morality because their internal structure it's too focused on allowing freedom to actually enforce any kind of morality - and thus you have all ethical-council Etherites traveling with umbrella shields to protect themselves from the Mad Scientists menacing them with orbital death rays -

          For evil's sake - think about that: You can have a mad scientists that creates literal "death rays" to menace the innocent. And they're not Nephandi. They're a core option to play.

          Nephandi aren't like that. Nephandi being the "dark refection", in my opinion it's more like this:

          Mages are special people supernaturally driven to make themselves and the world better - for whatever that means (like: for an egoist mage, a better world could be one where he can do whatever he wants). Mages are people that can't be wrong, that don't accept to be wrong, not even if the world it's against their ideas.

          Nephandi are the opposite of that: supernaturally driven to destroy and debase themselves and the world. They're nihilists that don't care about being right, but do care about proving everyone else it's wrong. They're almost ontologicaly evil, as they express the dissolution of anything ethical with the ultimate goal of perpetuating suffering 'til the universe gives up and turns back to oblivion.

          Or, to put it much more simply (and canon, too). They're the "some people just want to see the world burn" type

          Like a textbook example could be a "road to hell it's paved with good intentions" character that's apparently peaceful and helpfull because his way to make people fall it's to make them go to extremes in their ideals, so, give them rope to hang themselves - then make sure they get to a point of no return where it's "compromise your morals or everything you fought for it's gonna crumble". Then, if possible, make sure it crumbles anyway.

          That's a Nephandi

          Personally, I wouldn't go for it:

          One in a normal chronicle would be a whole nother level of PvP, even above what's normal on Vampire or WoD. You have one player actively wanting to destroy the literal spirits of everyone else on the table.

          That's not just being evil - you can have an evil character that's also loyal to their own and can work as part of a group. The Sabbat it's cartoonishly evil, but in their own way they are noble and make sense. They can be noble monsters and are fiercely protective of their own.

          A Nephandi betraying the group it's the best thing that could happen to the group. A *loyal* Nephandi goalpost it's to make everyone ideals and goals to be broken and twisted 'til they accept to have their Avatar Inverted. Maybe a young Nephandi that's trying to figth against it could be more playable, but there's no concrete support for this on the books

          And what about an "all nephandi" group? I think that's less polemical. I imagine it would be a story that's all about carefully destroying the world I built, on a conceptual level. I think it's an OK concept, tho not one I care about exploring right now - and all the players can be on the same page on this.

          SO:

          The RPG police isn't going to hunt you down for playing Nephandi. It's totally doable. BUT it's not true that the "evil wizard concept" and the Nephandi concept are one and the same

          I made this wordly thread mostly to say that you can play all sort of villanous, evil, wizards and don't be a Nephandi

              More to the point, Book of the Fallen doesn't say "thou shalt not"; it says "there will be consequences if you do". Not in the sense that the Canon Ninjas will swoop in and steal your books, but rather in the sense that playing something like a Nephandus takes a toll on the player. That's why the book aims less at enabling Nephandic player characters and more at enabling the reader to identify and confront the sort of abuse that the Nephandi represent.

              Put another way, the message is that this is dangerous, and should be treated with utmost care.

                  Not all Nephandi are bent on cosmic annihilation. In fact, only the "Exies" seem to be wholly on board with that idea. Other groups within their order seek to reshape the world into something that is more in line with their twisted desires.


                  Mammonites will do literally anything to turn a profit, and damn the consequences. Not exactly seeing the point in making money, if you have nothing to spend it on.

                  Goat Kids are more obsessed with indulging their vices, to the point where the other factions dismiss them as a joke. Despite the fact they are supposedly the largest faction of Nephandi.

                  Heralds of the Basilisk are trying to create an A.I God who will reward them for their service, while punishing those who did not aid in its creation.



                  The Black Man, first and presumed leader of the K'llasshaa, was promised by the outer lords that if he brought them to earth, they would elevate him above all other beings. A promise which they end up keeping during one of the potential end times scenarios, as The Black Man ends up becoming the planet's new dark overlord.

                              Something that is fairly consistent across the editions is that there are effectively two kinds of Nephandi beyond specific classifications based on practices and entities they might deal with:

                              1) There are the "evil mages" that couldn't hide among the other factions and are recruited by the Nephandi. Those that don't show potential for true Descension are used as tools. Either by the Nephandus that recruited them, or offered to some other dark being. These are implied to be the vast majority of the Nephandi that get caught and killed by the other factions; disposable pawns that harm the world while active but can't be used against the fully committed fanatics behind them.

                              2) Then there are the true believers in Descension. Some might be young and reckless, but older Nephandi that know the battle is a long one keep them safe enough to grow into the ranks of the most destructive mages. The older members of this group are the things that make even the most powerful mages in the Traditions, Technocracy, and the Disparates fear for the future. These Nephandi might have started by making deals with devils, but they have the power to render such contracts as meaningless as the Nephandi view reality itself. Early stepping stones to gain power, but things that can be moved beyond in time. Some never pledged themselves to any specific entity or cause. They will, over a long enough period of time, reclaim their agency because not even the cosmic horrors of the Outer Dark realms can keep them bound forever.
