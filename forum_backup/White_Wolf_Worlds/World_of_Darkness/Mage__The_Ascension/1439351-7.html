                            The way I look at it:

                            post-Anomaly VEs: Battlestar Galactica (reboot)
                            pre-Anomaly VEs: Star Trek (pre-Abrams)
                            Sons of Ether: Doctor Who, Star Wars, Star Trek (Abrams&co.)

                            As presented in their original Conventions Book, the Void Engineers practiced “Tecknology”, taking advantage of the lack of any rigidity in the Umbra to go all out on force field, reactionless drives, phasers, and all the other stuff that would have been Vulgar on Earth. They still fit the Technocracy's overall philosophy, as CajunKhan just outlined; but their science was rather squishy — so much so that VEs visiting Earth needed to wear a device designed to alter them when what they were about to try would be Vulgar.

                            The Revised Convention Book makes no mention of Tecknology, or of needing reminders as to what's Vulgar what what isn't. They do practice Hypertechnology, and tend to take it to further extremes than the other Conventions do (the opposite extreme being the Syndicate, who are so devoted to keeping everything Coincidental that others sometimes wonder if they're actually Sleepers); but these days, they keep everything as extrapolations of the Technocratic Reality Zones on Earth as much as possible, and work harder to justify how what they have is an advanced version of what the rest of the Union has. Though on the flip side, they're in a hot war, and can't afford to ignore a bit of Hypertechnology just because they haven't yet rationalized it.

                            The point is that they used to play it a lot more fast and loose than they do now. Before, they were thumbing their noses at their Earth-bound associates; now, they're making an effort to work with them.

                            Presumably, the main reason for the change is that the bulk of the ones who were threatening to jump ship were in the Umbra when the Dimensional Anomaly struck, and died, Void Adapted, or made good on their threat to leave, leaving behind the ones with Earthside assignments, a more grounded outlook, and better relations with the other Conventions.

                            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

I have spent my life watching politicians callously sacrifice tens of thousands, hundreds of thousands, and millions of lives for vaguely justified benefits that never actually seem to help anyone but themselves. I have had to fear more than once that I would be "sacrificed". I just can't accept a version of the greater "good" in which tens of thousands or millions of human (or any sapient) lives are decreed worthless, for anyone's benefit. It's as awful as killing people for their organs. I'd rather die than murder someone to harvest their organs for myself.
                                                        Except the thing is it wasn't a cold decision. In fact, dropping the nukes was a last resort. Noticing how above I mentioned that the Technocracy was 'attempting to part the storm clouds' because they were. They wanted to burn [Ravnos] with their orbital mirrors but they couldn't get through the clouds. In fact when Tieh Ju and her Hsien companion noticed their attempts (and she did recognize who they were) to disperse the clouds they pooled their efforts together to strengthen the clouds.

                                                        Dropping the nukes to disperse the clouds was their last option. And as mentioned above, the Technocrat who declared Ragnarok and ordered the nukes to be dropped hesitated, he dreaded what he was about to do. He did not just declare these people worthless and wipe them because he saw them as less than human. Nor did it have anything to do with race as if it was happening in North America or Europe he still would have done it.

                                                        Because again, Apocalyptic Battle that was the start of the Countdown to the End of the World or at least what seemed like it at the time. And this Technocrat had two bad choices, either drop the bombs and kill whatever mortals are left in the area alongside [Ravnos] or do nothing and let the rampage continue to an uncertain end eventually. He chose the less bad option because while it would kill more people, it would be far less dead than if [Ravnos] was allowed to continue.

                                                        What you do in his place? Personally, I'd would have done the same thing because it was the least terrible option available to me.

                                                        Before you say something about knowing the Wan-Kuei would win because of material in other books, he wouldn't know that and by extension you in his shoes wouldn't know that. And the truth is, we the players don't even know if that's true.

                                                        In the World of Darkness there are also fates much, much worse than death. Gilgul is one. You say the Technocrats' bombs didn't gilgul all those people. OK. So then....who did design (well before the battle) the magic which tore apart "countless" Avatars into jagged shards that then went on to gilgul countless more random people? Who decided that was an acceptable risk? Or who screwed around with the cosmic forces that could do that, without bothering to learn enough to know what the consequences would be? Was it the Relic nukes in the Underworld?
                                                        What? What are you talking about? We don't know if the weapons they used caused Gilguls. Again, while the events of the WoN (which again extents beyond just the use of the nukes) helped cause both the 6GM and the AS, it was not the spark that ignited those things. Had the sparks not happened, those events wouldn't have happened.

                                                        As for the consequences, yet again this was a last resort scenario we're talking about. We see the protocols of Ragnarok after it's declared and just how serious it is.

                                                        Operations Budget: Unlimited
                                                        Permissible Weapons: Unlimited
                                                        Permissible Casualties:
                                                        Local Inhabitants: 100%
                                                        Associate Personnel: 100%
                                                        Enlightened Operatives: 100%

                                                        Ragnarok is the protocol for the Worst Case Scenarios, where they're looking at potentially world-ending disasters and as a result it must be stopped no matter the cost.

                                                        The Week of Nightmares was the only time they ever declared Ragnarok. That alone should tell anyone how serious this was, that the Technocracy would pull out all the stops to end, damn the consequences because that is far outside their normal operating procedures on Earth.

                                                        Because the Week of Nightmares was just some fight between ancient vampires, it was a battle of unparalleled destructive power that wasn't just affecting Bangladesh but the entire planet. A battle that could have spiraled into the end of the world in it's own right. Which I think is something that people tend to forget about. They think about the Avatar Storm and the Sixth Maelstrom for what they did but treat the Week of Nightmares as a... warm up act. And on the surface, I get it. We got Ends of Empire where we're waist deep in the 6GM and it helped cause the Rev Ed lines of HtR, DtF, MtR and Orpheus. While the Avatar Storm dominated the Rev Ed for Mage and continues to be a major aspect of it. The WoN on the other hand was a brief affair that we only ever saw from afar.

                                                        But as you can see from what I've talked about, it was a serious and destructive event that pretty much put the entire supernatural side of the WoD on notice.

                                                        It's just... very hard for me to believe the (non-VE) Technocratic claim that all Mystick Mages and all the sleeper cultures who believe in their magic must be genocided utterly to the last because they're the irresponsible ones. The Himalayan War and Dragon River War were certainly insanely destructive and unjustifiable, but ... less so than blowing up entire worlds in retaliation. Less destructive than the results of industrialization (which, in the WoD, is actually destroying the whole Earth and Middle Umbra, not 'just' the biosphere). Also those Mystick wars ended many centuries ago. Are there more recent examples of Mysticks causing cosmic mass destruction? Were they equally responsible for Doissetep blowing up? I'm honestly asking.
                                                        Well the VE are kind of forgetting/omitting the misdeeds of their ancestors (or outright shifting all blame to everyone else) because one of their predecessors, the Void Seekers did do some bad things in earlier times. Including attacking African Technomancers who's beliefs weren't incompatible with the Order of Reason's and stealing some of their stuff. An act that outraged members of other Conventions, both for attacking like minded Technomancers but also for doing so without consulting anyone else.

                                                        The Technocracy can really be summed up as 'The Road to Hell is paved with Good Intentions' because that's really what happened. The Order of Reason started with noble intentions, to protect the Sleepers from the abuses of Mages, Supernaturals and Bygones of the time, and to elevate them. They wanted to create a world free of the monsters that preyed on Mankind, where the Arts of Man, not Magick could achieve Wonders.

                                                        In fact, Mistridge, the first shots of the Ascension War, was Hermetic Chantry that had become particularly corrupt and decedent, using and abusing the local peasants for their own whims. And the rest of the OoH knew that but didn't really care. Hell, they didn't even care when it was destroyed. Some were even happy that the Craftmasons saved them the trouble of potentially having to deal with them eventually.

                                                        But then their growing success and power corrupted them, and they lost sight of their noble intentions. They destroyed the Craftmasons, the Convention that very much was the soul of the Order and were the strongest advocates of the Common Man among them. By the Victorian Era they had become the very thing the Craftmasons first fought against and what they brought the others together to oppose. They became advocates of Imperialism and 'Scientific' Racism because it benefited them and the spreading of their Consensus. Where they were once at times willing to speak with their Tradition rivals, they now see them as nothing more than the enemy standing in the way of the Ordered Reality they wish to create.

                                                        Because Power corrupts.

                                                        And yet even with how far they've fallen, there are people within the Union that see how far it's fallen and fighting to fix it, groups like the Friends of Courage and the Harbingers of Avalon. Men and women who are fighting to right the Union's course and bring it back to the noble ideals it was founded upon.

                                                        Homo sapiens. What an inventive, invincible species. It's only a few million years since they crawled up out of the mud and learned to walk. Puny, defenceless bipeds. They've survived flood, famine and plague. They've survived cosmic wars and holocausts. And now, here they are, out among the stars, waiting to begin a new life. Ready to outsit eternity. They're indomitable. Indomitable.
