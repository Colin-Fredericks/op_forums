    Anyone ever a game using Fallen Tower: LV?

    01-22-2018, 02:37 AM
    I was just curious what kind of use you guys have gotten out of this. There seems to be a few good ideas floating around in there, but I was curious what others have gotten out of it.

    What kind of awakened security measures would you put in place in the casinos? Any difference between Tradition and Technocratic security?

    I am not opposed to players cheating the casinos, but I don't want to present the mundane or awakened security as being foolish or lazy.

    FTLV describes the games and security in some detail, but leaves magickal security mostly up to the GM beyond saying that it exists. The mundane security is probably a bit out of date (no mention of face-recognition software, for example)

    I was also planning on cribbing the plot from Lord of Illusions as one of the stories. In short, nephandic cultists go on a murder spree killing former members of a cabal that bound a powerful nephandus archmage long ago in an attempt to raise their fallen master. Ordinarily, I would say Oz and Zydeco would be investigating something like that, but maybe they bring the PC's along to help, or are otherwise indesposed?

              Mazes & Monsters with Tom Hanks is a (in)famous movie about D&D. The minotaur is tied to the Labyrinth. So, given that Black Dog is a parody of White Wolf, well, there's probably some game designer the writers were thinking of when creating Peter Clarkston.

              Security has changed since the book came out, so it should be even harder to cheat using mundane measures. As for magickal defenses, I would imagine efforts have been made to made Entropy and Forces and Matter effects which allow cheating more difficult. That is, being 'lucky' should be more difficult beyond a certain dollar threshold and having a lucky spin or magnets for the roulette table and loaded dice should happen less often. However, mages are going to want to be somewhat subtle and there are costs to anti-cheating measures, so they may not be at the low-dollar tables or low-dollar slot machines.

              I have yet to use Fallen Tower: Las Vegas in a game, but the most skilled NPC mage in the player's chantry has to go to a convention there in about 5 months of in-game time. That mage has some tickets to Blue Man Group and a suite booked at the Luxor, so he could be joined by the PCs.

                              My current campagin takes place in this setting. It's been two years now that we play there. I can tell you a bit about it. My main inspiration source is the book untitled Last Call from Tim Powers. In my chronicle, there is some kind of truce between the Traditions and the Technocratic Union in LV, because LV has a king, one that is named every 21 years during a mystical Poker game.

                              The concept is that every player of this poker game comes with power and influence (most are Mages, but not all). The winner of the game keeps the major part of the influence, becomes the king of LV and gets power over magick and Mages in LV (at least, mystical influence). In the game, my players are characters that just arrived at LV where the king is missing because he was assassinated a few years back. They learnt about this game and decided to participate. They built influence and are now almost ready. The Big Day is coming soon. The Mystical Powers of the king actually comes from the Big Spring Node which location is revealed only to the king of LV.

                              The first king of LV was Bugsy Siegel. He formed the King of Shards, a cabal of Mages that ploted to take over, keep and share the influence and power of the city. King of Sword was Siegel, king of Coin was Malcom, king of Cups was Carreau and king of Wand was a mysterious man that fled LV after the last game in 1977. Now it's 1998 and a new game is coming. Sigel is dead (do not check the death date, I changed them for the sake of the script ^^) and was killed by Carreau who now wants the power for himself. Malcom is sure that he deserves to win the game because he has seen that he's fated to a great destiny. King of Wands came back in LV and mascaraded as a medium. My chronicle is about politics and hidden plot mainly. They are in the middle of the mystical war between the three remaining Kings of Shards.

                              I also expanded the background of the Anasazi as poeple who discovered something from the future and a solution to a coming big catastrophe. As some of you have gussed, the next game is in 1998 and it will happen just before the Horizon War, the begining of the events that will lead to the coming catastrophe: the Avatar Storm. This is the event Malcom saw in his visions and believed he was the solution to. However, the Anasazi were dying and they needed help to transfer their knowledge of the future.

                              My players were of course part of Malcom's prophecy (otherwise would be lame ^^). They are the ones who are the solution to the catastrophe. They now know that they have to win the game to have access to enough power to meet the Anasazi somewhen in Time and exchange solutions to the other's problems. They will lead the Ansazi to the Big Spring (back in their days at the end of a time journey) and therefore lead them to Ascension to save them (hence their desappearing). The Ansazi will give them a magical Wonder they possess that would allow its owners to travel between Worlds when the Avatar Storm will come. My idea was to lead my players to the point when they have to choose between doing nothing or trying to save the Traditions.

                              Because there is a time loop in my chronicle, I regularly give them permanent paradox points to maintain the pressure. They understood their role a few sessions back and are now focused on winning the poker game to access the Big Spring Node.

                              What I love about LV is the mood. I often describe magick taking place in casinos, where everything and every dream is possible. In a world where Mages are chased on sight (thanks to the Pogrom), LV is a city of dreams and wonder where the Ascension does not seem that far away. The characters learnt to play poker, they learnt the codes and superstitions coming with the game. The political situtation is interesting because their backgrounds involved being chased by the technocracy and arriving in a city like LV with that kind of imposed truce between the factions was hard on them.

                              You can also deploy many side quests like :
                              - LV is consuming to much water and the Hoover dam is losing power. As I made this place a major Node for the Black Pyramid, this can be a motive to solve LV problems.
                              - I also used General Martin as a way to increase the pressure on the players. Indeed, I made the Iteration X Construct dig deep into the soil to try access the mysterious power source from which they already took Tass (in the form of minerals). This is of course a way to the hidden Big Spring and it is a way to annoy my players ^^

                              Sorry for the long post, I hope it will give some ideas. I truly advise the reading of Last Call. It is a fantastic inspiration to help describe LV and the mystical aspects of its casinos and card games. From that book, I only took the plot about the king of LV. The story with the Anasazi are my own ideas that I got reading the introduction of the Fallen Tower (why did they disappear ? etc.)
