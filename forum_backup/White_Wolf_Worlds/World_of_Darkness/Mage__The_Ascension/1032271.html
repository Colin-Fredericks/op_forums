    Managing Paradigm

    01-15-2017, 03:50 PM
    One of the discussions in the Vampire forums has drifted over into Mage territory, so I thought I would ask the people of the Mage forum to contribute to the discussion through a separate thread. We are discussing the importance of Paradigm and how much STs should use Paradigms to focus the abilities of Mage characters within their chronicles. My perspective is that Mages cannot use True Magick to do anything outside of the limitations of their Paradigms because their Paradigms are what allow them to create effects of True Magick. Could you share your perspective on the importance of Paradigms?

      Paradigm makes magic. Without paradigm there is no basis for the mage to focus their belief.
      You can't do lay-on hands healing if you don't believe there's a God or other Deity who is channelling their power through you.
      You can't do blood magic unless you believe there is magical energy in blood.
      You can't hack the world unless you think the world has a code to hack.
      You can't make all sound disappear by creating a vacuum unless you believe sound cannot travel in a vacuum.

      Without paradigm, there is no magic.

              Well, my understanding it's the same. A mage needs its Paradigm. M20 goes so far as to say that mages can surpass the need to use their Instruments/Foci with time and Arete, but that they never surpass their Paradigm.

              Then again, some paragraphs disagree, as there are situations in wich a mage can do stuff without believing strongly in what he's doing (it's +2 to difficulty and vulgar with witnesses). The rare defect Sleepwalker permits the creation of a mage character that doesn't believe in his own magick (or in any magick). Stuff happens when she realy wants to, but the very mage count as witness. But the spirit of the rules seems to be that, in general, a mage needs to believe in what he's doing to do something.
              It says "the fact that you don't believe that god wil send angels to help you, doesn't mean that you can't pray for it to happen". That's how Sleepwalkers do their stuff.
              However, this implies that the mage believes...otherwise, why pray instead of meditate, cry for help, or do some stuff appropiate to a non christian religion. I can't pray if I don't know the superstitions arround that gesture. In a sense, Sleepwalker doesn't mean that the mage "doesn't believe", but rather that has a weak belief. The kind of beliefs you have latent for your culture. Not enough to "properly" shape reality, but the Avatar can work with it.
              In M20 there are rules for grabing the focus of other mage and use it despite your Paradigm (+2 to the difficulty, for unfamiliar tools, and it's Vulgar with Witnesses). Like if you were Sleepwalking, but voluntarily. Which it's something I abhor, and goes contrary to any Mage book ever written, but it's there.

              There are instances where magick can be cast unconsciously. This it's called Wild Magick, and, obviously, isn't limmited by Paradigm. It has it's own rules, and is very rare. Usually it happens in the Awakening of the mage, before Paradigm "sets in".

              A mage can do anything, but only with great effort, this effort coalesce in the Focus/Paradigm.
              In other words, a mage's magick IT'S is Paradigm. The fact that the curses, magick words, and all other stuff works it's construed by the "reality warping" of the mage in action, changing the rules so that they adapt to the Paradigm. That's why, in a sense, everything that the mage does it's magical. That's why it's said that Mages ARE magick. They don't "do magick", they live it.

              This doesn't mean that Mages live in permanent solipsism. They have strong beliefs, but these can be contested, expanded, and even broken. In these situations, the magick of the mage changes. A mage can learn from other Paradigms, expanding his own to addapt to the new data. A mage can have a crisis of faith, or convert to other Paradigm. In this case, I see the use of the rules for Sleepwalkers, until the mage can solidify her Paradigm, but I don't see magick outside ANY Paradigm. Even the Sleepwalker needs to pray, after all.
              Neither I see this "movement of the mind" (the one necessary to do stuff outside your Paradigm), as something that can be done casualy. That is, outside a crisis of faith or something equaly deep. Albeit M20 disagrees on this (paradoxicaly, if we take in that they went out of their way to deny that a mage can ever surpass his Paradigm).

                          For all the talk of how "paradigm is everything," in application paradigm is largely window dressing. Cases were paradigm actually hard restricts a mage's capabilities are rare edge cases at worst.

                          Paradigms are how a mage sees magic in a very big picture way. Most mages can't just lay their hands on you and pray until you're miraculously healed in a brief moment. The vast majority of mages have some in-paradigm method framed by their practice(s) and instruments to use Life 3 to heal someone.

                          The "limitations of paradigm," are generally vastly overblown in terms of practical application to game-play.

                              I agree to a certain degree with the most common statements here, and of course with Heavy Arms observations, but I want to add something.

                              The game is about Paradigm, but not only this. It is about transcendence, including transcendence from one's own Paradigm. OoC this is represented through shedding Instruments/Foci as one ascends in the Arete ladder. IC this is further represented through a number of characters that, being or not able to make use of this, just grasped the truth: that magic comes from Will, not Belief.

                              At the end, the game offer simple rules for anyone, at any level, to just ignore Paradigm and do shit. At the end of the day, the power comes from the person. But even archmages will follow their Paradigm most of the time, because it is as much their will as the final effects. Belief shifts from being "how I think that the world works" to "how I think that the world should work".

                                                  I agree with this and with Manus. Characters can get REALLY creative and try to access parts of the possibilities of their Spheres, but for each Paradigm (old meaning, new meaning as a part of Focus is something completely different) there are things that are easy and things that are going to be difficult. And, when something is difficult, if there is some way to torture the Paradigm into making it happen, it tends to be something pretty complex and difficult and takes time, usually without the extra advantage of getting reduction on Diff.

                                                  Recently this came up in my current game. We have two characters with Mind 3, one in the NWO and the other a Cultist transcendentalist poet from the 19th Century (sorta like Thoreau if he was a wandering mountain man). The party was scattered across a small commune/farm doing their own thing when something came up. The NWO (who is the acknowledged party 'leader' insofar as such a role exists), wanted to set up a telepathic network, linking their minds together in a sort of "party line". But the questions came up as to how he would do that considering his Focus and the NWO paradigm. He finally just settled on contacting them all by a message to their miniaturized cell phone/bluetooth/ear buds (because the group has a sorta Son of Ether... actually a 1940's era Technocratic Virtual Adept, but he's "getting better"). It was easier to just use actual technology. Later the Cultist wanted to do very much the same thing, but he could. He drew pictures of them in his journal and wrote a sort of call and response poem about a fictive/actual telepathic meeting of the minds they had.... It made the communication a little weird for the participants as what they thought they were saying would echo in their heads as Hank's poetry and what everyone else would hear was that poetic version of their intended communication. But it worked.

                                                  We had the usual "Magely" discussion about the whole situation and it became obvious that there were also things that Douglas, aka Agent Martin, could do that would also be difficult for Hank to pull off.... Like using his Mind 3 (yes, yes, there was also Correspondence involved for all this), he could use the ear buds as a communication medium and use his Mind 3 to completely encode the communication. So they could sound like they were talking about the weather or sports, but Douglas could be dropping words with the right intonation and cadence to convey his actual meaning and, in doing so, actually "clue in" the other participants so they were all "in on" the code and would/could use it back. Hank would have had a much harder time trying to do that, as his Paradigm is all about "Truth" and "Beauty" and "Naturalness" and using words that way is lying and unnatural and therefore ugly.

                                                          Originally posted by Aya Tari View Post
                                                          One of the discussions in the Vampire forums has drifted over into Mage territory, so I thought I would ask the people of the Mage forum to contribute to the discussion through a separate thread. We are discussing the importance of Paradigm and how much STs should use Paradigms to focus the abilities of Mage characters within their chronicles. My perspective is that Mages cannot use True Magick to do anything outside of the limitations of their Paradigms because their Paradigms are what allow them to create effects of True Magick. Could you share your perspective on the importance of Paradigms?
                                                          I'm just going to quote the Guide to the Traditions here and where it discusses the four different styles of Paradigms:

                                                          • Rigid: Mages with a rigid paradigm know that their way is the only true way. What other practice might look like magic, but it is definitely not. Such a mage has to translate every rote or spell from another paradigm than his own into a way that fits his style.
                                                          • Closed: A closed paradigm acknowledges that other paradigms might exist, but assumes that they are incomplete or otherwise unfit for magic.
                                                          • Open: An open paradigm holds that other paradigms might contain a kernel of truth, but that his personal paradigm works best for him. He has no problem sharing and adapting knowledge from other traditions.
                                                          • Liberal: A liberal paradigm holds that everything is true and therefore combines multiple assumptions from various paradigms into a wild mish-mash.
                                                          Considering that the same book states that Mages with open and liberal paradigms can learn rotes, effects, and styles from other Traditions, I'm not seeing paradigm as the straightjacket you're suggestion.

                                                          If my paradigm as a Celestial Chorister is "God makes His miracles work through me," then it's really hard to justify why any particular Sphere effect is out of bounds. Unless you're trying to argue that the Chorister would see that God is somehow limited in His actions, and therefore can't generate a cloak of invisibility around the Chorister using Forces 3, or give them the ability to fly like an Angel with Forces 3, or let them call down fire from Heaven with Forces 3...

                                                          ...or become an invisible, flying predator drone while calling down fire from the heavens using multiple Forces 3 effects...

                                                          This is the problem of a liberal paradigm with a vague, yet valid description of "God does it," if you're trying to use paradigm as a straightjacket instead of a tool to enhance roleplaying. The more liberal the paradigm, the more you can justify using your Spheres to do, and the less incentive you have to play a rigid or closed paradigm.

                                                          Personally, I always prefer to think about the effect I want, then justify it through paradigm. Not think to myself "Would my Akashic monk believe he could fly using Forces?"
