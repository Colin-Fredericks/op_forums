              (I've probably mentioned all of these on the pinned thread, but honestly can't remember.)

              Books/Fiction
              • Frankenstein by Mary Shelly, the classic Regency era story about a man trying to play God and paying the price for his hubris.
              • The Invisibles by Grant Morrison and various artists, about a secret cabal of magicians fighting to save humanity from physical and psychic oppression by hidden overlords. (One of major sources "borrowed" to create The Matrix.)
              • Promethea by Alan Moore and various artists, about a woman who becomes the avatar for the embodiment of mystical knowledge, and as a vehicle for Moore, a practicing magician, to discuss his own philosophies about magic and reality.
              • John Constantine: Hellblazer by Jamie Delano and John Ridgway, the early adventures of the British occult-horror detective, before he turned into a safe caricature of himself, involving a fair amount of political satire mixed in with its modern style horror and character study of a man often forced to confront his own personal failings.
              • The Dresden Files series by Jim Butcher, starting as a small scale occult investigator and professional wizard in modern Chicago and gradually growing more and more epic in scale. One of its strengths is the main character and his allies often being forces to grapple with the morality of the threats they face and how they choose to deal with them.
              • The Walker Papers series by CE Murphy, about a Seattle mechanic who becomes a shaman, trying to balance her Cherokee and Irish mystical heritages.
              • The Magicians trilogy by Lev Grossman, about a group of students from the only North American college for magic and how their own choices tend to keep undermining their own fate.
              • The Blue Ant trilogy (Pattern Recognition, Spook Country, Zero History) by William Gibson, which are essentially cyberpunk novels set in the modern day, involving themes of corporate branding, international espionage, underground maker movements, and the post 9/11 security state. Also, working backwards, Gibson's Bridge trilogy (Virtual Light, Idoru, and All Tomorrow's Parties) set in a near future San Fransisco, and the classic cyberpunk Sprawl trilogy (Neuromancer, Count Zero, and Mona Lisa Overdrive).
              • The Jane Hawk series by Dean Koontz, involving one woman's underground crusade against a technocratic conspiracy of wealthy elites using nanotech mind control. Also his Odd Thomas and Frankenstein series. The majority of Koontz's work tends to involve the struggle of decent people contending against the amoral (and often psychotic) advocates of "progress" and control.
              Films
              • Lord of Illusions (1995) - Clive Barker's film about an occult detective and a magician verses an apocalyptic cult leader.
              • The Craft (1996) - Outcast teen girls turn to witchcraft for fun and profit, only to find themselves biting off more than they can chew.
              • Pi (1998) - Involving advanced mathematics, gematria mysticism, and secretive cabals trying to manipulate the stock market.
              • Dark City (1998) - Sci-fi neo-noir in which a man with no memory is pursued by the police and mysterious beings who are able to manipulate reality at will. Extremely heavy gnostic overtones. (The other major source The Matrix heavily "borrowed" from.) BTW, watch the director's cut, as it gets rid of the studio-imposed monologue at the beginning which completely spoils the entire plot.
              • Fight Club (1999) - Two men forming an underground fight club which evolves into an anti-corporate revolutionary movement.
              • The Gift (2000) - A local wise woman and psychic caught up in a murder mystery.
              • The Adjustment Bureau (2011) - In which two individuals fight to decide their own destiny when confronted by he forces which secretly govern reality.
              Obviously, most of my Mage games tended to revolve around Noir/Neo-noir elements and themes, as well as paranormal conspiracies, various subcultures, and people being undone by their own hubris.

                          The Canadian tv series, Intelligence. It depicts a spy agency doing the actual dirty work that they do, consorting with large scale drug smugglers, carrying out assassinations and scheming in order to get more funds. One of the central plots had to do with a mole. This really improved my ability to conceptualize Mage plots in a setting with a region-specific Ascension Truce. There are various opinions about the reason for the series being cancelled.

                          Also, The Box, a movie based on the short story, Button Button. This had previously been turned into a Twilight Zone episode.
