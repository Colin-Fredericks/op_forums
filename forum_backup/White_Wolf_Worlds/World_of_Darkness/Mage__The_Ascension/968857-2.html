    Originally posted by Sergeant Brother View Post
    I don't think that the Syndicate could solo the Giovanni so easily. Trying to ruin them financially would likely cause as much problems for the Syndicate as for the Giovanni and the Giovanni would likely be able to reestablish their wealth relatively quickly. The Giovanni have access to ghosts which would be incredibly difficult for the Syndicate to deal with and would allow the Giovanni to gain information on the Syndicate and attack them in a variety of ways. The Syndicate could assassinate some Giovanni vampires but I assume by "the Giovanni himself" you meant Augustus - I don't know if the Syndicate could kill him. Assassinations would likely cause reprisals and it only takes one Syndicate Mage to be captured by the Giovanni and Dominated/Blood Bond to open up all of the Syndicate secrets to the Giovanni and give them access to a servant with true magic. I don't even think that the rest of the Technocracy would let the Syndicate take on a major foe like the Giovanni alone, it would be like a single US state declaring war on a major foreign nation.

    Now, if they are sure that the Giovanni and going to destroy the shroud between life and death, then yes it may be worth the risk and would certainly justify getting the rest of the Technocracy (and maybe even the Camarilla) on board with taking down the Giovanni. But unless the Syndicate see that as an immediate possibility, they are unlikely to go to war alone against such a powerful foe. It just wouldn't be profitable.

    Of course, as haldir says, the game is about having fun and if you want to have the Syndicate take down the Giovanni in your game, have at it. Though I think that it would make more sense given the respective nature of each group and would likely be more fun as well if (given the Syndicate and Giovanni fight) that it was a more even and drawn out battle.
    How the Giovanni could reestablish their thousands-year-accumulated wealth quickly? Via generous usage of Domination, perhaps? And post-Anomaly Syndicate has their own secret Spirit division, namely Special Information Security Division. One of its primary task is finding, investigating and analyzing SPD products through Dimensional Science. Thus I think the Syndicate could counter Ghosts with relative ease.

    As for the reprisals, the reverse is also true. The Syndicate could capture any Giovanni Vampire and interrogate it with Mind magick many of their numbers are so well-versed at and exhume valuable information and secrets. Oh, and they could brainwash the Vampire, also, and use it as spies, informants and deep-cover agents.

    And to dispatch the August himself, the Syndicate could reliably send a bunch of Masters of Force, who the Syndicate has aplenty.

    Finally, remember even a single US state is able to beat a lesser nation with relative ease. But it seems you are assuming the Giovanni could go toe-to-toe against the Camarilla and the Technocracy, both are arguably the biggest and the mightiest power players in the setting. I have always thought the Giovanni is one of the lesser players, and only managed to survive thanks to cunning exploitation of incessant conflicts between the Camarilla and the Sabbat, two of the major players. But we are all aware of the fact that if only a fifth of US military assault the Maxico, the outcome is already predestined.

    In any case, I think the outcome could be differ whether it is the Mage-centric setting or not.

                                Originally posted by Enginseer-42 View Post
                                So essentially your argument is that we should fuck over literally every game line except Werewolf. Because to do otherwise is to be lazy. Even when there is an existing game line who's existing cosmology can neatly contain the others independent cosmologies with minimal disruptions?
                                It is a Flying Spaghetti Monster argument. Whether you are using a bearded man, a flying spaghetti monster, or a sapient lasagna, it still suffers from the same fallacy, a fallacy that is based on an unwillingness to accept that human beliefs are nothing to reality. Humans are only important to humans, the Earth existed for over four billion years before humans evolved and it will likely exist for over four billion years after humans go extinct. Mages, in their arrogance and their hubris, cannot accept that they are inconsequential to reality, so they create their own myths that support their relevance. Instead of the Consensus just shaping magic, they claim that the Consensus shapes reality (which is really like how small children occasionally refuse to believe that anything outside of their experience exists because it conflicts with their own unconscious narrative).

                                Of course, the reason why the Mage books support the Mage cosmology is that they are written from the Tradition perspective (just like Vampire is written from the Kindred perspective and Werewolf is written from the Garou perspective). The style of White Wolf during the 90's was to always support the subjective position of the supernatural creatures that they were focused on, regardless of how odd their beliefs may otherwise be, because White Wolf liked giving their creations voice. The adventures were also written from the same subjective position because White Wolf was always fair in that way.

                                My preference for the Garou perspective over that of the Mage perspective and the Vampire perspective comes from the simple fact that it contradicts science less than the other two perspectives. While many things about the Garou perspective are not terribly scientific, at least it acknowledges the basics of astronomy, evolution, plate tectonics, etc. The perspectives of the other major supernatural creatures do not even acknowledge even those simple and straightforward ideas.

                                            And the Traditions do not do the same to the people who endanger their Consensus? There are no 'white hats' in Mage, there are just lunatics who failed to Ascend who range from 'gray hat' to 'black hat'. That is the entire point of Mage, in fact, the disappointment and the insanity that comes from failing to give up power so that you can Ascend, the grasping desire to have your cake and eat it too.

                                            That is why I like the game, as the condition of Mages are literally the most tragic of all the supernatural creatures in the cWoD, because they are failures who have taken power (Arete or Enlightenment from the Mage perspective) over Ascension (Nirvana from the Buddhist perspective). Every Mage in existence has failed at least once, and probably more than once, to choose Ascension and, instead, chose power. Now, they may have good moral reasons for their choice ('I have to take care of my children', 'I have to finish a cure for cancer', or 'I am in love'), but they will almost inevitably continue to choose power over Ascension because power because the reason for their existence.

                                            The greatest tragedy of course, is that they think that they can gain Ascension as well as power if they cheat, by 'furthering' the Ascension of all of humanity, which would ironically mean the mass extinction of humanity. Whether they belong to the Technocracy or the Traditions, the Marauders or the Nephandi, they are all afflicted by the same tragedy that unifies all Mages. It is strangely appropriate that Mage attracts the type of players who would choose power over Ascension.

                                            When I would run my Mage games back in the 90s and 00s, I would give my players a choice with every successful seeking. On one hand, they could choose to Ascend and, in the process of Ascension, cause one small change to occur in the world of their choice (the equivalent of a level 5 Numina effect with 15 automatic successes, representing the energy released by the sacrifice of their humanity). In addition, I would allow them to create an equivalent character, with experience points equal to the amount that they earned, who they could introduce in the next session, though they would have a minimum of four sessions before they could have another chance at a seeking. On the other hand, they could choose to purchase a dot of Arete. Not one of my players ever took the first option, ever, because they unconsciously desired power for their character more than they desired Ascension for their character.

                                                            Also, Science is not the enemy of Magick or Faith. Sir Isaac Newton wrote extensively on all three topics. And there are plenty of groups within the Traditions that incorporate or espouse some form of Scientific or Technological thought. Not just the two technomancer Traditions, either, but a multitude of technomancers within normally mystic groups. Just taking one example, the Alexandrian Society, in the Celestial Chorus, are fully on board with Science. They just want to reconcile it with the existence of a Creator.

                                                            The reason the Traditions oppose the Technocracy is because the Technocratic Union works towards an autocratic agenda. The Technos would have the world dancing to the beat of one drum - the drum the Union controls. That drum just happens to be Science, but it could be anything if the Union thought it could control humanity. The Syndicate, for instance, would use nothing but Economics to control the world if it really needed to. If Magick became a known quantity in the world, the Syndicate would be looking for ways to monetize it (and, through the function of markets and supply/demand, control it).

                                                            I'm 100% positive there was a sidebar in M20 - fairly early in the core book - that explained this very thing. That Science wasn't the enemy - authoritarian elitists were.
