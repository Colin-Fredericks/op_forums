      A partial answer to this is that reality is influenced by human belief, but it isn't defined by it: there are factors in okay beyond what humans think. There are different views on what those “other factors” are (for example, Choristers point to the One while Etherites point to the inherent properties of the Ether, a notion that's not too far removed from the Technocracy's belief in the existence of objective laws of reality); but every faction that's being honest pretty much has to acknowledge that such factors exist. Even if they're not absolute; even if, with sufficient effort, these Earthly Foundations can be altered, they are still a thing that can't just be handwaved away.

      Magic started to “fail” in the Dark Ages because the Earthly Foundations shifted. The shift did not come from human beliefs; but it still happened. Why? Who knows? That's why I said that this is only a partial answer. If the Choristers are right that the Earthly Foundations represent the will of the One, then they shifted in the Dark Ages because the One willed it. If the Etherites are right about the Earthly Foundations are fundamental properties on the Ether, then the shift in the Dark Ages is evidence that the properties of the Ether can evolve over time. Other explanations of what the Earthly Foundations are will give other reasons why they shifted in the Dark Ages. And this being Mage, there's probably no way to single out one of these explanations as “the one true answer”.

          It's also worth considering that magic didn't actually "fail."

          One of the changes in human culture, esp. in Europe, at the time was a practical lessening in the power the elite had over the lower classes. Farmers continued to farm largely the same they had been for generations, while kings and aristocrats saw the reach of their power shrink rather drastically. The end of the "dark ages" saw the beginning of what we'd now call the middle class: people without overt political authority, but as a group enough importance economically and socially to force the ruling elite into concessions.

          Most mages at the time built their philosophies around older concepts of hierarchy. Gods, spirits, divine law, and so on, even for the Fellowships like the Spirit Talkers that didn't care about empires, inspire a conceptualization of society that is very structured. And tends to put mages at the top of that structure. Mages that refused to adapt saw their magic get harder. As Data pointed out in a more grand fashion, things shifted. But individual magical practices failing because the mages didn't account for changing conditions doesn't mean magic itself "failed." That's just the sort of hubris/arrogance mages are somewhat famous for having talking.

          If you look at the transition from the Dark Ages setting to the Sorcerer's Crusade setting... magic in many ways got better. It might not have been as easy for individual mages to master, but magic flourished among even more mages. For the dominant mages in any given area, magic wasn't harder to do, it was only if you tried to go somewhere else that suddenly things got weird for you.

          There's also some intention from mages there. The Order of Reason was trying to create a "pure" magic that was based solely on those Earthly foundations and was experimenting with how to bring that about with all of the older stodgy traditionalists wanting to keep doing what they always did around. The splintering of Reality, and then the spread and consolidation of Consensus, was not just some fluke alternation of the universal rules... it was perhaps the greatest feat of magic ever performed: literally changing how magic itself worked. If you look at the modern Technocracy did magic... ahem... Enlightened Science, actually fail? Or did it simply bend to a centuries long effort to make it the providence of a new group of elites? The Union might not have everything they wanted, but it's hard to say they failed many of their objectives for taking control of things.

                  You say that like they're ultimately different things. Modern paradox is caused by consensus, which is the product of the last few centuries of OoR/TU magical influence to alter the way humanity perceives the Earthly Foundations; and thus possibly be shifting those foundations themselves.

                  Consider the physics of dropping a steel ball from a great height that impacts the Earth. Did the ball hit the Earth? Did the Earth hit the ball? Which caused the damage both suffered?

                  While we tend to see things from a certain perspective (namely because the Earth is so massive and changes so little compared to its totality we view it as a static non-actor), in physics collisions are a mutual affair. Both bodies involved impart energy to each other, in one giant net event of various forces.

                  You can't separate things out. The end result of a steel ball impacting the Earth requires the ball, the Earth, and the initial push that causes the impact to happen. Likewise you don't have modern paradox-causing consensus without the magic that went into reshaping humanity's relationship with magic in the first place.

                  Though you can't call the Union's efforts "one" ongoing ritual. It's the impact of centuries of magical and mundane influence on the world combined. That's why the Traditions can't just change Consensus with more magic. The OoR might have demonstrated that it can be done, and the Union might have executed a specific outcome, but look at how much work that took to do.

                      The Triat.

                      Mage isn't centered around the Wyld, Weaver, and Wyrm the way Werewolf is. But it is entirely possible that the appearance of Paradox and Scourge in the Sorcerers Crusade era could have been because the Weaver decided to step up its “calcify reality” agenda, and the Wyrm stepped up its “tear down reality” agenda. Under this view, the Earthly Foundations started to shift because the Weaver and the Wyrm took action.

                                                          The upcoming Operative's Dossier has a system in it for Terranorming which could be interpreted as something like this: as written, it requires the use of a source of Primal Energy, such as a Venture, that can be dedicated to the Terranorming. This is as opposed to the “Shifting the Zone” rules found in M20. One way to interpret this is that the M20 corebook's rules represent shifting the Zone purely by manipulating the Consensus, while the Terranorming rules in the Operative's Dossier deal with altering the underlying Reality Zone.

                                                          In a nutshell, the Terranorming process books down to: “Reprime” the Zone by dedicating a suitably large source of Primal Energy to the task; this “softens” reality (the book's phrasing), which I interpret as making the Zone a bit more subject to change than they otherwise would be. Then you clear the area of disruptive influences, hold it for a length of time based on the size of the Venture that's needed (from one month for a one-dot Venture to half a century for a five-dot Venture) before capping it, “sealing” the new reality. (I have submitted errata to the effect that the “Shifting the Zone” guidelines in M20 should be referenced here; I'd put them just before the “Holding” step. We'll see if the errata makes it into the final book.)

                                                          This notion of using Primal Ventures as a mechanism for facilitating manipulation of Reality Zones could be expanded on. The Terranorming process is devoted to bringing about change; but I could also see a slight variant of it being used to “reinforce” the Zone, “hardening” the reality and reducing the influence of the Consensus on it. This would make that Reality Zone more “objective”*, downplaying the role of human beliefs in determining what's Vulgar and what's Coincidental.

                                                          This notion of “soft reality” vs. “hard reality” could also be associated with the rules for Paradox: if a RZ is sufficiently “soft”, everything's Coincidental, and Paradox is not really a thing. As things “harden”, more things become Vulgar, and/or the rules for Paradox become harsher: if a RZ gets “hard” enough, you might start bringing in some of the more severe Paradox rules from MRev.

                                                          That said, the primary purpose of this notion of the “hardness” of a RZ is to address the notion of how well or poorly the nature of the Zone reflects the local Consensus: in a sufficiently “soft” RZ, the nature of the Zone must conform to the Consensus; as it grows harder, discrepancies between the two become more and more possible.

                                                          Historically, the “failing” of Magic during the Dark Ages involved a gradual “hardening” of reality, until you reach the state of things described in Sorcerers Crusade. And that “hardening” doesn't have to be entirely, or even mostly, the result of human activity (Sleeper or Awakened).


                                                          * I put “objective” in quotes, because my earlier point was that the nature of the Earthly Foundations isn't known: they could just be impersonal cosmic forces, or they might be the Enlightened Will of Greater Spirit Entities (M20 p.487). But whatever their origin, a “hardened” Reality Zone would owe more of its rules to them than to the Consensus.
