    Force Fields

    01-10-2017, 09:46 AM
    How lenient are you with force field effects?

    Obviously there are numerous types and styles but for common Forces 2, Life 2, 3 or 4 (whatever you require to link in to a person so it moves) force fields whats your preferred method, as a ST specifically?

    I used to simply have them negate attack roll successes based on the Damage and Duration chart, as I assume most of us do. Lately I've gone even simpler and just have them negate 1 attack per success with 1 type of attack per success being effected. So 5 succeses would effect 4 attacks from say bullets and give it a days duration. I know this seems WAY too powerful on the surface but honeslty if non magical attacks are wasting your Mage when you are prepared for combat then you are doing something wrong. Of course this type of force field effect is totally vulgar.

    Oh, and I don't tend to have Force Fields act strangley with the surroundings. Aside from being more trouble than its worth most times I figure if it is counting as an active effect the Mage is maintaining he can manipulate it enough to not react off beat with the environment. And I find simple is better, and more fun.

          Originally posted by Tuch View Post
          How lenient are you with force field effects?

          ....

          I used to simply have them negate attack roll successes based on the Damage and Duration chart, as I assume most of us do.
          That's obvious a way to do it. Let me list a few:

          Types of absorption:
          * Outright negates whole attacks, X times where X is Arete roll successes. I think that's quite too powerful. That'd stop nukes with Forces 2
          * Automatically Negates attacks based on damage/duration chart. Quite powerful.
          * Negates based on a 1-to-1 success amount instead of duration chart.
          * Soaks attacks based on damage/duration chart. Realistically probably tied with the above, but requires more Arete successes to get to the same level.
          * Soaks based on 1-to-1 success amount. Weakest version.

          Duration:
          * Normal duration (With the exception of the X attacks topmost bullet point)
          * Ablative, negating/soaking/attacks also reduce duration successes, or at least reduce leftover soak/negation successes. Weaker.

          And I think that's pretty much it
          I've seen all sorts of versions and mixtures of the above being done by STs in the past..with the most annoying being an ablative duration, 1-to-1 forcefield in Revised - It was an orgy of ritual dice rolls to get an adequate forcefield up that was supposed to last longer than one or two combat rounds.


          Personally I am a fan of normal-duration, 1-to-1 soaking fields. It means low-Arete characters can quickly get a couple of soak dice, but high Arete characters won't automatically get like a 6-7 auto-negation force aura with a fingersnip.

          An okay alternative would be ablative forcefields that use the damage chart and either soak or negate. Keep it 1-to-1 with normal duration, or ablative with the damage chart. I think that's a good balance.

              * Soaks based on 1-to-1 success amount. Weakest version.

              ^This.

              You gotta keep in mind it's just Forces 2, can't be that powerful to stop any attack or else Forces 2 is just an easy-to-get alternative to solve a lot of Mage's problems, literally everyone would try to get that. I rule it out (and as far as I remember, it goes like that in RAW too) that for melee attacks, each success on Arete roll substracts one success from melee attack successes. You score 3 successes on Arete and That Bad Guy scores 3 successes on attack, and you're safe. Should he score 4+ successes, you get slapped (unless you also dodge on top of that, etcetera). As for ranged attacks, it states that /mundane/ ranged attacks (someone shooting at you, someone firing arrows at you) are stopped at rate of: 1 projectile if 2 successes, many projectiles if 3 successes.

              Duration goes according to duration chart, of course, but do I /really/ have to tell you that walking around entire day with forcefield around you is a bad, and very stupid idea? You're basically shouting "hello, I'm supernatural, I bump everything from distance". You cannot handshake someone, because their hand bumps in your forcefield. You approach to grab something but it just flies away (at best, might as well be something else unpredictable). Hell, your clothes might react weird to it, unless you've got Matter or already were wearing something. Basically, it's troublesome to just walk around with it for extended periods of time.

              The better alternative are wards due to much less side-effects, but wards require an established mage with many Spheres (or else you'll be leaving a lot of gaps to make it not-so-cool), but even then, you'd require ton of successes + Quintessence to ban many-many things away from yourself (again, gaps). Anyone who's got some info on you and is smart enough about it can give it a try to bypass your defenses.

                      This is exactly what I like to use Rotes for, describing exact Effects and the rules regarding them and working off the Magnitude of Effect chart instead of the Dam&Dur charts etc. For example in my current game I have a player who's Virtual Adept Character has developed a Rote that takes the form of a coat that has a reality coded "displace" program running through it's inlaid circuitry with a "return to sender" trajectory algorithm also programmed into it's function. It's a Matter 2 Corr 2 Forces 2 effect that teleports bullets around his person and on enough successes can even 'fire' the bullets (and arrows and other solid matter projectiles) back in the direction they came.

                      Functionally this is typically takes the form of extended casting to config the coat for use and requires 7 successes, the results of which make the wearer immune to bullets, arrows and any other fast moving 'ballistics' and also generates a 2 dice attack back at the target at diff 8. It's also not very coincidental and rapidly racks up Domino Effect in a firefight. Of course any damage to the coat causes the systems to short out and renders the rote useless.

                      Similarly is an Bad Luck field that is used by the cabal's Dreamspeaker. She has a coin/poker chip that she flips and whispers to invoking the spirits of fortune to watch over her and curse her enemies. This is Entropy 2, Corr 2 and requires 5 successes. The first 3 actions of Harmful intent that target the character suffers -3 successes on their roll. Once that is done it is done and the spirits can not be roused to watch over her for another day.

                      The Charts are really handy for working out on the fly or baselines of effects but there are some cool and inventive stuff that can be done with Rotes that doesnt have to fit with just the Chart results (several published rotes tend to do their own thing for example.)

                                                  There is a great deal of misconception about Entropy. Indeed, it's the hardest sphere to understand. Let me try to shed some light on it

                                                  Entropy 2 is the basic "conditional trigger" in ANY effect. If you know anything about computer language, its the most simple "if, else, or, and" codes.

                                                  If X, than A.
                                                  If Y, than B.
                                                  If Z and W, than C.
                                                  If K or J, than D.

                                                  To say that you need Mind 3 to "read the mind" of all wraiths to determine which ones commited suicide to trigger an effect instead of using Entropy 2 for the "suicidal" trigger, is just as absurd as saying that you would ALWAYS need Matter to use Entropy 3, Life for Entropy 4 or Mind for Entropy 5. And that makes no sense.

                                                  For example, I use Entropy 5 to make agent 234 defect to the Traditions. Very well. I'm succesful, I accumulate 20 succeses, all is fine and good. The agent WILL become a traditionalist.

                                                  But... How will that happen?

                                                  Well, perhaps on his next mission, he is sent to "neutralize" a Verbena girl. But, when he meets her, she reminds him of his deceased sister (who were killed by nephandi in a blood ritual, and the reason for him having joined the Union). After sending her to room 101, something begins to change in the agents heart... He start questioning many things about his own life. Later on, he is sent against a nefandi cult. Things go badly this time; still shacked by the capture of the Verbena girl, agent 234 acts on impulse in an emotional outburst of anger, and endangers the mission (causing the death of two other agents). The commander officer of the mission betrays him at his most fragile moment, saying "you are a disgrace for the Union!" and cowardly flees, only to be able to save his own skin and using this "inciddent" as an excuse to undermine the political position of agent's 234 mentor "for recommending such an instable individual". Agent 234 survives, but is now bitter about the bureoucracy of the Union. In his next and last mission, agent 234, now demoted to the Low rank of a MiB, is saved by a charismatic and kind Etherite that reminds him of his old father, and the long lost time of his childhood were he had a happy life with his family.

                                                  Agent 234 is no longer an agent after that, instead finding new meaning in his life as Professor Agentor (his new secret ID)
