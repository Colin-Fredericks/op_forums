              You don't actually need to get to god level enemies to have brutally one-sided battles in Mage, either in favor of your PCs or against them. If the game is going well, brutally one-sided battles are the norm the more powerful everyone gets.

              As a rule of thumb the game is ultimately, in its aspect as a game, about preparedness and creativity. When a decisive battle begins in Mage, it's result is already written by whatever the PCs did beforehand. Improvised spells may play a role, but they won't be enough to make it against any serious opposition of a "higher level". PCs need data, rituals, carefully chosen countermeasures and weapons.

              Remember: Spirits and Night-folk aren't only intelligent beings able to stack odds in their favor, when it comes to natural advantages they're ahead of Mages by a large margin. It's similar to fighting someone that already has a dozen or more spells cast beforehand, and on top of that higher dice-pools. If the PCs don't have the right answers for those, they're going to get steamrolled and that's it.

              Now, some advice that works for me most of the time:

              1- Don't make the battle an end in itself. Each party in it has one or more goals, and attaining those goals only tangentially requires confrontation. That means neither side has to kill the other to achieve their goals, and that's the simpler way to "balance" an asymmetrical confrontation. Buying time, reaching point B, destroying or protecting a third party or object, there are several ways to make the battle something else. That way your PCs don't have to overpower something impossible or nearly impossible, and if the enemy wins they don't have to obliterate the party, the PCs live to continue their story;

              2- Pay extra attention to what your PCs do to prepare for what they'll face. Give them the time and opportunity to find out what they'll face and what it can do, and to think how they can face those problems. Have at least one "correct answer" prepared, a step-by-step plan using resources they have that will have a good chance of succeeding given mostly average rolls and some room for failure. You don't ever have to disclose this plan or anything, it's just a proof-of-concept to be sure they're up to the challenge, they'll probably do something else entirely;

              3- Focus on making this preparation the bulk of the "battle". Let them stack as much advantages as possible, and nudge them to do so if they seem too confident too soon. Turn any idea on how to get an edge into a quest. Let them travel, explore, make alliances, try what seems impossible. Give them plenty of informants and advisors that can direct them to the things they want to try, how to try and so on. You can also use such informants to throw any ideas you have yourself, whether you think they're feasible or not, just to keep thoughts running;

              4- Learn aspects of the game you usually don't use in your games. Wonders, Ritual Casting, all kinds of Spirits, Bygones, Crafts, Sorcerers, anything at all, and help your players towards how to use and benefit from that thing. The road to fighting a god is the moment to really do a little of everything and try new things. It's also time to explore ways towards those things you don't usually go through, from searching legendary Wonders in ancient ruins to making contracts with mercenary Extraordinary Citizens trained to do battle in weird Reality Zones. Get Nightfolk involved that may help in some way, either by using their simplified stats in Mage or learning their own gamelines;

              5- Give the opponent a weakness, mechanical or not. Something they want or have to protect, but can't give it full attention. Materials or actions that affect them beyond the normal rules, like causing immense damage, disabling powers, reducing dice-pools or otherwise hampering their capacity to operate at full power. A greater danger they have to be careful not to attract. Anything that can either act as a powerful attack or debuff, or that limits or controls the actions of the enemy. This can either be the centerpiece of the battle, or just a final option if something goes wrong and you want to tip the balance in the PCs favor without an actual Deus-Ex-Machina;

              6- Enjoy the road, not the finish line. The battle has to be a dramatic climax for the story, but not the challenge itself. If things get balanced enough for victory to seem uncertain and the battle move back and forth for some rounds, that's wonderful, but be prepared to have everything sorted out much sooner for either side and the descriptions and exchanges make for the drama in lieu of the dice. Such a battle is just too unpredictable for you to rely too much on the system doing the talking.

                      Originally posted by quelsec View Post
                      To be frank i want to do an equivalent of the battle agains the Storm Eater but for mages not werewolves.
                      The thing is, they're Mages, not Werewolves.

                      Werewolves at high levels have immense dice pools, sometimes literally dozens of dice, a lot of passive benefits of all kinds as Gifts and Totem, possibly a lot of spirit allies to help in battle, they have Fetishes they make with far more ease to add to their capabilities, they're extremely hard to harm significantly for long even before adding defensive effects and items, not to say their many ways of keeping synergy within the group, so the Pack is, indeed, more than the sum of its parts in a way no other splat does, much less Mage, and they have all that without any preparation whatsoever.

                      It's a whole 'nother game. The only way to have a Werewolf-like battle in Mage is if the PCs decide on their own that they want to battle like Werewolves. It is doable for them to prepare by ritual casting buffs and defenses and stacking effects so they end up as combat machines. But that's strictly on the players, not on you, and is still more unpredictable, with a higher risk of things simply not playing out as you envision.

                      Mage is not Werewolf. Mage players have a lot of agency on how they'll deal with anything they face, the system actively rewards lateral thinking, and they have frail bodies without any of the advantages of a Werewolf (the single most buffed-up splat in the game by far other than Fera) or natural defense against absolute, unrestricted violence. They're literally as weak as any human, and their single real strength is their unlimited capacity to make shit up when they prepare.

                      Simply put, if you run a Mage game as a Werewolf game and pit them against Storm Eater, they'll TPK. They'll TPK against far weaker foes. And I'm assuming Arete 10 Archmages, they'll simply die horribly and fast.

                              A good enemy for a Mage would be like a good enemy for Superman. Someone who can negate the advantages of the mages. Mages aren't good at time travel, so a time traveler is a major threat. Conspiracies of ordinary mortals like the Mafia or some intelligence agency that sees Mages as a threat are very hard to see coming and can make the Mages outcasts. Religious organizations, even if they don't have the resources of the Society of Leopold, can be major threats. Other mages, even if they aren't as powerful, can leverage inside knowledge and social connections to hurt the PCs.

                              Remember, stronger than anybody isn't equal to stronger than everybody. Similarly, smarter than anybody isn't smarter than everybody.

                                          To be fair, I don't think even werewolves are that well-suited to battle enemies on that level. The Storm Eater was defeated by a ritual with a very high cost. The ritual to stop the Eater of Souls demanded the sacrifice of an entire tribe. It wasn't just werewolves showing up for a smackdown.

                                          And as everyone else pointed out, mages in WoD are the runts of the litter. They're sneaky and versatile; they're not meant to Godmode through a battlefield.

                                          So given that a) mages are super squishy, b) true magic is, in fact, pretty weak, especially compared to other splats and c) as previously mentioned, mages require a lot of prep to do their thing somewhat efficiently, if you want a spectacular battle scene, it might be doable, but you have your work cut out for you.

                                          Aside from stocking up on wonders, my best suggestion to fight a god might be replicating the DnD "memorized spells" approach with archmages using Time 4. Your archmages could prepare a lot of powerful effects as rituals ahead of time, and activate them as needed with triggers. One of these should ideally be a variation of The Shiva/Kali Murder Machine to buff you up for at least a scene to help mitigate your human squishiness.
