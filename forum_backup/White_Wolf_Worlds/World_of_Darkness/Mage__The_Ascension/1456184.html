    Mage 20 and Soaking Magickal Damage Question:

    08-05-2021, 10:36 AM
    So under the soaking magickal damage section of M20 it says:

    "Magickal attacks that unleash physical force – blades, bolts, storms, etc. – can be soaked like any other physical damage. The usual rules, presented in Chapter Nine, apply when soaking damage."

    and

    "All-out Reality-fucking, however, is hard to endure. Vulgar attacks of pure reality-alteration power (curses, transmutations, possession, etc.) cannot be soaked unless the target has countermagick or some other specific protection against the Effect in question."

    So here's a query, if a mage lets say used entropy (or life) as a pattern attack, and cast it in the form of a vulgar curse, would it be soakable? Your pattern is innately physical, therefore it could count as physical damage, however it would be cast in the form of a curse, "Avada Kedavra!" or something along those lines. How would you rule this?

          As a basic rule, I see Forces 3 attacks as being soakable, which is offset by the additional damage that all Forces-based attacks inflict. Damage from any other sphere is generally not soakable. Any sphere effect, damaging or not, can be countered by a mage/technocrat with the countermagick/anti-magick rules.

          The only thing I can think of that I'm not 100% sure how I'd rule it is a Prime 3 attack (with no other spheres) - I can make an argument for this being soakable, and also make an argument for it to be unsoakable.

          As for exactly HOW it's enacted, it's irrelevant. An Entropy 4 direct attack cannot be soaked whether it was done by a Traditionalist via some sort of curse or a Technocrat using an Entropy Acceleration Projector.

              It's not so much that Vulgar attacks tend to be unsoakable, but that most Aggravated (and thus unsoakable) damage tends to be Vulgar. Pattern damage certainly is, as is transmutation damage. There's nothing about going Vulgar that makes damage unsoakable if it wasn't already, though.

                                      It's.. honestly kinda vaguely worded especially if you consider the kinds of damage in 406 so I think its going to really depend on how you interpret it and you won't get a clear cut answer. I'd say treat it as conditional based on:

                                      - How the damage is caused and how it interacts with the target. This is perhaps the messiest. In broad terms damage involving macroscopic physical force/impact against a corporeal form is probably 'stamina' based soak (contrasted with other types described in the rulebook) Punches, kicks, knife stabs, bullets, etc. I think there are lots of ways you can inflict 'physical' trauma via various spheres (as is described in the 'Thunder Punch' section) but the effects/impact could vary greatly.

                                      I would also say that the other conditons for soaking a given kind of damage with stamina apply. Humans normally can only soak bashing damage and need either specific rules or gimmicks to allow them to soak lethal (or even aggravated, assuming such a distinction is drawn for mortals which is not always the case.) Mages are still basically human and need specific uses of magic to soak lethal and aggravated so those conditions would still have to be met.

                                      Complicating things is that 'aggravated' damage is depicted as incorporating all kinds of things like fire, acid, radiation, really bad poisons (not necessarily diseases though but I could see a case being made there), explosions which are all explicitly 'physical' atrocities. Although I'm not sure that explanation works well in a broader context (sunlight causing aggravated damage to vampires is more of a 'curse' than physical 'impact' or because the sunlight causes thermal burns arguably. Otherwise you could probably argue a specific combination of EM radiation simulating sunlight could also cause aggravated damage. As I recall this is verboten logic in even Mage.) Also looking more I just realized that if you spend a point of quintessence you can make even mind-sphere attacks aggravated just like with 'physical' attacks. Also forces is described as being 'aggravated' if it inflicts fire/electrical damage. I could also see 'matter' based attacks causing aggravated if it were acid say (not exactly something that suits 'forces' normally I think.) Certain attacks (radiation, electricity, poisons/disease) would also act on scales smallre than the eye can see (smaller than a knife punch or bullet) hence the macroscopic distinction.

                                      - You also have 'psychic' damage that in game terms is bashing and soaked by willpower that represents a form of 'intangible' damage to the 'consciousness or soul' (although like 'aggravated' this seems to be something more geared towards the 'awakened' rather than mortals given the distinctions made) - attacks by the mind sphere are the obvious one in mage terms but they also mention torture and shocks causing 'psychosomatic' pain which brings us to one of those aforementioned murky areas. Like the bashing damage might reflect purely intangible effects on the 'vitality' of the person (all nebulous) or maybe it could be mental anguish/stress being so extreme that it adversely affects the physical body and causes tangible damage (which means you could 'technically' argue it is soaked physically. Psychosomatic kinda trips it up for me in that respect, at least.)

                                      - then you have what might be considered the 'everything else' category in terms of damage which includes the aforementioned 'reality fucking' which seems to be a gamut of things which may not be directly damage causing. Tenatively given what is described (reality-alteration, transmutation, possession, curses) you MIGHT assume this is something that would not be aggravated damage but still very magical/supernatural in nature (meaning its not a macroscopic physical blow or psychic trauma) in which case its 'soaked' with countermagic or whatever appropriate defensive measure the target may possess against that attack. This is an obviously bigger gray area obviously given it can overlap with aggravated damage or how you define 'reality fucking' in terms of effects. Like reality fucking could still cause psychic or physical trauma in ways (it may just be indirect damage rather than direct) so you could still see a stamina or willpower soak. Furthermore in many cases the damage avoidance via countermagick feels like its better applied as 'defense' rather than 'soak' (like parrying or dodging basically) - the exceptions being where the effects on the pattern transcend sheer physical resilience in terms of altering it (Entropy-based disintegration, Prime-based attacks, etc.) *might* trigger a countermagic 'soak'... but then again if its aggravated you could argue its better handled by physical or psychic trauma.

                                      Ultimately I think it will be situational.
