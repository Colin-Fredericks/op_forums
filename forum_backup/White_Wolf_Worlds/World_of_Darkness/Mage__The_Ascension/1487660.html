    How would the Traditions react to the power of Blood Magic in the modern age?

    07-23-2022, 07:26 PM
    One of the big things that are kept constant between the game lines is how little each faction knows about one another. Let's say that for this scenario, the mages have more information on the kindred than usual. This information is about the different types of blood magic that many vampires use. The Order of Hermes probably already has an inkling of what thaumaturgy can do, but that was during a very early period in Tremere history when thaumaturgy itself was still pretty new, now in the modern age, thaumaturgy has advanced to the point that it no doubt makes practitioners of other schools feel threatened. Especially the ones that practice a blood magic tradition older than thaumaturgy itself.

    I'm just going to paste a list of all the types of blood magic used by vampires. None of you probably need it, but it may serve as a refresher on the Blood Magic Traditions.

    1) 4 different types of Setite Sorcery practiced by 4 different FoS bloodlines, with the most common being Akhu, and another lesser known type known as Nahuallotl which is most likely practiced exclusively by the Tlacique.

    2) Koldunic Sorcery practiced by the Kolduns of Old Clan Tzimisce.

    3) Assamite Sorcery/Dur-An-Ki practiced by the Assamite Sorcerer Caste, and a few Metheleusas. Becomes an in clan discipline for the Assamites in V5

    4) Sadhana practiced by the Indian Venture bloodline known as Danava, Daitya (FoS Bloodline), a few Indian vampires that come from other clans, and the Trimira (Indian Tremere) which accept other vampires from different clans.

    5) Different types of necromancy practiced by the Giovanni, Samedi, Nagraja, some Haitian Lassombra, and some Serpents of Light. Replace by Oblivion for the Hecate in V5.

    6) Abyss Mysticism practiced by the mystics of clan Lassombra.

    7) Thaumaturgy and Selinic Thaumaturgy practiced by the Tremere.

    8) Dark Thaumaturgy practiced by Baali, Angellis Ater, and practically every infernalist.

    9) Wanga practiced by a couple Samedi vampires, but used to a greater degree by the Serpents of Light.

    10) Anarch Sorcery that includes 4 different "Waves" (Think of them as different schools) practiced by the Anarchs.

    11) Sihr practiced by Islamic kindred.

    I can see mages who are burnt-out with fighting the technocracy see turning Blood Magic as a practical option, although it's not as powerful as true magick it's not prone to paradox and blood is far easier to aquire than quintessence. Dreamspeaker are the most likely to be interested in Assamite sorcery, Verbena would probably feel comfortable with Koldunic Sorcery, Cult of Ecstasy would probably find New Age Sorcery ("Wave" of anarch sorcery) appealing, there's Hativist Thaumathurgy for the Virtual Adepts, OoH would, of course, find themselves gravitating towards thaumaturgy, e.t.c.

    Edit: Forgot to include you need to be a vampire, but hey if Tremere thought it was a good option, so would others....

    So, what do you think?

                                          I can't see many Mages consider turning to Blood Magic as a viable alternative to True Magick, simply because the difference in power potential is so vast. If I were given the choice between becoming a Mage or turning vampire with Blood Magic, I would pick Mage any time. While high level Blood Magic is undeniably strong, and of any static magic arguably is the strongest, its potential is locked behind Generation. Even then, when you compare it to Archspheres, it pales in level of flexibility.

                                          Not having to deal with Paradox and becoming immortal are nice deal sweeteners of course, but are they worth killing your Avatar and losing access to dynamic Magick? I would argue no. Forget the Tremere for a moment and look at the other Clans that have dabbled in Blood Magic for far longer. None of them have ever managed to replicate the potential of True Magick despite several thousand years of development, and it wasn't for lack of trying. The Chakravanti who went to Enoch and turned Liches or Nagaraja are probably the best example of that.

                                          Vitae though is a completely different story. It's arguably the most magickal substance in existence- the insane amount of supernatural properties plus being basically unrefined Tass makes it incredibly attractive to Mages who know about it. In this regard, the different types of Blood Magic are an interesting case study in how to harness its power. Still: for a Mage, any Sorcery is like nibbling crumbs off the plate when they could have the whole cake.

                                          But! Dynamic Magick didn't stop evolving. Pillars turned into Spheres, which can be altered to fit Paradigm (as for the Conventions) or twisted (in case of the Nephandi). It's not inconceivable that some day a group of Mages decides to take a good hard look at Blood Magic and try to figure out how to employ it without having to deal with the whole Vampire shtick. A dedicated or altered Sphere for Blood Magic would be a terrible idea imo, but it's not outside the realm of possibilities.
