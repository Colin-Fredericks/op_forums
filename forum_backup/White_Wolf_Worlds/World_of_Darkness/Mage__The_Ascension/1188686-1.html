    I think the way I'd run the Disparate Alliance is as a group that isn't interested in a war against anyone. They're another layer of the occult underground that is fixated on survival.
    The Nine Traditions are a big group that has been bumping shoulders and exchanging ideas for a long time. They've got their rivalries and their ideas have affected each other. They have a huge presence in Horizon and spend a lot of time in direct combat with the Technocracy.

    As the Technocracy intensified its pogrom against the crafts, it makes sense to me that at first, a lot of them would throw in with the Traditions as in Revised, but others who are more interested in survival than war would make a support network with just each other.

        For the Disparate Aliance, the looser the better.

        That's why I didn't like their strict legal system in BoS - I really can't see most of those persecuted isolationists not closing files on a member that was found guitly of a crime that matters more to others than to them. Allowing their execution "for the beneffit of the whole".

        I would separate it in two groups: The cooperationists and the benefactors.

        The cooperationists are the heart of the Aliance. Groups that are able to work well with others but have beefs with the Traditions, that, coincidentally, are renown for their subtlety or their flexibility. Hollow Ones, Batini and stuff

        The benefactors are the outskirt. Groups that won't accept any outsider interference (for whatever reason), but may give a helping hand or accept it as a quid pro quo relationship. Like the Bata'a or the Templars.

        The cooperationists act like a loose but competent network of contacts and favors, they're the "real" Alliance, they have laws and stuff and sometimes join in multi-tradition cabals. Characterized by being as good or better at cooperation and non-intrusiveness as the Traditions these are the ones that have the big picture and organize stuff worldwide.

        The, politely called, benefactors, aren't really allied between themselves, but rather they are allied with at least one cooperationist faction. They don't have to obey anything from "outsiders" (esp. from heatens) - for most not having to obey the law of others was the reason to not join the Traditions in the first place - but rather have a relationship with factions that are sympathetic to them.

        This is easy to achieve thanks to the cooperationist inherent subtlety, multiculturalism or flexibility, they "dress" whatever the locals would find non-intrusive (you don't send a pagan witch to talk with the Templars, you seend someone from Avalon or a Hollow girl that fancies christian symbols). And they don't intrude. They don't demand them to obey laws, ask forgiveness or send help, but rather indulge in a favor network.

        But the greatest problem, one that wasn't adressed to my satisfaction, it's their role. Not-white mystics, multicultural, young, adapted to the 21 century -> They're "The Traditions but Better".

        I don't like that, the overlap it's way too much. Even if you like them, you have to ask yourself what's going to be the role of the Traditions (that were becoming multicultural themselves, as can be seen in the Book of the Traditions).

        The Disparate Alliance will trully end being the SJW group if they need to check-your-privilege the Traditions in order to have a raison d'etre. I couldn't ask for anything worse for such interesting (and, granted, needed) groups as the Ngoma, Bata'a, Taftanit, etc. These aren't "victims" that one should pitty, they are PROUD traditions that were willing to fight to the end rather than submitting.

        Moreso, it risks making the Traditions a bunch of white privilege european authoritarians that they don't need to be (and if they were, it was due to a flaw of the authors or the age, not a natural conclusion of their concept). Sadly, that seems the way things will go. Again, perhaps it's just me, but naming the new seat of the Council: "The Colony" has many negative connotations.

        I would instead take this approach: OK, they're the guys that refused to become all syncretist and join the Trads allied with the Trads that decided to leave because they couldn't stand the Hemretics (and other reasons). Then let's them be the (unwanted!) guardians of orthodoxy.

        The Traditions original goal was to preserve the old ways into the modern world, but they became too tied with each other and become...different (more hippy , but also more uniform). They failed to preserve the plurality of ways. They failed to understood that no single way it's the only way, because they crafted their own inner, secret, truth in the form of the hidden Paradigm: Were All God(s) in Disguise. So the Disparate are the (new) solution to the problem of Unity in Diversity. They persevere in the face of enemy and ally. They preserve the purity of Tradition. Even those that would bite their hand for offering it.

        On the side of the benefactors, you can play a True Traditionalist without being an Insufferable Bigot. If you want to play with a true Orthodox, the Traditions aren't for you because in the Traditions extremely divisive views are regarded as dangerous (you don't have to like each other, but you have to help each other). Incidentally, this it's the role that I use for the Crafts - the orthodox that won't compromise. Well, rest assured, the Disparate Alliance won't demand you to surrender your ways to be politically correct.

        On the side of the cooperativists, those that know or care about the agenda fight in order to allow *trully* different Paradigms to remain as strong as possible in an age where disolution (Nephandi), uniformity(Technocracy), and coalescence of ideas (Traditions) are the order of the day. To do so one must travel to hostile lands, not to bring peace but to bring the sword!. Expect nothing from the local mages, they don't even pretend to follow your laws. And do all of it knowing that something like the First Cabal isn't only impossible but undesirable because you don't seek compromise but to protect the other in it's otherness (if that makes any sense).
