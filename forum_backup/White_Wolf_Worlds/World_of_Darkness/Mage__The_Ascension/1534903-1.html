    Originally posted by Magus Maximus View Post

    Second, under my house rules, all Triggers require Entropy 2. It actually requires a combination of Entropy 1 + Entropy 2, but because Entropy 2 already obviously contains Entropy 1 already, there's no need to state Entropy 1 also.

    I'll explain: Entropy 1 is needed to "identify the circumstances", meaning that it is what the Ward uses to "identify" the targets. So, if your ward is supposed to activate exclusively on targets that are angry and wearing a red cloth made of cotton, then it is Entropy 1 that will make that selection and exclude targets that don't fit the parameters.

    Then Entropy 2 is the actual "Trigger", basically causing the spell to "fail" when the designed circumstances are not the ones specified. Entropy 2 therefore works as a sort of"switch on/off" for your ward.
    ​​
    You can do things this way too. And you could also use Entropy alone to set the conditions that end the time suspension part of the spell too, releasing the rest of the spell into the world. I'd probably require more successes for using Entropy than for using specific domain spheres. My rationale is that the domain spheres have an easier time detecting their own domain. Entropy can do it, but it is more complicated and hence needs more successes.

    I consider the trigger part of the effect to be included in Time 4. It's in the name of the ability, after all. So if other spheres are used to set the conditions, the mage doesn't also need Entropy to set the trigger. The same goes for Wards.

                        Ahhh.... I see what your reasoning behind it was now.

                        But that's not how the Time Sphere works.

                        The time Sphere is not "conditional". What I mean by it is that, you can't set conditions for it, except based on time alone. And by, based on time alone, it means based on some predetermined amount of time at the time of casting. For instance: "stop holding after a month".

                        Conditions can only be set by Entropy, because Entropy is essentially a "conditional Sphere".

                        I get it what you mean, you're saying "hold a spell until such a time upon which a bald man enters this area", and that Time 4 Sphere would then determine WHEN in the future there will be a bald guy passing, just as if the mage was scrying into the future himself, however the mage is not doing the scrying, the spell is, and then the spell simply says "bald guy, one week from now" and then the spell itself gets set to hold for 1 week. I got what you meant here. But Time Sphere doesn't work like that.

                        First of all, the problem is that scrying into the future (or past) is not precise; at beast, you could get an estimated time upon which a bald guy will pass ("a bald guy will pass sometime from now up to 1 week). And that lack of precision would cause the spell to automatically fail, because the holding of Time 4 requires a precise time frame.

                        Secondly, time holding a spell doesn't allow for the spell to "re-weave" itself after "use".

                        Even if it was possible for the spell to determine with precision the future event that you wish to target, the spell would remain on hold until triggered ONCE. After being unleashed, the spell then would be spent.Gone.

                        Therefore, Time 4 alone is a poor fit for a Ward, even if it could be used to predetermine a future event, because it only works once.

                        Entropy, however, can make a spell to "selectively fail", which is why it is the perfect fit for a spell with a "switch" and with predetermined conditions.

                        Entropy is also THE conditional Sphere.

                        The way I like to build effects in Mage is more or less like building a computer algorithm. Which is why I always return to the descriptions of the levels on each Sphere, and why Im always so adamant about them being well written (spoilers: they are not). And also why I also always go to the table for Duration, Reach and Successes needed for effects when I want to say that some X level on A Sphere is poorly written, because to me, the way it should work should be:
                        - Desired effect;
                        - Look at the best Spheres that probably fit;
                        - Look at the descriptions on each level.
                        - Try to segment the Effect into sensible parts

                        So, Entropy is the default Sphere for "If, Then" conditional effects, which is a common feature for computational algorithms.

                        "If a blind child passes, then unleashes X" is the bread and butter of Entropy 2. "If Im allied with Target, then no damage", is the basic algorithm for Entropy 2, Forces 3, Prime 2 to create "selective fireballs".

                        Rules as written, Triggers require Time 4 AND Entropy 2. The reasoning for Entropy 2 is as I described, but unfortunately the official text is a lot more vague than what I described, not really explaining it too well.

                        That's one thing that I really think that is missing in Mage, thorough explanations for the reasoning behind each Spheres selected for any given effect.

                        This lack of clarity causes the writers themselves to not know what their doing; that's by the way my own personal reasoning for discarding Time 4 from triggers. It seems that, the writers reasoning behind that choice is for the spell to "Remain on hold" until being triggered by Entropy 2 under the correct set of circumstances; but that to me doesn't seem right, because Entropy 2 alone already causes a spell to "selectively fail" without the need to be put on hold, and Time is a poor fit due to all of the explanations I gave above already - no precise time frame, and Time can only be used ONCE, upon which the spell is spent.

                        A conditional spell with a Trigger cannot, therefore, have Time 4 as a prerequisite for the Trigger, because even if Entropy 2 could bypass the "precise time frame" for its activation, Time cannot re-activate a spell spent.

                        Entropy on the other hand, doesn't require the spell to be "re-activated" as Time would, because in truth, the spell will ALWAYS remain "always on" all the time, the reason why the effects aren't constantly happening is because Entropy causes the spell to "selectively fail" at most of the time, except under the set conditions, upon which it "doesn't fail anymore".

                        Hence why, for me, all Conditional Triggers are done with Entropy 2 (+ desired effects).

                        But I can also see how some other Spheres may be required, like what Ramnesis said, for instance, a "Ward against Rats" may require Life 1 to "detect rats" plus Forces 3/Prime 4 to electrocute them (without needing quint points to fuel), although in my opinion, Entropy 1 (already contained in Entropy 2) can already do that without needing to add Life 1, but not because Entropy 1 is capable to discern the living Patterns of rats, but rather because Entropy 1 will simply "say to your spell" "I feel a disturbance in the Force. I think it is about time to stop selectively failing and to succeed in electrocuting something". Thus, Entropy 1 can dictate whatever target you desire, be it a Spirit, a mental construct floating in the Ether, an ethereal emanation of resonance, a rat or a piece of gold. Entropy 1 won't tell you what the Pattern is, but it will tell you "the probabilities have matched. Time to act", and Entropy 2 will "Switch On" the spell based on that.

                            Entropy 1 is basically the "Ubermensch" of the Level 1 Spheres, and the reason is because it can indirectly detect almost anything, but even if its indirect, it is no less precise.

                            Think of it as if we were nuclear physicists detecting particles. We have a machine that tells us when a particle passes by; we have no idea what particles are passing, we don't know if they are neutrinos or quarks or whatever, but WE KNOW WHEN A PARTICLE PASSES.

                            That's more or less how Entropy 1 works. Yes, you can identify if there are rats under the attic of your house. You won't get Skyrim green lights above your head as if it were Life 1, but you'll see them nevertheless as "quantum probabilities".

                            You can use Entropy 1 to spot a guy with a gun in the middle of a crowd, even thou you don't have Matter 1.

                            And that's because you go by "probabilities" or "destiny" alone.

                            That's why, Entropy 1 is the ultimate "Detector of Conditions".

                            And finally, Entropy 2 is the simple "Switch".

                            And that's what Triggers are, when we are building this Algorithm; Triggers require A) A Detector to detect the set conditions and B) A Switch.

                            Hence, Entropy 1+ Entropy 2
