    Originally posted by Aleph View Post
    I have this idea: why not use Mage Lore + Wits, instead of Occult.
    This way, it's still possible, but it's justified (and capped) by a much rarer form of Knowledge. The character needs to truly "understand" True Magick before using it's innate magickal powers as a bulwark against it's effects, and not many of the Night-Folk understand it to that level.

    This way , the average cub can't counter magick, but an elder (or any supernatural that has taken time to understand its enemies) may counter all but the most potent of mages (or spells).
    Lore Abilities, as Secondaries, aren't for every game. Also, there are lots of reality-warping threats in other games; Werewolf, for example, has Triatic Spirits, most notably Nexus Crawlers, Stasis Vectors and Great Vortices, that can all alter existence directly. Viskr Faction Ananasi also have their own exclusive category of rites for this purpose, and the Rite of Weaving is capable of a wide array of broad effects, much as magick is, only requiring a great deal more time and much more likely to go awry. Changeling Wyrd cantrips also work by inviting in something alien to the world in order to alter it, and of course, there are those beings in the Dreaming that are less affected by Banality than the surroundings are from their presence. The basic concept isn't really so unknown to the other games.

            The problem with all this is that Wits and Occult, as common, traits have no default rating. Assuming every night-folk has decent ratings in both and their relevant capping traits is overstating how hard they are to deal with magically. Though it is worth acknowledging that some night-folk are going to be very hard to effect with the M20 rules as written if their concepts happen to align with what's in the rules.

            For example, a Ragabash Homid would have to put a lot into Rage or Gnosis before they're countering anything. An Ahroun Lupus just needs the Wits + Occult ratings.

            Of course, the whole thing seems like it was really just done because for so long mages have been seen as so overwhelming powerful in crossover as most supernaturals have no defense against magic.

            I do think that it could use some tweaking, but I'd only really be worried about that for crossover games. In a pure mage game, night-folk are as good as basic countermagic as the ST wants.

                                        Here's the Houserules I've been using for my campaign so far.
                                        Mage House Rules
                                        Character Creation
                                        PC’s start with Arete 3, 9 dots of Spheres, and 1 dot of Avatar.
                                        Affinity Sphere: Pick your own.
                                        Martial Arts: Every dot of Martial arts must be accompanied with at least 1 dot in the following Abilities: Academics, Medicine, Enigmas, Art, Craft, Meditation, Stealth, Esoterica (Yoga, Tai’chi) These dots must be spread out, only 1 ability counts for each dot.
                                        Ability Changes
                                        The following Abilities may be substituted on your character sheet or added to it. All other secondary abilities do not exist in my game.
                                        (Talent)Animal Kinship,(Skill) Do, Archery, Riding,*Quick Draw (Knowledge)Pilot, Hypertech
                                        Quickdraw: Every dot adds 1 to your initiative for the purpose of drawing and firing a gun in combat. If using the bonus you MUST fire a gun. Can only be used with pistol sized guns.
                                        Hypertech:This ability functionally works as "Occult" for Technomancers, allowing the identification of supernatural phenomena and explanation of it, through a Technomatic paradigm. You still need awareness to detect such things, but your Mage can identify things such as Ghosts and what they are weak too, by explaining it through some form of psuedoscience. It's also used for handling and understanding wonders and instruments of a technological nature.
                                        A note about Esoterica: You may be wondering what this skill is. It’s for being able to perform occult style practices. “Herbalism”, “Demonology”, “High Magick”, and the like are examples of Esoterica.
                                        A note on Specialties: Some abilities require a “focus” to be used. These abilities can contain many focuses. The Abilities which require them are: Crafts, Art, Science, Esoterica. Additional focuses, however, may be purchased for 4xp, regardless of the number of dots in the ability.
                                        The Rule of 10: Any action involving an ability or attribute rated at 4+ dice allows you to count 10’s as two successes. All Arete rolls automatically count 10's as two successes.
                                        How can I take Multiple Actions with Magick?
                                        Life 3: Speed your body’s Reflexes up, Maximum 3 actions around.
                                        Mind 2: Speed Synaptic functions, Maximum 2 actions around.
                                        Time 3: Speed self up, no limit on actions.
                                        Correspondence 3: Limited, must be the same exact action, max 5.

                                        2 actions a round remains coincidental, 3 actions and above becomes Vulgar.
                                        Spirit Magick
                                        Spirit 1: See and Talk to Spirits and Ghosts.
                                        Spirit 2: Call Spirits and Ghosts to you.
                                        Spirit 3: Give orders to Spirits and Ghosts, limited.
                                        Spirit 4: Command/Dominate Spirit and Ghosts.

                                                I'm using the same house rules as I did in my revised Ed games. Using the Minor spheres means you roll so sphere plus arête on your magic rolls, using a major sphere means you roll arête alone.

                                                Mages suffer one paradox point per dot of the highest sphere used in the effect for vulgar Magic (plus one extra point if there are witnesses) but can 'soak' paradox with arête; mages are free to spend quintessence and willpower in this roll, difficulty is 6 (as with all non sphere rolls where a player rolls arête in my game).

                                                Counter magic on effects that influence a Mage can be performed without any spheres using an arête roll of difficulty 6 (in most cases) but this only counters magic that is being done or which has been done on said Mage (if the Magic effects multiple people then the counter magic only negates the magic done on the Mage who counters it)

                                                        I've always thought Avatar was too integral as a Background, and encourages min-maxing. I considered having it as a ten-dot Advantage like Arete and Willpower, but realised it would overlap too much with the former. You can channel as many points of Quintessence per turn as you have Arete, then. A simple fix, and a fairer solution, I think.

                                                            I was working on a set of house rules about Avatar, Prime, and Quintessence. These come from prior to the release of M20.

                                                            Avatar background and spending Quintessence

                                                            All mages may channel at least one Quintessence into their own effects, even if they don't possess the Avatar background trait.

                                                            Avatars and Quintessence absorption

                                                            Mages without the Avatar background may still meditate within a Node and gain Quintessence. Mages meditate for one day and then roll for at least one success on a roll of Perception + Meditation, difficulty 9. This gains them a single point of Quintessence per day of quiet reflection.
