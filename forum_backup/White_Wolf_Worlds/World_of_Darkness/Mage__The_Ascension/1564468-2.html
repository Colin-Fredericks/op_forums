    Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
    include ghosts only in those places where it makes narrative sense, and then not all the time.
    The question with ghosts is defining how much that is, because this tell something about your view of the cause of ghosts in the setting, how common they are. Or, out another way, how haunted the world is. This is narrative sense, and is world building.

    It takes unfinished business to form a ghost. But how many humans finish their business still alive in the World of Darkness? How many people die every year?

    Now, in real life, or best estimates are the total human population that ever lived is between 115 and 120 billion. It means the dead population is ~110 billion in the real world, and today 60+ million die every year. If 1/10 leave ghosts and 1/10 ghosts stay in the Penumbra, that's 600 thousands fresh ghosts every year.

    How much is that in the setting? How many people the world secretly has in excess, how many die every year? How many leave ghosts because of all the possible reasons someone living in the World of Darkness may have? How many of those stay close to the Skinlands in a regular basis?

    Your ghost population isn't just background, it is a statement. And you don't need all of them to be powerful as spirits, let most be weak and with no particular way to affect Earth and you can have as many as you want.

    Of course, you can as much want few of them. To stress how high the bar is, or unpredictable the process, or how many are prevented to stay on Earth for whatever reason (including but not necessarily the same as those in Wraith).

        It's simpler than it seems, just follow the numbers and study only one thing at a time. Anything you don't need at the moment just ignore, w

        Heaven and Hell are Realms. Anything you think off as another reality in the usual sense is a Realm. Sometimes multiple Realms that have the same name because they're different versions of the same thing.

        So you find them in the Astral Reaches, in the Spires.

        You can simplify a lot, but I'd recommend just ignore bits until they matter.

        Let's try the Minimum Viable Umbra, just in case your players want to go there:

        1. Gauntlet

        2. Penumbrae. Keep 3 and keep them separate, that increases size but simplify use:

        2a. Astral Penumbra
        2b. Spirit Penumbra
        2c. Shadowlands

        3. Expanses. They have some places to wander and also contain Realms:

        3a. Astral Reaches
        3b. Spirit Wilds
        3c. Underworld

        4. Realms and Zones. Everything, basically.

        Hell, Heaven, Hades, Arcadia, Pangaea, Abyss, anything at all is a Realm. Choose if it is in the Astral Reaches, the Spirit Wilds, or it is a Zone.

        Zones are just Realms that have connections everywhere.
        - Dream Zone (Reaches anything that dreams),
        - Digital Web (Touches every computer with a connection),
        - Aelder Bole (The giant tree, has passages everywhere),
        - Mirror Zone (Connects all mirrors).

        So the map is:

        Gauntlet
        |
        |> Astral Penumbra > Astral Reaches > Realms
        |> Spirit Penumbra > Spirit Wilds > Realms
        |> Shadowlands > Underworld > The Labyrinth
        |> Everett Volumes (any alternate Earth)

        Zones are outside the map.

        And that's it. Everything else is optional complications you can explore later. And every world you can conceive is just a Realm.

        What a Realm/Zone is? Whatever you want, if you want to define at all. The map isn't about what anywhere means, just how to get there.

        Horizon is for space. Everything above is Earth-like and you only have to travel with Spirit. For anything related to Horizon you also have to fly somehow.

            Rather than that, I would suggest to roll with the punches. You may find that while the whole cosmology it's very large and daunting, most mages will only interact with a tiny fragment of it.

            I.E: Virtual Adepts and computer-curious mages care about the Digital Web, but very few others do - so if one doesn't have those one likely doesn't need to pay much heed to it.

            *Focus on the Penumbrae, because that's the part of the Umbra they're going to interact for sure, then expand outwards.

            *A good idea to buld outwards could be to focus on the Beliefs of your players. These not only colour their interactions, but also inform you what interactions they want to have

            For instance: Let's say a mage believes in ancestor worship. Then you need spirits that represent their ancestors (could be ghosts), and these likely don't live on the Penumbra (unless he has a haunted house or something) - so you need an afterlife (that's a Realm) complete with whatever entities should reside on it (gods, heavenly envoys, whatever...)

            Now, where is this place on the map? - I would suggest to see what kind of magick the mage does, because this is what they will use to connect with them.

            We could debate if such a realm should be on the Underworld, where ghosts live. On the High Umbra, where gods live. I would err towards both ussing Helheim as an example (Hella's realm exists on both the Astral Umbra and the Underworld - these are conected and can be used to travel betwen worlds - but, realy, even nordic mages are unlikely to know enough to actually exploit this feature) - but it's less likely to impact play. Far better to focus on your players, and if that means you end with Ghosts on the Middle Umbra, well, just say the Realm has connections to the Underworld and that's that

                Basically what Aleph said.

                Focus on this:

                Gauntlet -> Penumbra -> Realms

                Then ask what the PCs think of other worlds.

                Your goal is to make what they think, possible.

                No less, no more.

                If a player wants animist spirits, their Magic access animist spirits.

                If a player wants the courts of the gods, their Magic access the courts of the gods

                If a player wants ghosts, their Magic access ghosts.

                If a player wants alternate realities, their Magic access alternate realities.

                If a player does not want something in their Paradigm, their Magic can't go there without help, so it doesn't matter.

                If a player wants something stranger, give them what they want and come ask if you need help.

                If a player wants to know what exists between Realms or in space, only then you need a little extra.

                Crucially: don't overthink.

                Forget the book. Its greatest problem is that it thinks too much. The book doesn't help, and if you try to reconcile what we say with the book before understanding, it will only get in the way.

                We've all been where you are now, and the most experienced ones got it with time and too much effort to disentangle things. And our big frustration is exactly that: the book doesn't help whatsoever. What we found in the end was a system considerably simpler than the constant highly abstract metaphysical spiel in the book makes it appear.

                And making the stuff you don't know how to handle into a Realm is actually the point.

                That's it in a nutshell.

                Only 5 things are not Realms:
                1. Earth,
                2. Penumbra,
                3. Astral Reaches,
                4. Spirit Wilds,
                5. Underworld.

                From those, you only have to understand the Earth (obviously) and Penumbra. The other 3 are in a need-to-know basis.

                How you reach a given Realm?
                However makes sense.

                Learning more of the cosmology make things easier to navigate, but isn't necessary.

                                Perfect. For the Middle Umbra/Spirit Wilds you can also look Werewolf the Apocalypse. It isn't a 1:1 translation, but will be tens of times more helpful.

                                Middle Umbra / Spirit Realms

                                Still the basic is: looks like Earth, but grandiose and intense. Nature is intense, industrialization is intense, pollution and decay are intense, but most things are recognizable. In Werewolf cities have way more Pattern Spiders than in Mage, so you only need the "same but bigger than life" part.

                                It's not a representation of the Earth, either, so you just make up geography as you go. There are some people that use it for shortcuts for this reason. Add spirits and myth shenanigans like roads made of stardust that go up to the sky or whatever.

                                You'll find all sorts of spirits wandering around and it is legitimately dangerous, but is not a lot, just more than the Penumbra. Most spirits will be on Realms. The place is full of passages back to the Penumbra and to Realms aplenty, they look anyway you want them to.

                                Beware floating roads made of Moon light. The Werewolves build and use them, and won't accept intruders. They almost always link two Nature Nodes back on Earth under Werewolf control, so they may serve as a marker of areas close to the Earth, but werewolves don't accept even their fondest allies and family near the actual Nodes and you'll generally find one or two dozen of them in one at any given time.

                                The Digital Web / Network Zone

                                The Digital Web can be accessed from any computer with a connection, anywhere in the multiverse. Easier with Spirit, but many groups know the secret to do it with Correspondence. A connection to the internet is ideal, but in a pinch you can brute force on any two linked computers.

                                Picture an illustrative representation of a data grid. The place is that made literal. Any SciFi movie or comic about navigating the web itself will work as reference. Player Number One not, just because it is a Second Life style instead, it is better for Nodes in the Web.

                                And that's it. Links and Nodes. From outside they look like giant connected structures of whatever material you want, and at least the size of a Metro tunnel. From inside Links are whatever transport system you can imagine. Nodes are specific computers or processing units turned into metaphor. Metaphor you can touch, drink, fuck, etc. Good references if you need are Tron and Reboot, since they're classic and won't add noise.

                                Mostly tech Mages use it to meet and chat, so it's just a big, weird tavern, but you can affect a computer from its analogous Node, or block a signal by blocking the Link in the Digital Web. It is a thing to use the Digital Web to bypass safe systems too tough to take with mundane hack.

                                Zones in General

                                Zones are your toys. Use them if and when you want unless a player decides to go there specifically. They're not there to understand, they're there for Deus Ex Machina. You can put a natural passage to a Zone wherever you want and the exit wherever else. Again, anywhere in the Multiverse.

                                Use, toy, scrap. If they want to go back, they go figure out how that works. Other than that, your only obligation is to be consistent on what a natural passage to a particular Zone looks like.

                                This is the secret: you don't have to understand them. In fact, no one understands them. The book is so arcane about them precisely 'cause it's saying in extremely purple prose that "no one has the slightest idea". They're just there.

                                        They can be whatever, even because it can have any kind of realistic environment, not just natural ones. So you definitely have portals to thematic realms, but also to most realms you may need a portal to.

                                        Remember: it is also the Umbra of cities, server farms, nuclear powerplants, toxic waste dumps and anything that has a physical representation in the world.

                                        So do you want a passage to Hades or Sheol? Since those places are defined by caves, they have passages in the Middle Umbra. Olympus probably has one, accessible through a path that start in the Penumbra of the actual Mount Olympus in Greece.

                                        If any realm seems close enough to something that physically exists in the world, it may have a passage in the Middle Umbra.

                                        On the Digital Web: first, if a character carries a computer with them, and today a smartphone counts, they have a possible link to the Digital Web.

                                        Also, having passages everywhere is a trait of the Digital Web. In the Middle Umbra they may look like giant roots, spider webs, anything that resembles branching lines or paths. You can look for a door inside the Link or just climb it until you're not in the Middle Umbra anymore.

                                        But apply what I said earlier: it is a Zone, it has passages if you want, where you want, for as long as you want, and you have no obligation to be consistent. Mapping natural passages to the Digital Web is a legitimate way of dealing with it if you want to play with that, but has no guarantees.

                                                Monteparnas's point about the rulebook being a "menu" is very apt. The rulebook (regardless of edition) is a toolbox. A collection of options all in one place (or, often, spread out over a bunch of supplements). Every item isn't FOR every ST/player. Each, rather, is the perfect tool for a particular ST/player.

                                                Most games will never have reason to use the Certaman rules. Hell, a lot of Order of Hermes games, or one involving OoH PCs, will never involve Certaman. And that's fine, because it's not FOR them. It's FOR the people who want highly ritualized magickal duels between Hermetic magi.

                                                The rulebook needs to get into the weeds of a LOT of topics, because it needs to be a resource for any kind of game the designers imagined (or at least that they considered likely to come up). It gets dense because LIFE is dense and complicated and philosophically varied. A game ABOUT that complexity and philosophical variance needs a lot of options.

                                                But that also means any given game mechanic or setting element is liable to only be relevant to a narrow slice of the playerbase. And that's fine.

                                                Shrink the scope of your creative effort to what's important to YOUR Chronicle, and everything else will fall into place.
