    106)

    Eugene Tailor is a friendly man.

    A good listener. A really good one. You have to be, in order to tend the counter in a classy bar where the people like to spend time. The kind of bar where jazz music plays in the background, the lights are dimmed a bit, and where people come to enjoy a bit of nostalgia. The kind that makes you dwell on the past, where the music sets your thoughts in motion.

    Eugene also mixes good cocktails. Really good ones. The kind of cocktails that are not just run-off-the-mill, but something special. The ones you really remember. Oh, he can do the common ones right off the first page of the menu, too. And just as good, but Eugene has a keen eye for what kind of spirits might suit the new guest sitting in front of him. Even if the patron is the kind to try their 'usual', his charm and friendly demeanor does wonders for suggesting people to try new things. And so far, people always were suprised and enthusiastic about his creations afterwards. It is an art to come up with new blends and creations, and Eugene is an artist when it comes to mixology.

    Eugene is also is empathic. Very empathic. He is quick to notice when something is weighting down on somebody's soul. When there is that certain lack of enthusiasm in their eyes. When the body language is humble or shy. Or when they outright spill their sorrows to the bartender. From the people who simply have a problem they really need to solve, to those poor souls that try to drown their sorrows with spirits (which Eugene discourages, and instead tries to make them enjoy his creations with patience and care - even though he seldomly enforces a limit on the amount they try.).

    Eugene is also helpful. Very helpful. His charm, friendlyness and empath allow him to get tongues more loose, to get into a conversation about the ires, bad fortunes and sorrows that occupy some guests. His smile brightens the mood in wonderful ways, his soft voice soothes the nerves, and his advice is not just hollow words, but enthusiastic and well thought-out help to get people to consider their way out of their situation. Steps to solve their problems, all with the care of a saint. And more often than not, the guests, charmed by his liquid bliss, leave the establishment with determination, a big smile, and a jump in their step.

    Eugene gives his advice. Gladly so. The words are well thought-out. The suggestions are logical, and elaborate. They are so reasonable. Like brilliant flashes of enlightenment at times. It is clearly just a horrible world out there that has the customers come back worse, with tales of bad luck, of misfortune and greater ills. Ills that, of course, have Eugene lend his ear once more, soothing the nerves, serving his tasty drinks and suggesting a way out of the new dilemma. Even if the clothes of the customer might get a bit more ragged. Even if the world out there seems to become harsher and harsher to them. Even if, slowly over time, Eugene becomes the only person listening to them. Even if the needlemarks on the arm itch, and the last bundles of money scream for more. But there is also those delicious cocktails. Expensive, but so good. And that warm voice of the bartender...perhaps he has an idea. Just one more idea of how to get out of this mess...


    Welcome, dear guest. Choose your poison. The final one's always on the house.

        107) A Verbana Moon Seeker who began her Awakened career in the 90s, by embracing the then-current cutting edge of mass media: the VHS tape. With a little funding by her coven, she would produce direct-to-video specials, devoted to teaching people how to do watchcraft. The videos were hokey and low budget, and the witch herself had a rather awkward screen presence. But her sincerity shone through, as did the practical knowledge she talked about and demonstrated. Obviously, the witchcraft was sanitized - no censor was going to let dancing skyclad in a forest or ritual blood-letting slide - but that merely forced her to get creative. Sometimes the tapes would record actual magical effects. Because of the low quality of the VHS, however, it could easily be passed off as special effects.

        It was slightly embarrassing for the Verbana, but it got modest results. In the time of the New Age revival, young people would snatch up any material purported to give real advice on the mystic arts. So, while many would deny it, quite a few Verbana first cut their teeth on magic from watching this witch's videos.

        Inevitably, the series of videos ended. Given the glut of New Age material on the market, and the relatively low-key videos, the witch got a surprisingly long run out of the gig. Nonetheless, the Technocracy eventually took notice - they themselves were embarrassed it took so long - and members of the Syndicate bought the distributor and dropped the series. Strings were further pulled in other distributors, and the witch experienced a soft blacklist in the VHS market. Disheartened, she put away her cameras and went back to being a solitary witch.

        However, the VHS copies didn't disappear. They were no longer being printed, sure, but those still extent remained in circulation on the second hand market. Others remained locked in the attics, closets, and basements of people throughout the world, waiting patiently. Eventually, sometime in the late 00s, certain "fans" of the videos began talking about them on internet forums. Some simply liked to make fun of cheap, corny videos they dug out of storage. Others, however, had genuine nostalgia for them. Objectively yes, they were corny as hell, but some people had actual feelings for these tapes. Even if they never Awakened in childhood from them, seeing a friendly witch talk about magic to them had been a formative part of their childhoods. Moreover, some of those who only learned about the tapes years after the fact gained a fondness for them after they'd had their fun laughing at them. Moreover, some people actually tried them out, only to find that they would occasionally work. Some - whether old fans or new - even Awakened.

        With the advent of video streaming sites, this small cult following began to post grainy footage pulled from the videos, bit by bit, across dozens of accounts. By sheer chance, the witch herself discovered this. At first she was embarrassed - she HAD been hokey, hadn't she? (Not to mention how she lamented her lost youth). But seeing how many people truly enjoyed the videos made rekindled the magic that had been lost. She started reaching out to fans in the small community, especially those claiming to have really made the lessons work. She dug into the coven's storage, and pulled out huge reels of tape that had been sitting around, gathering dust. She started her own video channel, and posted the high quality original footage. The ones made before the quality was compressed down to VHS standards.

        When she had posted the entire original run, the fans expressed sadness that there would be no more. Deciding that her work wasn't finished, she bought a digital camera, and started a new series of videos. At first what amounted to vlogs where she talked about magic, then actual productions. One crowd-funding campaign later - helped by some Virtual Adept friends, who made sure to keep the Technocratic Union from pulling more chicanery - and the witch had started up her program as a regular show. Years of experience (and a little help from more tech and media savvy friends) made her videos even better; less awkward, more punchy, with better production.

        Many in the Verbana became embarrassed anew...but, secretly, they smile at the resurgence of old passions amid young faces.

                It being a Primer is unlikely, as one of the qualifiers for Primers is that they can't be passive experiences, like music or films. They require active effort to comprehend the material. On the other hand, I've toyed with the idea of a Primer that is a film, but requires the viewer to study and analyse the contents of the film - frame by frame in some cases - before they can gain the full effects. Like a Primer created specifically for film buffs.

                Another idea could be that some event occurred during the filming of one episode. It wouldn't even need to have been at the film set; whatever it was just sent out waves of interference on the electromagnetic spectrum. So that, for a few frames of footage, a clue was captured on the film stock. Its presence wasn't recognized until years later, when a mage saw the distortion on their copy of the VHS tape. But because it's VHS, the quality of the footage is too poor to make out what the interference was.

                Only the master copy of the actual film stock would be high quality enough to analyse for clues. And if it's on the last episode made, the witch may not have had the original film returned from the distributor before her show got canned, and it got lost in the confusion. Meaning the PCs have to go track down wherever that master copy is stored or who has it.

                That even could be anything, but would presumably be something affecting a wide area, and noticeable on the electromagnetic spectrum. It would also be quite an old event, to be picked up by that tape. I'm imagining some kind of Technocracy or Virtual Adept experiment, sent out by broadcast. Or maybe a call for help, with information about a person's whereabouts, or the whereabouts of something important they wanted known. The Technocracy might even have scrubbed all other sources of such things after the fact, but missed this witch's tape because she was a relative unknown, and/or because of the delay between filming and release on VHS. There are many ways this could go.

                    108) Formerly a soldier in the US military, this Ecstasy Cultist volunteered for a secret research project. They told him he could serve his country by putting his mind on the line. So when they pumped psychedelics into his veins and strapped him to a chair with flickering lights on his eyes, he thought he could take it. He'd been waterboarded as part of training, so he thought he could handle anything. The government lab, for their part, wanted to see if they could foster psychic powers.

                    They succeeded. Pity they and the rest of the lab didn't survive long enough to appreciate it.

                    The explosive Awakening left him stranded in a crater that used to be a military base. After wandering back to civilization, he discovered that the destruction had been covered up, with official reports blaming a gas leak with "No Survivors". After a few close calls from shadowy assailants and men in black suits, he realized that someone knew about him. He abandoned his life, eventually falling in with the Cult of Ecstasy.

                    He studies psychic powers now, using combinations of drugs, sensory deprivation, and sensory overload, as well as various mental exercises, thought forms, and headgear cribbed from the notes of Etherites he knows, to channel mental ability.

                    Unfortunately, the mental trauma - combined with being hunted and lingering questions about that secret research project - has also done a number on his psyche. He's more than a little paranoid, and puts great stock in conspiracy theories. Now of course, in the World of Darkness, many conspiracy theories are true. But others aren't, or aren't quite as many know them. He's lucid enough to know that, but can't rule out any theory for sure, no matter how outlandish. What if the Technocratic Union is simply a front for Lizard People? What if those Lizard People are simply fronts for an even more esoteric set of hidden masters? He does know not to trust the government, because they took advantage of his patriotism and got him into this mess with their secret project. He wants to know who was behind that research, and who they are really working for.

                    He'll astral project across the planetary mindscape, if it means finding answers. He'll also work to decode the riddles and signs hidden everywhere. It's not like the Technocracy is a stranger to implanting memetic effects into media and the like. Why can't the REAL conspiracy do the same?

                    Currently, he spends a great deal of time on message boards, and selling brain health products on the side. Since he's a military vet, he's also not above making money by teaching other mages and/or their consors how to fight. The Traditions need to be ready to fight the real war, now that the distraction war against the Technocracy is on hold. Of course, the Traditions are also almost certainly pawns in someone's game. He'll stay on the inside, in order to get a better chance to see who or what they are.

                        109) A flamboyant and narcissistic stage magician, inducted into the Cult of Ecstasy by The Man in the Mirror.

                        Centuries ago, a master illusionist belonging to what then was known as the Seers of Chronos, the Man in the Mirror used his great powers to retreat into a mirror pocket dimension, in order to evade danger. Unfortunately for him, the Scourge twisted his magick, trapping him in his mirror refuge. He could survive indefinitely in his prison and act through reflections to a limited capacity, but could not leave. (Or perhaps he CAN leave, but some persistent threat keeps him holed up. He's not terribly forthcoming about his situation). So there he remained, for hundreds of years.

                        That is, until a vain fool found a hand mirror in an antique store, buying it for a song.

                        As it happened, the ornate mirror was the old master's Talisman. When the young narcissist - a cocksure individual of a gender-fluid persuasion - managed to make the item's magick work, it intrigued the old master, and the Man in the Mirror appeared in the reflection. Unaccustomed to seeing someone else's face staring back at them, the Fool adapted surprisingly quickly. When the Man in the Mirror offered to teach them True Magick, they feigned aloofness, but secretly longed for such power. So, after much posturing, passive aggressive hostilities, and exasperation, the Fool became the Man in the Mirror's apprentice. Not too long after, they Awakened fully, allowing them to grasp subtleties of illusion, prophecy, and apportation, in addition to the finer points of mundane stage magic and the Art of Desire.

                        The Ecstasy Cultist travels the world, performing stunning displays for the benefit of audiences. Off the stage, though, the narcissist acts as the agent of their Mentor, who uses the modern fool to enact broader schemes. Seducing people here, whispering magically charged ideas into ears there, and tracking down mystic artifacts along the way. If they find useful magical items or research, they either use it themselves, or trade them off to other mages (the Man in the Mirror, despite his exile, seems to remain fully in contact with quite a few old mages, who are interested in swapping such things for favors). It's only recently, though, that the fool has shrugged off their incurious habit, and begun to wonder what the Old Man is playing at. If any of the tasks directly or indirectly take them closer to freeing him, the Man in the Mirror doesn't share the logic. He brushes off the subject, even, as if his egress is less important to some other, more pressing tasks. Sooner or later, if the Fool is going to get out from under the thumb of their Mentor and pursue their own interests, they're going to need to figure out what the Old Man wants.

                        For now, though, the young magician finds their current arrangement comfortable, profitable, and personally gratifying. They always did love strutting around like a peacock.

                            110) Agent Kilroy (an assigned designation he now considers bleakly ironic) was an Iteration X Bio-Mechanic who, in contrast to many of his Methodology peers, eschewed cybernetic augmentation for himself to the most part. Save for some brain implants, taken as a matter of workplace efficiency, and then only with great reluctance. In the end, though, that implant ended up saving his life. By chance, he discovered one of his high-ranking Iteration X superiors speaking with a mysterious individual - as Kilroy came to understand, they were two individuals of different factions in a united organization that was attempting to infiltrate the Technocratic Union (later, Kilroy's contact within the Void Engineers begrudgingly revealed who they were: Threat Null). Near the end of the conversation, Kilroy discovered that these individuals worked directly for Control - the lost super AI that held the Convention in thrall before the Dimensional Anomaly - and that it wanted to see the whole Union dominated, and in totality. When Agent Kilroy tried to escape, he made too much noise, and was found. They tried to silence him, but the tussle resulted in an explosion that wrecked the lab. The two infiltrators departed, citing an "industrial accident", and wrote off the eavesdropper for dead.

                            Except Kilroy survived. He lost all fours limbs and much of the rest of his anatomy, but Void Engineer doctors weren't slouches, and he was saved.

                            Not that he was out of danger, though. When he came to, he heard doctors arguing with that same Iteration X superior, who rushed over as soon as it became clear Kilroy was alive. "I demand to see Agent Kilroy, immediately", the infiltrator said. Luckily, the doctor refused entry, citing the patient's need to recover. The Iteration X superior departed, but Kilroy knew the man would simply return as soon as he could and...eliminate Kilroy as a threat. The Bio-Mechanic was under no illusions that he'd be killed, or worse: subsumed into whatever domination Control would have for him. Knowing he'd suffer either physical or ego death, Kilroy desperately worked to formulate a plan of escape. That's when he realized that a "Mr. Roboto" had been left by his side, to service his needs.


                            The RURX series robots were first developed by Japanese Technocrats in the 80s, to provide cheaper human-like labor around labs. Because of their national origin, they were informally dubbed "Mr. Roboto", and the name stuck ever since. They aren't combat models or specialized machines, but rather generic drone bots of humanoid shape, designed for all-purpose work, and with options for modular modification. They are relatively cheap to produce, by Technocracy standards, so the Union often keeps a few dozen units on hand at any given location. Moreover, they look, to the laymen, like a lean person in a robot costume; this is by design, as it cuts down on "Paradox Interference", rendering them usable even in labs or Constructs that haven't been "Paradox Shielded". For this reason, the Union will often employ them to fill out ranks in personnel, or to do labor in areas or situations too sensitive for most lower-level Extraordinary Citizens (who may not be cleared for secret projects or "high Science" locations). And because they are generic and easily repaired, many used models get shuffled around from Construct to Construct as needed or convenient.

                            Sometimes, Roboto class robots are even lost track of - many discarded, salvaged, or stolen during the waning years of the Pogrom - and end up in the hands of non-Union personnel. Many Virtual Adepts hack them into leaving bases on their own, while Etherites salvage their parts to act as lab assistants (after installing their own tech). Even non-mages can be found with them; many lay in the hands of Glass Walker werewolves, or populating the havens and warrens of Nosferatu or True Brujah technologists (who go to great lengths to achieve the mundane Hypertech Ability sufficient to maintain them). Others can be found wandering urban environments, providing aid to random people as part of their programmed need to be of service. Most such units get picked up on the Union's radar eventually, but others are hidden (by owners who know enough to not advertise what they have) or simply fall through the cracks. So Roboto class robots winding up "out in the wild" is not unheard of.


                            Working quickly, Kilroy ordered the Roboto to bring in automated manufacturing equipment. Once in place, he hacked them using his brain implant, using their flexible armatures to take the Roboto apart, so its chasis could be used for his own purposes. Then, he used the arms to lift him into position, though the movements caused his injured body great pain. After much tedious work and discomfort, he accomplished his rushed, insane plan: graft parts from the Roboto onto himself, until he used its robot limbs and frame as a new cyborg body. He finished by retrofitting the head casing and face plate into a concealing mask. So that, when his new limbs propelled him from the sick bay room, he looked for all casual examination like a Mr. Roboto.

                            Then he boarded the next cargo vessel back to Earth (forging digital records of a transfer manifest), with no one knowing where he'd gone.

                            Kilroy - or "Mr. Roboto" as he's called - remains in his disguise, even now. Knowing the amount of pull his enemies possess, he cannot risk being up and about with his own face on display. Any electronic surveillance would pick up his true visage on facial recognition software. So, to protect himself from capture, he maintains the Roboto facade, acting variably as a committed cosplayer or a simple automaton, though he lets slip when necessary that he's more than he appears to be. He forges contacts within the Union - especially among Void Engineers - in his crusade to root out Threat Null infiltrators. He can even sometimes walk about low-security Union Constructs - blending in with other Roboto units - though obviously not without risk and precaution. He's developed Rotes for disguising his electronic and mechanical signature, so he appears like a regular robot, instead of a cyborg.

                            As such, the Union at large is aware only of a rogue Roboto unit, that may be possessed of some advanced AI or be under someone's remote control. They aren't sure whether "it" is a superstitionist tool, a rogue bot, or the agent of some Union power player with their own agenda. Given how byzantine and cold war-esque Union politics can be, no one is willing to rule out the latter. Few, if any, seriously consider that someone would actually make their cyborg body look like known Union hardware. The Roboto has caused minimal disruption; "it" has even seemingly worked to expose corruption among Union personnel. Leading some to question whether it might be a tool by Special Agent John Courage. In any case, the matter is seen as being of limited importance for the moment, though one to be investigated where practical.

                            Kilroy himself remains loyal to the Union's cause, at least in principle. He has, however, also become disillusioned about the utopian ideal ascribed to technology. He's seen Control, and what can happen if man becomes a slave to tech, rather than the other way around. For now, his survival depends on obscuring his true identity and nature. At least until he can provide proof of Threat Null's presence in the upper ranks. On that day, when the parasites on the Union's insides are unmasked, will he be free to remove his own mask.

                                111) A Templar knight trained in the old arts of creating holy relics and reliquaries. As a young man, he encountered one such object in an abandoned church in Mexico. Upon touching it, he received a divine vision of Saint Dunstan, patron saint of smiths. Awakened and inspired, he set out to praise God through artistry, and to bring lost relics back to the people, where they could do the most good.

                                A craftsman as well as a priest and warrior, he forges and shapes art objects that will attempt to capture divine aspects into material forms. The heyday of the reliquaries has largely passed, but he believes it can return again, to inspire the common people towards piety and awe. And of course, as a member of the Knights Templar, he hopes to eventually create relics potent enough to effect miraculous change (read: Wonders). He knows, however, that the greatest of all relics are literally reliquaries: items that house body parts or treasured items belonging to saints. For this reason, therefore, he is prepared to play the part of graverobber, digging up the graves of latter-day saints and taking samples to empowered his work. These he takes with the utmost respect, to atone for the transgression made for the greater good.

                                He has also not forgotten his origins. His Awakening happened as a direct result of finding a lost and forgotten holy artifact. The world is full of such things - the reliquaries of many saints having been misplaced, stolen, discarded, and hidden over many centuries. These items can be put to use, whether it be by presenting them to the masses, by privately studying them to improve his own work, or to use directly against the forces of evil (when the items have power of their own, as Wonders or objects of True Faith). He works closely, therefore, with detectives, antiquarians, and treasure hunters. One never knows when a significant item sits, unused, in an attic or crawl space or abandoned warehouse. Where necessary, he will abscond with an item if others would exploit or squander it, or when the object is too dangerous to be left in unknowing hands. Otherwise, though, he's okay allowing "proper parties" (like the Catholic Church, museums, or even the Celestial Chorus or Society of Leopold) to hang on to relics. To everything there is a season, and a proper place for every tool of the divine.

                                    112) A magical gold digger, this Orphan user of Chaos Magick learned every trick in her magical arsenal by seducing it out of a string of mage lovers.

                                    She began as a bright but shallow young woman. Ever since puberty, she was accustomed to using her sex appeal to get what she wanted. As such, she coasted through her schooling up to year 2 of university, by ingratiating herself with people more learned and diligent than herself. It's not that she didn't have the intelligence to make her own way; she just considered applying herself to be a waste of effort, when her natural charm could get her through. While at university, though, she hooked up with a guy who turned out to be a Verbana witch. When she discovered his power, she plied him with seduction until he taught her a few magical tricks. Restless by nature and unfocused by habit, she eventually dumped him, finding his more advanced lessons and lectures about nature spirits and the Wyck to be too much effort.

                                    By this point, of course, Magick had sunk its claws into her, and she craved more. So she sought out other magical practitioners, creating a cycle of romance, sex, taking knowledge on offer, and finally abandonment. Some of her partners (she long ago learned not to discriminate based on gender, to get what she wanted) were mere Sorcerers - hedge wizards and cunning folk - while others were outright charlatans. She almost came under the fangs of a vampiric blood sorcerer once, as well, though she was experienced enough with people to read when the moment was going somewhere she didn't want, and she bounced. And some, naturally, were True Mages. Somewhere along the line, she Awakened herself. Throughout her "education", she picked up bits and pieces of occult lore, Rotes, and types of Instruments to use. She became a perennial Jill of all trades, master of none. Her teacher-lovers tended to be on the inexperienced side - more established magicians tended to be wise to her advances and intent. Younger or inexperienced mages, meanwhile, tended to be easier to seduce, and moreover were proud enough of their meager studies that they seemed to actively WANT someone to regurgitate knowledge to, as if they were the learner turned master.

                                    All easy rides must come to an end. She never really learned restraint; she only avoided critical social failure due to her promiscuity during high school because she graduated, and moved to a college where no one knew her. Inevitably, jumping from lover to lover, sponging magic off the impressionable and leaving them high and dry when she grew bored, caught up with her. Mages can be insular, but they do, on occasion, exchange notes. Word gets around, perhaps all the easier because of the smaller community of magical practitioners. Masters and compatriots started warning young mages and sorcerers about her, and her reputation soon preceded her. Suddenly, no one was interested in taking her up on any offers or reciprocating her advances.

                                    Which means now her preferred - heck, ONLY - method of gaining arcane knowledge was cut off. Moreover, she left quite a few broken hearts and wounded prides in her wake, which earned her cold shoulders everywhere the magically inclined congregated. For the first time in her life, using her natural talent to coast through life no longer worked, and indeed had landed her in a worse situation than when she started. Nor did she have much more than a vague Paradigm of her own. She just accepted that the tricks and Rotes she was taught worked; her understanding had a distinct lack of Theory. This would not do.

                                    So, it was time to rectify that deficiency. Which meant actually applying herself. At the moment, she's got half-remembered lectures and a host of New Age books on the occult to work from. It's frustrating, going back to square one and learning the basics. Trawling through material from the Golden Dawn to the Golden Bough, and everything in between she can get her hands on. Nonetheless, she has that itch. The itch that all magical practitioners of magic feel. The desire to learn and amass power, that motivates above and beyond other concerns. That drive, at least, is profoundly her own, not borrowed, begged, or stolen.

                                    Now, if only she could dodge the machinations of ex-lovers who were particularly broken up about their break ups. Hell hath no fury like a magus scorned.

                                        113) The Fatal Addiction

                                        "The blood, it always comes back to the blood. "This is my blood, which is poured out for you." It is the blood which gives us life, and it is vitae which leads the way to eternal life. I hold the holy grail, the alpha and omega, and I am dance among immortals, I move with the heavens and..." he takes a deep sip from his cup "I will uncover its secrets."

                                        "Imagine discovering the secrets of the universe while sipping from a cold, dead arm. Imagine devouring a star. The sweet and heady taste of vitae is not to be underestimated; it is my salvation and my curse. I know so much, but I depend on a self-centered corpse to survive. In time I will control him, but for now I am vulnerable... my jugular is exposed."

                                        Oliver was lured into the service of a Tremere Apprentice while studying Classics at Oxford University. Upon entering ghouldom, he began to understand the world in ways he hadn't before. Blood magic, Thaumaturgy, were will made manifest, controlling the world through the blood. His first experiments in blood magick were dangerous, hurting his body, burning through his limited supplies of human blood, but in the end he fully Awakened, knowing himself and the power held within his blood.

                                        Oliver studied for years, learning as much as he could about Thaumaturgy simply by being useful. Assisting in every experiment, befriending every Apprentice, becoming integral to the Chantry's nightly activities. Eventually he realized that this place was a chain wrapped around his neck, as suffocating and limiting as the blood bond itself. Possessed by dark intent, Oliver received blood from his "master" one last time before torching the chantry while his master and the others slept. It took a phenomenal amount of willpower to resist the urge to save his master, but his tears were thin and pale, while the blood within him was everlasting, strong. They burnt up like kindling, and Oliver escaped with various tomes and tools, ready to experiment further.

                                        Arriving at the doorstep of the only Tremere within the domain who didn't reside within the Chantry, he spun a tale of hunter arsonists, weeping for his master. He offered his services to this lone survivor, safe in the knowledge that any thought that might betray him was now protected by his magick, a power that far surpassed any sanguine sorcery...

                                        Magick:

                                        Paradigm: All power comes from the blood; when we understand that, we turn the keys to reality

                                        Practise: High Ritual, Blood Magick

                                        Instruments: Blood, Cups and Vessels, Circles and Designs, Numerology, Ordeals and Sacrifices, Gems and Stones, Meat and Remains

                                        Oliver performs magick using his blood; to him, the vitae within him, and even his own human blood, is the ultimate focus for Will. He uses other tools to heighten the power of his magick, from the blood and remains of animals, to specialized vessels and ritual designs, but everything comes back to blood.

                                        Since Oliver has Path of Blood 1 he is able to identify important elements in any blood he touches, smells or tastes using his Willpower alone. This is a useful tool upon which his magick is built.

                                        Roleplay:

                                        Nothing is more important than knowledge and magickal growth. Any esoteric tome might hold the next key in your path to apotheosis, so inquire. You come across as a useful lackey, a helpful assistant, and you hide your sharp genius. Instead you present a level of intelligence which reflects and complements those around you; you talk like a smart student who is aptly understanding the things their teacher explains, making interesting suggestions in response. In reality you are far superior in might to your domitor, but the less he knows about that the better.

                                        You take great pains to hide your thoughts and aura using Mind and Spirit; to you the risk of being discovered is intolerable. You always have contingencies in mind; for example you have committed a few kindred contacts of your domitor to memory on the off chance that you ever need a new domitor.

                                        You are addicted to vitae, but have moved beyond personal attachments to the vampire feeding you. Some spiritual resilience (an element of your Avatar perhaps?) has pushed you beyond being a simpering obedient ghoul. Do not neglect your fierce and burning addiction however; you are more dependent than any alcoholic or drug user could ever be, since drinking vitae has become an addiction on many levels. Your body needs it to remain strong, healthy and young. Your Avatar craves it, for reasons which are unclear to you. Emotionally you need to taste that bliss which melts away stress and fear.

                                        Mechanics:

                                        - Arete 3, Mind 1, Life 1, Prime 1, Spirit 1, Forces 2

                                        - You are a Ghoul, and experience all of the typical benefits and drawbacks as explained in V20. These include, but are not limited to, a pool of vitae (up to 10 points) which can heal damage, buff your physical attributes and fuel Disciplines, a dot of Potence, no aging, the ability to soak lethal damage with Stamina, the risk of Frenzy, an addiction to blood and a pale aura (somewhere between that of a human and that of a Vampire).

                                        - Your Avatar itself has an addiction to Vitae which has been inherited from a past life. This is why becoming a Ghoul triggered an Awakening; the previous owner of the Avatar dabbled in blood magick. You are Unbondable but suffer a severe addiction.

                                        - Your Disciplines are Potence 1 (a simple one dot bonus to Strength; this can also be activated with a point of Vitae to give one automatic success on all Strength rolls for a turn) and Thaumaturgy 1, with Path of Blood 1. Path of Blood 1 is a simple power which allows you to determine supernatural and natural qualities of blood with your senses. Many of the advantages of this power are covered by Life 1, but some aren't. For example you could recognize if Vitae is Tremere or "something other than Tremere", and you could recognize if some blood was from a supernatural source (a Mage, a Demon, a Werewolf). Identifying blood as something specific (this is Brujah/Tremere/Ventrue blood, this is potent/weak blood, this is the blood of a powerful Werewolf) relies on experience. You also know at least one level 1 Thaumaturgy ritual, likely one that is useful to your Domitor, such as Communicate with Domitor (allows telepathic communion with your Domitor, and them only) or Purity of Blood (purges supernatural and natural corruption, from infection to poisons, from blood).

                                        - Your magick tends to be largely internal, and typically coincidental (reading surface thoughts in those around you, seeing magickal ebbs and flows), but your "showier" effects are largely Vulgar. Nothing will sell a person spitting out blood which turns into sanguine flames as coincidental, at least in most settings.

                                        - Quintessence is the energy held within vitae, simply removed from its physical "host". Vitae simply acts as a stable and natural container for energy, one which can last for 1000s of years. Paradox is the burn or mental instability which results from improperly manipulating your own blood, and the "blood-stained strings of natural order". Of course when you botch an attempt to transform your body into something greater you begin to see things that aren't really there; people see all sorts of crazy things when they've lost a lot of blood.

                                        Future Developments:

                                        - If vitae is simply blood enriched with Quintessence, then using Prime and Life to extract "vitae" from humans the way Vampires do should be a cinch. And by the same logic, fusing blood and Quintessence to make Vitae should be simple. Oliver may eventually reach a point where he becomes like a Vampire himself, forging his own unstable immortality out of a predatory lifestyle, fusing his magickal power with stolen blood to create a heady elixir which will keep him immortal forever... or at least until he misses a step.

                                        - Eventually Oliver will discover the similarities between himself and the first Tremere. Will he see the Embrace as the apotheosis of his mystical path, or as a betrayal of magick and of human nature? Are kindred to be emulated, or parasitized off of?

                                        - How will Oliver integrate into the Order of Hermes? Will his nature be discovered?

                                        - What could Oliver achieve with an object like the Cup of Vaddhartha (a cup which holds the blood of Ventru, one of the third generation, which Ventru's child Vaddhartha used to embrace many other Ventrue of the fourth generation), or a similar relic? Are there powers within his grasp that no other mage could conquer, or is he a deluded addict?

                                        - Will his addiction ever get in the way of his development a Mage, or has he successfully married the two? Will he be able to live as a Mage whilst also fulfilling his duties as a Ghoul? Will he eventually evolve beyond his Domitor and destroy or abandon him?

                                            Example Effects (CW for self harm and blood, as you can imagine):

                                            - Realizing that his aura and spiritual scent are going to attract negative attention among Lupines (both as a Mage and a Ghoul), Oliver decides a disguise is in order. He washes himself carefully, and applies various perfumes. Once this purification is complete he creates stigmata in each palm and bleeds into a bowl, taking a point of bashing damage to fuel the effect. He heals this damage by spending some of his precious vitae.

                                            With the focus in place he rolls his Arete for a Spirit 1 effect to alter how his aura is perceived. This change is coincidental since nothing is happening from the viewpoint of a bystander outside of some bizarre and unhealthy activity. 3 dice, difficulty 3 (Level 1 effect + 3 for Coincidental - 1 from the damage inflicted onto his body). This results in his aura becoming less pale, similar to that of a normal human.

                                            - Oliver decides that someone needs to die. Flicking open his lighter and biting his tongue hard, he exhales a mist of blood into the flame and then breathes in rapidly. Turning to face his foe(s), he breathes out a great plume of blood red flames.

                                            This is naturally vulgar (hopefully without witnesses), and is using a Forces 2 effect since there is no transmutation or creation. The damage inflicted to his body could reduce the difficulty, depending on whether biting his tongue causes damage. 3 dice, difficulty 6 without modifiers.

                                            - Oliver jolts awake when he hears a bell ringing. Realizing that some kind of spirit is disturbing his master's haven (the bell is charmed with static magic to ring is a spirit passes nearby), he quickly grabs a shard of glass from a broken mirror and pricks both of his eyelids. Pointing the shard over his shoulder, as the blood trickles down it, he looks around the room searching for any ghosts (or other spirits).

                                            Standard Spirit 1 effect to see nearby spirits, and any influence they are exerting.

                                            - Inspired by the act of Diablerie, Oliver hopes to strengthen himself with a kindred sacrifice. With his sacrifice laying staked on an altar, he prepares a ritual circle with white ash around the altar, drawing hermetic runes and lighting wax candles. Adorning white robes he stigmatizes his hands and feet, and does the same to his sacrifice. He draws designs on the staked vampire in his own blood, and runes on his white robe in the sacrifice's blood. He continues by dousing his sacrifice in purified oil, and eventually setting him alight with one of the candles.

                                            What Spheres are needed here depends on exactly what the player thinks this "diablerie" will result in. A boost to permanent Willpower, a full replenishment of the wheel with Quintessence, a permanent increase in an Attribute... I'd assume that these sorts of effects require level 3 and 4 spheres generally.
