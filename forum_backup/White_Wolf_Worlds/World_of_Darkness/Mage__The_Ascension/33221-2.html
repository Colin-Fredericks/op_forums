            Originally posted by DSPaul View Post
            The Syndicate is fighting for a world where everyone can have what they want, even the enemy. You just have to pay for it.

            To the rest of the Technocracy, the Traditions are "superstitionists" -- ignorant fanatics pushing false, self-destructive paradigms and toxic ideologies on innocent sleepers. They're bigoted reactionaries standing in the way of Progress and Enlightenment. They not only want to sabotage humanity's destiny, they want to humanity to turn its back on the notion of Progress entirely. There can be no compromise with such deluded zealots; for the sake of humanity, they and their twisted paradigms must be stamped out. They are the enemy.

            The Syndicate, on the other hand, sees the Traditions as untapped markets.

            The Syndicate doesn't want you to abandon your existing value system. They want to sell you the things they value. You need ancient grimoires, exotic reagents, sacred relics? Just contact your nearest Syndicate representative. Want to get away from the materialistic corruption of the modern world? The Syndicate can hook you up with meditation retreats, pilgrimage tours, and gated communities where you'll never have to so much as look at anyone who doesn't share your paradigm. Want to convert the sleepers to the One True Way? The Syndicate will set you up with a lucrative publishing contract, a nationwide speaking tour, and your own talk show. These are the guys who invented the Burqini; they can monetize anything.

            The Syndicate's ideal world isn't a grey, homogeneous dictatorship where all opposing belief systems are prohibited on pain of re-education. It's a rich multicultural tapestry where people of all creeds are free to communicate, socialize, do business, and pursue their own goals in life, without being judged or persecuted. The Syndicate don't want to rule the world. In fact, they're the only faction in Mage that doesn't want to rule the world; the only Convention or Tradition that isn't dominated by ideologues who view even their supposed comrades as "useful idiots." The Syndicate believes that all paradigms and belief systems have value, and they're eager to leverage that value for everyone's mutual benefit.

            The Syndicate genuinely wants everybody to be friends. They honestly don't understand why everyone else seems to find the idea so horrifying.
            I've started reading the revised CB and I think at their best, they're well-intentioned, but are misguided. I could see a Wall-E type of future where everyone is so used to having their comforts met that they've forgotten what is truly important. The people ended up fat, lazy and with no desire to advance themselves. I know the Syndicate claims that's not what they want, but it could happen.

On top of that, each level they are pressured not to fraternize with those in lower positions for operational security and to avoid influence trickling up the structure from “less informed” levels but it has the side effect of isolating the tiers with each level being “us” to those on it and everyone of lower rank being marginalized and lumped into successively lower levels of “them” who have less access to information and are theoretically are less competent than those above. Eventually they are reduced to nothing but statistics in the eyes of the higher-ups and no one really cares about the details of numbers, just the bottom line.

Some call it unpleasant but necessary expediency for the greater good; others call it criminal disregard for the rules and the rights of others. Personally I see it as more the latter which is why I prefer the Traditions even though they are not without their own glaring flaws.
                                        I am not sure I agree with this entirely. The NWO has a lot of conflict with the Syndicate (think about Obamacare/the ACA and how the NWO likely wants socialized/universal healthcare for a variety of noble [elevating/uplifting humanity] and potentially concerning [more control through government] reasons, but the Syndicate likely wants to keep pharmaceutical profits high and permit the free market to remain unregulated so the Syndicate can maximize profits; the Progenitors are likely caught in the middle between huge pharma profits funding their research and their desire to help cure/treat as many people as possible).

                                        There are certainly similarities among the Conventions of the Technocracy as a whole, but to write them off as a monolith is the conventional Tradition perspective (and likely inaccurate). Believing in "survival of the fittest," etc. in terms of a free market ideology is very Syndicate, but not necessarily reflective of other conventions.

                                                        Originally posted by Imperator View Post

                                                        There are certainly similarities among the Conventions of the Technocracy as a whole, but to write them off as a monolith is the conventional Tradition perspective (and likely inaccurate). Believing in "survival of the fittest," etc. in terms of a free market ideology is very Syndicate, but not necessarily reflective of other conventions.
                                                        I don't believe social darwinism is an altogether apt comparison for what the Syndicate believes and what they do. Hell, if you read the mid-splat short in the chapter on the Convention's history, it lays out the Syndicate as a Convention believes the root cause of society's ills is the problem of scarcity itself, which is what drives supply and demand, and from that value, wealth, and the power derived from the accumulation and employment of wealth.

                                                        The Syndicate's collective vision of Ascension is the eradication of scarcity itself. When you look at it that way, the Syndicate actually becomes a hell of a lot more noble than any other Tradition of Convention. How many others have such a self-sacrificial a view of Ascension?

                                                        What I believe is a more fitting statement, is they believe in an idealized Enlightenment (i.e. Adam Smith) view of capitalism and neo-mercantilism wherein a rising tide does, in fact, lift all boats and wealth, over the long term, will distribute itself according to merit opposed to intervening sociopolitics. What they're too hubristic to admit as a Convention, is they're fighting against the weight of millennia of feudalist, dynastic, imperialist, legalist, and absolutist rule, and that the iron rule of oligarchy is too firmly embedded in the Consensus to break in the span of mere centuries; hell, the very concept of international trade as a formal institution is only a development of the 20th Century, for crying out loud.

                                                        The social and political forces surrounding capitalism exert a corrupting force on the latter, not the other way around, and the Syndicate doesn't have an answer to that. Yet, anyways, and assuming the NWO is going to sit back and allow them to implement whatever solution they may find as it would invariably come at heavy price to their own Convention.

                                                        In short, they're Mages. And, they're trapped by their own apparatus and the weight of the Consensus, just like every other Convention. To bring it back to social darwinism, I believe that reflects more the existential morass in which the Syndicate finds itself as it's brought down by the weight of corrupting influence (power-obsessed leadership, losing sight of their goals, combating conflicting goals of other Conventions, Nephandic infiltration, etc.) which plagues every other Convention in its own way.
