    The Construct Flaw

    05-19-2018, 04:49 AM
    Planning on creating an android PC and thought the Construct flaw in the Anniversary Book (pg. 647) would be thematically appropriate. Unfortunately, its rather vague in how severe or freqeuent its social penalties are. Does anyone have any examples how the flaw could be used in actual play or perhaps have their own house ruled version?

      As the Flaw description says: It depends on how your character is different from humans. Is it unable to feel true emotions, just emulating them? Then I guess Empathy would be much harder, especially when trying to assess someones true feelings, but Intimidation could even get a small buff, for inhumanity.
      Is your character just not looking "right"? Then appearance-rolls might be harder and maybe people become rather suspicious when dealing with it, resulting in harder Subterfuge rolls.

      The description is deliberately vague, so the flaw can be customized to your specific needs. Write a description of how your character is different from a born human. What makes your android stand out? And how could that influence certain rolls?
      I'd be happy to see your results and to help further, if necessary.

              It's vague because they'll be different depending on the character. I had a construct character who's only outward sign of being a construct was that his ethnicity was hard to place. Of course, once he started getting injured that all would go out the window, because he had toxic waste for blood, and a terminator style metal endoskeleton.

              But in the end, the primary disadvantage was EVERY INTERACTION WITH EVERYONE. Because he firmly believed that he was inferior to Humanity. That any of his accomplishments were merely a result of the clever engineering of his designers. He owned his failures. But all his success he attributed to the Technocracy.

              He was a surprisingly depressing fellow once you looked past the Veneer of 'Arnie Terminator Guy'.

                          Does this seem appropriate for a two point flaw?
                          Well your ST could raise the point value of the Flaw.

                          As for defection, the Virtual Adepts, Society of Ether and (wild card) the Dreamspeakers seem like the best options.

                          With the VAs, your brain is a computer. With the SoE, your body is a machine. With the Dreampeakers, you are a machine with an Avatar, a spirit, and therefore a Technoshaman would bond with you quite extensively.

                          I could also strongly recommend the Enhancements background, to reflect some advantages of your android body. Just synthetic components which are too powerful to be given for free simply by being an Android.

                          If you went for a Spiritual angle, investing points in The Machine or Glitch as a totem would be a great move.

                          As for Paradigm... Tech Holds All Answers, Turning the Keys of Creation (some creator left systems which we can exploit to change the world, like making a machine which mimics some cosmic key to create an Effect), We Are Not Men (all Mages are inhuman on some basic level, superhuman even), Creation if Divine and Alive (if you add a Spiritual aspect), Reality is Data (if you focus on computing).

                          As a final note, you can take a merit from Book of Secrets which reflects a multi-faceted education, and affiliation with two Traditions. If a well-designed Android Awakens, then it's reasonable that they might learn from both the VAs and the SoE, or from the VAs and the Dreamspeakers (tech spirits, Glitch, the digital web), or from the SoE and the Dreamspeakers (mechanical spirits, The Machine, animistic spiritual awakening of mechanisms and technology). This has social benefits, but also allows you to choose two Affinity Spheres (say, Data and Prime, or Data and Spirit, or Prime and Spirit).

                                      Originally posted by Weirdboyz View Post
                                      Not really related to my Construct Flaw question, but I did want to ask a few questions about cybernetics and didn't think making a whole another thread was necessary.

                                      1. Does implanting cybernetics or nanotech into a living thing require using the Life, Matter and/or Prime Spheres or can you just rely on the Biotech skill?
                                      2. Are cybernetics always treated as Enhancements or is that only when they're permanent?
                                      3. This probably doesn't have a straightforward answer but since cybernetics aren't exactly a common commodity than one can buy at the local hardware store should they require a minimum Resource score when used as an instrument like money does? This is of course assuming the PC isn't a Technocrat who could go to their local Construct lab for this stuff.
                                      4. Can you make Primium with just Prime or do you need Matter as well?
                                      1: That depends. Life and Matter allows you to add something to a person's pattern; this lets you do things that real biotech and medical engineering would never allow for. So installing a pacemaker or a little electrode to help with parkinsons? Biotech. Giving someone's bones a titanium alloy mesh? Life 4 + Matter 4.

                                      2: Enhancements covers a few things. Xenotransplants, genetic engineering, and cybernetics. Cybernetic Enhancements don't have to be truly permanent, they're just hard to remove. You can choose to have "modular" implants; the rules for this are covered in Book of Secrets. Essentially this means you have a cybernetic module in your arm which you can expel and replace with another, mixing and matching for the current situation. Back to your question... cybernetic implants which are coincidental and possible under current scientific achievement are not represented as Enhancements, they're simply something possessed by some people, and plenty of Mages with the Cybernetics Practice/Focus. So you could easily have computer chips implanted in your skin, for example.

                                      With regards to 1 and 2, here's an example. Without using their Spheres (except, perhaps, to reduce the difficulty of this dangerous procedure!), an Iterator or Technoshaman or Etherite could implant an electrode in their basal ganglia, and hook it up to a battery which is accessible from the outside. Then they could play around with stimulating it. Without using magick, they could then tweak their own motivation and reward pathways somewhat (but getting anything good out of this would require understanding of neurology). And they could also treat themselves for any conditions relating to this circuitry. Now here's the important bit; this implant is also a good Instrument for their Focus. If they want to make this implant an instrument of a Mind 1 effect to boost their Wits and Perception and Intelligence (up-regulating the activity of the neural circuits), then they can do that, and make it Coincidental in most Reality Zones. In fact such an implant is so... weird, that it would likely qualify as a Personalized Instrument. After all, ANYTHING you've prepared and surgically implanted in yourself (even with an assistant) is a Personalized instrument by default.

                                      3: If you're a Mage with the cybernetics focus, then you need to justify where you're getting your materials from, yes. The Lab and Resources backgrounds cover this quite well. But the same applies to where a Hermetic is getting their candles and where a Verbena is getting their herbs. The components are available anywhere via the internet and specialist outlets, and the main barriers are skill and safety.

                                      4: In M20, no one knows how to make Primium except a niche part of the Technocracy, who holds it as their DEAREST secret. It is such a huge priority to keep it in check. If a Mark V gets knocked out and hogtied, their insides are turning into soup (self-destruct) and the Primium is dissolved and its chemical structure degraded in the process. In Revised the Etherites and Templars had recipes for it based on alchemically purified Gold and Silver, using Prime and Matter, but in M20 they cannot replicate Primium despite their best efforts. It's likely that if the Traditions were to get a big batch of Primium then they'd be able to reverse engineer a rote to make it.
