    As far as i am concerned, the average tradition mage, to put it mildly, is affected, in terms of morality, by having so much power without drawbacks.

    Let us say that the average, using the mathematical definition of the word, mage of the Traditions, not counting nephandi infiltrators!, has a humanity of around 6, to put it in vampire terms.

    By way of comparison, the average vampire, rounding down literally everyone, has a humanity of 5, in my own view anyway. (even ignoring those on paths of enlightenment)

    In short, the average mage is better than bloodsucking fiends, but not by that much. Of course, the answer would vary, with regard to individual tradition

    Of course hypocrisy is a natural part of humanity

                                Simple political binaries rarely present perfectly anyways. Even if side A is intrinsically evil and side B contains ways of being good, that doesn't mean every way of being on side B is good. Plenty of evil can be done in the name of fighting evil, and much more can be done when fighting something that isn't wholly evil.

                                Usually the real issues are the underlying reasons behind the discussion, which makes things much more complicated. A worthwhile conversation about freedom vs security, for instance, will not place either Freedom or Security as objectively good ends in their own right, and will instead focus on the deeper goods, like human value and dignity, that both sides (hopefully) are trying to uphold.

                                Which touches on one of the frequent tragedies of the Technocracy (and to a lesser extent the Traditions): they do fight for humanity, but they're always at risk of forgetting of why humanity is important. That's why the Syndicate often focuses on human statistics rather than individual wants, and the NWO often tries to manipulate humanity into more controllable forms. Indeed one of the classic principles of the villainous Technocracy is: Humanity is so important that all threats to it must be destroyed, including humanity.


                                Mage: The Ice-ension: An Epic Game of Reality on the Rink

                                                        Originally posted by CTPhipps View Post

                                                        The hubris of mages is that they claim that Mass Ascension can only come from above as a gift handed down versus a hand up.
                                                        What is your distinction between a hand down vs a hand up? Are we talking about the way mages go about Mass Ascension, or that they try at all?

                                                        I think there are ways mages could pursue Mass Ascension that are less arrogant than others. All of them run into a serious problem built in to Mage, though, which is that the very idea of Consensual Reality pushes mages to objectify people.

                                                        Trying to alter the Consensus means treating regular people's beliefs, desires, perceptions, and lived experiences as pieces on a game board. The Technocracy exemplifies this, but the Traditions aren't that far behind. To change the world, even for the benefit of the people, they have to override the desires of the people.

                                                        Of course, in many situations it is good to challenge people's beliefs, desires, perceptions, or lived experiences. Teaching comes to mind. But teachers, ideally, are teaching things they themselves believe to be true and beneficial. Mages are on much shakier ground.


                                                        Originally posted by Nyremne View Post
                                                        Well, is it hubris if it's true?

                                                        Most of humanity is not awakened, they're metaphysically blind, and mages have peeked beyond the platonic cave, even if they ended with massively different views of what it means, they still understand more than 99.9% of people.
                                                        But is it true? Mages have gained insight into the nature of reality. They haven't gained any special insight into morality or the nature of humanity. At best they have the ability to see additional ways the world could be, but they have no additional authority to determine what should be.
