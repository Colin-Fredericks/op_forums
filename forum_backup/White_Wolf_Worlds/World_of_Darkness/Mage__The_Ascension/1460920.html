                  thing is if my idea focuses on a agent sowing discord that only becomes possible at high levels.
                  There are three aspects to this:

                  1. Your goal, which is to sow Discord.
                  2. Your preferred technique, which is Hypnotism.
                  3. Limitation based on Ranks in hypnotism (three dots).

                  I will start with #3. The book says Charisma is the applicable attribute to roll with Hypnotism. Say you took four points in Charisma with a specialty that gives you double-tens on Hypnotism rolls. That gives you seven dice. With certain Spheres, you can cast effects that reduce the difficulty of a Hypnotism roll. Mind, obviously. With Forces, you could change the sound of your voice to appeal to a particular audience. With Life, relax the body of one subject. Then, there could be mundane circumstances that you arrange to your advantage to reduce the difficulty. With the difficulty reduced enough and that extra success from willpower spent, you could easily get five successes, enough to put the audience under your spell. This brings me to my point about #3. You are not limited by the number of dots/ranks in an ability, but by successes rolled during gameplay, which is variable rather than fixed. You can do things to increase your odds. If you botch, try again.

                  As for the first two, being a divisive politician whose speeches are laden with hypnotic suggestions would achieve the goal of spreading Discord and it uses Hypnotism. On a micro-scale, you could use hypnotism to seduce people and force them to compete for you, playing them off of each other. You could use hypnotism to describe something of value like a Node, ending with, "If we had a Maxine's Node, we could rock this city. Maybe she will push off soon and we can take it over.". Make it permissible to consider taking things of value by force or underhanded means.

                  Good luck.

                          Mind 3 allows a mind-link, which is two way. It's something you would do with members of your Cabal who you trust. It's also vulgar if done with a sleeper who is not an Acolyte. You can read memories with Mind 3. Mind 4 allows you to alter memories and control behavior.

                          Hypnotism is a mundane ability that does not permit mind to mind communication (telepathy). You are using spoken words to influence the person. It is difficult to alter memories Retroactively. When abusive cops convince an innocent person that they committed a crime by berating them for hours, it usually lasts just long enough for the victim to give a confession and then their real memory starts to reassert itself. The brain always store authentic memory somewhere. Memory can be deeply suppressed but not erased.

                          If you have caught a spy and the spy knows they have been caught, you do not have Rapport (a relationship of trust) with them. You might have to use drugs to facilitate Command Hypnosis that relies on fear and authority. The spy wants to be released unharmed (physically) and you are the one who controls whether or not that happens.

                          If you have identified a spy and the spy believes their cover is intact, there may be more to work with. You could claim that your secret base exists in the Umbra and that the only way to get there is through dream travel. Everyone has to lay down on yoga mats and listen to your guided visualization journey. Add VR gear, some drugged herbal tea and you can manipulate the person's senses so that they believe they have been to this secret dream-base. Build on this fiction to create whatever disinformation suits your strategic goals. If you know about some other enemy on their side, create an illusory meeting where that enemy is a spy working for you, bringing information about the spies own colleges. Let spy return and hope that they implicate one of their own allies.

                              Unfortunately it is distressingly easy to retroactively edit memories. The specific example of berating someone for hours to get them to completely rewrite their memories own actions may difficult, but getting them to alter details, motivations, orders of events, faces, etc, is simpler. In fact it can happen without intentional effort.

                              It's one of the known issues with eyewitness testimony, especially police lineups. Witnesses, even victims, often do not remember events as well as they think they do and will unconsciously edit their memories when given reason. There are documented cases of police lineups changing the memories of witnesses so that afterwards they permanently remember the wrong face even after it becomes clear that the person they remember was nowhere near the incident.

                              While I agree that it would be very difficult to alter a clear memory, oftentimes our memories aren't that firm and we are still backfilling them using subsequent information. Those are the kind of memories a manipulator would be able to edit and while they couldn't destroy or replace the memory, there's a lot they could do to alter it.

                                  Unfortunately that's true. Memory is far less reliable than we like to think it is, and there is no such a thing as "authentic memory" in the brain. Memories are edited frequently and can be altered permanently in many ways. Even entire swaths of population can have their memories altered by a similar event, causing several witness to share identical false memories.

                                  One such case I know off here in Brazil was 9/11. Due to an online conversation that occurred many years after the fact, a big number of people around the country clearly remember watching a specific episode of Dragon Ball Z when it was interrupted by the news about the attacks. It is a clear memory frequently accompanied of others about what they were doing or thinking at the time, or what their expectations were about DBZ at the moment.

                                  But there was no airing of Dragon Ball in the country that day as the news started more than 1 hour earlier than DBZ would start and the episode mentioned wasn't on the schedule for another week and a half (it was aired daily here in a nation-wide channel). To this day many people still argue that they clearly remember the episode being cut before Goku's transformation in Super Sayan 3.

                                  This is called Mandela effect, after another common false memory the world around about Nelson Mandela being buried in the 80's (he died in 2013). But many other phenomena of memory alteration exist and today it is pretty established that memories actually never remain unchanged after formation.

                                      I authentically remember returning in a car from a short trip with a member of my family who was driving. She had been advised by a doctor not to drive while on painkillers she had been prescribed. She thought herself to be able to handle the effects. We made it home to my parents' house. I was fifteen. I went upstairs to my bedroom, where I stayed, waiting for the family visit downstairs to come to a close so that I could make plans with friends to play D&D. It was a Saturday.

                                      There was a knock at the door downstairs. I heard two male voices at the door speaking to my family. I opened the door and looked down the stairs to see who was there. They were police officers who took note of me. I was asked to go to their police van for a conversation. They explained that the car had been driven down the road into a cement wall that separated a neighbor's yard from the sidewalk. They claimed that this neighbor had seen me drive the car down the street, into the wall, exit the car and return home. I denied the accusation while avoiding implicating the adult driver.

                                      I was handcuffed and put in the back of the police van and then driven in a circuitous path to the police station with no ability to stop myself from hitting the hard surfaces around me. At the police station, they attempted to alter my memory. It went on for some time. They failed and I was released. I later learned that the car had rolled down the hill, having been parked improperly. I would say that I did well for a fifteen year old kid who was unprepared to be questioned this way. It was my belief in the truth of what I remembered doing and not doing that prevented me from being convinced by two professional interrogators.

                                      In saying, "The brain always stores authentic memory somewhere.", I am referring to the Original memory that sensory input is converted into. Situationally speaking, it is usually difficult to alter someone's memory, because it is likely to meet with Resistance. If I dress well, go to a store where there is an inexperienced cashier handling a long lineup, pay for something and, upon getting my change back, say that I paid with a bill of higher denomination than she gave me change for, I might make some money or I might get permanently banned from that store. Somewhere in the cashier's brain is a brief memory of having seen the bill I handed her and there is a risk of provoking resistance by trying to mess with that.

                                      In game terms, we are talking about the Hypnotism Ability in Book of Secrets. It mentions creating a "memory that the subject will believe is his own" as one of the things you can do. The system is Charisma + Hypnotism versus subject's Intelligence + Willpower if they are unwilling. So, the authors are claiming that more Intelligent people have higher resistance to having their memory altered than people with lower Int. Does this reflect reality?

                                              "The brain stores authentic memories somewhere" seems more like an assumption you might build into your game rather than a hard truth. It is certainly a useful tool for defining what Mind can do, but I'm not even sure how we would go about proving it true or false in real life. Hollywood certainly likes it, though, I know that.

                                              Your example starts to show the issues. That clerk doesn't have one bit of sense memory he has multiple. The feel of the bill, the look of the bill, the look of the customer, nearby distractions like crying children, the sound of the AC unit, the terrible muzak playing over the store's sound system, etc. His memory of the event is built out of some of those individual sense memories but notably not all of them. Much of it is regarded as irrelevant. Change how the memory is assembled and you change the memory.

                                              There's no assembly of those senses that could make the clerk think he himself had opened the drawer and stolen $100, sure, but if you get him to doubt his memory of what the bill looked like you could get him to think he'd been handed $50 instead of a $20. And that's assuming he does have good sense memory of the bill, which is not a certainty.
