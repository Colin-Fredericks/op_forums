                Getting back to the original subject of the thread: in my head-canon, the spatial horizon is in effect the boundary of the solar system. The Solar System isn't the center of the universe, with the Deep Universe being all that's beyond it; rather, the Deep Universe contains a multitude of worlds, each similar to Earth with a surrounding “Conventional Space” separated from the Umbra by a Gauntlet. The worlds within these pockets of “normalcy” don't have the same base reality that we do, so when we visit them we are the aliens. However, they are not part of the Void, and visitors to these worlds do not need to fear Void Adaptation while they are there. Instead, they have to contend with hostile Environmental Conditions, which I borrow from the STV supplement Changeling: Countless Dreams (pp.27–33)*. This gives an option where old style VEs can do the Star Trek thing, exploring strange new worlds, seeking out new life and new civilizations, and boldly going where no human has gone before; meanwhile, new style void engineers can do the Avatar thing, where they find a Pandora like world and set out to plunder its native resources. Or at least, that's what they might do if they had the time and energy to do more than just be the last line of defense for Earth.

                Meanwhile, the actual aliens out there aren't really Umbrood; the ones that we've encountered who are are actually Void Adapted aliens.


                * For what it's worth, I highly recommend Countless Dreams, for both Changeling and Mage games with heavy sci-fi/space opera elements. It needs some tweaking to work without changeling, and probably could use some tweaking if you aren't interested in its specific premise (that the Countless are alien refugees in Earth who have fused themselves with human hosts to survive after a catastrophe wiped out their homeworlds); but there are a lot of useful ideas and game systems to borrow for exotic aliens.

                                    Ported back over from the other thread:

                                    Originally posted by Erinys View Post
                                    But the reason I challenged you was my question about game balance: is it a bad thing if mysticks get some unique advantages? Aren't Technocrats automatically strongly advantaged by the setting? They sure look like they are, but I'm asking if my impression is correct. I also usually root for the underdogs and dangit, the Technos are the ones who created the Avatar Storm. On purpose! So part of me just says they deserve to get burned by it. Not that there is anything resembling cosmic justice or fair karma in the WoD.
                                    The Technocracy did not create the Avatar Storm on purpose. They were involved in the series of events that lead to the Avatar Storm, such as frying an Antediluvian and setting off a nuke in the Underworld; but the latter was the action of a rogue VE, not something sanctioned by the Technocracy. And it triggered the Sixth Great Maelstrom in the Underworld, which certainly wasn't the Technocracy's intent; but don't conflate the Sixth Great Maelstrom with the Avatar Storm: the Avatar Storm kicked in a little bit after the 6GM arose, but it was a separate phenomenon.

                                    Likewise, the attack on the Ravnos Antediluvian wasn't done to trigger the Avatar Storm; it was done because an incredibly powerful monster was rampaging across India and was causing tremendous harm, and needed to be stopped.

                                    The Avatar Storm has been described by some as a massive Paradox Backlash. Nobody intended for it to happen. The Void Engineers, in particular, were devastated by it, precisely because they didn't see it coming until it hit them.

                                    And the reason why the Technocracy has a powerful advantage in the modern setting is because it worked for it. Not in nice ways, granted; but they put in the effort. It didn't just happen. Even with my pet theory that Technocratic Reality is built on the Earthly Foundations and thus is easier to get buy-in from the Consensus than other Reality Zones are, there was never anything inevitable about Technocratic Reality Zone dominance.

                                    Conversely, I wasn't complaining about the Union somehow being disadvantaged by the way the Umbra works (except the Void Engineers; but that's a separate issue in my mind); my main complaint in this regard was that only shamans and witches were getting the extended survival, which in turn carries implications about the nature of the Umbra which I've addressed in the other thread. I'm not thinking in terms of what would be just; barring the discovery of the Sphere of Telos (see the Time of Judgement book Ascension), there's no guarantee of justice in the World of Darkness. And whether or not there can or will be justice is one of those things that's a hotly contested point in the Ascension War: there are whole Paradigms (in the M20 sense of the term) devoted to that question.
