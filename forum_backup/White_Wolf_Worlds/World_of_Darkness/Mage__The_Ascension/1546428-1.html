                                            OK, so before I respond directly I'm going to point out another piece of evidence from the books:

                                            The alternative Technocratic Spheres for Correspondence is not Dimensional Science, but rather Data. It is not the study of "space" but rather the study of how things connect to each other. Putting aside the iffy implementation of Data mechanically (search this forum for plenty of discussion of that if you want), Data, like Correspondence, is not about space as Sleepers (and we the readers) think of it (aka this "empty space" being brought up). We are told this multiple times, over multiple editions.

                                            So, added in, I don't buy it. I'm citing books, I'm pointing out precise wording from the texts, and it seems like instead of any actual reasoning based on the text, I'm having to argue against vibes.

                                            Dataweaver, Ramnesis, quelsec, and Magus Maximus, for the sake of the OP trying to understand how the game works, can we please either point to direct passages in the text that support your positions (keeping in mind you guys aren't in agreement with each other so there's at least five differing interpretations flying around in this thread), or actually walk through the reasoning of how you're getting to your positions.

                                            "I have always seen it as..." doesn't matter. We've all played games "wrong" by not reading wording closely; sometimes for the better since TTRPGs aren't always written that well and we insert more intuitive rules without even realizing it.

                                            Here's what the Landscape of the Mind rote from Revised says:

                                            "• Landscape of the Mind — One of the most basic Correspondence exercises, this Effect opens the mage’s awareness to her surroundings without recourse to normal senses. With concentration and ritual, the mage can “feel” or perceive a great area of space, although extreme areas are both difficult to encompass magically (requiring many successes) and difficult to process mentally (generally requiring the aid of Mind magic for anything beyond a city block in area)."

                                            As I said before, this is vague, almost uselessly vague. Unlike Sense Connection and Whereami? this is gives no examples of what this is actually supposed to do for you practically. What even counts as a "great space" isn't even given any guidance since magical range charts in Mage are, you know, based on sympathetic connections.

                                            So, as I already did, we go back to the general description of Correspondence, and the general description of Correspondence 1... and we get a simple message from the book. Correspondence by itself is abstract and vague in what it tells you, and you add Pattern Spheres to be more specific.

                                            Now we could go back to Mage 2e, which doesn't even try to explain the line between when Correspondence alone can do something and when you need to mix in other Spheres. You have to try to figure it out from the rote examples - a spotty proposition at best - though at least 2e's Landscape of the Mind is more clear on what it's supposed to help you do. Though it only says you can sense "objects" not "targets" or "Patterns" in your immediate surroundings even if you can't perceive them with your normal senses. This is a better source for "you can see a gun in someone's pocket, but not electric signals in their brain," because a gun is an object and energy waves aren't. But that basically means Landscape of the Mind only really helps with Matter Patterns, which while not useless leads to some pretty counter-intuitive results (probably why Revised changed things the way it did).

                                            Sorcerer's Crusade isn't much help here (it doesn't include Landscape of the Mind, and it's Sphere descriptions are even sparser than M20, as each only gets a single page).

                                            We could also scour supplements... but that hit or miss, the supplement authors tend to be heavy handed with mixing Spheres, and which to look at is a host of debates in the making. But you're not going to find much in the way of "pure" Correspondence effects, and a lot of mixed Rotes for doing things that a lot of people are saying Correspondence can do on it's own. Since I was just poking around Sorcerer's Crusade to check, it has a navigation spell that uses Connection 2 (Correspondence is a newer name in the lore)/Forces 1/Matter 1 to "map" a route through unknown terrain. SC is nice because it actually explains what each Sphere is doing in the rote. Connection ramping up to 2 is a bit iffy on making a case for itself (essentially to get over the "great distances issue even if phrased very differently), but the Forces and Matter are specifically to put moving (rivers, fires, etc.) and inert (a boulder on the trail, a damaged bridge, etc.) obstacles on your mental map before they enter sensory range so you can try to avoid them without getting lost via detours. Which reminded my of one of my favorite little rotes from Hollowers Revised that's also a navigation spell that uses Corr 1/Entropy 2 to get from A to B. Corr 1 doing the sensing of the connection of A to B, and Entropy 2 making sure you're always picking a direction that maximizes your odds of getting to where you want to be... though it notes this method doesn't guarantee safety or speed, just that you stick on a true path to your destination. While I don't really want to go through my whole collection, I think the point is pretty clear that move multiple editions, the authors of the books expect Corr 1 and 2 to get comboed with Pattern Spheres for practical effects mages are going to actually use.

                                            So, yeah, we can stick with Revised, where there's at least some clarity, and the "Pokemon outline game" thing really doesn't apply, or we can go back to 2e and get into heated debates on what definition of object to use in the context of the text. But to me I'm not seeing any sort of case for this desire to make Correspondence 1 do things the books never specify it can do.

                                                    Originally posted by M20, Correspondence 1
                                                    Basic spatial understanding allows a mage to sense things in her immediate vicinity even if she can’t perceive them with her normal senses. Correspondence 1 is both one of the levels of sphere knowledge and one of the effects.                                                     The Correspondence 1 version of Thunder Punch allows a mage to find the "perfect place to hit", and this does not need another sphere.
