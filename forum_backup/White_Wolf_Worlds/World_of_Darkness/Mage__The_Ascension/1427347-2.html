                Even if Avatars reincarnate, that doesn't mean that they're limited in number; and even if they are, it doesn't mean that that number is less than the total population of humanity. Depending on where Avatars come from and/or where they go between incarnations, there could be a vast, possibly even infinite, pool of Avatars that have never incarnated before.

                And those never before incarnated Avatars are potentially different from brand new Avatars, which are described in the Child Mages section of Book of Secrets: those brand new Avatars are described as having a childlike innocence that manifests, among other things, as True Faith in the Child Mage. By contrast, an Avatar that is old but has never incarnated even once may have lost their innocence in some other way; no one knows what happens to Avatars between (or before) incarnations, and it may well be that guiding and empowering mages isn't the only — or even the most important — thing they do. We simply don't know enough one way or the other.

                The reason why Avatars incarnate may be to try for Ascension — though what Ascension means is also an open question. Is Ascension a way for an Avatar to escape the cycle of reincarnation, or is it a way to transform a mortal human into an Avatar? Or is something else going on?

                Wwe simply don't know.

                                                The book suggests that, like innocence, it tends not to survive contact with the World of Darkness: in adults, True Faith is generally accompanied by a strong code of ethics designed to preserve it, and it's really ready to deviate from that code and to lose the True Faith. Basically, it's treated as something that's easily lost unless you're meticulously self-disciplined and very careful to avoid losing it.

                                                Children are not known for being meticulously self-disciplined or very careful.

                                                As such, the True Faith of a child with a new soul tends to be a very fleeting thing, and will usually be gone before you know it. If protagonist characters weren't an exception to statistical truths, the typical “new soul” character would be one where the True Faith is part of her backstory, along with an event describing how she lost it. Statistically, it's not something you have; is something you had.
