    Originally posted by Nyremne View Post
    And every people with infinite possibilities to defend themselves. In a Ascended world, everyone is a godling, the fallen hide because they know they would be outmatched by everyone if they reveal their nature. Now imagine the same thing with billions of mages agaisnt the Nephandi.
    That's essentially the same as a gun rights advocate's argument: if everyone has guns, then potential victims can defend themselves from predators. Your argument here assumes either that the vast majority of people are inherently good, or that Awakening makes them so; I see no reason to think that that's the case. Power corrupts, after all. Or, as M20 puts it:
    The biggest problem with men and gods is that too many men consider themselves gods and too many gods share the vanities of men. This is especially true of the Awakened — men and women blessed with godlike power and cursed with human frailties.

    M20 p.38
    I'll get to what Ascension describes shortly; but the preview is that the Nephandi aren't the only ones you need to watch out for. Indeed, if regular human beings weren't so damned corruptible, the Nephandi wouldn't be capable of even a fraction of the damage they can do. And they're hardly the only agents of corruption out there: the more compelling version of the Technocracy-as-antagonists that I've seen is summed up as the road to hell being paved by good intentions: the horrific practices that they engage in are largely of their own doing, motivated by the noblest of goals; but the practices they engage in are nonetheless horrific. And for the most part, the Nephandi had nothing to do with that.

    The Traditions are also well-intentioned. But the whole point I've been making here is that good intentions aren't enough: if you're not very careful and very wise, unintended consequences will crop up and subvert your plans for an ideal future. And the more certain you are of your rightness, the more blind you will be to those consequences.

    Originally posted by Nyremne View Post
    Plus, a gun is a tool designed for the very goal of killing. Magic is the expression of the human potential.
    There's an entire Tradition that's dedicated to trying to prevent some people from expressing their human potential. They're called the Euthanatoi.

    Originally posted by Nyremne View Post
    There's only one in book reference to what could happen in a mass awakened world, in one of the scenario of Ascension, and it's not as pessimistic as you make it sound to be.

    "The Mad Ones are expelled from the cosmos; each Quiet birth a universe that lives and dies according to it's merits.
    The worshipful Fallen are consigned to the Hells of their dreams: what they always truly desired. Judgment annihilates the last barriers; humanity claims the right to reward and punish itself. Technocracy triumphs in all its forms. Gray Men exult in submission to the Control of their own will; transhumanists find the key to Singularity consciousness. Every technology is immanent.
    Others find their Unity, their Akasha, their True Ether, their uncorrupted tongue of Enoch, all integral links in the chain of reality. That Entelechy conquers the last barriers of magic and Paradox. Joyously, they move back through Time and create themselves. Thothmes, Bhât, the Himalayan War, MECHA, Concordia — all are part of the thread that is cast back through the ages to pull history again, to the moment of Ascension. There is always the One, but diversity — the Cycle and the Other — is never excluded. There are uncountable paradigms, but they don’t chain belief or power. In the end, they are games."
    It's a beautiful vision. Deeply flawed; but beautiful. And to the extent that it works, it's not merely “Mass Ascension”; it's “Mass Ascension plus Deus ex Machina”: that is, Telos, the Tenth Sphere of Judgment. This story element is introduced for the very first time in the scenario in question and literally nowhere else, and is almost literally a god coming down from in high and setting things right by ensuring that the wicked get punished and the just get rewarded. Without the intervention of Telos, Mass Ascension is a far less rosy prospect.

    Looking over the full scenario a bit more closely: what causes the Marauders to be ejected into their own private Idahos? What consigns the Fallen to Hells of their own making? A few paragraphs before the ones you quote, it's mentioned that most of the newly-Awakened remember their humble origins and remain humble. That's not typical human nature; so what's making them behave that way? The biggest single flaw of the Technocrats is that they're nosy moral busybodies who think they know how to run your life better than you do; and none of the events in the scenario up to this point are of a nature that would dissuade them from this view. So why do they suddenly switch to a live and let live philosophy? It's certainly not that they've suddenly Awakened; they've been Awakened this whole time, and that's been a big part of the problem. So why did not one of these things that could have derailed the happy ending go wrong? Arguably, Telos.

    …though at this point, I realize that I've been getting one thing wrong: it's not Mass Awakening we're talking about here; it's Mass Ascension. Since we don't know anything about the Oracles, we really don't know what happens when someone Ascends, or even what's involved in Ascension: all that is known is that those who are rumored to have Ascended have never been heard from again; and the common wisdom appears to be that the path from Awakening to Ascension is a long, arduous one fraught with peril — something that must be earned through a lifetime of effort, and not something that can simply be arbitrarily bestowed upon everyone. The goal of Mass Ascension strikes me as the ultimate in hubris when you consider that no one knows of a reliable way to even Awaken someone, let alone shepherd them through to Ascension. Heck, a case could be made that the reason why the Traditions generally don't focus their attention on helping the Masses is because they've got their hands full with their personal efforts to achieve their own Ascensions. The Judgment scenario that you referred to isn't even a Mass Ascension scenario in the strictest sense; and the only reason why it isn't a complete and utter boondoggle is because Telos is there to guide everyone to their proper destinies.


    And bringing it back to my original point, the Technocracy has no reason to believe that anything like Telos exists; so their vision of Mass Ascension isn't going to be the idealistic one described above. Their view of Mass Ascension is informed more by the idiotic buffoons who can't even handle the comparatively limited power that the Union has already provided them without screwing everything up. Working toward Mass Ascension under these circumstances is akin to giving the keys to the nuclear arsenal to the kids who have been beating each other over the heads with the baseball bats that you already gave them.

    And honestly, I doubt that most of the Traditions buy into the notion of Telos, either. There are several Paradigms, some quite popular, that are antithetical to there being an active principle of Judgment that hands or rewards and punishments to people as they deserve them. A World of Gods and Monsters, Everything is Chaos, Everything is an Illusion, Prison, or Mistake, Might is Right, and One-Way Trip to Oblivion (a favorite of the Hollow Ones) are Paradigms that don't allow for the existence of some impartial Force of Justice. So even among the Traditions, there's a tendency to not want to Awaken everybody; not without first preparing them to be responsible with the power that comes with Awakening.

    Because a Mass Awakening doesn't just unlock someone's potential to be the best they can be; it also unlocks their potential to be the worst.

                    Uh. Just to bring it up. The choristers are a lot more than just about the Christian religion and as written are about a group of people of faith trying to (and being unable to) make an ideal religion everyone can benefit from.

                    My point is that most traditions think and believe their version of a better world is better for everone.

                    The chorus thinks more prayers answered and more miracles guided by a benevolent "One" and if those who are "good" get more answered prayers then more people will become faithful and "good", and making everyone good and full of faith would be the next step.

                    The euthanatos thinks that if they keep people from committing atrocities on others and from undue suffering while stuck alive the overall suffering of the world will decrease creating a world where it is easier for people to develop into better people.

                    The akashics want a world of balance and harmony where people are free to slowly or quickly gain enlightenment and greater access to the akashic wheel, developing yourself over lifetimes.

                    The dreamspeakers think that closer toes to their gods and ancestors is great.

                    The VR adepts think a more connected world where data is free and digital reality can improve a person's quality of life is great

                    The etherites think adventurous science without too many restrictions will result in a better world for all.

                    Etc.

                    Obviously this won't work out as well as they might imagine in The world of Darkness or even a world closer to our own but...

                    All I'm saying is that most tradition Mages are interested in making the world better for the common man. A whole bunch of them don't care, but I think the majority do in their own somewhat misguided ways.
