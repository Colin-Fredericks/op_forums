            Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
            I would combine these into one broader effect of "Sense Mood." I don't think it's justified to go farther to the extent of specific emotions (I don't think Life 1 can sense the differences between happy tears and sad tears for example, both operate on the same systems, though it can tell the difference between tearing up because of allergies vs emotions).

            Reading Muscles is kinda insufficient as a stand-alone effect. It takes a tiny fraction of a second for an arm to interpret the instruction of "punch" and execute it (the entire process of the thought of throwing a punch to impact can take as little as 1/10th of a second for a trained fighter). Given the constant background noise of non-aggressive impulses in the nervous system, this really isn't helping you more than just watching someone and seeing their arm start to move into a punch. Maybe a niche situation of seeing through things like using a cloak or shield to hide what strike is coming.

            What's more useful is reading the subconscious signals that humans are broadly less sensitive towards than most other animals. "Reading muscles" to read attacks is pretty fallible just like polygraphs are (and they've been rather thoroughly knocked off their pedestal in pop culture). Being able to sense all of the subtle cues caused by things like agitation, relaxation, and so on are more useful because it helps filter specific actions accurately. Is someone coming towards you to give you a big hug or to tackle you? Knowing they're aggressive vs. happy is a good way to know how to respond before it happens.
            Good call about reading muscles and really any attempt to read future action. Regardless of where a mage reads it, his reaction will start significantly after the read action is already in progress.

            I am going to keep the Human Polygraph effect, though, because it is such a specific application of the exact abilities of Life 1. The only drawback being that, like many people, I have very little respect for actual polygraph tests. I will add a separate Sense Moods section, though, as you and Lord Revan have both pointed out the wider use. I also changed the name of Sense Unlife to Sense Nonlife.

                    Life 2

                    •• Alter Simple Life-Forms/ Heal Self
                    Simple organisms – viruses, mollusks, insects, plants, etc. – become clay in the mage’s hands. He learns to adjust their Patterns (giving a crab wings, for example), and heal or kill them. Although he can’t yet transmute them into other states of being, he can cause flowers to bloom or wither, help trees bear fruit, and so on. Focusing on his own Pattern, he can also heal himself or perform small alterations (hair color, skin tone, height, weight, and so on) to his basic form.
                    There are multiple significant abilities mentioned here and I am going to split them apart even if they are usually considered part of the same ability.

                    One important consideration is the difference between simple and complex lifeforms. We are given examples but no clear dividing line. Some classic possibilities include warm blooded vs cold blooded, chordates (animals with spinal cords) vs everything else, and live birth vs eggs. This is really a table decision.


                    Adjust Simple Life Forms (Alter Simple Life Forms)

                    The start of the simple life progression track. The mage can adjust patterns to some degree. The exact limits of that degree are unclear. Pushing a creature to do something it can do naturally is in (bloom, grow fruit, etc). Full transmutation is out, though dramatic changes like the addition of wings (limbs) are possible. The dividing line is unclear.
                    It is also unclear if this level allows for changes dramatic enough to cause pattern bleed. Most of the examples would not be as they don’t alter the target outside of its normal form and function. The winged crab might be. How the ST draws that line will have an effect on the utility of this power.


                    Them!

                    By dramatically enhancing the size of ants, a mage can reproduce the giant ants from the movie Them! How difficult this is depends on how far into the weeds we want to get; stories don’t usually get into the adjustments that need to be made to make gargantuan creatures physically work. We can reasonably ignore that and just size up.
                    If the table does want to get into specifics: Two big factors are the square cube law and the way insects breathe. The first means that volume (and therefore weight) increases much faster than cross sectional area (and therefore strength of its muscles and equivalents). The second means insects don’t actually have a breathing system that works at those sizes, so the mage will need to enhance that system or give the ants lungs. Those changes could reasonably be done at Life 2, though they may take more successes.


                    Heal or Harm Simple Life (Alter Simple Life)

                    The start of the healing progression track. Here it lines up with the alteration of simple life, but it will rapidly diverge so it gets its own entry. At this level a mage can heal simple life forms or directly attack them with Life magic. At least, I presume that's what kill them means in this context.

                    Side question: Do were-spiders count as Simple Life (+Spirit) in any of their forms? If so they are sometimes Life 3/Spirit 2 to attack and sometimes Life 2/Spirit 2.


                    Muscle Control

                    Attacks can include more than just physical damage. At the level that a mage can damage a living pattern he can also seize control of its various systems to change behavior or action. In particular he can usurp control of its musculature and move it around against its will. This is less like mind control and more like puppeteering.


                    Heal Self (Heal Self)

                    A mage is a complex life form, but healing doesn’t have to wait until Alter Complex Life, especially not personal healing. A mage can heal himself at Life 2. Presumably this means he can directly attack his own pattern for Aggravated damage too, though that might not come up too frequently.


                    I’m My Own Bio-Weapons Lab (the gentler name could be Generate Antibodies)

                    On some sidebars it is stated to be difficult to target patterns inside of other patterns. Though bacteria and viruses are simple life, the ones mages are most interested in are often found inside complex life, which may require higher ranks (at least enough for healing). Not so here, a mage can heal himself and affect bacteria at the same rank of Life, so he should be able to isolate strains within himself and either alter them or make minor alterations to himself in response.


                    Minor Self Alterations (Heal self?)

                    The examples given are more minor than the ones given for altering simple creatures. The changes seem to be limited to those found within the mage’s species. Still that is quite powerful, and includes a lot of possibilities for both disguise and physical adaptation. Thicker skin, faster clotting, and limbs adapted for running, climbing, or swimming should all be possible here.


                    Universal Donor

                    A mage can quite literally grow more of himself to give to other people. He can produce more blood quickly, in whatever type is necessary. He can grow excess skin for skin grafts, as well as regrow organs he donates. He can even alter his organs to match the various markers needed for transplant.

                    One important consideration. Spells have a duration, so some of the modifications to prevent rejection could wear off. That said, STs and players often implicitly ignore that limit when it comes to things absorbed by a body. We don’t usually have the molecules from conjured food or air disappear from the body when the spell runs out, for instance. Whether this happens with transplants is a table call, just make sure the players know the ruling in advance.


                    From Elsewhere in M20

                    Share Senses (From Spirit 2)

                    Spirit 2 claims that Life 2 has the ability to share magical senses and abilities with other creatures. Seeing spirits and contacting the spirit world are the examples. There is no complexity distinction in the writeup and contextually the ability would mostly be used on humans, so the indication is that Life 2 can share senses with any creature. Since this is not an effect that alters the creature’s pattern it does make sense it wouldn’t follow the standard alteration progressions.

                                Life 3

                                ••• Transform Simple Life-Forms/Alter Self/ Heal Others
                                Advanced understanding allows the mage to radically alter simple organisms (turning a tree inside-out); transform one into another (changing fruit into insects); or – with Prime 2 – conjure them from raw energy.

                                Working with his own Pattern, that mage can work substantial alterations on himself, growing gills, claws, armor and so forth. He remains essentially human, but begins to master the definition of “human.” Meanwhile, he also gains the ability to heal damage to other complex organisms, put them to sleep, or inflict harm upon their living Pattern forms.
                                Life continues its trend of being fairly direct about what it can do. Meanwhile, I continue my descent into disturbing, Cronenbergian examples.

                                Once again, I’m going to split powers that really should be considered the same thing.


                                Transmogrify Simple Life (Transform Simple Life)

                                Transform Simple Life (Transform Simple Life)

                                The continuation of the Simple Life track. These are essentially two different presentations of the same idea and the only reason I’m separating them is to make sure neither gets lost. A mage can transform one life pattern into another (transformation), like changing a bug into a tree, and can also grossly reshape a pattern (transmogrification), like the aforementioned turning a tree inside out.

                                Any change great enough that it can only be done at this level should be large enough to cause Pattern Bleed.


                                Pulling a Pokeball out of a Squirtle

                                A mage can shape hidden compartments in living things as ways of concealing objects. Large enough creatures could even hold entire rooms. If you set your expectations correctly, a vacation house in Monstro the Whale an oversized whale shark could be quite lovely.


                                Living Mech: Battletoad

                                With this degree of control over simple creatures, a mage could grow and transmogrify a creature into a living, pilotable biomachine with a cockpit and organic control system. Actual controls will be rather direct and jerky as they use the direct muscle control from earlier, unless Mind is involved or the mage spends a lot of time practicing driving the creature. Possibly instincts could be instilled to help, but that depends on if instincts are Life or Mind or both.

                                The nautical version of this would be Submersible Fortress: Megalodon.

                                The names assume frogs and sharks are simple life. Substitute beetles and squids if that is not the case in your game.


                                Unrestrained Growth

                                A mage can induce absurd levels of growth in a crab, causing rapid, semi-formed, cancerous growth like something from a John Carpenter Film. This growth might continue until it overruns and envelops an area. The subject doesn’t have to be a crab, it just sets up a hidden double meaning for ‘cancer’ if it is.

                                Heal or Harm Complex Organisms (Heal Others)

                                The continuation of the healing track. At this point a mage can heal anything and anyone. There are no limits anymore. He can also harm anyone, which is why Life 3 is so often associated with Life attacks. That said, attacks aren’t limited to damage and most effects that inflict some kind of normal debuff are possible here. Sleep is given as an example, but confusion, ennervation, lack of balance, etc should be possible.


                                I’ve Got Your Strings, To Hold You Down

                                Rather horrifyingly, at Life 3 a mage can puppet just about any living creature. This is exactly the same as the Life 2 version but it works on anything living.

                                Alter Self (Alter Self)

                                The continuation of the alter self track. The mage is no longer limited to features found within humanity and can now add small changes from other things. The intention appears to be that if the change can be described as ‘human with bonuses’ it is within the realm of Life 3, whereas if it becomes something else it requires greater ranks. It’s a good limit, though not quite as clear as it seems. Is a mermaid a human plus a fish tail, or is it something else? A human with hooves and a human with a bull head are both human-plus, but what about a minotaur? That’s going to be a call for the ST.

                                Also a call for the ST: are gills radical enough to result in Pattern Bleed? It’s unclear.


                                I’m All Ears

                                Features don’t have to be in quite the same place as they normally are. A mage can grow extra eyes and ears in strange places to improve his senses. He can also grow different kinds of ears and eyes to see and hear different things.
