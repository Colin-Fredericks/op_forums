    If you want the 1e books, The Traditions Gathered are the volumes collecting various traditions. Traditions Gathered 3 would get you the Chorus and Euthantos and Akashic Brotherhood. Traditions Gathered 2 has the Cult of Ecstasy, Verbana and Dreamspeakers. Traditions Gathered 1 has the Order of Hermes, Sons of Ether and Virtual Adepts.

    Also, given how M20 works on paradigm, an Orphan is a much more viable option. You have a specialty sphere of your choice and could make the practices similar to a preferred Tradition but have a character who is not a joiner.

                                    Here is the backstory for my second PC idea.

                                    Big Tom Miller always thought of himself as life's punching bag. Born to drug addicts, Tom and his older brother Rice, were often neglected by their parents, who were too busy trying to get their next high to be worried about their sons. When Tom was three, he and Rice were taken away and put in separate foster homes. Tom unfortunately, had even more bad luck when every single foster family he was placed in turned out to be worse then his original parent's. By the time Tom was 16, he turned to wrestling for his school's team in order to vent his frustrations. He proved natural at this and even managed to please his latest foster father, who, though not abusive, wanted his foster son to be tough.

                                    Tom became so embroiled with his physical accomplishments that he neglected his academic career. He eventually dropped out of school and was immediatley kicked out by his foster father, who had recently adopted him, saying he did not want a deadbeat dropout living with him. Tom, who immediatley realized his grave mistake, turned to crime in order to support himself and found, that he was a natural leg-breaker. Tom then spent the next 12 years in his life working for various criminals as hired muscle, occasionally finding himself in and out of prison a lot. When he was 30, Tom was nearly killed after a drug deal went bad, but somehow miraculously survived a gunshot wound to the chest.

                                    Tom, who was tossed into jail yet again, realized that if he kept up his criminal behavior, he would be dead sooner or later. During his latest jail sentence, he was deep in a excistential crisis about his life so far and how pointless it all seemed. Recently however, he seemed to get a new fixation on life and after gaining parole, immediatley disappeared from the public eye, causing his old criminal accomplices and his parole officer to wonder what the " Big Lug", as they affectionatly called him, was up too.

                                    My idea for this character is that he either met someone in Prison who introduced him to the concept of magic or he decided to try out the mystical arts as a way to give his life meaning again. What do you guys think of this PC? Also, i will give a list of possible traditions that he would join up with and tell why through the words of Tom himself.

                                                    That could be a actual thing i can use if i go Technocrat. Also, here is his reasons for joining either The Akashics or The Dreamspeakers.

                                                    With the Akashics, Tom shared a cell with a Asian-American who happened to be a nearly Awakened student of the Akashics. Tom eventually grew curious about why his cell mate was doing stuff like meditation and practically fasting every week. Of course, Tom got curious and asked his cell-mate, whom he was friends with, why he was doing it. Then The Cell-Mate went into a ramble about Inner-Peace, Martial Arts and concepts Tom could not comprehend. After having it explanied in simple terms, Tom actually grew interested in what his cell-mate was talking about and asked him where he could learn it too. That either leads him to skip out on parole and head to a monastery in Asia or find a Akashic master in America.

                                                    The Dreamspeakers have a some-what similar reason. In this one, Tom grew extremely sick and had to go to the infirmary. Then, a Dreamspeaker posing as a Shamanic healer arrived a few weeks later to heal a Native American inmate. Tom, being desperate, asked the Shaman to heal him too and, to his surprise, he agreed. A day after the ritual, Tom felt better and was convinced that it was the Shaman who healed him. During the rest of his time in prison, he researched everything he could about Shamanism and after getting parole, set out to find the Healer to ask him to teach him his ways.
