    Originally posted by haldir2012 View Post
    I'm unclear about Systems. Some of your examples imply that Systems is about higher orders of complexity - e.g., building a car would require Materials and Systems, because Materials on its own can transmute the steel struts and gasoline but can't handle an internal combustion engine. Other examples don't - e.g., you propose that Mind (or combined Data/Mind) is sufficient for all orders of complexity on mental systems.

    If you want the former case to take prominence, then Systems becomes too important. Other fields are fine for low-level stuff - e.g., Mind 2 to push an emotion into someone's mind - but Systems is required for all the 4-dot or 5-dot type stuff that's more complex, to a point that other fields become something like a vidare for Spirit - the &quot;flavor&quot; or &quot;direction&quot; of the Systems field.

    If you want the latter case, then it's hard to see a place for Systems at all. It feels like the &quot;weakness/breaking things&quot; part of Entropy except you can strengthen/repair things too. And like Entropy, it steps on everyone else's toes. Also, if you return the &quot;complexity&quot; to the other fields, they can become too powerful; Materials would then basically be both Life and Matter for the price of one.
    My main thought here was to take the phenomena normally covered by Life and Matter and to shift the dividing line from “living or not” to “structure or composition”. My main concern here is that this potentially messes with the Progenitors, whose views tend to focus heavily on, well, life.

    My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                Perhaps. I am thinking that some of these Fields (particularly the more broad ones) might require you to choose a mandatory specialty, a bit like the way some versions of the Storyteller/ing System requires you to focus the scope of the likes of Science and Academics. I think that might make Systems a bit more palatable, by making it less like an über-Sphere. The Progenitors could then focus on Biological Systems and Biological Materials, while Iteration X might have members who deal specifically with Cybernetic Systems (which would only apply to Patterns that mix biological and mechanical components), etc.

                                In this view, I might fold all of the Data stuff into Mind, and require a specialty in terms of what sort of “mind” you're dealing with. The default would be human minds; but Iteration X could instead specialize in digital “minds” (i.e., “Data”), Progenitors might sometimes specialize in animal “minds” (a specialty not geared toward dealing with “higher cognitive functions”, but quite capable of messing with emotions, instincts, and the like), and some Void Engineers might specialize in Exopsychology (i.e., getting into the heads of Umbral Entities).

                                The idea is that nobody would be able to do it all, at least not without making a concerted effort to develop multiple specialties, and that some “Fields” don't represent single areas of understanding so much as they represent sets of similar Fields — similar in the sense of “they can do similar things”, distinct in the sense of “they affect different kinds of things”.

                                Subfields would be like these mandatory specialties, but optional: if you want someone who concentrates on learning all about light to the exclusion of heat, electricity, radiation, etc., you could have him learn a subfield of Energetics that specializes in Light. It would be easier to learn, but more limited in what it could do.

                                Thoughts?

                                        “Partitioning the scope of the Field” is a pretty good way of putting it. From my example, some of the possible partitions might include:

                                        • Mind: human, animal, digital, alien
                                        • Systems: biological, mechanical, cybernetic
                                        • Materials: organic, inorganic

                                        Remember, this is a work in progress; at the moment, I'm still developing my thoughts on this partitioning mechanism. Some thoughts, though:

                                        1. Mandatory partitions wouldn't be cheaper than whole Fields — at least, the first one wouldn't be; whether additional ones would get a price break is an open question. By contrast, optional partitions (Subfields) would definitely be cheaper than whole Fields; but I'm not sure how much cheaper.

                                        2. There have been complaints that my naming convention is too bland. I responded with a line about Huffman Encoding: common-use things get concise names; more specific, less common things get more elaborate names. More importantly, more elaborate names are there for a reason, not merely fancy for the sake of being fancy. Adding an adjective when you apply a partition is an application of this principle, as it gives you a standardized way to refer to the Fields and their partitions. Assigning a concise name to a particular partition of a Field that sees a lot of use (e.g., referring to “Digital Mind” as “Data”) is another example of this principle in action.

                                        3. There have been complaints that Systems is too broad, and too close to being a “do anything” Sphere. Partitions are an attempt to address that: you should never be able to take dots in just “Systems”; you should be required to specify what type of Systems you're studying. My initial inclination is that each mandatory partition would get its own dot rating; but again, that's something that's still under development.

                                        4. So why go with “Fields with required partitions” instead of sets of Fields? For one thing, it cuts down on redundant Effects lists. Systems generalizes the Life Sphere, providing Effects akin to healing/harming/transformative magick in a way that can apply to things other than living organisms. You can recover Life from this by applying a Biological partition to Systems; but you can also establish what would amount to a Machine Sphere by applying a Mechanical partition to it. In both cases, you have the same list of Effects to work off of; the only difference is that one of the partitions deals with organisms while the other deals with mechanisms.

                                        5. Another reason to go with partitioned Fields is that the list of partitions is potentially open-ended. Do you want to try a Weather Systems partition? I can't guarantee that it would work; it's well beyond the scope that Systems was originally conceived to address. But give it a try. There's something to be said for encouraging creativity.

                                                It's tempting. My main concern is that Space deviates from this a bit: first, it also incorporates motion and gravity, as part of making it the Field of travel.                                                 Now, I did say that various partitions of a Field do similar things, allowing for some differences based on the subject matter. Heck, Data also partakes of that thread, with the Digital Web being at the heart of its concept of “remote”.

                                                There's something to be said for that sort of “code reuse”; but I'm not sure that partitioned Fields are the best way to handle it.
