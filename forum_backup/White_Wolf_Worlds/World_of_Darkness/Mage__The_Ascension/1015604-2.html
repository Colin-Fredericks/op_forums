                                    Ok, so conceptually you're not going to be swayed on the whole "Corr 3 teleportation is inherently vulgar" side of things.
                                    Let's look at it from a gameplay perspective then. One of the reasons you'd want Corr 3 teleportation to always be vulgar is to discourage your players from abusing it. If all it took was a crowd of people for it to be coincidental, then it would be really easy for the mage to go anywhere in the world, so long as there's a crowd. Every city would now be within reach. You'd be able to travel from New York to Tokyo in the blink of a traffic light without having to worry about accumulating paradox.

                                    What would this do to a game? Well, it would serve as a quick "get out of trouble" scheme, removing all tension in any city based chase. Oh no, a group of Technocratic Agents are after me, that's ok, I'll just run into the crowd and end up in Nairobi, no problem here!
                                    If it was vulgar, then it would be more a question of "Do I take the quick and dirty way out and risk the backlash, or do I find a more reality friendly approach?"

                                    It would also cheapen travel significantly. We need to assist in a pan-european smuggling operation. That's not a problem, I can go from London, to Rome, then to Athens, Luxembourg, Warsaw, Vilnius, and Valletta, and get back in time for tea tomorrow without a single concern for paradox. While I'm at it, I could stop by Beijing for dumplings. Any particular tea you want while I'm there?
                                    Why would you ever take mundane transport if Corr 3 between major cities was coincidental? Heck, even teleportation within cities has become inconsequential . Need to get to the other side of town? Find a crowd in the nearby mall, appear in the convention hall, and viola, nothing matters to you.


                                    Then, if we start to include other methods of "vanishing into the crowd" such as turning into air, or heck turning into a bird, then crowds become a weird kind of Node like place to do magic. "Uh oh, he's getting away! Quick, let's find a crowd to transform in!"
                                    It sort of flies in the face of the entire point of subtle magic, which is sleepers as an obstacle to be navigated around. Yes Reality Zones and gathering acolytes can turn otherwise vulgar effects into coincidental ones. But that's the sort of thing that needs to be earned, either through a lot of research into the local superstitions, or though careful grooming. Just having any crowd serve as "This is the place "vanishing" magic is coincidental" ad hoc subverts this quite drastically.


                                    Listing all this stuff does create an interesting number of gameplay scenarios. Perhaps a mage can use crowds and other psychological blindspots as focus of sorts for magic, under the idea that reality is only static for as long as someone is actively observing it. But it should still be vulgar, if not for the simple reason that paradox is a necessary component of this game, required to prevent magic trivialising everything.
