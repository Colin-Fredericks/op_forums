                                    Originally posted by Saikou View Post

                                    Prime 1 binds it to your life pattern though, the idea with Prime 2 is to incorporate the objects in to your body of light such that they can have utility beyond being decorative.
                                    Originally posted by Saikou View Post
                                    When you astral project, you leave your pattern behind. That is the difference between the two methods. As such consecration does not change what you can and can’t take with you when engaging in astral projection
                                    I'll say up front, that is a well thought out line of reasoning, but I can't accept it. Astral projection doesn't use a Body of Light, that's an Astral Sojourn thing. Your argument is a better one for why mages have no way of taking things with them on an Astral Projection than for Consecration not working. Indeed, if that was M20s position I'd be in complete agreement with you.

                                    Since M20 says that it is possible to take things on Astral Projections, though, we have to look into how that is intended to work and what it actually takes.

                                    I already said part of why I am giving this section the rating I am. When M20 states sphere rank requirements for an effect it is important to check what is happening against the stated abilities of the spheres themselves. Sometimes we have to try and intuit what the effect is, but here we are fortunate to have a direct explanation of the logic: "Certain objects can follow the astral form if they have been metaphysically bound to the mage by a ritual and a Prime 2 Effect." The key to bringing things over is a Prime effect that metaphysically binds the object to the mage.


                                    So now I go over to the Prime section and check if Prime can do that, and I find that there is indeed such an effect. Consecration, at Prime 1: "When infusing her Quintessence into an object, that mage may also consecrate the object with her personal energy. When she shapeshifts, steps sideways, or otherwise alters her Pattern’s metaphysical nature, that consecrated object will then change with her. In the process, it also picks up her personal Resonance… which, because it both identifies her and becomes essentially connected to her, is not always a good thing" The wording isn't precisely the same but it is very close. There's also nothing in Prime 2 that sounds anything like a metaphysical binding.

                                    At this point, it is fairly straightforward to me that Consecration accomplishes the intended effect and the Otherworld Travel section is wrong about the rank (and probably the # of successes too).

                                    But let me take this opportunity to show why I take this approach, because the Otherworlds Travel section makes a good example. It is not a very reliable section. As I've said elsewhere, it is quite inconsistent with sphere ranks, often tacking on extra with no explanation.

                                    More importantly, it really appears Consecration was completely forgotten when this section was written as there's a special method for bringing objects along for Astral Projection, another special method for bringing items on the Agama Sojourn, there's no method at all listed for the Astral Sojourn, and regular stepping sideways automatically brings most things along without any special method. That last one is especially notable because stepping sideways with objects is one of the main purposes of Consecration. We could perhaps argue that leaving Consecration out was intentional for the other three, but not for stepping sideways. Here it is clearly an error.

                                    That is why I take the approach I do in general and that is why I am saying that pg 477 is wrong in this specific case. Not only is it clear that Prime 1 can accomplish the stated effect, there are major issues with the entire section's approach to the spheres.


                                    Originally posted by Saikou View Post
                                    (and Prime 2 needs Life to consecrate living things as of M20)

Requiring Life to cast a spell on a living thing when you do not need Matter to cast it on an object is bad design and I will always consider it wrong unless there is a convincing and well stated reason otherwise.

                                                    Right, here we go...

                                                    Correspondence from HDYDT

                                                    22 – Corr 4/Life 4/Mind 2 to turn into multiple creatures. HDYDT uses different notation for the Correspondence rank restriction. While M20 will give the correct level of Correspondence used, HDYDT gives the rank a mage has to have to use it. M20 would call this Life 4/Corr 2/Mind 1 though a mage would still need Corr 4 to cast it. I’m only going to count this difference as a mistake once. This also requires Mind 1, not 2.

                                                    25 – Wow this one is hard to muddle through. Corr 5/Mind 5/Life 3 is given to control your own clones. I should specify that this effect presumes the clones were already created with Life5/Mind5/Prime 2, so this is not a co-location effect. Nevertheless the section also specifically references the Everywhere At Once section, which is a co-location effect. As best I can tell Corr 5/Mind 5/Life 3 is a mix of the two effects that manages to accomplish neither one, and sets the ranks too high besides. The Colocation version doesn’t need pre-existing clones and should be Corr 4/Life 2/Mind 1 The controlling multiple clones version doesn’t need Mind 5, it needs Mind 3 for a mental link, so Mind 3/Corr 3 (HDYDT terminology)

                                                    Also, for some strange reason, HDYDT keeps saying that cloning does not provide muscle memory even when Life 5 and Mind 5 are involved. I can’t follow the reasoning.

                                                    32 – Corr 4/Forces 4 to summon sunlight by opening a gateway to somewhere where there is sunlight. There is no need to use Forces if you can open the gateway. This is Corr 4.

                                                    34 – Corr 1 to be able to see while invisible. This would work. It is also unnecessary. Invisibility is already a Forces 2 effect and Forces 2 can also amplify or duplicate light and sound, so it is easy enough to bend light or sound around yourself while letting a tiny amount through and amplifying it as it hits your eyes and ears.

                                                    34 – Corr 2/Matter 2 to shoot at one place and have the bullet come out somewhere else. Correspondence is explicit that whole patterns DO NOT need additional spheres to be teleported. This is Correspondence 2 alone if the mage is changing where the gun fires from and Correspondence 3 if he is changing the bullet's location or direction in mid flight. The best explanation I have of how Correspondence 2 object manipulation works is that it is extending the mage’s natural reach. He’s literally picking up the object, he’s just not limited to his arm length. Without other spheres he can’t reach out and pick up a bullet in flight. This either needs bullet catching spheres or it is a full teleportation effect.

                                                    39 – Making it rain on another continent is said to be Forces 5/Corr 5. Controlling weather is Forces 4, not 5, so this is Forces 4/Correspondence 4.

                                                    40 – Corr 3/Forces 3/Entropy 2 to make minor tweaks to the weather. There is so much wrong here.
                                                    • First: Entropy doesn’t need help in controlling probability
                                                    • Second: Forces doesn’t need help controlling Forces
                                                    • Third: Controlling weather is Forces 4, and I thought this had more to do with complexity than scale.
                                                    • Fourth: If Correspondence can bypass the area restrictions on Forces (and I like the idea), it can do so on Forces 2, so this would be (as HDYDT calculates Corr) Forces 2/Corr 2.

                                                    41 – Really analyzing Realm creation is beyond what I want to do right now. Corr 5 to do a major feat not covered in the normal Corr rules is at least a reasonable line of thought. It’s fine by M20 logic. I would, however, allow alternative spheres.

                                                    45 – A rare moment where HDYDT says something consistent with M20. Pulling Quintessence from a distant periapt is Corr 2/Prime 2.

                                                    54 – Corr 2 and Mind 2 to extend sensory magic to another person over a long distance. Technically true, but overengineered. It is Mind 2 to extend sensory magic to another person in the same room, but you don’t even need that to extend it to someone by touch. Since Corr explicitly allows a mage to touch someone from a distance, it alone is enough. Of course, extending it by touch doesn’t really make sense, so I’d rather it be Corr 2/Mind 2

                                                    54 – Mind 3/Corr 3 to extend that magic to an unwilling person. It is reasonable to say a Mind 3 attack is necessary to extend those perceptions in this case, touch or no. So, in HDYDT terminology this would take Mind 3/Corr 3 if the person is out of sensory range.

                                                    55 – Corr 2 added to a [Sphere] 1 perception effect allows perception at a distance.

                                                    62 – Battlefield sense. Correspondence 1 to see the big picture of the entire battlefield. I think this would be Corr 2, since Corr 1 doesn’t extend the range of your perceptions.

                                                    62 – Mind 3/Corr 1 allows the mage to see the area and command through a squad of allies. This should be Corr 2 for the sight at distance, and a mage needs to have Corr 3 to be able to use Mind 3 at a distance

                                                    62 – Correspondence is one of the spheres that can “read an opponent”

                                                    67 – Correspondence 1 or 2 to use Slipstream which adds +1 to the difficulty to hit the Mage per success. Correspondence 1 lets the mage dodge by micrometers. Correspondence 2 allows them to appear slightly elsewhere, which is a new ability for that rank. I can’t think of a reason to use a higher rank to do the same effect, but if a player wants to then I suppose it works.

                                                    68 – The Time Dilation section suggests that a Time Dilation effect can be “attached” to the target via Correspondence 2 or Life 3. Correspondence is not a locking sphere. If it were, it would let the mage lock the spell to a location and spells default to locations anyways. Given that M20 consistently uses 2 as the rank for locking, I think either the locking rules weren’t written when this section was or the rules were forgotten.

                                                    70 – A basic restatement of Correspondence teleportation capabilities at each rank. There’s nothing wrong with it, and nothing new, although the author wants you to know how very, very vulgar layering space on top of itself. Seriously, you need to know it is vulgar.

                                                    75 – Entropy 3/Correspondence 3 to clear a crowd via pattern manipulation. The Correspondence 3 allow Entropy 3 to be cast beyond sensory range. (HDYDT terminology).

                                                    76 – Correspondence + Mind to call a cab or Correspondence + Entropy to catch one at just the right time. Assuming the cabbie is out of range the former is correct. The latter one less so. Entropy doesn’t need the help as it is adjusting the odds of a cab showing up, not calling a specific one.

                                                    77 – Sometimes it seems like HDYDT can’t decide whether Corr 1 or 2 is correct so it suggests both. Here it is suggesting using either one to scope out potential teleportation destinations. This was probably meant as a placeholder and the wrong one was meant to be edited out. It wasn’t. Correspondence 1 can’t do this. Correspondence 2 is the correct option. This is, otherwise, a clever and useful effect.

                                                    77 – Correspondence 3 to blink- that is to make several quick teleports.

                                                    77 – Correspondence 3/Forces 2 to enhance the cover of smoke and darkness for a teleport, creating potentially coincidental short-range teleportation. HAP/HOP arguments aside, the spheres are the correct levels for what they are doing.

                                                    78 – Tether is an extremely badly explained effect. It initially sounds like tethering is a necessary step for teleporting things to the mage. It is actually a way of binding an object or creature to a mage so that it always counts as within sight distance. That is a good effect. That said, this section falls into the classic scope mistake and assigns spheres based on the complexity of the target: Matter 2 for inanimate objects, Life 4 for complex organisms, etc. There is no indication that tethering involves any kind of pattern manipulation, so complexity shouldn’t matter. This should either be a locked Correspondence effect (so Corr 2/Pattern 2) or it should be something that the sphere of connections can do on its own (so Corr 2). As a side note, this is quite a situational power. Having a piece of the thing you want to teleport also drops the range to 1 success. For instance, if you really want to be able to easily summon your cat, keeping a small amount of cat hair on you (which is almost impossible to avoid anyways) is a lot simpler than setting up a Life 4/Corr 4 spell.

                                                    79 – Colocation requires 1 success per extra location plus successes based on the most distant/foreign location. This is about as low a requirement as this spell could reasonably cost, and is in accordance with M20s Correspondence charts. It also says that Life 3 is required to independently act in each location, which counters M20s Life 2 requirement, although in this case I think HDYDT is closer to the mark.

                                                    79 – HDYDT practically quotes M20 verbatim for a bit regarding rank and successes. Spirit 4 additionally allows the gate to go to a realm (which works since Spirit 4 is the rank to bring other people into the Umbra), and Mind 5/Spirit 4 allows it to go to a personalized Astral Realm or Demense. I don’t see the purpose of Spirit 4 in that case.

                                                    80 – Warp Gate seems to be another description of teleportation. The only reason it even has a special name is to include it in the Gates and Portals section. I’d flag multiple parts of this section for editing, but as an effect it is correct.

                                                    80 – Correspondence 5 to shrink internal dimensions. Bog standard use of Spatial Mutation. This is the last time HDYDT will get anything right about Correspondence 5.

                                                    80 – The Bringing “There” to “Here” struggles to handle Correspondence 5’s spatial distortion effects and gives a base set of rules that do not take into account the size or subtlety of the effect. According to the list, almost all of these effects are Vulgar, inflict a minimum of 5 points of paradox (as opposed to the automatic 1 of a normal vulgar roll), are automatically Outlandish or Godlike (so 10 successes base), and also says permanency of these effects requires 10 successes (The default for gates rather than the 6+ of the chart). On the very next page HDYDT will start contradicting this.

                                                    81 – Bridging Similar Locations is about using Correspondence 5 to create Spatial Mutations that connect two locations. Functionally that is an identical effect to making a gate, which is normally Correspondence 4, and the description of how a mage does this with Correspondence 5 would be a perfectly adequate description of how they might do it with Correspondence 4. Also, this costs 5 successes plus familiarity requirements, which is both higher than it should be based on the magical effects chart (duration + familiarity requirements) and lower than the previous list indicated (10 successes minimum)

                                                    81 – Indefinite mutations gives both a vague description of the effect (both stretching a hallway for miles and compressing the distance between two locations are given) and a vague sense of how to calculate the required successes (mostly ST judgement, but compressing a 90 mile trip by an undefined amount is 6 successes while a trip halfway around the world would take 10 successes). No reason is given for why this effect gets more difficult with distance when the nearly identical Bridging Similar Locations does not. Many of the examples given don’t seem to pass through the intervening distance so there’s no reason that distance would even matter. Also, subtle and potentially coincidental examples of this spell are given, which is correct but contradicts the list on page 80.

                                                    I’ll add that an obvious use of this ability, hiding buildings and neighborhoods in existing terrain Harry Potter style, is not mentioned.

                                                    81 – Manipulating local physics in gross and vulgar ways (Inception, MC Escher paintings etc) is not specific enough. Three possibilities are given. For the first and second (Corr 5/Matter 4 for physics defying landscapes and Corr 5/Forces 4/Matter 4/Prime 2 for landscapes with impossible elements) I can come up with examples that should be Correspondence alone or the other spheres alone. For the last (Corr 5/Life 5 for Gigeresque landscapes) most of what I can come up with is Life 5 alone. Of course there will be examples where are the spheres given are needed, but this section is thoroughly unhelpful for determining that.

                                                    82 – The given systems for transposing locations are arbitrarily harsh and suspend many of the normal rules of Mage in order to make things harder:
                                                    • 10 successes per layer + 10 successes per quarter mile area beyond the first at base… for reasons
                                                    • Normal rules for duration are suspended. This effect costs an additional 1 success per 10 minute duration… for reasons
                                                    • Normal rules for modifiers are suspended. This effect cannot have its difficulty reduced by tools, quintessence, or anything else… for reasons
                                                    • Normal paradox rules are suspended. This spell’s paradox isn’t determined by the roll but by the duration. At least 5 points per 10 minutes of the effect… for reasons
                                                    • And oddly enough, familiarity with the locations in question doesn’t matter. There are no additional success requirements for unfamiliar locations.
                                                    Co-Location is supposed to be one of the signature abilities of Correspondence 5, but I haven’t seen this much animosity towards an effect or this many invented penalties since M20s writeup of the Vampire Lawn Chair.

                                                    82 – While Moving Mountains is not as actively hostile to the effect it describes, it has all the rest of HDYDT’s flagrant disregard for helpfulness and sense. This is, for all intents and purposes, a grand scale teleportation spell (Corr 4) except instead of moving everything in a location, this spell just moves the location. It’s essentially a roundabout way of using a higher sphere to do the same thing less effectively, because it has a higher difficulty, automatically incurs 5 paradox (why?) and costs extra if it needs to be permanent. If it’s not permanent, instead of everything popping back into place at the end, it crumbles into dust. To prevent that costs at least 10 successes and 10 quintessence, which is absurd.

                                                    It also has a cavalcade of improperly added spheres: Corr 5/Forces 5/Matter 5/Prime 4. Again, Correspondence doesn’t need help unless it is transporting part of a pattern. A mountain or building might require Matter 3 or 4 to separate it from its foundations, but otherwise Correspondence is fine on its own. Prime is an even more baffling addition.

                                                    This is a great marauder spell. I struggle to see why anyone else would bother.

                                                    At this point, I’d say things have gotten as bad as they can get, but we haven’t gotten into Warding. That’s coming after this next one.

                                                    90 – Correspondence 2 to pull a small living creature from somewhere else, Corr 4 for a bigger one. This time with no extraneous sphere charge. It is stated that Life 2 or Mind 2 can get them on your side, which is actually a rare case where HDYDT goes too low on the pattern spheres.

                                                    Now we are into the section I was dreading most for Correspondence… Warding. This section is badly organized and has almost endless ways of getting things wrong.

                                                    95 – Correspondence apparently can start warding against objectives at Correspondence 2 and is an application of Thickening Space. Keep in mind that M20 described thickening space in terms of blocking scrying and nothing else. This is a dramatically new interpretation of the ability and one so tenuous that even this paragraph has to put “thickens” in quotation marks. There is, however, one example in M20 which refers to a Corr 2 ward, so I do think this was always the intention.

                                                    95 – Despite this not being a ranged effect, HDYDT makes it very clear that warding still uses the standard rules relating Correspondence to other Spheres. I don’t see the logic. Neither does HDYDT because it will forget it said this.

                                                    95 – Wards can be locked onto things, but either they don’t use the normal locking rules or the normal locking rules were forgotten. Instead of the usual [Pattern Sphere] 2, they now use Matter 2+, Forces 3+, or Life 4. There’s an additional Prime 2 + 1 point of Quintessence requirement if the ward is meant to move around. Considering that moving the spell is one of the primary reasons to lock it, that’s a hefty tax. It’s also not an ability of Prime, nor is it a requirement of any other locking effect.

                                                    Time to imagine that soul destroying color that doesn’t exist again.

                                                    96 – Warding against Objectives, the Corr 2 version of Ward, now gets its writeup and it is a mess of harsh and inconsistent rulings. There is no rhyme or reason to how supporting spheres are chosen. Despite this somehow being an application of “thickening space,” many, but not all, of the permutations set the supporting spheres as if they, not Correspondence, are doing the heavy lifting. Some set them so high that they could accomplish the same effect without Correspondence. Some aren’t even wards. I don’t think any two spheres are calculated the same way.
                                                    • To block Matter the mage has to have Matter 2 for bricks, 3 for somewhat complicated materials, and 4 for really complex things like computers. This doesn’t correspond to the normal progression of Matter.
                                                    • To block Forces, the mage has to have Forces at the required level to transmute that force, that is Forces 3 for most forces, and 5 for the really potent stuff.
                                                    • Life, though, can block entire classes of creatures at 3, regardless of type.
                                                    • Forces 3 can make something airtight. Not Matter 2, which would actually make sense, Forces 3.
                                                    • A nuclear blast takes Matter 5 as well as Forces 5.
                                                    • Life 4/Mind 4 is required to block types of people… for reasons. Mind 4 can do this on its own.
                                                    • Mind 4 is required to block specific people… for reasons. Mind 4 can do this on its own.
                                                    • Spirit 4 can ban against spirits. I think it can do this on its own.
                                                    • Entropy blocks misfortune, which is an effect Entropy can do on its own.
                                                    • Time 5 can set up a ward where time flows differently on either side. Like Entropy, I think Time can do this on its own and at lower levels.
                                                    • Prime 4 might be able to ward against resonance. It’s an optional rule.
                                                    • Mind is the only sphere listed that affects how the ward expresses itself with the option to send bad vibes at Mind 2 and Life 3/Mind 3 to give a (presumably non bashing) psychic shock. Since in this case the spheres in question are doing something specific, it makes sense. I should point out, though, that Secure the Scene from M20 lets Mind do these things on its own.
                                                    • Of course after reading what this kind of Ward does with a Mind augment I now have the question of what it does by default. That’s not stated in this section. The wording of Minor Ward makes me think this is supposed to be the same thing, (-1 success on any rolls that involve the objective per success in the casting roll) but Minor Ward has different Sphere requirements and this doesn’t match with the success calculations of Warding against Objectives. The other possibility, suggested in the terminology of traveling wards, is that the substance is banned, which is super powerful and well beyond the ability of Correspondence 2
                                                    Also, the costs of this spell are insanely high.
                                                    • This kind of ward costs a success per level of duration (warding comes with its own chart) plus a success per “objective”. A permanent ward costs 10 successes plus a success per “objective.” Extending the ward out from the surface of the area warded costs an additional 3 successes per yard. Note that M20 has an example of this that costs much fewer successes for permanency.
                                                    • A temporary ward also costs a point of quintessence per objective and may also cost an additional point as a base cost (it is stated differently at different points).
                                                    • A permanent ward costs a base point of quintessence, plus a point per objective, plus a point per sphere involved including Correspondence I have no idea why this should be so expensive.
                                                    • Locking the ward requires an extra point of quintessence.
                                                    • A triggered ward uses Time 4 to activate when conditions are met, and costs a base 1 quintessence + 1 per objective, and takes only 1 success.


                                                    97 – Minor wards sound like they should be the same things, but they apparently use Prime 2, and just Life 2 to hinder living creatures or Spirit 2 to hinder spirits. For this, they subtract 1 success per success from the rolls of the barred thing. Prime isn’t doing anything and this costs a quintessence point plus a point per objective. Whether this is meant to be an alternate cost to Warding against Objective or that section was just cut and paste with no editing, I just don’t know.

                                                    98 – Minor Area Ward. Essentially the same effect as Minor Ward, but with a Mind 2 effect saying Get Out or Stay In. No quintessence cost is given, though I suspect that’s just because this is a variant of Minor Ward.

                                                    99 – Major Ward continues the trend. It is based on Correspondence 4’s Ward/Ban ability. That’s the extent of its reasonableness. Every Major ward costs the standard ward costs, plus a point of quintessence per sphere involved. Permanent wards cost two quintessence per sphere involved. I don’t even see the need for a single point of quintessence for warding so this cost is wildly out of place.


                                                    100 – The Ban variant of Major Ward costs Correspondence 4, scope spheres, and, for some reason Prime 2 or 4 to “invest energy into the ward.” There is no reason this spell should require that. Ban/Ward is one of the signature abilities of Correspondence 4, it should not require another sphere as a base requirement. Scope defining spheres to tailor specific applications are fine, a baseline requirement is not.

                                                    Of course, the suggested scope spheres are also too high, once again being set like they are manipulating the substances in question rather than setting the terms of a Correspondence effect. Life 3 or 4 to ban living things is not only too high, but also the wrong combination for manipulating, Spirit 4 and Mind 4 are so high they can accomplish much of the effect on their own.

                                                    This also costs a minimum of 5 successes even before covering scope or duration, which means HDYDT considers this to be at minimum a Mighty Feat.

                                                    However, banning an individual also requires having something close to that target, like a true name or an important item. This is completely arbitrary. At most it should take more successes to accomplish the effect without that. Also, if a mage has something important to the target, they have a Correspondence connection to them and do not need another sphere like Life to define the target.

                                                    Seriously, this reads like a list of requirements for specific scenarios that were accidentally merged together into the requirements for all wards.


                                                    100 – The Co-occupational ward uses Correspondence 5 to make the banned things pass through the protected things. No extra requirements are added beyond those of Ban, so despite this being hyper-inflated in terms of cost, it is probably still less costly than Co-location was.

                                                    100 – The luck ward uses Entropy 2+ and lets a mage make an Arete roll when an event could result in “good or bad luck” within an area. What this roll accomplishes is not stated in this section, and it is suggested that we should use the rules for Fate and Fortune 30 pages later. This can only happen one time for each quintessence spent on the ward. No reason is given for why this specific version of ward uses such different rules.

                                                    101 – The psychic ward is an expansion of the Objective Ward option to use Mind to mentally attack people within the ward.
                                                    • Mind 2 for suggestions of unease. No need for Correspondence since there’s no actual barrier to entry.
                                                    • Mind 3 for mental pain, which uses the casters Arete to make a “vulgar magic attack.” Since, by the wording, this does’t stop the offender from entering the area Mind 3 can do this alone, no need for Correspondence. Also, the section then takes the time to spell out what the difficulty would be if it were vulgar, then says that because it “remains invisible” it actually uses the targets willpower as a difficulty. I have no idea why it wasted the wordcount.
                                                    • Mind 3 or sensory stunning, Mind 4 for mind control, etc. No need for Correspondence, though.
                                                    • Mind 4 or Spirit 4 to just bar a person or set of people. Mind 4 can do this without Correspondence and Spirit 4 makes little sense.
                                                    • Mind 4 or Spirit 4 to bar people with a certain mood. Same as above.

                                                    102 – Paradigm wards ban people who follow forbidden paradigms and makes their magic Vulgar With Witnesses. This requires Correspondence 5/Entropy/Life 4/Mind 4/Prime 4, it also costs 3 quintessence and 3 successes per paradigm barred. For once, I will not complain about a ward having a quintessence cost, as quintessence reinforces reality. That said, I don’t know why it would be 3 points. There is also no reason to include Life and Entropy in this. There’s also an optional variant where intruders have their beliefs challenged by a Conceptual Entropy assault, which is normally not something that can be done without complex interaction and rapport.

                                                    103 – The Time Ward uses Correspondence 5/Time 5 to do something Time 3 or 4 can do on its own.

                                                    There’s more wrong with wards, but I’m getting so tangled up in it I have to stop.

                                                    105 – Correspondence 5/Prime 5/Spirit 5 to craft an extradimensional ephemeral realm to act as a spirit prison. I need to look deeper into realm creation for this, but Correspondence 4 was enough to trap things in a pocket realm and spirit binding is Spirit 4, so it’s hard to see why this has such a high requirement.

                                                    109 – Time 1/Corr 1 to see the right place at the right time, or Corr 3 to be at that place. This is actually good.

                                                    112 – Incorporating Corr 4 into an Anchor point allows a mage to return to it from a great distance. This is sort of a normally closed gateway, so I’m okay with this.

                                                    113 – When using Time Freeze a mage has to add Correspondence 4 to bind a location in stasis. Magic defaults to being on a location, so you don’t need Correspondence to set that at all, let alone Correspondence 4.

                                                    113 – On the other hand by incorporating Corr 4 a mage can create a metaphysical bubble outside space and time. I’m not sure what having the bubble be outside both space and time accomplishes, but this is the correct combo for that.

                                                    116 – Corr 2 to cast at a distance. Check.

                                                    116 – Corr 3/Mind 3 to connect multiple people across distance. Corr needs to be 3 because of Mind 3 (by HDYDT terminology), so check.

                                                    122 – Life 3/Corr 3 to shut down or overload nerves from a distance.

                                                            Ramnesis
                                                            Point 25 it does specify that mind 5 is to transfer your entire mind over to the other clone/clones.

                                                            point 34. If you want light to go around you, it goes around you. There is nothing written in any edition of mage that allows the selective push of light to your eyes and not anywhere else. So this feels more like a self made ruling clashing with what is written. Forces 2 is a blunt instrument. And generally speaking your effects can only do a few actions, get it to bend around you, or to amplify, or to diminish. These are all separate effects. In the same way you can't simultaneously damage one person and heal another with the same effect or create two gates with corr 4 to different locations with the same effect. This is why using alternate senses via first rank spheres is useful if you wish to no longer rely on light.

                                                            Even allowing for this rather generous interpretation of what Forces 2 is capable of doing with a single spell, if you allow light to to hit your eyes, this means all but your eyes will be invisible. Which to the outside observe will be seen as a big black bar floating in the air where the light going to your eyes does not reflect. Anyone you see will see this black bar.

                                                            The other point 34, regarding corr and matter. This is apportation, and it has always been the case across mage editions including 2e and revised that if you want to apportate a matter pattern, it is corr 2 matter 2. Correspondence on its own only affects the self typically until the 4th rank. Whether you agree with it or not, you have to admit it is being consistent with previous editions of mage. This is the nature of corr 2 apportation. If anything, the sections which imply that Correspondence alone can do these things is what is actually inconsistent with what is established. And I do believe that this is more an act of omission of the need for Matter or Life rather than confirmation that Matter or Life is unnecessary. Same with how HDYDT also says periapts need Prime 3 but neglects to mention Matter 5, which is spelt out as necessary both in BoS, and revised's FBDF. Omission is not confirmation.

                                                            point 39. Forces 4 is unlikely to cause rain unless you already have the conditions for rain immanent and you need only rush it along. Forces 5 enables one to convert wind momentum in the cold energy, which causes rain to occur regardless of what is going on. So I think it is not unfair to use Forces 5 for illustrative purposes in this case.

                                                            Point 40. This is again another transmutation effect, this is why Forces 3 is at minimum required. The idea is that you are transmuting the forces pattern within the weather, such as wind speed in to heat, cold in to stillness needed for cloud formation, etc. In theory you could try to do this with Forces 2, but it would be far more limited as you would have fewer things you could pull from. What Entropy 2 does is it allows you to specify a desired endpoint for what is a random outcome. This is always how adding Entropy to effects works as Entropy can specify where you want things more or less to end up, but not the specifics of how to get there, that's where other spheres come in. For example you could use Entropy 3 to specify a car crash happens, you could use Forces 3 to force a car to suddenly stop in the road. But using them together means you can force a car to stop in the middle of the road, and guarantee that because of this a car crash happens. It's the same idea with the weather stuff, you can fluff about the elements of climate with Forces, but with such a small grip on control there you are essentially hoping that this amounts to something tangible, what Entropy 2 does is it allows a bit of control over the random elements of climate to make it so the changes you introduce are more likely to chain reaction in to the kind of weather you are hoping to see.

                                                            Point 62 Corr 1 allows you to see around objects and perspectives. Think of it like being able to see all planes at once. Within your 360 view you can see not just the person in front of you, but what they are hiding behind their back as well. You can see the painting on the wall and what is behind the frame, you can sense not just the table but what is under the table. This aspect is what I believe the effect is referring to.

                                                            Point 68, Really it should be Life 4 to lock the effect to another person, all things being consistent. Though the idea of locking an effect via correspondence is interesting if we consider the Chain effect. Might be that you could get around the need for Life 4 to lock an effect by locking it into another pattern, and then using Chain to tie the connection to the target to the level of body sample. I imagine the main discouraging factor is that it requires a tonne more successes to pull off, which does make it less useful in a high stakes situation.

                                                            Just as a general note:
                                                            The way locking works is that you are transmuting the subject to incorporate the magic being cast. Matter 2 is to lock an effect to a simple homogenous matter pattern, Life 4 is for other humans. Life 3 would be for yourself, and Life 2 for simple life patterns such as plants and some insects.

                                                            So any time you see Life 4 as necessary to lock something in to a human's life pattern, that is actually being consistent.

                                                            Point 76 same point as before, Entropy 2 can be used to enhance the odds, though I might argue this is more of an Entropy 3 effect since this is a specific outcome rather than something based on random chance.


                                                            Point 96 to make one thing clear, I don't like the correspondence warding rules presented here either. But I there is one point to state here. Matter progression has always been like this, Matter 2 allows transmutation of homogenous items, Matter 3 for things with a bit more complexity to them, and Matter 4 for mechanisms. It's like how Matter 2 can turn a stone in to a nugget of glass, Matter 3 can turn that stone in to a wooden box, and Matter 4 can turn that stone into a clock.
