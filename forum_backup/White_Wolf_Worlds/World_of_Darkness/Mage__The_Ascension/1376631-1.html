        Fair enough. Though some things are easier to mess with than others; and “where powerful Avatars end up and whether or not they Awaken” strikes me as one of the harder things to change — especially if you want it to be a lasting change that carries over from generation to generation. That is, you're not just messing with the destiny of Avatars (which by definition are the things in the setting that are most likely to be able to say “no” to magical attempts to influence or control them); you're trying to establish an influence that will persist into the future — something that has what MRev called Historical Inertia. Otherwise, your family's magical birthright will die out just as soon as people stop believing in it; and considering how much belief is likely to be necessary to make such a thing possible, it wouldn't take much to cause it to die out.

        That said, MRev also had something in it about psychopomp spirits that exist to guide Avatars to their proper hosts; and Dark Ages suggested the possibility that the reason why their era of mages never heard voices in their heads was that the psychopomps were doing an exceptional job matching the two. I don't really buy the latter; but if the former is true, then I could see the psychopomps being influenced to send an inordinate number of powerful Avatars to a particular family. I still don't think it would help with the Awakenings of those Avatars (the psychopomps, even if they exist, have nothing to do with that); but it would increase the number of potent Avatars in the family.

        I strongly agree with the idea posted earlier in this thread that magic that runs in the family is going to be more like Sorcery than Awakened Magick.

        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                Originally posted by Penelope View Post
                                “Saladin’s army routs the Crusaders at the battle of Hattin in 1187, killing the last king of Jerusalem and basically ending the Merovingian bloodline.”
                                The last King of Jerusalem was Guy of Lusignan, and while he was captured at the battle of Hattin, Saladin spared his life, kept him prisoner for a year and then let him go. He eventually ended up as the King of Cyprus for a few years before his death in 1194, with his younger brother succeeding him. Guy is from the House of Lusignan, which originated in the Poitou area of western France in the 10th century. While they don't have any known connection to the Merovingian Dynasty (not that you couldn't invent a fictional one if you wanted to), they actually have a couple of interesting stories of their own. There's a series of medieval folk tales and stories about Melusine, a female spirit/faerie/something connected to fresh water and sacred springs. These stories mostly came from the British Isles, Northern France and the Low Countries, IIRC, with one of them that she personally built the Chateau de Lusignan with magic as gift for her husband "Raymond", who may or may not be Hugh I/Hugh Venator ("huntsman" or "hunter"), the founder of the House of Lusignan. This is one of a number of variations of the Melusine story, in which she marries a minor nobleman or night with the promise that he never witness her bathing. Naturally, he eventually sees her bathing in her true form, and she gets mad, turns into a dragon, and flies away. However, this story usually involves them having a number of children, and her continuing to watch over and protect them and their decedents. It's one of those things that makes a pretty cool family background for a Hermetic, Verbena or other mage.

                                Guy became King of Jerusalem by marrying Sibylla, Queen of Jerusalem (who was crowned after the deaths of both her brother and her young son from a previous - and very brief - marriage). They had actually already been married, but when her son died, a number of regional power players demanded she have her marriage to Guy annulled and pick a new husband to ensure strong allies for the kingdom. She agreed, on the condition that she got to pick her new husband. And as soon as she was crowned Queen, she announced she was remarrying Guy and he was going to be the King. She, along with the two daughters she had with Guy, died of disease shortly after he was released by Saladin. She was part of the House of Chateaundun from Anjou, which I don't think is considered to have any ties to the Merovingians either, as far as I know. But she is distantly related to Eleanor of Aquitaine and Richard the Lionhearted.

                                As near as I am aware (and I don't pretend to be an expert), the idea of the Merovingians being somehow blood decedents of Christ didn't come up until several centuries after they'd been supplanted by Charles Martel (the Hammer) and the Carolingian Dynasty. But like the Lusignan, they have some interesting stuff to play around with. The dynasty was founded by Clovis I, but his grandfather Merovech is the legendary founder of the line, and in some stories is the son of some sort of five-horned sea monster referred to as "the beast of Neptune". This "descended from a fish" concept is what the authors of The Holy Blood & The Holy Grail used as the basis for the "descended from Jesus" idea (which they've largely since admitted was a hoax). I personally think Robert Anton Wilson's The Historical Illuminatus Chronicles has a much more interesting take in which the Merovingian line are human-alien hybrids that resemble HP Lovecraft's Deep Ones and their Innsmouth hybrids.

                                In my own games, I have a faction within the Celestial Chorus who devote themselves to cultivating and protecting various "sacred bloodlines". While some of them are fixated on the whole Priory of Sion thing, others involve the descendants of the Incan royal family, the Japanese royal family, and various Indian and African linages. When and if members of said lines Awaken, they trying to scoop them up and initiate them as quickly as possible.

                                    As far as Awakening running in a family line, my answer is "maybe". I don't thing Mage ever said anything official about it one way or another (at least not pre-M20). My own take is that Avatars are at least semi-sentient, and will at times develop a preference for certain recurring traits, including genetics (as well as possibly reincarnated souls, certain beliefs or personality types, etc.). And families where this happens will frequently end up including a lot of Acolytes and even Sorcerers as they become involved with the Tradition or Craft (or even Convention). This includes the Akashic Brotherhood's 48 Clans of the Vajrapani, various Verbena families (such as the Crombys) including those that try to trace their lineage back to the Wyck, Etherite attempts to create Doc Savage and Tom Strong lines of supermen, "cursed" Hollow One families, and so forth.
