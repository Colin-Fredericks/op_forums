                                            For what it's worth, I would consider myself probably as far into the strike zone of Mage's ostensible politics as it is possible to be, and I still find Brucato's self-mythologizing and editorializing off-putting. Not for its existence but, frankly, for its immaturity and its occasional philosophical incoherence. I don't want a dry, scholarly tome, but I wouldn't mind one that didn't provoke so much cringing embarrassment. Brucato imagines Mage as a vehicle for educating his readership and provoking them to action - which would be fine, if if his assessment of our knowledge and our character wasn't so... limited. I don't mind the occasional lecture - but only if the lecturer knows what the hell they're talking about.

                                            It would be hard for me to find words to explain how irksome, how alienating I find M20 to be, after two decades as a fan, player, and storyteller. Should its trajectory set the tone for what is to come, Book of Secrets is probably going to mark the point where I begin to transition from fan to ex-fan. I don't think this is because I have never grasped the True Heart of the game. I think it's because Brucato's Mage is such a personal project that M20 is caught up in the eddies of his preferences (and biases) - which means stripping away the good work that other writers have done in the sandbox.
