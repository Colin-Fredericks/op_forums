                                Rather than "mystic weight", I'd say "baggage of paradigm". I read the Entropy prohibition as something more like, "As the Batini paradigm is so focused on the unity of all people, places, and things, those following this paradigm are unable to conceive of Effects that degrade those things to their component parts. These Effects are most often based in the Entropy sphere. A Batini who wishes to create such Effects must amalgamate their Tradition's paradigm with another which is more accepting of these possibilities - for example, they might take the Dual Tradition merit."

                                To transpose in some DA:M terminology: while the Traditions have created a shared set of "Pillars" for all to use, each paradigm still has a Foundation underlying it. The Foundation used by the Batini denies the entropic end of all things such that an effect exploiting that entropy cannot be based in that Foundation.

                                As for whether you believe it makes sense to have this concept for the Ahl-i-Batin or not, your mileage may vary.

Rules you CAN toss, but, to a degree, if you do, you either have to "go 100% Orphan" (the Brucato Way... which was somewhat hard before the advent of Focus in M20, but doable), have your mage game become idiosyncratic enough that it starts moving outwards to a personalized home version or make it a "rules on the table" game where players say things like "I use Forces 3/Mind 3 to turn all the darkness into love...." with little to no attempt to have casting be anything other than an exercise in rule application.

The whole "people playing the game.. bring to life [the magic] in descriptions." Not that it wasn't something that wasn't an issue that was wrestled with regularly, it was just never handled (past the odious, but understandable, first edition this -Sphere-to-this-thingee) other than mostly by fluff. However, that fluff was actually describing something actual if not really manifested in the crunch to any significant degree. And that the current rules of Focus are ;largely pulling towards individualized philosophy and practice of magic and more away than toward any sort of Sect-based philosophy and practice of magic. Moreover, this isn't MY idea or an artifact of my understanding of the game. It's something that was specifically addressed by the author/editor in the decisions he made in creating the new rules. It's a change and s deliberate one.

                                            01-05-2018, 08:09 PM
                                            In 2e and Revised, the lists of suggested Instruments were provided directly by the metaphysical sect write-ups, and the closest you got to Paradigm and Practice were, again, the sect write-ups. If your mage didn't join a sect, she not only didn't have an affinity Sphere; you also didn't have any guidance on how her magick worked. M20 reorganized this by providing lists of sample Paradigms and Practices, having the Practices provide the lists of associated Instruments (and Abilities, BTW; that's new crunch IMHO), and having the sect write-ups list likely Paradigms and Practices.

                                            Heavy Arms is right in that the Focus sections for each sect in M20 are equivalent to the Philosophy and Style sections in 2e and the Philosophy and Theories and Practices sections in Revised, just phrased in a way that utilizes the Paradigm and Practice write-ups provided later in the book. This gives the M20 sect Focus write-ups a more modular feel than their predecessor counterparts, but doesn't really make them any more or less flexible in terms of determining how a member's magick works. This is spelled out in Book of Secrets (the Role of Culture, pp.168–169):
                                            “The Focus entry on each two-page spread of Mage 20 describes the preferred focus of the metaphysical society in question, and although certain individual variations are allowed — say, a Verbena witch using a computer — members of that society employ most, if not all, of the focus elements mentioned within that entry.”

                                            On the other hand, the reorganization means that you always have guidance for how one's magick works; because you always know that mage's Paradigm and Practices. This is true whether or not he has joined a metaphysical sect — although your choice of sect does guide your choice of Focus. What was previously an Orphan or Hollow One who had an eclectic style patched together from a mishmash of magical beliefs (and little guidance for the player) is now a mage with the Practice of Gutter Magick. The big change here is that M20 doesn't carry the assumption that Orphans primarily practice Gutter Magick.
