    How to fit Primers and Mage training programs into the game?

    04-06-2020, 10:41 PM
    I personally like the idea of Grimoires and Primers, but am not sure how to fit them into the game. My personal fan idea of a Primer involves a huge tome made from the skin and bones of a complete pack (all Auspices) of werewolves (they were dicks who tried to wipe out the Chantry for its Node). The book generates its own Delerium effect as though it were a Crinos, and on top of having to have Intelligence 3 and having to know the mortal, hedge magic version of Enochian, you also have to have a Willpower of 10, to allow the Delerium to wash over you, and through you, and guide your mind and spirit to enlightenment, rather than just break you both physically and spiritually. It is in fact going to be a center piece for a story I'm writing up for a group I'm putting together, and I wanted thoughts and insights on how I could employ it.

    My question, is can Primers really exist in the setting when it is supposed to be so hard to Awaken and Mages are supposed to be so very rare? I personally don't mind making mages slightly more common, and I really like the idea of tools that help you achieve that final push into Enlightenment. Perhaps I should just ignore a few aspects of the game to help the story go forward?

      Of course they can exist; the Sons of Ether were founded on one. The catch is that reading the Primer doesn't guarantee that you'll Awaken; a lot of other factors need to align. Think of the Primer more as a catalyst: there must already be something driving the potential mage toward Awakening, and in a manner that suitable to the nature of the Primer; but it can't be so strong of an impetus that she Awakens on her own. Reading the Primer weakens the barrier that's preventing her from Awakening enough to allow her to Awaken.

      A Primer that's read by someone who isn't ready to Awaken, or whose Focus isn't going to be compatible with the teachings of the Primer, won't do anything for that reader.

      My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

          Also, as presented in Ascension's Landscape on the Storyteller's Vault and even work's such as Paul Strack's Mage Demographics back from 1990s discussions of Mage, there isn't a set number of mages in the world. Each group should chose how frequently Awakening occurs, the survival rate of mages to various power levels (as in, do most mages that make it out of the Apprentice stage end up becoming a Master?) and other questions.

          The OP's proposed primer is considerably narrower in usability than any of the primers and grimoires in Forged in Dragon's Fire and other Mage books from Mage: the Ascension (I'm not that up on Sorcerer's Crusade or Dark Ages) except the Uranian Manual. Even that doesn't require Willpower 10 to use.

          Mage being a game about reshaping reality beckons to each group to make minor tweaks of things they don't like to be more fun. If you are discussing things with others, just be kind and let them know when departing from canon.

                  Given that the point of a Primer is to Awaken people and spread the creator's Paradigm, I don't think anyone would make one as difficult to engage with as the example the OP described.

                  Except Hermetic House Tytalus, I suppose...


                  More to the point, Dataweaver is more or less correct. A Primer alone won't drag a person into Awakening. The person must be able and willing to meet it halfway.

                  First, they must have the reading comprehension to grok the lessons the text is trying to impart. Reader must be able to understand the material, rather than it washing off like water off a duck's back. One imagines a Primer is complex enough that you really need to contemplate it in order to absorb the implications.

                  Second, the reader mustn't be resistant to accepting the lesson as true. The biggest obstacle in the way of a person's Awakening is their own attachment to the world as they understand it. Awakening requires - and I will never apologize for the pun - a paradigm shift. Present all the evidence you like, the reader has to choose to believe it. The Primer will take a person to the edge; the reader must decide to make the leap.

                          There's a biblical adage that “faith without works is dead”. If you profess a belief in something, but it doesn't affect the way you live your life, do you really believe it?

                          So yeah: reading a Primer and nodding your head and saying “yeah, that makes sense” and doing nothing else isn't going to lead to an Awakening. To Awaken, you must attempt to put the lessons in the Primer into practice.

                          The point is that the use of a Primer is something intensely personal; you're not going to be able to use one to mass-produce mages. Conversely, it's not without value; it does make it more likely that the reader will Awaken — and arguably, that's not just because the arguments in its pages are persuasive. It is, after all, a Wonder.

                                      As I read Forged By Dragon's Fire, a Primer awakens someone if they're open to the magical teachings within (so you can't be reading the thing merely as a scholarly pursuit thinking it's all hogwash and accidentally awaken), you affirmatively incorporate and perform the teachings contained within, and have an Intelligence Trait of 4 or higher. If all those things are true, X number of weeks later (the book says how many--it's like between 1 and 3 months), the person awakens. No roll as I recall--it just happens. NOW, good luck findings someone with (a) that level of Intelligence (statistically rare), (b) who is genuinely open to potentially radical and heterodox ideas (i.e., the substantive content of the Primer), and (c) who is going to buy in enough to actually 'get with the program' and do what the Primer says.

                                      Mind you, I think a shortcut to getting people to take it seriously is to do some wild crap in front of them so they'll take it seriously, but it's still not gonna be easy-peasy.

                                                          This.

                                                          As I see it, in M20 terms a Primer has a Focus: it pushes you to Awaken by revealing a Paradigm and a Practice which you must embrace if the Primer is going to be of any benefit in your Awakening. And Practices have Associated Abilities which arguably every practitioner should know. There isn't a hardwired mandate in the books to this effect; but if you have Martial Arts as your Practice and you don't have any dots in a Martial Arts Ability, you're doing something wrong. This holds for Primers, too: you really ought to have some dots in the Associated Abilities of the Practice that the Primer is trying to teach you. That, rather than a mandatory four dots in Intelligence, feels like a more appropriate prerequisite to Awakening through a Primer.

                                                          Also: while they don't show up in every Practice, look for Academics, Belief Systems, Esoterica, and Science as very common Associated Abilities that tend to represent knowledge of the underlying theoretical foundation of a given Practice. In fact, going through the Practices described in the core book, the only one I could find that didn't have at least one of these particular Abilities listed was Gutter Magick. If I had to pin it down to just two Abilities, it would be Esoterica (for mystical mages) and Science (for technomancers).
