    Zaibatsu was a term for industrial/business conglomerates during Japan's imperial period (1868 - 1947) and were almost all forcefully dissolved or broken up by the Allied Occupation Forces after WW2. Modern Japanese business tends to involve Keiretsu, which are somewhat similar but are, in economic terms, horizontally integrated (meaning they tend to be a single group that controls most of the production of a single type of product - think how Disney controls such a huge chunk of the entertainment industry, or the way the Kraft Heinz Company controls a huge swath of the finished processed food market) rather than using the vertical integration (a single group that controls every single step of their product, such as the way Standard Oil owned it's entire process, from drilling to refining to transport to direct sales to customers).
    The name can make a certain amount of sense as a name the Japanese members of the Syndicate - or rather the Invisible Exchequer - used to refer to themselves after the Meiji Restoration, but after WW2, it'd be archaic, and any time before the 1870s would be anachronistic.

    As for Saensaeng, "sean", depending on which characters are used, apparently either means "tea plantation" or "to defer" in Japanese. "Saeng" is apparently a Korean word that can mean student or to survive. However, there is a Korean term, Sangsaeng, that roughly means "mutually life-giving". Its apparently frequently used by South Korean politicians to mean "win-win", as in something that's mutually beneficial for all parties involved. This may have been the term Dragons of the East was trying to go for, but there's no way to know.
    I feel I should point out that what Dragons of the East did in having the Japanese originating Saensaeng move to Korea and take after WW2 and be largely made up of militaristic generals believing in supremacy of Japanese culture (and possibly race) is the sort of thing that is likely to be extremely offensive to most Koreans. The Japanese treatment of Korea when it was their colonial property, especially during WW2, is still a major issue in their relations. While not quite as bad as having Israel's branch of the Technocracy be run by a conspiracy of German supremacists who happened to show up in 1945, it's pretty close.

    With the Miao Guan, there's a GuanMiao district in southern Taiwan. I think - but am honestly not sure - that it's somehow supposed to mean Temple Barrier or something along those lines.
    For Taiping Tianguo, Taiping apparently translates as "great peace" or something to that effect. Tianguo apparently means Heaven.
    For Zi Guang, apparently guang means light. I think Zi may mean "from", but I'm not certain.

        My theory is that while the Union as a whole is globalist, the Elemental Dragons are closet nationalists. They're not hiding their existence from the rest of the Union (that notion, as pointed out in TR, is stupid); but they are hiding their agenda, which is to establish China as the dominant world power.

        To that end, the Yellow/Earth Dragons are fine with the rest of the Union seeking a globalist agenda, as it's much easier to subvert a single governing structure to serve one country's interests than it is to have to dominate countries one by one. They're also very big on developing and deploying the Social Credit system by which they seek to control the Chinese population, and eventually the world population.

        The White/Metal Dragons are behind China's efforts to become the leaders in 5G and AI technologies, with the idea being that if China is manufacturing all of the hardware for the next-generation Internet, the Dragons will be able to insert backdoors at the hardware level greatly enhancing their ability to monitor and manipulate everything in the upcoming Internet of Things. In short, they seek to dominate the Digital Web.

        The Black/Water Dragons aren't just involved in organized crime; they're also the driving force behind China's One Belt/One Road policy, where China has been making lavish loans to impoverished countries, bankrolling massive infrastructure programs; and when the countries in question are unable to pay back the loans, China seizes the assets. In this way, the Black/Water Dragons are helping plant Chinese bases around the globe. They've also been instrumental, in cooperation with the Yellow/Earth Dragons, in building up China's technology through state policies that steal copyrights out from under businesses that seek to do business in China — essentially state-sanctioned corporate espionage.

        The Red/Fire Dragons are doing what they can to arm and staff those bases with the most advanced technology possible. They've also been involved in geo-engineering projects like the creation of artificial islands in the South China Sea in a land-grab (well, sea-grab) effort. They're also seeking to develop China's space program with the goal of being able to eliminate rival nations' satellite networks and achieve Space Superiority when the time comes.

        The Green/Wood Dragons have been developing China's bio-warfare capabilities. It's not known whether they're behind the current global pandemic (deliberately or not); but it's possible.

                                                    Originally posted by Penelope View Post

                                                    My interpretation is that the Saensaeng began in Heian Japan (where they were known as the Court of Chrysanthemums, which actually is Japan’s imperial flower), then they spread into Korea in the 13th century (where they became known as the Saensaeng).
                                                    I think the main lesson to be taken away from No One of Consequence's post is that the names given in Dragons of the East are essentially made up, and are probably not worth keeping. I think that's what TR was getting at when it ridiculed the “Taiping Tianguo” name as something a Convention would adopt.

                                                    Note that TR has also reshuffled some of the designations and retconned a lot of material: while you can probably keep much of the stuff about the Wood Dragons and Water Dragons (and even with the latter, I have some concerns), the other three are significantly revised. As near as I can tell, the group that was previously referred to as the Miao Guan is now the Yellow/Earth Dragons; what was originally the Earth Dragons (formerly the Saensaeng) are now the Red/Fire Dragons; and what was originally the Fire Dragons (a.k.a. the Zaibatsu) are now the White/Metal Dragons. Given that the Zaibatsu were associated with Matter and the Saensaeng were associated with Forces, I tend to prefer these revised associations. It also scraps the original notion that “Metal Dragons” is the Elemental Dragons' name for the Western Technocracy, and opens up the possibility of using “Spirit Dragons” as a designation for the Wu Lung.

                                                    That said, a case could be made that most or all of the Elemental Dragons should also have Prime as an associated Sphere, given their shared interest in Chi. Metaphysically, that more than anything else distinguishes them from their Western counterparts. On the other hand, it may also be the aspect from Dragons of the East that most needs to be toned down: the whole Year of the Lotus seemed to be obsessed with Chi. And while the Dragons of the East aren't technically part of the Year of the Lotus, it's pretty much the same mindset. I also think the Technocratic Sphere variants should feature among the Dragons of the East, with the Yellow/Earth Dragons and White/Metal Dragons getting Data, the Red/Fire Dragons getting Dimensional Science, and the Black/Water Dragons getting Primal Utility.
