    @Quantumboost: my contention is that the Consensus can't do what a Master of even an Adept could do — or if it can, it requires a high-density population with a remarkably high consistency of beliefs. Certainly, a lot of Sleepers with shared beliefs can enact changes that a few Sleepers (or a lot of Sleepers with conflicting views) can't; and I'd be willing to hypothesize that this takes the form of a higher effective Arete in terms of what sorts of changes can be imposed. But even with that, I wouldn't take it as a given that the population densities needed for, say, Disciple-level changes (let alone Adept, Master, or Archmaster) can physically be achieved.

            Consider just how many determinations of what is possible or impossible, or how precisely things work, are Adept- or Master-level effects. Is nuclear fission operating the way it does an Earthly Foundation? That's a Forces 5/Matter 5 effect, there, so by those standards Reality Zones can't cause or prevent that. Creating the Gauntlet in the first place was a Spirit 5 effect, and making it stronger or weaker permanently is a Spirit 4 effect; I'm pretty sure the Consensus can shape how thick the Gauntlet is. Can human belief generate Nodes and Junctures? That's a Prime 5 effect.

            Oh, and one more - changing what's Coincidental or Vulgar in an area?

            That's a Spirit or Dimensional Science 4 effect.

            I'm... pretty sure that the Consensus can do that.

            There are other examples, but... yeah. What constitutes how many dots an Effect requires isn't always magnitude of the change so much as depth and precision of the change.

                                                    In case some people find it useful, here are some of the ways I've seen Mage interpreted over the years. All of these are supported in varying degrees by the books.

                                                    1) There is no structure to reality. Nothing really exists and the Consensus just makes things happen anyways. The harder the Consensus believes something, the harder it is to change their minds.

                                                    2) Without a Consensus or some other similar force Reality looks like the Deep Umbra. Consensus creates the structure of the world and things happen because of that structure.

                                                    3) There is some kind of structure underlying reality, possibly left by the Pure Ones (if they ever existed). Whatever the case, the Consensus can completely override and rewrite that structure.

                                                    4) There are a few pieces of unchangeable reality that underlay Consensual Reality (mostly for ST benefit). Everything else can technically be rewritten, although much of it won't be.

                                                    5) There's a whole bunch of rules that can't be changed, but many more rules are just products of the Consensus. The big things can't be changed, but other parts of reality can be completely rewritten.

                                                    6) There is a complete and defined reality that underlays Consensual Reality. The Consensus doesn't change that structure, but can force things to happen against it.

                                                    7) There is a large complicated reality underneath Consensual Reality. The Consensus can change the way it manifests to a large degree, but not always in predictable ways.

                                                    8) Everything is True. Consensual Reality just dictates which True things manifest. Some of you might recognize the main proponent of that one.


                                                    It's not an exhaustive list, and many people mix two or more of these, but it gives an idea of just what ideas Mage inspires in people. Mage is a game that offers a lot of possibility.
