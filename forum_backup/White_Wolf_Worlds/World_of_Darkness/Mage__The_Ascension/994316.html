    Sorcerers and Mages

    10-27-2016, 09:36 PM
    Why aren't sorcerers and pychics used more in mage? They seem like a perfect antithesis to mages and their viewpoints by way of "you see how you want reality to be, we see it how it is".

    They also present an interesting point on the consensual reality vs static reality argument. if humans don't believe in magic, why is it still available in a form usable by Anyone? Is consensual reality a pipe dream spouted by the traditions? A lie about an easy fix to regain their glory when no such thing exists?

    Not to mention their ability to use counter-spells, a starting sorcerer not particularly focused on countering magic can have four dice of counter-magic to subtract from the successes of any spell(and a starting mage has only up to 3 dice of arete to cast magic with). This makes it seem like sorcerers are the vangaurd enforcing reality as opposed to mages who bend reality.

      The way I see it, mythic threads exist when a significant minority of Sleepers believe in them and the majority doesn't oppose their existence. That is, there's a difference between not believing in something and actively disbelieving it; and Mythic Threads are what happens when the majority can't be bothered to reject what the minority believes.

      Bringing this back to the topic at hand, humans do believe in magic, even in Technocratic Reality Zones — at least, enough people believe to make it possible, and not enough people are hostile enough to the notion to make it impossible.

      And if the Earthly Foundations are biased toward a given set of metaphysics, the support for it in the Consensus can be smaller and the opposition to it larger and it will still function.

          And if the Earthly Foundations are biased toward a given set of metaphysics, the support for it in the Consensus can be smaller and the opposition to it larger and it will still function.
          Personally, I would go a step further, and say Mythic Threads can be supported just by being known. Myths, legends, folk tales, and fiction keep concepts alive in the public consciousness. And, because people know them and value them (even if they don't think of them as true), a portion of their total emotional and mental attention is invested in them.

          Consider the superhero. In less than a century, comic books and other media have popularized the idea of superhumans and super science. The recent rash of superhero movies have only reinforced the cultural impact of them. This creates Mythic Threads - not because most people believe there truly are people who have these powers or their toys, but because the ideas stick with them. In a way, people want to believe in superheroes, even if their "rational sense" tells them they are just fiction. In comes Mythic Threads, creating a place for superheroes that might not be fully supported by the Consensus, but has a presence within it.

          Sorcery and Psychic powers, in many ways, are just like that. The very ideas of Fortune Telling or Telekinesis have persistent influence on even the "rational" person's mind. The common man - the one who doesn't actually believe - still fuels the Mythic Threads, albeit in a way perhaps less potent than the True Believer.

              On the difference between Sorcerer and Mage, it comes very much down to those Mythic Threads. A Sorcerer can't usually use any old method (Instrument), and expect to get a result. They need to do research, find a teacher, or experiment until they discover (or rediscover) techniques that work. These, of course, being those Instruments ingrained into the popular consciousness. It's why Sorcerers still use the same tools after centuries; they still work. In this way, though, it means that Sorcerers are ultimately dependent on the Mythic Threads, and a well-established thread of belief, and that of the collective consciousness.

              Mages are the only ones whose capacity for magic can be fueled by the Mage's belief alone.

                                  Unless you are referring to a *specific* mage (or cabal of mages) holding this view/belief, that statement is skewed to say the least. Sorcerers, as presented, are by no means the "antithesis" of Mages -- not at all. They are simply a different side of the metaphysical coin. As stated in different Mage books over the years, one of the benchmarks for (M)ages doing what they do is that as long as (S)orcerers (and other numinically endowed mortals/beings) can still do what they do, that actually *supports* what most (M)ages do in the long run. (That is, as long as The Consensus continues to support and not overly oppose sorcerers, they act as the baseline/bulwark that actually *supports* what the more "properly" Awakened do.) They aren't the antithesis, at all, any more than the lower organs of the body are "antithetical" to the brain -- the brain (higher self/what is the more proper "us") is supported by the "lesser" organs beneath it (and it, in turn, helps guide and support the organs in return). Many sorcerers follow the (P)aths (directly or by rote pattern) that many mages left in their wake while expanding (or, admittedly, at times constricting) Reality(tm); just as sorcerers in many cases metaphysically support the more dynamic magic(k) of mages, the ever-expanding workings of the latter provide (over time) further hedge magics for sorcerers to attempt to master (whether mythic, technomystical, or any of a number of other possibilities). Many larger cabals of mystics will often have "more talented", "natural" witches (Mages) working hand-in-hand with their more "studied, salt-of-the-earth" brethren (Sorcerers, or the Psychically Endowed). This is particularly true, for example, among a number of Verbena, Hermetics and even more so among the Choristers, who routinely interact with more "mundane" faith healers, theurgists, and pseudo-hermetic-cum-religious crusaders. Other groups have similar, if often smaller in ratio, counterparts of Mages and Sorcerers -- it just only been peripherally addressed over the various editions/versions of the game setting over the past 20+ years vs. being a more focused aspect.

                                                  To expand on the above, the Order of Hermes, the Verbana, and the Choristers have a heavy base of linear practitioners. Not just as hangers-on, but as a sizable part of their membership.

                                                  The Order of Hermes teaches their initiates Paths of Sorcery as a matter of course. Whether those initiates Awaken or not is immaterial, as both maintain a place in the Order's hierarchy. Granted, the Order has had a long history of bias against their Sorcerer base - there's an impression that if they don't Awaken, they are somehow inferior. And this is a problem that the Tradition as a whole has wrestled with, for good and for ill. The Revised era - where many of the old Masters were lost - set up the possibility for Sorcerers to leverage their collective political might to gain greater respect. After all, the Sorcerers may be asleep, but they are still Hermetics, and that makes them entitled to respect from their fellow wizards.

                                                  Plus, they are also the ones who often do the administrative jobs. And you never want to piss off the bureaucracy. They can make life for their Awakened "betters" a living hell, without needing to rely on spells.

                                                  The Verbanae, on the other hand, has an entire faction - the Gardeners of the Tree - revolving around long family lines and traditional rites. They are the ones who keep the Old Ways, and chart the family trees. And when the Awakening doesn't (usually) work on a hereditary basis, the only way for these families to pass their knowledge on (in lieu of carefully preserving old books, oral tales, and magic items) is to teach Paths of Sorcery from one generation to the next. Any given family member Awakening is considered a good thing, but not the ultimate point, next to which all linear family members are merely stepping stones. The Old Ways as a Paradigm is an end unto itself, whether those arts are preserved by a Sorcerer or taken to new heights by a Mage. Indeed, the Gardeners value innovation much less than preservation. You need to talk to the Moon-Seekers if you want to innovate.

                                                  The Celestial Chorus is, at its core, a religious community (well, more like a series of loosely allied religious communities). Anyone who wishes to serve the One (in whatever guise pleases them) is a valuable member of the Tradition. As are the members with True Faith (assuming you interpret the Celestial Chorus as having both True Magick and True Faith, of course), and even those with no Numina whatsoever. Everyone has a place in the divine order, and everyone has a part of God's plan. If some of those people should show the capacity to work miracles, that doesn't devalue the work that the more humble members do. Indeed, the Sorcerer's actions - or even the non-powered Chorister's actions - can end up being more integral to God's Plan than the miracle worker. God moves in mysterious ways, after all.

                                                  One could easily argue that Chorister Mages must guard themselves from the hubris of thinking themselves better than their "lessers". The faith healer working to make the lives of a small community better doesn't need to worry about Paradox, you remember, and what is Paradox to a religious Mage but the sign of the One's displeasure? Vulgar magics used to be called Vain...

                                                      I think that you might be mistaking how mages actually see the world. You seem to be understanding that mages understand what we call the "Purple Paradigm," which is to say the belief in the malleability of reality and the effect of the Consensus. In effect, metaknowledge. For the vast majority, let's say 99.9% repeating, this is not true. Each mage faction, and by extension, each mage, believes that they've found some way to do the supernatural, but within rules as they understand them. Magick works for Hermetics because they say the right words and draw the right symbols. Magick works for Choiristers because they say the right prayers and sing the right songs. Magick works for Etherites because they built the right machines in the right way according to the right SCIENCE. You get what I mean. What's important to note, however, is that they very rarely believe that they're all doing the same thing. In fact, most of the time, each faction in Mage believes that they're doing something different than the others! Magick to a Hermetic is something completely different from the magick of a Verbena witch, in their own minds, despite the fact that they very much are not.

                                                      This plays into what Sorcerers do. Sorcerers use the methods that mages have developed, methods that have made themselves into the Consensus somewhat. This is why the Paths are so limited in scope, because they've been well defined in what they can do by the belief of those who practice them. It makes them reliable and teachable to the non-awakened. In this way, Sorcery actually reinforces the belief systems of mages since they have "proof" that the methods used by sorcerers (that were developed by mages in the first place) are viable to the non-Awakened. This is also why Disbelief is a thing in Sorcerer: the Consensus affects them too.
