                                          Originally posted by Sergeant Brother View Post
                                          Honestly, I don't think that it makes sense for the Technocracy and Pentex to exist in the same universe. I existence of one undermines the importance of the other. That is the downside of crossovers.
                                          You have to come to some concessions if you want a World of Crossover-y Darkness. If you can't encompass them within the constraints of your visions of the game, of course the overlaps don't work.

                                          If Pentex is as all-encompassing evil as presented in Werewolf, the the point that it informs, invents, subverts and encompasses all corporate culture, there isn't room for the Syndicate. Or only for a very small Syndicate (as part of a very small Union as part of a very small overall number of mages who have a very small overall effect on a world going straight down to Wyrm-ish Hell).

                                          If in your Mage game, the Syndicate is in absolute control of the world economy, it HAS to know about Pentex and it would be idiocy to be clueless about their RD aspects, including stuff that screams "NEPHANDI!" in blood-colored neon. Especially considering that Pentex is so obviously and openly involved in so many high-profile aspects of commerce. The entire Syndicate would know about it, not just SPD and SPD would have a very hard time keeping a secret relationship very secret, and, though it's entirely possible, coming up with a reason why the Syndicate is not doing it's job and leaving a significant portion of the world's economy in the hands of RD's in, in terms of long term cost-benefit analysis, a remarkably BAD idea. Said reasons (the whole Technephandi thing <ick>, Hubris as described above... "We can control them. They can be controlled...", etc.) do make enough sense in context of Mage to work better than seeing it through Werewolf goggles.

                                          Probably the best route to take in a crossover world is that there is a whole lot of mis-perception and propaganda going on. The Syndicate no more controls the economy than Pentex does. The world is big enough that both can exist and brush up against one another, but, if each is focused elsewhere, convinced of it's own supremacy in its area, etc. they effectively co-exist. Kinda like the Camarilla and Union think they both control pretty much everything (except that which they don't and is on the slate for future hegemony) Sure, the Syndicate has a big lever by which to make changes and can be the tail that wags the dog, but a lot of that power is invested in advancing the status quo. Pentex has Maeljin Incarnae keeping it on the down low when the Syndicate's plan-scopers are doing their worldwide Entropy/Primal Utility/Correspondence/Time Procedures to massage commerce.

                                                      When Sabbat was made to be a playable DnD faction (in the first guide), they changed that. They introduced Demons as their main problem as well as Lupines and Vicissitude Umbral disease (among other crossover things). Then, in Revised, they changed it once more and made the Sabbat less DnD (Demons are still a problem, just not the central problem).

                                                      It was the same for the Technos, when they made it a playable faction, they had to fix the "flawless" aspect of the Union (either with Pentex or something else).

                                                      As for the Anarch/kuei-jin problem. The main reason Anarchs in San Fransisco were (almost) obliterated and put down was because one of their Leaders surrendered to the Kuei-Jin (I'm surprised you didn't even know that). Kuei-Jin have been playing them fools ever since.

                                                      There are other examples as well: Bamboo Princes with Tremere/Brujah, Genji with Sabbat...the pattern repeats itself, always for the same purpose: adding some flaws to the factions.
