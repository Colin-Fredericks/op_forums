                  Given the setup of Mage... the reclassification of Pluto could be an actual Technocratic scheme to depower the Tradition stronghold there (or a number of other Future Fates that you could tie in like trying to put up a firewall against Threat Null).

                  That said, I think you have your initial premise a bit backwards. The Nine Spheres were not picked from traditional astrology because most traditional astrologies only have 7 celestial bodies besides Earth: the Sun, the Moon, and the five visible planets (Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn). Though some older versions thought Venus was two different planets. The Mage creators retrofit the accepted nine planets of the time back into the cosmology of Mage. Keep in mind that Pluto wasn't even officially discovered until 1930 because it's that hard to detect (though there was some evidence of it existing before that to cause scientists to look for it). For everyone that's yapping about Mage here, Pluto has always been part of our known world. A hundred years ago nobody would have known about some planet named Pluto.

                  The reclassification of Pluto is actually easier on Mage as a game because instead of suborning any major occurrence of a set of nine as something with Sphere correspondences regardless of how well that meshes with the real world inspirations for the various Mage groups, you can play a 7 body astrologer Mage that doesn't have to care about how the discovery of lots of other things floating around in space well after their belief systems were formalized get labelled by scientists. Even if that's a different problem with integrating various metaplot stuff, etc.

                          I would say it depends a bit. In the New Age style astrology, there tends to be a love of adding in the dwarf planets as an additional layer of astrology (you can find astrology charts that include brand new correspondences for Ceres, Eris, and so on).

                          I mean, there's some decent Mage story fodder for debates between the older versions/reconstruction style astrologers, and the New Age style ones. It's hard to put a real world rating on what "most" do, because I'm not aware of anyone that seriously collects data on that. But there are certainly old school mages that want to do things the way things were done centuries ago, and others that want to update to keep more in-line with Consensus if only because they're younger and just disregarding centuries of discoveries they grew up with feels wrong.

                          I also think how much the Traditions should or would care about these things would come down a lot to how much they perceive it as an active effort of the Union, or just a side effect of the Technocrats' manipulations of Sleeper science. If enough Tradition (or Disparate) mages are convinced the Technocracy is up to something, they're going to care simply because you can't really just ignore that. It might not be because what school kids are taught to call Pluto, but because whatever the reason to start pushing that is good for the Union and thus bad for everyone else in the world of mages.

                          Of course, if it's not really a plot and just Sleeper science going to the logical conclusions of things the Technocrats set in motion decades ago, then it's more likely for most mages to not find a reason to put energy into it over all of the other things going on to worry about.
