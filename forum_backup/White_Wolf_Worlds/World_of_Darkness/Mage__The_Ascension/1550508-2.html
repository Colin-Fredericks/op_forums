            I'm not a betting man, but I would find a very safe bet to say that Mage 5th edition will go exactly this route, of making the Hollow Ones, the gutter mages that do not follow any particular traditions, are not scions of big organized legacies... WoD 5th edition has unraveled most big organizations into provincial enclaves, or taken them out of the player core of the game, turning them into allies, enemies or other npcs. It has been so with V5, it was strongly so with H5, and a tendency (not fully staked in W5).

            Besides, there has always been a terrible dissonance between the gameplay, ruleset and fiction (or even aesthetics) of Mage. Much is shown and spoken about a guerilla covert war, a survival for the magic and awesome power that a mage can attain to change the world. Raincoats, alleys and secret encounters in downtrodden bars... Tim Bradstreet style. That doesn't really happen if you are a studen of hogwart in a big worldwide millenium spanning organization with hundreds or thousands of members, that are coordinated with many others (the Traditions), making up many thousands strong. Full of facilities, artifacts and teachers that would have no reason to leave a mage hanging in ignorancy.

            So yeah, I think they will shift to a punk Mage style game. Reduce the scope, leave only few members of the Traditions, making it feel like a dying culture with some few elders keeping it alive, like the Jedi after Order 66, or a any other group after cultural genocide.
            In the same manner, the Technocracy will still be a threat, but headed by some warlord-type of strong leaders, that are trying to take control of the Union. Which is in disarray for some new or old metaplot development. Agents are cuthroat and desperate in some way.


            That's my take. Mage in WoD for me has lost the mystique (thats more of an Awakening thing), so it is more a game about taking power to change the world. What better way to do that than from the absolute bottom, Mages can and will ascend, that is what their Magick can do. Not a scared and powerless civillian, but a potentially almighty mage, trailing their own path or taking up the mantle of the fallen Traditions.

            Strange... When coincidence seems too convenient, I prefer to call it fate.

            -Blood Omen: Legacy of Kain d=

                Mage the Ascension never had that mystique. It was not really the point. It's a game about absolute power and what you do with it, where mysticism is by design just window-dressing.                 I do think you're right about M5, anyway. Unless White Wolf Reborn shifts gears drastically (possible, but unlikely), they'll go all in on Punk Mage, and then the Hollow Ones are prime candidates for new intended protagonists as suggested in the Opening Post. And the elements for doing so are already set in motion by the end of Revised (Threat Null and the aftermath of the Avatar Storm), so it may even look better from a continuity perspective than the current lines.

                And I won't blame them too much by now. While I dislike the 5th Edition, it's as valid a game as any, if there's people for whom this game will click, all the better. I do think that the best path forward, if one day the IP comes to better management, is to split WoD in two and keep both versions as equally valid. (I won't stop pointing out bad writing, though).

                                                        This would be a departure from the direction of WoD5e. The identities of the clans and tribes, may be changed or vague, but they are promoted as a defining feature of one's player character. They didn't come up with a list of verbs to characterise each tribe only to push Forsworn as the default.

                                                        The changes to V5 and W5 mostly open up x-splats so that any character of the player's choosing can be included, provided that it's a character concept that fits the mores of the given playable society. For example, Tremere don't have to be in the pyramid and Black Furies don't have to be women (or metis men) or even particularly feminist.

                                                        I wouldn't be surprised if the institutions of the Traditions and maybe Technocracy broke down. But the new player will still be presented with a set of Traditions, possibly alongside what we call Conventions. You wouldn't need the Hollow Ones to play a scrappy, free punk because you'd be able to play a scrappy, free punk hermetic, progenitor or kahuna. But not necessarily in the same historic, defined subcultures of NPCs in the existing versions of Mage.

                                                        Hollow Ones will only be the main protagonists in M5 if pretty much all relevant mages, outside radical NPC factions, are Hollow Ones in practice and not in name.
