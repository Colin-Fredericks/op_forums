                        The problem with the position that the Consensus shapes reality is that the Consensus then does change people if it changes reality. The genetic and phenotype changes that you mention change people biologically and psychologically. Unless you are saying that human biology and human psychology have no impact on humans unless they believe so, you cannot take the position that the Consensus cannot impact people if it changes reality.

                        Here is an example. Did cancer exist before it was quantified by the ancient Greeks? Did it only exist for the ancient Greeks and spread like a contagious disease as the idea of cancer spread throughout the world? Do people only get cancer because they believe in the existence of cancer? If the Consensus shapes reality, then cancer is a creation of belief and people only die of cancer because they (or society) believe in cancer. If the Consensus only shapes magic, then cancer is just a misfortune that may be cured with magic (or science).

                        The same position can be taken with any disease. If the Consensus shapes reality, then people get diseases because they (or society) believe in the disease. If the Consensus only shapes magic, then diseases are misfortunes that can be cured by magic (or science).

                        We can even get more applied than the example of diseases. Why do guns kill people? If the Consensus shapes reality, guns only kill people because the Consensus tells the attacker and the defender that guns are lethal. Since the historical record is pretty clear that guns killed people from cultures that had never previously encountered guns, you have evidence of the clash of two forms of Consensus. The European Consensus was stronger than the non-European Consensus in the case of guns (and diseases), allowing Europeans to commit genocide on non-Europeans. If the Consensus could not change people, then non-Europeans should have been immune to bullets (and also to the diseases that the Europeans brought). If the Consensus only shapes magic, however, then guns (and diseases) killed the non-Europeans due to physical laws rather than because the non-Europeans had an inferior Consensus.

                            They do, but keep in mind that those changes were made as alternate explanations for what people were already seen to be like. Men and women wouldn't undergo a massive change in personality the moment X and Y chromosomes were accepted because men and women already existed before that. At most you'd see a kind of slow change of dispositions in the sexes, but only as a generality, nothing that forces people to behave a certain way.

                            What you wouldn't get is a sudden change that drastically and forcibly alters behaviors and opinions. There's an inertia to belief. Day to day life remains much the same even after a complete paradigm shift.

                            Here is an example. Did cancer exist before it was quantified by the ancient Greeks? Did it only exist for the ancient Greeks and spread like a contagious disease as the idea of cancer spread throughout the world? Do people only get cancer because they believe in the existence of cancer? If the Consensus shapes reality, then cancer is a creation of belief and people only die of cancer because they (or society) believe in cancer.                             The same position can be taken with any disease. If the Consensus shapes reality, then people get diseases because they (or society) believe in the disease. If the Consensus only shapes magic, then diseases are misfortunes that can be cured by magic (or science).
                            This isn't that hard to explain in a Consensual Reality. Cancer might not have existed before then but wasting diseases almost certainly did. There's no discontinuity, no "one day there were no wasting diseases, then BAM! Cancer!" What spread was not disease itself but a new idea of how disease worked. And yes, that new idea, and all the ideas that came after it gradually changed the way diseases did manifest and made certain symptoms possible or impossible. Gradually.

                            And yes, it is entirely plausible that people only get sick because societies believe they get sick. It's a nasty consequence of the metaphysics of Mage, but there's nothing game breaking or theme breaking about it.

                            We can even get more applied than the example of diseases. Why do guns kill people? If the Consensus shapes reality, guns only kill people because the Consensus tells the attacker and the defender that guns are lethal. Since the historical record is pretty clear that guns killed people from cultures that had never previously encountered guns, you have evidence of the clash of two forms of Consensus. The European Consensus was stronger than the non-European Consensus in the case of guns (and diseases), allowing Europeans to commit genocide on non-Europeans. If the Consensus only shapes magic, however, then guns (and diseases) killed the non-Europeans due to physical laws rather than because the non-Europeans had an inferior Consensus.
                            Guns kill people because severe organ damage and blood loss kills people. This is something that was well known millennia before guns were ever invented. The Consensus changes involved with making guns possible involved convincing people that controlled explosions could semi-reliably make pieces of metal fly really quickly in a controlled way. That's why, when you take a gun into a different reality zone and it fails you generally get a gun that won't fire, not bullets that bounce off of people.

                            Likewise most, if not all of the world's societies had an idea of plagues and disease and sometimes even a knowledge that newcomers bring disease. So they wouldn't have been paradigmatically resistant to European illness. Non European Consensuses weren't inferior, they just weren't resistant to the ideas Europeans were using to take over.

                            ,And if we're honest, they didn't have the backing of a culturally imperialistic, monomaniacal cult of Mages that was sanitizing the world before the Traditions could even get their boots on. The Order of Reason played a big role in the advancement of the Western consensus.
