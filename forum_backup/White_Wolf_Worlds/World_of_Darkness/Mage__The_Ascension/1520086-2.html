                Originally posted by travisjhall View Post
                I’m not a fan of adding Spheres into effects for nothing at all. (Sphere ratings are not really an in-setting thing anyway. The character would usually be thinking “I need to reduce my weight” rather than “I need to include Life 2”; Life 2 is just the game system representation of a set of abilities that will tend to develop together because they are related and of similar difficulty. Just like how real people don’t have one of five levels of strength, with everybody with the same number of dots having exactly the same lifting capability, and even when people have similar overall levels of physical strength one may bench more, while another may have higher grip strength. Reality is more complex than game systems. But I digress…)

                But we can have effects that do things on a scale that doesn’t show up in the game system. A basic damaging effect done with coincidental Life and coincidental Matter have exactly the same result in system terms (and if the players rolls exactly one success, these will inflict 0 dice of damage - but that doesn’t mean the effect necessarily does nothing at all). Or look up-thread at my preferred resolution of self-healing as simple feat vs standard feat: self-healing a paper cut still requires Life 2, despite not restoring a health level. Chi Flight’s use of Life 2 is itself an example of this, with the flight being possible with just Forces 2 but Life 2 making changes to how that flight works that show up as nothing more than how we would describe it.

                In the post where I said I don’t really see why Chi Flight is such an issue, I pointed out that Prime 2 in the effect is having an effect. It “channels the chi force (Quintessence)” - that’s moving Quintessence, a Prime effect. It’s not moving enough Quintessence to show up as anybody or anything gaining or losing a full point of Quintessence (not counting spending a point elsewhere in the effect, because spending Quintessence is possible without Prime) but as I point out in the previous paragraph, the effect doesn’t have to be great enough to show up in system terms to count. (You could possibly make an argument that channelling Quintessence is actually Prime 3, but given an effect below what shows up mechanically, I can see why they decided to be generous.)

                I understand that you would like to see a mechanical difference associated with the addition of Prime 2 into the Chi Flight effect (and as you want that, making up a new game mechanic is reasonable - it’s not my place to tell you that your goal is wrong, although I might have things to say about the effects of your implementation) but nothing in the written rules says that there has to be a mechanic associated with the inclusion of a sphere in an effect; it can just be at the level of description.


                And if the mage believes they can only do effects that include channelling chi (even if only a tiny amount of it, much less than a single point of Quintessence)? Or perhaps that they can only do certain effects when they also include channelling chi? Or they believe that channeling chi is part of how they must go about achieving certain effects?

                Frankly, if a player tells me they want to add Prime 2 to basically everything their character does to represent channelling chi, I’m not going to fight them on that. They’re setting up a milestone for their character on the path to ascension - learning that channelling chi is not necessary for many effects - and I’d be looking to include that story beat in the chronicle at some point down the track.


                While I don’t think HDYDT’s Chi Flight actually violates that principle, I think it’s worth challenging this assumption. Can you find anywhere in the rules that actually states this?

                BTW, Saikou, I think it would be unwise to assume that just because someone disagrees with your conclusions, they don’t understand the basics of the game system. You wrote an awful lot of words describing in great detail something that really isn’t being disputed by anyone (arguments about Forces 2 vs Forces 3 in specific examples aside).

                ​
                On your last point, I would like to make something clear. My default is to try and have no assumptions of the person I am speaking to what so ever, this means not assuming that something is taken for granted as understood. This is why I explain my viewpoint out in such detail, so anyone, regardless of their interpretation or their familiarity with the rule, can understand my point of view. It is not a comment on how little I think you understand the system, but a means to fully express my thoughts in as accessible a format as possible.

                now, there is something of a contradiction in what you say. You state you are not a fan on spheres being added to an effect that contribute nothing at all, but at the same time state you are fine with spheres being added which do nothing noticeable. Gameplay wise, this is functionally the same thing, as if a sphere is not contributing to the effect at all.

                I think it is a bit disingenuous to say an effect that rolls zero successes has the same output as any other effect that rolls zero, simply because this is an example not of the effect, but of not casting an effect. It fees like arguing that not climbing a mountain is the same thing as not buying a doughnut. In literal description it is since the effect is you don’t do a thing, but such a statement can be so broadly applied as to be rendered meaningless and unhelpful.

                Especially since if we are looking at a Life effect vs a matter effect when it comes to damage, the Life effect would be targeting the person, while the matter effect would be targeting their clothes or equipment, that alone would be a difference, and even a single success would reflect in that, either in their clothing becoming slightly worn or discoloured, or their body suddenly feeling a bit achey.

                regarding the Forces 2 Life 2 effect for flight, I disagree that it should be identical to the Forces effect alone. Forces 2 alone would affect the area as a whole in regards to gravity, letting you do your jump (as well as anyone else in the area) extra successes could also be used to affect the area you plan to land, assuming nothing bounces you off course (and again, others would feel the effects of such gravity manipultion)

                changing your weight in addition to this would first of all be a part of the effect that only applied to you, and second would allow you to land easily without needing to set up another patch of low gravity where you desired to jump to. Better still, you could land on quite delicate things such as tree branches and paper doors, and the impact of your foot against them would not cause them to break.

                the Life effect does indeed change the nature of the whole effect.

                going back to your point of “prime 2 is needed to channel Chi essence” I state here that one should not let focus dictate which spheres are required to do any basic effect.

                chi healing would be the go to example. It states outright that Chi healing is the same thing as Life 2/3 healing, but “because it involves energy” you must add Prime 2 and also spend a point of quintessence for your trouble.
                this goes a bit beyond requiring an extra sphere which does nothing, but actually requires an extra sphere which makes the effect more expensive (a point of quintessence) for no actual gain. You are essentially being punished for using chi to do something that not using chi would have allowed with less expenditure. And all of this comes across as the writer basically saying “if you believe in chi, all magic you do must now cost quintessence, so Prime is now mandatory for you to follow your practice properly” and that isn’t a great feel.

                it is why I describe my approach on a bit of spirit. The shaman with just Forces can influence the spirits of the elements like most other Forces mages, even if they are doing so by channeling the elemental spirits to move fore about. However, if they add Spirit 2 to the effect, they can also open up a direct line of communication with said elemental spirit while they are channeling them. This means they can simultaneously direct the fire and discuss with the spirit how they wish the fire to behave, resulting in the fire moving in ways that Forces 2 alone would not be able to manage, and would last far longer than the end of the effect, since once the mage is done, the spirit can keep going.

                if you want to encourage your players choosing to put barriers up against which effects they have access to, I highly recommend you use the system of Practice and Instruments to do this, such that they cannot work on the self, for example, unless they learn how to meditate as is in line with their paradigm, or that they can’t make the gun without getting to the correct level of hypertech first.

                if, however, they insist on wanting a specific sphere before they can access a specific power they already have access to (say, Spirit to converse with the spirits of nature for their Forces effect) then give them the benefit of the conjunctional effect similar to how I have described, not only is it thematically appropriate, but it makes the struggle all the more rewarding as it allows the mage not just to get access to base effects, but to actually explore the full implications of their paradigm by attaining this level of attainment with the sphere which seems prominent in their practice. And it does so in a much better way that simply telling them “well, you’re a shaman with Spirit 1, therefore you can’t do Forces effects, even with your 3 dots of Forces”

                and unless you plan to fast track them getting the levels of Spirit required, thereby undermining the entire drama of said struggle, it is not really fun to be a mage who can’t use any of the spheres they got at character creation just because “their paradigm means their sphere load out is no longer viable”
                i would highly discourage this mode of play.

                                                            The flow chart I use goes like this:

                                                            1. Determine what the effect is supposed to achieve
                                                            - know what the player is trying to do to reality
                                                            - determine if their spheres enable anything approaching this
                                                            - (let player know what range of options for achieving said effect are within their sphere capabilities if necessary)

                                                            2. Determine how that effect may be made manifest
                                                            - Ask player to describe what their mage believes their effect is doing (we skip to this step is which spheres are being used is pre established)
                                                            - Consider the internal logic of the character, i.e. what they believe they are doing, and how that would alter/colour the effect
                                                            - Discuss with player what within their own focus is required for such an effect to be made manifest (practice/instrument choice)

                                                            3. Set up the roll

                                                            this is where I start to consider process based determinism

                                                            - Consider based on what the effect does, whether they way it is cast fits in with or violates local reality zone, and if the effect is even observable, whether or not this is coincidental or vulgar, assign diff accordingly
                                                            - apply modifiers based on the table
                                                            - consider number of successes required to meet the minimum expectations of the effect
                                                            - consider if it is possible to “try again”, how much time is required between rolls, and whether or not a ritual is the only way to go about said effect via their focus.

                                                            4. Interpret results (assuming threshold successes met)

                                                            This is where process based determinism really gets the focus

                                                            - start with spheres used, this will determine exactly what aspects of reality were affected to bring about the effect.
                                                            - next consider mage’s belief, this will determine how said effect manifests, whether it be a spiritual presence, a shift in energies, a sudden shudder, a flurry of petals… this will affect not only how it looks but also the nature of the effect, healing agg via prayer will look like a soft glow over their body and leave a holy restorative resonance. healing via spirits may attract other spirits to the area, while healing using modern science might be a lengthy process requiring maybe 1-2 days of ‘casting’
                                                            - lastly, consider the instruments used, and the consequences they might have. Healing via prayer would require time and concentration, as well as no interruption in most cases. Healing via the spirits requires the correct offering, with the offering chosen determining what spirit will offer healing, and at what potential price (such as, a water spirit may heal them, but the healed mage may now have an aversion to fire for a while), and healing via medical machinery may require a medicine check to avoid creating short term physical flaws that persist for a short while (or a long while if they botch)

                                                            (some of these points may be better considered at stage 3, especially in terms of time if the mage is going to take multiple rolls to achieve their effect, and they would like the option to abort mid way if it looks like it will take too long.)
