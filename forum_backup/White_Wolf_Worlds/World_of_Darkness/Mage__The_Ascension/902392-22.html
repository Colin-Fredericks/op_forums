Such items also singe and burn any clothing that she is wearing.

        462) The Azure Impurity [Flaw or Burn]: For centuries, metalworkers and alchemists were stymied by a bothersome impurity in ore, that would render otherwise good quality metals weak and impure. Eventually, the source of this impurity was found: Cobalt. Even though it came at the crossroads between when Alchemy gave way to early Chemistry, Alchemists (such as the Children of Knowledge and specialists within the Order of Hermes) would hardly begrudge this. To them, it solved a nasty conundrum, and provided them a new material to study, which they rapidly incorporated into alchemical praxis. It's also just a handy element to have, as Cobalt in its pure form possesses a striking blue hue. Even Taftani were quick to (quietly) master the extraction of Cobalt - despite the druj involved - as it allowed them to more easily incorporate stunning blue colors into their glass-blowing projects. (The Taftani could always do this, of course, but the discovery of Cobalt made it coincidental to the point of requiring no Awakened power to enact.)

        Nonetheless, everything of magick is touched and warped by Paradox. When Alchemists and Craft Workers do transmutations, reshaping, and other magickal Effects, they can run afoul of the Cobalt Impurity. Manifests more overtly than in the past, materials (or sometimes creatures) affected by the mage's power begin to show signs of Cobalt's presence, with veins of blue appearing in the body, or else leaking or being caked by an azure residue. Clothes that are altered through alchemy may show blue stains, and potions and other consumables will dye the imbiber's mouth blue, even if the substance wasn't originally such.

        That's the Flaw manifestation. As a Burn, it usually occurs when the Alchemist or Craft Worker does work on their body. The previously cosmetic blue stains may become corrosive or numbing. Or else the mage will be afflicted with upset stomachs, and forced heave out vomit stained a stunning blue. The taste, naturally, is metallic.

        Magestuck: The Ascension

            463) Too Subtle [Flaw]: When you spend as much time and energy being as discreet and covert as the Ahl-I-Batin, sometimes it can go too far. When a member of the Subtle Ones derives Paradox from magick to mask their presence or intent, they find themselves becoming TOO subtle for their own good. While the Flaw is manifest, attempts to communicate are twisted into what scholars call "Cryptic Bullshit" (technical term). If the mage meant to speak, write, type, hand sign, or otherwise convey simple statements of fact, they are wrapped up in riddles, ambiguity, and muddled symbolism. If the mage is TRYING to be cryptic, of course, this Flaw doesn't make their job easier. It only kicks in when they are attempting to be clear and transparent. It is especially frustrating - and therefore especially common - when manifested at a time and place where the Batini most needs to be understood.

                    465) Privacy in Sleep [ Paradox Flaw | Mind, Mind and Life ] The affected Mage cannot fall asleep at all if there are other humans in the same room that she is in. She has be alone in a room or be in the outdoors to be able to fall asleep. If she is in the outdoors, then the presence of other persons does not prevent the affected Mage from falling asleep.
                    Vampires, Garou, Kuei-Jin, Changelings, Mummies and Wraiths ( including Risen Wraiths ) do count as humans in regards to this Paradox Flaw. Demon Fallens don't count as humans in regards to this Paradox Flaw. Anyone present in the Umbra is not considered to be in the same room as the affected Mage in regards to this Paradox Flaw.
                    A Mage using Correspondence 4 to co-locate herself ( polyappearence ) into the same room as the affected Mage does not count as a person present in the room in regards to this Paradox Flaw.

                    The affected Mage still feels tiredness. She can partly recuperate through resting and relaxing, as normal ; but this of course will be harder as more time passes since the Mage's last sleep. A room is defined as an area in an artificially-built structure/building that has walls and a roof ; a room is clearly separated through some means from other areas in a structure/building.

                        466) "My Precious" [ Paradox flow, Prime ]
                        This flaw affects mages who likes to hunt for Quintessence, and use it to purge themselves from paradox.

                        This flaw makes the victim hungry for Quintessence. They will need it. This will be their obsession. Technically, the mage will become really addicted. Without being full of quintessence, the mage will be unable to think of something else without a willpower roll.
                        During this flaw, the mage is so focused by external power sources that they wont be able to focus enough on her avatar to naturally gain dots of quintessence.
                        Victims of this flaw will do everything in their power to gain energy. They will empty their batteries, destroy their wonders, extract the prime out of their pets, and when nothing else is there, they may try to extract it from their own patterns ..

                        The only way to get past this addiction is to live with zero dot of quintessence for a month.

                            467) The Shine and Glow of Vanity [ Paradox Flaw | Prime and Mind, Prime and Matter, Prime and Correspondence ] This Paradox Flaw tends to affect Mages who often apply Conjunctional Prime 2 stabilization to Magickal Effects when it is not needed at all. "Just in case" is often the supposed reason and a convenient excuse to cast more of the pleasant and vaunted Prime Magick, and observe and/or feel - just for the sake of it - the particular way(s) it interacts with other kinds of Magick. Another reason to do so is to show off to other persons.

                            If the affected Mage has any Quintessence stored in herself or in her Avatar, then this Quintessence will become active ( often when the Mage touches or starts holding a mundane item), and then will be expanded into any mundane ( non-magical ) items that the Mage touches or holds with her hands - making the item shine and glow brightly ; the color(s) of this shining are usually related to the affected Mage's outlook, Paradigm and/or Resonance.
                            Mundane humans tend to be unaware, ignorant and/or dismissive of this shining and glow. Large items - larger than the average book - will only have parts of them glowing.
                            If the affected Mage will have stored Quintessence in herself or in her Avatar, but won't be holding any items for ten to thirty minutes, then the Quintessence will become active and be expanded into making parts of her clothes shine ; if the Mage wears none or very little clothes, then the Quintessence will be expanded into making the hair on her head glow ; if the Mage wears none or very little clothes and is bald, then the top of her head will be shining and/or glowing.
                            The more Quintessence is expanded due to this Paradox Flaw then the brighter, more varied and more vibrant the shine and glow.

                                468) The Wall Knight [Spirit]: Not all Paradox spirits need to go on the offensive to make the lives of mages miserable. The Wall Knight was first documented during the Renaissance, as a heavily armored humanoid, carrying a shield nearly as tall as a man. Variants of this spirit have appeared as far east as imperial China, as far south as the prairies of Zululand, and as far forward as modern urban warzones. Wherever humans have employed shields, the Wall Knight appears, bearing ones made of anything from wood to metal to animal hide to even bulletproof plastics.

                                The Wall Knight is attracted by magick intended to defend, or to restrict movements. His mission is to do the same thing. His enormous shield allows him to block people's movements, which he employs in tight corridors and narrow walkways. One would be surprised how infuriating this can be, when a mage desperately needs to pass, and the Wall Knight does not acquiesce to this wish. The Wall Knight will also appear in battle, and provide protection for the mage's enemies, allowing them to attack from generous, mobile cover, or else to use the knight to block pursuit. Given the Wall Knight's great protection (6 dots in armor total, at least) and his willingness to get in the mage's way, it can be more of a challenge to get through his as it is to take down the mage's actual adversary. When the backlash is severe, the Wall Knight will go on the offense to a degree, using his broad shield to shove the mage, either pinning her against walls or knocking her off ledges or into other environmental hazards. Usually, though, the spirit is just there to be an obstacle and hindrance, rather than a threat.

                                    469) The Kill Circle [Spirit]: One of a very rare subclass of Paradox phenomenon, wherein two mages doing magick in close proximity to each other contribute to a common backlash. Appropriately enough, this one occurs in response to mages locked in combat.

                                    Darkness falls upon the two mages, and from that darkness arise a hoard of monsters. Fur-covered, horn-headed, clawed fingers, red-eyed, and with gaping, toothy maws. Fearsome creatures, to be sure. But not mindless. Indeed, they hoot and holler and cheer, and shout a simple phrase, over and over:

                                    "FIGHT! FIGHT! FIGHT!"

                                    These monsters form a circle around the two mages, encouraging them to continue their battle. No one is leaving the circle until the fight concludes, one way or another. No, not even through teleportation or flight or turning into some incorporeal substance. The monsters have countermagic with which to counter attempts to escape the Kill Circle.

                                    Note, though, that the countermagic ONLY works to prevent the mages from escaping. The mages could always use their magick to attack the creatures directly. Given their number and ferocity, however, it's a much harder fight. Far more trouble than squaring off against their original opponent. And if the mages were fighting in the first place, they probably aren't friendly to begin with.

                                    If the mages proceed to settle accounts with each other, they have a circular space in which to do so. Careful, though, as the monsters at the edge of the field will slash and grab at anyone within arm's reach. They'll remain where they are for a time, but may begin shrinking the circle, if they feel the fight is taking too long, or if the mages think they'll just wait out the spirits. These monsters came for blood. Whether a mage falls in battle or gets pushed or backed into range of the monsters, they creatures will seize and drag them into the darkness. Perhaps the mage is doomed to be killed and devoured, or perhaps they will be extracted to the monsters' Paradox Realm, where they will be held captive. In any case, both the monsters and the darkness recede, leaving the victorious mage alive.

                                    If the mages DO decide to take the fight to the monsters, they aren't AS tough as they seem. True to form, they are beasts, and beasts are cowards and survival-focused first and foremost. If a dozen perish or become bloodied, the rest may scatter, banishing their troupe. That is, unless the combined Paradox backlash between the two mages was particularly severe. In that case, they won't leave until one of the mages becomes their meal. Maybe even both, if they are incensed enough.

                                            471) Face It [Burn]: A smaller version of the mage's face appears somewhere on her body. The mage is wracked with nausea and micro-convulsions, until the miniature face starts puking blood. The fluid flies from the face's mouth, nose, and eye sockets, making retching noises as it does. All of this blood comes from the mage's own supply, of course, leading to health loss. The face will groan and gnash its teeth, both before and after the blood-letting. After that, the face remains as a Flaw for a while before it fades, although incurring more Paradox may cause it to become sick again.


                                            472) Assassin Come Due [Burn]: Often kin to the paranoid world of spies and double-crosses, members of the New World Order never know when they'll suddenly find a bullet in their back. With this backlash, the agent is minding their own business, when suddenly a shot rings out and pain flies through their body. Despite their honed instincts and senses, the agent finds to their horror that they didn't see the attack coming. Looking in the direction of the shot will reveal only a momentary glimpse of their enigmatic gunman, often leaned in from around a corner, scoping from a rooftop, or peeking out of a raised manhole cover. The attacker is nondescript, wearing gloves, sunglasses, a trenchcoat, and a hat turned low. The exact appearance varies but the effect is the same. It's just this anonymous assailant, and their smoking gun.

                                            And just like that, allowing only the briefest glance, they slip out of view. Sometimes, the figure may make some enigmatic remark, like, "Mister X sends his regards", or "Glory to the Exchange". As if they were delivering a concise reminder of conspiracies and layers of plots they agent cannot possibly be privy to, but which the agent knows are always, eternally, hiding just around the corner. With the business of inflicting damage on the agent done, the figure departs, leaving no evidence of their presence (not even a spent shell casing or whiff of gunsmoke), and giving no means of following their trail. They have disappeared, because Reality itself conjured them into being for that singular moment of ballistic retribution.

                                            Many NWO members spiraled into Quiet, their paranoia driving them up a wall over the enemy that doesn't exist. For their enemy is their own Paradoxical actions, which they externalized when they abdicated responsibility for their own magick.

                                                    474) Red Trail [Flaw]: When magick is used to kill, the perpetrator can end up leaving evidence of their wrong-doing everywhere they go. They track bloody footprints wherever they go, regardless of whether they stepped in blood or how long ago the kill was. And their touch can leave bloody hand prints, regardless of how clean they hands seem to be. These prints tend to remain scarlet long after regular blood should have dried into a ruddy brown. For as long as the Paradox's duration lasts, the mage will leave a trail, leading from the victim to the mage.


                                                    475) The Burning World [Quiet]: Fire is a common tool among mages; flame magic specialists among Forces users are a dime a dozen, as are mage groups whose whole Paradigms include fire as a major element of their ethos and cosmology. For this reason, it's not uncommon for Paradox to take the form of flame, and even to have it invade their minds.

                                                    A mage with this Quiet begins to see, hear, feel, taste, and smell evidence of fire, everywhere. At first they sniff the stink of smoke, and spy small fires where they ought not to be. They hear phantom crackles just beyond walls, and doorknobs feel hot to the touch, as if infernos blaze beyond the closed threshold. Wisps of black smoke issue from under doors or from holes, and the character feels increasingly warm, regardless of the weather. As their Quiet worsens, the fires all around them grow more intense. Walls appear to be laced with patches of flame, and a layer of black smoke coats the ceilings. The mage may be wracked with coughing fits, and their skin is always coated with a light sheen of sweat.

                                                    At its worst manifestations, the mage lives in a world constantly ablaze. Every building they walk through is an inferno, and their own clothes have been blackened by soot. Strangely, the mage's affinity for fire means the flames never burn or consume anything. Structures remain standing and clothes remained intact. Other people might be burned by the heat, though, if they stray too close to the walls. For everyone else, the manifested Quiet feels like an intolerable hell. For the mage, they are hardly bothered. For them, it's all normal.

                                                    Magestuck: The Ascension

                                                        476) Those Unkind Streets and Roads [ Paradox Flaw | Prime, Prime and Matter, Life and Matter, Matter ] The affected Mage feels readily tired ( as if at the end of a long day of being active and having no rest at all during it ) every time she or he is on a street or a road paved by means that mundane humans use - be it bricks, stone blocks, or concrete. This Paradox Flaw affects the Mage when she or he is on a street or a road and outside of a building or a vehicle ; unroofed vehicles do fully count as vehicles for the purpose of a Mage not being affected by this Paradox Flaw.
                                                        This tiredness is one that can somewhat be dealt with - the affected Mage can try to change her or his attitude, consume or use substances ( including magical ones ) which remove tiredness or give more energy, use their willpower or mental techniques to ignore the tiredness etc. . But these are short-term solutions at most, working for ten to fifteen minutes at best ( though there might be exceptions to this amount of time ) ; and the affected Mage is unable to use a given solution again for a day, to several days, to possibly tens of days afterwards if she or he again becomes affected by this Paradox Flaw.

                                                            477) Gleam of Exchange [ Paradox Flaw | Prime and Mind, Prime and Matter, Prime and Correspondence, Prime, Matter and Forces, Matter and Correspondence, Matter ] The affected Mage's eyes shine very brightly - though not in an obvious way ; many persons would just note that the affected Mage has beautiful eyes - for a few seconds every time the affected Mage successfully buys or sells anything ( completes the transaction ) in an exchange which involves currency. The color of the shinning can be anything, from a deep blue glow to a subtle green-yellow brightness ; the color will usually be somehow related to - as per the affected Mage's outlook(s) and/or Paradigm(s) - the Paradox Backlash that resulted in this Paradox Flaw.

                                                            The color of the shinning of the Mage's eyes will not change ; unless the Mage is affected again by this Paradox Flaw, due to for example suffering a Paradox Backlash.
