                  The basic reason they don't is that Pentex is a major global actor that they can't destroy without calling significant attention to their own dealings - made willingly and knowingly - with a force that is, at its core, inherently opposed to the end goals of the Technocracy. They have over 134 offices worldwide, possess major market shares in pharmaceuticals, weapons manufacturing, private military companies, waste disposal, mining, energy generation, oil extraction and refining, food processing, logistics and distribution, mass market media shares, fishery operations, cosmetics, raw plastics and paper production, tobacco products, global stocks and real-estate investment, and healthcare. Through Tellus and Sunburst, they have major shares of the computing world. Nastrum brings a major market share in the aerospace and military sector. Their sticky fingers even go into education and childcare.

                  Endron alone has seven nuclear plants in America with 5 more slated to be built as of 2000 - and if on schedule, now built and operational. They control the most important oil pipeline in the entire Middle East, the Aswan-61 pipeline. Magadon has over 60 production plants and a dozen research plants worldwide, and a 22.5% market share in 1998. In 1999 they made 24.8 billion dollars gross, and in 2000, posted a net profit of 8.115 billion. O'Tolley's has a 25+% market share in the CONUS - for reference, McDonalds, that veritable juggernaut of Greasy Meat-Based Capitalism, only has a 19% market share IRL. In 2010, an Endron rig spilled 250 million gallons of oil - and that barely put a dent in the company's pockets. Deepwater Horizon was 210 million.

                  That's why the Syndicate and the Technocracy doesn't 'crush' Pentex. You don't crush Pentex without also crushing the entire Western economy with it. It isn't some pesky little tick the Technocracy can pull out - it's a tapeworm wound so deep through the entire Western economic model that if you try and brute force it out, you're going to quickly find yourself elbow-deep in blood wondering why everything is screaming. Not only would the Syndicate taking the action expose them to massive problems with the rest of the Technocracy, the Technocracy simply can't crush Pentex without prolonged, difficult struggle. And before anyone goes 'well they're magic!', the global financial recession happened when the Syndicate got too 'experimental' - and Pentex have their own nephandi to call on.

                  Pentex is the ugliest side of the concept of the corporation magnified to an extreme. It doesn't just 'go away'.

                                      They have the help of true magic, the help of spiritual entities, the help of hedge mages, and most terrifyingly of all: they have the help of very good lawyers. That Pentex is so vast isn't actually unbelievable - it's exaggerated, but it's in the same league as real-life 'megacorporations' like Berkshire Hathaway, ExxonMobil, General Electric or Morgan Chase. Companies already become unimaginably vast and powerful without recourse to magic IRL - why should they not do the same in the World of Darkness? Nestle, PepsiCo and Kraft together more or less completely control the processed food game, and actively influence global geopolitics and economic policy of entire nations. The largest publicly traded company in the world, ICBC, has assets equal to a full 30th of the world's total GDP and a GDP greater than all but the top three GDPs in the entire world. Corporations already grow into monstrosities without the aid of true magic. True magic and literal deals-with-the-devil just make it easier.

                                      Additionally, you don't have to be bigger than the Syndicate to pose a problem. All you have to do is comprise a significant portion of the world's total trade to make it unfeasible to simply eradicate you in a single fell swoop. Add to that the part where it isn't in the interests of the Syndicate to take such drastic action lest it be revealed to the rest of the Technocracy what they were getting up to with the SPD (which helped Pentex grow, to boot) and you have a recipe for a company that is more or less untouchable by the Syndicate without unacceptable collateral damage to the Convention itself and to the broader Western world.

                                      In a pure mage game, the Syndicate is going to be a more powerful force. In a werewolf game, Pentex will be. In a combined view, it's up for grabs, but Pentex is still a major player anyway you slice it if you involve it in a chronicle. It cannot be eradicated quickly without causing massive shocks through the economy - using magic to do it would result in another 2006 Incident and it's too big to eradicate fiscally - or implicating the Syndicate in the eyes of the other Conventions. The only way for them to approach it and not ultimately lose (even if they destroy them wholesale and it isn't noticed, the economic collapse it would create will not earn the Syndicate any goodwill and they're already in a bad political position within the Technocratic Union after the 2006 Incident) is to piece by piece disassemble them, and that's a game Pentex can play very well.

                                                  The problem with approaching Pentex like this is that corruption (whether extreme or relatively minor) is so endemic through middle to upper management that you can't just decapitate the Board and say 'job's done, let's go home'. Any viable replacement from within the company is likely to either already be a Cultist/Fomori/Etc or to rapidly become one. You'd need to fundamentally alter the corporate culture, and the only ways to do that are either going to require the aid of the rest of the Technocracy - thus exposing the Syndicate to scrutiny for their dealings - or be slow and subtle. Unfortunately, slow and subtle is also how Pentex works. Most of their subsidiaries aren't even openly linked to the company, but are held by shell companies that own shell companies that own shell companies that comprise major shareholders of a subsidiary, and while mages should be able to get by that much easier than mortals or garou, it isn't guaranteed. This is without the argument that Pentex itself has become an incarnation of the Urge Wyrm of Greed, which would make any such effort utterly pointless as the organization in and of itself is thus a corruptive influence on the world.

                                                  It's why Pentex makes a great adversary. You can't just chop some heads off and call it a day. You've got to confront the reality of an enemy that, at the end of the day, cannot be confronted with brute force alone, whether that force is a klaive or magic. It's the sort of enemy that takes years of hard work at the highest levels to rip out root and stem - and that's without considering the strong probability of contingency plans for if active hostilities begin, like unleashing the fomori worldwide, triggering meltdowns and oil leaks, burning down the Amazon, etc. Pentex's end-goal is to destroy the world on its own terms - if it can't set its own schedule, I don't think it'll just lie down and die. You'd have to fight it very carefully to avoid them poisoning the earth on the way out, which again makes them a great adversary.

                                                  Assassin's Creed had a great line about Abstergo, and it goes for Pentex too. "The average American household contains three dozen Abstergo-owned products at any given moment. If you tried to purge them from your life completely, it would be a full-time job." Just sub-in Pentex for Abstergo.

                                                          There's a difference between people in an organization knowing, and the organization itself knowing. Its almost frightening how compartmentalized an organization (of any size) can be, particularly when the higher-ups don't want to deal with a particular problem at the moment. Memos get shoved to the bottom of the pile, in-person reports get disqualified ("its not really that bad, is it") or rationalized away ("they're just trying to get more funding") . . . If the boss doesn't want to hear it, sooner or later, you stop telling them.

                                                          Which, incidentally, seems to have directly led to the revolt during World War II. The higher-ups in the Technocracy were so ecstatic that they were on the brink of total world domination, they didn't want to hear about any allegations of Nephandi corruption. It got to the point that the field agents, who were staring at the evidence every day, finally broke down and cut a deal with the Traditions to fix the problem.

                                                          So there are almost certainly plenty of Technocrats who know about Pentex - its just they're not in a position to act on that knowledge. But I suspect that if you started digging through Technocratic after-action reports, you'd find a surprising number of "accidental" shootings of Pentex agents, "collateral damage" that wipes out entire Pentex facilities . . .
