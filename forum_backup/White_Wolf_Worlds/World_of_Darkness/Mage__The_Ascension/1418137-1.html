    Originally posted by HorizonParty2021 View Post
    As a storyteller, would this work in your chronicle?
    To clarify the Meaning of the question, it does not exclude storytellers who use their own homebrew or interpret Magick rules less strictly. There are a significant number of storytellers with homebrew whose ideas are represented in the other posts on this forum.

    The Intention of asking the question this is to obtain a broad sample from storytellers based on how they actually play. It's obviously important to engage with feedback with STs who follow RAW more strictly, because it ensures that the greatest number of STs and players would find the story believable.

    There are only two homebrew items that I have mentioned. Alternative Conjunctional Magick does not change the question, because it is only my character who is described building the quarrying facility.

    Alternative Paradox was an idea that I had, but was not forced to develop in a hurry, because I properly conveyed to my players the dangers of doing vulgar Magick, not only minor backlash, but its potential to attract the attention of the Technocracy. They avoid doing vulgar magick enough that I can use the old paradox flaws and "escape from this micro-realm" diversions without running out of ideas.

    When discussing RAW, our discussion skipped over something.

    Originally posted by HorizonParty2021 View Post
    How Do You Do That, p45, right column, under heading: Conjuring Material Forms,
    "For obvious reasons, this sort of thing tends to be vulgar Magick, although a sufficient level of concealment can make it seem coincidental." It then refers to the Axis of Coincidence chart M20,p533-534, which emphasizes Hypothetical Average Bystander (HAB) and Process Based Determinism (PBD). What would the average person believe? How do you do it?
    The direct quote from How Do You Do That, published more recently than M20 Core, refers to a sufficient level of concealment. Before we even get to the Axis of Coincidence, "sufficient level" has to be examined. There are two things being proposed, drawers versus an entire infrastructure building intended to make the production pass under the masses' incredulity.

    The Mage who clears out his garage and gets some storage cabinets from Walmart is supposedly better protected from paradox, because the HAB can't squeeze himself inside the drawers. If the drawers belong to the Mage and he's the only one who uses the garage, the HAB knows he didn't put anything in the drawers, because he follows the Mage at all times. Is "HAB" a part time position, filled by different HABs working in shifts? Can a Mage do vulgar magick in the bathroom? If not, how "Average" is this bystander, psychologically speaking?

    The Consensus established by the Technocracy assumes that the survivability nearly eight billion Sleepers depends on increasing production. The Consensus is fueled by Sleepers. Paradox is an enforcement agent that rests on eight billion hungry, greedy Sleepers. Paradox punishes someone who works within the consensus to improve the economy? The Mage's own greed is not a sin in the eyes of Paradox. The Market Consensus rests on the Syndicate's ideas about what is good for the masses.

    Consider an opposite scenario. A Hit Mark, Biomechanic Mage attacks a crowd of Sleepers with vulgar Hyper-tech Magick (They are activists whose protest threatens a construct's operation, let's say, and he's in Jhor). In this instance, any Mage in the area gets a temporary reprieve, allowing him to use vulgar Magick only against the attacker to stop the massacre. Hostile Umbrood busting the Gauntlet - same thing. Can we not draw conclusions about when Paradox punishes the Mage from the instances in which it allows vulgar magick?

    I am incorporating ideas presented in the replies into my chronicle. I think the questions I just posed are worth reflection for anyone interested in improving player experience. Giving players a chance (as well as structured rules) is what brings people back to the table - or the podcast.

        The spirit of the theory it's to provide a more concrete alternative for the very vague question of Mage that's the RAW way to determine (from 1e to M20) if something it's Vulgar. That question is: Is this feat something that would be regarded as impossible?

        HAB interprets this question as "it's Vulgar if your mage it's doing something that would be regarded as impossible by an average person that's watching".

        That isn't propper to follow you into the bathroom doesn't enter into the equation. HAP it's allways watching you. The difference from the more strict observer it's just that, as an observer, has the perspective of a bystander that doesn't interact with the scene, nor possess superhuman senses.

        As a "hipotetical", the observer it's always in the scene unless it would be impossible for an average human. But as a bystander, the observer doesn't have a particularly advantageous view of the scene. That's why it's said that it can't see what do you have in your pockets, it doesn't have X-Ray vision, and can't open your pockets to see what's there

        Again: HAB it's not a person, it doesn't have a mind, nor occupies any space. HAB it's a question: "what if someone was watching me?".
        That's what the observer is.

            That being said: The fact that the presented """system?""" it's way too vague (and, yes, prone to abuse) doesn't just disappear, I agree with you.
            If, as a player, I wanted to use this feedback loop, my character would also have Scientific Mystic/Technogasi merit and lots of occult, science, esoterica, cosmology. He would involve Mages with different paradigms, having each play a small role in the exchange without telling them the totality of what he was doing. That way, none of them have any doubt in what they are doing.

            There is even a way in which this could be included in a chronicle, as a Storyteller, without it being a consequence-free endeavor. The Syndicate's Enforcement methodology has a subgroup called InSpectres, who are tasked with infiltrating Chantries. So, a Syndicate spy with all the merits and abilities from the first example has a Tradition identity crafted for him. He approaches individual Mages with different paradigms and involves them in exchanges that facilitate the feedback loop. When the targets compare stories, they begin to figure out how their differential value of materials is being exploited. If they continue to participate after knowing the feedback loop is happening, it can have a variety of consequences.

            It may undermine each mage's own paradigm, making him susceptible to recruitment by outside Technocrats. The spy relays this information back to his construct. They start to develop Quiet. The quintessence each gained over time is suddenly all drained away from them at an inopportune moment.
