    Seances, Ouija Boards, and Paradox

    12-05-2024, 10:03 PM
    A situation recently came up in game which I'm having trouble finding a good example of in the written material.

    If a mage were to attract the attention of a spirit for purposes of communication, using some form of traditional tool such as a seance or Ouija board, does consensus consider the mage "responsible" if said spirit gets it into mind to perpetrate antics which would be vulgar for the mage to have performed themselves? I'm talking about things which couldn't easily be written off as the tricks of a professional sleeper medium.

    I'm of two minds about this. On one hand, the mage isn't meddling with the tapestry to vulgar effect themselves. On the other, there is the concept of getting burned by a fire you started, given that the spirit wouldn't have been inspired to supernatural tomfoolery otherwise (or at least not this particular instance of tomfoolery.)

                                                  In this particular instance, the mage was asked to help her aunt a bit with a mildly annoying poltergeist, who had previously been reasonably subtle as far as hauntings go. However, the wraith was taken by surprise when confronted by someone with abilities it had not experienced previously and became unhinged (with some inspiration from its shadow.) It then proceeded to unleash a good bit of Pandemonium which went well beyond the pale as suspension of disbelief goes. At that point I was prompted to wonder if consensus might engage in a bit of finger wagging, given that there was an active Spirit effect involved in the whole incident. However, I suppose being forced to deal with the aftermath of it was punishment enough for a bit amateur spiritualism gone very wrong.
