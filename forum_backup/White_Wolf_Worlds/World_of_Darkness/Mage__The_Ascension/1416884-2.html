    Originally posted by Aleph View Post

    You're not going to defuse this, though, by refusing to place jews in any positions of power within your fiction. On the contrary, one risks to generate a mystification of the subject, if it becomes tabu. Wouldn't be a much better service to say that A jew that happens to be powerful, isn't necessarily evil?, facing the stereotype head on by depicting jews in a different ligth instead of refusing to depict them at all?

    To say that the Board of the Syndicate "it's obiously made of Jews" would be a fascist caricature. To make a Jew golem, not so much. The nuances here being: A) It's ItX. They're not the secret bankers of anyone - they're just the secret scientists B) It's A Technocrat, that just happens to be A jew, not "jews" - the Lions of Zion are still enemies, and not the secret masters of the Technocracy. C) Nobody was saying that the golem it's the de-facto leader of ItX, that would be taking the thunder from Kepler - Inner Circle > Control

    And even then, saying that a jew *can't possibly* be on the inner circle out of fear it would be regarded as a fascist caricature...I would think that should be avoidable by *not* making the character a vulgar caricature - you're not contractualy obligated to pay homage to ALL fascist expectations. Art allows us to re-define stuff: Orcs were a racist stereotype, but that doesn't mean one should forbbide them. Orcs are regarded as cool nowdays, and often aren't the bad guys.
    I would take things a step further. If you are playing Traditions, your characters are organized into a Cabal. The very name "cabal" implies clandestine behavior. Being clandestine is a survival necessity in a world dominated by the Technocracy. Trying to get some institutional influence is on the priority list of a Cabal, because if they want to do Ritual Magick and score successes, having large numbers of Acolytes is highly recommended.

    Let's say you have a Jewish character, a Tradition mage, and his Tradition is Not Celestial Chorus. Let's make him a Virtual Adept. The Cabal has formed a tech startup company that makes VR games for the psychedelic community. Somehow, the company markets to this audience while maintaining deniability on the issue of hallucinogens, which are beginning to be recognized as having therapeutic uses. It's a corporate conspiracy and he's a company executive. They are trying to put micro-dosing gamers in contact with Umbrood to trigger an awakening among sleepers. The Umbrood are related to Native American mythology and have nothing to do with Judaism.

    A Jewish character is doing something clandestine, in cooperation with other characters who are not Jewish, to achieve a goal that is not related to his Jewishness. When he is not devoting his time to the goals of his Cabal, he does culturally Jewish things, seen in a separate context from the intrigue plot.

    If this were a Netflix show, it would be up to the writers to properly establish the context, the premise of WoD (a world of conspiracies), before the tech startup plot is introduced.

    A Jewish banker and another Jewish banker hatching a plot is objectively objectionable (a little word salad).

    If you want to look at a real example of conspirators self-identified as Christians, check out, "The Family: The Secret Fundamentalism at the Heart of American Power, 2008, Jeff Sharlet". He did interviews still on YouTube about the book.




    Thank you for passing time with me in conversation. My Hacks.

                Originally posted by Eldagusto View Post
                So how about some different ideas or even fleshed out characters that make up Control. I liked how some hints of Inhuman forces are taking over the Conventions like The Computer In Autochthonia, but this usually is depicted outside of Control. But it would be funny if Control was just five different inhuman god things standing on each other’s shoulders wearing a human trench coat and maybe they don’t even know about each other.

                There is the idea Control is heavily if not completely compromised by the Nephandi.

                And then there is the fact that Conventions constantly reimagined themselves so Archscientists In Control would kind of be the only remnants of their older conventions, as well as the idea of many being archaic, so the irony of the most advanced Scientists are fellas that would use clockwork nano-machines made of wood and glass.

                But how many members would you imagine are in Control? Have you made actual characters for Control in your games?

                I like the idea after the dimensional anomaly that banished masters and above beyond the gauntlet the techies lost most of their control over individual members so while they are organized they are much less supervised with much less oversight. They still have a hierarchy and duty, kind of like the Garou Nation, but they have more freedom to achieve their goals as they wish it’s just you are cut off from most resources unless you tie yourself deeper into the organization and duties.
                The way I would handle the question of Nephandi, is to first imagine who each member was before any got tricked into that Caul business. You want them to be part of a story with conflicts. NWO vs Syndicate is the one everyone thinks of. Let's imagine something more original.

                They are in a place removed from the Earth and are observing it from a distance. The agenda is both to prevent beliefs from spreading that undermine the Consensus, pushing it backwards AND to stop hyper-tech from coming out that would cause paradox-like effects or global backlash, "market corrections". There is a schedule that moves at a safe, specified pace, the Time Table.

                One of them is the most vocal dictator when it comes to enforcing this Timetable. There might be another who is thinking, "You are hobbling us.", "us" being their own convention and many lower level transhumanists throughout the Union.

                I could picture a power triangle between the Void Engineer, the Iterator and the Syndicate rep.

                The Void Engineer is the woman on the cover of the first VE book, sitting on sailing ship. She gets a lot of information from Earth Frontiers Division and she is worried about global warming. As a Dimension Science Master, she is familiar with the Triat, consisting of the Wyrm, Weaver and Wild. She knows that Gaian Umbrood must play an important role in combatting the Wyrm. She has difficulty convincing her fellow technocrats that there is a Spirit of the Earth. She often listens to the song, "Carbon" by VNV Nation. This annoys the Syndicate rep who is quite proud of the accomplishments of the petrochemical industry.

                The Iteration-X cyborg man is ready to terraform the planet, because he thinks that we can just adapt humanity to a completely different biosphere through cybernetics. If necessary, robots could store human embryos in a facility like that depicted in, "I Am Mother.". He wants to desalinate the Oceans completely to ensure there is enough fresh water for 11+ billion people.

                The Syndicate rep is a man from Alberta, Canada. He believes that large parts of the Earth can be sacrificed for economic progress, as has been done with the oil sands project, and that technology will solve the problem later. To sustain the global economic bubble, he realizes that humanity needs to get into asteroid mining, building geosynchronous high-orbit solar collectors and otherwise building space infrastructure. He needs the Void Engineer, but is not down with her Gaian-agenda.

                Take any one of these and you can add them to a power triangle with the remaining two conventions. Determine who is Nephandi AFTER fleshing them out as they were before getting duped. Then add Nephandic personal horror to their story.

                                                    However, I like the idea of longstanding Chantries. The Avatar Storm wipes out everything the traditions accomplished and forces them to be second-rate technomancers by adaptation to the Technocratic Consensus.                                                     The Avatar Storm so diminishes the Traditions that the idea of The Crafts being of any interest, as a third group, has even less appeal.                                                         To be fair, most of the Horizon Realms fell before the Avatar Storm. The Storm merely finished off the last of them. And it's arguable that if they hadn't been weakened prior to the Storm, they might have been able to weather it.

                                                        As for the Void Engineers: frankly, they were the faction most devastated by the Storm; not even the Dreamspeakers suffered as badly as they did (largely because the Dreamspeakers are less about crossing over to the Umbra than they are about communing with spirits).

                                                        And far from devastating the Traditions, the Avatar Storm likely saved them: it forced the Technocracy to back off and take stock of what they were doing, giving the Traditions a chance to recover. Sure, the Traditions lost their Archmasters; but the Traditions had never relied on their Archmasters the way the Technocracy had relied on Control.

                                                        In terms of Void Engineers vs. Umbral Lords, I definitely agree that the relationship tends to be antagonistic. I tend to look at James Cameron's Avatar as an example of a crew of Void Engineers laying siege to an Umbral Realm in order to mine it for Quintessence (excuse me; “Unobtainium”). Most of the peaceful explorers in their ranks left when the Avatar Storm hit (the culmination to the persistent rumors that the Convention was getting ready to abandon the Union), and the bulk of the ones who remained were the more militant Earth First types, reminiscent of colonialism when they're not focused on defending the Earth from alien invaders.

                                                        The Traditions got taken down a peg by the Avatar Storm; the Conventions got taken down two pegs, except for the Void Engineers who were nearly destroyed.

                                                        And the Crafts were hurt even less by the Storm than the Traditions were: the bulk of them didn't have any Archmasters in the Umbral Realms in the first place, so they didn't lose anything. Where they did get hammered was when the Union lashed out at them, forcing them into hiding (and in the case of the Kopa Loei, being driven so far into hiding that everyone thought that they had been wiped out). And had that not happened, the Disparate Alliance would never have happened either. But that's all separate from the Storm.

                                                        As for the Nephandi, the idea as I see it is that they've been infiltrating everyone: Technocracy, Traditions, and even the Disparate Alliance (which was founded specifically to fight the Nephandic infiltration). It's what they do: infiltrate and subvert. I tend not to go with the idea that the Technephandi run the Union; but that's also part of the reason why I prefer the post-Storm notion where the Union got cut off from Control long enough to establish an independent leadership of their own that wasn't particularly receptive to Control coming back and trying to reassert itself. Control may have succumbed to Nephandic infiltration; but the Earthside Union (including the rechristened Void Sentinels) hasn't, yet. The Nephandi are working hard to subvert them, too; but they're not there yet, just as they haven't successfully subverted the Traditions. Yet.

                                                            Incidentally, I'm personally rather fond of playing Enlightened Scientists (not technocrats, thank you very much; the arrogance of scientists, engineers, and academics thinking they know how to run your life better than you do is a major turn-off for me); but I wouldn't play the Traditions as second-rate technomancers, even (no, especially) in the wake of the Avatar Storm.

                                                            The Avatar Storm didn't force the Traditions to adapt to the Technocratic Consensus, unless you consider the harsher Paradox rules of MRev to be an effect of the Avatar Storm. But even there, it's not so much that they had to align their views to the Technocratic Consensus as it is that they actually had to look at the Consensus that they, as a whole, had gotten into the habit of ignoring; and when they looked at it, they decided to do something about what they didn't like. In my head-canon, the rise of the Avatar Storm was followed by efforts by the Traditions to enact paradigm shifts in their local Reality Zones, leading to the Technocracy losing a lot of ground that it had been taking for granted. The Consensus is not, and never has been, predominantly Technocratic; that the Technocracy ever thought so is just another mark of their hubris. But after the Reckoning, it gradually became even less so as the Traditions managed to strengthen Mythic Threads in those Reality Zones that remained Technocratic, and even managed to flip a few of them into being Localized Zones. Riffing off of something you've mentioned elsewhere, I tend to think that pre-Storm mages were more likely to unthinkingly accept Technocrat-friendly Paradigms like materialism than more recent mages are: when you go back to basics and reexamine your assumptions, you're more likely to uncover the ones you've just been taking for granted.
