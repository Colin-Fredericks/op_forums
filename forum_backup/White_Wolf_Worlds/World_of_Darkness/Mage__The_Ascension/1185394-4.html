    Originally posted by Saikou View Post

    I dunno, I actually really like the avatar storm. It made the umbra much more mysterious by adding an extra layer of danger to its traversal. It also gave the characters a real incentive to stay on Earth, or at least primarily operate on Earth, rather than just mill about on Mars forever because "look ma! no paradox!!".
    Well, I always saw the Avatar Storm as White Wolf's attempt to enforce themes of personal horror in a game that was never intended to be a game of personal horror in the first place. Basically, around the time of Revised Edition, a lot of the old guard had left and there was a new generation of developers who believed that there was "One True Way" to play WoD and they used the metaplot as an attempt to police the setting and enforce their vision of thematic purity. This is especially true of Vampire and Mage.

    Mage was never about angst or personal horror, it was always a game of pulp adventure and rebelling against The Man. Revised sort of lost sight of that. Now others like Revised, and that is fine with me. To each their own, but I always saw the Avatar Storm as a massive mistake at best and a giant middle finger to the fans at worst.

    If you like the Avatar Storm, that's cool. But I never cared for it, and in my games it never happened.

                                                I'll admit I'm more of a 2E guy myself, despite coming into the game in the 00s, but in defense of Revised it wasn't a conspiracy against people having bad wrong fun with Mage. I remember coming across a interview with (I think) Achilli, where he said he and other WW Dev's received lots of questions from players at where to begin with Mage. They felt it was too much of a big game and easy to be overwhelmed by the sheer diversity of options. So the goal of the Avatar Storm was to focus the game on its core issues.

                                                However, the edition for me and my group put us off because it wasn't clear about a lot of where it was going. It went from stating the Ascension War is over, but yet the Technocracy was still pushing its concensus, the Mauraders were still a threat, and the surviving Nephandi were still corrupting everything. So basically the war is still going on. There's fannon that says there's a Cold War between the Traditions and the Technocrats, but I never got that impression from any revised books. Plus the 'new' goals of the Traditions doing more personal stuff than attacking constructs or hiding away in horizon realms seemed odd to us as well since that was our impression of what the mages did anyways even from 2E books. I never thought engaging in open combat with Hit Marks and MiBs was the sum total of the war over reality. It was just as much a conflict of spreading ideas and protecting the cultures the Traditions arose from, or with the Technocracy promoting their very controlled version of science and narrow interpretation of pan-humanism over superstions, rogue ideas of technology, and divisive 'primitive' concepts like cultures, countries, and religions.

                                                So in the end I like M20's compromised approach to resolving the edition wars. You can return to the situation of 2E while acknowledging the positive additions Revised brought. It's funny cause that's how we resolved them. We had something like the Avatar Storm happen, but for vastly different reasons and it only like lasted a week.

                                                And the Alliance is about the easiest thing to ignore for now.
