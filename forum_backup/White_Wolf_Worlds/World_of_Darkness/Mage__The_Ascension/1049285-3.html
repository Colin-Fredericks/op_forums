    I think in the sense of a first principle, yes, but there's already a number of objections that have been stated in thread that need to be address in revisions of the starting point at this juncture.
    When I put this in it is going to read "Reality is almost completely malleable but consensus, which is the sum of all belief and expectation. If you can get 10% of the local people to believe something it is possible, 66% and it's likely, 91% and it's certain.Everyone has the same potential impact on the Consensus, though some beliefs are stronger than others."

    I don't think that address the fundamental question of whether the mathematical model is the best model to use. As the mathematical model grows increasingly complex, the question of if it remains the most parsimonious model for this gains in validity.
    I'm not planning on robustly testing every permutation, but the issue of contradictory beliefs doesn't go away just because we drop the math. I'm going to have to tackle that head on,


    First off, let's keep the cat out of this. I know that it's popular, but remember that the cat is a joke about how collapsing quantum wave functions does not apply above the quantum scale. Schrodinger's whole point was that the cat is either alive or dead because cats aren't quantum scale phenomena and the life of the cat is not dependent on the observer in real world physics; just because you don't know which state the cat is in yet doesn't mean it's both.
    I'm well aware it's been massively understood, that's actually why I brought it up. Because his example was misunderstood a significant chunk of the population (possibly even the majority of people who are aware of it) think something can be in two states at once (so long as we don't see it). If real world people are okay with that actually happening, how do we view it as an impossibility in our model?

    Second, we haven't established what happens during any sort of contradiction, we've just established a model that allows contradictions to occur.

    As it stands, it is impossible to have a contradiction without observation, because all contradictions so far are the product of conflict beliefs. Until the principle is further refined into how reality manifests contradictions so that the can exist externally to persons, we can't really say how that plays out.
    If it were possible, I'd love to test if a reality that only exists when seen actually functions. Considering spirits count as observers I don't think that's possible. Another time, perhaps.

    As to "is there a problem?" I'm not sure where the problem is. If the problem is in working out the principle, than yes, there's still a problem because the principle isn't sufficiently advanced to model any of this yet. If the problem is just in the "do unobserved contradictions cause a problem with the goal of a universe superficially the same as our own modern world," then no, those aren't problems.[/quote]

    The latter was my thinking. It's pretty clear it is time to change the principle, I'm just trying to figure out the easiest way to cover the objections so far, and I wanted to make sure I wasn't being arbitrary. I'll try to have a new revision up tomorrow.

    Can you give an example?
    I brought up the Blue/White Dress incident in response to BenjCano's objection earlier. Yes, it's obviously an optical illusion, but if the dress were actually blue to some people and white to others, it would present almost exactly the same way. This kind of thing couldn't happen every day, but my intuition is that strange one-time incidents don't invalidate my model world.

                            So what I'm seeing right now are a couple of significant problems involving contradictions. There's the issue of the same person holding two mutually exclusive beliefs. There is issue of a nigh certainties being in competition with distinct possibilities. There is the issue of drift outside of observation. And there is the issue of very visible strange behavior when half the population says one thing and half of it says another.

                            A lot of this can be handled by introducing some kind of principle of consistency, such that strange behavior either happens rarely enough to be ignored or consistently enough to look normal. That's yet another exception to the current model that belief is reality, so I'm going to propose instead a belief makes reality. That means that once reality is in existence it doesn't need belief to continue, but will solidify and erode in the presence of confirming or contradicting beliefs respectively. This still means a lot of strange things can happen, but existing reality still has a say in outright contradictions.

                            If it helps, my mental model is a freezing sea filled with icebergs of solidified reality. Belief builds up or melts down reality to fit.

                            Ramnesis 6: Reality was created by belief and is almost completely malleable to the sum of all belief and expectation. Belief and expectation strengthen acceptable parts of reality and erode unacceptable parts. The shape reality is pushed towards is known as the Consensus. Things are possible if reality permits/demands they happen or is too weak to stop them. Everyone has the same potential impact on the Consensus, though some beliefs are stronger than others. At some point in the past, certain basic ideas like physical interaction and gravity were entrenched in the Consensus, these are largely self perpetuating. Nothing can change who affects reality or change anyone's personhood, or their mental or spiritual state.

                            How's that for a substantial change?

                                    I think it's going well (consider the nature of my current critiques a good sign for how this seems to be going). That said!

                                    Objections to Ramnesis 6:

                                    The statement of "reality was created by belief," is problematic. It opens a huge box that seems unnecessary. It's like trying to claim that evolution explains the origin of life, when evolution is based on a presumption of life existing with no statement on how it started. This should be rephrased to, "The current state of reality is created by belief..." to avoid complicating the presumptive nature of a subjective reality "starting" somehow. How reality started doesn't matter, only how reality is at any given moment and how it will be taking account the effect of previous moments matters.

                                    "Acceptable" seems a questionable semantic choice. It implies a judgement without any source to be making this judgement. What if this was something more like, "Belief and expectation create and strength coherent parts of reality, and erode incoherent parts." This sets up a lot of the other sections. Strong beliefs are more effective because of their coherence (this applies to Consensus and to things like a mage's Focus and why some paradigms aren't successful: they're incoherent and incapable of a strong impact on reality). Entrenched ideas are empowered because they set up a chain reaction of coherence compared to other options; emergent patterns happen because coherence makes for stronger beliefs.
