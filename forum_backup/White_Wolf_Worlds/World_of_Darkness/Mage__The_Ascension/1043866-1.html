    Ten years is sensible for vampires because they are immortal. A decade is a drop in the bucket for them. Even then, I've read the ten year figure as the maximum in range. Their powers are even more intuitive than a Mage's occult abilities.

                    (Just jumping in -- glanced over responses, but not addressing any particular one, just in the overall...)

                    This all comes back around to the mild dissonance that some players get relative to understanding the broader "fluff" (which is *every bit as important* as the mechanics/crunch... just important in a different way...) behind the dots in question. A newly (A)wakened mage could develop her 6 dots in Spheres (and possibly her 3 dots of Arete) in one fell swoop, with the months/years after that simply reflecting her ability to refine/finesse her powers. Conversely, a more by-the-books, academically-minded (A)wakened mage (such as oft-found in the Order of Hermes or some Etherites) could study for years -- a full BA/MS/PhD battery -- slowly and gradually developing his magic/(A)wakening, rote-by-rote, enchanted item-by-enchanted item. Both are handled on a mechanical level by the same spread of numbers. It's no different in presentational concept to how some people have to study/practice for years to become skilled/nuanced singers while others literally start singing with perfect pitch/expression at age 3. The fluff details are important for that side of the coin, but the mechanical side measures out the same.

                    As far as answering the OP: no, not at all. 6(or7) dots in Spheres isn't too much to start with. It's important to remember that the "starting level" for WoD characters -- and Mages in particular -- has always been predicated on the assumption that the characters in question have sufficient background/experience to be "comfortable" with what they are (in applied concept, if not in specific salient details). That is, they aren't the same as "1st Level DnD" characters -- they have more (B)ackground and presumed (E)xperience (or natural talent that steps in for experience) than 1st Level DnD characters do. You can always scale back to a lower degree for you game so as to reflect an earlier level of development and/or the situation wherein the character(s) literally awoke just that night/a few hours ago... but that's a specifically different kind of game/set of thematics at that point vs. the more "standard" Mage game. (Plus, remember: those 6 or so starting dots act as a balance for most other splats starting with 3 *but* having a number of salient, built-in abilities/powers that mages don't normally have; thus, mages get more dots in Magic(k) to start with. So it really is very balanced, especially as mages -- as written -- start with very small dice pools for their powers/spells compare to most other splats and usually have to prep their magic(k) in advance and/or take their time to do "x". That, again, is more than fair...)

                                        Not to start an "argument", but I've noted in several of Fardn's other posts/threads that he seems to confuse/conflate his own world view (...paradigm, if you will... ) of How Magic(k) Works(tm) with the inherently flexible (especially on a narrative level) qualities that Mage, as a setting/system, has. That is, similar to his other thread assertion about not seeing a Mage (in his games, just to be clear) as having an INT of less than 3, he's confusing/conflating the idea of years of dedication (which can often be the case for many mages, don't get me wrong) as being "mandatory", as such, with raw talent/predisposed ability not being "a thing" in the game/setting. That's not the case, at all. Just as some vampires have been stat'd as waking up with their 3 or so dots after being embraced (or within a matter of days after such, with little or no "instruction") while some other vampires take years to develop even that modicum of ability, some mages must work very hard to develop their talents... while some others quick literally simply Awaken(tm) with said knowledge/power. (This can be for any number of reasons: they had years of previous mundane occult knowledge but no Awakened Magic(k)(tm)/Avatar to make that magic(k) *true* -- their "spells", which they may not even think of as such, become empowered then-and-there and they're out the gate and running. Similarly, a mage with no pre-existing occult understanding/what-have-you could (A)waken and possess powers/understanding that, to their mundane mind, is strange and fantastic... yet old hat to the (likely powerful) Avatar/god-shard that is a part of their soul (as was the example for a Prime:3, et al, mage of another player from a few years ago: he simply *knew*, intuitively, via his previous incarnation(s) "how Prime(tm) works"... even though he had no idea what "Prime" was/is, relative to the use of that specific term. And there are many other examples to boot that go in more varied examples...)

                                        Sidenote: similar to the idea of a (M)age with, say, an INT:1 (perhaps one who, in his mortal self, was born with Downs Syndrome, or similar, or who simply is more intuitive/instinctive about his magic(k), or who works his will via a cult of personality -- represented by high PER/WITS or CHR -- vs. being in any way academic or intellectualized about their powers, which can often be the case with a number of Spirit Talkers/Dream Speakers, or some backwoods Verbena or country Choristers, or Orphans, etc...), which Fardn doesn't "grok" as being appropriate for a Mage(tm) character (again, relative to his game/setting, just to be clear), there are many very legitimate applications of the points/rules, as-presented, that literally don't require any ST hand-waiving/fiat to be "x" way -- those options are already available in the setting, as-written. Some mages start out the gate with their power/understanding while many others don't. But neither of those farther-end-of-the-bell-curve examples preclude the other in any way, shape or form.
