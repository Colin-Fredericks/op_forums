Entropy 3 never explicitly says that it only works on Matter patterns, and even mentions giving people bad luck already... people are certainly not Matter patterns.

If you want a real wrench in this discussion the "Common Magickal Effects" chart lists cursing and blessing someone as an Entropy 3/Life 3 effect, despite what the Entropy section says.

                            I agree the authors probably working thing in terms of specific philosophies, but this has lead to multiple editions of Entropy being a problem child in the game due to the vagueness of what the text says it can do; both by itself and in combination with other Spheres.

                            Originally posted by quelsec View Post
                            Sphere levels do not exist in the game world (except maybe as a order of hermes paradigm) and therefore should not be used in connection to other paradigms in any way. Its just a mechanic.
                            The numerical ratings don't exist in the game world. That said, mages are completely aware in-character that increasing skill in a Sphere equates to increasing control over the purviews of that Sphere. Every long standing group of mages, not just the Hermetics, understand that there's things an Entropy 1 mage can't do, but an Entropy 3 mage can, they just don't use numbered rankings to describe it. They give people titles or labels based on feats they can demonstrate.

                            While it is important that "Entropy 3" doesn't actually exist in-universe, it's equal important that Entropy 3 is an abstraction of something that does exist in-universe, and that is something mages are aware of, discuss, and categorize.

                            Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
                            I want to ask, are you more focused on how the division affects mages in universe or how it affects players playing mages with those paradigms? For my part I'm mostly focused on the spheres as game mechanics, so the latter matters more to me.
                            In this case, I'm not sure ho separable these considerations are. Players have to filter effects through their paradigms, and paradigms are shaped by how the game presents the functioning of magic. They're intertwined. A division of what counts as predictable that has a large impact on paradigms in universe inherently affects players playing mages with those paradigms.

                            If that holds true for energies as well, then as systems get more complex they get easier to work with, right up until the moment they get too complex and bump up to the next rank. That being the case it is important that the demarcation between ranks be clear and easy to use. If weather is split amongst two ranks, how do we make the line clear?
                            It doesn't hold true for energies.

                            Something I think is a functional analogy: energies are like a fluid. They conform to the shape of their container.

                            The hard part is that the complexity of the "shape" of energy in the system is not directly correlated to the complexity of the system itself. Computers are fairly complex systems, but as a container of energy they're explicitly designed to make energy acting within them to be as predictable as possible. The Earth's surface is a fairly complex system as well, but weather as an energetic function of it varies wildly in predictability. Gravitational orbits are very simple systems... but then the nth-body problems set in.

                                                    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post

                                                    Agreed, though I want to make sure we don't take too much from that connection. Game rules also have to consider balance and playability, and decisions made for those reasons probably shouldn't filter back down to the in universe game. Skill with a sword does not break down into five distinct and measurable levels of ability. Neither does strength. Those are artefacts of the system.

                                                    In universe the Spheres are huge, complex fields of study that some mages think may be connected to the metaphysics of the universe (they may be right) and others think may just be an effective framework for describing it (similar to what some think math is). Progress along them will often be gradual rather than abrupt and some paradigms will access some capabilities better than others. That doesn't mean that there aren't accepted abilities for each rank, but it is more likely that those abilities were set down as requirements to achieve the recognition for that rank than that they are metaphysically the product of that rank.

                                                    Out of universe there is no value in breaking down the spheres to match that smooth progression, and the distinction of what goes in what rank is at least partially one of intuition and game balance rather than deeply considered philosophical thought. Thus there is a limit to how much of what we define in the Sphere ranks is actually a metaphysical fact in universe. And if we do set abilities in a way that one or more paradigms can't fully use, that doesn't mean that in universe those paradigms are proved wrong.
