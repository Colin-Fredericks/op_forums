        No, and I agree with Aleph in this.

        I just had the same idea: stop Difficulty at Sphere Level 5.

        Just that. The character buys the new levels, we proceed to make the rules, but for Difficulty calculation only it stops at 5.

        That makes perfect sense: our system is already distinct from regular Magic rules, the requisites are greater and so are the successes needed. There's no actual reason for any rule from previous levels to remain if they don't make any contribution.

        We can transfer the increase both to the steep requirements of Archspells or to the number of successes required, as those are subsystems we are making up anyway, so we have the level of control needed to balance things there.

        And I'd say an Archmage is actually likely to roll the final ritual as Coincidental. Sure, the Archspell is too powerful to not be Vulgar for all descriptive purposes. In fact, so powerful we'll have a whole process of dishing Paradox along the way in smaller batches.

        The side effect is that it does free up the final ritual to follow more or less regular analysis. The absurd Paradox was already generated and distributed, the final step is a trickle in comparison, but at least this they can try to damper with a Chantry or Realm. We may not use this, too, and still capping the diff calculation at Sphere 5 is sound.

                    Originally posted by Tjalei View Post
                    Sphere 6 being the cap and allowing for weird and conceptually experimental stuff is the best way to run arch mastery. I'd agree fully with the op on that except I'd go farther and say there is no sphere 7. They're unnecessary, oracles or whoever is beyond arch masters have the powers of plot. Like Caine they don't have stats.
                    I agree on lvl 7 being unnecessary.

                    There are a few things I thought out for some of the lvl 6. For instance, Time 6, you can remove yourself from the time continuum, which means you could go back in time, kill yourself as a baby and be completely fine, because you're no longer scored in the time continuum.

                    Then for Entropy 6, you'd be able to manipulate the PROBABILITY SPACE - not just manipulate probability, but the SPACE - for instance, you could create causes without consequences. A bullet is shot, it hits someone, it goes inside the skull of a person, but since you removed the very probability space of that bullet to cause injury, the bullet simply causes nothing.

                    You could also create a superposition of probabilities, by getting a total sum of probabilities to go above 100%.

                    Say for example that I determine that the chance a person dies from a bullet is 100%, but the chance that same person survives from that same bullet is also 100%, so now you have 2 outcomes for a total sum of 200% chance of happening.

                    You'll force reality into a superposition state, upon which the man you shot is both death and alive AT THE SAME TIME.

                    The man goes home have dinner with his wife, but at the same time (and at the same home), the wife is also receiving the news of her husband's murder by police officers.

                    With Prime yes, you could make things that are "magic" become simply mundane. Anyone can levitate stuff by saying "vingardio leviosa" at your home because you've re-written the very metaphysics of your place. Levitating stuff using that phrase becomes as mundane as using apps on a cellphone.

                    The Mind 6 I based myself on the book 1984 to formulate. You can change concepts GLOBALLY. I would name it "Newspeak". Just like in the book, the concept of "peace" gets eliminated, and people can no longer think about it simply because the very concept is nonexistent - there's only "war" and "not-war".

                    There's also one interesting example from real life... Portuguese has the word "Saudade", which is the word used for when someone "misses" something.

                    But to "miss" something is a negative feeling - you can only "miss" that which is absent.

                    The word "Saudade" however is a POSITIVE feeling - it's even hard to explain in any other language other than Portuguese itself, because no other languages in the world actually possess that very concept of which the word relates to. So, I can't even explain to any others the difference between what the word "Saudade" conveys which cannot be simply covered by the word to "miss" something, because simply put, no other languages in the world even have that very concept.

                    So, Mind 6 works similarly - you can create or erase entire concepts of cognitive reality.

                    Those are some of the ones I was thinking about

                                You can also explain why the word is hard to translate directly. Such as the English word Gobbledygook, which is a very specific shade of nonsensical language because it was invented as a mocking term for deliberately vague and confusing political speech and specifically references the sound turkeys make. That may be too much information to easily work into an actual translation, but it can still be translated and conveyed.

                                Also, there is such a thing as close enough. I do suspect that Gobbledygook has such a specific meaning (and lack of meaning) that there isn't a perfect match for it in another language, but how often is that specificity actually required? Or even intended? In every case I've seen the word used a close approximation would convey the intended information, and most languages will have a close enough approximation.

                                        Those are actually all very common through languages. And the main reason we always use Saudade to talk about unique Portuguese word is because it's the most beautiful one.

                                        I also like how different languages encode subtle but important distinctions in nuance even in analogous words.

                                        "Cool" in the sense of nice can be directly translated into Portuguese (at least Brazilian) as "legal". Almost same valence.

                                        But there is a subtle difference between using a word related to cold to say that, and a word that means "legal". The implied aspect of low heat, slow motion (metaphorically), calm, is absent in Portuguese.

                                        The Brazilian equivalent has a more high energy/pitch implication, open expression, or sometimes low-key presence, being usable in more contexts, including formal ones. It's positive in a broader sense.

                                        One multi-word term in Portuguese that's also nonsense: rebimboca da parafuseta. It's a made up piece for a machine, used to say a mechanical problem is actually ridiculous or made up, or sometimes as a comic placeholder for any undefined piece of machinery.

                                        And whenever you see a term in English that's actually Latim or French, it's probably actually universal, especially if it is deliberately spelled in the original language. But Shanghaiing was new to me, indeed.

                                        But hey, here we took your "delete" and created "deletar" (verb), "deletado" (adj), and did the same to "start" and several others. Mouse in Brazilian Portuguese is "camundongo", but the computer input is "mouse" with a more open "o".
