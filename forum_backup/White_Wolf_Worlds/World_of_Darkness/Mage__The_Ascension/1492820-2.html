    Science is done in the dark in our world too. There are various assumptions in the method itself that we don't know are true and we press on with it anyways.     The method was developed to deal with a world that we didn't (and honestly still don't) know would play along. It's going to have some utility even in a subjective reality.                                                 I'll note that Rich Bastard's Guide to Magick eliminated one monopoly that the Union had gained in the Syndicate Convention Book (i.e., access to Ventures); and the upcoming Lore of the Traditions has the Celestial Chorus develop an analog to Ventures (“Stable Wellsprings”). So there's some precedent for breaking the Union's monopoly. I'm not inclined to completely break the “mass production” monopoly, at least not in this context; but I could see making it an “Enlightened Science” thing instead of a “Technocratic Union” thing — bearing in mind that, barring the likes of Navalon and independent Enlightened Scientists, the Union is the only game in town.

                                                Agreed. And while I'm trying to keep house rules to a minimum, this might be a place where another house rule would be appropriate: namely, replace the expenditure of a dot of Willpower with a straight-up XP cost, based on what the Device rates as the Wonder Background.

                                                I'd be inclined to apply that across the board, instead of having it be a distinguishing factor between the Awakened and the Enlightened; while there's something flavorful about spending a dot of Willpower for crafting a Talisman, it has some drawbacks: the effective XP tax scales with your Willpower, not with the thing your making; meaning that the more Willpower you have, the more XP it will cost you to make Wonders without depleting your Willpower — and that this is true whether you're making a one-dot Wonder or a ten-dot Wonder.
