    I'd like to point out that not all technomancer machines are single-function. That lightning gun might have variable power levels, range modifiers, adjustable spread, etc. If the scientist wanted to set off an EMP burst to short out local electronics, he needs buttons or knobs for that. Need to charge a piece of metal into a magnet? That requires further fiddling with controls. And if you wanted to stick wires on it and use it as a portable generator, all the more functions need to be applied for.

    The lightning gun having just a point and trigger interface presumes that the gun only has the one thing it can do: shoot a bolt of lightning as hard as it can. But Etherites (and Virtual Adepts and Technocrats) are inventive people, and love their flexibility. It makes sense that, if they spent many hours designing, building, and testing a machine, that they'd want to cram as much variability in as they could. Hell, testing alone may necessitate that the scientist be able to see how their machine works at very amounts of power. They stick a dial on there to allow them to go from high to low voltage, and bam! The scientist can just keep the dial, and they've got a more flexible tool. Or they are so impressed by their genius, that they don't bother modifying the gun to take that function out. And when that dial could allow them to switch from a taser function ("Stun"), to man melter ("Unlimited...POWER!"), it makes perfect sense to keep it.

    This often leaves such machines - usually prototypes - in a state where the inventor knows perfectly well how to make it do whatever he wants, but a Sleeper can't make heads or tails of it.

                    This logical inconsistency is the biggest difference between the technocracy an the technomancers. In fact, this is one of the reasons why a lot of technocrats can't stand working with etherites.                     This is what makes Etherites mystics at their core. Yes their focus is science, but it's a form of confirmation bias rather than investigative analysis. They don't try to discover what works and doesn't work within the confines of reality like most sleeper scientists would. They have a theory, often this theory has little to do with consensual reality, and their work is all about applying their theory to as many things as possible.

                    One Etherite might have the theory that the universe is a giant clock, so all they need to is insert the correct gears into the correct configuration in order to wind up or down certain aspects of the universe. Thus they make clockwork contraptions which do exactly that.
                    Why don't they work when another uses these devices? Fundamentally because the other does not see the universe as a giant clock. But if displayed with this information, they will not think "hmm, my theory might be incorrect" but rather "well, they work for me, so the theory must have merit"

                    Etherite scientist who are aware of the shifting consensus, however, might be a bit more understanding about the limits of their personalised theories, but by that poit, they've already invested far to much time and effort into them to hen go and decide it was all meaningless.

                    From the technocratic point of view, Etherites are mystics, and one promotion the value of steam power for time travel is just as damaging as a verbena who promotes homeopathy for curing cancer. They both do not work outside of the mages observation, but te mage is far too arrogant to even consider this.

                                                I think it's a non-starter hypothetical: It's entirely feasible that you can't make a lightning gun so simple that it's really just a matter of pulling a trigger. There's configuration concerns and dials and gauges; enough complexity that it's not surprising that an unAwakened mind can't juggle all of them at once. It's no different than why most Sleepers can't unlock their chakras or use their latent psychic powers: it's intuitive once you know how to do it, even effortless, but getting someone to that point is quite difficult. If you had streamlined the science and simplified the interface to the point a Sleeper could understand it, it wouldn't be dynamic magic anymore - it'd be an Extraordinary Device or just, y'know, an object.

                                                Think of giving a normal rifle - a relatively straightforward device - to a caveman. Intuitively that's a point-and-click device, but you're neglecting that we're from a culture where mechanical objects are common and guns are known qualities. Could Thog intuit that the device was a weapon? Would he grasp it correctly? Could you explain to him why he needs to hold it just so? Simple concepts like ammunition (and that it can run out, and that Thog can't just put more metal rocks in the gun's "mouth"), that it's a tool and not an entity, etc. etc. all hinge on a fair amount of unacknowledged "cultural" knowledge about the world and its contents that Thog just doesn't have.

                                                Have you ever tried to walk a grandparent through using an internet browser, pointing out exactly what to do, and met with limited success, despite hands-on, explicit instruction? That's us, with the Ethertie's lightning gun.
                                                Agreed on the first comment. For an example, take a look at the "Memory TV" invention in Meet the Robinsons. Of course it can show people's memories, IF you know this really counter-intuitive way to set the dials, turn the knobs and push the buttons in exactly the right sequence (which might change depending on the situation... the memory was always supposed to to be the user's "best" memory, so it would probably take some additional modifications to get any other type of memory...)

                                                As far as the "Caveman and the Gun". Absolutely not. A gun is REALLY simple. A "caveman" (for whatever value that term has... Neanderthal? Paleolithic hunter? Dweller in the underground cities of Cappadocia?) knows all about aiming at things and hitting them. Handing someone who had no idea of modern technology but who knows how to use an atl-atl and with a little bit of non-verbal communication, a couple of attempts, and "Thog" will be nailing shots right left and center. This was proved again and again all over the world when guys with guns (usually Europeans) showed up in areas with people who didn't have them. Usually the gun-disadvantaged will start seeking out firearms as they are hella good at doing damage easily and quickly. If they don't, it's not because they don't "get" guns. It's because they don't want them (resisting acculturation usually).

                                                As to whether "Thog" would know it's a weapon, that's a separate question and isn't really in the remit of the discussion. The initial condition wasn't "the norm finds a lightning gun in a trash can", it's the creator handing it off. "Thog" might not know it's a weapon from first principles, but he's not going to be stupid about it once he knows what it can do.
