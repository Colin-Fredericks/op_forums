      Going from M20 alone you're supposed to make shit up as you go. But overall:

      1. There are 3 different Umbrae: Astral, Spirit Wilds and Underworld. Each time the character uses Spirit Magic they'll connect with one of them, not all at the same time.

      2. If you're on Earth/Material, what you see isn't "the Umbra", but an intermediate thing called Penumbra. It is divided in the same way. When you think Spirit World as a direct reflection of Earth, this is the Penumbra

      3. Ghosts and Specters exist in the Underworld and its Penumbra. You decide how numerous they are in the Penumbra, can be a lot, but don't have to.

      4. Astral Penumbra is usually quite empty, but sometimes things from there come to the world. When you think genies, angels, demons, aliens and other things that don't exist, they're usually from the Astral.

      5. If you look in the Penumbra of the Spirit Wilds, usually there's no spirit nearby or just a few passing by. Except for one thing: everything you see is actually a dormant spirit. Dormant spirits just work like objects, but you can use strong enough Spirit Magic to awaken them.

      6. Most spirits of all three types are actually in Umbral Realms. Those are not visible from Earth at all, they're other worlds.

              Well, my previous post was flagged as Spam. Don't know why. Here it goes again:

              Originally posted by ~Ozz~
              when a player uses Spirit to see beyond the Gauntlet, or actually passes through it, what exactly do they see?
              Well, this is an interesting one as Mage makes things a bit complicated here.

              Essentially, when you use the Spirit Sphere to do those things, you see or access a place called the Penumbra. The Penumbra it's sort of a spiritual reflection of our own world - so you get to see a landscape very similar to our world.

              It's important to note that these aren't "the same places" of Our World. A car scrapyard it's going to have old cars on it's umbral reflection, but stealing one it's not going to steal a car on the real world. They're, rather, ephemeral junk that exist on the Umbra because it's significant for the spiritual reflection of a car scrapyard to have at least some cars.

              The structures are only going to be present insofar as they're significant. Buildings are going to exist on a City's penumbra, but they may not be the same buildings - and they may be empty, or just have the stuff you would expect on them. A School would likely exist on the Penumbra, but it's not going to have a spiritual copy of the bag you just forgot inside on the Material Realm. However, it's more than likely going to have textbooks on it's library.

              There's not "people" (humans, there are spirits, and there may be ghosts). So there's going to be roads, likely, but there's not going to be many cars (there could be some parked cars, and maybe even a spirit-car driving itself, tho). There are going to be hospitals, but no doctors or patients.

              Also, what the mage sees it's going to be strongly influenced by their Belief. That's where the concept of Vidares enter: Your mage it's going to be attuned to one of the Three Worlds: The Middle Umbra, the High Umbra or the Low Umbra. The Penumbra your mage has access to it's going to look like one of these.

              So, roughly:

              *If your Mage it's the spiritual type, or it's otherwise attuned to the natural world, they're going to see the Vidare Spiritus.

              This is, rougly, the Werewolf Penumbra - so if you know or played that game, you're set. Some STs just default to this one and ignore the other Videre out of confort. In case you didn't: This Penumbra reflects our world's most visceral and vivid elements, with a tendency to higlight, natural elements, artificial forces, and also corruption. It's strongly affected by 3 spiritual forces: The Wyld, an agent of chaos and pure creation. The Weaver, and agent of order that likes spiders a lot. And the Wyrm, and agent of corruption that likes Cronenberg a lot.

              On a City's Penumbra you will see strong energy and electrical spirits near high voltage areas, spirits representing hunger on places striken with poverty, spirits of sickness prowl inside hospitals. There's also a lot of spiderwebs, because Cities are the turf of the Weaver.

              Buildings are going to exist according to how important they are or were for the feelings of the people. A new house may nor appear reflected here, while an odler house that was inhabited for many years, but demolished on the real world may still be there and persist for many years (until the new building gains enouugh "weigth" to replace the older one).

              As you go into more natural areas, such as into the sea, spirits representing forces of nature and living beings become more prevalent. Places that are contaminated, plagued with crime, or otherwise "not nice" have Cronenberg horrors, and other "not nice" things waiting for you.

              *If your mage it's more into reason or abstract thinking they see the Vidare Astral.

              This Penumbra reflects the world as it's concepts and toughts. Your City it's going to reflect what people thinks about it. If it's a vibrant turistical place, things could look tackier, and as if they were trying to catch your attention. Places of education have spirits of the concepts that are being taught. Hospitals may still have spirits of sicknesses and pain, but are more places of healing and hope.

              Much like above, buildings may not match their "Material Realm" counterpart. But instead of thinking about emotional weight, think about ideas. A church symbolizes stronger than a random house. Places of importance look more important. Places that are insignificant may not even exist.

              *If your mage it's into death-related magick, or otherwise very dark and gloomy stuff, they peer into the Videre Mortem.

              This Penumbra reflects decay and entropy. It's like our world, but more ruined. Buildings appear more broken, cars are all rusty, trees appear dead or dying. It's spooky-land. Here's where you're going to see ghosts and specters of dead people - assuming they left one, as not everyone leaves a ghost. Places of importance that may be exaggerated, or "more present", are also going to follow this idea of death and decay.Graveyards are going to be very "potent" here.

              Essentialy, it's the Shadowlands from Wraith. So if you know that game, youre done.

              Interestingly, one can't enter this place with just Spirit, one needs further magicks (If I remember correctly it's Entropy 4/Spirit 3/Life 2 for Agama Sojourn - a state where you leave your body comatose and become a ghost-like creature yourself to enter the Land of the Dead). There's also a 3 points Merit that allows to enter). If this seems like too much, I would suggest to houserule it

              I don't know what manual you're using, but on M20 there's a much longer and more detailed description of each place than what I can make here. I highly recomend to get it, or the Book of Worlds, to better understand the landscape.

              Originally posted by ~Ozz~
              I understand that there are spirits associated with pretty much everything, but how many spirits are there in practice? Are they common and widespread, or are they relatively rare?
              As said, on a particular place they are sparse.

              A small forest it's going to have a lot of trees on the Penumbra (regardless of Videre), but may have only one or two actually conscious spirit-tree. It's going to be the most venerable (or otherwise significant) of the bunch. All the other threes are just inert ephemera constructs. Same with everything else.

              In general, there's going to be a handful of spirits appropiate to the elemens and concepts you want to higlight when describing the landscape

              Originally posted by ~Ozz~
              And what about ghosts and spectres?
              You can see and interact with them trough the Vidare Mortem, or if you deliberately seek to find them with Spirit. All Spirit powers work on Ghosts, however you can't see where they live unless you have the Videre Mortem. And you can't enter where Ghosts live w/o much more powerful magick

              So the penumbra of a city would be fairly "empty" with most of the spirits dormant, maybe some ghosts and some pattern spiders where it makes sense? No need for incarna/elementals/banes
              I would say there's going to be some of the latter. Incarna tend to live on their own Realms, not on the Penumbra. But maybe a City Father's avatar gives a stroll on the Penumbra sometimes

                                      I’ll add that M20 is hazy and contradictory with some of this. There are two elements in particular you probably want to take a look at and decide how you are going to handle them before your players start asking hard questions. These are actually quite interrelated but I’m going to treat them separately.

                                      1)
                                      Officially the Vidare Astral, Vidare Spiritus and Vidare Mortem are not three different Penumbras, they are three different lenses to view the Penumbra through. If two mages are looking at the same place in the Penumbra (either in person or across the Gauntlet) and one sees through the Astral lens and the other the Mortem lens, they are still both seeing the same place. One may see the idealized form of a city while the other sees a run down decaying husk, but they will both see any objects, spirits, mages, werewolves etc that happen to be there.

                                      As best I can tell this is largely to avoid confusion and accidentally splitting the party up when they cross the gauntlet. On the flip side it does tend to mean you need to describe the same place in multiple ways even though the differences are largely cosmetic. It’s probably best to go with the Mage standard for this if you aren’t looking to make the Umbral realms a major part of the game.

                                      2)
                                      Mage is very unclear on whether the Penumbra seen through the Vidare Mortem and the Shadowlands are the same thing. It seems to say it both ways, but falls more often on the idea that they are separate places. I think the idea is to make interacting with ghosts harder for some reason, because if ghosts are in the local Penumbra mages would be able to see them when the look through the Gauntlet, regardless of Vidare.

                                      If you and your players aren’t planning on doing anything with ghosts or the Underworld this largely doesn’t matter. If you want to make working with ghosts harder, make the Shadowlands much harder to reach. If you want it to be as easy as interacting with other spirits, consider them the same place as the Penumbra. If you want it to be only a little harder keep the Shadowlands and Penumbra the same place, but give out difficulty penalties for seeing ghosts for anyone who doesn’t use the Vidare Mortem. In fact you might consider doing that one anyways until they start seriously interacting with ghosts.

                                          Originally posted by ~Ozz~ View Post
                                          So the penumbra of a city would be fairly "empty" with most of the spirits dormant, maybe some ghosts and some pattern spiders where it makes sense? No need for incarna/elementals/banes
                                          Basically, yes.

                                          As Aleph shows, it's more complicated, and as Ramnesis said, the game is fairly inconsistent, but for practical purposes that's the baseline if you don't want something else.

                                          Some other practical points:

                                          1. A dormant spirit is basically furniture. What happens is that in the Spirit Wilds/Middle Umbra everything is technically a spirit in the literal sense, even the floor. So "Dormant" only means this thing can be targeted by the Spirit Sphere, otherwise it's irrelevant.

                                          2. Usually a venerable tree or other things that you find as an active spirit are still fairly weak for a spirit. Not because they can't become a stronger spirit, but because when they do they are also more likely to not spend all their time in the Penumbra.

                                          So assume most of the few spirits you find around are Gafflings. If only a very important spirit will be on the power level of a lesser Jaggling.

                                          Anything stronger will be a special case at your discretion, the only regular exception is the City Heart / City Father/Mother, if the city is big and old enough to have one (your criteria). This is a greater Jaggling powerful enough to be Totem, and may have tougher Gafflings and lesser Jagglings as servants.

                                          3. Don't take special as rare. It's as rare or common as you want and can change as the politics and events of other worlds change. So you need more spirits? Bring them. Powerful spirits? Do as thy wish.

                                          It isn't particularly rare for those things to happen, so nothing breaks if you decide an Incarna is holding court in New York.

                                          4. Likewise, huge areas almost empty of active spirits aren't particularly rare. Completely empty, or a place where even dormant spirits are few, are weird. But you're ok with a Penumbra that's so empty you only find a stray Gaffling every now and then in most blocks.

                                          5. Astral Penumbra is even more variable. You don't have to feel a need to put any spirit in the city at all if you don't want, because they have more access to a Realm called the Vulgate, so its even easier to justify them not sticking around.

                                          Yet for the same reason they have a more direct contact with Earth from there, so it isn't abnormal to have a lot of spirits in a place you think should have.

                                          6. Ghosts are the slightly more complex point, because they will define a lot of how you want to run themes of death and lingering dead.

                                          The game has usually assumed they're far more common in the Penumbra than other spirits, sometimes even forming parallel communities with rules and stuff.

                                          You don't have to do this, it is perfectly fine to decide that most of them go to the actual Underworld, but the default would be a few times more numerous than other spirits if you make those uncommon.

                                          And if you really want to emphasize that people leave unfinished business and linger around after death more often than not, you can have a city with a Ghost population as big as a 1/10 of the living population. The game won't break.

                                          7. You adjust how hard it is to affect a spirit by messing with three things: number of successes, giving them a Counterspell/Resistance roll, or adding Quintessence requirements.

                                          Number of successes is the simplest path to make it a challenge, but still feasible. The catch for the Mage isn't just that the spirit requires more to affect, but that they can usually react to the Mage during a ritual.

                                          Counterspell has a number of possible rules and can become quite nasty, because spirits and Night Folk can have pretty high dice pools. I recommend using this only against effects that are useful with only 1 or 2 successes.

                                          The good thing is that you don't have to be consistent about it with spirits: the ability to resist and to which type of Magic can be a case by case thing.

                                          Quintessence is useful when you want to emphasize that there's something fundamental about the nature of that spirit, it isn't just a monster, but an aspect of reality.

                                          And in practical terms to turn any binding ambition into a fetch quest.

                                          You decide a Resonance and/or Synergy it has to have, a number of points, and let your players figure out how to get it. That way you don't have to make the Magic itself hard, you turn the preparation into a storyline.

                                          8. Other than that, just be bold and make decisions. Are there really three Penumbrae or only one? Do Astral spirits, Wild spirits and Ghosts interact? What's the difference between a Vidare and the Penumbra? What exactly is the difference between the Penumbra and the space between it and other Realms?

                                          The answer to all of those is whatever, the game is obnoxiously vague and inconsistent, partly on purpose.

                                          Ideally the answer should be "whatever the Mage believes" and the whole thing should change rules based on Paradigm at the same time that its denizens exist independently of Paradigm.

                                          But since in practice the ideal is hard as fuck to actually play and that's not a rookie difficulty, it is legitimately inconvenient for everyone, just ignore and do your own stuff.
