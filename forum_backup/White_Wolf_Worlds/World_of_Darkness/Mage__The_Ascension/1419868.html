    Euthanatos killers, gamblers, no school-teachers?

    10-23-2020, 08:26 PM
    The Euthanatos, as they are presented, remind me of janitorial staff carrying away garbage slower than the rate at which it is being dumped on them. They are supposed to be guiding people through multiple incarnations, helping them achieve the divine purpose assigned to them by higher powers.

    In practice, as they are depicted and commonly played as characters, they seek out people who are spiritually corrupt, past the point of redemption, and kill them. "Society produces all these malignant souls who have fallen short of their Dharma. It's my job to eliminate those idiots before they harm anyone... because I dig Hinduism. It's where it's at.", says the murder-hobo who just killed all your most original, thoughtful NPCs.

    Why are there no Euthanatos involved in early childhood education? Wouldn't that be the most efficient way of steering people towards their Dharma. A school teacher comes in contact with newly incarnated souls at a time where there is the most opportunity to inspire them toward the good.

    Less hit men. More teachers.
    What do you think?

    Thank you for passing time with me in conversation. My Hacks.

                  The common Euthanatoi/Chakravanti stereotype is that of a serial killer inspired by vaguely eastern mysticism, true enough, but this has never been the best presentation of the tradition, even when it was what White Wolf itself was somewhat preoccupied with.

                  Any paradigm that does not explicitly call for pacifism will have ideological room within their ranks for militant and violent practitioners. From the Templar of the Celestial Choir, the Executioner Priests of the Verbenae, or too one particularly radical Etherite that uses the psychic energy of human suffering to power their creations; every tradition has room for dark and bloody elements. The Chakravanti have simply been burdened with the archetype more then others.

                  The Chakravanti are dedicated to a variety of cyclical concepts which are perhaps best exemplified by eastern philosophies such as Karma, Dharma, and the like. They are not limited to the Vedic sphere though as numerous philosophies have embraced enough precepts of reincarnation, cyclical existence, and the persistence of energy through the churning of entropic wheels. Wherever such philosophy exist (particularly alongside sympathy for the mystical) there will be a place for the Chakravanti.

                  The philosophies that form this tradition‘s core are mystically nuanced and are often misunderstood, particularly in the west where they are foreign. I am not an expert either and thus everything I say on the topic should be taken with a grain of salt, as well as everything I say in general. I am, after all, a stranger on the internet.

                  The Chakravanti are concerned with the cycles of life and existence. Things come to be, exist, and are cycled back by entropy. Perhaps their is an escape from this, perhaps not. Regardless the cycle occurs and human souls (alongside the matter that make up the physical vessels) will go through this process. As people live they accumulate karmic drag which may drift them closer towards harmony or towards corruption.

                  Those who are truly corrupt have become stuck in the wheel, disturbing the whole revolution and thus threatening every other soul. Because of their concern for the wheel itself (and the greatest number of souls) the Chakravanti must sometimes, tragically, remove a soiled bit of chaff from the wheel and return it to the cycle so that not only will the wheel turn unhindered again but that the soul will have a chance to redeem itself and live a new and better life.

                  This is how it is meant to be but not entirely as it is, because the world we live in is full of corruption and material chaff clogging the wheels of the universe. This is a cause of concern for everything but the Chakravanti know that only they understand it truly. The dire straits and isolation cause some to become radical, acting more and more judiciously as they choose those who must be slain to cleanse the wheel. Many who walk this path without understanding will risk falling to the darkness of the nephandic path.

                  Yet this is a tragic extreme and does not represent the whole. The Chakravanti can be found in every walk of life and while their philosophy is somewhat regionally bound, the global environment allows them to fulfill their duties anywhere. They are teachers, gurus, historians, journalists, and social workers alongside so much else.

                  They are Awakened. Whatever form they take, their lives are never inconsequential, and as Chakravanti they shall always seek to bring harmony to the wheel.

                              The Euthanatos are also teachers, but they aren't as deeply invested in needing to control formal educational institutions to the extent that other Mage groups are. The Celestial Chorus is deeply tied into the world's religions and it's much easier to make people devoted believers if you get them practicing it when young (and can explain away the stuff that doesn't make sense when they get older). The Order of Hermes needs highly educated people for its base paradigm and they are elitist, so they'd get involved in education to raise up the right portion of Sleepers (not enough energy to raise them all up so each Hermetic would decide who has potential and work there). The Sons of Ether are scientists, so they'd be in science education and the Technocracy needs its technicians.

                              Since Euthanatos are about the duty to the Wheel, one could certainly see the modern educational system as a problem and start being a teacher. It reminds me of James Zambrano in his Mage: the Podcast guest appearances. A Native American or Goth (he's a member of both groups in real life) could belong to any faction in the Mage setting. They'll more easily fit better than others, but when you do play a character, take the time to do the homework so you make a real person.

                              Bad players can make any character type into a murderhobo, even if the background on the sheet and their prelude say otherwise. Bad players can also show a lack of respect for NPCs. In many types of RPGs, this is rewarded and killing your foes usually ends your conflict with them. It's just a bit easier to make murderhoboes or act like murderhoboes if you don't do the homework.

                              I'm glad the OP has players now who want to explore the richness of Mage. There are so many things to do in this game instead of just killing them all and taking their stuff.
