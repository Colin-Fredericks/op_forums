    My problem with the idea of playing a Nephandus is that they lack free will by definition. For example, the nephandi can't just choose not to be cruel. They have to be focused on the greater evil. I find that to restrictive.

    Yes, I've played characters bound by vows. But it is a choice to keep a vow. Nephandi don't get choices. There's a group of evil mages in Dragons of the East who are being compelled to do evil. The struggle of these mages to fight against the evil they are trapped in is dramatic. The Nephandi by definition can't struggle anymore. The cannibal mages can still make free will choices to restrict their evil acts. A Nephandus can't even form the thought to want to reject evil.

    What a bore.

                        Just to address one point I saw rereading the thread: Nefandi are not Mages. Not in the ontological sense.

                        This is important because their powers don't follow three fundamental rules:

                        1. Doesn't come from their own Will;

                        2. Doesn't require Paradigm; and

                        3. Don't leave "normal" Resonance.

                        Doesn't come from their own Will:

                        Nefandi are channeling erasure. Their magic is innately destructive, even when it appears to generate a creative effect.

                        Every single effect a Nefandus produces is unraveling reality regardless of their goals, their will being a tool for Oblivion rather than the other way around.

                        Doesn't require Paradigm:

                        This is important and not a small thing. They choose Instruments out of convenience or habit, not of belief. Some may believe the need, but they don't, actually.

                        As long as their powers destroy reality (with or without their knowledge), anything goes.

                        Don't leave "normal" Resonance:

                        Resonance usually reacts to a lot of factors. It depends on the Mage, the area, the spell, the goal, the Quintessence/Tass used and so on. It is a poorly written, vague rule, but still central to the game, because Resonance codifies the relationship between Magic and the world, its effects on the Mage and its interactions with other things that carry Resonance.

                        Nefandi only leave Nefandic Resonance, which may be treated as a singular signature or small family of them, but ignores all factors anyway. It's stronger than normal, unique to them and unavoidable for them. The game sometimes presents means for them to "cover" this, but not to not leave it there. This is the mark of the unraveling they promote, and as any Resonance can have further effects if the ST so chooses.

                        One must be a Mage to become Barabi, but the result is not a Mage, even if it is something superficially similar that uses the same rules for ease of play (that's also stated). Whatever they Inverted Avatar is, it effectively turned the character into something else that's a servant to Dark Magic rather than a user.

                        But that said, I agree "no free will" is a bit of an overstatement. They have some agency, limited as it may be. Unraveling the universe isn't a plan or goal for them, it is fate.

                                                            That's pretty much it.

                                                            As long as they can spin it in any way where their actions made someone's life suck more, it's a win. As a trope they are designed to be an easy "invincible faction" you can ascribe victory to almost regardless of what actually happens.

                                                            Playing a Nefandus is playing someone whose personal stakes don't matter to a story, if they exist at all.
