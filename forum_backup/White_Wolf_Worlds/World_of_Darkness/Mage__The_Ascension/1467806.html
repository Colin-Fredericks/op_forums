    Help With Refining a House Rule

    11-14-2021, 02:52 PM
    I'm writing this hoping that people here more knowledgeable than myself could help me refine a House Rule that I've been using for my Mage games.

    One thing that has always bugged me about the Mind Sphere, was that at the 5th rank of the Sphere, you can create a sentient mind but that nowhere before that does it allow anything similar. Something I've house ruled very generally in the past, and something one of my players wants to utilize is my ideas that the Mind Sphere should be able to create a mind of varying degrees of intelligence/autonomy before hand. It opens up the door to some interesting ideas that I've always liked. I struggle with coming up with a more precise idea of what each rank of the Mind Sphere would do, however. Specifically his character is a programmer and he wants to be able to make programs with limited amounts of intelligence.

    Rank 1 would not change. As with all of the other Rank 1 Sphere's it's more about the general knowledge of it but without being able to do much to effect it.

    Rank 5 would also not change, as that's when you create fully self away intelligence.

    Rank 2 I was thinking something similar to an instinctual intelligence like an insect. The mind would have a very general agenda that it works towards, but with basically no problem solving skills to achieve this goal. If it's a living creature it would know how to eat and sustain itself, as well as a rudimentary understanding of avoiding danger but otherwise simply does what the mind is "programmed" to do.

    Rank 3 I was thinking closer to an intelligent animal. Still acts primarily on instinct, but is now capable of problem solving to work towards its goal. It would be able to "learn", but only things that help it get through any obstacles presented in a fairly direct fashion.

    Rank 4 I was thinking an intelligence that is capable of multiple complex tasks, and can learn efficient and more subtle ways to accomplish those tasks. Something that to an outsider could seem alive if they only briefly interact with it but is still limited by programming. It's never going to ask "Am I alive", and isn't going to learn anything new unless it directly relates to it's "program".

    Has anyone ever used a similar House Rule or have any ideas on have any ideas that I'm completely missing within that house rule?

          In terms of refinement, there's an issue to tackle before rating different levels of intelligence (which is thorny):

          How does this interact with other Spheres? I'm pretty sure one of the reasons this was always overlooked (I hesitate to call it a house rule, the Spheres can never be fully described to cover everything they can theoretically do at any given level) is because it has been long standing rules to not require Mind for creating intelligences via the creation of a pattern that would naturally be that intelligent below a "sentient" level.

          If you use Life to make an otherwise-normal cat, you don't need Mind to give it a cat's level of mental capacity. If you magic up a smartphone out of thin air, the OS will be as smart as it would normally be.

          Mind only comes into the picture when you're trying to make something significantly smarter than what it would "normally" be, or when trying to replicate human-level intelligence (giving into the conceit that our intelligence is uniquely special, but Mage is very human-centric in that regard). The area between many potential targets (other social animals, advanced but mundane AIs, etc.) and full Mnd 5 intelligence is really small, and kinda pointless. If you're going to be making a super-smart cat... why stop short of Mind 5?

          So, to that end, something you need to establish is how creating "lesser" minds works in the larger picture. Do you want it to require Mind 2 to use Life to make ants that still function as ants instead of being bread-dead ants? Do you want to add the capability to making body-independent ant-like intelligences for some reason? Do you want to establish more variety to adding Mind to increase "native" intelligence? Etc.

          Because that really matters to how you start doing levels of Mind rankings.

          If you keep to the idea that Mind is there to alter from base-line, then rather than ranking levels by the end result, you can be more in-line with Life and Matter by ranking based on the target or concept.

          This also resolves I think a weak point in your idea for Mind 4's "limited AI" thing. To me, that's not a "lesser" intelligence, that's a hobbled intelligence (maybe I'm too much of an Asimov fan here). You're creating something with the potential to be equal to a human mind, but then cutting off it's potential by adding roadblocks to it realizing its full potential. To me, that's like saying you can make a dragon with Life 4 instead of Life 5, by making a dragon, but then causing its wings, fire breath, and size to all be so stunted it's just a very large lizard in practical terms.

                  The comparison with Life and Matter raises questions.

                  A cat is sentient, but what is the equivalent object that would also be sentient? The question is important, because you want Mind to apply equally to something inhabiting a living body and a nonliving object, like a computer. A smartphone OS is hardly sentient.

                  If you used a Life 3, Matter 3 effect to turn a normal sentient cat into a robot-cat, would it retain consciousness?

                  If you used the same Spheres to turn a toy robot-cat that isn't sentient into a biological cat, would it gain consciousness? If so, after the effect duration wore off and it reverted to being a toy, would it retain that consciousness?

                  What biological form that lacks consciousness would you use Mind to make conscious? A mushroom colony, perhaps?

                  Do you want a separate Mind system for AI? I think it would be easier if you were tailoring it to making sentient AI, leaving questions of biological life to one side so you can focus on imbuing AI with consciousness. I would think that an Akashic Brother with Mind 5 and no knowledge of either hardware or software would be unable to imbue AI with sentience. Your character's programming skill is relevant to the outcome.

                  Here is a suggestion: Leave it as Mind 5, but with some feat of computer programming, you temporarily increase your effective Mind rating for the purpose of creating a conscious AI. Mechanically, I think the difficulty would be 7 or 8 and you would need a large number of accumulated successes for each additional point of Mind you wanted to add. Make it an adventure sort of like making a Wonder. Have it involve research, talking to other top AI coders in their field, getting access to some closely guarded code, assembling a beast of a computer that can run continuously (so you don't kill the AI by turning it off).

                  For inspiration, I would check out Lex Fridman's Channel, which I think used to be called The Artificial Intelligence Podcast: https://www.youtube.com/user/lexfridman

                  Thank you for passing time with me in conversation. My Hacks.

                      Originally posted by Vulcan7200 View Post
                      Would you have any suggestions on how to "rank" Mind the same as Life and Matter, with it being based on the target/concept?
                      I think you need to start with defining some concepts. This is just good enough for a game, not trying to get into deep philosophic issues. So these definitions are just for the purpose of make a ranking that people can make sense of:

                      Sentience, despite how commonly used, is the capacity for having feelings and emotions rather than just experiencing stimuli and reacting. Most non-animal life has no sentience, and most computer intelligence systems lack sentience as well (a computer doesn't have a feeling about what buttons you press, it just executes the commands).

                      Sapience is the capacity for learning. Humans named ourselves Homo sapiens for this reason, as it's how we most strongly separate ourselves from other animals: the capacity for wisdom and learning.

                      Then we have two axes to consider. "Low vs High" refers to depth of experience. Low sentience means some level of feeling, but minimal and hard to separate from purely instinctive/programmed responses. High sentience means deep displays of emotional experience that's quite common in social (even if not eusocial) animals. "Simple vs. complex" refers to the how much stimulus an intelligence can incorporate into processing a response.

                      Humans are generally familiar with this, because we start as both low/simple sentience and low/simple sapience at birth, and develop into high/complex in both as we age. High/complex in both categories is what we associate with "human-like" intelligence.

                      So to construct a ranking, I think that gives us a solid basis (assuming as a basis increasing sentience and sapience goes hand-in-hand):

                      Mind 2 deals with low/simple minds (ants unless you let people target a colony as one pattern, simple computational systems).

                      Mind 3 deals with two "increases." So moderate/moderate (most social animals), or high/simple (social animals not known for being smart), or low/complex (virtual/sufficient computational intelligence systems that can't really account for emotions but can handle large complex data sets).

                      Mind 4 deals with three increases such as"smart" and expressive animals like cephalopods or most primates..

                      Mind 5 deals with four increases, aka getting us to the human-like intelligence level.

                          My take would be different. Partly because I don't think divides in the "quality" of a mind are a good idea, partly because I think it too abstract but with few practical applications that can't be done with just programming, partly because I'm awake at 3:30 in the morning and I'm not very coherent now.

                          But instead of defining a "level" of mind, I think it works best by identifying discreet functions and applying them at different levels to create a "look-alike" mind.

                          For now, a fast and loose (as far as I can be those) take could be this, using as base whatever we already have in the Sphere of Mind:

                          1 - Emotional Processing

                          The mental construct gains the basic ability of absorbing a big amount of data and process it into a feeling. Emotions are extremely effective ways of making sense of complex stimuli and responding to them.

                          A newly created construct needs a set of basic emotions to be built in, along with initial instructions on what causes them. The construct is now capable of perceiving thing and developing a feeling, although any action on the feelings demands other Spheres or mundane systems to be activated by the emotions themselves.

                          With time such a construct may develop complex emotions, as experience builds up new associations, but it still lacks any notion of cause/consequence or meaning, being unable to adapt its actions, just to perceive and feel.

                          2 - Empathic Communication

                          The construct develops the basic notion of alterity. It recognizes other sentient beings and can read their emotional expression through a medium established at the casting, and use this medium in order to relate its own emotions if it has them, or to try to provoke an emotional response.

                          Notions of cause appear as the construct can be imbued with the capacity to associate complex memories with emotions and understand this process on others, but it can't necessarily remember the evolution of the emotion itself.

                          Build-up in experience will create a library of memories with emotional links that can crisscross in a complex web. The construct may come to develop new emotional patterns from this by itself, and develop new strategies for imparting emotions. Yet, the content of those memories is still concrete, and aside from expressing emotion and pointing things to an interlocutor, the construct can't use complex language.

                          3 - Associative Thought

                          The construct can finally associate memories and emotions to symbols and ideas. This is enough to learn languages on its own, develop imagination and dream.

                          Concepts at this level are simple, as it uses language to refer to concrete notions and emotional states. Experience and recursive associations with increasingly complex networks of memories, symbols and emotions may create a fascinating and extremely intelligent construct. Yet, it can't really achieve breakthroughs like complex abstractions and metalanguage.

                          4 - Formal Abstraction

                          The construct can learn true abstract concepts, fully idealize concepts it has no concrete basis for, develop linear chains of logical conclusions and theorize about existence. Only a few mental abilities are still out of reach, and the construct is completely able to live as a person, or to apply those abilities in data treatment.

                          Ontological analyses are still beyond its grasp, though. It can't comprehend or develop metalanguage or metaphysics. Critically, its moral development is stunted by a lack of complex moral cognition processes. In the Model of Moral Development of Kohlberg it can't go past Sage 4 Conventional Moral, and even at this it is extremely simplified.

                          While formalized concepts and theories of such nature aren't your common human experience in any way, though, the instinctual exploration of them is. The construct don't lack the cognitive ability to engage in this conversations, but the capacity to think about existence above and beyond personal existence.

                          5 - Ontological Consciousness

                          The construct is a complete mind.

                          ================================

                          My goal is to have something that can be referred quickly and built upon. To avoid any strict notion of Intelligence, as each level can be extremely intelligent or dumb on its own right, but instead is a more "complete" existence. And to avoid equating Mind to problem-solving in a simple way, much less to logic and mathematics. Computers already do that, it's easy.
