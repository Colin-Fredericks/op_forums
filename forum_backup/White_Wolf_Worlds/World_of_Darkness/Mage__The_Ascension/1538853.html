      A combination of multiple timelines (what the Technocracy calls Everett Zones) and changeable time, with a Paradox-like effect that attempts (not always successfully) to punish the time traveler and to revert the timeline. Everett Zones are, in effect, Paradox Realms created by severe backlashes against temporal meddling; while less severe backlashes injure the time traveler or summon an equivalent of “Paradox Spirits” to attempt to set the timeline back on its original course. It's possible to change the timeline; but doing so is hard, and carries serious consequences.

      The main difference between this and actual Paradox is that you don't need to work any Magick in order to generate temporal “Paradox”; you simple need to be “in the past” and doing something disruptive. The Storyteller then determines how disruptive your action in the past is, and has you roll a pool based on that judgment call to determine how much “Temporal Paradox” you generate.

      It doesn't even matter how big the change is, if you're careful about it: you could make a huge change to the timeline, but in such a subtle way that it's not going to alter the history books; and that change will effectively be “Coincidental”, generating no “temporal Paradox”.

      My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

          And in place of a paradox realm, Being entirely unstuck from time, constantly being launched randomly from one time to another with little to no control as to when you end up.

              It depends on the point of the game. A game based around time travel usually needs the possibility of temporal change to keep the stakes interesting. It allows for the goal of changing history, or the need to avoid changing history. That said, I suppose you could have a good action style game where no changes in time will stick, the main goal of time travel is information, and the enemies will stop at nothing to avoid the heroes getting back with the vital intelligence that will win the modern war.

              One of the ideas I like is temporal convergence, where not only can multiple timelines proceed out of a single point, but multiple timelines can proceed into a single point. To put it another way, there can be more than one past someone travels into by going back in time. It would give a good platform for a Temporal War as each side fights to reinforce their own past and thus their own strength in the present.

                              Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
                              Mind 4/Time 3 for a mage to project their mind into someone in the past and act through their body, psuedo-quantum leap style. I say psuedo because as I recall Sam was actually physically back in the past and everyone else just saw and heard the original person.
                              Isn't Time 3 just a few seconds (turns) of rewinding time?

                              I don't think you can reach far in the past with Time 3, but I'm not sure.

                              Now, about the "alternative universes", that is not a line I favor too much, because it is meaningless for the game world, since whatever changes you enact "there" won't affect anything down "here", unless you're bringing something vital or precious from "there" to "here".

                              As for simply traveling to the past, unless you can't actually affect the present by altering it (no matter the consequences), it is also useless - as in, it serves no purpose for the narrative.

                              So, my take is:

                              You travel to the Past, you enact changes in the Present.

                              This however, as anybody with a minimum of common sense can realize, is absolutely shaboinky for any story and would quickly turn narrating games into a nightmare.

                              Therefore, the way I deal with it is with what I call as "Time Ripples"

                              So, instead of treating Time simultaneously (Past, Present and Future happens all at the same time), I give it a sort of "delay", upon which it actually takes some time for the changes in the temporal continuum to fully manifest, and those changes come gradually in the form of "waves", rather than an instantaneous alteration.

                              It's as if time is a pool of water, and when you throw a stone into the Past, it creates waves of temporal alterations through it.

                              Let's talk about the Future for a while so that I may explain. Now, let us imagine that, in the future, there's WW3 between NATO and the Brics.

                              The world is devastated in nuclear Armageddon, but the Brics nations "win" (interpret this "victory" as you wish)

                              This is seen in a vision by an Oracle in the PRESENT. Desperate to avoid such outcome and because the visions aren't precise, he decides to step forward into the Future to learn what causes this catastrophe. There, he learns that a chain of events caused by the assassination of the president Kennedy - not the old Kennedy, the modern one, elected president of the US in 2028, and assassinated in 2030, will end up leading to WW3.

                              Now (2024), our Oracle finds out that he has 6 years to save the world.

                              And he does. By 2029 he reveals the secret CIA conspiracy that plotted the assassination of yet another Kennedy, and doom is avoided.

                              Or is it thou?

                              Well, meanwhile in the future, the temporal ripples begin to be unleashed. Reality begin to be distorted by the ripples. Some history books rotting at the wastelands of Washington DC no longer tell about RFK assassination in 2030, but rather about his two mandates as president and how he finally signed the agreements with the Russian oligarchs that deposed Putin. Some weeks later, the city of Chicago reapers, intact. A few moments afterwards, the Kremlin stands intact in the middle of Moscow's wasteland. Then the forbidden city reappears in the wastelands of Beinjing. And so on, so forth.

                              To the sleepers, they are absolutely incapable to perceive those changes; they don't even notice it. If one asks the sleepers, they'll often offer conflicting tales about the chronology of history and about the perceptions of things in the Present.

                              For the awaken thou, all these changes are immediately spotten. Not only that, the awaken will start to develop a DUAL MEMORY of the Past; they'll know how history went in the "formal" way, but they'll also be aware of the "alternative history" unfolding.

                              Now, back to our story: what happens is that, a group of Sino-Soviet mages consider this divergence as unacceptable, because despite of all the calamity, the final outcome was deemed as "net positive" for the forces of the "New Axis".

                              They then identify the point of distortion, from which the time ripples emanate, as being in 2029 when the CIA conspiracy plot was averted, and decide to go back in time and rectify this situation.

                              So, now our heroic oracle (I'm sorry, I forgot that "Oracle" in Mage are the demigods Mages that reach Ascension. What I mean here is an "oracle" with lower case "o" - aka someone that sees the future) has to contend with a bunch of really angry fascist Eastern Mages hellbent on stopping him.


                              For this types of stories, it is always good to put a time frame upon which afterwards the temporal ripples finally cease and the temporal continuum gets stabilized and solid.

                              In the case above, let's say that the "Cabal of the New Axis" from the future only have 6 months to undo the changes.

                              After that time, the ripples finally cease, and they'll begin to cease to exist in the Present, until disappearing completely. And their future versions will no longer know the two alternative versions of history, and they'll never know they even went to the "Past" (their Past, our Present). In fact, after those 6 months, THEY HAVEN'T. If they fail to stop the oracle in 6 months, the only person in the world that will know of that alternative future will be our heroic oracle, and that Future (which was once certain) would forever remain as just an alternative Future that never came to pass.

                              The same holds true for traveling to the Past.

                              Doing that also prevents idiots trying to assassinate baby Hitler (or teaching teen Hitler how to be a good painter) every single weekend. Changes too big (like removing Hitler from history) send Time Ripples so large that EVERY AWAKENED in the world - and across multiple timelines! - can easily spot.

                              That's almost guaranteed to send the Technocracy, or the Traditions or BOTH against the baby fool that attempts such stupidity.

                              Oh, by the way, if someone gets back in time to murder baby-you, you'll get in that "quantum-state" where you're both "dead and alive" for some time while the ripples are dispersed, giving you time to get back in time to prevent your own murder as a baby.

                              Because of that, small changes in the past are easier to achieve. If your girlfriend gets assassinated in your remote wooden shackle and you get there when the murder just finished the deed, so you kill the murder and decides to go 1 day back in time to kill the murder before he does it, nobody will ever know that this small change has ever been enacted (unless there was some Mage using Time 1 at the place where the time travel was used in either way, and that would've been quite the coincidence to happen).

                              Killing baby Hitler or Baby Napoleon or stepping in a butterfly in the Jurassic period however would be immediately perceived by every mage in the entire world (and in the case of the Jurassic butterfly you'd end up having time travellers being summoned to prevent this from the pre historic times to Mages living in the Galactic Empire)

                              What's more, the time Traveller is always immune to "Time Stabilization". Back to the example of the "New Axis Cabal". 6 months after they fail in their mission, the heroic oracle is the only one in the world that knows about the alternative future and about the ravenous Mages from that alternative future that failed to stop him. To the rest of the world, those guys never existed. But the hero remains them, because he is now a "time anomaly", because he now exists in the Present and in an alternative Future that never came to be.

                              What do we do with these "time anomalies"? Well, that's up to you guys as STs. Maybe nothing, the time traveller hero's life keeps going normally but he has a very interesting "science fiction" history about alt-history to tell his friends.

                              Or, if you're feeling particularly sadistic, maybe he could be visited by some interesting paradox-spirits next time he suffers a backlash... Maybe the "New Axis Cabal" comes to torment him but not as the "ghosts" of future Mages that don't exist, but rather as vengeful Paradox Spirits...

                                                          Personally, I prefer to enable as many different notions of how messing with time can work as possible, rather than settling on just one. To this end, I'm considering a notion of timepoints​​​​​, where the nature of the timepoint determines which set of temporal mechanics are in effect.

                                                          By default, a timepoint is fluid, and the event in question can be changed easily. If you create a temporal inconsistency (such as killing your father before he meets your mother), then history now has an inconsistency in it. History doesn't try to correct the inconsistency, though the time traveler may face consequences akin to a Paradox Backlash for having created the inconsistency.

                                                          But there are three other types of timepoints:

                                                          A Static timepoint resists changes. If you try to create an inconsistency, chance events will conspire to prevent you from doing so; and if you succeed anyway, further events will conspire to contain and mitigate the consequences. Eventually, the original timeline will be restored.

                                                          A Dynamic timepoint is a lot like a fluid timepoint, except that when you create an inconsistency, it gets shunted off into an alternate timeline. Individual timelines when examined in isolation can contain inconsistencies; but the larger multiverse of timelines when viewed as a whole remains consistent. Dynamic timepoints are also sometimes called decision points, because even without Time travelers meddling with them they tend to “split the the timeline” based on what people involved in the event decide to do.

                                                          An Entropic timepoint is one where time meddling is discouraged by an extreme version of the Butterfly Effect: creating an inconsistency here works exactly opposite of creating an inconsistency in a Static timepoint, as the timeline essentially shatters and entirely unpredictable changes spiral out of control. An example of this can be found in the 2011 Flash Comics event called “Flashpoint”: Barry Allen attempted to go back in time and prevent his mother's death, and succeeded; but when he returned to the present, Aquaman and the Atlanteans were at war with Wonder Woman and the Amazons, much of Europe was submerged, Bruce Wayne was replaced by his father as the Batman after the mugger shot Bruce instead of his parents, and “the Superman” was being kept in a vault after government agents had found his spacecraft when he first arrived in Earth.

                                                          Time Magick lets you identify which type of timepoint a particular event is. It lets you trace a chain of events forward or back from a particular event, letting you identify future or past events that are causally connected to the current event; and with sufficient ranks in Time, you can even travel to them. When departing from a Dynamic timepoint, you can control which timeline you travel along. Near the upper ranks of Time, you can even mess with what type of timepoint a particular event is, letting you affix an event in history by making it Static, shifting it to a Dynamic timepoint that splits the timeline whenever you mess with it, or poisoning the event with Entropy so that messing with it blows up in the face of the meddler. With effort, you can also restore the timepoint to its default fluid status, removing the added consequences involved in messing with it.

                                                          Again, Mage shouldn't lock itself into one way of handling time travel hijinks; it should provide a framework that enables whichever approaches the Storyteller wants to use. If you like history to be unchangeable, lean heavily on Static timepoints; of you like the notion of a multiverse, lean heavily on Dynamic timepoints; if you're enthralled with the Butterfly Effect, lean heavily on Entropic timepoints. If you just want to keep things simple, stick to fluid timepoints.
