      I don't think it really matters so much who the Primi are as that many of them have changed. What matters for each group's game are: 1. How important the Primi are to the Chronicle 2. If Primi are important to the Chronicle, what their roles are in the chronicle.

      The New Horizon Council Primi are listed here:

      https://whitewolf.fandom.com/wiki/New_Horizon_Council

      For my campaign, which is set in 2040 (being in the future lets me avoid any of the current issues of politics and pandemic), I took the New Horizon Council and an old Council of 9 (after the Avatar Storm masters getting blown up) as being politically different. Kinda of following the Manifesto book, kinda of not because some of that part of Revised gets a little out there for me. Now everyone is unifying again and in 2041, the Traditions will seat new Primi on the Council of 9. Several Traditions already have their candidates. The PCs aren't really political and the chantry's NPCs, mostly Order of Hermes, are backing the Primus of House Shaea, Daira Kashaf. Other Order of Hermes mages are backing Sharad Osai of House Thig and then I have a yet-to-be-named Master from Prague who is a member of House Solificati (Children of Knowledge are a Craft now, but I'm figuring some that would have went to the Order of Hermes in Revised remained) as the third candidate. The Celestial Chorus, Dreamspeakers, and Sons of Ether have yet to announce the candidates as they have not reached a consensus on whom should represent them. I'm going with the seats are still the seats.

      However, since the release of M20 Book of the Fallen showed there's some really bad mages on the internet, I have the Digital Web as pretty damned by the Nephandi. In-game events have shown that the bulk of the world being terrible is another 20 years of digital development, including Augmented Reality being common. I'm running a world that is very cyberpunk, except for the PCs. And because players weren't wanting a huge world population and one person who was a bad fit is no longer playing with us aggressively shouted down thinking demographically and we came to 'current population' in 2020 as the resting point for 2040, a lot of bad stuff has happened in the world to keep populations like they are in 2020 (and it wasn't Covid that did the numbers because that would be triggering). So, in our gameworld going to Hell, The Virtual Adepts could lose their seat if they aren't lucky. So much of the world sucking is they opened the Digital Web to morons and the morons ruined the place . The problems with removing the Virtual Adepts being would the Ahl-i-Batin return or there be an open seat for Correspondence or some new council configuration. So, I think it will end up being that the Virtual Adepts stay because one of the current players is playing a Virtual Adept and wants to rebuild the Digital Web right. . .

      I'll probably have of the Council of 9 candidates come up if the players care. If not, it's background and they'll get a news flash about their Traditions and any new pronouncements. They can work with that thread if they want, or we can do other things.

          As far as personal head cannon (and it's been maybe five or six years since I've actually had the chance to run a Mage game):

          (In my head-cannon, the Grand Council tended to meet at Doissetep every nine years, though this would sometimes occur at other Traditions' grand chantries. With the recent - the value of recent being "a year or three before the current game - destruction of Doissetep, it was decided to establish a new truly mixed Tradition Grand Chantry, Horizon, on earth. This generally defaults to right outside Seattle, but could just as easily be anywhere. A lot of these names may be familiar, but I've tweaked and altered them here and there.)

          The Akashic Brotherhood: Actual leadership of the Tradition is more or less governed by a mental collective of Masters, Archmages, and possibly even a few Oracles from within the Akashic Record. Representation to the Grand Council has always been a pair of Masters representing the complimentary forces fo Yin and Yang (usually a man and a woman, but that's not a hard set rule). Currently, the "Yang" is Raging Eagle, a mixed race (black and Chinese) man from the Wild West era who was one of the Vajrapani's greatest warriors until his retirement a couple of decades ago to become a Kannagara monk. The "Yin" is Gentle Mountain, a peasant girl orphaned during the Tai Pang rebellions and raised as a Buddhist nun of the Kannagara.

          The Celestial Chorus: The Tradition is lead by a council of elders - Masters and Archmages from around the world - known as the Curia, who meet within the Temple of the Sun. This council elects the Pontifex Maximus as the Chorus's leader. (Depending on the political situation, the Pontifex may be the true leader, or just a figurehead for the Curia, or somewhere in between.) The Pontifex usually represents the Chorus on the Grand Council, but sometimes will send a stand in. The current Pontifex is the relatively young Master known as Peter Bonhoeffer, a German of Lutheran background and education whose main focus is on internal unity, building bridges with the other Traditions and resisting the Technocracy's cultural influences. His main rivals within the Curia are the much more radical Yoruba priestess Naijjda Bantu and the arch-traditionalist Puritan Constance Creed, both of whom believe they should be Pontifex.

          The Cult of Ecstasy: The Cult is traditionally led by two members representing masculine and feminine energies. The Shakti Divya is Marianna, the current mistress of the Balador grand chantry, who in her early life was a young Italian noblewoman. Or prostitute. Or artist. Or ... well, the story seems to change frequently. All or none of them may be true. Regardless, she's lived a long and interesting life full of adventure, joy and intrigue (and more than a little heartbreak and tragedy at times) and is one of the most famous of the Bringers of Joy. As the most famous and notable representative of the Cult in the Western World, she is usually the representative to the Grand Council. Her masculine counterpart, the Shakta Divya, is the enigmatic figure known only as Wolf (in whatever the local language is), who is rarely seen outside of Asia, but has recently been spotted in Latin America more than once. No one is really sure if he's a traditional Shivite or one of the Aghoris (or both).

          The Dreamspeakers: Since the Tradition's founding by Star of Eagles and Naioba, they've always had the leadership shared by a male and female Master of different cultural traditions. The current male leader, and usually representative to the Grand Council, is Tom Smithson/Laughing Eagle. A 19th century Apache medicine-worker and spirit-warrior who fought long and hard against the Technocracy and continues to do so as a cultural conservator and historian/storyteller. His female counterpart is Tasygan, a Siberian shaman and storyteller born in the 18th century. As she is the most likely to die or Ascend first, many are looking at Adambara, an Australian Aborigine ecologist and environmental activist as her successor.

          The Euthanatos: Leadership of the group as a whole is made up of representatives from the five main cultural blocs - Indian, African, the Mediterranean, Northern Europe, and Mesoamerican - with the modernist Entropians starting to pester for representation. Which of these leaders represents the Tradition at the Grand Council is decided by drawing of lots. The current winner - or loser as some of them joke - is Senex, "The Old Man", one of the oldest Thantoic mages in Africa and a legend among the Tradition. The others are Idrani Taktsang, the mysterious Iago (a masked identity passed down among the Golden Chalice), Caitlin the Black, and 12 Jaguar.

          Order of Hermes: Each House is lead by a Primus, the collection of which makes up the Order's Inner Circle. (House Ex Misc has a group of lesser Primi who select a Primus for the House.) The eldest, most powerful, and most learned member of the Circle traditionally runs the show as the Primus Magna. Currently this is the Arch-Magi Porthos Fitz-Empress bani Flambeau, who is several centuries old - allegedly an apprentice when the Ascension War started - and can really only successfully interact with lessor mages by playing the Merlin archetype role to the hilt. Horizon is very much his personal pet project, and naturally he sits on the Grand Council. Other notable Primi include Stephen London Masters bani Fortunae, Maraksha Kashaf bani Shaea, and Anne Hardwych bani Guernicus.

          Sons of Ether: The Tradition is managed by a Council of Deans from the Grand Collegium, who are every bit as fractious, combative, and egotistical as you would expect a bunch of academics to be. They elect one of their own to serve as representative to the Grand Council. This is not a popular position, as it is often seen as detracting from time one can spend working on their own Science! and various projects. The current unhappy and unwilling representative is Dr. Julian Spence, an Australian specialist in the fields of cosmological physics, dark matter chemistry, and transmutation technology. His two biggest headaches among the Council of Deans are the arch-traditionalist Scottish engineer, explorer, and metallurgical alchemist Lord Primium, and the Canadian radical femynist revolutionary and eugenicist Dr. Amazonia. (There's an irony here in that Spence is a long time advocate of sexual equality, represented by the fact that while many Etherites take their "shadow names" from various science fiction authors, scientists, or specialized terminology, his is taken from Catherine Helen Spence, the famous 19th century Australian suffragette and social reformer. He's also one of the proponents for officially changing the Tradition's name to "Society of Ether", but keeps getting blocked by various parties, including both of his rivals.)

          Verbena: Much like the Dreamspeakers, the Verbena were founded by a married couple, Nightshade of England and William Groth of Germany. However, as Groth died before the full forming of the Nine Mystic Traditions, they've always been lead by a woman, known by the title of Voice of the Goddess. And until the 1920s, that woman was Nightshade herself (who opted to pass away rather than ascend). Since then, the Voice of the Goddess has been one of Nightshade's last apprentices, Lady Charlotte Quay, a pure blood descendent of the Wyck and minor member of the British aristocracy. (Her Woodwyck Downs estate is one of the Verbena's strongholds in England.) She prefers to represent her Tradition on the Grand Council in person. Lady Quay's right hand and enforcer of Verbena protocol/rules, in a role traditionally referred to as The Huntsman, is the mysterious master shapeshifter known as Samual Haine. If anything should happen to Lady Quay, her most likely successor is expected to be the American hippy earth mother Heasha Morningshade, which is ironic given that the two quite frankly loath each other.

          The Virtual Adepts: The Adepts are a strict meritocracy and always have been. Since joining the Traditions in the 1970s, they've been lead by their modern philosophical prophet and individualist firebrand Roger Thackery (who was friends with Alan Turing during the War). However, Thackery was recently assassinated by the Technocracy. In his place, leadership has now fallen to Dante, a former child-prodigy master hacktivist, and Digital Web crusader. Other up and comers in the Adepts include X-Cel (aka Catherine Bass), a radical punk performance artist, electronica musician and voice of the voiceless, and the faceless military genius and militia survivalist prophet known only as Tunnel Rat.

          The Hollow Ones: Leaders, gurus and trend setters among the Hollowers tend to be a totally local or regional thing. People like the enigmatic Neville Sinclair of San Fransisco, Mary Lou Vannoy the "Bitch Queen" of Chicago, and Tokyo's Jao Shingan ("Wicked Tyrant's Eye of Truth"). Seattle's Jeremy Case would very much like to represent the Hollowers on the Grand Council, but really lacks any sort of backing to do so. If anyone could actually become a leader of the group on a global basis, it would probably be Bryce Grimm, owner of Grimm's Occult Specialty Shoppe (which seems to co-exist in multiple cities simultaneously).

                          Thanks again for the ideas and insight. I printed both this page and the wiki page to use as reference. Then I did a copy/paste of names from the wiki, changed a few people or ranks, and viola. Now, I of course did not use all of it. I improvised one primus for a scene.I can revise and adjust things bit by bit as identities get introduced into the saga.
                          See, I am working with challenges of my own creation. To get a game going, I sold out my integrity as a DM lol. I first intended a one shot, maybe two games, just to turn my group on to the new edition. So I created a few pregened characters they could choose or they could create their own. Two players just wanted to use od characters, and so I let them. One recreated the character, and the other just transferred dots.
                          Whatev. I was only planning a single story as a trial run. I took "Paying the Cost" and dialed it up a few notches.
                          Everyone had fun, and they want to keep going. Every week I prep and wind up improvising. Because of the disconnected way the characters came to be, I am having trouble tying them together. I am running four solo stories. Tried to get people to take on side characters so they are all involved. Moderate fortune there.

                          Anyway, the Primi. The player that just transferred dots, that character is quite powerful and the player is not aware of how powerful she is. So to continue the saga, I had her attend a meeting with other Kavadi elders, wherein the old prima retired, and the player was elected. She only ran to oppose her rival who was calling for open war and to leave the council.

                          Thus, I can keep that character entertained with politics and high level shit that challenges them. At the same tim, going with the side-kick theme to unify interest, I gave her the Apprentice of another player. Julie Branton (it is fun the way that story was resolved, a lot of timey-whimey issues). This further allows me to let the player re-learn the game while letting them have a roleplaying challenge. win-win.

                          Taking the Primi thung further, over in the Order of Hermes, I had Tytalus the Founder return to the mortal world in the modern day. He is apparently the real deal, and quite powerful it seems. But something is wrong. There is politicalll turmoil and he may make a populist run at Hermetic primus. Many adore him, but some are quite opposed. Most against him are members of House Tytallus actually. They are like Never Trumpers.
                          Tytalus is not as bad as Trump. He is far worse.

                          The way I am using that as a hook, in an effort to slowly tie the game together, is that another player is a Hermetic magus (secretly NWO) that is trying to get close to "Tytalus" to learn more about him. Morgan is a drag queen, but Tytalus does not mind. He has a medieval mind and just rationalized it in the same way as written by the Venerable Bede (the girl just did too much physical work and her insides needed room for muscle so...)
                          (Really, an actual Medieval author wrote that)
                          Currently, Tytalus and Morgan are in Mexico City. Tytalus is there to meet with his brother. It was announced on the radio that Alex Jones (the wod version that spreads disinformation for the NWO) has just been found dead in his hotel room.
