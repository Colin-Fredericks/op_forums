    1001 Wonder Ideas

    10-19-2016, 12:34 PM
    As some of you may be aware, I'm working on my own homebrew Mage system. Currently it's using Chronicles of Darkness as a base, but once it comes out I plan to switch to StoryPath. Why, you might ask, is this its own thread instead of in the homebrew system thread? Well because I need Wonders to stat up, and I could use some help. You can feel free to post as little or as much as you like, from one sentence ideas to full write-ups.

    The groups I still need examples for are as follows:

    Iteration X
    New World Order
    Progenitors
    Void Engineers
    Syndicate

    Order of Hermes
    Verbena
    Euthanatos
    Sons of Ether
    Virtual Adepts
    Celestial Chorus
    Cult of Ecstasy
    Dreamspeakers
    Akashic Brotherhood

    Now, let your imagination soar. I'll start you all off with something.

    Labcoat (5 Point Wonder)
    While some Etherites prefer more exotic clothing, such as Victorian gentleman's garb, few Etherites will go so far as to denigrate the classics. This labcoat is close to the platonic ideal of scientific clothing, and this example of the kind in paticular is treasured among those Etherites who can afford one. The labcoat provides four dice of countermagic against all hostile effects that would target machines that the Etherite can use as a focus. Additionally, machines that the Etherite can use as a focus are considered to be under the effects of a Matter 2 improve machine spell at Potency 2, so long as the Etherite is actively using the machine. It has 10 points of mana storage, one of which is spent each time it uses countermagic.

      I'll be thinking up Wonders for the groups listed here (I've got one in mind for the Order of Hermes already), but I wanted to tackle this Etherite one first. Also, I have little experience with the nWoD systems, so I'll stat according to oWoD rules. Make adjustments as necessary.


      The H.E.E.D. (8 Point Device; Arete 5, 10 Quintessence)
      Formally titled the Hollow Earth Exploration Device, the HEED is a drilling vehicle designed, as the name suggests, to explore the deep depths of the Earth. Many such machines have been constructed by the Society of Ether over the years, as a necessary alternative to entering the Hollow Earth via natural tunnels (which have grown more and more difficult to find and/or unreliable as the Consensus grows stronger). This particular model was invented by brilliant Scientist and former Technocrat Doctor Adrian Stone, whose subterranean adventures have become legendary in the Society and infamous in the Technocratic Union. While the man's current whereabouts are unknown - as is his original model HEED - schematics of the machine have circulated among Etherites for a few years now. It's only because of the great technical expertise involved in building it - and the rare materials used in its construction - that stop HEED derivatives from choking the underground.

      Powerful Matter procedures have made the outer hull virtually indestructible; wheels, sensors, and windows (actually reinforced steel rendered invisible) are proofed against all the wear and tear the underground can throw at it. The drilling mechanism - a combination of rare Earth metals and potent lasers - can clear solid rock from the machine's path as fast as half the HEED's top speed (~60 kph). It can even pass through the notoriously difficult Matter Gauntlet separating the surface from the Hollow Earth (see the rules for Alternate Gauntlets in The Infinite Tapestry, p. 21). Finally, the HEED is large enough to seat six crew members comfortably, and possesses life support systems more than up to the task of weathering the extreme deep ground environments.

              The Coat of Arms (3 Point Artifact)
              Not to be confused with a similar kind of Wonder that allows one to pull weaponry from its pockets and folds, this Coat of Arms was created by a Chakravanti seamstress, in order to aid her work. This ornate red silk coat has three additional sets of sleeves, as well as a number of pockets for storing various supplies. Despite its humble origins, the martial applications of this piece cannot be denied, making it a popular choice for recreation.

              As the name implies, this Artifact - when used by an Awakened Will - allows the wearer to grow up to six extra arms, which appear within the folds of the redundant sleeves. These arms provide the user with the standard benefits of multiple limbs, including bonuses to grapple attempts and extra hand to hand attacks (though the latter necessitates either Time magick or splitting dice pools, as normal). The base idea behind this Wonder has been copied and built upon over the years, but has lately fallen from use as a result of its inarguable Vulgarity.


              [For the record, I cribbed the Coat of Arms from Kill Six Billion Demons. The H.E.E.D. in my above post is a classic bit of pulp science, but was specifically inspired by the recent comic, Cave Carson Has A Cybernetic Eye.]

                  Bending Reality With Python (4 Point Grimoire; Arete 2, Correspondence 1, Forces 2)
                  Also known as the "Advanced Advanced Guide to Python", this text contains a treatise on the philosophical underpinnings of programming and the universe. Written by a self-taught, "script kiddie" Virtual Adept, it attempts to speak in plain terms of advanced uses of the Python programming language. It also contains a library of resources that users may incorporate into their systems, to expand the powers afforded to them. Incorporated into this are a number of pre-written programs (Effects), including the finer points of the command import antigravity.

                  An apprentice level Mage may, upon reading this text fully and thoroughly, buy their second level of Arete without the need for a Seeking. Rereading the text allows them to buy the first level of Correspondence at 7 exp instead of 8, and reading a third time will teach Forces 2 at 14 exp instead of 16. This Grimoire (or Principia as the VAs call it) is circulated on the internet as a PDF, though rare disk versions are available (and coveted) at high prices.

                      The Armor of God (Special Talisman)
                      Straight from Ephesians 6:10-18, the Armor of God is a set of equipment endowed with God's mighty power, so that the wearer may stand against the schemes of the devil. Belt of Truth, Breastplate of Righteousness, Shoes of Readiness, Shield of Faith, Helmet of Salvation, and Sword of Spirit. Naturally, such potent imagery has inspired faithful Mages, and motivated them to forge such items. Rarely, however, is the full set created at any given time, especially given the special, spiritual requirements involved. For obviously, any true believer understands that the Armor of God is, first and foremost, metaphorical of the qualities each piece represents. Truth, Righteousness, Faith, etc. So it is maintained by faithful Mages (of both the Celestial Chorus and Templar Knights) that before one can create and wield the Armor in a physical sense, one must embody those self-same qualities in their heart of hearts. It is said that the only known Master smith to produce the whole set ended up never using them, but instead disappeared shortly after its completion. The cynical claim this master was claimed by the Paradox of creating such dynamic items. But the preferred narrative says that this master used the process of forging the Armor as steps in his path to Ascension. Whether he truly transcended out of Enlightenment or because of True Faith so high it made him too holy for this wretched Earth, he left a legendary arsenal in the war against all evil things.

                      System: The legendary set is presumed to be one Talisman in multiple pieces, but more common incarnations find each as separate Wonders. The Belt of Truth uses Entropy 1 and/or Mind 1 to provide the wearer with the ability to tell truth from lies; a more potent version might even use greater Mind magick to force everyone in the area to speak truthfully. The Breastplate of Righteousness provides Armor +3, and when activated emits a holy light in all directions. The Shoes of Readiness make the wearer fleet-footed with Forces, or even allowing the wearer to cross vast distances like a pair of seven league boots; the downside is that the shoes must be worn for at least an hour before the Effects may be used. The Shield of Faith carries 3 dice of innate countermagic, in addition to its 2 armor dice when used defensively. The Helmet of Salvation protects the wearer from blandishments and temptations, providing a -3 difficulty to resist Mind Effects, or the powers of other supernaturals (infernal or otherwise); it is rumored that the true Helmet of Salvation, if placed on the head of another, cures insanity and dispels delusions in the rightness of evil causes. The Sword of Spirit, finally, deals aggravated damage, both to fleshy and spirit beings, and does Strength +2 damage.

                      Again, any one of these pieces of equipment could aid a believer greatly in their fight against the forces of darkness. To actually assemble the full set would be an entire chronicle unto itself.

                                  Marble of Second Hand Sight (4 Point Artifact)
                                  A common creation of Verbana with a practical bent, this glass eye can restore sight to those missing one. Numbers of this wonder skyrocket following periods of great war, mundane or otherwise. So popular is this function that many Mages from other Traditions seek them out, or elect to create their own. What makes this particular iteration special is its secondary function: it can be removed and placed as a watchful spy glass. (Without this function, the Artifact costs 2 points). These glass eyes come in a variety of eye colors, with some even being set with precious stones.

                                  System: The primary function uses a permanent Forces 1 Effect to provide the user with Light Sight...so, basically normal vision. Obviously, it can't be used by a person with two whole eyes (whether those eyes are functional or not), so it's only useful for those missing eyeballs entirely, or who are willing to make the sacrifice. At the start of every scene, the user rolls Arete (difficulty 4) to maintain the permanent Effect. Rolling 1 success is necessary to cancel the Perception penalty for a one-eyed person, and a person without functional eyes counts as one-eyed. Additional successes can make a no-eyed person have normal vision, and give a one-eyed person difficulty reductions to vision-based Perception (at -1 per success).

                                  The secondary function uses Forces 1 and Correspondence 2 to provide the benefits of sight from the glass eye, even when it is not in the person's head. The glass eye must have been inside the user's eye socket at least once in the past week, and the connection is severed if someone else puts the eye in their own socket (whether Mage or otherwise). To "prime" the Artifact for effect, the user breaths on the glass eye and polishes it briefly with a piece of fabric or wipe (tissue paper, handkerchiefs, and the front of a shirt all count). By popular demand, some wonder makers have created modified versions of this Artifact that allow a user to establish a connection by popping it in their mouth (in addition to the usual eye socket), for use by perfectly sighted individuals, though these are rare.

                                  At any time, the user may cover the eyelid the Marble once occupied, and begin seeing from the eye's perspective. This represents a rare instance of a Wonder that can be used without the Mage being in direct contact with it. This is facilitated by a weak sympathetic bond formed when the glass eye was inside the user's body. Unfortunately for the user, this also means the item has a connection that can be exploited for Correspondence magick. Because of the enchantment on the item, it always counts as a close Personal item of the last person who used it (regardless of how personal the item otherwise would be), so long as it is within a week of it being placed.

                                      Bottomless Pipe (2 Point Artifact or 3 Point Talisman; Arete 2, 5 Quintessence)
                                      Loathsome a habit though it may be in the popular consciousness now, smoking is and has been important to many people, in many parts of the world, for a few reasons. Since these people often include Mages, it's no surprise they've bent their wonder-making prowess to the problem of never having enough to smoke. Enter the Bottomless Pipe. Whether it's tobacco, cannabis, other psychedelic herbs, or less savory fare, these Artifacts have been created from North America to Japan for the purpose of always being full when the user needs it. They come in every shape and size imaginable, from the distinguished English calabash to the Native American ceremonial items, from the Japanese long-stemmed kiseru to the African carved gourds, from the Indian chillum to the glass bong of the modern hippie.

                                      Though not, surprisingly, the iconic crack pipe of the inner cities. This is not because Mages addicted to crack cocaine or meth don't have a desire for unlimited highs. Rather, it's because those so enslaved to these drugs tend to not have the patience for Wonder crafting. Those who do tend to either lose themselves to the drug if they have it, or suffer withdrawals and die or break the habit before the Wonder can be completed. This results in a relative scarcity of such pipes, for which most Mages are eternally grateful. Of a similar caliber is the Bottomless Opium Pipe, though it is rare for a different reason. During and following the Opium Wars in China, such wonders were actually comparatively common among the local Mages. But given that opium addiction unhindered by a need even for supply inevitably destroyed their creators - and any Mages who got their hands on the items - they were scourge on top of all other problems of the age. In a rare moment of cooperation that wouldn't be duplicated until the modern day, the Akashayana and Wu Lung made an agreement to destroy any such items they found, a pact that persists to this day.

                                      Less harmful Bottomless Pipes - usually of Tobacco or Marijuana - are less stigmatized within the greater Mage community, and thus proliferate more readily. Tobacco pipes have waned in popularity in recent years because of health risks. To circumvent this (and to smoke in peace), many Mages either create or layer additional enchantments onto their pipes that counteract the harmful effects of the smoke. It's also not uncommon for Mages to use their pipes as Instruments in other magickal Effects.

                                      System: The Talisman version of the Bottomless Pipe is as its name implies: it uses Matter 2 and Prime 2 to ensure it never runs out of material to smoke. However, it also costs a Quintessence point for each scene of nonstop smoking it provides. It's for this reason - and because of the reduced Sphere and Permanent Willpower requirements - that the Artifact version is far more common. This uses Matter 2 to convert any organic fibers placed inside it into Tobacco, Weed, or other relevant smoke-able. Water is obviously required for hookahs or bongs, but the items may be used with anything from cotton fiber to grass clippings to even hair. Since the bowls these organic materials go into are usually opaque, the act of smoking it is usually coincidental. There's nothing impossible about someone lighting literal grass in a pipe, and then inhaling the smoke. It's certainly odd as hell, but not impossible. Granted, if the Mage offers the pipe to a sleeper who witnessed the inert material going in, and they realize the switch, it might become vulgar really quickly. Owners must be careful, therefore, and load their pipes on the sly if they intend to share.

                                          Bag of Holding (4 Point Artifact)

                                          If this staple of high fantasy didn't already exist among the Order of Hermes, the creation of Dungeons and Dragons (or whatever equivalent exists in the World of Darkness) ensured that someone would try making it. Truthfully, storage devices capable of holding a functionally infinite number of items have been made (either as Instruments or full Wonders) throughout the world, whether they be bottomless chests or wardrobes, Akashic sackcloths, caves of wonders, or magicians top hats. Portability is merely one reason that the Wonder comes in bag form. The current popular iteration is the Backpack of Holding, worn by many former House Thig, now House Verditus alumni. But technological versions have cropped up among most technomancer groups, from the Data-powered briefcases of the NWO to the folded-space foldable gates of the Society of Ether.

                                          System: Regardless of form, the principle is the same. Correspondence 4 (or Data 4) and Matter 2 are used upon opening the container, allowing objects to be slipped into a small pocket located outside space. Living things aren't covered by this (without adding conjunctional Life 4), so only objects can be stored. At any time, the user may open the container and retrieve the object, usually while doing something sufficient to identify what they desire back. A common trend among classier Mages was to carry around a bag with a combination lock, with each possible combination opening to a separate storage space.

                                          Because it was used to store the items, the Bag of Holding remains connected to each of them. Any Mage may cast a Correspondence 1 Effect on the bag to sense that there are items stored "within" it. A Correspondence 2 Effect may be used to trace the items from the bag, allowing the Mage to know what items are connected to it. This may require a Wits + Enigmas roll, per Storyteller choice. Since the Bag of Holding is at least an Artifact (if not a Talisman), the Mage may retrieve said items afterwards, assuming they understand how. Many such receptacles have been found over the centuries, pregnant with treasure of masters long passed. Some bizarre instances have even contained their previous owners, through various mishaps or purposeful actions.

                                              Truly Universal Remote (10 Point Talisman; Arete 4)

                                              Technomancers of various stripes have employed remote controllers as Instruments. This potent Talisman was crafted by a an egotistical Virtual Adept to embody the power of universal control. Naturally, Paradox claimed its creator, but the Talisman itself has passed from hand to hand long afterwards. It was last seen at a garage sale in upstate New York.

                                              System: This remote is equipped with a number of built-in Effects, and can be employed by savvy Mages for still more if used as an Instrument. Different Time functions (buttons) allow the user to Pause, Rewind, and Fast Forward any person or object the remote is pointed at. Forces allows the user to manipulate ambient sound in the environment, making it louder, quieter, or muting sound entirely. This Sphere also affords the remote its most mundane function: the ability to remotely turn any electronic device on or off, at the touch of a button. Naturally, all but the last power are fairly vulgar.
