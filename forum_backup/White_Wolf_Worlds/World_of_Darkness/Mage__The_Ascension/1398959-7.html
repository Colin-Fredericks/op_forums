                                                On the Void Engineers: yes, the Week of Nightmares happened. And then the Dimensional Anomaly happened, the Void Engineers were decimated, Threat Null appeared along with an onslaught by Umbrood, and concerns about what's happening on Earth were once again on the back burner.

                                                Originally posted by Czernobog View Post
                                                Speaking about rotes - IMO they should definitely be more than a fluff thing, but allowing players to use them to transcend their arete and sphere dots seems a bit too strong (especially as Devices need to be charged with Quint and Enhancements both need to be charged and come with permadox).
                                                the idea I'm working with is that Rotes would need to be “charged” with XP instead of Quint (and would take longer to acquire), and that the Permadox/Genetic Flaws that Enhancements have come from them being an unnatural distortion of the subject's Pattern; Rotes aren't. Though I wouldn't be against the notion that performing an Effect by Rote is actually disadvantaged in some way if you don't have sufficient Spheres or Arete to back it up.

                                                But the real reason behind my suggestion is that it enables formalizing the hack that appeared in Gods & Monsters for Hedge Magicians: rather than handling Hedge Magic as a separate magic system that requires a page count comparable to the Sphere Magic system, you can handle Sleeper Magic using an extension to the Awakened Magick system that takes up maybe an additional page or two; and Rotes that transcend Arete and Sphere limits are central to that.

                                                And that also factors into the suggestion of making Hedge Magicians a bigger part of the setting: you can't do that if you have to essentially double the page count devoted to magic to present separate Sleeper and Awakened systems; we're already having to make space-saving considerations such as whether or not to devote space to Tradition/Convention/Craft write-ups in the core book or to put the bulk of that material in a supplement and focus on independent mages in the core book. When those are the sorts of decisions under consideration, two separate magic system's in the core book is a non-starter.

                                                So if you want to pick up on the “Rise of the Extraordinary Citizen” metaplot idea introduced in the Revised NWO Convention Book (i.e., “ever since the Dimensional Anomaly rolled around, the number of Extraordinary Citizens has spiked; and nobody knows why”), you're going to need something like what I'm describing.

                                                And conceptually, I much prefer the notion of Sleeper Magic as a subset of Awakened Magick: that is, the Sleeper magicians have a Focus (Paradigm, Practice, and Instruments) just like the Awakened do; but because the Sleeper magicians don't have Arete or Spheres, the only way they can practice magic is by Rote.

                                                If you still want Sorcerers who use Hedge Magic Paths in your games, you can publish a Sorcerer II book that goes back to World of Darkness: Sorcerer's notion that Hedge Magic is a distinct phenomenon from Awakened Magick, with the added twist that it's also distinct from the Sleeper Magic that would be featured in M5. This new edition of Sorcerer would exist to provide the rest of the World of Darkness with magicians without using Mage to do so. In that book, I would have mages explicitly recognize that Hedge Magic is no more related to Sphere Magick than vampiric Disciplines are. I wouldn't even publish the book as a M5 supplement; it would be a generic World of Darkness book in much the same way the original Sorcerer was.

                                                And as such, it wouldn't really be relevant to M5. For M5, I'd insist that Hedge Magic never even gets referenced; just use the “Magic by Rote” option to handle acolytes and extraordinary citizens who actually know a trick or two.

                                                My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                        Originally posted by Czernobog View Post
                                                        Unless you spend XP every time you cast a rote that's not really comparable. The drawback of Devices is that they have to be recharged every so often with Quint or Tass - and if you run out of such, they stop working.
                                                        Ah. I misunderstood what you were saying; I thought you were talking about the Quintessence that goes into creating a Device or Talisman, not the Quintessence that's needed to use it. In that regard, I have no trouble whatsoever saying that each use of a Rote that's operating beyond your Spheres costs a point of Willpower.

                                                        Originally posted by Czernobog View Post
                                                        I'm looking at this from a game design perspective, not a lore one. From an OOG point of view, Permadox is intended to balance having a Device implanted that is extremely difficult to remove on account of it being literally part of your character's body. Genetic Flaws ... ugh. They don't seem to have much mechanical support, literally including things like &quot;is bald&quot; or &quot;talks quickly&quot;.
                                                        Game design follows from lore; or at least it should. In this case, the reasoning behind the drawbacks of an Enhancement are a reflection of the fact that the character didn't develop the Enhancement's capabilities himself; so he has to pay some sort of price for taking a shortcut to power. Rotes, as I'm envisioning them, wouldn't be a shortcut: you spent the time and effort to learn them (as represented by the xp expenditure), so it's now a natural party of you rather than an invasive and decidedly unnatural alteration. Thus, no additional drawbacks beyond the aforementioned Willpower cost.

                                                        Originally posted by Czernobog View Post
                                                        I'd be happy to use &quot;Magic by Rote&quot; to model the actions of sorcerers, but allowing PC mages to go above their sphere and Arete ratings seems to remove the point of Arete and Spheres.
                                                        Two points here:

                                                        First, it doesn't remove the point of Arete and Spheres at all, any more than Enhancements do. You're still better off in the long run developing your Arete and Spheres than relying on your Rotes; it's just that the Rotes give you a preview of what the Spheres are capable of. What the Spheres bring to the table that a collection of Rotes doesn't is versatility: each Rote lets you do one thing; what a dot in a Sphere lets you do is… well, very much not just one thing.

                                                        Second: I'm not talking about modeling sorcerers using “Magick by Rote”; I'm talking about presenting a separate kind of Sleeper magician from Sorcerers and Hedge Magic, one that literally is a Sleeper using Awakened Magick in a limited way. I am not saying “use Magick by Rote if you don't have access to the Hedge Paths rules”; I'm saying “consider using Magick by Rote even if you do have access to the Hedge Paths rules”.

                                                        And with that clarified (I hope), one of the differences between these “Rote-only mages” and sorcerers is that these “rote magicians” can't do anything that an Awakened Mage can't, while a sorcerer can. If a rote magician can perform an Effect that he has studied and mastered without having the Arete and Spheres that define an Effect, then an Awakened Mage should be able to put in the same time and effort to study and master an Effect without having the Arete and Spheres that define that Effect — though if he attempts to perform that Effect by Rote before he has developed his Spheres to the point that he doesn't need to do it by Rote, he has to labor under the same restrictions as the Rote Magician.

                                                        Originally posted by Czernobog View Post
                                                        Also, HAP/PBD should be enshrined as the definitive way magic works. RBD and the like should be variant options located in the Storyteller's Guide.
                                                        As a general rule of thumb, decisions should be made by the developers on each of the optional rules for Magick that appeared in M20, with the magick system in M5 either incorporating the rule as an integral part of the system or ignoring it; and the various optional rules that get left out of the core book could then appear in a supplement (such as an M5 Storyteller's Handbook).

                                                        That said, one reason Brucato presented the notion of Subtle/Uncanny/Extreme in Mage Made Easy was an attempt to get around the whole “HAB/HOO, RBD/PBD” issue by shifting the decision to something easier to judge. I'm not sure he succeeded; but it was a valiant attempt, and I wouldn't mind if M5 were to try something similar.
