    Originally posted by Muad'Dib View Post
    So you think it is possible for other Nightfolk to reach some kind of agreement and understanding with the Nephandi ?



    From what I read in the M:tA books, it doesn't seem so to me, at all. They seem like a faction of a type of Mages that would be called "Black Mages" by many ; if you need a criterion, then a definite one would be a large measure of contempt for mundane humans and disagreement with the idea of the so called "Ascension" . Additional qualities of the Nephandi seem to be a certain kind of selfishness, loyalty to one's group, and willingness to take risks.

    I think what makes the fact that the Nephandi don't wan't ruin the world especially visible is that the three main factions of the Nephandi - the Infernal Nephandi, the Malfean Nephandi and the K'llasshaa Nephandi - seem to be capable of cooperating on a large scale and in a variety of ways. I think that a purely hostile faction agenda and goals would not have united such different and varied groups of Mages.


    M20 Core Book outright says that the Nephandi are not servile and that they don't serve the Nephandi patrons in the sense of having strict, very limiting obligations.
    The Nephandi Mages can relate to the Nephandi patrons in various ways - some could be called their majordomos or representatives ; others view the patrons as a distant source of inspiration, others see a way to learn more, for example through direct delving or contact, or by the way of comparisons and contrasting from afar.



    Also you might ask from where comes the "destroy the world" part that often gets repeated in descriptions of Nephandi ? I would say it is either the Traditions' and the Technocracy's (and maybe other groups of Mages are also involved) propaganda, or maybe what the Nephandi tell to other Mages to scare them.
    Or maybe the Nephandi sometimes tell this and other things - or try to create such an impression, and other impressions - to other Mages to create a certain kind of atmosphere that definitely separates the Nephandi from other Mages, events and relations wise. The result is that the Nephandi Mages as a faction are in a kind of isolation, which benefits them in a variety of ways. For example it enables them to make recruitment into a multi-layered "dark" 'temptation' , that can also be done safely for both the recruiting Nephandus and the potential candidate. It is so much easier to arrange for safety of a potential Nephandus when all the friends of a Mage leave them and take five steps back while saying "You joined the Nephandi, and they literally want to destroy the world !" ; this reaction of shock makes arranging of safety easier.
    Imagine a Tradition Cabal trying to describe the Nephandi, with this extreme perspective of theirs, to a young, new Tradition Mage - this perspective of the Nephandi is so far away from truth and so blown out of proportion that the Tradition Mages are to an extent limited in their capabilities of acting against - both directly and indirectly - the Nephandi Mages ; due to, for example, their irrational outlook on the Nephandi faction and the Nephandi Mages.


    I'm under no delusions that everything would be peaceful between everybody, nor even that most would agree with them. But I do believe that certain individuals or even groups of individuals would be in favor a new world envisioned by the Nephandi. Many of the antagonists factions such as the Sabbat, and the Thallain hate the current state of the world and wish for a return to how things used to be. When they were free to lord themselves over the world, instead of hiding in the shadows like lesser beings.

    The Sabbat only care about the Masquerade in the sense that it ensures their immediate continued survival. Their ultimate goal is to restore the world to a state prior to the inquisition, when vampires were free to act openly in society, and hideous Szlachta prowled the streets at night looking for spare parts.

    The Fomorians and their creations likewise ruled during a time of chaos and barbarism, before those pesky Tuatha taught mortals how to act like civilized beings. Reducing mortals back into primitive neanderthals wouldn't be so much 'destroying' the world for them, as it would be restoring it to its proper form.


    Or, if we want to go even further, we could say that the new universe the Nephandi envision doesn't even have humanity in it. Mortals had their chance to rule the world and they royally fucked it up. As mages, the Nephandi could create a new universe where the monsters and other supernatural entities have alternative means of survival besides everybody relying on the same species.

    We're talking about entities who have the power to shape reality according to their will. The possibilities are only limited by the player's imagination. Just because the universe that exists right now is going bye-bye doesn't mean there is no more World of Darkness. It just means we're getting a... New World of Darkness.


    And yeah, I agree that the interpretation of the Nephandi is probably just overblown propaganda. There are two sides to every story and most people don't like to paint their enemies in a positive light. Traditionalists see the Technocracy as stifling and tyrannical. The Technocracy see Traditionalists as dangerous fools. If you're looking at things from their side of the fence, then sure, they might appear to be that way at first glance. But if you try to see things from the other side and explore their ideas further, it becomes clear that things are not so black and white.

    The reason they paint the Nephandi as such reprehensible beings is because nobody on their side is willing consider the idea that maybe the universe has run its course and that we should start over from scratch.



    Hell, I just realized as I was typing this line that many of the ancient cultures believed in a cycle of destruction and recreation. The gods would create a universe, it would progress for a time, then once things were looking pretty messed up, they'd send down some event (like say, the End Times) to destroy everything. After which, the surviving powers would sweep up the pieces and build a new universe in its place.

    How do we know this cycle isn't sustained by the efforts of the Nephandi and their 'evil' patrons?
