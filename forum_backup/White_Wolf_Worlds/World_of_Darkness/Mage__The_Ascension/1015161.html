    Pattern Mages should not be boring.

    12-10-2016, 01:12 AM
    The designers have missed the potential of this Essence, relegating it to mostly Technocrats and Sleepers. Pattern doesn't have to be about the status quo--patterns are more often about meaning. Finding patterns is a key feature of creative and mystically-oriented minds. You could find worse definitions of mysticism than the inclination to find meaningful patterns in the world.

    The prototypical mystic mage with a Pattern Essence isn't some stodgy conformist. He's an eccentric with an intricately structured view of the world that is unique to him. His life is dominated by ritual. Everyday events are imbued with meaning. Pattern could easily be as common among Tradition Mages as Dynamism, or more common.

          Ditto that agreement. An orderly nature does not preclude having interesting facets to oneself, or having interesting goals or conflicts. It also doesn't cause one to cease having human dramas, as the base troubles of humankind can strike architects and anarchists in equal measure. Pattern Mages still love, suffer loss, butt heads with peers, become subject to moral dilemmas, etc. Certainly, they are inclined towards putting the world around them in its proper place, but the world resists such attempts at every turn. And they can be guilty of hubris, just the same as any Mage. Moreso, perhaps, because they try to impose a system of their making on Reality.

          Even Technocrats are not immune to this. They aren't all Machines (and even those with the Machine Nature are vulnerable to chinks in their armor, or the results of their inflexible thinking). They are human beings (more or less). An NWO Perfectionist must often compromise their need for order in the process of accomplishing goals, leading to internal conflict. A heavily augmented Iterator cyborg may find more control of their body, but words like "tinman" and "steelhead" may wound him deeply, alienating him from humanity because of his modifications. Building a black market drug empire can prove way more messy for the Obsessive Compulsive Progenitor than they originally thought, in addition to weighing heavily on their conscience.

              I didn't though this was the prevalent view of the pattern essence.                   The way I always viewed it was Pattern Mages move slowly, but when they do move it's like an Avalanche. A Pattern mage will sit there and build something that lasts working out every kink. Working out everything that could go wrong.

                  As an Example, Two Hermetics, One with a Pattern Essence, and one with Dynamic Essence, are trying to destroy a Technocrat stronghold.

                  The Pattern one will go about forging magical swords, Rings of Power, Solid things that, should he die, will live on and aid his allies in the fight.

                  The Dynamic one will hunt down Tass, and perform some, previously, untried ritual to grant him a power boost. Meeting the same power as the Pattern Hermetic, but leaving no institutions or notes behind.

                  Pattern is about Building and Protecting. You take what's there, put it into order, and defend that order. Whatever it may be.

                  Dynamic is about Creation and Freedom. You make something new, and so aren't bound by the existing order, but at the same time.... you aren't pre-disposed to make lasting things.

                          A lot the time I've noticed that the Essence works hand in hand with the Nature, and often with the Demeanor as well.

                          In our current game we have two Pattern Essence mages:

                          One is a NWO CSI-type. Or he was a CSI-type before he was tossed into a group of Tradition mages. His nature is Idealist. He's always looking for ways to create situations that improve things for as many people as possible. That means when he's making plans (and that's half of what he does), his plans have to take account the effects as much as the objective. The other half of what he does is look for patterns around him to figure out what is going on. Lastly, his Demeanor is Conformist. He wears his MiB suit to the exclusion of anything else (if it will be a problem, he'll put up a Mind 2 "Don't pay attention to what I'm wearing" before he'll consider a disguise). He takes care of his that poor suit and remains meticulous in his personal appearance and regular in his habits (or as regular as he can be considering the circumstances). He gets upset when the situation gets chaotic and goes out of his way to try to regain a sense of order. Because of his behavior the other (player& their) character's consider him the leader of the group. He's all about Pattern in about six ways, top to bottom. But he isn't boring. He's careful, sure, but he's decisive. He may be passive in that he is frequently not a primary actor, but the primary actors are usually acting based on his directions.

                          The other is a Verbena who is the party caregiver and sort of their moral center. His nature is Benefactor. He's all about aiding the others, whether that's healing them when they get hurt or supporting them in achieving their objectives. He's not as oriented towards patterns in the world at large as the NWO guy, but he does go out of his way to help out people outside the group, especially in trying to set them up in situations where they won't get into the same difficulties again. However, his Rogue Demeanor doesn't really reflect much of that. He is a hillbilly rumrunner from Prohibition. He's constantly sipping hooch from his hip flask (which never seems to empty...). He can be rip-roaring hell-raiser and is the group's primary up close and personal hand to hand fighter. (He's also really really quiet. He has the Shy Flaw and used to have a horrible stutter). But there are reasons for his behavior. That was "fitting in" back in the 20's when he was part of a gang comprised of his brothers and a cousin or two in a constant cat and mouse game with revenuers. Now his behavior is slanting more towards standing back and watching as he tries to figure what the dynamic is for this new group and how he fits into it.

                          Neither one of them is boring or pedantic or passive.
