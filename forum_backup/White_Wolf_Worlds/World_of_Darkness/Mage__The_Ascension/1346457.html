    Attribute specialties vs Ability/Talent specialties

    11-06-2019, 11:00 AM
    Hola!

    So as it's come time for my character to begin picking some specialties, I've noticed but one more annoying little discrepancy about M20.

    For the most part, specialties are relatively straight forward- but what happens as your character progresses into lategame, and you begin to have more Attributes/Talents at 4+ than not? It seems to me that one could easily shoehorn himself by accident.

    Attribute specialties and Talent specialties cannot coincide; ie, one cannot get a Melee specialty and a Dexterity specialty that provide the bonus on 10s for the same roll. Seems like common sense, right? After all, we don't want people rolling 10s at 3 succ if that were the case. In the same sense; If one has a Stealth specialty for Sneaking already, one cannot also get a Dexterity specialty for Sneaking (but one could get a Dexterity specialty for Ambush instead of Sneaking, it's a tricky distinction, right?).

    So, like I said, the problem seems to arrive with me; I began to take specialties in Abilities as my character progressed- and have now found myself incredibly limited and shoehorned as to what Attribute specialties I can take, so as to not double up on their effects.

    What Dexterity specialties could a person consider taking; if they already have Stealth 5, Melee 5, Athletics 5... and multiple specialties for each talent? There seems to come a point where it becomes superfluous. ''Well, you could take Lightning Reflexes...'' - ah but then what would you take at Dodge 4 and 5? Catching my drift? And this goes for all attributes; but mostly Dexterity because it's literally rolled with nigh every physical action one can take.


    All that aside..

    Wits seems to be incredibly useless. Only 2 rolls involve it in all of m20 (outside of Certamen); Seafaring/Sailing and Deception. For example, listed specialties out of m20 for Wits are as follow -

    "Specialties: Combat Reflexes, Street Survivor, Cunning, Feral, Fox-Witted "

    So after looking at those, you as well as myself are probably wondering ''Well what on earth could X, Y, and Z be?" For instance; combat reflexes. What is that? It's not like you can get an Initiative specialty; and I'm not exaggerating when I suggest Wits is the most underrolled attribute in m20. I simply can't find a use for it. "Feral''. What the hell is that? It's like the m20 authors just added words without even stopping to consider what their possible functions could be.

    Like I said; wits is rolled a few times in certamen, like for blocking- so a Wits specialty could be taken there, perhaps; but that still isn't indicative of the ones suggested here, or by Wit's base function as it is.


    Could you guys help me with my creative limit here? It's not that I don't understand the function of specialties, rather, I can't seem to actually think of USEFUL ones to use (specifically for Wits); and the fact that Dexterity is rolled with basically every action makes it hard to pick an appropriate Specialty for it, without having to sacrifice that same specialty for the correlative Ability/Talent.

      The core Specialty issue is an issue that goes back way farther than M20 (and well beyond Mage).

      Ultimately, yes, if you're not careful, you can end up with Specialties that overlap a lot and rather seem valuable. And the game allows you to pick Specialties that seem like they apply primarily to unrolled mechanics with no benefit for that.

      Though in some of your examples you're being very specific with your Attribute Specialties; those are supposed to be broader. You shouldn't have a Dexterity specialty that so close to Stealth.

      Wits is a fairly common "ad-hoc" Attribute in play, as it's going to be frequently called on when one of the sample rolls in the book doesn't apply; the example Specialties kind of reinforce the idea that you're going to be using it more on the fly.

              I'm a bit suprised by the downplaying of Wits here;
              Wits is needed every time a character has to mentally quickly react to something - as opposed to Intelligence, which is more aimed at moments where the character has time to really think and analyze, or Dexterity which is more physical reflexes. Or social attributes, when you had time to prepare or are not under time-constraining pressure.

              Wits+Alertness is a roll that was heavily used in my last Tabletop to quickly react to sudden events that sprung out of nowhere - while Wits+Subterfuge, Wits+leadership or Wits+Empathy is a combo used when the character is required to quickly deescalate a situation, talk down a person from doing something stupid under pressure (say, prevent a jumper froim doing the deed) or come up with a lie on the fly, when you don't have much time to think of something.

              You can spin that on and on - Whenever the character has to mentally react quickly and *now* instead of physically or having the leisure to pause and think, A character with a low Wits score is easily beaten in many situations by taking the leisure and comfort of time away from them.

                      Originally posted by ChiefFirewater
                      Attribute specialties and Talent specialties cannot coincide; ie, one cannot get a Melee specialty and a Dexterity specialty that provide the bonus on 10s for the same roll. Seems like common sense, right? After all, we don't want people rolling 10s at 3 succ if that were the case.
                      The way I do this, they can coincide, but the bonus does not overlap.

                      That is, if you apply Specialty to a roll you gain one extra success for each 10 of that roll regardless of the amount of Specialties you have that could apply to that roll. Because specialties can be very broad they may apply to several rolls, it's not rare to have two that could apply to a single one. But there's no additional advantage.

                      This is a very average RPG practice to avoid spamming bonus sources (D&D 5 and Proficiency come to mind), and I'm pretty sure it's the RAW for Mage (but I would have to look). Having specialties that overlap too much it's sub-optimal this way, but nothing prevents you from having them.
                      Other than that, I think this is an issue you should bring to your ST.

                      It's the ST job to remember that Skills/Atributes exist, and to use them. In your grup Wits gets under used, in other groups Intelligence get's under used or even Charisma/Manipulation as they preffer to interpret dialog rather than to roll.

                      You could also suggest your ST what Attribute + Skill you want to use for a given task, reasoning why it would be plausible to do so. Maybe the ST just didn't thought about that approach at the moment.

                                  I tend to view attributes as 'innate ability' whereas talents/abilities are more specialized training/knowledge. Exceptional attributes may give you some innate level of 'ability' or compensate for lack of knowledge/skill but knowledge/skill taches you how to use what you have effectively.

                                  That makes which is 'better' entirely situational, although depending on how one wanted to play it you could also say that having both high would be ideal (talent + knowledge)
