        The Nephandi are not nihilists. Nihilists believe there is no such thing as objective meaning. That is to say, that "good" and "evil" do not exist in the sense that the laws of thermodynamics exist. Nihilists do not want to destroy the universe. It's just that if they want to protect the universe or help people, they accept that their desire to protect the universe and/or help people is just a personal preference, neither "good" nor "evil".Nephandi, conversely, DO believe in objective good and evil, and are on evil's side.

                                    The Nephandi of M20 are not the Nephandi of the earlier editions. Brucato remade them for M20 to reflect a rather traumatic episode in his life that occurred sometime after he left Mage: a girl he knew befriended him and through that friendship very nearly tore his relationships apart. That's why M20 consistently focused on the “true Nephandi” being a distinctly human evil that operates by getting their enemies “shooting at shadows… or at one another… while winning them over to your point of view, all the while seeming like their best friend in the world.” (p.228)

                                    These new Nephandi are explicitly not “slaves of the star-squid” (pp.225–226): “They don't serve Outer Gods, in truth. Nephandi simply recognize such beings as reflections of their own Enlightenment… a savage wisdom that see the Absolute in every living thing and knows what's waiting at the end of All.” (p.226)

                                    And they're not interested in what comes next, if indeed they believe that there's anything that comes next: it's not “burn everything down in order to let something new grow”; it's “burn everything down”. If they can talk you into burning things down by hinting that it's a step toward a renewal, they're more than willing to do so; but that hint of a renewal is merely a ploy, not something they believe. No; the most sympathetic their actual view is is that they're performing a “mercy killing”: Reality is dying a slow, painful death, and there's no hope for a recovery; so they're trying to hurry it along. And even that argument might just be a ploy to lure you deeper into their philosophy.

                                    They're the true villains of the game; and Brucato changed them in M20 to reflect a true evil that he hadn't realized existed before he encountered it personally.

                                    The Nephandi of the earlier editions still exist; but they've been demoted to being “stalking horses” for M20's brand of Nephandi: they're the shadows mentioned in the above quote, the distractions the true Nephandi get you shooting at while they become your best friend.

                                                For a long time, my interpretation of the Nephandi have been as people whose internal drives are geared towards the corrupt, cruel, destructive, and profane. It's not a matter of Reason - there's nothing reasonable about the Nephandi's goals, though they spin a lot of Logic about them that can entice or fool other people. Rather, their philosophical arguments exist to justify - to themselves more than anyone - what they feel in their hearts.

                                                What they feel, of course, being hate, loathing, bitterness, and sadism. While any normal person can learn to gain joy from cruelty or satisfaction from annihilation, for the Nephandi these are their ONLY pleasures. It's an emotional problem more than anything, and it's a problem packaged into their spiritually damaged Avatar. Their only positive reinforcement - their only spiritual fulfillment - arises from being the greatest bastards they can be. Every other aspect of Nephandic philosophy is built around rationalizing this in the Nephandus's mind, and secondarily towards giving a framework to be fed to other people in the effort of conversion.

                                                Under this framework, it's not impossible for a Widderslainte to be "redeemed", at least in theory. It's just that being genuinely good comes neither naturally nor easily to a person born with an inverted Avatar. Nephandi, like normal people, have a free will that is complicated by internal drives that predispose them towards valuing certain results over others. Whereas normal humans usually value things like love, prosperity, and generation (some to greater degrees than others), Nephandi value hate, tragedy, and destruction.

                                                It's also for this reason that I don't dismiss the classic Nephandic trifecta of Infernalist/Malfean/K'llasshaa out of hand. Sure, I agree with Brucato in that many Nephandi would see no reason to follow Dark Powers. But I see that more as being for the same reason that normal people are sometimes atheists. They don't need some "Absolute" to justify their hate. They may even see such a thing as patently absurd, when the world is "so obviously meaningless".

                                                On the other hand, I can totally see other Nephandi taking a measure of comfort from worshiping a Dark Power. The gods that "normal" people serve reinforce many of the qualities that humanity as a whole desires; there are gods of love (and lust), justice, fertility, knowledge, and sport. For a Nephandus, these deities tend to look down upon at least some of the Nephandi's practices and desires. They can't relate to these religions, or to their adherents.

                                                The existence of Dark Gods, though, is a breath of foul air for a type of mage that has only known suffocating freshness and purity, to their dismay. A dark god encourages and validates a Fallen mage's feelings, and makes their urges seem almost right. For the Malfean mage, the Wyrm is the ultimate personification of blessed Death, calling upon those in the material world to slide everything towards oblivion. The K'llasshaa have their Great Ones, whose mentalities are as alien to the material world as the Nephandi feel themselves to be, or at least the Nephandi think so. Infernalists probably buy into the hype least, but they can probably empathize with beings for whom Evil is their only Good.

                                                Regardless of how sincere an individual Nephandus may be in service to the Dark Powers, there's something comforting about not being alone in their desire to further the cause of Greater Evil. This is also likely why, even if Nephandi can't stand each other, they aren't also at each other's throats (mostly). They may feel loathing for their peers (just as their loath humanity, life, and themselves), but those other Fallen bastards are also the only ones who really "get it".

                                                So every bit of casual betrayal between Fallen "buddies" is taken in stride. They'll all come back in their next life to do it all again, so there's no point in letting hard feelings disrupt cooperation too much. And Nephandi have to become very used to doing unpleasant things (acting good, or tolerating other Nephandi) in order to fulfill their dark desires in the long run.
