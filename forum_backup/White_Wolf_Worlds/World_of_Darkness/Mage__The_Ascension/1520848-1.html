                            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                            Earlier editions have already done the “strip the Traditions of their cultures” thing already, with such moves as having the Euthanatoi include factions from India, Scandinavia, and Ireland, or how the Dreamspeakers are “a bunch of magical traditions from all over the world united only by their belief in animism”. The Celestial Chorus lumps in anything that could remotely be considered monotheistic; and so on.
                            Earlier editions didn't strip away their cultures, they added to them. They made the Traditions more nuanced, by giving more touchpoints with Real Life cultural practices.

                            What Paradox has been doing, and what it will likely do with M5, is take away connections to Real Life. Flatten the setting and its participants, not deepen them. That is the difference.

                            Anyone could play a "broad strokes" version of a fantasy mage archetype. It was perfectly acceptable. It's just that if you wanted to dig deeper, you could go very deep. Because Real Life magical practices are stranger and more nuanced than anything a game developer could make up. As MtAs went on, it added more reference points for the Tradition archetypes at play. So it was easier to play a Mage in a deep way, if you wanted to.

                            M5 is set to make playing a broad and nonspecific mage character non-optional. You WILL play a generic, street level urban fantasy character with a magic system cribbed from a used book store's New Age aisle. Or M5 has nothing to offer you.

                                    If M5 was modeled in the same way as H5 and W5 and following Justin Achilli's interpretation of MtAs (the core conflict is self-determination (Traditions) vs. authoritarianism (Technocracy) ), this is how I would picture M5:
                                    • The Technocracy is winning
                                    • The Technocracy is evil and is willing to accept all sorts of ills of humanity (misinformation, conspiracy theories, financial corruption, addiction to technology, environmental exploitation etc.) for the sake of maintaining control
                                    • The Traditions have failed, especially the older, experienced Mages who are too stuck in their ways
                                    • The only way to exist is to forge your own path to Ascension (the emphasis is on individual interpretations of Tradition-creeds rather than belonging to an organization)
                                    • Ascension for humanity has never been further away
                                    • Instead, helping and pushing individual people towards Ascension is the focus (aka "street-level")
                                    • Sphere-magic is severely limited by a PC's own perspective on their magic (the definition of what counts as "vulgar" is broadened)
                                    • Paradox-dice are there to punish players for either trying to do magic outside their limited "lane" (defined by the PC's own perspective on their magic) or trying magic that's considered too powerful by the system

                                                    I've actually been giving this a lot of thought recently. I agree that it would be all be impossible to divorce the Traditions from their cultural roots. They are just too ingrained for some of them, although I do think that the Dreamspeakers could be saved, as their Tradition was not founded in a specific geographical location as many of the other ones. I've been experimenting with tying a Mage's Avatar in a way that V5 does with Predator Type, i.e. having a Avatar drive a Mage into getting certain traits even before Awakening and giving bonus die to those traits based a irl magical archetypes. For instance, a Pattern Avatar Mage following the Alchemist archetype would get an extra dot in Matter, an extra dot in either Stamina or Manipulation, an extra dot in Science or Persuasion, and an extra dot in Resources. This alchemist Mage would also get bonuses in casting Matter effects and gain dots in Matter faster than other spheres.
                                                    This of course would mean that one doesn't get their Primary sphere from their Tradition. Instead, they would get possible Secondary Spheres that they would learn a little slower than their primary, but still faster than other spheres. Instead, a Mage would get an Initiation similar to a Legacy Attainment from M:tAw, a magical effect that would not trigger Paradox. Each such Initiation would give a Major function that would cease to function if they changed Traditions, and a Minor function that could possibly stay with them otherwise. This Initiation would also have some sort of drawback that could cause problems in the long run either to the Mage themselves or how they interact with others. Such abilities have been hinted at, such as how Dreamspeakers can interact with Changelings and the Dreaming without being enchanted, but have never been fully explored.
                                                    These changes are just the basic ones I've thought about that wouldn't break the game too badly, but I'm still afraid that M5 could be the one that really could end the new editions for a lot of people, although I have spotted some fans elsewhere on social media that 'like' how the new games are doing.

                                                        M20 has already divorced Primary Sphere from Tradition; so to me, that's a non-issue: Traditions haven't been the determinators of a mage's Magick for about ten years now.

                                                        I do think that there ought to be more to Avatars and their relationship to the mage; but I'd leave it up in the air as to whether the Avatar guided the mage toward a particular Focus before the Awakening, or if the proto-mage's experiences and Awakening shaped the nature of the Avatar. Either way, the Avatar ought (normally) to be compatible with the mage's Focus; if it isn't, you have something akin to M20's Demented Eidolon Flaw, but more generalized. One thing I could see them doing would be to say that Avatars can be altered, kind of like how Mage: the Awakening allows mages to shape their souls to get Attainments. But in Mage: the Ascension, this generally isn't a good thing: it's a generalization of the Inverted Avatars of the Nephandi and the Permanent Quiet of the Marauders. I would, in this way, say that a Technocrat's Genius is a similarly altered Avatar, but with the effect being less blatant: it “merely” results in the Genius being Circumspect and the Technocrat not manifesting Supernatural Merits.

                                                        Have you seen the STV supplement Prism of Focus? In it, each Practice is assigned a strength and a weakness — one thing that it's especially good at, and another thing that it's not so good at. It also has Specialized Practices, variants of the standard Practices developed by various Traditions, Conventions, and Crafts that gain an additional benefit above and beyond what the standard Practice normally provides. I'd like to see something along these lines implemented in M5.

                                                        But the more I think about it, the less need I see for the Traditions themselves: nearly everything that I found useful about them in the earlier editions it's handled by Focus in M20; and going back to the M1e/2e/Rev centrality of Tradition to Magick would be a massive step backward, IMHO.

                                                        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
