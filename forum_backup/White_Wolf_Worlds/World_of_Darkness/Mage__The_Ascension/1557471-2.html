    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    OK, rather than doing my own big response to all that... I gotta ask... where do you think you're going with all that? If you're trying to prove that the Nephandi have a point, it feels a lot more like you're missing what the books are saying about them than communicating that conclusion. The idea that you're conflating a potential cyclical nature to the WoD (something hinted at by the number of factions that believe in a wheel of ages cosmology, but not something ever demonstrated conclusively) with the idea that the Nephandi just want to end this instantiation of the universe doesn't really hold. The Nephandi aren't trying to reset the world and replace it with something better. They're trying to end it - including an potential new versions of it - and will settle for making it as worse as possible in the meantime; including making worse new instantiations if necessary.

    It also feels like you're grasping a lot. Not just from Mage, but from other game lines. You're pulling in things that seem far outside of their context or relevance to this discussion. For example while the WtA cosmology clearly puts the Triat in a cyclical nature, it never posits that it exists as part of a cosmic cycle of universal instantiations. They operate on a much more direct cyclical nature of creation, existence, and destruction that befits the WtA animist assumptions. The Wyld, Weaver, and Wyrm operate solely on the universe they currently exist in with no known care for preexisting or potential future ones. That's no what they "do" as much as one can discuss the motives of such entities.

    This also holds true to equivocating about the idea of the Traditions wanting to "end" the world. There is a massive difference between wanting to change the world in a massive way, and wanting to cause the world to cease to exist and ensure it never comes into existence again. "The end" can be used in an imperfect sense where it signifies a transition rather than cessation ("the end of an era" does not mean that time itself has ended), or a perfect sense where in means the total lack of what previously was ("death is the end of life" means at least for an individual that there was a living organism and now there is dead matter as what made it alive is now ended). It doesn't help your points to swap between the two meanings.

    So again, where are you really trying to go with all this?
    My original point was to highlight the fact Nephandi are actually playable, due to how Ascension presents a free form sandbox style approach to magic. But at some point, we got sidetracked and it became a discussion about rationalizing their long term objective.


    That said, I'm still reluctant to accept the idea that all Nephandi want to completely erase reality from existence, as that kind of makes the whole Exies faction redundant. Once again, the end times scenario definitely proves this is not the case, as the K'llsshaa end up taking over the world after the Outer Lords are brought into our reality.


    Even the Nephandi's own wikipedia page, and youtube videos states that a lot of them seek to reshape the world into something that is more appropriate in line with their own corrupted world view. Such a view may be anathema to humanity on a conceptual level, but that doesn't mean the universe is going to blip out of existence.


    -------------------------

    One idea I see as being a potential inspiration comes from the games 'Lust for Darkness" and "Lust from Beyond' It's a story about a cult of pleasure seekers who are trying to free an imprisoned god that caused its home universe to stagnate by making society so obsessed with indulging carnal pleasure, that they lost the ability to focus on anything else. Their every moment, every thought, every action is motivated solely by the overwhelming desire to engage in sexual debauchery.

    Society has collapsed, the very concept of self control has become alien to them, and the people have devolved into mindless sex addicts. Yet, despite its flaws, their god's universe continues to exist.

                We clearly have severe ontological differences in how to approach the material. Instead of looking to the game in order to perceive the core message, themes and moods that it's trying to convey, there's these... "appeals to lore", that are at best out of context and, at worst, disingenuous. Instead of taking the discussion to the themes that are explicitly in the books, there's these "gotcha/ummm actually" answers that ignore core fundamentals of the books, as if the writers themselves can't grasp their own work. It's there: Hope and Transformation; Defiance and Reflection. On the very pages I cited (and that I won't cite anymore, for the risk that I will become a parody of myself), but actually Consensus and Paradox are these gargatuan forces that impede player agency. So why play at all? The use of paradigm "The paradigm of the Dreamspeaker/Sons of Ether/etc." as if there is a notion of a unified paradigm throughout the factions, ignoring the indiosyncrasies of belief portrayed in Mage and how paradigm, as a mechanic, interacts with other aspects of the setting, like focus and instruments. Even the apocryphal nature of WoD, it's there by desing, so that Storytellers can decide which version of the metaplot (if at all) they are gonna use in their own stories. WoD, and specially Mage, is composed of works that are not static in their presentation, like a mediocre Marvel movie or some such, in which there's nothing to it except what's given in a silver plater. It's not a book or a movie in which the work just passively asserts itself to the viewer, but a game that dares you to interact with what's trying to say about so many complex themes, with each argument in order to "prove the writers wrong" just diminishing the work to one more besides all other grimdark-ish settings.
