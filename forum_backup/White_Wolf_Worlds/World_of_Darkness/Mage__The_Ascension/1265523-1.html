    My Technomancers' Toolbox would've included that. An overview of the basic branches of science, "dead" sciences (alchemy, phrenology, eugenics, etc.), various fringe theories (cold fusion, perpetual motion machines), and some of the weird cutting edge stuff (zero point energy, quantum computing), and how the various Traditions might use them.

    Originally posted by Aleph View Post
    A Revised Book of Chantries would be good too, as stuff changed and many chantries were destroyed.
    I rather wish that Mage (along with Werewolf, Wraith and Changeling) had lead with a book about chantries in general - how they work, how to design them, how PCs can found their own, how to use them in your games, etc. - and later on done a Book of Chantries that detailed 9 or 10 interesting locations around the globe.

    As for a Players Guide, I think my ideal one would've been:
    • An in depth look at the physiology, psychology and metaphysics of the Awakened. How they think, how their bodies work, the impact of Avatar and Essence on personality, questions of lifespan, insanity (without becoming a Marauder), reincarnation, reproduction, and all those assorted niggling questions players tend to have.
    • An expanded look at the various traits - Attributes, Abilities, Backgrounds and the rest - with alternate creation rules, new uses for skills, what it means to have a Trait above normal human limits, different ways backgrounds can manifest, and merits/flaws.
    • An expanded look at each of the Traditions (and the Hollow Ones), including basic history, philosophies, their leadership, primary chantry, and a premade character template for each.
    • A chapter on how individual Mages fit into the Awakened society, starting with their Cabal, and moving up through the levels of Chantry, city/geographic area, Tradition, and the Nine Mystical Traditions as a whole.
    • More about magic, starting with each Sphere and what going above Master level might mean for each, and a bunch of new Rotes and Talismans.
    • The initial group write ups for the main Crafts (I probably would restrict this to eight of them, otherwise it would be far too big).
    • An appendix about creating unAwakened characters (acolytes and others), including three or four basic sorcery Paths.
    Or something similar to that.

                I'd like a series of smaller books — averaging, say, 32 pages or less — dedicated to M20's Foci (I'm sorry; but sometimes you just can't get away from the plural of the word). Each one would be centered on one Practice.

                It would start with a chapter on Theory, which would discuss the three or four most relevant Paradigms in terms of how they relate to the book's Practice; then a chapter on the Practice itself, which would discuss the Abilities, Instruments, and Spheres as they relate to the Practice along with a selection of Rotes that don't just specify what Spheres you need and what effect you achieve, but also what Instruments you might use and how you'd use them, and what Abilities could be used to enhance the effect. It would also discuss how the mystic/technomancer divide applies to the Practice: some Practices heavily favor a technomantic mindset, others favor a more mystical Monday, and still others see frequency use by both.

                It would then wrap up with a selection of Wonders that someone of that Practice would find useful — or, in the case of Practices that are heavily into making Wonders, the sorts of things a practitioner might make.

                My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                            You know what I'd like to have seen more of? Adventure modules.

                            Mage has always been very light on pre-packaged adventures or stories. Lots of material that suggest plot hooks the Storytellers could implement, but few complete stories.

                            The reason why is obvious, of course. By nature of their expansive powers, Mages can do theoretically anything. Which makes it difficult to plan a story around, at least in the manner that White Wolf's writers were always accustomed to. Just look at how rail-roady the Vampire modules had to get to accomplish their narrative ends. And Vampires have finite supernatural abilities at their disposal. I suspect White Wolf looked at the Spheres and gave up on ever formulating adventures the players couldn't easily - trivially - bypass.

                            My argument is that this stems from a failure in the writing process. Or, rather, a too strict adherence to traditional writing. (See the aforementioned Vampire railroading). If you're going to create modules for Mage, you need to have less "Plots to Follow", and more "Problems to Solve".

                            Mage, being an open-ended experience, needs more open-ended conflicts. Villains that aren't shackled to individual schemes, but engage in schemes to pursue their more general goals. Conflicts that don't rely on the game hitting the exact story beats, but where the "plot" organically arises from the NPCs pursuing goals and the players acting in response to them. Preferably, the conflict is like a multi-headed beast; even if the cabal solves one problem, it doesn't invalidate the menace of multiple others. And if you had a module like this, they would need to explain the motivations and personalities of the principle NPCs, such that a Storyteller always knows how they would react to a given situation.

                            And, of course, the modules would need to take into account all nine Spheres, and how the presence or absence of them can further the narrative towards some conclusion. Everyone always complains about murder mysteries being impossible to run, because "anyone with Time 2 can solve it instantly". Those who put a little effort in will know that it doesn't have to be that simple, and a Mage module would need to put just as much care into filling those holes. Even if it's as simple as "there was a Time ward blocking post-cognition", or "the killer was wearing a mask", or "you see the killer...and you have no idea who this is".

                            In any RPG where players can specialize in their characters's capabilities, you need to leave multiple ways to arrive at the answers. Pre-packaged adventures aren't impossible in Mage. They just require a radically different design philosophy, in comparison to a linear succession of areas and story beats that follow a single plot.

                            Magestuck: The Ascension

                                            (I figured I'd throw this out there. Someone may want to take part of it and develop it for the ST Vault or something.)

                                            Chantry Sanctum and Cabal - A book of gatherings and strongholds for Mage: The Ascension

                                            Chapter One: Sanctums
                                            An in-depth revisitation and expansion of the Sanctum background, including optional rules for Sanctum creation by use of building points and features (such as tools, security systems and the like). Selecting a location, how to prepare and equip the site, and mystical attuning oneself to the Sanctum. The idea of naturally occurring Sanctums and how they fit different Traditions. Mages sharing Sanctums and how that works, as well as what happens when a Mage takes over and claims a preexisting Sanctum. The specific needs and specifications for each of the Traditions and for the more common styles of magic (enchanting/craftwork, religious ritual, spirit-talking, etc.), and ways to do personal customization. And the idea of Sanctum resonance and having an affinity for a certain Sphere.
                                            And a collection of sample Sanctums: The Craftsman's Workshop, The Family Crypt, The High Tech Lab, The Meditative Garden, The Occult Mystic Circle, The Primitive Shaman Lodge, and The Private Alter.
                                            Chapter Two: Cabals
                                            How and why Cabals form. The common types of Cabals - Single Tradition, Cultural (those who all share similar backgrounds and/or mystical styles), Research/Discovery, Combat (especially in the Ascension War), and Political. Roles within the Cabal, including The Face, The Fixer, The Muscle, The Pointman, The Pillar, The Leader, and The Devil's Advocate (members may take one multiple or none of these roles). Rules/guidelines for pooling character background points for the Cabal, including for group Mentors or Followers. And understanding the Cabal's place in the greater Awakened world, such as relations with other Cabals, with various Chantries, and role in the Ascension War.
                                            Then three sample Cabals to serve as possible allies, rivals or enemies of the player characters' Cabal.
                                            The Voodoo History Society - a research Cabal devoted to unearthing secret history, exposing conspiracies and recovering lost wonders. Led by Dr. Patricia Talib, Hermetic linguistics prodigy of House Bonisagus; with Stewart Little (Virtual Adept historian and journalist), Raveka (Dreamspeaker Baruti ghost-chaiser and storyteller), and John Goodson (Euthanatos homicide detective and truth seeker).
                                            The Lunatic Express - a war Cabal who perform random and often violent attacks against what they perceive to be Technocratic interests. Led by Lucian Shepherd, Akashic radical anti-materialist anarchist of the LiHai; with Diamond Dog (last of the die-hard Virtual Adapt cyberpunk warriors), Hashim (radical Ecstatic gnostic terrorist fanatic), and Mary Ninekills (Dreamspeakers AWOL US Marine of the Red Spears.
                                            ​The Diamond Heart Gang - a political Cabal focused on accumulating financial power in as flashy a manor as possible. Led by Diana Chaucer, Verbena financial witch; with Kim Ben-Hwa (former Korean boy band idol now face and negotiator for the Akashic She Ren), Professor Bruce Botwell (Etheric utopian political theorist and stock market mavin), and Jenifer Lebeau (financial prodigy and member of a Celestial Chorus sacred messianic bloodline).
                                            Chapter Three: Chantries
                                            The role of Chantries in Awakened Society and their place in the Ascension War. How Chantries are founded and created, including those planned and designed vs those that spontaneously or accidentally come about. How Chantries grow and evolve, their defense and security, and the recruiting of new members. Leadership and management of Chantries, the positions of responsibility (similar to some of the ones you'd see in Werewolf Caerns), and diplomacy with outsiders. The Logistic of Chantry management, including staffing, housing and utilities, mystical resources, and keeping away unwanted attention. Fitting personal Sanctums into a Chantry, and other unique features and abilities a Chantry might have (stable gateways into the Umbra, wellsprings of Quintessence, etc.)
                                            Several sample Chantries (or at least the broad outlines of them) that can be dropped into most city settings without much trouble.
                                            The Fort Building - an old building originally built along mystical parameters (similar to Ghostbusters), renovated into loft apartments that now house a variety of Bohemian artists and eccentric oddballs, several of whom serve as Acolytes and other allies (and as potential Changeling crossover hooks if you want to go that way), with the Chantry proper taking up an entire floor to itself as well as a number of hidden rooms scattered throughout the building.
                                            Fox Glove Farms - A small family owned organic farm that also plays host to an artists colony, a Wiccan coven and/or a children's day camp.
                                            The Greymalkin Detective Agency - a long established private investigation firm which specializes in cases of an unusual nature, often employing individuals with strange abilities (including the Mage Cabals which make up the Chantry).
                                            The Time Warp Theater - a renovated Art Deco theater building specializing in showing classic films and doing live stage shows (usually plays or performance art but sometime music), time and space sometimes play by different rules here, with patrons (PCs or otherwise) finding themselves going back to critical points in their lives or in history, like an episode of the Twilight Zone.
                                            Turnkey Labs - a private technological think tank and engineering research lab that specializes in pushing the limits of what people think technology can do, mainly because they do a lot of work in technomancy. Their overall philosophy is one of technology as a means of wonder and liberation rather than control and conformity.
                                            Also a brief description of the central Chantry for each of the Traditions (The Akashic Record, etc.)
                                            Chapter Four: Councils
                                            The local leadership of a geographic area (say, Seattle), usually including several local earthbound Chantries, with members representing said Chantries or Cabals (or sometimes Traditions or other local power blocs). The various rules and protocols said Councils establish, various positions (Heralds, for example). Issues of etiquette, diplomacy, justice and punishment. Official council gatherings and Tribunals. PCs as members of the Council and taking roles of leadership in the region and the Tradition.
                                            Appendix: Wellsprings (ie, sources of Quintessence)
                                            The hows and whys of Quintessence flowing into the material world, the impact any Resonance from the flow might have on the location and nearby people, conflicts over them that might arise with other supernatural beings.
                                            Sample wellsprings, including the Hidden Glade, the Ancient Ruin, the Physics Collider, the Playground and the Mausoleum.
                                            A look at some legendary sites such as Stonehenge, Ayers Rock, Mt. Fuji, the Nazca Lines, the Tunguska blast site, and the Trinity test site.


                                            And then an alternate Book of Chantries that would've detailed 9 or 10 interesting places around the world, probably half of them single Tradition ones and the other half mixed. Not sure exactly what it would include, other than the Coyote Caravan, a traveling Chantry of the Cult of Ecstasy's 5th World Tribe, a collection of hippies, Bohemians and other oddballs which roams the American Southwest spreading their beliefs of a more natural and spiritually oriented lifestyle of music, handcrafts, and vision quests. That and probably Grimm's Occult Specialty Shoppe, which would use Correspondence magic to colocate in multiple cities at the same time. Other thoughts that had occurred to me were an Akashic capoeira school/candomble temple in Sao Luis and a Jerbiton Hermetic piazza in Milan.
