            Not really, every roll past the first for a specific effect increases the difficulty by one, at least in Revised (each failed roll on a ritual effect). If you have Arete 3 and Prime 3 and wanted to make a Hadouken, you would have to make a difficulty 7 roll, with a minimum number of successes equal to one (for targeting another Pattern) plus one for every two health levels of damage plus one for aggravated damage. Since you are not directly targeting the Pattern of the target with Prime, your target gets a chance to dodge.

            So, let us say you wanted to destroy one of the Kindred. You would need to get a minimum of six successes (one for targeting another Pattern and five successes for eight health levels of aggravated damage). We will assume that the Mage is spending one point of Willpower and one point of Quintessence per roll, as Willpower and Quintessence are difficult to recover, in addition to the one point of Quintessence required to charge the aggravated damage effect. The effect then will take three turns to cast (using average values), with difficulty 6 for the first roll, difficulty 7 for the second roll, and difficulty 8 for the third roll (+1 difficulty for every two other magical effects ongoing).

            Now, the utility for a Mage is that the range of the effect is LoS (when they are not using Correspondence), so a Mage on the roof of a skyscraper can do massive amounts of damage. If the Mage is an assassin, all they need is LoS to their target and three turns to cast the effect. Now, any supernatural creature with Awareness will have a chance to detect that they are being targeted, so they might be able to dodge, but any mundane (or any mundane object) will be unable to dodge because they will not detect the effect. So a beginning Mage character with Arete 3, Prime 3, Avatar 5, and Willpower 10 would be one of the most frightening assassins in the cWoD because they could easily kill people mortals without leaving any evidence of the kill (though they would still suck in close combat), but they would start attracting attention if they made too many impossible kills in a given area during a given interval.
