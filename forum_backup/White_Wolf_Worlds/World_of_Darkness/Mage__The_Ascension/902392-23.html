    478) Those Beyond Notice [ Paradox Flaw | Prime, Prime and Mind, Prime and Matter, Mind, Entropy ] The affected Mage is only capable of hearing the words of mundane humans who the Mage sees at the moment the mundane human speaks. The affected Mage does not hear any words uttered or sounds vocalised by mundane humans who they aren't currently seeing.

    ( Persons who are Sorcerers, and also Ghouls, Kinfolk, Acolytes of Mages etc. are not considered to be mundane humans in regards to this Paradox Flaw. )

        479) Making Oneself Known [ Paradox Flaw | Prime and Mind, Prime, Mind and Entropy, Mind ] The Mage affected by this Paradox Flaw cannot check other persons with Prime and Entropy magickal senses and sights, and cannot scan, read and divine a person's emotions and/or thoughts, unless the affected Mage first personally introduces herself or himself to a given person with a greeting and a name ( or a nickname ) that the Mage has honestly used before for herself or himself.
        The person's reaction/reply to the greeting and introduction does not matter - what matters and counts is that the affected Mage introduces herself or himself.

        This requirement is in addition to any other requirements, like for example those due to a Mage's Paradigm, or a Spell's or a Rote's specific requirements.

                481) Hive Minded [Quiet]: Some controlling mages liken their behavior as them sitting at the center of a massive spiderweb of resources, information, and influence. With this Quiet, however, the mage is more akin to the Queen of a hive. Members of the Order of Hermes, Wu Lung, Syndicate, and New World Order most often incur this backlash, though any sufficiently powerful and influential mage may earn it, if they stand at the head of their own personal empires, businesses, conspiracies, or cults.

                The mage's surroundings take on the characteristics of a hive, walls being covered in honeycombs or small holes. In their ears ring the sound of buzzing wings and clicking mandibles. They start seeing colony insects walking or flying around, though the mage bears them no mind. They are of no concern, for they are part of the hive. As the Quiet grows, so do the insects and the patches of honeycombs. Sooner or later, the mage's own subordinates will take on the likeness of insect drones. This begins with clothing in shapes and colors reminiscent of hive-building insects, like black and yellow stripes, or helmets with large eye designs and mandibles. It grows worse as those around the mage seem to develop antennae or compound eyes in their heads, and their limbs seem armored and jointed.

                Once the mage starts externalizing their Quiet, it's as if they stand in the center of a huge hive complex, made from rough organic walls or large honeycomb panels. Hobgoblins in the form of insectoid minions surround the mage, and the she feels as though she is the hive's Queen. With this Quiet comes an autocratic bearing, first subtle, then later more acute. The mage also becomes disinclined to leave the center of their operations, the seat of their power. It is not the role of a Queen Bee or Queen Ant to leave the hive. That's what the workers and soldiers are for. The mage may demand that flowers be provided, for pollinating insects do love their flowers.

                Marauders fallen to this Quiet inhabit huge hives in the Umbra, attended by legions of insectoid followers. Those Marauders who have mastered Life may even populate those hives themselves...

                Magestuck: The Ascension

                    482) Out The Airlock [Realm]: The perpetual fear of any space-based Void Engineer is to have their bubble of habitability opened or ruptured, and the decompression sucking them out into the airless void. So potent is this fear that Void Engineers can invite it upon themselves with Paradox, no matter where they happen to be. If they are in an indoor environment, the nearest door, window, or other threshold opens up (where applicable), revealing a sucking vacuum and a view of an infinite sea of stars. If there isn't such an opening in close proximity, the walls will rupture, a hole being formed. If this backlash occurs outdoors, either the nearest opening to a structure will serve this purpose, or a rupture will open up in the air itself.

                    Regardless, the Void Engineer will be rendered weightless - as if in micro-gravity - and the force of the vacuum will draw them towards the hole. If the character cannot arrest their movement somehow, they will be drawn through the opening and banished to the cold, airless void, far from home. It's no wonder that some paranoid Void Engineers always wear space suits, regardless of context. They never know when they may be banished to stars, left to die horribly in space.

                    Magestuck: The Ascension

                        483) Infinite Corkscrew Recursion [Realm]: A rare Paradox Realm, that can affect any mage for any reason, but is most frequently encountered by mathematically minded mages. Mathematics, of course, being often preoccupied with fractals and recursion. Mages get drawn into the Realm as the world around them begins to twist and curve, elongating until their surroundings become like the inside of a corkscrew. Except it's not JUST a huge, infinitely long corkscrew pattern. It's a corkscrew whose component spirals are, themselves, composed of yet another, smaller corkscrew pattern. Naturally, those are themselves composed of smaller corkscrews, down through infinitely small patterns of spirals spiraling upon themselves. As far as mages can tell looking outside the corkscrew, this pattern continues UP as well, with them existing in tunnel-sized spaces that curve into still larger corkscrew patterns. The mages, essentially, are consigned to a Realm of infinite recursive corkscrewing from both ends, each "layer" being orders of magnitude larger/smaller than the last.

                            484) Two Dimensional Thinking [Quiet]: A not uncommon affliction among certain tribal groups in the Americas, Africa, and other places, where flat plains are the norm and where things like multi-story buildings, subways, and air planes were not. Obviously this Quiet has abated in recent years, as even the most traditionalist aboriginal groups have been exposed to such things. It was also not unheard of among military strategists throughout history, regardless of national origin. Again, the advent of technologies has lessened the likelihood of this Quiet. Nonetheless, it pops up every so often.

                            In essence, this Denial Quiet rejects the third dimension - the z-axis - in the mage's experience. There is no going up or down for them. The do not acknowledge stairs, elevators, ladders, multi-story buildings, basements, or even particularly inclined slopes. To the mage, there is no other plane except the one they are on. Even if they walk up an incline, they won't register that they've changed in elevation. It's as if their world is a flat plane, with obstacles around them only as tall as they are. Nor does such a mage acknowledge the existence of airplanes, submarines, helicopters, or more mystical means of changing elevation, such as flying brooms or carpets. In the rare event that a mage contracts this Quiet while personally being capable of flight, they will either forget this fact, or will never think to use it beyond moving very quickly, mere inches above the ground. Flying up is a foreign concept.

                            To such a mage, people moving up and down levels may appear to disappear entirely, as their Quiet-addled mind has difficulty processing ascent or descent as concepts. When their delusions begin to manifest externally, methods of doing so cease to exist for everyone while the mage is around. Until then, though, the mage is at a disadvantage against opponents who can make use of the third dimension.

                            At the most severe, the mage lives entirely in their own personal Flatland. There are only two dimensions, with objects and people being differentiated by color, size, and what shape can be guessed by the length of their line.

                                    486) Past Echos [Flaw]: Prompted by a mage's use (or misuse, or abuse) of Time magick. The character is followed by a faded afterimage of herself, delayed by a few seconds. It acts out all of her movements. Some such afterimages fade in and out, but others are constantly present. Certain Akashics claim this backlash is a manifestation of the mage's karmic impact on the world.


                                    487) Lead Shoes [Flaw]: A response to Matter magick, the mage's footwear turns to lead, iron, or concrete for the duration of the backlash. Any alternate footwear they would put on is similarly affected, though the difficulty to remove shoes made from dense, unyielding materials usually makes this point moot. The mage will have difficulty moving quickly or with stealth, so long as they wish to enjoy the benefits of footwear.

                                        488) Bioluminescent Fluids [Flaw]: A result of magick used to manipulate light or shadow. The mage's bodily fluids - blood, sweat, saliva, etc, - glows faintly. Barely noticeable (if at all) in a well lit room, but obvious in the dark. Usually glows a single color, like green, blue, teal, red, etc. Can make stealth a challenge. Also leaves residue behind that can be found, where applicable.


                                        489) Chess Movement [Flaw]: For centuries, mages who fancy themselves master manipulators - from the Ahl-I-Batin to the Syndicate to the Nephandi - can find their physical movements constrained. Just as they would turn the world around them into a game of chess, Paradox forces them into the role of pawn. Or any other piece, really. The mage's ability to walk is limited to the paths delineated by a particular chess piece. As pawns, they can only move forward, one step at a time (unless they are attacking someone, in which case they can move one step diagonally). If there are a Rook, they can only move in four directions, while a Bishop has the same problem but on a slightly different axis. A queen can move any direction (but only in straight lines, and must stop completely to change direction), and a king is similar but at a snail's pace. The oddest is the knight, which forces the mage into irregular L-shaped paths (although, somewhat handily, she isn't obstructed by anything on the way, even people or walls). When this happens, the ground/floor around the mage sometimes conveniently becomes marked by a series of grids, in an alternating checkerboard pattern.


                                        490) Aqua Attraction [Flaw]: As one of the four classical elements, Water factors often in the magick wielded by mage. Ergo, it also becomes wrapped up in the backlashes against them. Effects cast revolving around water - water walking, water breathing, creating mist or rain, directing waves, freezing water into ice, etc. - can cause the mage to be overly charged by elemental water. Medieval mages would speak of an imbalance of humours, that created an elemental pull for liquids as the body tried to correct itself. The backlash causes quantities of water in the environment to become attracted to the mage, as if by gravity or a magnet. Small quantities of it will hop over the lips of containers and extrude from porous materials, then flow over the ground towards the mage. She may quickly find herself the focal point for tiny rivulets of water, all across the ground or other surfaces. At more severe backlashes, larger quantities of water will be attracted all at once, and with such speed that they thwart the pull of gravity altogether. Flying at the mage and splashing her relentlessly. In any event, the mage is left damp, as water droplets cling to her. If the backlash gets too bad - or she is unlucky enough to be near large volumes of water - she may become the center of a sphere of liquid. If she wasn't affected by a water breathing spell before, she better hope she can cast one, lest she drown inside a spherical watery hell that refuses to let go.

                                                492) Chameleon Skin [Flaw]: A response to shapeshifting magick, this backlash causes the mage's skin (and sometimes clothes) to take on the color, pattern, and texture of objects and surfaces they come into contact with. If they place their hand on a brick wall, that hand will mimic the masonry, for instance. If they're right up against the wall, most or all of their body will blend in. What makes this is a backlash is that it triggers when the mage doesn't want it. The Paradox won't manifest in such a way as to readily allow the mage to exploit it for cheap stealth enhancement. Even if it did, the camo only masks the skin, not the mage's overall shape. While their outline will be broken up at a glance, it will make them all the most attention-grabbing when they are noticed.


                                                493) One of the Crowd [Flaw]: Similar to the above, the mage's shapeshifting or illusion magick causes their body to shift in response to their environment. This time, though, it's to make them responsive to the company they keep, not the set dressing. The mage's skin, hair, and eye color will appear like the nearest person or persons, trying its best to aggregate any disparate appearances. The mage's clothes, too, will appear in the style on display. While this can let a character blend in with a crowd, their guise will change dynamically from moment to moment, making it difficult to move around or even stand still unnoticed.

                                                    494) The Belonging Kind [Spirit or Quiet]: A deep, primal aspect of human psychology is the desire to fit in. To conform to the standards of the tribe. To not stand out, lest it lead to alienation and loneliness. Even singularly eclectic individuals - who despise conformity in all its guises - are not above this urge, from time to time. Few are as singular and as eclectic, of course, as the Awakened, and their inborn alienation is greater still. For, indeed, how can a mage really connect with a sleeper, when the gulf between their experiences and power is so great? Some mages struggle their whole lives with this disconnect from their fellow humans, while others put their blood, sweat, and tears into making themselves fit in. Both sorts of mage can attract a Paradox Spirit of the Belonging Kind.

                                                    As a Spirit, one of the Belonging Kind appears in whatever guise is most appropriate for their surroundings, and affect the mannerisms of whatever company they keep. If they move locations, they'll have changed shape covertly on the way there. And their speech and body language shift to suit whomever they are talking to at the time. When they are summoned, the mage may not realize the Paradox Spirit is among them until it's too late. Any object they require - from car keys to cash to business cards - are extruded from membranes in the folds of their clothes. Their demeanor is invariably friendly, but their motives are malicious, directed towards the mage who drew them in. If mere mischief is in order, they will use their people skills to sow discord in a group, in the hopes of bringing trouble to the mage. Spirits of the Belonging Kind most enjoy spreading rumors, vicious lies, and shocking truths about the mage, with an eye towards instilling in the crowd that the Awakened person in their midst does not belong. That they are deviant, weird, unsavory, untrustworthy, or of some class - or Kind - of person that is antithetical to the group.

                                                    If the backlash is more severe, however, the Belonging Kind will attempt to engage with the mage directly, in the hopes of tempting them into going somewhere private. There, the Belonging Kind will assault the mage, often by drawing weapons from the membranes beneath their clothes.

                                                    As a Quiet, meanwhile, the change is wrought on the mage herself. She finds herself unconsciously mimicking the speech patterns and mannerisms of others, and will habitually dress in a manner befitting the environment (regardless of usual habit). If she has sufficient ability, she'll use magick to change her appearance to suit the environment, whether by changing her body (with Life), her clothes (with Matter), or how people perceive her (Forces or Mind). She will do anything to fit in, regardless of expense, effort, personal taste, or morality. If it means going along with the crowd, she'll do it. At its more severe levels, the mage becomes one of the Belonging Kind, a social chameleon who has no persona of her own, but only what she assumes from others. When alone, she does not sleep, but rather roosts on furniture in the dark, resting for when she can prowl again; she wears whatever appearance she last used.

                                                        495) Blinded by the Light [Flaw]: Among mages of a holy bent - Celestial Choristers, Templar Knights, some Batini and Wu Lung, even certain flavors of Verbana or Hermetics - the divine power they wield can be so intense as to be overwhelming, even to themselves. Divinity simply flares too bright for mortal eyes. With this Flaw, when the mage employs a lighted Effect using Forces or Prime, they themselves are blinded by its intensity. Weak backlashes can be merely inconvenient - unless in combat, where every second counts - but strong ones can be debilitating, striking the mages blind for hours or even days. Moreover, this form of Paradox has a habit of afflicting not just the mage, but all her eyes that are looking. The Storyteller may require other characters to make rolls to avoid being blinded alongside their ally. Suffice to say, Awakened who have been on the receiving end of collateral blinding quickly learn to avert their eyes when their holy compatriot is about to summon powerful Effects.

                                                        A variant of this Flaw occurs among technomancers, as Paradox causes them to be "blinded by Science".

                                                            496) Electricity is in My Soul [Flaw]: Frequently afflicts mages with cybernetic augmentations, or those who habitually channel lightning. Their body courses with an electric charge. Their touch carries static, and when wounded they bleed arcs of electricity rather than blood. Their eyes - the windows to the soul - flicker like lightbulbs. The Flaw could cause electronics to spontaneously activate at the mage's touch, or even to overload and short out. More than one unfortunate Virtual Adept, Etherite, and Iterator has accidentally fried their own hardware this way.
