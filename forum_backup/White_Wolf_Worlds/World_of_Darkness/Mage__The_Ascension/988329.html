      Originally posted by artuard View Post
      1) two players have a tendency for power gaming, is there anything in particular that i should watch out ? To nip it in the bud so to speak.
      Okay! This is a big one.
      Mage's Magick system is incredibly flexible, and incredibly potent. With all the spheres and all their levels combined, there is next to nothing that isn't possible.
      So something you *will* run into, given that you are not experienced with the system yet, is that something that seemed like a good idea at first, rule-wise, regarding a specific type of Magick or effect...becomes Bad Stuff once your players learn how to blow it out of proportion.

      So both you *and* your players need to be in agreement, that there will be events of 'Yes, I know I allowed that a few games ago, but I now see how that rule is bad at this level. I'm learning'. You as a new Mage GM need that flexibility, and they need to give it to you.

      I also highly encourage you to ask questions here regarding certain kinds of Magick, effects, rules etc. once your game starts. Or heck, even before. Lots of threads to read, too.

      2) Would you have any advice for a new game, and this system? General info of what to do and what to avoid? etc.
      An important thing to drive home to the players, and for you as a GM to remember, set-up and use is... that Mages are normal humans. If they are not working magick, they are plain, normal humans. They have friends, family, they need money, get tired, hungry, and whatever else comes with the human condition. They don't soak Lethal or Agg, without use of Magick they have mortal healing times...

      Watch out for you or your players treating a Mage (at first at least) as something non-human like a Vampire or Werewolf. The vast majority of Mages grew up as normal humans, still have human ties and interests. Just because you can do Magick doesn't mean you suddenly turn into ice-cold-combat-beast that is-not-shaken-by-anything either.

      ..and balancing a normal life and magick studies can be...interesting.

      3) Finally, should I start with the awakening as preludium? And if so, should it be done solo or as part of the main game?
      If there is no encounter planned pretty much immediately on awakening with the other players, solo it. If you are playing Orphans, then you could do two awakenings solo, have those two players meet in a session, have the third player awaken solo, meet the other two til the group has met.

      If they are part of the Traditions or Technocracy, it's usually good to have the players meet after they have been in the faction for a while, or at least all have been introduced into their faction already, so it's all solo awakening sessions.


      Also, if a player already has Mage experience and knows how to describe awakenings and what they include, they simply can make the awakening part of their background writeup if they are willing to come up with it. That gives them the chance to let them develop their own nuances and events of the awakening, the people involved etc., and you don't need to do a solo session with that player.

          Ummm.... firstly this is the Forum for oWoD Mage.

          As for your actual questions... Mind you this is from a point of view of Revised. Others here have more (read: Any) Experience with M20.

          1.) EVERYTHING. The entire system is designed in such a way that if you look for it, there are things to smash the world over your knee with.

          1.A.) Paradox. By default, Paradox in revised maxes out at 12 for a botched vulgar effect of sphere 5. The reason for this is because, as I understand it, the ST is meant to ad-hoc paradox appropriate to the effect.

          1.B.) Ritual Magic can get silly. If you let your players sit in their sanctum and perform a ritual almost ANY sphere can cause mass devastation. It has a potential to backfire of course, but you can get things like the Charles Atlas Training camp. Or the Holy Blade of Death where they've stacked umpteen bajillion successes into a relatively low sphere effect to get devastating results.

          1.C.) Crafting requires more investment than the above, but depending on the version used can get completely silly.

          2.) Personally, I'd say don't treat this like you would Vampire. The very nature of the system defies a lot of attempts to keep the game on rails. Correspondence allowing teleportation, Being able to see through Time, and so on. The sheer wealth of Options Mage gives means you'd be hard pressed to keep up with them all. So instead I'd advise keeping a living city. Have your foes be less "One big bad guy who personally does everything." and instead have them operate through proxies and such. Encourage scuffles over territory with other mages to distract from the main foe. In general, don't allow them to just find the bad guy and kill the bad guy.

          3.) Depends on what you want. Personally, Every game I've played has started after the players were already awakened and somewhat established.

                                      Mage 20th Anniversary Edition, (Or M20 as we call it) is indeed part of oWOD. If it's got the Technocracy in it, it's old world. If you think you can handle the girth of this tome, then by all means go with it. If you're struggling to get through it, though, Revised can offer your a smaller amount of stuff to read (though the rules are somewhat different for combat, paradox, and some spheres), also this book is very much a product of its time, and M20 offers up a number of useful updates, making mage fit in with 2013 trappings. Some people really like 2nd Ed, but I'm not a huge fan of that book personally.

                                      When it comes to planning a chronicle, it's always a personal and subjective thing. Some people need whole books filled with plans to feel confident with their game, others literally right up a few notes the night before. It's all up to what you feel like you need. That being said, be prepared for the players to ignore a huge amount of what you've set up, and get ready to embrace whatever plans and wishes they throw in. I tend to keep things pretty fast and loose. The extent of my planning is Characters and Events. If there's a particular encounter I want to happen, I'll keep a lot of details vague until the time comes to throw it in, then I'll use the context of the scene to fill in the gaps on the fly.
                                      The biggest headache you can create for yourself is preparing too much and getting yourself stuck in a narrative corner. If there is this one shiny thing that the Players must have or the game falls apart, then they have to get it. If they miss it just because of a bad Perception roll, then you're going to have problems.

                                      The best advice I can give regarding plot is "Consequences are your friend". Nothing your players do should go unpunished. If they kill something, someone is going to make them answer to it, be it via tribunal, police, or revenge. If they piss of a demon, the demon's coming after them. If they anger a Chantry leader, it's unlikely they'll get far with negotiations. Paradox is the ultimate consequence for abusing magic, and never be afraid to throw in an extra point or two if they do something particularly outlandish.

                                      In my opinion, if you want to encourage new people into the system, you need to let them feel just how powerful the spheres can be. Give special attention to their Avatar and personalise the way their avatar might communicate with them based on the Essence they choose. Be sure to go deep into Paradigm, encourage them to personalise how they do magic. This means working with each player individually on how they think magic works and what they believe they are doing to reality when they are making changes, and encourage your mages to have in character debates/arguments about what actually is happening when magic is being done. These kind of conversations are what Mage is all about.

That only makes them miserable and encourages them to game the system in another way and boy, can Mage be gamed. The player is where the problem lies, deal with the root problem instead of trying to impose limitations. Can you guys establish why they feel the need to power game and deal with that? Fine.                                                       Even if you end up using the Revised guide over M20 (M20 is, after all, a brick of a tome), you absolutely should at least read the chapter on Focus (what M20 calls Paradigm). It explains how Mages view their magick better than anything else Mage: The Ascension ever published on the subject. It divides things neatly into three categories - Paradigm, Practice, and Instruments - and gives a wealth of examples for each, for a very wide selection to choose from.

                                                      If you can, get your players to read this chapter as well, though you might need to copy/paste the text if they don't have their own copies. It's worth the effort, though, as it gets them thinking about not just what they want to do (the Spheres), but how they want to do it. It's all very flexible, too, letting you pick, choose, and mix options in theoretically infinite ways. This is because all Mages have at least 7 Instruments, and can have anywhere from one to three Practices. Once you get them thinking about how Practices can meld together, their imaginations will run wild.



                                                      Once you do that, and the game starts, make sure to talk to them about how their chosen Focus affects their options. Because of course, while the Spheres are broad, how a character thinks they can use those Spheres can be significantly more limited. For example: from a scientific standpoint (Paradigm), Darkness is merely the absence of Light. But from a more mystical or fringe science worldview, Darkness is a force unto itself, in polar opposition to Light. This distinction can be very important to a Mage, as it makes the difference between being able to use Forces to manipulate Darkness directly, or if they can only manipulate indirectly by manipulating Light.

                                                      Or, you have the difference between Germ Theory and Miasma Theory of disease. A Miasmatist - because they have an explanation for why disease happens - can still manipulate illness with Life. But since their scientific model doesn't incorporate micro-organisms, even if they buy the modern conception of Germ Theory (most Mages born in the modern day are familiar with Consensus Science, even if their methods deviate wildly from it), they don't have tools capable of interacting with germs directly. Same with a witch or shaman, who says that diseases are caused by spirits. They have no traditional methods for interacting with bacteria and viruses, and so can't work with them isolated from their effects on the body.

                                                      Between mystic groups, there are differences in world view that make certain Instruments not an option. An Akashic, for instance, can't use True Names, because they might not believe True Names have any power (they don't even believe there is such a thing as a Self, separate from one's environment). A Hermetic, though, can use True Names, but won't get anywhere trying to perform Effects by denying their Ego (as a Hermetic's conception of Self and Will are integral to their ability to do magic).

                                                      These are just to fringe cases. One's Focus is built on an internally consistent system for why the world works (Paradigm), and how one can exploit that system (Practice). Combine this with the fact that the Spheres are broad enough that it's hard for any one Mage to understand all its principles (read: Specialties). So a player Mage is going to be limited - at least out of the gate - in how they can use the Spheres. This is not an insurmountable problem, however, if the player character is willing to put in effort. Either by studying what members of their own Tradition have already learned on a subject, or by devising a theory that circumvents Paradigmatic limitations.

                                                      How does a Hermetic, for instance, manipulate Radiation? That's a good question, and one worth exploring with further study. If the Hermetic is diligent, creative, and lucky, they could devise a theory within the Hermetic Paradigm that allows for them to act on Radiation in much the same way they act on wind or fire. (For the record, Radiation has been a known quantity in Science for a while, and therefore has been studied heavily by Hermetics. As such, this is one where study and information trading comes into play, assuming the character doesn't want to go to unnecessary effort).
