    Qlippothic Spheres for everyone!

    06-02-2017, 10:05 AM
    Through some unfortunate events, my current character gained an intuitive understanding of qlippothic spheres (without the whole Barabi Infernalist etc thing). In game terms, she automatically had half the dots (rounded up) in qlippothic she had in her regular spheres.

    Although dangerous, it had a neat flair to it. But as White Wolf never (and for good reasons) published a Nephandic source book, I was limited to the descriptions in the Book of Madness.

    In our campaign we took the 9 spheres (for several reasons like, it dividing reality into the smallest parts you can think of without having something as overpowered as space-time etc, paradox spirits, shard realms and so on) for cornerstones of the universe. This assumption makes it much easier to extrapolate to the qlippothic spheres.

    My ST and I came to the conclusion, that the qlippothic spheres theoretically do the same as the normal spheres, but (especially with the more squishy spheres like mind) certain things (destructive ones) are easier than others.

    Especially the first dot can be really interesting, since it gives the character knowledge on how things break apart, making it possible with, for example Qlippothic Matter knwoledge, to destroy something particulary sturdy or with Qlip. Mind, how to break someone most effectively.

    We defined normal spheres as Wyld magick, Qlippothic, as Wyrm magick and static magick as Weaver magick. (as the first being a purely creative power, the second leads things back to oblivion and the third being a result of reality and defined by stasis)

    As that, qlippothic magick, in metaphysical theory, makes things tear themselves apart (for example dissolving a fireball by directing the energies inside against another so everything dissolves into quintessence, flowing back into the universe. (You essentially act like the Wyrm itself, with the disadvantage of not being able to use this quintessence)

    This quintessence, tainted by the things that broke the former pattern apart, lingers as microquintessence in it's surroundings, explaining the strong resonances qlippothic magick leaves behind.

    An example in play: My character, an orphan mind mage, captured 4 Vampires and decided to indoctrinate them for her cause. She initially casted a Qlip Mind 4, Mind 4 Entropy 3 (you could as well not use Entropy 3 but it's her style) effect [With Qlip Mind 4: Dissolve the blood bond and existing loyalties inside their minds (Qlip being extra proficient in dissolving things) Mind 4 to let them think that her arguments are right and Entropy to just say the right things] as a ritual in which she talks to the chained vampire for a couple of weeks until they are broken, to rebuild them afterward)

    A thing you should note though, the powers of the Wyrm are not to be messeed easily. If she hadn't already been amoral, she wouldn't have been for long with using this powers. Falling is always a possibility and should tradition mages discover her dirty little secret, they probably would think she is fallen, or will soon.

    Also: it can give you an edge, but as alwys with mage, you have to use it creatively to get something out of it. And if you use it too much, the storyteller can always gift you with a quiet.

    What do you think of my idea? Do you like it, have something to add or think it is so EVIL you have to be at least a nephandus to use it? :P

          The challenge with Qlippothic spheres is to make them something other than the normal spheres with Entropy added. Entropy 1 added to any of the spheres would do the same "find the weak spot" bit, no Qlippoth involved.

          My thought is that for each rank you have in the Qlippothic sphere, you can add a guaranteed success to the spell but the spell becomes automatically destructive and obvious - in essence, you're adding a wrecking ball.
          • You are an Etherite with Qlippothic Forces 3. You are designing a Ignition Gun to set a target aflame, and you need it to be as good as possible. So instead of creating a field around the target that draws in ambient electromagnetics to create the flame, you set it to end the quantum spin of every atom in the vicinity to power it. Now the gun has free successes when you fire it, but it not only harms the person but also everything they're carrying as the quantum structure breaks down. And when your nemesis Dr. Electric reaches the place where you fired it, he'll easily see the remaining atoms with zero quantum spin, sticking out like a sore thumb.
          • You are a Hermetic with Qlippothic Correspondence 3. You're being chased into your basement and need to teleport to the Chantry for safety. You really, really need this spell to work, and you left your sympathetic link to the Chantry in your bedroom upstairs. So instead of invoking Raphael the Archangel of guidance to carry you across the distance, you call upon Belial whose evil lives in all hearts and is thus in all places at once. You get free successes on the roll, but both the thugs running down your stairs and all your friends in the Chantry suddenly feel their soul shift slightly as you use it to yank yourself through.
          Obviously this is overpowered as all hell. Mostly I think that's a feature rather than a bug; everyone in the setting is super-scared of Nephandi but it's never really clear why, and this makes it obvious. You could limit it by saying most barrabi only have the portion of their spheres they've relearned in Qlippothic fashion since visiting the Cauls, so while they can do a Forces 3 blast they can only "befoul" it by 1 because they only have Qlippothic Forces 1. Of course, that means widderslainte who've only ever learned Qlippothic spheres are especially scary...

                      Sure - it just depends on what sort of Nephandi you want in your game. If you're running a Technocracy game and you want the Nephandi to be a creeping infestation of the Union, slipping under the radar and finding a home in the hardened hearts of your colleagues, then certainly my hack is way overpowered. If you want your PCs to encounter a single Nephandi deep down in the sewers and and you want them to quake in fear of this one guy, then my hack makes more sense.

                      To reply to your edit - yes, this offers PCs a path to lots of mechanical power, but I don't think that disallows it as an option. Consider Vampire - any PC who can figure out a way to disable someone of higher generation can diablerize him and become more powerful, no XP required. In theory that PC could continue doing so until he has elder-level power and can kick everyone's ass in town. The limit on this isn't mechanical, it's social - somebody will notice the veins in your aura, somebody will notice all the missing ancillae, etc. Likewise here - if all of your effects break down everything nearby, people will start asking questions - and if you're a Tradition mage, you'll have both your colleagues as well as the local Technocratic squad on your ass.

                      Finally - I like that it implies destruction is easier than the alternatives. To risk politics for a moment - it's pretty easy for a military superpower like the US to go into another country and blow stuff up. It's much, much harder to build a new country out of what remains, and it's also hard to modify the existing country to be what you want. I like the idea that most mages simply learn the "harder" version of spells - fireballs that don't drain all the energy in a 10-foot radius, or mental influence without leaving the target a drooling zombie - and part of becoming Fallen is figuring out that you can do a hell of a lot more if you don't care about the mess you leave behind.

                                              But, from your initial post, there is absolutely nothing to distinguish the mechanical uses of the "Qllippothic" Sphere from those that would make sense for someone with the exact same Spheres, similar Focus and the willingness to court certain types of Resonance (and the possible consequences thereof). Entropy 1 has ALWAYS been about breaking things down as a major component (same with Entropy 3, 4, 5, etc.) Her use as described broadly and from the example seems not so different. I guess it feels eeeeeeeevil/naughty/forbidden to the character and/or those with whom she hangs or something?

                                              The fact of the matter is that the Qlippothic Spheres have never really been anything special. They aren't even normal Spheres reskinned or set up with internal alternatives in a meaningful way, like Date or Primal Utility. Which is something of an outgrowth of the fact that Nephandi are really poorly defined as being something different from normal mages. By which I mean, Marauders ARE different. There are systems associated with that difference that actually matter to how the characters are perceived, how they function, etc. Decension hasn't been made meaningful in any similar way. It's just Ascesnion with chocolate sauce or nuts or something. Nor have Qlippothic Spheres. It's far to easy to overlap "normal" mages of various sorts whose Focus is nearly identical to those attributed to Nephandi, with the only difference being a subjective/game narrative of "Oh, the Cauls! The Cauls!"

                                              To me, this characters seems like she can be handled most simply by having her being drawn further and further towards certain types of Resonance and all that entails. It's not really anything other than the flavor of her magical practice that is changing.

                                              More complicated would be to have it be a shift in her Focus, but the rules on doing anything but abandoning Instruments (if you're the sort of mage that's into that) are vague to ethereal. It's more complicated because, if it's not just handwaved (which isn't really fair if any other character wants to do something similar), it's going to take some digging down and making up some new rules to graft on to Focus as written. (Though, to my mind, this would be "not a bad thing" and is probably what I would do in this circumstance...)

                                              Most complicated would be to figure out some mechanic like Marauder Quiet and make Nephandi qualitatively different from not-Nephandi/not-Marauder mages of other sorts. Good luck with that, but that does seem like the most promising area of all.
