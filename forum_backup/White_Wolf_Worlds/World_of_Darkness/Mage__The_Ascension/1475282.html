    M20 Permanent effects on humans

    02-22-2022, 10:18 PM
    Greetings,


    I am not very familiar with M20 Ascension so maybe the questions are badly stated, if so please correct me:
    1. What level of the Life (and others if needed) Sphere is required to permanently* remove physical conditions** such as poor eye sight, renal insufficiency, etc?
    2. What level of the Life (and others if needed) Sphere is required to permanently* gain the Merit Unaging or its effects?


    Thanks to all,


    *By permanently I mean not subject to a dispelling effect.
    **Conditions will be almost anything that is a physical flaw, i.e. Impediment (this is the main one), Addiction, Permanent Wound, Aging perhaps.

      Traditionally in Mage, rewriting a pattern permanently (be it a life pattern, matter pattern, spirit or whatev) is simply a matter of 'more successes than usual!'.

      The M20 feats table (page 502) suggests 20 successes for that.
      In Revised it was more along the lines of 'Double the successes required', but in M20 you need less total successes in general unless you use the optional Revised-style split-successes rules for arete rolls, so 'double the required successes' like in Revised might be too low in many cases.

      On the other hand, that ~20 successes guideline for a truely permanent life-pattern rewrite explains why there are canonically Mages with physical limitations that never got fixed. It's probably quite easier for matter patterns.


      Now, that said, something very important!
      Effects can only be made truely "non-magically" permanent (i.e. non-unweavable and detectable), if what they are doing would result in non-supernatural results in the end. Things that are accepted by normal reality as..normal.
      Fixed eyes? That's fine, working eyes are a 'normal' thing. (Ignoring the way of how they got fixed).
      Special and weird materials that, however, make chemically and physically sense? Sure. Watch the Mage's paradigm though, it needs to work along those lines. i.e. scientific paradigms work best, and still 'impossible 'materials probably can't be perma'd safely.

      Not aging? That can't be made permanent at all in a non-magical manner, unless the Mage's paradigm involves a very strict set of scientific viewpoints which relate to cell telomeres, and genetic setup that for example allows things like the RL lobsters to (supposedly) be effectively immortal.
      I'd say that until somehow this is actually brought to the Masses' normal reality through actual labwork (i.e. probably by Technocratic measures or Etherites somehow managing to push it to the mass media, and in a way that can be replicated through mundane means after that), it's not possible to perma-unmagic this effect due to unbelief and paradox. It will always stay an ongoing magical effect til then.

          There's a bit of play here. Growing and regenerating limbs is listed as an impressive feat, which is four successes. Permanently altering a pattern is 20 successes. Which one removing physical conditions falls under depends on how we interpret patterns. Does regrowing a lost limb also restore the pattern of the limb, or is the spell just bringing the body back to a state where it matches the pattern. Given that aggravated damage is considered to be actual pattern damage I'm inclined to say the former.

          If so then healing physical conditions, even those not caused by injury, is likely an Impressive Feat (4 successes). If not, healing conditions due to injury is Impressive while healing "natural" conditions is Legendary.

          It is unclear whether the regenerating limbs requires an additional 6+ successes to make it permanent, that probably depends on the needs of your table. "Permanently rewriting a pattern" should not require an extra 6 successes, though, else why would you call it permanent.

Regrowing a recently cut-off limb, closing wounds etc. would be simple healing, as you shift things back to the still unchanged pattern.

                  Originally posted by Ambrosia View Post

                  Aggravated damage is not necessarily pattern damage. It *can* be in the case of direct Life or Prime attacks, but mundane acid, while aggravated, does not touch the pattern instantly, just like a club doesn't.
                  The description of Aggravated damage seems to vary from book to book, and in M20 from section to section. M20, pg 336 indicates that pattern damage is one form of Aggravated damage. Pg 408 breaks the other way and indicates Agg damage is always pattern damage. I tend to go with the second as it gives a metaphysical distinction between Agg and Lethal, and because it explains why Quintessence is necessary to heal Aggravated damage.

                  Now, that said, I remember from Revised that fresh wounds, be it bruises or cut-off limbs, essentially is initially a mismatch between the physical side of things and the pattern.

                  Over time, the pattern adjusts to match the new status quo - pretty much probably going hand-in-hand with the natural healing process. So, when you've got scars, a naturally healed stump etc, then fixing that would be a pattern alteration. Regrowing a recently cut-off limb, closing wounds etc. would be simple healing, as you shift things back to the still unchanged pattern.
                  That's another way of resolving it. In some ways it makes sense that a long lost limb should be harder to restore than a fresh one. On the other hand it also makes sense that a long lost limb should be easier to restore than growing an extra limb. I suppose that might be more a paradigmatic consideration than a systems one, though.

                  More notably, that's a really good argument for why replacing a lost limb permanently isn't a Legendary feat. If the loss of a limb permanently changes a pattern over time, the regain of one should have the same effect. Some mismatches between Pattern and form correct over time. At the very least that is a rational way of adjudicating it.

                                      You need to watch for "Pattern Bleed." Making an obviously unnatural change in a pattern permanent can require regular ingestion of Quintessence. And you court paradox.

                                      However, if the change is subtle and hard to notice, you've got more leeway. Say a man had testicular carsanoma when young, and lost one testicle and his lymph nodes in his chest and abdomen. Replacing what was lost, especially as it's all hidden from public view, would be infinitely less trouble than say functional wings. The less visible any change is the safer. A mage of average looks, who was never famous, risks far less paradox in extending their life through magick than a famously good looking Hollywood Star who is now a mage. The more eyeballs an enchantment attracts, the higher the risk of paradox.

                                          Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
                                          Thanks for the reply.

                                          But what about the Unaging merit? If you can gain benefits equivalent to removing flaws, certainly is conceivable to gain merits, including Unaging (cheap at 2 dots).
                                          Merits and Flaws in M:tA already consider the fact that Magic exists, that means two things:

                                          A) Merits frequently circumvent some of the hardest limits of Magic, usually by ignoring Paradox, Pattern Bleeding, and similar effects;

                                          B) Flaws frequently state directly the rules for how they operate when someone tries to overcome them with Magic, so just magicking them away isn't a thing;

                                          C) There are rules for dealing with any effect on a PC that gives something worth XP. When A) and B) don't apply, adapt and use those.

                                          Specifically on the matter of Unaging, there's a rule in Revised IIRC that states that effects to stave aging away start to accrue Permadox at 100 years, not to say that they may come to attract unweaving and regular Paradox far before when meaningfully witnessed. This Permadox starts at 1 and increases every 50 years.

                                          I recommend following this ruling or something similar if you want to stay close to the canon lore, because the change in Consensus that started it, making permanent immortality impossible, is well established and had several consequences through the setting.
