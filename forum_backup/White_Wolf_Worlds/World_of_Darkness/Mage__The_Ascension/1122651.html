                                              I think it highly depends on what game you want to run.

                                              in a pure Mage game where vampires play a lesser role, I agree with the above posts - there are many, easy ways to pierce Obfuscate. Heck, almost any sphere can do it:

                                              Life+Matter - Sense the pattern of dead flesh of the Vampire hidden from sight
                                              Forces - Sense the motions of the air pushed away by the invisible vampire, sense its motions, sense the subtle, tiny things it moves from its mere steps.
                                              Correspondence - Sense the lack of empty space where the vampire is, sense the changes in space.
                                              Mind - Resist the actual Mind effect of Obfuscate
                                              Spirit - Sense the Aura of the Obfuscated vampire.
                                              Prime - Sense the quintessence pattern where none visually should be.
                                              Entropy - Entropy *might* be able to do it as well, if you rule that it can sense the absolute entropic stasis that is inherent in Vampires

                                              One could argue that in the Vampire game, Auspex actually uses the Spirit and Forces combination to break through Obfuscate.


                                              Now, wether all that is actually as utterly easy as it sounds here depends on the game the ST wants to run.
                                              If they want to make things harder (or simply more atmospheric) and require more roll successes, they could rule that the mind-bending abilities of Obfuscate actually try to hide the *results* of the sphere's Magicks detecting the anomalies from the Mage's mind;
                                              This is not the ability to influence True Magick itself - not at all - but Obfuscate simply making the Mage's mind ignorant of what the Magick is telling them. Which is essentially how Obfuscate works in the first place - the mind becomes oblivious to the obvious.

                                              And then, of course, there are the various attempts of crossover-rules by the hard numbers, requiring sphere level at X to piece Obfuscate at X. I'm not a fan of that, at all. I loathe it, as those rules bluntly compare sphere levels to discipline levels because of the sole reason of 'same numbers', without any regard of what those numbers actually mean in the different gamelines, and their difference in potency.

                                              Personally, and in order to make things more exciting than 'I use sphere X's first level with a single success and notice something is wrong', I would rule that the Mage needs to roll successes equal or more to 2x the Obfuscate user's discipline level. This makes the whole process independant of the silly 'Sphere level vs. Obfuscate level' rule, while still giving more potent Obfuscate using NPCs (or players) an edge. Many mages won't go 'I churn out a 10-success sensory effect' at the start of their day or scene.
                                              And if that is not balancing enough, in case of a full Players vs Players Con one-shot crossover or the like, the Mind sphere could be *required* in the mix to see through Obfuscate's mind influence.
