          So to start, the way M20 describes it, at least in the section I am looking at (Magical Feats, M20, pg 537) a ritual is just the term for an extended casting roll. The implication is that if your character is intentionally casting over several terms, you use ritual rules. Your Arete 3, 15 success example, then, is a ritual since there's no reasonable way to get 15 successes on a single Arete 3 roll (odds of getting that through exploding dice is around the odds of winning two major lotteries back to back, give or take an order of magnitude).

          But in the case where the attempt was to cast in a single round and that attempt fell short, Aleph is right, both failed rolls and partial rolls incur a +1 going forward, which is why the book suggests that if you get two of those in a row its a good time to intentionally cast as an extended roll. Paradox would also not accumulate with each roll, that's a ritual thing.

          Now as you point out, M20 doesn't have a rule for failed magic of any type generating paradox, which is odd because M20 also says that Vulgar magic ALWAYS accumulates paradox. And M20 is the one that put that in all caps. But whatever the case, as written no paradox is incurred on either kind of extended spell until it succeeds or there is a botch.

                  No, there's no rule stating that if something is too hard you can't attempt it as a simple action. I'm saying that the odds are so bad that it simply doesn't make sense to even try. The player can't reasonably believe he'll accomplish the roll in a single roll and the character can't reasonably believe he can manage that complex a spell so quickly. You technically can use the Simple Roll rules in this case, but it doesn't represent what is going on in the game so why would you?

                  To be clear, you made this a very easy distinction with your example. The odds of getting 15 successes with a dice pool of 3 is around 1 in 1,000,000,000,000,000 (give or take a few zeros). If I were to make a roll once a second for the next 10 million years the odds are still against me ever getting that result.

                                          Originally posted by Lord Revan View Post
                                          Failure does not end future attempts, thisnis clear in page 504 and on the 2nd paragraph that says: if you fail, check Failed Rolls (which allows to continue).

                                          Honestly, this is still very confusing to me…
                                          I really don’t know why it even points to that section. Firstly, the failed rolls section is explicitly about rituals. Even if it wasn’t, all it says is that after a failure the difficulty goes up by 1, which the Picking Up section already said, and if the failure goes above 9 we should use the Thresholds rule, which is actually an optional rule for all rolls with a difficulty above 9. There’s nothing new here.

                                          Rituals and Paradox is explicitly about rituals and only about rituals. You can apply Potentia-dox to simple rolls if you want, but its a house rule, and the effect is to make picking up where you left off even more risky.

                                          But maybe it would be helpful to summarize what I see the rules saying:

                                          For regular non-casting rolls there are simple rolls and extended rolls. Simple rolls are things that can easily accomplished in a short period of time. A single success is enough to pass, even if more would be better. If you don’t get at least that single success, you can often try again at a +1 difficulty.

                                          Extended rolls are more complicated tasks that require a set number of successes, known in advance. They tend to take longer for a roll than simple rolls though this is largely a matter of what is dramatically appropriate; It is possible to have an extended roll in the middle of a pitched battle. Failed rolls during an extended roll do not raise the difficulty of continuing, nor do they take away successes already gathered.

                                          Now spells are similar - there are still simple rolls and extended rolls (rituals). But there are two complications we need to deal with. First, even seemingly simple spells often require more than one success, and we don’t want to make every spell that requires more than one success a ritual. Second, big magic should probably risk more Paradox, but rituals tend to be much safer than simple rolls so we need a way of making them carry more risk.

                                          So the changes the book makes for casting are:

                                          Spells that require more than one success can still be cast as simple rolls. But since one success may not be enough there is now a safety net: simple spells can be continued if they don’t get enough successes but the price is that the difficulty goes up fast: +1 difficulty for each roll after the first, regardless of if any successes were rolled or not. It’s an ST call whether successes can be kept through a failed roll. Simple spell rolls use standard paradox rules.

                                          Meanwhile, rituals follow the normal extended rolling rules with a few significant changes. First, unlike regular extended rolls, each ritual roll with no successes brings a +1 difficulty on all future rolls. Second, every roll after the first increases the potential paradox of the spell by 1. There are a few other changes but these are the main ones we are discussing here. These rules make rituals a more reliable way of approaching big magic, while still increasing the paradox on a catastrophic failure.

                                          Like with non-spell rolls, simple spell rolls tend to take a shorter amount of time than each roll in a ritual, but ultimately the ST should choose the time a roll takes by what is dramatically appropriate.

There are several reductions, like taking extra time and research and preparation, that rituals naturally benefit from. Or at least it doesn’t take a mage much to make sure they apply. Meanwhile fast casting is an automatic +1 unless the mage has practiced the spell (like if it’s a rote or he’s cast it several times in play).

                                                      Also, two things about time. Simple spells are not always a roll per round, and rituals are not always long drawn out affairs. Yes there are recommendations for how long rituals take but ultimately the time should be based on magical style and what is dramatically appropriate. Simple spells, like simple rolls, can actually represent minutes or hours of time. And your mileage may vary but in the right situation I’d let mages cast rituals in combat time (though I’d make them make lots of skill checks to avoid messing something up).

                                                      I would recommend trying it out in practice and seeing how each type of roll ends up working out.
