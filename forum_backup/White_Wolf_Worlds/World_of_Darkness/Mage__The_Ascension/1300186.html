    Technocracy : spontaneous infrastructure or technocratic magic?

    03-26-2019, 11:41 PM
    Hello,

    I have revisiting a lot of technocracy books (and reading Panopticon Quest) as of late and I have reached a aspect of technocratic procedures that I have considered many times but I do not fully understand. For a technocrat to preform 'magic' they must be an enlightened individual with a technocratic paradigm, which traditionally implies that they are a scientist or engineer or some other educated background that understands science and believes in it. As far as I understand though a mage doesn't need to have a high intelligence or wit score to be enlightened, and it is implied that technocrats are not all geniuses with a omniscience background. This doesn't stop an individual from accessing other union technology however, a NWO agent with life spheres and low intelligence can still use a stim pack as a focus to boost their physical strength or a damage control agent from the progenitors can fire a phase rifle straight from Iteration X that they can just magically use/ somehow have. In these cases an individual is accessing tech they don't understand or is not their particular focus, they might be a highly studious character or soldier with limited background, but if they have the spheres and the enlightenment they can still create a bevy of effects.

    Other times it seems that technocrats can spontaneously create effects that are retroactive through "training" or other efforts they seemingly did with no actual prep-work that is discussed until the exact time where the effect is needed. A syndicate enforcer can use a detonator as a focus for a forces procedure to spontaneously detonate C4 explosives that were never actually planted, or a void engineer can say that he activates his armor's psychic shielding and use that as a mind to resist psychic assaults.

    My point is that the technocratic paradigm becomes incredibly confusing at times because there is an unclear line drawn with whether effects are actually preformed by these individuals or if its the technocratic paradigm in action. Does my NWO agent need to actually plant a hidden weapon in their coat every morning to make it appear with the holdout weapon procedure. Does my space marine need to have an in depth knowledge of their power armor to modify its technology and create these effects? Or do they just believe that its shielding that is spontaneously has will do the trick and block out psychic interference. Does enlightenment actually translate to an understanding of technology that is purely academic or is it more of a belief that tech will work for you?

    Or am I just a silly idiot and is this how people justify spontaneous effects in game?

    This might be a silly question of semantics, but I think about it almost every time a procedure comes up in the convention books. I think my explanation of my question might be a little roundabout but the core question is 'does enlightenment represent a technocrats ability to create effects based their own understanding of of reality, or is it their enlightened will that allows them spontaneously change technology to create effects and procedures?'

    Let me know what you think of my big mess
    -Brassfist

      First off, it is right to feel confused in this situation. Example magical effects are frequently treated differently depending on who is writing them. This has led to more than a few discussions... and arguments... and flame wars (in Mage's younger days).

      I can't give you a direct answer because you are actually touching on multiple different issues. Instead let me share a few subjects you are touching on that might help you figure out how to handle this:

      The first is the rules for skills modifying magic rules. They are somewhat optional, but they are meant to reflect the learning, experience, and actions that a practitioner of a certain kind of magic might need to take. They are there to show the differences between two Mages of equal spheres and unequal intelligence/learning. That's one way of representing the difference between the inventor of power armor and the space marine who uses it.

      The second is that while the player always knows when their character is casting, their character does not. There are scenarios where Technocracy agents are making things happen (usually malfunctions or collapses) but they aren't aware that they are. That's where some of the perception that the Technocracy is spontaneously casting are coming from.

      Frankly, spontaneously casting is a very important thing and the Technocracy would consistently be at a major disadvantage if they didn't have a few ways of doing it, so there have been multiple attempts to set up ways that they can. The Syndicate and NWO especially are known for embedding useful things in regular technology so that they have more abilities in urban areas. I wouldn't go so far as to say they plant actual explosives in factory made fuse boxes, but I would expect there are things hidden in regular fuse boxes that make them easy to overload, or in antennae that make them easy to hijack.

      Fourth, one of the powerful elements of the Technocracy is that they are more standardized in paradigm than the Traditions are. It makes sense that members of the Conventions are able to use each other's basic tech even if it is not their primary foci.

              If the spontaneous casting relies on hidden features, then at least within the fiction it must be set up ahead of time. It exists, in universe, with justification. If a technocrat uses an embedded feature in an off the shelf camera to, I don't know, activate a secret facial recognition program then that embedded feature was always there. He didn't unconsciously add it at the moment he picked it up, he had foreknowledge that it was there. If he was unlucky, and the camera he grabbed off the shelf didn't have that feature (because even the Technocracy can't seed every camera) then he couldn't use the effect without consciously modifying the camera itself.

              Out of fiction, some groups can get away with adding things to the scene that make sense even if the ST didn't know they were there when the scene started. Some also roll a die to determine if a fortunate piece of seeded tech happens to be in the scene. Both of these cases assume the elements were actually always there even if they hadn't been seen previously. Also neither of these has anything to do with the characters. The characters aren't doing anything, the players are just deciding what makes sense in fiction.

              Now two things to keep in mind. There are no syndicate factories that are churning out products for the public. If the Technocracy is seeding technology they are doing it by using bribery or corporate espionage to get their small changes into sleeper created parts. That sets a limit on how ridiculous the seeded tech can be. A TV will not transform into a robot, the modifications would be too obvious. It might be able to record, or think, or explode like a console on a Star Trek set. It also sets a range on how prevalent the tech could be. It is produced on a global scale, but it is hard for even the technocracy to reach every company and every factory.

              Second, none of this applies to the other way the technocrats spontaneously cast, which is by observing facts about the world. Earlier you used the idea of a Syndicate Agent determining where to shoot to collapse a roof. Well in the right circumstances he doesn't have to shoot. If the firefight (or some other thing that could cause observable damage) has already happened he can 'calculate' that it is about to collapse in 5...4...3...2...1... In that case the roof was not about to collapse and the player is absolutely making an Arete roll to bring the house down. The character may not realize they are doing anything though.

                      It's sometimes worth simply boiling it down to Vulgar or Coincidental when it comes to this sort of thing; one of the features listed as something to consider with Vulgar/Coincidental is Resources. Are you working with something that already exists (the taxi down the street) or something that didn't exist (a taxi you create out of thin air).

                      If a Technocrat's explanation for blowing up a lightbulb to inflict a huge amount of damage to a building is "well it has secret technological features", that's bullshit. It's just made up. That's Vulgar, simply because the internal explanation doesn't even make full sense within the Mage's paradigm; a lightbulb shattering could be coincidental, but the Mage's own internal justifications have to make sense. Just in the way that someone could just happen to develop a cold, but an Akashayana couldn't infect them by sending them an email, since that's not how their magick works, and they don't believe in it. Instead the Technocrat should actually use their Paradigm (worth remembering that while the whole union has highly standardized and formatted Practice, which makes it very easy to work together, personal Paradigm will vary, from Tech Holds All Answers to Might Makes Right and Everything is Data) to determine how they make the lightbulb explode.

                      "Well by tweaking the wavelengths traveling through space around me using my personal disruption gear, I can concentrate a lattice of electromagnetic energy around the lightbulb to blow it up". Simple. Much better than pretending something always was a certain way.

                      This problem only really seems to come up with Mages who aren't proper scientists in and of themselves; black suits who are the agents of the technocracy. In this case you simply have to remember that NOT ALL MAGES CAN DO EVERYTHING. Sometimes Technocrats are at a significant disadvantage because they simply can't justify blowing something up or conjuring fire, they are limited to scaring people with suit/sunglasses and Mind, blasting people with a gun with forces, finding the perfect part of a building to destroy to bring the whole thing down with Matter/Entropy... you get the idea. Some Mages are limited. Some are stuck at their desk with every effect taking 5 hours because to them alchemy is this slow process. And that's fine. If you don't want your mage to be like that, design a Mage with a paradigm/practice that allows improvisation. There's a reason that Techgnosi / Scientific Mystic is an expensive merit. There are huge benefits and drawbacks to both technomancy and mysticism. Having the benefits of both is powerful.

                      So replace your "I can do stuff with my gun, mirrorshades, fake IDs and car" NWO agent with a "I have latent psionic abilities, and they aren't deviant because it's all been signed off as perfectly scientific and rational" NWO agent. Same aesthetic, same Spheres, same cool concepts, but you now have an actual justification for bullshitting things like turning spoons into throwing knives. After all, your psionic powers can do all sorts of weird things, just expect nosebleeds and hallucinations if you overdo it, thanks to inverse psychic feedback (Paradox). And while your powers are rational, they are way ahead of The Timetable! The Technocracy want you to be discreet with them; throwing things around with your mind in front of the masses is highly unmutual.
