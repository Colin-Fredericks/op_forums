                            Originally posted by Accelerator View Post
                            So what do the technocracy do when fighting the Greek gods and the various enemies.
                            As stranded mages on a different world, it kinda depends on them. It wouldn't be out of character if some just want to go home, but having that barred I imagine they would try to establish a "new Technocracy".

                            A typical Syndicate would earn money and influence, trying to capitalize whatever this world knows as power to fund the project. A typical NWO would infiltrate the goverment, the army, or the police. It-X and Progenitors would start producing stuff and helping people. VE would try to understand how EDE's (Spirits) work in this world, analyze what threats are possible to face and what treaths are to be avoided. All would want to understand how the world ticks (for instance, how to get Quintessence here) and who or what are their enemies (and potential allies).

                            After that they would probably try to go as normal, trying to undermine superstition, reinforce science and kill reality deviants (dangerous ones being more of a priority than good ones).

                            A few changes are to be expected dependant on the characters (i.e. they could choose to neglect Room 101 if they aren't fans of brainwash. They could chose to be more or less hostile to the supernatural). Control isn't here the keep them in check, but it should be mentioned that they choosed to do this and why.

                            Originally posted by Accelerator View Post
                            I mean, the Greek gods say that they represent fundamental underpinnings of western civilization. Without them, the world goes poof.

                            So is it way out there for the technocracy to make something like The Loom of Fate, a physics engine for reality itself?[
                            Short story: Yes, it is way out there. But let's analize why.

                            Such a cosmic reality warping feat would fall, at least, under the highest ends of Magick. Let's try to *map* the kind of magick we're talking about, because the kind of absolute knowledge and infinite power such a feat would require goes way, WAY, beyond having all Spheres at 5. But, believe it or not, similar feats (though perhaps not *quite* as potent) were mentioned in Mage.

                            *Voormass final solution:

                            One of the ends of the world for Ascencion faces us against Archmaster Voormass plans to put an end to Death itself. By breaking the Wheel of Dharma and replacing it with *himself* he hopes to create an universe of absolute statism where nobody will die. Ever. Nobody would be able to live either in this new world because there will be no *change*, but he sees that as a slmall price.

                            The book doesn't asume that Voormass can actually pull this out, it merely assumes that if he *casts* his spell Ascencion could become unreachable regardless if he fails or not. But being a little generous and assuming the crazy archmaster knew what he was doing, this is certainly an universal alteration.

                            Now, I would think that to create an engine to sustain reality should be harder than Voormass plans to stop Death. Death it's only one aspect of Reality, you hope to replace all of them

                            And Voormass, to have *hope* of achieving a lesser goal had Entropy 7, a unique relic chargued with the acumulated power of 1/4 of all Avatars of the world, had stolen the divinity of an aspect of the godess Kali, connected some of the most powerful corrupted Nodes of the world to suply Quintessence with the right Ressonance, and was casting the spell from the Shard Realm of Entropy itself (the Realm of the Umbra that represents and empowers the ArchSphere he was going to use)

                            *The fabled Archspheres

                            ​Masters of the Art talks about Archspheres, Spheres above 5 and up to 10 (albeit they're described only to the 9 level). The 9th level of Archspheres usually has some power with cosmic implications, like the power of creating new laws of physics (Forces 9) or the power to create universes with Prime 9 (ontologically dependant on the universe where they are created). I would say that the power to stabilize a universe it's arround that level (8-10. 10, I think).

                            But mages on that level (Oracles and Exemplars) are beyond the Ascencion War, and thus beyond Factions

                            Bottom Line

                            These feats are things that would be difficult to achieve even for the collective might of the Technocracy. One of the reasons the Technocracy hopes to control the Consensus it's because they can't just tell the universe what to do (Reality!, I want no more dragons!). The Consensus it's the combined power of the Avatars of millons of humans, and thus manipulating it it's like a big ritual to warp reality.

                            Now, if it's out there for the Technocracy itself, then it's way, WAY, out there for a group of technocrats stranded on another universe to try anything similar. They just wouldn't have the know-how nor the power. Unless, perhaps, the stranded mages were the Inner Circle of the Technocracy,

                            There's a chance?

                            A posibility could open if one of the technocrats reaches Ascencion as the culmination of the character's arc. Anyone can Ascend, it isn't the realm of powerful archmages (it's actually harder for them to Ascend) , and there are legends of Ascended mages being able to affect all of Reality at once. A technocrat could create or become "the Loom of Fate" that way, but Ascension it's not a mcguffin to be tossed as a solution to a problem, it's a story. It *has* to be a heavy focus of the plot and the character to not sound super forced.
