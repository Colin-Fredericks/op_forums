    ItX Repo Man and Cybernetics/Biomod Enhancements Background

    05-31-2018, 05:06 PM
    TL;DR if you have ideas on efficient and horribly inefficient choices for the Enhancements Background (in contrast to achieving something similar through a sphere effect), just feel free to shout those out to me and avoid reading the post below! Thank you so much! Although we are using M20, there is a likelihood we can adapt something from Guide to the Technocracy or other source materials (Technomancer's Toybox, etc.).

    (A side rules question: How does an Avatar 0, Prime 0-2 character work? Can they channel any quintessence at all without Prime 3 and an effect involving rolled successes? Must they always do an effect involving rolled successes? Can a Prime 2 character spend any quintessence ever to lower the difficulty of an effect, or instance? Etc.).

    Hi All,

    I have kind of a two-pronged inquiry/goal today: (1) probing a character concept; and (2) trying to navigate pitfalls or learn hidden gems about the Enhancements Background in M20 (more of a mechanical question, but also influenced by commentary on what conceptually works best). In line with the TL;DR above, you're also welcome to just scroll on down to item (2) and respond only to that.

    (1) Character Concept:

    The game is for a new ST (who has played M20 and C20 but is not a super experienced roleplayer or anything), and the players are just two rather experienced players (who should be fairly helpful for someone as a new ST, including taking the load/burden off of the ST).

    We are running as Technocrats.

    The other player wanted to play a construct who is not awakened at all but awakens (obtains empowerment).

    I wanted to play a hard science concept (our main group of Technocrat characters for our main game have many people who skirt the line of unmutuality and include social science or something that is not necessarily science at all in their paradigm/instruments). I had also suggested playing around with the Enhancements background in part because I typically don't go anywhere near that stuff and because we hadn't had someone as a more traditional, cyborged out (at least partially) Technocrat.

    I think we can explore themes related to transcendentalism and what it is to be human, which has involved to some extent eventual sphere choices and other choices (Avatar strength, etc.), that would point toward the construct being 'more human' than the real human (my character).

    We decided the construct was a progenitor satellite (satellite in the loosest sense of the term) who was placed in a fairly 'average' location (wherever that is), the progenitors lost funding (or were finding the project too expensive, there may be some Syndicate subtext there), and they managed to almost (or barely) cover costs on the project by selling off the construct to ItX. ItX figures they can train this construct as a competent, indoctrinated, but disposable asset (though potentially something that can be an improvement!) for the same work my character does.

    We settled on my character being a sort of Iteration X "Repo Man" (in a loose sense). I will have a B.S. or M.S. (should have gotten my PhD in this job market), so I won't be dumb (possibly even mental attribute primary; social tertiary at least for attributes). I ended up being tasked to be someone who knowledgably retrieves the maguffin from the field, or is smart enough to approach eliminating or addressing a threat intelligently (essentially an investigative/retrieval/recovery/clean-up role, with less subtlety than Black Suits but used because ItX might not want to share with another Convention, etc.). Anyhow, I imagine my first task will be to retrieve this construct, then start trying to train him (potentially as my replacement?). Notions of obsolescence (of my character/me) will pervade the story either leading up to or after the other character's empowerment, especially when that character/construct has a higher Avatar/Genius rating than me (and maybe even when he has an intuitive grasp of the Life sphere [and possibly the Mind sphere] and I don't, etc.).

    I welcome exciting story concept thoughts you might have on this. I haven't offered all details because this is already a fair amount to read, but I'm happy to go back and forth more as people have interest.

    Of particular help might be if any merits or flaws scream out as amazing to take for either character here (I doubt the construct is going to take the Construct Flaw, and he might not even have any enhancements of any kind, thereby not having an genetic flaws, either, but I still welcome flaws or merits suggestions for either character!). I'm quite fine not having any merits or standa-lone flaws for the character because the Enhancements Background is such a huge beast of a thing to add to a character (at least in some cases).

    (2) Enhancements Background

    Holy shit is this a mechanical mess (pun sort of intended)! You have the Enhancements Background, which costs 2 freebie points per dot and gives either a permanent paradox point or genetic flaw (can one get a genetic flaw for a subtle cybernetic enhancement, or is it always permadox for cybernetics, for instance? I think it's the latter), and each dot gives +1 attribute (no attribute higher than 8, can split between at least two attribute boosts, if not more) or 3 points to spend on devices that are appropriate as cybernetic/biomod/gengineered enhancements (with examples of those in the book). There's also a "background cost variable" (or something like that) on boosting attributes in the Biomod (but also applicable to Cybernetics and gengineering routes?) that seems to suggest instead that each attribute increase of 1 is just a device point cost, not a background cost (I think that might be doing it wrong, and the background cost variable is more refer back to the Enhancements Background rule generally).

    Anyway, I'm trying to sort out what might be really efficient or amazing things as enhancements and what is just better done as a sphere effect. The balance here is interesting; this character is being built standard (so 15 freebie points). I am not sure it's wise to go below Arete 3 (even with using an attribute + ability roll to reduce the difficulty of the effect by up to 3, which our ST will be completely fine with; our main game has a house rule of using the attribute + ability roll to add successes from that as dice to the Arete roll, which means the new ST won't see an issue with a lesser prize of difficulty reduction I think, and we have told him we will assume RAW unless he wants to deviate).

    The point here is that Enhancements 5 (which if cybernetics must be permadox, is 5 permadox points, good god) is 10 freebie points, and Arete 3 is 8 of 15 points. In other words, an Arete 3 (aka Enlightenment 3), which seems necessary, limits Enhancements to 3 or so, assuming I don't bother buying much of an Avatar/Genius rating or increase Willpower (both things I generally prefer to do in most games, though I often don't start with Prime, so maybe an Avatar/Genius rating is kind of irrelevant for a Prime user?). I'm leaning toward Enhancements 2 at this point, but I wanted your guidance.

    I figure one eye will be cybernetic (but be mostly undetectable as fake?) and/or have cybernetic attachments to the eye (I also need to look into whatever it is that Iterators have put in their skull casings; I think I would choose the more modern version of that, not the older one that involves cutting out part of the brain, and the ST might just give that to me as a standard thing instead of requiring background points). The cybernetic eye could really just be an instrument (apparatus/focus) for 1-dot sensory perceptions with the proper paradigmatic explanation, of course.

    I was thinking about having at least one cybernetic arm. I was also considering having my skeleton crushed or broken in so many places that they basically repaired the whole thing with Enlightened Science, and they went ahead and laced that bad boy with some Primium with the idea that as a bit of a brick, I can investigate, likely won't die, and if I do, the body should be mostly recoverable (perhaps along with the memories if they extract them properly, I dunno). This can also justify putting me on riskier assignments (and might actually help a starting Mage not automatically die to a single vampire or werewolf, assuming I also have solid plans in place).

    Is there any thing particularly efficient or amazing in the Enhancements Background? Increasing an attribute is super efficient relative to freebie point costs, but that can be a bit boring. I was thinking just for story purposes having +1 strength with anything involving my cybernetic arm (assuming that's sheathed up with actual organic skin/tissue of my own or super convincing synthetic alternative). That said, I imagine I'm more often than not using aggravated damage bullets to obliterate anything I actually need to kill (Prime 2, choosing the right ammo as well, firearms just being so damn effective, etc.), so a Dexterity + 1 or just not bothering with it seems fine to me.

    Primium countermeasures seem nice, but expensive. I was kind of thinking just going with the 4-point, 2-dice version because one has to get the Skeletal improvement (2-point cost, required for any 3-dot enhancement, of which Primium is for any rating of it). So 6 point cost = Enhancements of 2 dots (and a 4 freebie point cost, leaving me with 11 to spend or 3 if I get Arete 3 and choose no flaws, and that is before investing in an Avatar/Genius or Willpower beyond base, either).

    Also, the Skeletal Enhancement (2 free health levels) seems super efficient at a glance. Am I on point about that? This looks too good not to take for anyone who ever might be in a serious fight of any kind, even if one took nothing else.

    For 2 dots of Enhancements, I can get armor that soaks 4 dice against bashing and lethal, and 2 dice against aggravated. But with Matter 2 (or is it 3?), I can do enhanced armor up to 5, with no dex penalty, and with Prime 2 it can soak aggravated. Is this not just a waste of 4 background points, especially if I have Prime 2? I might be more interested if Matter 3 is required for enhancing armor, but I dunno.

    The enhancements involving heightened senses seem to be a waste in a game where one has strong magickal perception options at 1-dot of their power. This character likely will have Forces 1 and Prime 1, so I don't see the point of enhanced cybernetic senses.

    Aggravated damage weapons (the ridiculous plasma cannon, and aggravated damage claws or horns or something silly) all seem rather meh in light of decent mundane options that can just be made to do aggravated damage with Prime 2, no?

    Part of me is prepared to just have Cybernetics be an Instrument for my procedures and leave it at that.

    I will say the Primium (along with any Prime countermagick I can muster) is partly there to prevent some asshole Life mage from ripping apart the character's cybernetics without much effort.

    Is an Avatar 0 stupid with Prime 2 (was asked above, but I put it down here anyway)?

    Would a flaw involving having lost a limb matter at all if it's just cybernetically replaced? Would the flaw represent the risk of a Life mage more easily making the arm inoperable, or is that counter-balanced by some other types of mages being screwed by the arm being cybernetic?

    Any and all advice is welcome. If you bothered to read this whole thing, thank you so very much. You are awesome, and I welcome responses of any length if you have them.

          Okay so a few things. Enhancements isn't just one thing, it's a few things. You can pick one of them, and they all have flaws and upsides. I'm not sure on exact names, but I understand the ideas.

          1: Genetic Engineering. This adds to Attributes, and gives Genetic Flaws. You are enhanced down to your DNA, so this won't be lost even if you're cloned. If your germ-line is altered, and you're fertile, your enhanced attributes might be heritable!

          2: Cybernetics. This gives the Enhancements (Reinforced Skeleton, Online Access, Primium), and gives Permanent Paradox. These can be removed since they're tech plugged into your body, and can be made to undergo Rejection with Life/Matter, harming you horribly.

          3: Biomodification. This is where machine and living organism is interwoven to the point where it has formed a synthesis. It gives BOTH Genetic Flaws and Permanent Paradox in exchange for Enhancement dots, but the advantage is that these can never be removed (short of carving out parts of your body aggressively) and can't be forced into Rejection. They're a part of you.

          Now if you go for Cybernetics, Enhanced Skeleton is a GREAT move! It's very powerful, but this Background is expensive and comes with lots of flaws You have 15 points to work with if you get Enhancements 5, so invest in this, 10 points of Primium, and then spend your other 3 dots on whatever you wish. I agreed with my ST that 3 points buys you a reinforced powerful computer implanted in your brainstem, through which I could access the internet and do whatever you can do with a computer.

          Why invest in Primium? Well at 10 dots of Primium you have 5 dice of countermagick. At difficulty 7 that will result in 2-3 successes on average when countering magick directed at you. Cyborgs have plenty of primium because getting their body to reject their cybernetics if extremely effective, and Primium is a great defense against this.

          As for points... you have 7 dots of Backgrounds as default. Put 5 into Avatar and 2 into Enhancements for Enhancement 1. Then you need 8 Freebies to get Enhancements 5. That leaves you with 7 freebies. Take a 1 point flaw and invest in Arete 3. Done.

          Here's a run-down of my cyborg PC (Cyrus, or Psi), created with normal Mage rules. I got ST permission to take more than 7 points of Flaws however, using the Book of Secrets optional system.

          Primary: Physical, Secondary: Mental, Tertiary: Social

          Abilities are focused on Craft, Technology, Hyper-Tech, Computer. Hyper-tech 3 means that your understanding is great enough that *any* hypertech you find, even if it was created by someone with wildly different methods, is somewhat understandable to you. This opens up a lot of potential tools.

          Arete 3

          Correspondence 1, Prime 1 (Affinity), Life 1, Matter 1, Forces 1, Mind 1

          Enhancements 5 (10 points in Primium, 2 in Skeletal Reinforcement, 3 in an Internal Computer)
          Avatar 5
          Node 2

          Willpower 10

          Merits:

          Computer Aptitude (-2 difficulty to Computer rolls; he uses computing and hacking as part of his Focus, so this is useful)
          Hands of Daedalus (-2 difficulty to rolls involving crafting, tinkering, creating... furthermore, any object you've had time to toy with becomes a Unique Personalized Instrument. So that muscle car you spent a few hours tuning gives you -2 difficulty to Arete rolls if it is the Instrument for an effect, such as making a quick escape using Correspondence 2 and Time 3.)

          Flaws:

          Degeneration (7) (Takes an unsoakable level of Lethal damage each week; furthermore his body has absolutely no natural healing process and needs to be repaired with Medicine, Biotech or the Life Sphere; this is because he was a prototype for a cyborg line, rather than a model that was meant to last a long time)
          Hit List (Progenitors)
          Probationary Sect Member (Traditions)


          Now this is only possible because my ST allowed me to take more flaws, but it shows how much you can get for a starting PC if you have a strong sense of what you want to invest in.
