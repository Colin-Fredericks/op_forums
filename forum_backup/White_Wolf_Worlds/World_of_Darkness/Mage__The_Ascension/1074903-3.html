                                    Honestly, I don't. We're talking about Europe in the 1000s and 1100s. Long distance travel and communication is fairly limited, even when we take magic into account. Individual Houses are very insular, distrustful of one another, and secretive.

                                    Mistridge isn't going to happen until 1210. There aren't a lot of outside threats. So the Houses are their own worst enemy. Most Chantries probably didn't have regular visits. And if someone is scrying at an adversary and encounters an impenetrable field, they're less likely to think "Tremere is turning into a Vampire" than they are "Tremere is plotting something, maybe against us!" and shore up their own defenses instead.

                                    And then there are a few other factors:

                                    The Tremere were really good at directing cultural prejudice to easy victims, like Diedne in the Schism War.

                                    The scholarly nature of Hermetic Magick would have made it relatively unremarkable for the Tremere Council to spend a decade or two holed up in Ceoris "studying".

                                    It's not really clear how widespread knowledge of vampirism was among Mages of the time period. It's very possible that should an outside Mage see a Tremere Vampire, they might notice a lot of very strange characteristics, but not the cause. Heck, if the vampire is less pretentious, she can even give an explanation: "We were working on a new immortality potion, but it seems to have backfired. Hopefully this wears off soon because it's really inconvenient!" Actually, Hermetic pride can work in her favor under those circumstances. What Hermetic Mage would want to admit to something that embarrassing if it isn't true?

                                    Per the source books, the House conversion was fairly slow, which means that when Tremere needed to send a messenger or ambassador, he'd likely have a number of blood bound ghouls who could serve that purpose too and avoid detection. Any weird magical observations can probably be explained away in a similar manner as above.

                                    It's not foolproof obviously, and per the wiki article (a dubious source, I understand, but sadly I don't have my books in front of me), there were some people who suspected them of diabolism in the 1100s, but they weren't charged. Presumably there wasn't enough proof. And Tremere's ties to Tytalus and Flambeau were probably an advantage too, since those Houses were the most aggressive during the Schism War. We're less likely to see obvious fault in our friends/allies than we are in the people that we dislike.

                                        This, especially.

                                        It must be remembered that for Kindred, the Tremere are an outside context problem. Cainites and mortal magicians have kept their distance since time in memorial, because both know just enough about each other to be wary. The Tzimisce in particular never gave House Tremere any mind; they competed for few if any resources in the lands they inhabited, and the Hermetics rarely interacted with the spirits of the land. (Hermetics as a whole are more interested in the spirits of the High Umbra).

                                        For the first few years after the Tremere inner council become vampires, the Tzimisce hardly notice, much less care. The missing elder would more easily be chalked up to reclusive behavior, or having gone into Torpor. Once the death is confirmed, any Tzimisce investigating (which is likely only to be one or two lords at first) would more readily assume another Kindred committed the deed. Those weird wizards doing it would be inconceivable. It's only when the Tzimisce realize there are new vampires running around the lands surrounding Ceoris that the idea would even enter the Tzimice's minds. It would take far longer for the few Tzimisce who know about it to spread that information to the rest of the Clan, AND make them see the gravity of the situation. Why should a Voivoide hundreds of miles away care about magi-turned-vampires? Sure, it's presumptuous and insulting, but there's other work to be done.

                                        From there, the Bystander Effect kicks in hard. Even Tzimisce lords who care about the Tremere are more willing to let one of their peers take cracks at the fledgling Clan. And if all of them think the same thing, that means they are all paralyzed by inaction. Once someone finally does take action (probably ones who knew the fallen Tzimisce elder personally, and had what passes for fondness among the Clan for them), they wouldn't necessarily commit all their forces to the effort. The Tremere are small and weak - hardly any of their number are vampires, and barely vampires at that. The Tzimisce's rivals, meanwhile, are strong, and ever watchful for opportunities. Those Tzimisce who choose to engage must keep some of their forces and resources in reserve, lest they be blindsided by a peer. Moreover, no one trusts another lord enough to readily work with them, as the army of one could decide to stab the other in the back. Together, the Tzimisce could easily crush this bloodline of usurpers. But Clan Tzimisce is not united. Clan Tremere IS.

                                        Same goes for the Gangrel, who are even less united in purpose, and have fewer resources to commit to the war effort. They wouldn't even get involved at all, until Gargoyles start getting made (and only after a period of time where the Gangrel figure out where their buddies went).

                                        All of this adds up to one important fact: the enemies of the Tremere lose the initiative. If they'd struck early and en masse, the Tremere could have done little. But by delaying, it gave the Warlocks the time they needed to develop Thaumaturgy, and all the tricks that power would bring. Bought them time to get their vampiric act together, so the mortal parts of the House wouldn't see what their masters had become. Bought them time to create Gargoyles, and other monsters, and wards against ghouls and spirits and vampires. Bought them time to get help from the rest of the Order of Hermes. Bought them time to trade favors with the Ventrue, who already had a rivalry with the Tzimisce, and had need for magical assistance. Bought them time to locate a sleeping Antedeluvian, and devour them.

                                                    As individuals and as a clan, we strive to master the knowledge of our newfound methods of magic, which we call Thaumaturgy. Already, we have learned to duplicate much of what we lost in becoming immortal.
                                                    Both make it clear that what magic they possessed before was lost when they became vampires and they had to develop a new way to do the same effects they had done before.

                                                    Clanbook Tremere Revised pg 13
                                                    Tremere unearthed ways to apply old magical theories to the new blood. From these roots he developed Thaumaturgy, the means to shape blood and other elements by manipulating the very Curse that animated Kindred. Knowledge of Thaumaturgy spread througout the clan, a replacement for the mystic arts the members of the house had left behind once they became undead.
                                                    Again the material presents the case that the Tremere lost whatever magic they had and quickly developed a new way to duplicate the effects.

                                                    DAV20 pg 62
                                                    the fledgling clan worked rapidly to develop new spells and rituals to protect themselves. The speed with which they created their new Discipline, Thaumaturgy, shocked other vampires.
                                                    This implies that the Tremere's work in adapting their mortal magics into Thaumaturgy happened very quickly and early in the clan's history.

                                                    From the above as well as the previously mentioned material in my other post we can determine two things. First that a mage embraced by a vampire loses the mortal magics they held though to be fair most of the material implies awakened magic. However in the same way it does not mention that they continue to use other magics they had in life. Second Thaumaturgy was developed to replace the magic they lost. It doesn't say that the Tremere used magic from their mortal days to buy time while they developed Thaumaturgy, nor does it say they developed some aberrant form of magic which they later discarded for Thaumaturgy. All of it says roughly the same thing; that the Tremere lost their mortal magic and raced to adapt and develop a new power which could replicate what they could accomplish previously.

                                                    Additionally Thaumaturgy's early paths and rituals (those listed in DAV materials) were directly adapted from the various magics they had studied and used in their mortal life as mages. As stated in my previous post Gargoyles were developed in 1121 thus Thaumaturgy 5 must have been achieved by that point. Considering that Thaumaturgy 5 had to have been reached by 1121 and the number of Paths (which have 5 levels listed) and Rituals available in the Dark Ages (1197 in the original Vampire: Dark Ages) I can only assume that the fundamentals of Thaumaturgy must have developed fairly quickly which is reinforced by the bit in Guide to the Low Clans about the quicker Pillars flowing easily into sorcerous paths and the DAV20 mention of the speed of Thaumaturgy's development shocking other vampires. The converting of magical power to XP seems to help explain how the Tremere developed Thaumaturgy so quickly. While they lost the magic they held, they still possessed all the knowledge and framework around that magic. Once they started applying it to vampiric vitae they rapidly found they could create similar effects to what they used to have, which would lead them to continue to convert the other powers they once had hence Thaumaturgy 5 in under a century and a multitude of full paths in well under two centuries.
