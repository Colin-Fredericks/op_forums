    Why I hate sorcerers rules, or why they don't fit in Mage

    01-27-2017, 10:46 PM
    So, one thing that has always bothered me: sorcerers.

    I mean, they are just another kind of weak supernaturals, not exactly guys that works "consensual magik".

    According to Mage, when I turn my TV on, I'm making a Forces+Correspondence effect without knowing. All sleepers are " sorcerers" of sorts, or "technosorcerers" if you prefer.

    I don't have a list of pre defined "powers" that I need in order to use other "effects". For example, I don't have:
    1 dot - turn the TV on; change channels;
    2 dots - Use a cellphone
    3 dots - Use a computer
    4 dots - Use the internet
    5 dots - Play videogames

    That's just ridiculous and absurd.

    Besides, the powers of Sorcerers in books... Are completely unrelated to anything.

    They are simply thaumaturgical paths... But they should be related to PARADIGMS

    So, there should be akashic sorcerors; acolites that train meditation, breathing exercizes, that believe in the balance of the Chi, that practice martial arts.

    Hermetic sorcerors, that study exoteric geometry, elemental analogy, and so on

    Virtual Adept sorcery, which relies on computer programming and virtual reality

    Technocratic "Sorcery" based on strictly "scientific" views.

    Sorcery is a form of magik after all, but is static Magic. But the SOURCE of their power is the same: the Avatar. So, it's also paradigm based.

    The way I do it? Instead of giving "disciplines" to sorcerous, I give them rotes. That's right, I give sorcerers rotes from Mages. But, those rotes are static effects. For example: Spiritual Ward against Wraiths (Spirit 2). The sorceror that learns that effect, can only use it that way. An effect "Ward against wraiths except stygians" (Spirit 4, Time 4, Entropy 2) is another effect and must be learned separatly.

    So, mechanics: I make all rotes as Knowledge Skills, therefore going from 1-5. Experience costs: level of the highest Sphere used for the effect multiplied for the current level on the effect. The first level always costs as much as the higher Sphere.

    For example: Fireballs (gotta love the classics). Forces 3, Prime 2. So, this "skill" will cost

    1 dot - 3 XP
    2 dots - 3 XP
    3 dots - 6 XP
    4 dots - 9 XP
    5 dots - 12 XP

    An effect to turn the skin into steel - Life 4, Matter 2
    1 dot - 4 XP
    2 dot - 4 XP
    3 dot - 8 XP
    4 dot - 12 XP
    5 dot - 16 XP

    So, all sorcerors would be paradigm dependent, and could actually do a few impressive things, but would be VERY restricted. And more: now, few people would REALLY be able to tell sorcerors and mages apart.

    For the dice roll: just roll the skill of the sorceror instead of Arete (so, sorcerors can't go beyond LVL 5, which I think is well within the concept, since Arete 6+ allows abandoning the foci - sorcerors can NEVER abandon their paradigms, because that would mean he would not be able to use the effect he learned)

    What do you guys think about that?

          Sorcerers are not meant to be one trick ponies though, they were meant to be competent practitioners of linear magic that were capable of functioning in a crossover environment much more easily than Mages (the original Sorcerer book was a crossover book that had no more to do with MtA than it had to do with CtD, VtM, WtA, or WtO). Linear magic is also able to thumb its nose at the concepts of the Consensus and Paradox, though every form of linear magic is associated with some magical tradition. In addition, I like the fact that Sorcerers can own Mages by shutting down their effects every turn by using countermagic and, with each turn that Mages attempt vulgar magick that Sorcerers shut down, they risk a catastrophic botch (and probably waste valuable Quintessence).

                              Karlgust
                              ​I think the main problem you are having is that WoD is a kitchen sink setting. That means that the stories you tell with it will be set in a world that has a whole lot of stuff in it. There are more than a dozen varieties of vampires. There are more than twenty kinds of magic. There are ten million billion shapeshifter types. And that means that there are a lot of stories you can tell. It means that you can tell a story that revolves around any one of those unique types of vampires, or one that involves people using any of those kinds of magic. But there are also stories you cannot tell. You can't tell a story where, like in Lightbringer, there are strict rules of magic and everyone follows them. You can't tell a story where anyone is particularly surprised that they don't recognize some new shapeshifting monster, because there are so many of those things that hopping around that no one expects to be able to identify them all. In short, you can't tell a story where uniqueness is unique. Nor can you tell a story where the edges of things are well defined, because there are too many edges to define.

                              "Sorcery is static magic" is a bullshit line meant to explain why vampires and werewolves don't explode in a cloud of paradox, because the world as Mage writers wrote it doesn't allow the existence of vampires and werewolves. It's a cludge meant to rectify the glaring error of world building. And it's a cludge that made WoD more of a kitchen sink than it was before. Personally, I like reductionist fantasy more than kitchen sink and would cut the sorcerers entirely given the chance.

                                                          Also, I think all these extra options help enrich the environment of the game. Sure trying to include everything at once sacrifices focus, but that was the point of them. I don't think WW ever expected storytellers to look through all their books and decide what role every single Fera, Craft, Clan, etc would take in their story, rather they were trying to fill in a few of the games, give illustrations around the dark corners.

If mage was just Traditions and Technocracy, you have yourself a great number of cultures which are ignored or simply absorbed into the others. The Islamic batini would just be a sect of the chorus, losing their unique Arabian focus. Similarly kopa Loa would just be another example of "everything third world's a dreamspeaker".
