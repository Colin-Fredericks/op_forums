    Magick's insight

    03-24-2026, 04:39 PM
    Magick, as described in the setting, follows the intent of the caster. But I wonder how much agency does magick has.

    First, a few questions to illustrate where my reasoning comes from.

    In M20 core, the only time that you need dots in knowledge to cast effects is when using Matter 4 to create a complex object (gun,car, computer).
    Note that I'm not talking about requiring abilities dots to use your instrumens, that's ok. The issue at matter is requiring dots for the effect per se.

    Then in how do you do that (p.28) has this sidebar:

    Dude, Do You Even Know What You’re Doing?
    A character won’t know how to do something unless she’s had the opportunity to learn how to do it right. Mere imagination
    is not informative enough to, say, turn a baseball into a sphere of high-grade plutonium, or to turn a bamboo reed into
    a functional katana. Sophisticated tasks demand specialized knowledge.
    In game terms, then, the Storyteller may rule that the creation and/ or manipulation of sophisticated elements, energies,
    and/ or compounds (nerve gas, pure weapons-grade plutonium, telephone signals, video recordings, etc.) may also
    demand an appropriate scientific focus and the associated Abilities (Biotech, Computer, Science, Technology, etc.)
    and specialties (Security Systems, Molecular Chemistry, and so forth). After all, it makes no sense for a tradition-bound
    Amazonian medicine-woman to be able to craft uranium or wipe computer data no matter how many dots she has in
    Matter and Forces. She simply wouldn’t know what it was she was trying to do.
    The same principle is also true if the character’s trying to use the Spheres to craft “primitive” materials and objects unless
    the character also has their associated Abilities (Crafts, Poisons, etc.). An armchair hacktivist isn’t likely to be much good
    at forging swords, working leather, or formulating effective tree-frog venom. Sure, he could look the process up online,
    but the literally hands-on skills involved can’t be downloaded – only practiced through experience.
    So, you need knowledge in science/tech/whatever to turn bamboo into a katana. This results in a few questions:

    -Why does it takes scientific knowledge to make that effect but doesn't take Medicine dots to cast a healing spell? Or Science dots to cast an invisibility spell? Afterall, the human body and light are as much (I'd say even more) complex as those devices

    -What if the mage is not a technomancer and does not have a scientific paradigm? He can't make those things?

    This rule seems very weird to me. And before we blame hdydt, BoS also has a similar sidebar at page 127.

    Now, we could just consider these books as optional and bad and ignore that, but the Matter 4 in M20 core remains. Why does a mage needs to know how a computer works in order to create/modify one? How much does a mage need to know? To the level of the billions of transistor that makes a board? Does the mage needs to guide "magick" step by step saying "ok, now connect the ram memory there, and the HD, and the..."? Shouldn't magick just be ablt to follow the intent of the mage?

    So, the real question is, how much magick knows and connect the dots???

    Then other questions might emmerge. If magick can connect dots based on the caster intentions, what happens if the caster wants to spawn someone else's journal (which he himself have no knowledge of the exact content)? Or spawn a book he never read?

    What if a Life mage turns himself into a male platypus without knowing that they are venomous? Would he be venomous?

    I am very eager to see your answers =)

      I think you're making an option into an absolute. To quote the sidebar itself, "In game terms, then, the Storyteller may rule..."

      That clearly indicates that this is not a hard rule. It might not be satisfactory to many people, but this feels a lot more like, "it's fair for the ST to rule a character have dots in some relevant Abilities to perform Sphere effects that work off of specific knowledge when it feels unlikely that the character would really know how to do something specific."

      It's also pretty clear to me that Focus is part of the point at hand. Healing doesn't require Medicine broadly because most characters are going to heal via their Focus and their Focus is going to explain how "healing" works via magic. If you heal by balancing someone's humors, Medicine isn't really the important Ability to possess. If your humor manipulating mage tries to perform magical brain surgery that goes beyond their Focus... that's when asking for some Medicine dots comes in.

      So I don't think it's about what "magic knows," it's about what's presumed as part of a mage's Focus and how to handle when things stretch the limits of applying said focus to Reality.

              I don't really see a point in answering questions that presume "the ST may" do something at their discretion as "the ST must do this." The sidebaris phrased with that distinction. As far as I can tell the answers to the questions either address themselves or come down to a large degree of ST preference when you treat this as the optional control lever for the ST I feel the text is clearly pointing too.

To alter my example a bit, if your a Chorister that prays to heal people, you use Expression to use your prayer Instrument, regardless of what you're praying to fix. But considering how many prayers are going to be from archaic languages that don't even have modern words for specific modern diagnoses, you're going to struggle to figure out the right prayer to modify someone's chromosome 11 HBB gene if you don't know what that is or what it does. There isn't some prayer for that lying around. Inventing your own new prayer would still require you to learn enough to know what on Earth you'd be praying for. Tradition mages that want to blend their non-Consensus beliefs with Consensus-based rules of Reality have extra burdens over mages that have a Focus more closely aligned with Consensus.

              While the sidebar doesn't say this specifically, I see no reason not to extrapolate the "primitive" statement mean this comes into play in other ways in other Reality Zones. A technomage that's in a Reality Zone where all healing requires prayer to work as a fact of that bubble of reality, then completely lacking an understanding of what prayers to say means all your Life and fancy tools would get much harder to use effectively.

                      Based on the totality?

                      Mage functions on patterns. Mages can magically deal with broadly defined patterns easily. Using Matter + Prime to create a book doesn't require anything more than knowing what a book is. Creating a specific book requires knowledge of its specific pattern. A mage doesn't have to necessarily "read" a book to replicate it, but they need to have access to a specific book's pattern to create a direct copy of that pattern. Magic can scan patterns directly instead of the mage having to read something, understand it, remember it, and then copy the book from memory later, but magic can't accurately create a book the mage has no idea of the specific pattern is. The mage can copy a book in a language they don't understand if they have it in front of them because the pattern contains the distribution of paper and pigments to replicate the text.

                      The same applies to a platypus. If your character knows what the pattern of a male platypus is, they can turn into one. They don't have to know every feature of it personally. They can be surprised to find out that male platypus feet have venomous barbs on them because they didn't study the pattern, they just copied it.

                      The sidebar is related as it's basically just a method of adjudicating knowledge of patterns.

                              Mage is somewhat counterintuitive because of the way patterns are constructed metaphysically. A platypus is a Life pattern. You only need Life 1 to look at that Pattern and gain the magical insight to turn into one.

                              Complex machines (and things like certain Forces patterns) are presented in the books as blurring the lines between being distinct patterns and an assembly of patterns. Unlike a book, a computer is more than just an arrangement of Matter into a specific pattern that has meaning through how pigments are arranged on pages. The things a computer does are an emergent property of the physical computer and the Forces patterns moving through it.

                              Ascension, again in totality, isn't particularly consistent on why it draws the lines it does. Life gets a complete pass on Life patterns being single discrete entities despite the chemistry and physics at play, while Matter and Forces don't. That's just the way the game has been written over the years.

                                                  The complexity stuff is very funny to me, because with almost no argument at all you can turn it against the ST (in good fun).

                                                  Me, playing a Hermetic: "I use Matter 2 to turn air into a volatile anaesthetic. It's a simple reaction converting one gas into a different gas, obviously chemically impossible, but it's Magick."
                                                  ST: "Anything that knocks people out sounds pretty complex."
                                                  Me: "Here's a diagram of the compounds in bread, and here's a picture of the gas I'm trying to make. Which looks more objectively complex?"

                                                  So anyways, strictly RAW, Matter 2 can turn air into anaesthetic gases. Should it? Well, that's a question for your ST, but honestly I can't think of an answer that says 'no' to that (in a general way, not a focus-specific way) which doesn't undermine all of the logic on which the Matter sphere is built.

                                                  All this to say, there are some very, very simple molecules and compounds which are extremely dangerous. If your difficulty in Mage is based on complexity, then it rewards people who are intelligent enough to think of extremely simple yet powerful effects. If everything is based on how big the 'result' is, that sounds fair, but can wind up being very arbitrary.

                                                  For example, turning a rock into bread. It's no big deal, I doubt anyone cares. If you go by complexity, it's either doable or not doable, with no arbitrary nonsense. But if the game is about survival in a place with total starvation, suddenly the "how big is the impact" model will care a LOT about this stone to bread situation.

                                                  One thing I do value is consistency, so whatever a ST does, it should be consistent.
