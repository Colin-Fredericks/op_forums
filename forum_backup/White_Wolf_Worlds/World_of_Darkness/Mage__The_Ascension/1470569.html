    Wonders background cost

    12-15-2021, 09:12 PM
    I know there are some threads that discuss this topic and maybe it doesn't have a definitive answer, but it doesn't hurt to try.

    M20 has this table in the background section about wonders as well as in the toybox that says each dot of wonder background gives 1-3 wonder points. This means a wonder with a lvl 5 sphere effect would cost 4 background points, if the wonder points are double the highest sphere lvl and 2 background points, if the ratio is 1:1. Note that the wonder background doesn't have the $ symbol to show that the background cost is doubled.



    Now, after this table in the toybox section, we have the descripiton of a wonder with a lvl 4 sphere effect costing 8 background points (actually 9 because it has +1 in Arete), which means wonders should cost highest sphere lvl effect x2 in background points.



    So, we have 4 options for a lvl 5 effect wonder:

    A) Costing 2 background points, which is ridiculously low, but makes sense according to the table
    B) Costing 4 background points, which makes more sense, but we have no reason to think that since it's a mixture of both table and description

    C) Costing 8 background points, being another mixture of both table and description, meaning that you get the doubled wonder cost from the table (10 points), see how much dots it costs (4) and then double it.

    D) Costing 10 background points, following the description and slmost all the examples provided by M20, but ignoring the table

    So, I ask now: is there any official FAQ somewhere explaining this? What you think is the right way to calculate it?

    I believe option D is the most used and accepted. On this ase we should just throw the table away?

                      Buying Backgrounds for xp was always a polemical thing. Rules allow it, but in general I see it makes more sense to just gain and loose them in the story (finding/building) - and charging xp for these things would be supremely bizarre (like, a character found a magic want but no xp? Can't use it. Weird. It becomes more so if you can't meet people, make friends, or buy property that will aid you w/o expending xp).

                      The second case (you just spend xp and the ST suddenly puts a wonder in the char) offers some potential. It could be understood as the character expending downtime to, say, do some favors to a Master in exchange for a Wonder. Instead of, say, expending time hitting the books to learn that extra dot of Sciences or training Martial Arts. Ultimately, it's abstracted growth that happens mostly off camera in all cases

                      I wouldn't use this, but if I did that's how.

                      As for the rest:

                      I'm not 100% sure if this helps or makes things worse, but I think there's some wordplay there between "dots" and "points" there.

                      As you note, the book says "that 4 dot Arete 5 Talisman then..." .This indicates that there's a difference between the Background "dots" and the amount of "points" they actualy cost. Dots and points aren't the same.

                      It get's worse because it clearly could be interpreted that buying a Wonder with freebies costs more than with the points you're given to buy Backgrounds (weird, because Bacground points cost 1 = 1 with freebies).

                      But if we make the logical jump to say that freebies and Background points are the same since they cost the same, I think that maybe one can make some sense of this (and why the example costs 10 points)
                      Originally posted by The Toybox
                      An individual Wonder can have up to one separate power for each dot in that Background. A three-dot Wonder, for instance, can have up to three different powers, although many three-dot Wonders have only one or two abilities. Because each Wonder uses Sphere-based Effects, those dots depend upon the Sphere Rank of the object’s most powerful Effect. A Talisman, for instance, that uses a Forces 4/ Prime 4 Effect would be a four-dot Background, its Arete Trait as high as 4.
                      ...for one extra Background point, that four-dot Talisman could have an Arete of 5...
                      My interpretation: Sphere & Effects "cost" dots, not points. Extra Arete costs points instead

                      Hence: A Wonder with a 4th level effect it's a 4 dot wonder. And a 4 dot Wonder can cost from 10 to 12 xp/freebies/background points. Because that variable number of points it's what the 4th dot costs. If we err to the minimum, it would have an Arete of 4 and cost 10 points. It could have an extra Arete added for one extra point (now it costs 11 points) that's not going to change the amount of dots the Background shows (it's still a 4 dot Talisman)

                      It's similar as how the amount of Effects a wonder has doesn't get reflected in the cost, only the amount that it could have (except this time it *does* cost you something to increase the value to the maximun). If that wonder had Arete 8 instead, it would cost 13 points and would figure as 5 dots Wonder in the sheet

                      Does this mean that the dots on the sheet reflect little about the real value and power of a Wonder? Yes, yes it does. It isn't a very nice interpretation, but it's all I got

                                                          Wonder rules change a lot between books, and aren't particularly clear in any edition. And you can imagine examples that contradict the rules are going to be present (to be fair, Rotes sometimes contradict the general rules of magick). Idealy, one would want to call these "exceptions" - but for that they need to be less than the normal.

                                                          Revised Core it's very fuzzy about the limitations, it even says that the level of the Wonder and that of the Background don't need to correspond.

                                                          FbdF it's, in my opinion, the best book about Wonders yet. But it's far from perfect.You have a more complex system that varies depending on the type of the Wonder - which makes a lot of sense since wonders of different types have different capabiliters, and contemplates stuff like - say - than a Wonder with one Effect and a Wonder with two Effects shouldn't cost the same. On the other hand the layout of the book isn't great and mixes a lot of fluff with crunch. Also, the examples sometimes seem wierd - like that one you mention, it doesn't seem to correspond with the rest - and the system isn't always clear.
                                                          Relevant examples of these systems:
                                                          p.20: An Artifact point value it's equal to the Sphere ratings of all it's Effects; double this total for Artifacts with continuous Effects (+/- flaws, merits or Paradox handling).
                                                          p.48: Talisman ... point cost is equal to their Arete plus 1 point per (extra?) Effect plus 1 point per 5 Quintessence they can store.

                                                          If the +1 point per Effect ain't talking about extra Effects, then the ONLY Talisman that has the cost right it's the Shuriken Glove. But I want to give them a generous reading and say the wording it's about adding Effects, and the Gloves are the exception or a typo (or maybe it has something I'm not seeing...).

                                                          Personaly, my understanding it's that the Background taken at level 5 as allowing a single level 5 Effect Wonder for sure, or a combination of lower levels that add up to 5 - but more than that could be negotiable at ST discretion. That's what I've used in the past. Perhaps there's other Revised systems I'm not familiar with.
