    Originally posted by tasti man LH View Post
    ...yeah in general I wouldn't put too much stock in the 1e depictions of the factions, since those had the Traditions being Paragon Good Guys and Technocracy as Objectively Evil guys. Something that 2e-M20 walked back from.

    Unless, of course, you prefer them to be that way at your table...
    I admit, generally I prefer to play Technocracy games but it's with the caveat that I tend to play my Technocracy games like I play my Vampire: The Masquerade games. I've run about six Technocrat campaigns and about 2 Tradition ones in my entire history of gaming since the Nineties.

    How do I run the Technocracy?

    I draw from the old TV show La Femme Nikita and emphasize the paranoia and fear of being a Technocrat. Your bosses can and will order you "cancelled" at any given moment and you have no option of resigning or disobeying orders. Brainwashing happens but most Technocrats have a low level of it so they can have very low morale. They buy deeply into the "hard man, making hard choices" ideology with the view that if you're not willing to kill a innocent man to save 100 (or who your superiors say will do it) you're not playing ball. You do, however, get face to face with lots of much-worse people in the world. Corruption is very casual with cloned sex slaves, embezzlement, mortal prejudices, and use of Union power to increase your own personal power. In-fighting is constant between Conventions and individual constructs.

    How do I run the Traditions?

    I've never run a Chantry game. Both my campaigns have been street level with the premise that the Technocracy is always watching you and if you're found out, you will be killed. Your family will also probably be kidnapped, brainwashed, or cloned and replaced if you don't run from them as well as cut all ties. You have to live off the grid and maintaining a mortal identity is incredibly difficult. Expect your mortal identity to have been suddenly wanted in six states for felony murder, pedophilia, or other crimes you didn't commit. The Traditions when they showed up are effectively lunatics who live in Narnia rather than try to live in the real world. They're often completely at odds with "normal" people and aren't especially concerned with their lives.

    I view these as my own canon versus a proper version of either. Effectively, I ran Disparate games instead of Tradition ones.
