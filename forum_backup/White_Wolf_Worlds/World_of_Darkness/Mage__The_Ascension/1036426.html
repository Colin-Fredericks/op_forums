      Different experience with cWoD books, think fluff, lots of fluff. More seriously, cWoD Mages do not have a morality system and are limited by Paradigm, Paradox, and Pattern. Paradigm is broadly what individual Mages believe that their style of True Magic lets them do. Paradox is the response by Reality, influenced by local Concensus, to Mage violating natural laws. Pattern is the foundation of reality that Mages add, change, or subtract.

      If Mages use vulgar magic, they accumulate Paradox. If they alter a Pattern for an extended amount of time, the Pattern suffers Pattern Bleed. So if a Mage gives themselves superhuman attributes for extended periods of time, they will accumulate Paradox (because it is vulgar) and suffer Pattern Bleed (because it lasts for more than a day).

          First big difference: there is no Atlantis, and if there is it is not important at all. Magic is paradigm based. Magic can take any form from high rituals with lots of symbols and chanting, religious ceremonies with prayers and sacrifices, building complex machines that run on science fiction rules, or simply taking a few pills to put you in the right mind frame. Science is basically the almost globally accepted form of magic, and this global consensus is managed by the technocracy. These guys often fight to keep reality predictable and mages out of the way.

          id suggest reading up on the spheres you are interested in, as there will be subtle differences on what each rank allows you. Assume nothing from Awakening is accurate.
          Pay special attention to Entropy and Correspondence which were both massively reworked conceptually. Entropy is the magic of fate, chance, and decay, while Corr. focuses more on the concept that space is an illusion and all locations share a single point.

          Anyone can become a mage. You are not born a mage, you just awaken. All humans have sleeping avatars that can awaken, and once they do, their mage is capable of enforcing their will to change reality. In order to do this they need a paradigm and a focus. The paradigm is what they believe magic to be, and the focus is the tools and methods they use to enact change, be it those symbols, dancing, computer code, blood, or those previously mentioned pills. Magic can either be vulgar or coincidental. Coincidental effects are those that the average observer would not consider as all that weird, like causing a short circuit, making someone forget what happened yesterday, drawing a perfect hand in poker, or looking into the future via Chrystal ball. Vulgar effects would be blatantly reality bending things like throwing fire about, teleporting about, giving yourself superhuman speed, making trees grow out of a seed in the span of a minute. Vulgar effects always give you paradox, and if a sleeper watches, that's even worse.
          There are sleepers who can perform a rudimentary kind of magic that stays in the confines of reality and therefore does not trigger paradox. They are called sorcerers.

          Nodes are a thing in this game as is quintessence, which makes certain magic possible but otherwise can lower the difficulty of arête rolls.

          There are 9 traditions, one for each sphere. Each with their own group of beliefs on how magic opporates from the pagan Verbena to the eastern warrior monks of the Akashic Brotherhood. Read through these guys as you may have to pick one of them to be a mage of.

          The rules will be different mechanically too. For example, rolling 1s will eat up successes, and rolling 1s without successes will be botched. If you roll botches, not only have you failed, you've screwed up. Try not to botch arête rolls, that way lies paradox.

          When paradox accumulates, your ST gets more and more power to throw nasty things at you, and in the most agreviois form of this, that can be things like Quiet (mage madness) paradox spirits, permenent effects, or paradox realms.

          Any questions?

              I'll expand on some of the points others have raised.

              M20 revamped what previous editions called Paradigm. Personally, I think this is the best material, as it breaks what it now calls "Focus" in three parts: Paradigm (the Mage's worldview), Practice (the methodology used to exploit the assumptions of that world view), and Instruments (the tools used to enact the Practice). I like this better than previous editions, on the grounds that M20 gives extensive examples of Paradigms, Practices, and Instruments, something that previous books didn't present as well. If you're using M20, and you read nothing else from it (the book is quite large), read the Focus section (in addition to the Spheres, Paradox, and Traditions sections).

              Worth noting is that while the Focus chapter lists individual Practices, you aren't limited to just one. As the book says, most modern Mages blend two or three Practices together. A Dreamspeaker might mix Shamanism with Medicine Work, a Hermetic could use High Ritual Magick and Alchemy and Dominion, and an Etherite could combine Weird Science with Yoga and Martial Arts.

              There is no "Death" Sphere, like Awakening. Parts of what would fall under Death are shuffled into what MtAw would call "Fate" (here called Entropy), with the rest falling into other Spheres like Spirit, Life, Matter, and the like.

              There is no such thing as Reach in Ascension. You either can or you can't cast a specific Effect. Paradox is doled out either from casting Vulgar magick, or from botching a magick roll of any kind. It's for this reason that you'll want to consider spending points of Willpower and/or Quintessence when a spell is important, or you don't want to risk a failure/botch. Willpower points spent give you an automatic success in cWoD (so you don't botch), while spending Quintessence lowers your difficulty by -1 per point spent (by the way, cWoD usually modifies rolls by raising or lowering the difficulty of the roll, rather than giving or taking extra dice; for this reason, you want as many minus modifiers as you can, and as few pluses). You can only spend Quintessence points in one turn equal to your Avatar background (or, if the Storyteller uses the variant, to your Prime score, but that's an optional rule not talked about in M20).

              Like Saikou said, non-Mages are capable of using magic of their own. It's analogous to the mortal powers from the nWoD book Second Sight. A typical Mage game is about Mages - dur-hey - so you don't need to worry about the particulars of sleeper Sorcerers or Psychics.

                      1. Yep. Spirit is your one stop shop for most things ghostly, but how ghosts differ from spirits will depend on how much Wraith your ST has read. Doing Entropy magic on a Wraith can lead to interesting results. I have to warn you though, Necromancy is typically frowned upon by most traditions though. Also, if you force a Wraith to do stuff, that's a good way to annoy them if there isn't a mutual benefit, so if your ST knows what Wraiths are capable off, you may be in for a bad time if you abuse this power.

                      However, if you want to create a ghost, that's easy, you can use pretty much any sphere to do that. Or a knife, a knife works too.

                      2. Yes. Vulgar magic will typically give you 1 point of paradox fail or succeed, and more if it botches, while doing magic in front of sleepers will lead to a HUGE amount of paradox if the effect botches. Also magic in front of sleepers has a higher difficulty rating than unseen vulgar magic. Making a habit out of this is going to make you very popular with the technocracy though.

                      3. Not in the way Awakening has it. If you do magic in front of a sleeper, they might try to explain it away themselves, but the difficulty of their presence is in the casting, not in how the effect holds up.
                      In certain circumstances, sleepers watching your effect might actually be beneficial. For example, if you enter a church filled with sleepers who believe in Lay-on hands healing, then healing someone from near death with little more than a prayer and a touch will go from Vulgar with Witnesses to Coincidental.
                      The sleepers define the local consensus, but the mage's will is stronger.

                      4. Nope. When paradox happens, it's out of your control. You are at the mercy of your STs whims.
                      That being said, it is possible to bleed off accumulated paradox if it's low enough. If you don't do vulgar magic for a week you can lose a single point if below 5, otherwise not doing any magic for a whole month will bleed of a single point below 10. If you have higher than that, then it sticks until it backlashes. (these are optional rules though, so ask your ST how they're playing it).

                                      Necromancy is generally seen as unsavory, yes, although it has its adherents within the Traditions. The Euthanatoi are the ones who use Necromancy the most, as many of their factions (the Pomegranite Deme and the Madzimbabwe especially) would otherwise fall into other Traditions if not for their practice of death arts. Given that and their reputation as remorseless assassins, the Euthanatoi are often seen as necessary evils within the Traditions, by those who don't know them personally. It's also worth noting that the morality of Necromancy depends on how they are used. Dealings with ghosts that are built on mutually beneficial relationships, or are done to put troublesome spirits to rest, are considered more legitimate than Necromancies forcing ghostly compliance, for the user's own selfish purposes. And while killing people to gain "raw materials" for animated corpses and skeletons is obviously frowned upon, there is a long tradition of grave-robbing within some magical groups (I'm looking at you, Society of Ether).

                                                      If you're going to start bringing Werewolf lore into your Mage character, you should probably run it by your ST first so they can tell you whether they are willing to handle that sort of stuff. cWoD gamelines don't fit together nearly as neatly as nWoD does. Contradictions are rife and they require a heck of a lot of ST interpretation in order to sort out, and even then certain players might be a bit difficult about the STs decisions on the matter. Depending on what experiences your ST has had, they might want to put a severe limit on how involved things get.

                                                      So, one big thing that will define your character in Ascension is this: How did he awaken? Only the rare few mages are born awakened, usually they awaken as young adults, though children and those later in life have been known to awaken too. The Awakening is often, but not always, the result of a traumatic event in the life of the mage. This event will usually dictate aspects of how their magic manifests as well as predispose them towards particular spheres and traditions. The Euthanatos, for example, tend to have near death experiences trigger their awakening, while the Celestial Chorus will feel more like a calling or a point of clarity. There are outliers though, so don't feel bound by this.

                                                      Your mage's drive to be "better" than everyone else screams of political ambition. This could be a good fit for the social and political hierarchies that are in place in the Order of Hermes. Perhaps your mage envies the power of the few Archmages (those with sphere ratings above 5) that they have seen.

                                                          The Order of Hermes is a pretty good place to look in general, as one of the most interpretations of the Hermetic worldview is that humans are nascent gods.
