    I'd say less that their Avatars compel it and more that their Avatars are shaped by it. Or rather, it's a two-way street; determining what comes from the Avatar and what comes from the mage, or even whether that distinction is even meaningful, is a difficult question; intentionally so.

    That said, in my own head-canon, Geniuses tend to be Circumspect by default (with the Demented Eidolon Flaw representing any non-Circumspect Genius) whereas other Avatars tend not to be Circumspect. This may have something to do with Conditioning; but I apply that rule to all Enlightened Scientists, not just those in the Earthside Conventions: the Void Engineers, Navalon, and independent Enlightened Scientists also have Circumspect Avatars by default, despite being free from Conditioning. So the idea that there's some sort of connection between your Avatar/Genius and your Focus isn't new to me.

Just because the Hermetics, Choristers, Akashayana, and so on are pseudo-Theosophist, that doesn't mean that groups like the Wu Lung, Ngoma, Bata'a, Hypollytoi, or Templars have to be. And the Technocracy shouldn't be.

                                        So by changing the elements inspired by Gnosticism, Hermeticism, and Theosophy to being part of an in-setting faction's Focus instead of being an absolute truth of the setting, the groups outside of the Traditions are more free to not be beholden to those elements. It doesn't mean that they can't be; the Batini, as you say, are. But they don't have to be.

                                        Play this pseudo-Gnosticism shared among the Traditions as part of the reason why they're still together after centuries. And this further helps differentiate the Traditions from the Disparates, to whom they'd otherwise look almost identical.

                                            Yes, I agree. Though I view that as a holdover from an earlier time when Mage was written as 19th and 20th century occult philosophy vs modernity. I’ve spent quite some time trying to rework the Traditions so that they better map to what they claim to represent.

                                            The very idea about the "Avatars" and about "The One" and "Ascension", that's 101% Gnosticism. "Awakening" is just "gnosis", the idea that you open your eyes to the truth due to divine revelation - aka GOD speaks inside your mind the truth.

                                            That's right, we are pieces of the divine ("The One") that got fragmented, and now our job is to "remember that we are God" (aka to "ascend").

                                            Sounds familiar to anyone?
                                            The Matrix. The Truman Show. They Live. Dark City. The Lego Movie even has Will Ferrell playing the demiurge.

                                            Gnosticism is a very easy idea to make a story around. It has a natural tension between what seems to be and what actually is. It’s endlessly adaptable. It doesn’t lock you in to a specific religious or moral system. It naturally maps well to specific steps on the hero’s journey. Etc.

                                            Also, because we have only a general understanding of gnostic theology, we tend to overapply the label (hence “endlessly adaptable” for movie purposes). However, many, many secret and/or occult societies promote the idea that there is a greater truth open to the enlightened (i.e. themselves). That’s even the idea behind Plato’s cave allegory. So it’s a trend that goes back well before Gnosticism.
                                            ​
                                            Mage has an unfortunate tendency to blur the lines between the beliefs of some factions and the rules of the game. One of the ways this manifests is in expounding upon things like the shards of the One as if they are known truths of the game. The Technocracy gets its share of these too, if you know where to look. It's not a sign that the game is promoting a specific philosophy, but that it is built on a mishmash of various convenient philosophies.

                                                    My own take on it is that an Avatar is “whatever it is that lets you do Magick”. Which is extremely vague; but that's intentional.

                                                    But rather than doing away with Avatar related mechanics, I prefer to lean into the ability to customize them, to represent what the Avatar is in your case.
