                                      Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post

                                      Actually, most of the Traditions have their own internal "secret police" created to handle this sort of thing without having to rely on intervention by outsiders. The Akashic Black Hat Society, the Verbena's Silver Blade, House Quaesitor, the Etherite Council for Scientific Ethics, etc.
                                      Be that as it may, it depends on which book you're reading. At least among the Houses of Hermes, after the virtual destruction of House Janissary, the Euthanatos (who, incidentally, were largely responsible for Janissary's decimation) took on the role of police. Quaesitor might be the judges of Hermes, but, lacking an internal group to enforce those judgments, the Euthanatos had to fill that role. It is a state of affairs few are happy with, but they have the skills to investigate corruption (especially Jhor-corruption), and put an end to them. They are also an outside third party, which make them politically expedient for the mire that is the Order of Hermes.

                                      Even were that not the case, they'd investigate - and intervene - anyway. That might mean turning over evidence to the given Tradition's own authorities, or by taking matters into their own hands.

                                      It's worth noting that, if a mage happens to be killing people regularly - without it being willing victims - just to gain Quintessence or other forms of power, it's easier perhaps to ask forgiveness than permission. A target of summery execution is not necessarily going to be sympathetic in that case, so long as the evidence can be presented.
