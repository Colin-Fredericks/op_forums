    Charms for warding against Quiet

    09-13-2026, 08:39 PM
    I was reading How do you DO that? in order to see what spheres and levels of said spheres would be needed to craft a charm that would make the user not only capable of seeing and go against illusions made by other Mages, but also capable of identifying and unravel lies ingrained in the user's psyche (be it those lies told by others or the user themself). The more I was reflecting on this particular idea, I concluded that what I really thought about was essentially a charm that would defend the Mage against Quiet, or at least make it so it's easier to "Rise Out of Quiet" (for example, diminishing the difficulty of the tests).

    But then, I ran into some problems. First, the wards exemplified in the book are mainly around defending against external factors, not internal ones, which makes sense given that the sub-section explaining how to use wards makes part of the large chapter on Summoning. I went to the chapter focused mainly on Mind effects (Uncanny Influence) and although it gave me some ideas (mainly that such charm would need Conceptual Entropy i.e. Entropy 5), but again most of the scenarios of use in the chapter deal with an external force trying to influence you and not your own demons trying to wreak havoc in your mind. Lastly, I'm not even sure if such ward would be possible, given that the chapter on Summoning makes it quite clear that "wards cannot be set against Paradox", which makes me think that it would also not help in dealing with Quiet.

    So... what to DO here (pun intended)? If such a charm is even possible to build with RAW, it would need obscenes amounts of Quintesscence (and Prime 4), at least Mind 4, Entropy 5. Maybe Life, since there are biological components that alter your mind state?

      The first thing is to ignore that pike of garbage, HDYDT will require you have 3 Spheres at 4 to brew coffee if it can find anything argument for it.

      Now, you do have two different problems here, and understanding the distinction is essential, but also just how obscenely deep both are.

      1. Protection against inner "lies". That seems straightforward, but isn't. What is a lie? What is an ingrained one? How would you define the type of information you want the Magic to flag?

      A lie is a matter of perspective, and Mage is a game based on the whole reality being a matter of perspective. Magic is to Will your beliefs unto the world successfully. How can a Magic born of your Subjectivity understand an objective truth and force it over the Will that empowers it in the first place?

      Semantic details become very important here to analyze if this is actually a pretty simple and straightforward Effect, or actually the kind of fundamental question of Reality that can drive several chronicles.

      2. Quiet is not about self-deception, even if the symptoms can include this, and crucially it is not about your mind. It's about your Magic.

      What sets Quiet apart from "regular" disorders a Mage is also prone to develop is that Quiet is a Magical phenomenon affecting your powers and how you relate to them. I personally find it easier as a Psychologist to explain Quiet as your Magic going insane rather than yourself.

      The symptoms then are following in reverse: it is not that delusions and hallucinations within your mind trickle into your powers, but rather that your Magic is going haywire, tainted by surges of power and Paradox and unbalanced Resonance, and it affects you and your mind like it affects the rest of the world.

      Understanding difference between symptom and cause here is also important, because without one expression the cause will find another. A person who thinks they're being surveiled by Martians don't have a problem because they believe that, they believe that because they have a problem.

      The delusion is not a conclusion, it is an expression that was put there for a reason, which is why they're so resistant to reasoning even if otherwise rational, and also means of you do manage to break through obstination and dissolve the delusion, the vulnerability the problem caused (not necessarily the problem itself) is exposed to a potential crisis while the mind finds another expression. You solved nothing.

      With Quiet this is even more marked because the whole mental breakdown doesn't really hold much of a key for the cause of the Quiet. Yes, it uses your psyche to give form to itself, and yes, your inner demons are very probably behind why you used your Magic the way you did. But a Quiet itself is a Magical phenomenon and not the madness' content, what solves it is to rebalance the foundations of Resonance.

      How can we actually try those things?

      The first will depend on what you call a lie to self and what kind of Truth you want to get out of it.

      For the Charm against Quiet, I'd say you can get some basic stuff with as low as Mind 1, Prime 1: an Effect that both observes your mental state in a general sense and your flow of Resonance, Paradox and Quintessence to verify the heavier imbalances.

      You can get a lot against Quiet simply out of knowing sooner you're in Quiet, or at imminent risk. In fact this woukd also be your best bet overall, as anything stringer would also ne veering closer to Paradox and the risk of being flipped by it from a protection against Quiet into an amplifier of Quiet.

                      This is a really good description and it pairs well with one other aspect of Quiet that I keep struggling to put into words, which is that there is a break between perception and reality but that break happens outside the mage, before reality reaches his senses. The magic often isn't altering the mage's mind in any direct way, it is affecting the experiences his mind receives and processes.

                      There’s a horror short story I once read with one simple premise. The main character has a device that he can use to erase his own painful memories. He starts by only erasing his most painful ones, but then the worst of what is left become his most painful ones. Soon he is erasing embarrassments and social faux pas. Only now, he’s missing so much information and experience that he frequently has awkward moments where he gets caught not knowing things he should… and he erases those moments too.

                      In the story we meet him at a point where this has been going on for a very long time, and he now has so little experience at dealing with awkwardness and embarrassment that he’s actually getting violent when those moments happen. In an expected horror story turn, he kills someone, and the indication is this isn’t the first time though he certainly doesn’t remember any others. Nor will he remember this time.

                      Now I have no idea if this scenario would actually lead someone to violence and that's not why I brought it up, but the story illustrates the idea. The problem doesn’t lie within the main character’s mind, it lies with what reaches it and what gets to stay. His mind is processing as well as it can, its just getting (or in this case keeping) a deviant perception of reality. Mages in Quiet are in a similar situation. They are intentionally or unintentionally changing the reality that reaches them. They see clearly, they are just looking at the wrong thing; a magically induced echo chamber.

                          That is indeed a good example.

                          One exercise I like is to follow how Jhor is so widespread in the Chakravanti:

                          Euthanatos paradigm defends that the Wheel must turn for the good of all, and their primary role as its keepers is as healers. Death is not a primary goal, it's for lost causes, ideally select with high criteria.

                          Mastery of Entropy means their simplest tool to judge this criteria is Magic to look a person's place in the Wheel more or less directly. That means they cast Magic to judge fate and future morals in the worse cases possible, young and healthy bastards. They don't need to do it with people they think that can get better, only with those that may be lost causes.

                          Rince and repeat Magic used in horrible people to see if their path has any chance at improving with the occasional "no" weighing heavier by the day.

                          Forget how that affect a person's outlook. This changes Resonance. It gathers the desperation of the Mage when forced into this analysis, it gathers the symbolic weight of the bad results, much heavier than the good-ish ones. Captures whatever the target did in the past to be under scrutiny this heavy, and may do yet if trends are followed. The feelings of sorrow and anger the Mage must feel either toward the target, their victims or themselves. The Magic is changing.

                          A Mage can dive into their demons for a series of reasons, and they may find solace and heal the marks, that's whatever comes afterward. But Resonance reacts to Magic, especially if it is important, and their wholesome moments aren't probably when their greatest Magic work is performed. The Magic remembers that bad place keener than the Mage.

                          Low key Jhor is probably the Magic responding in accordance to accrued Resonance. Results get darker. Readings emphasize the bad stuff and mute the good. It's initially superficial, but little by little the Magic itself leans towards the worse judgment for people who would get a pass before.

                          Rinse and repeat.
