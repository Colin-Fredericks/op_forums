            Originally posted by Bluecho View Post
            Fair enough, though personally I wouldn't agree that Santa Muerte veneration (or, indeed, Brujeria for that matter) quite fit with the religions and practices the Bata'a are into. I classify the latter as needing to have imported African beliefs somewhere in the syncretism in order to qualify. Whereas Brujeria - and Santa Muerte worship as a subset of same - is typified by specifically American native beliefs mixing with Catholicism. Without the African element, it's a sufficiently different beast that it should be approached as its own magical tradition. The elements of having been ripped from one's country of origin and enslaved in another are significant enough that it changes the ethos, and a faith that lacks that must be examined distinctly.

            Hence, why I tend to see practitioners of Brujeria and other native Latin American magics as falling more readily under other groups. The Celestial Chorus for those who take the rebranding of old gods into saints seriously (those for whom the Catholic part of their syncretism is important). The Verbana for those who worship the old gods AS the old gods. The Dreamspeakers for those who lean more towards animism and spirit work. And the Euthanatoi for those who specifically call upon Santa Muerte, as they literally venerate death.

            That said, all of this is our attempt to force a specific and living set of cultural practices into fictional organizations. In the context of Mage, it comes down to politics and a mage's self-identity. So I wouldn't say it's an invalid choice for a follower of Santa Muerte to join the Bata'a, or any other group, or even to try forming the own Craft, separate from all others.

            (I will also fully admit that my classification laid out in the first paragraph is an arbitrary one. I was just explaining why THIS arbitrary division between Santa Muerte and Greater Brujeria, and the African-derived faiths, is perhaps important from a thematic and cultural standpoint. You may, contrarily, maintain that the faiths being syncretic AT ALL are reason enough for Bruja and Brujo to team up with Bokors and Voodoonistas.)
            It's a little off-topic, but, I can see the Bata'a being open to embracing the followers of Santa Muerte because of the similarity in the fundamentals of being a mystic/religious tradition based on syncretism of European beliefs with those of an oppressed group in the context of colonialism and it's aftermath in the Americas. Though voudoun and hoodoo are almost entirely syncretic between African beliefs of the slaves and the European beliefs of their masters (specifically Catholic, but that weakens with hoodoo), there are other members of the Bata'a who come from syncretic faiths that included considerable influence from the beliefs of the indigenous peoples as well. There is an accident of history going on here.... There just weren't any more Native Caribbeans left when the slave trade that brought the Africans to the New World But in South America, there most certainly were, oftentimes the two groups were enslaved alongside or together. (And, with some aspects of hoodoo that there were some instances where Native North Americans and escaped slaves did combine beliefs to a degree, like in the Great Dismal Swamp)

            So, potentially, the Bata'a would see Santa Muerte as being very much like, say, some of the similar mystic traditions from South America. It's very much an Aztec/Catholic hybrid with a specific focus. So a hand might be extended in siblinghood.

            Sure, there may be an issue of various mages of Santa Muerte being pulled into various different groups, the Bata'a, the Dreamspeakers (since the Aztecs would have been sorted into the Dreamspeakers by the Traditions initially in the typical othering of non-Europeans back in the beginning), the Verbena (since there is DEFINITELY a lot going on that would make the magic between the two similar enough to argue for it), the Euthanatos (because, ya know, "death mages") and, yes, the Nephandi and/or going it on their own as a potential new Craft. There is also the conceptual problem of how many mages there are, how many mages Awaken within or adopt the praxis of Santa Muerte beliefs and how that plays into the idea of Santa Muere mages being able to recognize each other and organize enough that there is the possibility of their identification as a discrete group. If the Santa Muerta mages don't internally organize, they are going to get drawn up into whatever group nabs them early on and integrated into that Sect. If they do organize (which seems to be the more basic assumption in Mage, e.g. that different mystic praxes self-identify and organize and the find some articulation with the existing Factions), they there would end up being some kind of selection process whereby a Sect and the Santa Muerte mages as a group would choose each other and they would become one of the constituent groups (like the House Bjornaer choosing the Verbena over the Order, House Luxor choosing to join up with the Etherites, etc.) or divide up (like the Valdaerman choosing to dissolve into Dreamspeakers, Verbena and Euthanatos).

                            I, too, disagree with the notion that Nephandi are naturally inclined towards subservience. They are still mages, albeit broken ones. And mages are free to determine their own Paradigm, whether that be one of self-actualization and apotheosis, or willing service to a higher (or, in this case, lower) power.

                            It depends on their outlook and worldview, and how they contextualize their emotional drives (which, in this case, are towards evil). Some Nephandi may, like regular mages (and mortals in general, for that matter), decide that atheism is the most rational view of the universe. Or, if they acknowledge that there are greater powers in the universe, that they aren't deserving of the Nephandus's attention or loyalty. They may decide to pursue Descent on their own.

                            Other Nephandi may find fulfillment in serving powers greater than themselves. In a similar way that normal people find joy and comfort in a god or gods, a Nephandus may so find joy and comfort in the belief in dark gods or infernal powers. Especially if those lower powers or alien beings resonate with the same black, despicable values that the Fallen feel innately. It is gratifying for a Nephandus who loves to revel and kill to have a dark god from outside the universe who encourages reveling and killing, and would teach the Nephandus new ways to revel and kill if the mage would only help them enter the universe.

                            Still other Nephandi - like the Infernalists - may not particularly like or dislike demons, but simply find it pragmatic to work with them. Seeing their patrons, less as things to be worshiped, but as business partners. They figure that if the demons turn the world into an extension of Actual Hell, that would be pretty grand (since it would mean humanity would suffer for all eternity). But of course, the Infernalist doesn't really want to be part of those suffering masses. So they figure, if they work with the demons now and help them bring out hell on earth, they could score themselves more comfortable positions in the new world order. Until that day comes, they can get plenty of fringe benefits from working with hell. Plus, they may figure, "Hell, I'm damned anyway, might as well get my damnation's worth now while I still can".

                                    Originally posted by Muad'Dib View Post
                                    Is there anything that makes these Nephandi that you described any different from Infernalists?
                                    Part of the Nephandi are, actually, Infernal Nephandi. The others just serve different masters. There are, though, two main differences with non-nephandi Infernalists.

                                    First, the Avatar: Infernalists have their same regular Avatar, struggling for Ascension, on which the demons have some right; demons may have enough rights to actually command it, but it's not like the Avatar (and therefore the Mage's soul and true drive) is willing to obey, he just have to. So you have a Mage willing to serve demons with an unwilling Avatar. Nephandi, having an inverted Avatar, are instead naturally driven toward their masters' desires (like in the widderslainte case, with the Avatar poisoning the mage's personality from the very start).

                                    Second, the will: Infernalists have bound themselves in servitude for power (on their current incarnation), while Nephandi have a more complex approach to their masters. Quoting from the M20:
                                    "They don’t serve Outer Gods, in truth. Nephandi simply recognize such beings as reflections of their own Enlightenment… a savage wisdom that sees the Absolute in every living thing and knows what’s waiting at the end of All."
                                    So, it's more like recognizing that your true nature is embodied by a specific alien entity in the Deep Umbra. You're still serving it, but your unity of intent makes you act more as allies.
