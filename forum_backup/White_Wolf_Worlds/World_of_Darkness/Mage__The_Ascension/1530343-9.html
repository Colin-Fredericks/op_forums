            “The world was flat before the Consensus” isn't a statement about whether or not reality is subjective; it's a statement about whether or not “the world is round” is an Earthly Foundation/Cosmological Constant: if the Consensus was able to change it, it isn't.

            What answers the question of whether reality is subjective is whether or not the things that the Consensus can't change are the result of some unimaginably powerful entity (such as God, Gaia, or the Weaver) wills it to be so (in which case reality is subjective) or if the things that the Consensus can't change simply are what they are. Now, reality certainly isn't purely objective, because both the Consensus and the Awakened can change it (in the form of Reality Zones in the former case, and in the form of Magickal Effects in the latter case); but it might be purely subjective, if the parts that the Consensus can't change are subjective.

            That said, I don't consider that too be a particularly interesting question, because it doesn't really affect anything that matters. What's more interesting is the question of what the Consensus can and can't do. In the earliest editions of the game, there wasn't much that the Consensus couldn't do; but in M20, the only thing we know for a fact that the Consensus can do is to define where the boundary between Vulgar and Coincidental Magick is; and even that's limited by the Earthly Foundations. In M20, each Awakened is arguably far more powerful than the Consensus, because mages can actually do things; the Consensus can only decide, somewhat, whether or not mages get punished for doing things.

            Let me repeat that: the Consensus can't stop a mage from doing something; it can only punish the mage for doing it. Likewise with Cosmological Constants, or Historical Inertia, or Earthly Foundations; these things don't forbid a mage from doing anything. They merely decide what the mage gets punished for. The only thing that arguably restricts what a mage can do, besides the mage's own Focus, is Countermagic; and neither the Consensus nor the Earthly Foundations do Countermagic. Since even an Arete 1 mage can do Countermagic, that's a remarkably weak Consensus.

            Now, the Consensus might also be able to make some things mundane that would otherwise be Coincidental, meaning that you don't need to make an Arete roll to do it; this is a widely held bit of fanon, and it's essential to any theory that holds that the Consensus can actually do something other than play reality cop. But surprisingly, it's never explicitly confirmed in the book.

            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                A slight correction to the above: I overlooked one aspect about Reality Zones. In addition to defining whether or not an Effect is Coincidental or Vulgar, it can reduce the difficulty of a Coincidental Effect or increase the difficulty of a Vulgar Effect, by up to 3; and that can be considered “hindering Magick”, as increased Difficulty tends to mean fewer successes. In fact, with the rule for Thresholds in place, a high-rank Effect that is defined as Vulgar with a significant difficulty increase could be thought of as not merely “disliked” by the RZ, but actively forbidden: a Master Effect with +1 Difficulty becomes Difficulty 9 and needs an extra success to succeed. The mechanism of getting there is a bit different; but the result is similar to a limited sort of Countermagic.

                And sometimes a simple house rule can have profound effects. I have a “reverse Threshold” house rule that says that if you have enough Difficulty reductions to bring the difficulty below 3, the extra reductions get converted into automatic successes. Applying this to RZs: a one-dot Simple Feat (requiring one success) that's defined as Coincidental with a –2 Difficulty reduction will instead automatically succeed; and if that Difficulty reduction is –3, the Effect could be a two-dot Simple Feat or a one-dot Standard Feat. Note that this is independent of your Arete; so it's arguable that even Sleepers should be able to take advantage of this. As such, the “reverse Threshold” house rule implements a limited way for the Consensus to push low-level Simple and Standard Feats beyond Coincidental, and into the realm of the downright mundane.

                That approach has its limitations: if that's how a RZ can integrate Effects fully, to the point that they're not even considered to be Magick anymore, it can only do so with one- or two-dot Simple Feats or one-dot Standard Feats. Still, that can cover a lot of ground.

                            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                            My view is to define the Consensus specifically as humanity's influence on the nature of reality, instead of being “any and all sentient entities that influence reality”. Which isn't to say that the Consensus is the only thing that influences reality; rather, I want a term that I can use to describe “what the people in this town believe” as distinct from “what the people in this town, as well as the spirits in this town and whatever other potential influencers that may or may not exist, believe”. For those mages who believe that everything's subjective once you've included all of the influencers, the word for the latter is “Reality”; but it's one of those things where the distinction between “what is innately true” vs. “what is true because that's what the Weaver believes” is moot.
                            You can say that with conviction, because Spirits don't actually (or don't necessarily) have free will. They are bound to their roles and their nature. A Fire Spirit cannot decide to be cold, the Weaver cannot decide to no try to instill order. In that sense, spirits are pretty constant.

                            So, if the Spirit's "Consensus" is constant, the only variable in the equation are the humans.

                            In order to truly change the so called "Earthly Foundations", or better called "Cosmological Consensus", you'd have to do like Wyrm and Weaver does, and try to actively promote a sort of "spiritual genocide" at the Umbra level, in order to promote an active change of the spiritual landscape of the Spirit words.

                            That "spiritual genocide" don't just include extermination of "undesirable spirits", but also their replacement by new ones; that could very well be the case of my example on the answer above about diseases being caused by curses vs microorganisms. I'm not saying that this was in fact what happened, but I'm saying that it COULD, this could be introduced as a de facto occuring in any table; not that the Union did it on purpose or that they knew what to do or how to do it, but when they introduced "microorganisms", spirits representing those were immediately born (or the OoR created them with Dimensional Science 5 even), and unknowingly to the Techies, those new "spirits of microorganisms" started to wage a spirit war in the Umbra against "cursing spirits", and they got aided by the Union relentless pursuit of curses and faith healing; this stablished a positive feedback loop upon which those new spirits reinforced the work of the Union and the work of the Union reinforced those new spirits, until such a a time where that old "Earthly Foundation" got effectively changed - while at the meanwhile, everybody thought that it was "just" part of the (normal/human) Consensus.

                            That's just a possibility mind you, and not set on stone. But it does gives more malleability to the players without screwing the setting (no, a Marauder still won't be able to make the "anti-grav spirits" become dominating in the Umbra).

                                                    Whether or not reality is subjective has to remain ambiguous in order for the premise of Mage to work. Some mages have more open paradigms; others do not. If one group is demonstrably wrong, then one attacks the basic theme of Mage. I think that getting too deep into the mechanics of the Consensus misses this crucial point. Mage should not be bound by the settings of other games. Many of the limits imposed on the Consensus are due to concerns of game balance. Mages should not care about God or Gaia, unless they are involved in their paradigms. By having to establish that God manifested the curse of vampirism or that reality is governed by the Triat, you necessarily soften the game's core theme. The battle for reality is not just about rendering one's preferred type of magic Coincidental in a given area. It is about standing for what you hold. Mechanically defining the influence of the Consensus can dilute what the game is ultimately about; that is, will-workers of vastly different stripes trying to spread their worldviews and attain Ascension.

                                                        This is what I meant by Subjective Reality has to be complicated. And I mean it's complicated in the Facebook Relationship sense.

                                                        There has to be a way that the Traditions can think that they are all doing similar things while preferring their specific paradigms. There needs to be a way for the Technocracy to think that it can alter reality while still thinking it is operating off of consistent (or at least semi consistent) principles.

                                                        That sounds difficult, but it is quite common in our world. There are competing views about physics and metaphysics and theology all the time. Sometimes it is obvious to reasonable onlookers which side is right, other times there are compelling arguments on both sides and it takes years to work through who is right. That's because reality is complicated and it is difficult to take it all in at once. A complicated Subjective Reality would provide enough justification for the competing points of view we see.

                                                        That doesn't mean it needs to be complicated for the players. We don't have have to have in depth explanations of the Traditions metaphysical arguments or comprehensive charts of the data the Technocracy has gathered. We can simplify it to a short and quick explanation with and cover the rest with an "It's complicated" disclaimer.
