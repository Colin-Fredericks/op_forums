    Mayo vs Mustard

    06-21-2017, 12:16 PM
    I bought all the Mage Ascension books back in my much younger days, but when Awakening came along I switched over to it because the inherent core of that line appealed more to me (exploring mysteries vs fighting a pointless fight for reality). I never hated Ascension, just thought it was firmly based in its time. I have recently started reading M20 and its associate books, partly out of nostalgia, but also out of curiosity as to how the game line might have evolved to better reflect the current zeitgeist.

    ​While reading the more balanced take on the main factions, it struck me that (in my mind) the difference between mayo lovers vs mustard lovers is kind of a good analogy for technocrats vs mystics. Mayo is bland and homogenous and appeals to a large group of people because of its utility and ability to add moisture/binding to what ever it is added to. Mustard tends to be have a large variety (spicy, yellow, brown, Dijon, honey, wholegrain, powder, ect.) and literally adds spice to whatever it is added to. Even when mayo tries to be more than it is meant to be (I'm looking at you chipotle mayo) it just trying to be more mustard like. Mustard usually fails if used as a binding/moisture agent because it overpowers when used that liberally.

    ​Kind of sounds like technocrats vs mystics to me. Here's the thing, I am firmly a mustard lover, but mayo is too useful not to use in cooking. Ever tried adding moisture to a tuna salad with just mustard - not fun, so I use a small amount of mayo. I know plenty of mayo lovers that add a little mustard to their sandwiches along with their mayo for the spice.

    ​I like that some of the emphasis in M20 has shifted away from the use vs them of technocrats vs mystics and more towards holy crud the Nephandi have body snatched our leaders and that's how we got here because at the end of the day I will never put mayo on one of my sandwiches, but I don't have a problem if you want to.

    ​Thanks for letting me ramble/muse...

                      Originally posted by PMárk View Post

                      That's an interesting thought. How'd you define the current zeitgeist and how Mage is out of sync with it? I'm asking this, because I came late to MtA (during the late 2000s) and stayed since and I never felt it's core out-of-time (specific minor elements, yes, but not he whole and not the important themes), but that could be just me and my worldview.
                      ​As I said in the previous post, there was a definite feel of impending doom/apocalypse to the original line, which was completely understandable with the upcoming turn of the millennium, and even later in 2012 with the end of the mayan calendar. With things like the Lance Henricksen show Millennium, Rapture themed works and other media feeding/tapping into the same zeitgeist. It seems so silly now, but there were serious concerns about the Y2K bug wiping out computer systems as we know it, as well as other general hysteria. If there was any doubt about these themes White Wolf finished off each line with their end of the world coda book.

                      ​We still have apocalyptic memes in media and our society, but they seem to have shifted from the world will end to society collapsing/stagnating.Things like the housing crisis and the failure of big banks have shifted a lot of peoples concerns to a much more personal level. It's hard to worry about the end of the world when you are worried about the next mortgage payment. Yet, with all that there are so many things that feel like progress (even if they are heavily resisted in various arenas) that don't seem tied to the rampant consumerism of the 90s. Forms of Democracy have gotten a second wind (and the hit again) with things like the Arab Spring, gender and sexuality equality are much better than when I was a kid (although they still have a long way to go), Captain America is black and Thor is a woman. Many YouTube stars are banal glory seekers, but so many others are able to artistically flourish in a way never before because they control the platform and their message. It is a very paradoxical time (no pun intended).

                      ​One of the reasons that I shifted to Awakening (which to be fair has far less weighty philosophical underpinnings than Ascension) was a desire to move away from the nihilism inherent in the original game line. What I have read so far of M20 seems to have shifted away from that, which I am thankful for.
