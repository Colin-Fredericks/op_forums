    If you want to play Awakening, play Awakening, it's a fun game. It is not, however, Ascension, and I already pointed out why you can't directly translate what Awakening does to what Ascension does. But importantly for this? The WoD and CofD handle significant flaws very differently. In Ascension you have to take the Blind Flaw, which gives you points up front, specific mechanics, and is part of your Pattern (so removing it with Life is a lot more involved than healing damage). In Awakening you take a Persistent Blinded Condition. That only gives you Beats if it actually impairs your character (which means it isn't if you use magic to overcome it all the time), and the ST can simply remove a Condition from your sheet if it never actually comes up. Again, very different design mentalities between the games.

    And as for why beyond that? Because that's not what the rules actually say. If Ascension wanted it that way, it would say that's what it does.

    If you want to house rule it? Great! But don't conflate that with what the books say so we can actually have fruitful discussions that answer questions people pose.

                    That's not how the rules are constructed. They need to say a Sphere can do something for it to do something. Forces says you can enhance your perception to see a type of Forces Pattern within the framework of your functional senses and Focus. Forces 1 lets you perceive sounds around you with superhuman efficiency, but it doesn't mean you know how to echolocate (something you could train yourself to do without magic) or magically give you a sonar-sense. Matter 1 lets you know things about objects basic material properties, so you can know you're touching wood, or stone, or glass, but it doesn't mean you get a mental image of the total object's size, function, and so on.

                    The Perfect Moment section of HDYDT? provides examples of most of the Spheres getting to do The Perfect Moment with just their level 1. So that's not a Time specific thing (even Correspondence 1 gets a The Perfect Moment example). It's literally an example of how Time 1 really isn't that special. The Perfect Moment is also just one of dozens of ways to flavor Magic Enhancing Abilities. If you bought up Echolocation as a Secondary Skill Forces 1 doesn't need The Perfect Moment to reduce the difficulty of your echolocation rolls. The magical ability to directly sense sonic Patterns is plenty of justification for that. The Perfect Moment section actually supports something I've been getting at here: Time 1 The Perfect Moment effects are only good for simple actions that are intuitive and don't require significant understanding of the situation or subjects to get right. It goes on with all of the other Sphere examples to illustrate how more complex situations need other Spheres to get The Perfect Moment instead because the knowledge of those systems is more important.

                    Just like The Perfect Moment, where you're supposed to use other Spheres for more detailed situation when Time alone doesn't impart enough, Correspondence 1 only tells you so much rather than blanket super-senses.

                        Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                        That skips the second paragraph saying, "Combined with various Pattern Spheres, the mage can determine exact sizes and distances to creatures, objects or forces." Yes it doesn't use the words "outlines" or "shapes," but if you need Pattern spheres to judge exact sizes, then the level of detail of Correspondence alone you're attributing doesn't make sense. And "outline" is an exact size, even if the details within the outline aren't perceived. The presence of that clause puts the inference on "outlines and shapes" under the Pattern Sphere add-ons, because it includes size, as dimensional measurement, not more specific measurements associated with other Spheres.
                        Since I addressed that specific clause, it clearly doesn't skip it. Like I said, outlines could be a function of the second paragraphs distance and size, but they also could be a function of the first paragraphs "exact directions and distance" and "contours of space." Indeed, it is difficult to see how those couldn't be used to make an outline map of the surrounding area.

                        For the record, if I thought the two paragraphs actually contradicted I would suspect the problem was in the second, not the first. Not only is the first more consistent with Landscape of the Mind, but Mage does have a tendency for sphere bloat so I err on the side of simplicity. But here's an illustration of what I think is actually happening:

                        Let's say Johnny Depp was hiding amongst the animatronics of the Pirates of the Caribbean ride (I actually don't know if that ride uses forced perspective, so for the sake of the example lets assume they are life size).

                        Correspondence 1 can build a map of the area. It can show the humanoid figures, tell the difference between them and the rocks, and see them move. It can do this all whether the lights are on or off and regardless of which direction the mage is looking.

                        What it cannot do is easily show the difference between the animatronics and Johnny Depp. That requires either additional spheres or context clues. With Mind, the mage could figure out the exact distance and location of the one that has a mind, with Life, he could figure out the exact distance to Depp, with Matter, he could figure out the distance of the animatronics. Without those, he's stuck using context clues. Someone sneaking up behind him probably isn't an animatronic, but even then he wouldn't know if that someone was Depp or someone else.

                        And if Ian Malcolm was wrong and when Pirates of the Caribbean breaks down the pirates really do try to eat the tourists, the mage would only know is that a humanoid was approaching him, not what it was.

                                Though it's very much off the original subject, being able to sense one sort of pattern or another is highly useful, but that usefulness depends to a certain extent on the user having enough knowledge to understand the meaning of what they're seeing. Knowing the exact material composition of the substance in a vial, for instance, doesn't reveal the fact that it's deadly to living things without some knowledge of applied chemistry or toxicology. This is why every Etherite character I've ever had in a game started with different physical science skills, and why Verbena without any practical and useful understanding of living things (represented on paper as skills) get funny looks from their peers.

                                That said, pairing basic pattern sensing with that sort of knowledge can be hellishly effective. Life 1 can give a mage diagnostic tools which mundane paramedics and emergency room physicians would sell their souls for, when said mage actually knows what to do about what they're perceiving. In a similar sense, I've had more than one NPC discover that something an Etherite hurled at them was not actually magickal, and therefore not subject to their usual defenses against magick, even though they used Matter to very quickly locate the components they used to throw it together.

                                            I fully acknowledge that I'm not expressing a literal reading of the text, and I'm playing it somewhat fast and loose: I'm not even going to attempt to support my suggestions with textual references, because my goal isn't to present what the book explicitly says. Rather, my whole purpose here is to point out that the premise behind Mage's Magick system is that it can do literally anything, even things the original writers never thought of. I point you to the notion of Primal Ventures, something that got added to the game (first through Primal Utility in Convention Book Syndicate and then later incorporated directly into Prime in ​The Rich Bastard's Guide to Magick) literally a decade after the gameline's publication had originally ceased. Prime deals with Quintessence; but the notion that organized human activity could be a source of Quintessence is a very recent development.

                                            In the same way, I'm proposing that we shouldn't be restricting ourselves to what the text says a Sphere can do. If we come up with a concept that the original material never addressed, we should look for a best fit. In this case, the concept that seems to have been overlooked is Size. In particular, I find myself thinking of movies such as Fantastic Voyage and The Incredible Shrinking Man, as well as comic book characters like DC's Atom and Marvel's Ant-Man/Giant Man. Growth and Shrinking. And microscopic vision is part of the "sense" stage of size manipulation. To me, this feels very much like a "manipulate dimensions" thing, where by "dimension" I'm referring to the likes of length, width, and height rather than "other Realms", the latter of which is what Dimensional Science means by the term.

                                            And while M20 didn't follow up on it, Convention Book: Void Engineers included not just Dimensional Science but also a different variation on Correspondence than the Data one presented in Convention Book: NWO: Void Correspondence, which dealt explicitly with distance. Correspondence isn't just about connections; it's also about geometry. And scaling up and down is very much a geometry thing. So Correspondence for changes of scale.

                                            With all that said, I'm not going to try to tell you how to make your case, or even what case to make. You want to explore what precisely the published materials do and do not explicitly support? That's your prerogative, and there's nothing wrong with that. But by the same token, don't try to tell me what sorts of arguments I'm supposed to make. Let's present a many different viewpoints here as we can, and let the OP decide for themself which, if any, they find valuable.
