    Learning, Mentors, Libraries etc.

    11-09-2020, 08:18 AM
    Hi everyone,

    I'm looking for some ideas how to handle learning new things or improving during downtime.
    How do you decide how much time it takes to find a teacher, if the PC doesn't have the Mentor background?
    Can such a teacher power learning time as a Mentor would? If so, what advantages does a Mentor give then?
    How long does it take to learn a new Sphere or increase it? What's about Abilities and Attributes?

    Thank you for reading!

      Finding a teacher question is a bit too variable to answer for a PC without a Mentor background. You'd want to figure out how rare the teacher who can teach that subject at the desired level is.Destiny or Status or other Backgrounds that aid in navigating Mage society should help find the teacher.

      For sphere learning time, the Bitter Road had a chart on page 73. The chart is set for a Library or a Mentor, so if the teacher doesn't count as either a Library provider or a Mentor, the time would be the default time per RAW. Mentors speed up learning time and help PCs. If using the sphere learning chart, then the difficulty would be [sphere level learning+3] rolled against the Mentor rating. Successes after the first would reduce the time by that factor--thus 2 successes would halve the time and three would make it one-third.

      There's not a similar chart that I am aware of for Abilities and Attributes. However, keep in mind that when you start getting into 3-dot Abilities you are in the professional range and few people are that good. 4 dots and 5 dots should be very uncommon. The closer a PC was to those levels, the more plausible it is for them to pick up the next dots. Someone with nothing in an Ability should not get up to professional level in a month. A PC at three dots who's experienced and whose concept makes that Ability important might just get 4 dots shortly into play as you reflect new learn.g For attributes, the 4-dot and 5-dot range are quite unusual. Thus, the time to increase an Ability or Attribute is up to you.

      I use GURPS and its learning times to benchmark my Abilities and Attributes learning. Basically, keep doing something and you'll learn a little about it each day. Put in enough time and the XP in Mage, and you get better.

      Or, check out Ars Magica, which has very detailed rules for downtime research.

          1, 4

          Difficulty Modifier: Whenever a Sphere effect has significant impact on the setting AND changes the caster's Resonance, storyteller records successes scored. All 1s, including on successful rolls, are added to a Quiet Bank. Divide the success total by 10 or more to get a number of Aptitude Points. Actual experience using the Sphere where there is resistance (Earth Consensus or realm with no bonus to that Sphere's use) accumulates a bank of Aptitude Points that can be spent to reduce Library/Mentor roll. Perception Effects do little to change the world, while risky higher level effects are more rewarding. When the player spends Aptitude Points, they pick up that number of temporary Quiet until the Quiet Bank is depleted.

          I think this makes the game more balanced and advancement time more realistic. The carefree Orphan actually gets something for his hard learned lessons in bending the Consensus. The well connected Hermetic can take his time, probably raising Life to ensure he has the longevity needed.

              The base GURPS assumption is that 200 hours of directed study are worth 1 character point. That's enough to buy a mental skill at the lowest level or learn a simple combat move. The GURPS Basic Set covers this, though GURPS Social Engineering: Back to School goes into far more detail than the Basic Set if you want it.

              It's less important in Mage to have one specific system for how characters learn than figuring out one that works for your group. Since being a Master mage is supposed to take decades and being an Adept isn't that quick, either, your times to learn those levels are best reflected as much longer than the lower sphere levels. If you use the Library rule from Bitter Road, there's definitely a reason to get good libraries and mentors. Shaving off time to study is a big deal. . .

                                  Backgrounds typically are earned and raised trough play and the narrative. Getting a "teacher", with mechanical benefits, should be represented by giving points on the Mentor background.

                                  This doesn't need to cost xp. Traditionally it's given free - you paid for it by interpretating the whole process as part of the narrartive (and minmax getting angry would be a bonus because WoD hates them). Getting someone like that to teach you personally should be quite a feat on part of the player - I think not having to achieve *that* trough playing it's the big advatage of buying it at char gen.

                                      For Forces 1, that's something easy to learn compared to a lot of other spheres.The Chorister wanting to learn Forces 1 will, assuming you are using the Bitter Road chart, need about a month to learn with some help from a teacher. This isn't a month of all-day study, but it is a month of regular learning and practice.

                                      So, if the potential teacher is going to teach the character, they need a compatible magical style--perhaps Paradigm and certainly Practice and Instruments (because there may not be the big shared belief but you've got to have a way to make it work). Alternatively, the character can learn some new Instruments pretty easily.

                                      The trainer will want some favors or other service for their time and expertise. If a member of the Celestial Chorus, it would likely be less costly than for a member of a faction that doesn't like the Celestial Chorus. This would be some worthwhile favors--helping Sleepers or gathering information or even trading some other information. The Celestial Chorus character could provide these favors directly or find others to do them--NPCs would likely want something to help the character unless they are friends. PCs can help as they wish.

                                      Tass is a good form of payment as well.
