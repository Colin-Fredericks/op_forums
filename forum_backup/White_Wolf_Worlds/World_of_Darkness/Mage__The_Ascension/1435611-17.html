                                                        Originally posted by Erinys View Post
                                                        You all have some good points, folks.

                                                        The notion of science-as-superior-technology is very much the mainstream notion that supports the notiion of white culture as superior. In real life, I am all in favor of trying to view the world objectively, and yet real science (in the real world) is agnostic on the existence of God and such. The core of real science isn't dogmatic atheism or racial superiority, it's objectively accepting the world as we observe it, preconceived notions be damned. Real science requires the humility to always say "I could be wrong."

                                                        In the WOD? Real science would look at the supernatural elements of the world and acknowledges they exist and are significant, including the entire spirit world and the obvious efficacy of Mystick magick. It can't be anything but delusion to call supernatural elements "reality deviants", and to bend over backwards inventing a complex, anti-Occam's-razor delusion like Tychoidean cosmology to explain away the obvious reality of the spirit world. The Technocratic paradigm only works on a basis of anti-science censorship. And in the real world, idiocy like Euro-American fire-suppression also isn't remotely scientific, it's just an exercise in blindly declaring that white ways are always superior to Native ways, and ignoring the constant avalanche of evidence disproving that BS.

                                                        Disclaimer, I don't own any Mage corebooks. But to see how many people read the Mage message as "science is evil" suggest to me that the messaging hasn't been effective in conveying how utterly deluded and non-scientific the Technocray really is. They ought to be depicted as non-mainstream scientists, who manipulate sleeper scientists and silence the ones who discover or invent anything Control wants to destroy. The ones who erase the history of non-European scientific practices and kill off everyone who practices or remembers them, so they can continue lying about white people "inventing" science and suppress the scientific knowledge of other cultures. Their thing is mechanical technology, not the scientific method.
                                                        It's more complicated than that. First off, of all the factions within the entire World of Darkness, the Technocracy has received the most change and evolution across the editions. Seriously, early 1E Technocracy is utterly alien compared to the Technocracy of M20.

                                                        Like in the second MtAs book, the adventure module Loom of Fate, sees the Technocrats attempting to replace a dying Weaver Spirit by turning a young Orphan mage (a Mage with no alignment to any Tradition, Convention or Craft) into a new Weaver Spirit. All because early MtAs tried to have loose connections to WtA and the Triad with the Technocracy being connected to the Weaver. M20 Technocrats would look at those Technocrats as at best lunatics, at worst Nephandi or Marauders pretending to be Technocrats.

                                                        But because of that evolving view of them, depending when you came into Mage and what books you read, will HEAVILY influence how you view them. For example I came into MtAs with the Rev Ed which had taken a middle of the road stance on the Union. They were shady and certainly villains to the Traditions but at the same time the Rev Ed in the beginning was depressed and apathetic, with the Ascension War begin waged between the two factions fizzling out, both sides loosing as the Masses chose instead to wallow in muck than reach for something higher regardless of what either side were selling.

                                                        Basically I saw them as bad guys but not necessarily the worst of the bad guys.

                                                        Then I got to 2E's Guide to the Technocracy and saw just how nuanced and complicated the Union was. The Technocracy for me moved from being antagonists to being alternate protagonists of MtAs. Certainly they were more morally questionable in their actions than the Traditions but it gave the whole line a new layer of depth to not just them but the whole line. They were trying to protect the Masses from the various dangerous supernatural things in the WoD while trying to advance Humanity and down the path of Enlightenment. Their path admittedly but it was still more than the Traditions seemed to be doing, as all they seemed to be doing is waging war on the Technocracy. Though likewise, with the Traditions that's much more complicated than it seems at first glance. This was just my more simplistic view of things as I first dived into MtAs.

                                                        Plus, the Introduction Chapter of Guide had a really good opening.

                                                        It was also around that time that I started coming into contact with the larger WoD fandom, mostly at local stores and players but also some online interactions. That's when I first discovered that there were people that strongly hated the Technocracy. And while I could understand some of it, I couldn't completely understand it.

                                                        Then I read the 1E Convention Books and understood where those people were coming from...

                                                        Because, yeah the early books treated the Technocracy as one-note villains and at times, really the over the top of their villainy.

                                                        The Technocracy of M20 is in line with the trend of Guide to the Technocracy but with the Union realizing two things. First, the previous generations of Technocrats were... well... *Points to Victorian Mage* and trying to get away from that. The Euro-centric nature of the Union is giving way to a more Global Union. Some of the more recent books have straight up have Technocrats criticize the actions of their predecessors or talk about how their demographics are changing with the world.

                                                        The second is much more alarming for them, that for all their plans and Time Tables and attempts to elevate Humanity, they have forgotten to factor into an important thing, the Masses themselves. The Sleepers like the toys the Technocracy provides them but they don't want to actually reach out for Enlightenment. Indeed, in many ways the Sleepers are rejecting Logic and Reason and Science as much as they are Magic and Spirituality.

                                                        Case in point, the Progenitors HATE Faith Healers, the Anti-vaxx Movement and the like. So much so that despite the Union ending their Pogroms against Supernaturalist Mages and wanting to get away from those methods, some Progenitors want to restart it in hopes of ending that nonsense and all the harm it's doing because some of those Mages have helped spread those ideas because they either line up with their Beliefs or to weaken the Beliefs the Technocracy is trying to impose.

                                                        Would somebody please summarize for me the Zulu, Chinese, and Japanese technomagic factions in VAM? I would like to know some about them.
                                                        Chinese Dalou’laoshi (or Five Elemental Dragons) were a group that already existed, originally as a group of Asian Technocrats, part of the Union but different. You know, back in that Year of Lotus stuff. Certainly they weren't the worst but they were kind of not great either. The modern take on them now is as a specifically Chinese group of Technomancers that would go on to join the Order of Reason/Technocracy.

                                                        In VAM, the Dalou’laoshi are still independent Technomancers though they have been in a loose partnership with the Wulong, the Chinese Craft that has long dominated the Courts of Imperial China. However it is not a happy partnership for the Dalou’laoshi as while the Wulong are happy to use their works and inventions, they have long been hindering the Dalou’laoshi's attempts to increase their influence within China as the Wulong don't want anyone to challenge their stranglehold on power within China or the social order that supports them.

                                                        But with the Europeans entering Eastern Asia, bringing with them new ideas and inventions, the Qing Dynasty crumbling and all the rising chaos being generated by all that, the Dalou’laoshi find themselves at a crossroads. In earlier years, the Order of Reason had approached them with joining but the Dalou’laoshi rebuffed them as like the Wulong, they were very China-centric and kind of looked down on those outside of China.

                                                        There's now three rising factions within the Dalou’laoshi, centered around what they're going to do in response to this all. The first group thinks that joining the Order is the best option for them, that doing so will not just ensure their protection but give them a seat at the table when it comes to deciding the future of China. Second wants to join with the Wulong and Akashayana in fighting the Order. The third and likely smallest of these groups are those that seek to utterly reform China, to modernize it technologically and socially, pushing out the Order, the Europeans and the old Imperialists. In fact, few even dream of creating a pan-Asian alliance of magi against the Europeans.

                                                        Historically, the first group wins out after the Boxer Rebellion that second group backed failed and the Dalou’laoshi becomes a part of the Order/Union.

                                                        The Jidai (or Next Era) of Japan are a new group for MtAs (at least as far as I know) so there isn't much on them. They were Japanese Technomancers that came together during Oda Nobunaga's reunification of Japan and likely bolstered by the country's trade with Europe during that time but went into decline during the period of isolation that followed.

                                                        When Admiral Perry's Expedition shattered that isolation, the Jidai saw as their chance to rise. While Japanese magi panicked and saw their Magicks being threatened by the changing Consensus, the Jidai thrived as never before. They embraced modernizing Japan, believing it to be the only way to maintain Japanese independence.

                                                        But it's also likely that they will help bring about the expansionist Empire of Japan in the coming decades.

                                                        Currently they are independent of the Order but thanks to the importing of Western experts while sending Japanese observers to the West to aid in the modernization of Japan, the two groups have come into contact with each other. At the moment, both groups are looking to use the other for their own advantage.

Basically some Zulu Magi saw what the Boer and their technology was capable of against their warriors and were horrified by it. In response, some of these magi abandoned their previous ways and are embracing Technomancy to compete against the Europeans. That said, they are still holding to some of their older practices. So they are probably the most mystical of the Technomancers of this Age.
