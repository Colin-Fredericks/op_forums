            It's “liars or dupes”, and is my shorthand for “every Technocrats either knows that they're altering reality and is lying about it (the liars), or they think that they aren't because their superiors are lying to them (the dupes)”. Saying that the whole thing is just propaganda but they seem to have bought into their own propaganda is very much a “liars or dupes” argument. To be clear, my main objection to the “liars or dupes” line of argument has nothing to do with how believable it may or may not be; it's entirely about the viability of the Union as protagonists in the game. To my mind, that's hard to do if they're either fundamentally mistaken about the nature of reality or are lying about it.

            That's why my own preference is to say that they have a valid point. They're not deceiving or being deceived as to the underlying nature of reality; they just see it differently than the Traditions do. And their view as to the fundamentals of reality holds up about as well as the Traditions' collective view does — which is to say that it's not perfect, but it addresses some issues that the opposing view doesn't. In particular, overly simplistic versions of both views exist, and agents and mages on both sides who buy into these overly simplistic models are wrong, while agents and mages on both sides who push these overly simplistic models are being deceptive.

            It's still true that Conditioning exists — though that's also something I tend to downplay, as I don't like the NWO dominating three other Conventions by brainwashing all of their agents. Frankly, I don't see why Iteration X, the Progenitors, or the Syndicate would stand for it; and every bit as much as I dislike the “liars or dupes” interpretation of their view of the fundamentals, I also dislike portraying the Union as “the NWO and its lackeys” — which is what you get if you put the NWO in charge of brainwashing everyone. In my games, the Void Engineers' “counter-Conditioning” isn't as unique as it seems: every Convention (including the NWO) has similar programs in place to ensure that the NWO doesn't go too far, and/or has arranged to police its own, with what the “Conditioning” section outlines being a standard that they pay lip service to (and sometimes even enforce) rather than being an iron-clad fact.

            And finally: while it doesn't excuse Conditioning by any means, the notion that there's some truth what they're indoctrinating does take an edge off of it. Let's take something that most of the readers here are likely to agree as being true: Climate Change. The Technocracy indoctrinates people (even their own) to believe in it. That's bad, because you're taking away peoples' free will — and even the Union understands that there's a time and place for it. But convincing people that it's true without some sort of Conditioning takes time, time that the Union doesn't always have. So they're willing to make some concessions on free will in the interest of pragmatism. But it doesn't make Climate Change any less true.

            More generally, that's my view on the Technocrats' basic doctrines: they do have a point (there are at least elements of reality that are objective, and building on them is better than ignoring then); but they don't always go about making their point in a good way. Just like the Traditions.

                                    I always believed the difference between Magick and Enlightened Science was relatively easy to thematically distinguish.
                                    From what I always interpreted, Technocratic Operatives basically saw their Enlightened Science as basically loopholes, backdoors, short cuts, and secret manipulations of both the laws of physics and reality.
                                    Essentially they were able to use thought to produce a reaction through incredibly complex and abstract scientific processes.
                                    Like with hurling lightning with their bare hands, ignoring how out of character it would be, of course. A Technomancer would see it as their Enlightened Mind is able to create certain reactions in their brains and bodies that basically cause a specific chain reaction with their hyperscientic theories, essentially using their own minds to create their Procedures.
                                    Like how you are always producing electromagnetic waves that change with your thoughts because your brain is moving bio-electricity. This incredibly subtle reaction then causes an increasingly powerful chain that builds up thanks to these "occult physics" and eventually that charge has built enough energy through Primal Energy that functions as the smallest building block of reality. They then hurl the lightning their altered brainwaves was able to, basically, calculate into existence.
                                    Even if an Operative isn't super smart, they have been taught how to actively trigger these reactions, and as such utilize these Procedures.
                                    When they are weaker they need tools and inventions to help activate those abstract laws of physics because the weight of Consensus prevents them from intuiting it all through their Genius. But at higher levels their Genius is able to more directly impact the Consensus by sheer power of its hyperscience.
                                    Think of it like how a shaman will use a certain herb to help heal sickness. They claim its a gift from the god created by his falling tears when he watched his mortal lover perish from sickness. Granting his lover's people a means to prevent what happened to them from happening again.
                                    The Technomancer would say that the herb simply has properties that trigger certain chemicals in the body that help increase immune system response, as well as stimulate the body's natural healing. No mumbo jumbo required, and if you know these properties you can use it more efficiently and for multiple things.
                                    I always assumed that was the differences between them.

                                            I'll have to disagree with you here. All of the Conventions acknowledge the Masses; but messing with the Consensus is primarily an NWO thing in much the same way that messing with Tychoidean Cosmology is a Void Engineer thing, or messing with Hypereconomics is a Syndicate thing. Each Convention has its own specialty; and while each acknowledges that the other specialties exist, they generally leave questions about them up to the experts in those fields.

                                            And this compartmentalized nature of the Technocratic “paradigm” means that the aforementioned acknowledgement of the other specialties is often no more than lip service. In practice, there isn't a unified Technocratic paradigm, but rather five interlocking paradigms. The idea that reality is shaped by the beliefs of the Masses is not something that any Convention other than the NWO truly believes beyond “the NWO says it's so, and that's their area of expertise; so I guess I'll roll with it”.

                                            And even among the NWO, the idea that physical reality is shaped by the minds of the Masses is something of an outlier belief: they generally leave questions about how and why their gadgets and devices work to the Conventions that specialize in such matters, such as Iteration X. And Iteration X does not generally believe that machines work because the Masses believe in them.
