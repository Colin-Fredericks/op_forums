              In general, most Traditions and conventions have a formal ranking of, of course, mundane < Sorceror < True Mage. Even if the lines blur and depend on Tradition and Convention, from a sheer standpoint on the things they can achieve at the top tier, Sorcerors are just at an disadvantage there.
              That is not to say though, that The biggest Sorcerors are lower than the lowest mage disciple - as I said, the lines blur, and I bet there are several Traditions where a very prominent and able Sorceror can at least get good respect and position - if not past a certain point because of sheer ability True Magick gives you.

              The Hermetics, and the NWO are probably the groups where a Sorceror/Extraordinary Citizen has probably the least ways of becoming an equal to any Mage/Enlightened Scientist.

              On the other hand, the one singular Tradition/Convention that puts Sorcerors on literally equal terms...are the Void Engineers. They don't care if you are an enligtened mind, or are using psionics, or whatever. if you pull your weight, if you fight the good fight, and make a mission work.. you deserve as much respect in their eyes as the biggest of Enlightened Scientists. This goes for Sorcerors/Extraordinary Citizen as much as even a mere mundane, non-enlightened, non-extraordinary VE being a goddamn badass hero.

              And you know, that's kind of cool in my eyes. The VE's always have been, in a big ironic twist, the most down to earth of all groups in many aspects.

                                                      Originally posted by mark View Post
                                                      do we know of any canonical cases where offices in the nine traditions are held by sorcerers? i mean the "federal" bureaucracy not within a tradition itsself
                                                      How far down the ladder are you willing to go? There's no canon cases of them holding positions of note, but they are indicated as holding plenty of the functionary offices in places like Horizon. This is generally done to justify the size of the Tradition infrastructure when mages as supposed to be rare.

                                                      It's also worth noting that canonically, the idea of mages and sorcerers actually being separate things in the eyes of mages is relatively recent. Until the evolution of magic between the end of the Dark Ages and the start of the Sorcerers Crusade era, the lines between "linear" and "dynamic" magic where considerably more blurred. Years of training in what mages in the modern era call linear magic used to actually lead to becoming a full mage for those capable of it. As the Traditions formed, many of them continued to instruct potential recruits the same way as they always did, but Awakenings became more 'random' and individualistic instead of the result of putting recruits through magical training until their full potential revealed itself.

                                                          Setting aside the complete lack of any support for linear magic or it's users in M20, the question would also amount to "Who is doing the testing to determine such things and what are they testing for?"

                                                          If you get points for versatility, the dynamic mages are going to get the props, but if the test is about being able to demonstrate specific power benchmarks, then linear sorcerers might do just as well, depending on their specialties (and if they are "on the board" to determine what gets tested, they very well might skew things towards tests that put them on a more even playing field).

                                                          For example, if the Hermetic so many Spheres at Level X, so many at Level Y, actually works out to some sort of demonstration of those levels of power, then an appropriately trained sorcerer might be able to demonstrate appropriately powerful acts of magic to meet the requirements. Mages could keep out the linear mage riff-raff by having the appropriate testing require demonstrating high levels of versatility for each benchmark (not just "manifest X amount of flame" but "manifest X amount of flame...okay, now lightning.... okay now fly...."
