    HIT Mark vs. a werewolf (firearms question?)

    02-22-2020, 10:11 AM
    One thing I remember about the Storytellers guide to the technocracy is that there was a little blurb about playing a HIT Mark. It was basically not advised because they were perceived as too tough, and there was a joke about having a place to hang a bunch of Get of Fenris pelts.

    Were they really that tough? I can only imagine that they had a pretty good soak (9 dice), and a Chain-gun which, I assume, will be able to tear through werewolves.

    How would that work, would we assume that the bullets are silver or just made of Primium (which, in an earlier book, I am pretty sure was aggravated, but you could soak). The stats for a Chain-gun are Difficulty 7, Rate 3, Can fire full auto bursts and 3 round bursts, Damage 8. Looking at the dice pool for a HIT Mark in 20th edition it looks like their Dex+Firearms would be about 5. If a HIT Mark was shooting at a werewolf, what would be most efficient? One shots, 3 round bursts, or Full Auto?

    Futhermore, how does rate and multiple actions work for Firearms? I tried finding the rules but the M20 Book is 600 pages and I am getting lost trying to find it. I imagine that maybe firing 3, 3 round bursts would deal the most damage but what is the actual rule for firing multiple rounds at the same target?

                  Plus, to stop guns from firing you would still be looking at difficulty 8.

                  Plus the fifty foot range limitation. There's a good chance they're dead before reaching 50 feet of the Hitmark. Not 100% but at least 50/50.
                  Firstly, shutting the gun down is an unnecessarily precise approach for Jam Technology. You'd just have to shut down the computer processors that allow the cyborg to function as, well, a cyborg; as these are presumably some really advanced integrated circuits, that's difficulty 4, and has the benefit of bringing said cyborg to a grinding halt, possibly even ruining it for good as life support systems for the organic parts fail to function.

                  Secondly, the range limitation isn't much of a limitation, because most places where a combat cyborg would freely operate aren't going to be wide-open, and those that are generally have different issues for said cyborg, such as a sudden sinkhole opening up, surprise quicksand, flash flooding, and other terrain complications.

                  Thirdly, the specialized Gifts that specifically target the chaingun are typically used at a lower difficulty, and might have a longer range. Jam Weapon a first rank CoG favorite, works out to vocal range and has a difficulty based on the Willpower of the weapon's user. Jam Gun from Werewolf: the Wyld West is also less burdened. Gremlins is more burdened, but as a benefit, anything it works against is ruined for good.

                  As for the issue of "artifacts", you can't have it both ways, by declaring that technology is magic in setting, but then claim that it's magic but not subject to being grouped as technology by spirits. Too bad ItX skimps on that area, else the quick circumventing of their tech might not work.
