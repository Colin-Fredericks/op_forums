          Most of these things seldom become an issue, because the PCs rarely amass the personal power needed to field armies of dragons. In such a case it would allways be the question how you would handle dragons under normal circumstances. I would make them massive Thaumivores, requiring them to consume vast amounts of Quintessence to stay in concensual reality, in whatever form is appropriate to the specific dragon (lava, virgins, gold, etc.). They would also fizzle rather fast once they confronted large groups of humans. So burning down villages may work, but attacking Manhatten on the back of your awesome dragon may be a bit of a bad idea. This would probably show in the form of the dragon sicken and dying quickly in an allergic reaction to some big city toxin.

          Building a flying car or a lightsaber is vulgar and will incur the associated cost in paradox each time it's used. It'd again only fail with a large audience.

          As for something vulgar that is lasting (say your mage would like to turn every bullet shot at him into a flower and does a long ritual with many successes to accomplish this for a long time) : I would personally assert paradox each scene the affect becomes active (thus none at creation, since the field itself is invisible and coincidental as long as it's not active, though the mage would have to roll for vulgar with witness difficulty if he wanted the shield to be active in front of witnesses - even if he was in a sanctum). For effects that are blatantly vulgar the effect itself may be eroded aswell if witnesses. So a mage causing his skin to emit a strong light for the next year would (besides getting ridiculous amounts of paradox outside the umbra/sanctum) find his spell fading if he stayed around sleepers. If he managed to hold of paradox somehow from taking care of it with backlashes. Again it kinda depends on the amount of sleepers witnessing here.

          Alternativly it wouldn't be too far fetched to assign an amount of permadox for vulgar effects with long durations, especially those that are consistently on and thus vulgar.

              Aw. I actually posted my response on the other thread (I fail at forums, sorry). I hope you don't mind me putting it here:

              --
              [It] depends on the type of effect. If the effect is perpetual, than the rule that I'd use is the one for permanent pattern alterations like gills, certain cybernetic enhancements, or raising an attribute over 5: namely the mage would have a point (or more depending on the properties of the effect) of permanent paradox which would not be burned until the effect stops. I like this rule because it is something manageable, but it still encourages the mage to be cautious and avoid too much additional vulgar magic (as backlash, when not all the paradox can actually get burned off, can get pretty nasty.)

              If the effect must be activated to be used: like a wand of fireballs, then I would apply the same temporary paradox that I would if the mage was fast casting that same effect every time he uses it.

                          Don't forget paradox flaws. Those can be fun for the small niggling cases where a paradox spirit is a waste.                                       Aleph, You should first remember that this game IS about insurmountable power. With preparation a Mage can do anything. And yes, this includes blowing up in the face of the Consensus regularly.

                                      The game is about delivering consequences for your decisions, but never about stopping you from taking such decisions. If a player really can and want to attack Manhattan with a giant five-headed multicolored dragon and a plain-face villain mounted in a nightmare, that is exactly what the player will get.

                                      It isn't just the rules. It IS the spirit of the setting. Suspension of Disbelief applies here, don't think too much why the setting didn't blew up yet. It is because of internal logic, not real life logic.

                                      People at the World of Darkness aren't like normal people. They are more apathetic. They are less informed (and real life people aren't all that clever, either). And on top of that, in Ascension's case, they are regular target of magic(k). The Technocratic Union works with the same level of power to control damage, and they're not the only ones. One of the perfectly viable plot hooks of the Tradition's PCs is the mad Mage trying to do completely unreasonable shit. Like conjuring Tiamat and the Venger. And that's a problem precisely because it WILL work.

                                      With that said, I would stick to normal Paradox rules first for the casting. IF the casting didn't accrued Paradox (maybe a Sanctum), then normal Paradox could accrue when the magic is activated.

                                      But if the effect was continuous and without Paradox at the casting, I would just give Permadox. It is the most sensible response in my opinion. Being harsher than that in Paradox, like every scene, is tantamount to prohibiting the effect, what isn't fair in my book, but can already make a hard time for the Mage on the near future.

                                      For the use of Spirit magic, I would use spirits in general. The same way that Mind magic have consequences if you abuse, the Umbra have landscapes and denizens. Those critters are hardly an issue on most occasions, but frequent abuse can attract attention from the ones that CAN be an issue. They don't need to be Paradox spirits.

                                      You can also pervert the effects, attract strange attention, or send magic and magician together to other Realms, even Paradox ones. I find this better than just unraveling magic, I usually avoid to unravel it at first.

                                      So, in the end of the day, the dragon-rider will end up in a Paradox Realm full of bigger, nastier dragons, while the Union control damage making most people forget the incident or covering it with hyper-technology and clever use of Mind, Space and Time, and a cabal of Traditionalists just finish Venger in an alley.

                                                      One thing to remember is that by the standards of many other game settings cWOD is overpowered with it's supernatural abilities. The rules are fairly loose so that the ST (and players) can have ample freedom to develop their stories. This does not mean the rules/setting are bad or poorly designed only that responsibility on the ST/players part must be taken to not "break" the desired setting for the story.

                                                      This is the reason I personally never recommend WW games to younger beginning players.

                                                      At some point we have all power gamed. Doing so in the cWOD makes a mess real fast due to the most limiting aspect of curtailing what can or can't be done is the setting, not hard rules. If player's, and even ST's, want to abuse the system its not very hard to do, and if thats the style of game that is wanted there is nothing wrong with that. If however that is not the type of story being told and a player(s) wants to "rules lawyer" their way to outlandish circumstances expext the ST to "Golden Rule" the consequences.

                                                      I realize that many times people consider a game that needs house rules or the Golden Rule of GMing to work must be broken or is being ran by an incompetent GM. It should be remembered though that the game rules aren't written by lawyers. If they were then most loopholes and contradictions wouldn't be there to be exploited. The book would probably be 3 times as big though and we would all need law degrees to understand it.

                                                      I guess my point, if i still have one, is that its pretty easy to find ways in Mage to do some ridiculous things. There are also plenty of ways to resrict them though not always by hard, fast rules. If your player constantly feels the need to challenge these things then maybe Mage isn't the game for them.
