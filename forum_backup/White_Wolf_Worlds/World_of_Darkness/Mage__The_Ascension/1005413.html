    A Mage's Guide to the Galaxy

    11-19-2016, 02:31 PM
    Been reading over Book of Worlds and Infinite Tapestry and just wanted to try and get this all straight in my head.
    First of all, bellow is my understanding of the basic set up of Spiritual Cosmology

    Mundane Earth

    ==GAUNTLET==

    Penumbra
    -Astral Videre
    -Videre Spiritum (WtA penumbra)
    -Videre Mortus (WtO shadowlands)
    - The Dreaming (CtD "penumbra")

    Near Umbra
    -High Umbra
    -- Vulgate, Afterworlds, Spires, Courts, Epiphamies, etc.

    Middle Umbra
    -- all the various realms such as Flux, Scar, the Aetherial Realm, the Abyss, etc.
    -- many zones such as mirror zones, null zones, and vistas, the Digital Web, and the Maya (CtD).

    Low/Dark Umbra
    -- the Tempest, the Isle of Sorrow, the Far Shores, the Yellow Springs, the Labyrinth, etc

    Grand connecting realms
    -- The Pattern Web, Alder Bole, Mount Qaf

    ==FIRST HORIZON== (Earths Atmosphere)

    Ether Space/True Horizon

    - Mundane Planets: Sun (maybe), Mercury, Venus, Luna, Mars

    - Shard Realms (planet penumbra),
    --Venus's realm of dinosaurs and varied biomes, where the Seasonal Realm used to be.
    --Luna's Spiritual realm, Arcadia (assumed), where the Dark Side VE base used to be.
    -- Mars's ancient civilisations, where Doissetep used to be.

    - Shade Realms
    -- S.R. Corr, (City of Brass turned Escher paradise)
    -- S.R. Life, (Evolution gone insane), where the seasonal realm used to be.
    -- S.R. Prime, (Possibly the entirety of the Umbra, especially the Aetherial Realm seen in WtA)
    -- S.R. Forces, (Energy gone mad), where Doissetep used to be.

    - Various Horizon Realms
    -- Seasonal Realm, Victoria Station, Balador, Gernsback, Autocthonia, etc.
    -- The Deep Dreaming (CtA)

    ==THE FAR HORIZON== (Asteroid Belt)

    Deep Umbra
    ((The distinction between mundane and spiritual planets breaks down here, if you believe you are in the umbra, you're in the umbra, if you believe you're in mundane space, you're in mundane space.))
    - Shard Realm
    -- Jupiter and it's (far too many) moons
    -- Saturn and it's (far fewer) moons
    -- Uranus
    -- Neptune
    -- Pluto
    -- The Shenti (Realms for the three Resonances)

    - Shade Realms
    -- S.R. Matter (Hollow Worlds (I like to imagine this as a "space is solid rock, and solid rock is space" inversion of our universe))
    -- S.R. Time (The Time Stream itself, best not to go here)
    -- S.R. Spirit (Seeking land with Avatar Influence)
    -- S.R. Mind (Seeking land with Internal Conflict)
    -- S.R. Entropy (A gateway/barrier/preview into any of the numerous "end of the world" scenarios we've found, Gehenna, Ragnarok, Oblivion, take your pick.) Probably where Vistas come from.

    - Alternate Realities
    -- Hyperion War Realm (Where all the Jupiter Moon space wars still continue)
    -- Philosophical Realms (Realms where non-canonical victories of Nephandi, Mage, Technocrat or Marauder over various Jupiter Moons has taken place)
    -- Paradox Realms
    -- The Deep Dreaming (CtA)

    - Deep Horizon Chantries
    -- The Corpernicus Institute
    -- Concordia
    -- Several Nephandi Labyrinth and Marauder refuges.

    Void
    - Everything Else

    ((I've decided to ignore Antihelios aka The Red Star since that was only introduced to foreshadow and bring about the various "End of the World" scenarios that were supposed to end the game line)).


    ++Methods of Travel++

    This is what I've gathered on how to get from the various A's to the numerous B's

    ==Barriers==

    --Gauntlet--

    Magic
    - Step Sideways (spirit 3) for Middle and High Umbra
    - Astral Project (Mind 4) for High Umbra
    - Astral Sojourn (Mind 4/Prime 2/Spirit 3) for Middle and High Umbra
    - Agama Sojourn (Entropy 4/ Life 2/Spirit 3) for Low Umbra

    Other
    - Gateways
    -- Shallowings
    - Dreaming for the Maya

    --First Horizon--

    Magic
    - Spirit 5 to open Anchorhead
    - Mind 5 to unteather

    Other
    - Use Spaceship to navigate Horizon
    - Search for cracks in the Horizon
    - Enter already opened Anchorheads.

    --Far Horizon--

    Pretty much the same as the above, but it's much more difficult and much more dangerous.

    Traversal thereafter can be via Ship, Projection, or simply "walking" across depending on how you view the universe.

    ==Corridors==

    These are alternative methods of traversal which get around some of the barriers above.

    - The Pattern Web - From any near umbra world to another
    - Alder Bole/Mount Qaf - Climb up to the high umbra, or down to the low umbra (find it's branches in the high and its roots in the low)
    - The Aetherial Realm - From the middle umbra to the very edge of the Deep Umbra. This allows direct entry into any of the Shade Realms provided the Spirits of each planet are happy with you, you can also access the Shenti from here. It should be possible to return to the "normal" deep umbra from here too, but then you're going to have a very hard time returning. Technomancers may never use this path.
    - The Maya - By entering the Near Dreaming, it is possible to navigate the various Trods and Pathways to get effectively anywhere in the Umbra, from the Near Umbra, to the Deep Dreaming. This is the most dangerous method of all as you have the least control over where you may end up, and can only be done by Dreamspeakers or by those with a Fae guide.

    ==Backdoors==

    Other little connecting methods that get around the more "classical" methods examined above. (some of these are just my own musings).

    - The Hollow Worlds - Perhaps by falling into the hollow world in Earth, provided you believe in the hollow earth theory and can be lucky enough to find a portal, you are then able to explore the Shade Realm of Matter, and with enough time can dig your way towards Jupiter, where you can exit in the Deep Umbra (this would probably take a great deal of time, and the chances of getting lost or stuck are much higher)
    - The Virtual Web - By fully entering the Virtual Web, you can then exit a module in Dystopia, one of the realms within the Middle Umbra.
    - The Afterworlds - There is a chance the Afterworlds of the High Umbra and the Far Shores of the Low Umbra take place in an overlapping realm, allowing a backdoor entry of sorts for Mages and Wraiths alike. Chances are such an overlap is not well advertised, and if it was it probably isn't all that easy to exploit.
    - Oblivion and S.R. Entropy - There is a chance that a link between these two distant areas exists, and that if one enters the S.R of Entropy, they may be able to emerge in the deepest depths of the True Malfean Labyrinth and the bottom of the Tempest, this may then indicate a reverse trip is possible, though the penalty for being wrong would be severe.

    Assuming that there are no convenient portals to such realms, these backdoor ideas give a wealth of possible traversal methods in order to get to specific yet almost unattainable locations.

    How viable do you guys think this may be? And what other methods are you aware of?

              Ambrosia You're right, I completely forgot about that. I'd probably classify it as a "corridor" though as you could technically get anywhere from there. In many ways it's like the Maya/Dreaming, except way more imprecise and much easier to get trapped within.
              I've always imagined The Null Zone in the following way. Imagine a reality where the only things that actually exist if what you directly perceive. You look at the wall, and the moment you look away, the wall ceases to exist, but will return just as you look back. This happens with every sense, the moment you go out of earshot of something, that sound just stops, the moments you lift your foot of the ground, the floor beneath you ceases to be, and as long as you're not looking down, will not return until you place your foot back down.

              Reality is constantly shifting to meet what you expect to see. As a result the world is only "part" there. The far side of objects do not exist until you either feel them out of turn the object around. Similarly, if you go to look inside the boot of a car, anything you can't see, the front, the tires, the engine, ceases to exist until you return. The back of your head does no exist unless you feel it or someone else is looking at it.

              It's a perfect blend of the Metaphysical Trinity. Dynamics made by perception, Stasis enforced by expectation, and Entropy brought about as the perceiving body moves on.

              The Null Zone is then being able to see all of this from another perspective without influencing it at all. Depending on your paradigm you may see it all being held together by bits of string and wooden boards; portal like panels which spring up in response to how the "perseveres" move, like a video game from a noclip perspective; as a black void which only "pops" into place as people chat what they view; a mess of chaos which is forced into alignment in response to the will of the viewer; that there are Descartian demons and spirits throwing moving around packages of Sense Datum into appropriate locations at the correct times. It could be all of these methods are employed in various cases depending on what's required.

              Things can only "be" in this world if they are being seen, and this may mean that the vast majority of the world is dark with scant moments of activity depending on where any form of sentient minds linger. It could instead be a series of corridors that weave in all these minds, which in reality are right next to each other. Someone standing on the edge of Ganymede could be standing right next to someone in a library in Phoenix Arizona, but their senses don't allow such closeness to be usable.

              Again, depending on the paradigm, it might not just be Humans, of Sentient Creatures that are capable of perception, but that all living things have a hand in shaping the forms of the world, perhaps this rule even applies to spiritual entities, perhaps there is a greater being, a deity or group of deities who is able to perceive parts of or the entirety of the world at once, in such case the Null zone would be more like a "hub" point than a mass expanse of space or a winding section of corridors.
              Alternatively, it could be the only think that exists in reality is your own mind, and everything else is but a product of your own perceptions. This type of Null World would probably manifest similarly to the "Deity" one, except it is them that it in the centre of the hub and with no real knowledge on how to take control of anything.

Lots of things to consider, lots of possibility for peril.

                              Barriers like these are meant to be restrictions for the player, but tools for the ST to add extra intrigue and puzzle to the adventure. It's a case of "Everything is possible to reach, but if you want to get there, you'd better work for it!"

The big difference between the True Horizon and the Deep Umbra is simply that in the deep umbra, there is not real distinction between mundane and umbral, everything is all a mesh unto itself. The moment you cross the far horizon, you're in the realm of anything goes so long as you can imagine and believe it.

                                      If you wanted, you could just have your Etherites have a Spirit 5 Break the Dream Shell style talisman attached to it. You'd have needed such a thing to get past the Near Horizon barrier anyway, so it's sort of a moot point. Again, getting rid of the Far Horizon barrier makes the Deep Umbra much less mysterious and reviled. It goes from being "Woah, things are getting serious" to "Ok everyone! We're off to Jupiter!!". If that's the tone you're going for then fine, but I prefer my space exploration to be filled with uncertainty and constant moments of "is this really a good idea?". These obstacles provide a necessity for the players to think about their options, to consider how they might approach rather than just swoop in guns blazing.
                                      Perhaps they could look for a powerful mage who can assist with these barriers, perhaps they could ask a spirit to help, maybe there's a way through if you just navigate well enough, maybe a quick pit stop in Mars could drum up some hints, maybe we could get their via the Hollow Worlds and dig our way to triumph!

                                      And yeah, because the Deep Umbra has no real distinction of conventional and umbral, those two ships would be in exactly the same place for their fights. It's only things like the Hyperion realm, the Shade Realms, the Philosophical Realms, and the various lost Horizon realms which would require flying into bubbles of reality. Otherwise you're in deep space.
                                      This could be another thing which adds layers of "what's going on?" to the players. A void engineer ship approaches, but are they from the same Earth as they? Are they a historic vessel from the Hyperion Realm? Do they perhaps hail from an alternate future via the Shade Realm of Time? Did we accidentally fly into a Philosophical Realm where the Technocracy won and have started to colonise space? or perhaps one where the Void Engineers and the Etherites are still allies? Lots of things to consider, lots of possibility for peril.

                                      Barriers like these are meant to be restrictions for the player, but tools for the ST to add extra intrigue and puzzle to the adventure. It's a case of "Everything is possible to reach, but if you want to get there, you'd better work for it!"
                                      Well, to be completely frank, you don't know what you're talking about when you start talking about how I ran my game, selected which rules did and did not apply and did so in such a way as to make the stories we, as a team. were creating. No "opportunities for conflict" lost. Some that make no sense are avoided. There are tons of problems with the system as is that gets in the way of many, many, many (most) types of Outer Space, Space Opera to competence porn. This game you are borderline disparaging is regarded by those who remember it VERY fondly as one of the most "Mage" of all the Mage games we've played over a couple of decades, and, to several, is the best Mage game we ever played.

                                      There is no reason the Asteroid Belt makes any sort of sense as a boundary except that someone set it that way in 1st Ed. It's about as arbitrary as anything can be and fits with a particular view of the setting of mage that is more than a little wonky. Having most of the Solar System in the Deep Umbra makes the Deep Umbra the kind of loose and difficult to encompass concept that makes this an endless source of fruitless discussion. Why does Jupiter need to be in the Deep Umbra? Is there something completely different about Jovian space? Do the moons occasionally eat each other or change into conceptual matrices? Do planet-sized Cthuloid monsters swim in the Red Spot? Do mortal astronomers just not notice this in ANY way even though the whole point of letting the pesky mundanes have those telescopes is to reinforce the Tychoidean paradigm? If there's a "natural" place to put the boundary between Conventional Space and the Deep Umbra with regard to Conventional Space it's at the heliopause. That makes a TON more sense than the Asteroid Belt. In fact, those VE's have been pushing that all that stuff past the heliopause is deep or interstellar space. And the boundary between other Umbrae and the Deep Umbra occur along their own "natural" fission lines. And, probably, all those Deep Umbrae are the same Deep Umbra.

                                      Saying, that not using all of these rules so that you can make the lives of the players more complicated, or, apparently, not letting it be too easy for my lazy-minded players just rings false. The Umbra travel rules are completely arbitrary, ridiculously baroque and completely self-contradictory. If you actually start running a game that involves a lot of Umbral travel using all those rules, you will have to make concessions. 100% will HAVE to. There are just places they don't play well together. There are also tons of neat little gaps between the finicky little specific rules sets that your game will fall into sooner than you think and you'll be backpeddling and wasting valuable playing time or having to riff it all out. In addition, you will be spending LOTS of time having to manage a corpus of rules scattered across several different sources in a gaming milieu that began with the "simplify all the fiddly rules so we can get to role-playing, not roll-playing".

                                      It all smacks of defending the status quo simply because it is the status quo and refusing to examine it for possible flaws and seeing if the game might be improved by doing so. And rigidly applying that status quo to a set of games predicated on the Golden Rule.

                                                  The problem with the Mage cosmology is actually quite a large one. It's premise is fundamentally that the Solar System as seen by scientists on Earth is the TRUTH. There IS an Asteroid Belt. There IS a division between the inner and outer Solar System. Mars, Venus and Mercury ARE rocky small planets, Saturn, Jupiter, Neptune and Uranus ARE gas giants and Pluto IS something else. And so on. All the mage-specific folderol is tacked onto that. The Asteroid Belt is the Horizon. Horizon Realms are actually located on Asteroids and on or around inner system planets. And so on.

                                                  That's a HUGE problem because it essentially picks one view of the universe and deems it CORRECT. And that POV is the Technocratic one. That means they're right and everyone else is wrong. The best you can do is try to play "pin my cosmology on the planets". (It also causes a few more crossover problems than already exist because it's pretty difficult to square, say the Werewolf cosmology with Mage's at all, as Aya points out.... And Mage is supposed to be expansive in it's ability to accommodate various belief systems.)

                                                  For example: Doissetep is on Mars. It might be a slightly off-kilter Mars, one step over from the one with the Rover on it. Or not. But that doesn't matter really, because it is definitely not the Mars of the Ptolemaic model of the Solar System with all the chiming ethereal crystal spheres in an interlocking divine celestial Antikytheria Device writ cosmic. So the "Mars" of the Tychoidean system is the REAL Mars and the best the Traditions can do is go to their Mars and wriggle a little to find an amenable dimensional pocket. What has that Mars got to do with the ethos/uber-paradigm/praxis of the Hermetics? And it starts getting much much worse when you start taking into account beleif structures that would be better served by something more akin to the Werewolf model for various cultures, mythologies, belief systems and magical practices around the world. Like, say, many, many, many of different groups that were jammed together as the Dreamspeakers. Mars may not even BE a place. It may be a person who wanders across the fields that we percieve as the night sky. A Dreamspeaker might not magically conceive of a trip to a place called "Mars" (appropriately translated), they would being going to talk to someone who is that red dot in the sky.... And they would never be "walking through outer space" to get there (making for something fairly odd to throw up on the screen of Voidship command deck....) They would step sideways and find/take the way to where one can wander the "Fields of Night" or what have you.

                                                  The only reason to hold onto this seems to be inertia. That's the way they first thought of it as a stab at a compromise of belief systems. But Mage has moved WAY past the way they were originally thinking in any number of ways. Look back at what "paradigm" meant and didn't mean in first edition compared to 2nd, Revised or M20. There's been evolution of the way the ideas have been handled and paradigm/ethos/Focus have moved from being decorative to a central focus. There should be a similar evolution here. And it would MASSIVELY simplify this boondoggle.

                                                  What is Conventional Space? It's the slice of the Umbra that the Technocrats are exploring. It's THEIR Umbra. Other groups have different but equally valid ways of perceiving and interacting with the heavens that are appropriate to them. Why do all us (Western, scientifically literate) mortals think of Conventional Space as the Truth? Because that's what the Union and the Order have been selling us all the way back to when the Celestial Masters, who were not just looking at THE Truth, but seeking for A truth that worked for them, and those magical optical devices along with all the related paraphernalia is what the Consensus bought lock, stock, barrel and Toys R Us kid's first telescope. But that doesn't mean that an Etherite is going to go up into "Space" and not find something more like Barsoom than what the Mars Rover is sending back pictures of, nor that a Hermetic might end up "going into space" in way that seems more like a Baron Munchausen story or something similar to Dante, with various realms that have to be appropriately traversed to go from level to level of the Ptolemaic system....
