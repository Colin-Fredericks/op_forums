                    I wasn't a fan of the whole 'there are no entities the Nephandi serve' personally. I preferred the 3 general divisions presented in the Book of Madness where you had the infernalists who serve demons, devils, and other hellish entities (example Nergali Baali); the servants of the Wyrm and Maeljin Incarna(examples Pentex, Black Spiral Dancers); and the servants to the Outsiders (example K'llasshaa). While these groups have some similarities each of their end games prevent long term cooperation as they are mutually exclusive; the various infernal entities want to make reality into hell and rule it, the Wyrm servants want to destroy reality, and the Outsiders want to return to reality and alter it to fit their alien whims. Even within one group you end up with divisions over methodology; for example Black Spiral Dancers refuse to work with the Maeljin Incarna, differing infernal entities and their cults will fight for the right to rule (the Outsiders are simply not developed enough in White Wolf to tell what kind of divisions exist although I imagine it would be similar to the issues that affect infernal entities). This all leads to each group of Nephandi being somewhat unique with its own particular feel based on which group they align with which I think makes a better foe than a generic monolithic Evil.

                        If it's any consolation, I actually did my own variant of the Nephandi which worked very well:

                        Cyclical Nephandi

                        The majority of Nephandi are merely broken shattered shells of former people. They hate the world, they hate the universe, they hate themselves, and they hate everyone else. Cyclical Nephandi are this, certainly, but they have an ideology which unites them in more than the psychopathic spiral of destruction which all other Nephandi seem to aspire to. Many Traditionalists don't actually believe Cyclical Nephandi qualify for the moniker despite going through the Cauls and being as passionately devoted to the destruction of everything as other Nephandus because, well, the idea of them having an ideology beyond destruction goes against the purpose of what a Nephandi is all about, doesn't it? To many, they are simply spectacularly evil mages and may even have a warped form of Ascension planned.

                        The Cyclical Nephandi argue, in fact, they are the true Nephandus and that all other examples of their kind are merely Infernalists or people suffering a cosmic temper tantrum. They are a cooler kind of malevolence and quite proud of their calm, methodical pursuit of oblivion for reality. So, in simple terms, what is the Cyclical Nephandi ideology?

                        This world is wrong.

                        This is certainly an attitude most mages can get behind. It is the basis for the Ascension War in fact. Cyclical Nephandi, however, take it to a larger extent. The world is not just wrong, it is a prison and there is absolutely nothing wrong saving in. Why have death? Why have the Shadowlands? Why have a human race which could CONCEIVE of something like this World of Darkness and then believe in it enough to create it? Why value a race which seems to have won the Ascension War from the Union and Traditions by choosing aggressive ignorance in the face of hope? Cyclical Nephandi have a Gnostic view of the world which does not necessarily believe there is anything better beyond this reality but that since mages are divine entities that they could undoubtedly make something better if it wasn't weighed down by all the garbage of the universe.

                        It is not enough for Cyclical Nephandi to kill everyone (though many think that would help), that would just leave a world full of Wraiths or Trapped avatars. No, they have to UNMAKE reality so that it never existed. Only then can the Pure Ones reassemble or the mages can be freed to their true potential or, simply, something else emerge from the void better than the failed experiment which is humanity.

                        The scary thing is they're not necessarily wrong as if the world as is, is destroyed, a suitably powerful mage could make a new one in its place without the cosmic detritus of the current broken system. A broken vase can never be fixed perfectly so why not just forge a new one? Or simply accept life is a failed experiment with no point? Let the spirits replace mankind or sweet release from all things? These are the subject of spirited debates among the Cyclical Nephandus.

                        What, if anything, will replace mankind when it and all of its works are swept away is something there is often disagreement about but it is not THIS. So they can work together toward the destruction of everything. All they need is the Cauls to remove any pesky conscience or hesitation to doing what they need to do. It gives them a purity of thought and purpose which would make them pity other mages if they still had that in them.

                        Cyclical Nephanus are servants of the Abyss, Oblivion, and the cosmic force of Entropy without Renewal. Some are interested in physical eradication of the world and work to foster war, conflict, and destruction. Others are interested in a spiritual destruction of the world, one soul at a time, leading mortals to not want to live on. Others hope to break down reality's barriers so Oblivion consumes this universe entirety. A great majority believe mankind unconsciously wishes for death already and the slow march to extinction will result in the Mass Consensus eradicating itself. To win, the Cyclical Nephandi do not have to achieve their goal.

                        They simply have to make the other Traditions and Technocrats and Dreamers do not.

                        And they're doing a great job.

                                    The way I played it in my big Mage game a decade ago, is that Nephandi (or at least, the first generation of them) were originally a conspiracy of Entropic mages who realized destruction is an inevitable part of the great cycle, and that without Entropy the Tellurian would simply reside in perpetual Stasis, which to them of all potential outcomes was the worst, particularly for the fact they extrapolated that Paradox is inextricably linked to Stasis, and the opposite and equal to Quintessence. They saw the rise of Stasis as the death of True Magick -- not just for Traditions and Crafts, but even for the Order of Reason. So, to avoid this outcome, they decided to see to it the Tellurian received the Good Death as quickly and painlessly as possible, to spare not only mages but sleepers an infinity of mere, hopeless existence.

                                    And yes, in that game the Chakravanti were the originating point of the Nephandi, and from whom that viewpoint expanded to other societies of mages. Needless to say, a group of death mages evangelizing the end of the world as a way to forestall the death of True Magick (even though this very philosophy is what fully half the world's mages believed in anyways) wasn't taken well by other mages...and they ended up facing some catastrophically bad press.

                                    Then (in a self-fulfilling prophecy), like the Chakravanti before them, they started succumbing to Jhor, worshiping alien powers, inverting their Avatars, and the rest was history. They invented the idea of Descent, and the like. Making them more akin to really, really misguided Marauders than anything else, really. So, the original "true" Nephandi quietly rejoined the Chakravanti, slipping their own beliefs and teachings in with the Traditions' innate Buddhist, Hindu, and Zoroastrian philosophies, creating the Euthanatoi -- forging a Tradition uniquely trained and positioned to combat the fallen Nephandi while normalizing their own beliefs the Tellurian would eventually need to end so that it may be reborn.

                                                    I respectfully disagree with CTPhipps, as I view the various Nephandi philosophies as being a front from a significantly more irrational reason: they just want to corrupt, despoil, and destroy. It's hard-coded into them, and informs their actions on an emotional level. Everything else is after-the-fact rationalization.

                                                    Personally I think it makes sense. All regular Mages are driven towards Ascension by their Avatars, and said Avatars don't necessarily care about what the Mage believes. They just want the Mage to ascend. It's a positive, not necessarily philosophical goal. It's more primal, these positivist emotional urges towards becoming better.

                                                    The Nephandi are the direct opposite. Their inverted Avatars want Descent, and fill the Nephandus's heart with hate, bitterness, and sadistic desire. And such an Avatar is created by a conscious decision by the Mage (in at least one of their incarnations) to reject positivism and embrace negativism. To forsake Hope in favor of Despair.

                                                    This possibility would actually justify the different factions of Nephandi, and their apparent inconsistent motivations. It's not that the Nephandi want to destroy creation...they just hate it. Their hearts are filled with foul black ichor, driving them towards atavistic violence - or violation - against anything and everything around them. Different groups, obviously, have different ideas about how they ought to express that hatred. The Malfeans are, of course, all about annihilation. They've jumped straight to Oblivion and see that as the only true way to sate their urge to purge. The Infernalists aren't quite so suicidal, but would be happy seeing the world turned into a new wing of Hell (hopefully with them promoted to cushy positions as reward for their loyalty). And the unpronounceable ones want the Great Ones to free them to revel and kill, and teach them new ways to revel and kill. A desire for unfettered wickedness, blended with a heavy dose of cosmic madness.

                                                    And then you have independent Nephandi - the ones who think the others are stupid for tying themselves to demonic patrons - who want to see the world burn on their own terms. Unfortunately, that means also not having big, cosmic powers to lend a hand to their schemes. They retain their freedom, but by that token remain handicapped in other ways.

                                                    Lately, the question of how the Nephandi first came into existence has bugged me. What I've been going with, after some thought, is based on a sometimes mentioned idea in the books, about Nephandi whose Avatars inverted without going to the Cauls. And this being accomplished by the Mage's mind being so filled with negative emotions, that they essentially went through a Caul of the heart. Without assistance by an evil labyrinth, they just...auto-Fall, by shear force of despair.

                                                    (Come to think of it, this is not unlike how Witches are created in Puella Magi Madoka Magica...).

                                                    My theory about where the Nephandi come from is simply that, in the deepest depths of history, this scenario happened for the first time with a Mage. Maybe the Pure Ones had left by this point, or maybe the actions of this newly Fallen Mage were the reason they were toppled in the first place. Whatever the case, you had a Mage so dark, so wrong-minded, that they twisted themself into becoming an enemy of creation. Maybe this Mage - through multiple incarnations perhaps - met others who had experienced the same Fall, and banded together. Or maybe he or she was alone for the longest time, and "wanted company". Whatever the case, this Nephandus and possibly others worked to find a way to engineer the inversion of Avatars.
