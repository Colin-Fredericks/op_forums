    Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
    For my own part, I would limit knowing the exact outcome to purely random systems and have the information gleaned get more vague as the situation gets more open ended.
    I would personally avoid the label "purely" random. Whether or not truly random events are even possible is a philosophic matter, and the vast majority of random number generators used by humans are pseudo-random either by imperfections in a system (no dice are made 100% perfectly and then roll with perfect technique on a 100% perfectly constructed surface), or by design limitations (computers aren't actually capable of generating truly random numbers on their own, and either use a pure algorithm that approximates randomness, or uses a sensor that can detect a suitably random external event; in high end computation usually both). Something that's probably more important than nitpicking over pure vs. approximately pure randomness, is the ability to sense odds shifting. Texas Hold Em poker is very popular because the odds shift as play proceeds. Knowing your starting odds is great, but knowing if your odds shift as other players fold is even stronger.

    Which also leads to an implied power here: the ability to detect how random a system is; which is sorta combining sense fate/fortune and sense weakness at the same time as a lack of randomness would be seen as a weakness in a RNG. Given the Syndicate's presence in the world of gambling, they'd likely have monitoring of large systems like lotteries or online betting for anomalies before they would be detectable by normal statistical quality checks. This isn't necessarily the same as detecting cheating though, as most games of chance are weighted so the house wins more than they lose. For mages trying to juice their odds of a payday, thigh would be more like scanning which slot machines have been set to be "looser" than the rest; something casinos do to increase the perception of having a chance at a big pay day.

            Entropy 2

            •• Control Probability
            Now the mage can control the factors he could only sense before. Pulling the strings of apparently random events, he can influence activities and results – directing the fall of dice or cards, repeatedly hitting weak spots, and directing people and things toward a conclusion of his choice. Of course, it’s easier to control small events (the winner of a horse race) than large ones (causing a six-car pileup). In game terms, large alterations demand more successes than small ones do.
            Entropy 2 really has one ability, but it is used in several ways. Initially I separated directing the fall of dice and influencing activities and results. Intuitively it seems like the former is controlling the full events and the latter only bringing about a specific outcome. Functionally, though, they are used the same way and the main difference is scale. I can split these back apart, though, if you all think it is worth it.


            Influence People and Things (Control Probability)

            The mage can influence the results of random and/or chaotic systems. How this happens isn’t quite explained, though it does involve pulling the strings of apparently random events. Given the examples, it appears the mage is stacking up the results of one or more events to bring about a specific result.

            Intuitively, this power tends towards focus on results rather than on process. Partly this is because most of Entropy’s direct effects unlock with higher ranks, but also because this seems to work in situations with more factors than the mage could reasonably process.

            I should point out that repeated use of this power is the poster child for the Domino Effect.

            Side note: The wording here is interesting because ‘conclusion’ could mean an outcome or it could mean a judgement. Given the examples of horse races and car accidents, outcome is clearly an intended meaning. Influencing judgements is a trickier matter. Directly affecting thoughts is an Entropy 5 power, but affecting quick judgement calls, emotional responses, and instinctive reactions may be an intended meaning as well. That’s an ST call.


            How to Win at Everything

            The mage can repeatedly make things play out in his favor. He can win at gambling, carnival games, and at the cash register (hey, they gave me an extra $20, what luck*). Since even games of skill still have the potential for distraction and random chance, this should at least influence them.

            *For him. Not so much for them when the till doesn’t add up.


            Look! A Convenient Distraction!

            The mage can engineer events to provide a convenient event such as a distraction or a jammed door. The classic Hollywood example is making someone drop their groceries, which lets the mage slip by or start a conversation, depending on his intentions.


            Penultimate Destination

            A mage can also manipulate trends and events towards damaging outcomes for a target. The car crash above was a clear example. On the flip side, a mage can manipulate events towards the miraculous and make situations that should have led to tragedy for himself or others instead be near misses.


            Repeatedly Hit Weak Points (Control Probability)


            This is an interesting power because like the others it is listed as an extension of manipulating apparently random events. On its own, it appears to be a way of exploiting weak points. Now in theory that is a step up from finding those weak points with Entropy 1, but mechanically these two are often used the same way. (See Thunder Punch).

            In the context of manipulating probability, though, this gets a little bit more powerful and allows a mage to direct events towards flaws in a repeated fashion. Not only does this mean he can make the same rare event happen multiple times (the guy who keeps stepping on rakes) but he can also make multiple events land in the same place.


            Why Is It Always the Eye!?

            By manipulating events a mage can share the exploitation of a weak point with other people, even those who do not know about it. General attacks will tend towards those weak points without any conscious direction.


            It Keeps Happening

            The mage can direct seemingly random events to repeatedly cause the same problems, whether that be physical strikes against an existing wound or mental reminders of past trauma.

            As a particularly dark example, a mage could probably make someone encounter repeated triggers for their PTSD. I should point out that may be extremely uncomfortable at the gaming table, so check with your fellow players first.

                Originally posted by quelsec View Post

                This is not true in practice. You can do damage with lower entropy, just not decay damage. Examples:

                - making an enemy slip of a roof by damagin a roof tile the enemy is standing on (entropy 2)
                - Making breaks fail when some one tries to stop a car before hitting your enemy (entropy 2)
                - Making a airplane fragment rip of and target the place where the enemy is while the visible plane is above (entropy 3)
                - Making that the pipe you hit is filled with hot steam and erupts at the enemy (entropy 2-3)
                - Making is so a car explodes after shooting the gas tank (entropy 2)
                Most of those are Entropy 3 because they rely on specific failures in materials or mechanics. The steam pipe arguably isn't even Entropy as it either has steam or it doesn't. Whether it breaks or not, could be Entropy 2, though. It is admittedly not always easy to see the line between directing events and decaying an object, but your focus on the specific method and materials pushes towards rank 3.

                More obviously Entropy 2 effects would come from directing an random or complex situation towards a reasonable, if unlikely, result. They would be more like:

                - Making an enemy stumble near the edge of a roof due to an inopportune wind gust or bird or any of a number of random factors.

                - Distracting someone with spilled coffee or windblown paper when they need to be pressing the brakes.

                - Having a swaying pile of crates fall over at an inconvenient time.

                I don't want to pigeonhole Entropy 2 into only working when the game world is undefined, but it is easiest to see opportunities for it when that is the case.


                I'll add that as a system hack I'm not against treating some Entropy effects as attacks in order to use the rolled damage as a framework to narrate out the effects. That's explicitly an idea for a house rule, though. Also, as Entropy 2 specifically says that larger events take more successes, some of the successes would have to be spent there.

                                                    Entropy 3

                                                    ••• Affect Predictable Patterns

                                                    Things eventually break down. At this Rank, the mage can control the speed at which material objects fail or decay. That’s easier to do with complex machines (cars, computers) than it is with simple ones (walls, stones) – after all, more things can go wrong with complicated things.

                                                    At this Rank, the mage can also start controlling the fate and fortune of objects and people, giving them good or bad luck by controlling the probability of events around them. Again, large feats demand more successes than smaller ones.
                                                    Two abilities are given here. Entropy 3 can:

                                                    Speed or Slow Decay (Affect Predictable Patterns)

                                                    By direct read this mainly concerns the speed at which things decay, however the examples bring up complex machines which can have sudden and dramatic failures. This would imply that both are within the realm of Entropy 3.


                                                    Tune Up/Grit in the Gears

                                                    The mage can tweak an object or machine so that it works for much longer without breaking down. Alternatively he can make it degrade much faster. There are a number of


                                                    Jam Mechanism/Percussive Maintenance

                                                    The mage can trigger sudden failure modes like jamming guns and slipped belts. This could reasonably work in reverse as well with a problem suddenly clearing up. Mechanical problems suddenly clearing up without explanation may sound too unlikely for Entropy, which is why I used the name Percussive Maintenance. The repair abilities of a good thump are well known.

                                                    That said, in my troubleshooting experience unexplained improvement not only happens, it happens frequently enough to be irritating. After all, If I you can no longer figure out what the problem was, you can’t prevent it from happening again. I know I am not alone in that experience, so I have no problems with a problem just clearing up. But I digress…


                                                    Fortune and Misfortune (Affect Predictable Patterns)

                                                    This power lets a mage make himself or someone else lucky or unlucky. What counts as good or bad fortune is going to vary. This is a very open-ended power that is highly customizable based on the intent and paradigm of the caster. In addition to that this either relies on Fate to guide it, or it is a spell that can make decisions. Entropy is like that.


                                                    Always Lucky

                                                    The target always does better than average on games of chance, or in dangerous situations


                                                    Never Hit a Red

                                                    The target almost never encounters a specific inconvenience or problem. In reverse, this could curse someone to encounter those same problems much more frequently than normally.


                                                    Murphy’s Rules Lawyering

                                                    All actions, or all actions of a certain kind, are made at difficulty due to constant random accidents. A mage could curse someone to never be able to get out the door on time, or never get an uninterrupted night's sleep. In the same way, a mage could protect someone from interference with the same kind of random events just unleashed on others.


                                                    Recursive Entropy Manipulation

                                                    While mucking about with fate and fortune should be detectable by Entropy 1 abilities, a mage should be able to manipulate his fortune to make it less likely anyone with Entropy 1 is looking. This might seem a little paradoxical, but one way it could work is by creating sudden bursts of good fortune in people other than the mage, thus causing the distraction.


                                                    From Elsewhere in M20:

                                                    Destroy Durability (Affect Predictable Patterns) (From Magic vs Objects)

                                                    Ambiguity Resolution – What Rank?

                                                    Magic vs Objects speaks of damage inflicting effects on material objects. These attack the Durability trait of the object. The section does not mention what rank this is possible at, though. The two options are Rank 3, which controls the entropy in material objects, and Rank 4 which is explicitly when Entropy can attack a pattern directly.

                                                    My best take on this is that decaying an object has to be capable of degrading its Durability, so it must be possible at this rank. It may not technically strike the object’s pattern directly but it functions like a damage dealing spell anyways.


                                                    Missing Powers?

                                                    Speed or Slow Decay… of Energies (Affect Predictable Patterns)

                                                    Ambiguity Resolution – What Counts as a Predictable Pattern?

                                                    Now we come to a bigger dilemma that I didn't initially catch until I was looking over Forces: Does Affect Predictable Patterns also include forces and energies? On the one hand, the text clearly specifies material objects. However, the name Affect Predictable Patterns seems to apply to more than just objects. At the very least, it is a better fit for energies than Affect Living Patterns or Affect Thoughts, which are the next two ranks.

                                                    Alternatively, maybe Entropy intentionally can’t affect energy patterns at all. That doesn’t seem likely to me, though.

                                                    Overall, my take is that it should work on forces, but I'd like to hear what you all think.
