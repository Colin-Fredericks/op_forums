    A couple of questions on extended spellcasting rolls

    10-18-2020, 10:04 PM
    1. When a mage spends Quintessence to reduce the difficulty of his Arete roll, does it apply only to one single roll, or to every roll made during the casting of that one Effect? I had been pretty sure it was the former, but I can't find it clearly stated in the book anywhere.

    2. The optional rule on M20 p. 541-542 indicates that casting a Great Work takes five hours per roll. Per "Rituals and Stamina" on p. 541, a mage can work on a ritual for one hour per Stamina point before having to start making Stamina rolls each hour (with increasing difficulty each time) or else spend WP to keep going.

    So, realistically even a mage with Stamina 5 is going to have a pretty tough time attempting two Great Work rolls in a row. The good news is that you can take a break from a Great Work...but then you have to spend a WP and make an increasing-difficulty roll for each break. (At least this roll is Attribute+Ability, and you could plausibly refresh some WP in the 48 hours before you have to get back to your Great Work. But still.)

    Am I missing something? Is this actually meant to be as difficult as it seems to be? Are you supposed to be buffing yourself with some kind of Eldritch Caffeine rote in order to keep yourself going through these rituals? If this was a game-balance issue intended to make Effects requiring 10+ successes into genuinely epic feats, making the whole rule optional would be a little bizarre.

          Yeah, the rules are difficult... but there are ways to deal with it... and those ways are actually good for the setting:

          1) Great Works are easier to do in Sanctums (high level ones significantly decrease your need to spend Quintessence on difficulty mods) or in the Umbra. Being in a place where buffing yourself to superhuman endurance isn't vulgar is a huge plus.
          2) Great Works are easier to do with a group of mages capable of sharing the same magical style.
          3) Great Works are easier to do with a bunch of acolytes helping sort out the detail.

          So it very much behooves masters to cultivate students that are indoctrinated into their magical style, a community of believers in their paradigm, and to claim/guard special places to do their most powerful magic. Or in other words, there's a really good reason for there being Traditions/Crafts/Conventions instead of every mage just doing their own thing. When you can get -2 difficulty, +5 successes, and +5 dice to each ritual roll... you can see why the book offers some pretty harsh options to keep that in check. You can also see why groups of like-minded mages are as well positioned in the setting compared to ones that focus on individualism.

                              Something hopefully helpful - an example from my most recent session. I had determined that there was an effect in play over a wide area created by two NPC Mages. One is an ItX -Statistician. The other Syndicate - Media Control. A TV show produced by the Technocracy is used to diffuse the effect. Correspondence 4, Mind 4, Entropy 3, Time 4 - Selecting a possible future the TV show is based on, and then seeding it into the minds of the population to permit some version of that reality to be created, in opposition to current cultural values. Media controversy about the show's message is also used, because the injection of an idea into the Consensus doesn't require positive emotions, only strong ones. The production of a season finale episode is how I imagined the Ritual, benefitting from the sleeper/EC actors' best performances. It takes a few days to shoot an episode, in this case.

                              Where I left room for the players to detect and overcome the effect was the tech-Mages' accumulated successes being spent partly on reaching a large audience. The players don't yet have the resources needed to get forty successes. Plus, they are merely at the stage of analyzing the effect. They used Magick facilitating Ability rolls. They did not have all of the Spheres to completely understand the effect, metaphysically. I allowed perception + awareness to give them some clue and to rise above the effect long enough to carry on. They rolled Intel + Research to analyze the message and mundane tricks (actor sex appeal, controversy, outrage) used to insert it into public consciousness. Intel + Science to devise a step by step mundane method to alert small groups of people and train them to spread awareness, advancing critical arguments against the Union's propaganda.

                              In an upcoming session, there will be a chance to write a paper, Intel + Academics, bringing social scientists to their side. One character has Merits related to singing and will be able to record music that speaks to the values they are defending, Charisma + Art. As they gather resources and sleeper allies, they can start to plan a "ritual" type event that overcomes the Technocracy's success so far.

                              My technocrat NPCs have positioned themselves to ride the counterwave, instead of sending ECs to crash the event. Tradition mages incurring a large amount of paradox is not particularly advantageous to the Union, since it could produce Marauders. There's a story reason why one faction might allow the other to attempt their ritual, if you find it hard to imagine how the players could fortify the location.
