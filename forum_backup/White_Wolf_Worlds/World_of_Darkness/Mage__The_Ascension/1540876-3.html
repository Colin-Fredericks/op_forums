        There are definite parallels between the Seers of the Throne and the Technocracy. Setting aside the technology issue, the Panopticon closely parallels the NWO's surveillance state objective, and the Hegemony closely parallels the NWO's attempts at social engineering through political entities; and the Ministry of Mammon is very much akin to the Syndicate. And I wouldn't be surprised to see the NWO and the Syndicate attempting to infiltrate and possibly incorporate their Seer counterparts.

        The main difference between the two is that the Seers of the Throne are more purely a study of seeking power for the sake of power; the Technocracy was designed to represent a more subtle trap that intellects tend to be particularly vulnerable to: the tendency to fall in love with your theories and to want to bend reality to match your models when the two disagree.

                            Of course, so is the Technocracy.

                            The "Seers=fascists, Technocrats=communists" comparison is imperfect (in particular, the Syndicate represents the Capitalism that the workers will rise up against in Communist theory); but it's surprisingly informative of the essential natures of the two: substitute "the Exarchs" for the State, and fascism is a perfect match for the Seers. (And since the Hierarchy is fundamentally about the State, it's an exact match for them.)

                            As for the Technocracy, the parallel is that both Technocrats ("rule by experts") and Communists are operating on theories about how to being about a Utopian future; and both encounter the problem that Utopias don't work in the real world and inevitably lead those who are truly invested in bringing them about to compromise their ideals in an effort to force it to work. The Technocracy differs from Communism in terms of what their end goals are; in the same way that fascism is properly specific to the Hierarchy while other Ministries have fascism-adjacent ideologies (e.g., Paternoster is all about how organized religion can be used as a means to enslave), the NWO's end-game comes closest to Communism, while the other Conventions have their own ideas of what Mass Ascension should look like.

                            Another parallel to the Fascism vs. Communism comparison is that there's a sort of horseshoe-like effect where they're so polarized opposite that they sort of bend back around and start to resemble each other. So too with the Technocracy and the Seers: the former seek to empower humanity while the latter seek to enslave humanity; but the Utopian ideals of the Technocracy have caused it to "bend around" to the point of pushing for totalitarian solutions to the fact that reality simply isn't operating the way they think it should. So Seer and Technocrat both find themselves attempting to control people.

                            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                        The first thing I want to call out is that Communism and "the American view of Communism through cherry-picked and shallowly explained examples" are very distinct things. And fascist-like authoritarian regimes are far from something relegated only to communism and actual fascism. Liberal authoritarian states like Bukele's El Salvador are far from a rare thing. This isn't just a myopic view of communist ideologies (which are many), it's also a dangerously myopic view of authoritarianism and how it happens.

                                        Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                        There are definite parallels between the Seers of the Throne and the Technocracy. Setting aside the technology issue, the Panopticon closely parallels the NWO's surveillance state objective, and the Hegemony closely parallels the NWO's attempts at social engineering through political entities; and the Ministry of Mammon is very much akin to the Syndicate. And I wouldn't be surprised to see the NWO and the Syndicate attempting to infiltrate and possibly incorporate their Seer counterparts.

                                        The main difference between the two is that the Seers of the Throne are more purely a study of seeking power for the sake of power; the Technocracy was designed to represent a more subtle trap that intellects tend to be particularly vulnerable to: the tendency to fall in love with your theories and to want to bend reality to match your models when the two disagree.
                                        The similarity isn't surprising when we remember that 1st edition Technocracy was basically every conspiracy theory about secret world domination given form, and only later would it get nuanced explanations. The Union is meant to be a case study of a system corrupting its core goals to perpetuate itself.

                                        Yet, precisely because of this, small differences in thought go a long way, so I don't think the Union would want to be buddies with the Seers in principle.

                                        But what I think is kind of lost in this debate is that this Union is stranded in another world. Are the humans here even real humans for them? Is this Earth just as valid and worth of their work as the Earth they came from? How does this place even works, what's out there? Those are not simple questions that we really can assume any actual group would default to a given answer.

                                        Void Engineers in particular would have a field day, but not because of Hedge, Space or whatever. Instead, it's because the very world they're in is alien and strange, ripe for exploration. They don't know which laws of nature or supernature work here (and they're not the same), they don't know what's happening behind the scenes, they don't even know the basics of human psychology for the people from this world. And news flash: there are relevant species-wide differences.

                                        For every other Convention things are very different. It isn't just that they'll have to decide what to do about each supernatural society, is that they're cut from the very institutions that define them, and have defined them for a long time. As those institutions have been put in place by their oldest predecessors and evolved along the thing that would become the Union, the modern Conventions never before even existed without such structure.

                                        What's the Syndicate without knowledge or access to any company at all? What's the NWO when its whole membership is half a dozen guys (if that much) stranded in an unknown city where they don't know anyone, there are no insiders in any institution and they don't even know current politics? What's the Progenitors and Iteration X without labs, financing, contacts in academia and related companies? The fun of such a game would be exactly the fact that those Agents are completely Lost in Space.

                                        Not to say, as I already explored before, that we don't really know if their Magic will even work here in the same way, or any way at all. All similarities aside, CoD and WoD are based on radically different metaphysics, and the very concepts that power each Mage splat simply do not exist in the other setting. Reality in CoD is mysterious and unknown, but set. There's no fluctuating Tellurian that let someone alter it just with willpower, even if there are forces that react to willpower to do something. WoD has no Supernal Realms housing the true laws of reality. They are metaphysically incompatible, they are not the same splat, and even if their powers work the same way, it will be for unknown reasons that may become themselves a matter for investigation.
