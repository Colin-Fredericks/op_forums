    Consensus cannot be wrong. Consensus is global belief, sometimes separated into different reality zones.

    If certain reality zone has people that believe people can fly, then Mages can fly there and do so coincidentally, despite the fact that gravity dictates you cannot.

    Mage is a game of belief, keep that in mind. Consensus simply cannot be wrong, because it is not as closely tied to actual laws of Earth as you might think. It's all about belief.

        A close reading of the reality zones rules reveals that they technically only apply to Awakened Magick: they determine where the line between Coincidental and Vulgar lies, and they possibly give an additional ±1 difficulty modifier if the Magick resonates with or against the zone. The party line of the Technocracy, though, is that they are using the laws of the world, so they will change them in their favor if they can.

        Note that Primal Reality Zones are also stated as possible default states for the Reality of the World.

                                            With all due respect, I've seen several claims here about technology falling in different regions of the world; but in my reading of M20 the only thing remotely like that that I've found is mention that technomagick tends to fail in primal zones. Not “technology”; “technomagick”. The assertion that a laptop would fail deep in the Amazon Jungle appears to be based on the assumption that these two things are in fact one and the same; and while that is a valid assumption to make, it's not an established fact. Note that every example given in the Reality Zones section speaks of the effect of the zone on magick, techno or otherwise. There are no examples that I'm aware of that describe, say, the laws of physics changing in a Reality Zone. If you find one, please post a page reference and quote the part in question.

                                            As for Format Space: that doesn't create conventional space, per se; it erects a Gauntlet between conventional space and the Umbra where no such barrier previously existed, isolating the Umbra and its laws from conventional space. I'll grant that that's a bit more than just changing the Vulgarity or difficulty of magick; but it's not exactly altering the laws of physics either. And it's also from a Revised sourcebook (albeit a late one), not M20; so its validity isn't a foregone conclusion. It's probably valid; but I can't guarantee it.


                                            All that said: I'm partial the idea that the Consensus does potentially alter the laws of reality — in small ways, and only when a significant portion of the population's beliefs are aligned. This is most commonly known as mythic threads; and other than being another thing that alters the difficulty of magick, its only known effect is as a possible enabler of hedge magic. Remember, the Consensus is the cumulative effect of Sleeping Avatars; I don't see it being able to achieve anything an Arete ·· mage can't do with sufficient successes, if that; and even there, I wouldn't expect much in the way of successes. These are Sleeping Avatars, after all; any actual changes would have to be based on something to the effect of Wild Magick. It can mess with Awakened Magick, but not much beyond that.

                                                        The matter is complicated by the fact that a lot of modern consumer and super-high-end technology is heavily reliant on infrastructure to do much of anything - and anyplace that has a lot of technological infrastructure set up is by nature friendly to technomagick. The Sleepers' efforts there make it so. Cellphones don't work without cell towards. Electronics don't work without power sources. Most of the stuff designed to go into unfriendly environments is hardened or made more resilient in some fashion, and I imagine that in the WoD a lot of it essentially requires what amounts to an Extraordinary Citizen or an 'incursion' of a tiny bit of Consensus.

                                                        So it's actually somewhat hard to distinguish between the "Consensus can actually make things work or not work" model - "the Consensus is weak here, so technomagic like electrons and things that Technocrats assume like electrons don't work right" like electrons that are how Technocrats work magick don't properly exist" - from the technological paradigm - "the tech isn't hardened against unfavorable environments and relies on infrastructure that's absent, what do you expect from consumer-grade crap/big hefty installations?"

                                                        The big factor in my mind is that in the Earth's Umbra, technology in general isn't reliable. It's basically a 'pristine' place where external laws aren't exposed except by the observer... which means that when you remove Consensus directly, you don't get nice objective stable modern physics etc., or even a refinement thereof. You get... weird subjective whatsis with only a few established commonalities.

                                                        (And of course, the Consensus can't accomplish things outside the realm of a sufficient-Arete mage with a bunch of assistance couldn't... but then, considering what someone with Mastery of Life, Matter, Forces, Prime, Spirit, Mind, Entropy, Correspondence, and Time can pull off, let alone Archmasters? ...that is not very much of a constraint at all.)
