    65) Once upon a time in Arabia, a ghula - a blood-drinking cannibal fairy or djinn of the desert - did as her kind often did, and got a man of wealth and vitality to marry her. She drank her husband's blood as he slept, in order to fuel the creation of their children. But, so this story goes, the man's great piety before Allah rubbed off on the desert fae, and in time she renounced her evil ways. No more did she feast upon the flesh of the dead, and she raised their children to revere Allah and the Prophet.

    This member of the Ahl-I-Batin is a descendant of that union, and he carries the fae blood in his veins.

    He is a man of dark complexion and brilliant green eyes. His fingers end in retractable claws, which he employs sparingly, and his teeth are sharper than most. For this reason, he rarely smiles - a habit born of years of needing to hide his family's peculiar heritage. When Paradox afflicts him, it often manifests as deep wrinkles on his face, and the inside of his mouth grows as green as his eyes. A throwback to his distant progenitor.

    He is most at home in arid climates, but he will travel anywhere in search of fairy lore. Especially related to the djinn. A High Ritualist and man of deep Faith, he also draws upon a wealth of Crazy Wisdom, culled from his encounters with and research on the Kindly Ones. Changelings and those also of fae blood are of particular interest to him. As a scholar of theology as well as folklore, his current goal is to explore a theory that would reconcile the existence of fairies and other creatures within Allah's design.

    Magestuck: The Ascension

        66) A Dreamspeaker Spirit Smith, whose area of focus is construction and renovation of homes. A house flipper by trade, he buys run-down homes and moves into them, working to repair them. But in the act of flipping the house, he also slowly works to awaken the building's spirit. He also works to attract positive spirits, ward the building against harmful ones, and help any lingering ghosts resolve their unfinished business and move on. He rounds out his magickal improvements to the property by scrubbing away distasteful Resonance, strengthening parts of the structure against damage and the ravages of time, and layering enchantments into the walls that push occupants towards caring for the building's upkeep. When he believes his work is complete, he hands the house over to a Consor - a real estate agent, who works to make sure the property finds its way into the hands of people who will treat it well.

        Some activists within the Tradition criticize the 'speaker's approach as playing into the hands of the Syndicate, and even contributing to gentrification by raising property values. The spirit smith understands these objections, but continues nonetheless. To him, he isn't helping the Syndicate, but exploiting their economic systems for a greater purpose: spreading the influence of the spiritual world in the beating heart of "respectable" society. If the new landowners believe their house is alive, they may feel inclined to respect and care for it. (It helps that our house flipper makes the spiritually improved house known to local mages, so they can focus their efforts on the new occupants and the expansion of their beliefs.).

        As for gentrification, the spirit smith likes to seek out the aid of local youths, when it comes to fixing the houses. He can only do so much himself, and more hands makes the work go faster. This also serves two more purposes. First, it allows the smith to steer the youths of low income areas towards practical skills, and away from gangs and criminal activity. Teach them useful trades, so they can better their own lives. Secondly, the on-the-job training isn't just limited to home repair. The most promising workers get turned onto the wonders of magick, and the reverence for spirits. He never has enough time to truly initiate these youths into magic, Awakened or otherwise. But he can get them interested, so their training can be continued by local Dreamspeakers (or members of other magical groups, like the Verbana or Bata'a, if Dreamspeakers are scarce or uninterested). Through this, he can do his part in spreading the shamanistic Paradigm, and undermine the Technocracy's ideological stranglehold.

        Finally, he serves the Traditions more directly by occasionally providing them with properties suited for turning into Chantries, in those places where they don't otherwise exist. Spiritually awakened and warded buildings make for better places for mages to meet. The spirit smith hopes to one day learn the secrets of creating Nodes, that he might make this process all the more effective.

        If he's guilty of one major failing, it's his rage when his projects get mistreated. He travels extensively between home flips, looking for new properties. When he's in town, he'll check in on previous flips. When he can't, he'll contact local Dreamspeakers (or anyone with Spirit 2) to check in on it. If the house spirit says that its owners are bad owners - neglecting basic maintenance or disrespecting the home - the smith gets angry. Depending on how terrible the new owners are, he may decide that a lessons need to be taught.

            67) A police profiler, his job was to understand perpetrators - how they thought - in order to better catch them. He attracted the Technocracy's attention after helping stop a notorious, occult-themed serial killer. They saw in him an acute mind, and an ability to grasp complex subjects quickly, given enough research material. So they set him to work learning about the mentality and methodology of Reality Deviants, so as to combat their "menace". He was afforded access to materials otherwise forbidden to regular Union personnel: the body of occult lore captured from the Technocracy's superstitionist enemies.

            Obviously, the Union would keep an eye on him, to ensure his burgeoning expertise in mystic theory didn't blossom into Unmutual practice. Unfortunately for them, they should have been watching more closely.

            The profiler - soon a knowledgeable NWO Extraordinary Citizen - found his specialty in short order: Hermetic theory. Soon, however, the Union couldn't shield him from the obvious: they weren't fighting crackpots and charlatans, but people with real, honest-to-goodness POWER. Power they got, seemingly, from adherence to superstition. He accepted that these Reality Deviants were employing some sort of Science they did not understand...but it made his interest in the theory of magic turn into a minor obsession. He could better do his job, he'd say to his superiors, if he knew better what the mystics were doing with their deviant Science. That meant more books, better reports, access to ritual tools. His immediate superior almost denied the request on the spot and sent him to Room 101...but the profiler's sincerity came out in the superior's Mind senses. And they DID have a group of Hermetics giving them trouble. They needed their expert, and they needed his understanding unclouded.

            And they just happened to have captured one. The profiler could certainly learn much from a live subject to interrogate. The Union would simply need to watch their man more closely.

            The profiler got to peruse the "wizard's" captured effects. His books of numerology, his seal pendents, his magic wand. When the profiler got his meeting, the first thing he asked was "why is your wand like this, and not like something else?" In his mind, he was just trying to take shortcuts on research - he wouldn't need to comb through texts in eight different languages and three ciphers, if the expert would divulge his secrets. The wizard, surprised and intrigued, used the profiler's curiosity against the Union. After extensive discussions, over multiple sessions, the Hermetic finally planted a dangerous seed in the Technocrat's head: Hermetic Magick was a cerebral, academic art, but the codes and protocols and theories were all in service of something less explicable. The symbology existed to convey deeper truths, that could not be summed up in words. Hermeticism was at once systematic and sublime. The only way to truly understand a wizard's mentality...is to perform the rituals yourself.

            This germ of an idea incubated in the profiler's mind, to the point where he couldn't sleep. For three nights, he lay awake, tortured by the niggling doubt. A part of his mind rebelled - strongly - against pursuing Deviant ideas into practice. Another part, however, couldn't stop thinking about it. So, seizing his notes and the ritual tools he had available, he created a makeshift ritual space in his living room. He worked until dawn, performing the rite. Just to see what all the fuss was about.

            To his surprise, the magick worked. Worked better than he could have imagined. As a blackout was thrust upon the neighborhood, he felt an ecstatic revelation. And something in his head cracked. He had forced himself into an Awakening, and broke his Conditioning in the process. Through his new awareness, he realized that the Union would almost certainly punish him for what he did. Take away every ounce of knowledge of Hermetic High Ritual Magick, and make him a perfectly obedient, orthodox Technocrat.

            So he did what any loyal Union member would do in that situation: he grabbed as much wizard crap as he could, returned to the Construct, and personally broke the wizard out of his cell. It was a harrowing hour, sneaking through the building and knocking out what guards he needed to. If the Union had been as attentive as they should have been - if they hadn't also been distracted by events elsewhere - his rash actions would have failed. Instead, he got to the wizard, returned his tools, and asked only one thing in return: for the Hermetic to take the profiler with him.

            He could not go back. He didn't want to be a boring bureaucrat. He couldn't let all his progress along the Path be wasted.

            Now he's a member of the Order of Hermes, House Fortunae. He couldn't be happier.

                68) A short woman - max four feet tall - with dyed pink hair and pigtails. She's a mechanic, with a sardonic sense of humor and a complex about her height.                         70) As a newly Awakened Tormented Widderslainte, you feel you should consider yourself lucky. You Awakened when defending yourself – with your then meager skills learned at a self-defense course – from a group of thugs who first tried to rob you, and then to murder you. Your Avatar's laughter served as a lightning fast inspiration, and working together with it you managed to pinpoint the weaknesses of the thugs, and you scorched their flesh when you filled a broken-off bottle with your sadism and strength. You killed all five of them; however, the day after the next day you noticed you were already shadowed, and in a week a trio of no-nonsense looking government agents went into your apartment at night, and were explaining to you in vague terms how in your case there is no need for evidence, as your very existence might as well be an admission of guilt. Fortunately, you were saved by an Ahriman of Nephandi bursting in, their Spells disarming the Technocrats before the Nephandi killed them with guns and knives. The attractive and beautiful leading Adsinistrati was very glad to find out that you're a Tormented Widderslainte, and even more glad to confirm that you are Awakened - she and her Ahriman were running on a tight time schedule and with limited materiel, and thus she wasn't sure if she would have had the time and the means to torture you into Awakening.

                        The Ahriman barely got a few sentences of explaining to you that they are part of the Nephandi and that you're a Widderslainte, when they sensed a follow-up team of Technocrats, with support, arriving. The Nephandi immolated the bodies of the killed ones in your room, and cast a Spell on your apartment so that it wouldn't be visible to searches of the Technocracy – at least for the next few days. The Adsinistrati hugged you, told you not to tell anyone anything about the Nephandi or that you're a Widderslainte, and went out with her fellow Nephandi to avoid the Technocrats. You haven't seen them since. The Technocracy – at least as far as you are aware of – has not bothered you.

                        You lived a nervous few days of not knowing how to proceed – experimenting with patterns and shades gleaned on the city streets, and mystic outlooks suggested by what was written on internet websites. On the fifth day of you trying to get a handle of your new existence – and you worrying about the safety of the Nephandi that saved you – you were approached in a coffee shop by a group of four True Mages, in the early twenties; they bought you dinner and you began conversing. More out of a sense of wanderlust and a desire to meet someone new than because of anything else, you spent a night with them. They pegged you as being of the Dynamic Essence, and you went along with it. They suspected nothing about you and the Nephandi, and didn't even mention the word. One of the True Mages calls herself a Hermetic, another – a Verbena, and two of them refer to themselves as Hollow Ones.

                            71) Coming from a working class background, this boorish man used a combination of aggressive financial action, a spontaneous Awakening, and a heaping dose of dumb luck to turn a succession of business startups into unexpected successes. Which he sold, acquiring large quantities of money very quickly. As such, it was inevitable he would be scooped up by the Syndicate...despite him being a poor fit, even in a Convention that keeps fingers in organized crime.

                            Like a crude combination of Loadsofmoney, the speakers from Whales, and Kill La Kill's singular Dosh King, he is a man with a rich man's bank account, a poor man's understanding of how to use it, and a Trump's undeserved cockiness and unchecked belligerence. He eschews the tailored suits of his Syndicate peers, instead usually running around in jeans, t-shirts, and a sports jacket (bearing the logo of the team he owns). Intimidation and throwing around cash is his primary means of getting things done, along with a set of beleaguered assistants who "are good at getting things done" in his mind (and so, because of his assurance in his money's ability to secure competence, they inevitably are). Even his habit of "dressing down" can have an effect on those around him, albeit not in the way that clothes usually aid Syndicate reps. If nothing else works, he can always rely on swinging his weight around, both with his assets, his fists, and his ever-ready wad of cash (this time, as a literally weapon to slap people up with or to establish his credentials as someone with the power of money...loads of money).

                            Naturally, other members of the Convention loathe him. To other members of the Syndicate, he is an embarrassment, a personal insult, a crime against decency, a betrayal of the Convention's values, and a clear liability. Most Syndicate members distance themselves from the idiot in their midst as much as possible, quietly hoping his many character defects will eventually bring him low. Some even keep tabs on him, in order to both make sure that his inevitable reckoning doesn't damage the rest of the Syndicate, and more covertly to make sure he can't use his own Adjustments to dodge "justice". If that means letting him squander his money until nothing remains, that cash will doubtless be put to better use by the free market (while his business assets are quietly bought up by respectable Syndicate ops, of course). If that means letting the superstitionists destroy him, that makes him a useful shield for "proper" Syndicate business. If that means preventing him from escaping police investigations or civil suits, it can only serve to enhance the public's confidence in the power of the system to correct economic abuses (because Dosh knows the Syndicate needs a win in that arena). If that means letting him die in a knife fight because his incautious Adjustments forced violent market correction (or his belligerence and lack of caution got him a knife in the back when he wasn't looking), well...

                            ...let's be honest, most Syndicate members wouldn't cry at his obituary.

                            Of course, this particular man is nothing if not ballsy and stubborn. It may not be as simple as letting him bring doom upon himself. He's not a brilliant man, by any means, but he CAN be cunning at times. And ferocious like a wild animal if backed into a corner.

                                72) An Iteration X AI programmer and cyberneticist, who installs intelligent systems to run Technocracy compounds. With the lifting of the ban on Iterators learning Dimensional Science, he has become fascinated with matters in the other dimension (the so-called "Umbra" in superstitionist jargon). Upon discovering that objects and even buildings become reflected on the other side - and that the two intertwine - he began seeing possibilities. Their awareness is, by default, inert, but can be awakened with the proper (or improper, in the case of the superstitionist) application of Dimensional Science.

                                Testing a hypothesis, the Iterator has been working to install a sophisticated AI into the structure of an old, established Construct. But not just in the physical walls of the building, but into the pseudo-physical walls in the other dimension, and linking the two. Creating a potent, logical mind, loyal to the Technocratic Union, and marrying it to the other-dimensional presence that has already married itself to the building. Awakening the building, so to speak, but in such a way as to not just give it a "soul", but a mind. One more to the Union's purposes. At the same time, creating a bastion of logic in the other dimension, from which further Dimensional Science missions and experiments can be launched.

                                Essentially, the programmer and cybernetics expert is trying to give the building's spirit a cybernetic implant.

                                    73) "The Fiddler In The Mask". "The Jester Prince". "The Man Of A Thousand Masks". In truth, though, it's not known if this mage is a man, a woman, both, or neither. Their skin never shows, their outline androgynous. Their manner and bearing mutable as the tide, changing with each exchange of their ubiquitous masks. They often come in a variety of costumes - to match what they wear on their face - but their seeming default is a form-fitting set of dancer's pants, pointed shoes, a long-sleeved turtleneck, fashionable gloves, and material covering their head, neck, and face. One wonders if they aren't wearing a full body suit, like something from a stage dancer. They are also verifiably an accomplished vocal performer, capable (through skill, technology, or magic) to change their voice at the drop of a hat, mimicking a large range of sounds and accents. Judging their true identity through voice is, therefore, impossible; it changes as easily as their change their face.

                                    For indeed, the Fiddler is a shapeshifter, using their masks to assume different forms where needed. Perhaps the person one met at an art gallery or the supermarket was them in disguise. Masks also allow them to call upon a variety of different powers, including fading from notice, moving at a rapid pace, taking to the air, etc. Their abilities are enhanced by the use of stage magic techniques, their vocal ability, graceful movements, and their supernatural use of the violin. A bard, actor, and magician, the Fiddler considers the whole world a stage play, and thus Affectation is as good as Reality. Too bad about the lost script, though...

                                    Some mages chalk the Masked Fiddler as being mad. A Marauder, and nothing more. More open-minded Awakened, however, understand that this mutability of identity and garish performance are just as valid a means of obtaining power. And that any mage will seem mad, when examined from the outside. Indeed, the Fiddler is not a Marauder or weighed down with Quiet. Though they may be subject to madness, their is yet method in it. The Fiddler understands that people - the Actors in this farcical stage play - wear masks in their day to day lives. Personas they project to the wider world. How the Fiddler differs is that they do not take to this idea of a "True Self" that somehow lies "Behind The Mask". There is no such thing. Or, at least, whatever lies behind the masks humans wear is so ineffable as to be impossible to truly grasp, save by the transcendently enlightened (those who have achieved Ascension, who have discovered how to leave the stage and became part of the audience). For all practical concerns, there IS no True Self; it's Masks all the way down. Even when a person is alone, they still project a Persona to themselves, for they do not know how NOT to play a role.

                                    For this reason, the Fiddler sees every Persona - every pattern of behavior and combination of word and deed - as being equally "True" to the person's being. Every Mask one wears, every role they play, every performance they give is AS accurate a measure of who they are. In the Fiddler's case, they simply choose to assume as many roles as needed to make sure the Play runs smoothly and has a good conclusion. They seek to work within society to better it; and they see the best way of bettering the lot of Actors (that is, the whole of mankind) is to make them realize the fact they have forgotten. That they are, in fact, Actors playing roles. That it's all a production, and can be altered to make the story better.

                                    Sometimes that means working to expose the nature of mutable reality to people, like other Awakened do. Sometimes, that means exposing more "mundane" facts, such as a politicians indiscretions or crimes, or removing their veil of anonymity that allows random acts of cruelty on the Internet to go unpunished or unjudged. It means exposing the Masks we wear as masks, and making everyone aware of which Actors are pulling multiple, unsavory roles in the drama of human experience.

                                    Because even if all the world's a stage and all of life a fiction, that doesn't mean it isn't important. These performances - and the playing of them - matter a great deal to the Actors who pour their hearts and souls into them. Even if it is a story, that story matters. Should it not, then, be made a story WORTH telling and a performance WORTH putting on? While the Play should never be boring - which is why the Fiddler chooses such a colorful path - it can still also be fulfilling and fun for everyone involved. Even if that means certain Actors should be called out on their abuses.

                                        74) When you're a young, hopeful girl in Japan, and you suddenly Awaken, what do you do? Become a Magical Girl, of course!

                                        That's what she did for three years, between ages 13 and 16. Prancing around with molded plastic scepters and other accessories, transforming her street clothes into frilly dresses, righting minor wrongs in the name of hope, justice, and friendship. Maybe it was the right climate that many of her Effects got past Paradox (and what Paradox she experienced wasn't as harsh, or she powered through its manifestations). Maybe there really WAS something to the idea of having supportive Friends (What's this background called? Cult?). Maybe she just got really lucky for a long time.

                                        Whatever the case, her good fortune eventually ran out, and she got in way over her head. Facing a foe that took their conflict much more seriously than she was used to, the Magical Girl limped away with a debilitating injury and crushed spirits. She nearly died, and her enemy went on unopposed. "Disheartened" did not do the feelings she felt justice. She lost hope in her crusade. And while she was laid up in hospital, recovering, she slowly lost contact with her friends. For a Magical Girl like her, Hope and Friendship weren't just hollow platitudes. They were the source of her power. The ties she forged with others gave her strength, while powerful emotions allowed her to push beyond the limits of what was possible. Cut off from her established relationships, and in poor spirits, she lost access to her magic. Even when she recovered, little of her magical might followed.

                                        So she spent the remainder of her adolescence a normal girl. She went to high school and then to university, majoring in 3D modeling. She got a job, and her own apartment. She even had a couple of romantic relationships that ended up going nowhere. When she used magick at all, it was in simple, utilitarian ways. Her passion simply wasn't in it anymore, and because of her Focus, since hobbled her. Mage outsiders - if they could see her or know her plight - would have wondered if she was on the verge of sliding back into a sleep-walking state.

                                        Mages, however, do not enjoy boredom for long. They are cursed to live interesting lives. Fate conspired to put the former Magical Girl into a position to witness a violent crime in action. With help from the "proper authorities" a long way away, and lives on the line, she couldn't stop herself from intervening. For whatever reason, fortunately, she never got OUT of the habit of keeping a small plastic wand with a big yellow star on the end inside her purse. By the end of the day, when the adrenaline died down, her heart and mind were racing still. She didn't sleep much that night. Couldn't possibly. Not when that crime she stopped was, obviously, a small part in something much larger. Something the police seemed strangely unwilling to investigate.

                                        She had to get back in the game. Not just out of need to correct a problem she saw before her, but for very personal reasons. She couldn't sit back passively when she had the power to make real changes in the world, for the better. Nor could she simply fade back into her old routine. The Magical Girl had to come out of retirement...no matter how embarrassing the idea sounded, after years of being a Serious Adult. There was nothing for it, of course. Waving around plastic weapons in colorful clothing, while openly proclaiming her intent to punish evil in the name of justice, were simply the only ways she knew HOW to do magick. She would need to work on that, and work UP to going full Magical Girl again.

                                        A thrift store sewing machine and a 3D printer would help her get back in the game. Now if only she could find some new friends. Or, maybe, see what her old friends were doing...

                                            75) A Virtual Adept cyborg, with a specialization in Mind and Spirit. One day, she did battle with a malignant spirit program - a Digital Devil - wreaking havoc on the Digital Web. After a long, tense encounter, she thought she'd managed to delete the ephemeral virus...until she realized the devil had taken refuge in her brain implant.

                                            With quick thinking and a frantic bit of hacking (and a bit of luck, in that she already had systems in place for isolating malware that got in her BMI), she turned the digital devil's hiding place into a snare. A score of her implant's internal memory was ceded - ground lost in the bit war - but she managed to contain the beast inside. Mostly.

                                            She's the Virtual Adept who walks around with an imprisoned cyber demon in her head. And it is not happy. While it can't take control of her, it can sometimes marshal enough processing power to haunt her with visual and auditory hallucinations. She dare not try to go in and erase the beast, lest the attempt break its shell and let it run rampant. Nor can she readily replace the hardware in her skull, and couldn't trust that the switch over wouldn't somehow give the beast a chance to escape to the net or another machine. So at the moment, she's stuck with the malign passenger; she is its host, and its jailer. Lucky her.

                                                She's the Virtual Adept who walks around with an imprisoned cyber demon in her head. And it is not happy. While it can't take control of her, it can sometimes marshal enough processing power to haunt her with visual and auditory hallucinations. She dare not try to go in and erase the beast, lest the attempt break its shell and let it run rampant. Nor can she readily replace the hardware in her skull, and couldn't trust that the switch over wouldn't somehow give the beast a chance to escape to the net or another machine. So at the moment, she's stuck with the malign passenger; she is its host, and its jailer. Lucky her.
                                                Thinking about it, I can think of a way to progress this character. If she ever does get rid of that digital devil, she would suddenly find a large amount of core memory vacant. And while a smart (or lazy) Virtual Adept might just use that space for "normal" processing, a more adventurous soul might opt to keep using it as-is.

                                                With just a few modifications, that empty space could provide room for a visiting Digital Web denizen. Hopefully, one that is less evil than the previous occupant, and who will be allowed inside for more limited periods. In essence, our Virtual Adept adds Mediumship to her list of Practices, as she allows spirits of code to ride her like a Loa rides a practitioner of Vodoun.
