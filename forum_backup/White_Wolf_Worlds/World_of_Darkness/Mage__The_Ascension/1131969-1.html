                Dreamspeakers

                + Anyone can join the Dreamspeakers and they are perhaps the easiest Paradigm to share with anyone else. This is partially because they are a Tradition in name only. They're actually just a collection of a dozen loosely aligned chantries, Crafts, and master-apprentice relations.

                + They really-really hate the assumption everyone is from a "primitive" society.

                + Dreamspeakers are probably the mages least hung up about paradigm since just about everything is achieved by personal vision quests and communication with spirits. They are quiet comfortable with people spewing complete crazy and going, "Yeah, it's possible the universe works that way."

                Oddly enough, they have the smallest number of Marauders.

                Euthanatos

                + Euthanatos who opt out of the Ascension War often spend their time helping ghosts achieve Transcendance, caring for the elderly, or comforting the bereaved. There's actually a lot larger number of Euthanatos than the Union (or Traditionalists) believe who are closer to the Sleepers than most.

                + Progenitors almost never defect but if they do, it's usually after talking to the Euthanatos.

                Order of Hermes

                + While they'd never admit it, the Order of Hermes actually came up with a lot of the Void Engineers current physics models about the universe, infinity, and multiverse. They're genuinely confused about who the hell leaked them, whether it's a good thing or a bad thing, and why the Union would do it.

                + The Celestial Choir is a sycretic peace and love religion. The Syndicate is fundamentally pro-science. So, who the hell is exerting so much influence through faith-based advisors and massive anti-science corportions? This is actually the Order of Hermes who wield a staggering amount of power through minions they basically hand stock portfolios.

                The Traditionalists would be furious about this. The Technocracy actually assumes this is the main purpose of the Traditions as they couldn't wrap their head around the fact it's 90% just people studying.

                The Sons of Ether

                + You can't qualify as a Son of Ether unless you have a Intelligence 3 plus Science 3 score. You may love steampunk but you actually get tested on your ability to talk about the subject once the goofy costumes come off.

                + There's an order of Sons of Ether which exist for the purpose of killing "scientists" who endanger the population at large with pseudo-science. This organization is very small but is composed of those who have seen what happens with placebos, homopathetic therapies, and general quackery getting spread to the public. The hypocrisy of this group and its similarity to the Technocracy bothers a lot of people.

                Virtual Adepts

                + A substantial number of Virtual Adepts do actually believe the world is an illusion or computer simulation. This requires a Merit called "Plato's Cave 3pts" which allows them to discard Foci much easier than other Technomancers.

                + There is a tabletop gaming contingent of Virtual Adepts who don't use computers at all. Do not touch their dice.

                + The Virtual Adepts include a staggering number of misogynist sexist assholes. The Etherites have actually gotten better at this while the VA have gotten much worse.


                Author of Cthulhu Armageddon, I was a Teenage Weredeer, Straight Outta Fangton, Lucifer's Star, and the Supervillainy Saga.

                        (1) In my WoD games, the Hollow Ones don't exist. I've never really liked them, and it doesn't -really- make sense that the goth subculture (any more than another subculture) would have a whole pseudo-tradition. When 1st Ed came out, my group just assumed that the Hollow Ones had been included to appease the goth-leanings of the White Wolf fanbase and jettisoned them. The original Hollow Ones Tradition book didn't help matters either, as it portrayed the group as adolescents that were constantly moaning or trying to "out-cool" each other. I understand that other people like the HO, and that's fine...they're just not for me.

                        (2) Rather than the original lumping together of the various shamanistic traditions in "Dream Speakers", I have their common paradigm as being the Weaver/Wyld/Wyrm (a la WWtA)...in the same way that the CC have "The One" as a common element of their various paradigms.

                        (3) I prefer to emphasise the players as protagonists (rather than members of boggling large traditions), so I tend to drastically reduce numbers of Awakened (both Tech and Trad), though this is probably the result of our country's actual population. Our game takes place in Australia, so as our city, Melbourne, has only 3.9 million people...the players are, in fact, the only tradition representatives in the city.

                        (4) In line with (3), very few mages on "Earth" have Arete 4, and still fewer have Arete 5. The high-powered "archmages" are all in other Umbral realms (e.g. Horizon realms), which are unreachable. Doissetep is completely radio silent. (I can't go into more detail here, as my players are likely to see this thread.)

                        (5) Whilst there are vampires, there aren't any sleeping antediluvians. (However, in VtM games, they -do- exist, but the Technocracy/Nephandi/etc don't exist). I feel that with WoD crossovers, you can end up with too much top-end power overlap.

                                In my head-canon, the philosophy of Focus as outlined in M20 (Focus is Will shaped by Belief through a Practice that alters Reality) is Tradition dogma, but no more fundamentally true than the Technocracy's philosophy (which aligns more with knowledge and understanding than belief and will) or the Disparates' philosophies (which are all over the board, but tend not to be “Grand Unified Theories” of metaphysics: “we know how our magic works, and we know that your magic works; and that's what matters” is their notion of a unified theory).

                                There is no Disparate Alliance; the Disparates simply aren't capable of anything approaching that level of cooperation. The Traditions are more or less as described in Guide to the Traditions, and the Technocracy is more or less as depicted in the Revised Convention Books. The main exceptions are that the Syndicate's Special Projects Division has nothing to do with Pentex or the Wyrm, but is more along the lines of the protagonists of Fringe: scientists who would be technomancers if not for their loyalty to the Union. And the Dimensional Anomaly has largely subsided, leaving the Void Engineers in an even more precarious position with respect to Threat Null.

                                The Nephandi and the Marauders aren't factions so much as infections; and they're joined by a third infection, the Timori (sometimes called Skeptics or Banishers). Each of these three infections is invited with an enhanced and permanent version of Quiet: Marauders have permanent Madness, Nephandi have permanent Morbidity, and Timori have permanent Denial.

                                            Originally posted by Muad'Dib View Post
                                            Can you write more about them? How does a True Mage become one of the Timori?
                                            The key is “One True Way”-ism. A mage who gets so wrapped up in his way of doing things that he starts to be incredulous of other approaches starts to develop Denial of everything that doesn't match his worldview. Eventually, he gets so stuck in a rut that his Denial starts to become permanent in much the same way that a Marauder's Madness is permanent. From that point on, any magick that he witnesses that he considers to be Vulgar generates an extra dot of Paradox for every point of Denial he has. This is true even if the magick is in fact Coincidental.

                                                    Metaphysics
                                                    - Paradigms are paradigms; Mage's universe isn't arbitrary but it is polysemous - there are multiple equally-accurate methods of explicating it, and disputes between mage factions are not over "facts" but over matters of principle and ideology.

                                                    - Will is Crowleyan and reality is a term of art - the world is what we make of it, in much the same sense as this holds true in the real world.. "Magick is the act of using Will to change reality" is a true statement but only because "magick", "will", and "reality" have technical terms that make most of the complaints about "consensual reality" basically nonsensical.

                                                    (I think these two just are canonical, but I've met so many players that would swear up and down that they are not that I don't take them for granted.)

                                                    Factions
                                                    - The Union doesn't use the common framework of "good field agents, evil hypocrite management, some of whom are Nephandi" - its problems unfold as a consequence of its ideology, and they take place uniformly up and down the chain of command. Most everyone in the Union thinks they're doing what's best. Field agents are more likely to encounter things that challenge their views, in the same way that Traditional Masters tend to be cloistered and stubborn compared to disciples. As noted above, the Union's not wrong about the metaphysics - they have an ideological issue, and serve as a foil for other well-meaning vanguardist mages (Order of Hermes, etc.)

                                                    - The Traditions have a crisis of leadership; most Masters have calcified (ironically mirroring their truly ancient Techno counterparts) and are content to reside in Horizon realms and issue pronouncements and directives to disciples. These Masters aren't planning to blow up the internet and de-invent the automobile; rather they are caught in the notion that their occult politicking in the Horizon is all that is necessary to safeguard the principles of Ascension. This is akin to the pre-Craftmason dereliction of duty in the DA period: Most Traditional Masters think that using magic to save the world is something they can do in their libraries, without ever meeting any Sleepers, and they can find high-minded philosophical excuses to never roll up their sleeves and actually help anyone on Earth.

                                                    - The Adepts have a small but stalwart core of traditional anarchists, survivors of the retreat from the Union. These serious-minded types often work as mentors to temper the Adepts once their initial "wild disciple" period is over, but most other mages mistake them from Hermetics - they're academics and philosophers, and while their knowledge of hypertech mathematics is unrivaled, they don't care too much about the internet except as a tool for social organization.

                                                    - Hermetic House Luxor gets an overhaul and is back-ported a century and change; they're a masonic occult society that taps into some of the american exceptionalist ideological and mystical movements - they operated in the 18th through 19th centuries and basically though the Americas were Atlantis. Some of the Craftmasons that escape the purge end up with them, but Luxor never rises to high prominence and a lot of its membership jumps ship to the Etherites when they arrive. (I run the occasional colonial-era Mage one-shot, so have thought a bit about what sort of factions I'd imagine were running around then.)

                                                    - When the Dreamspeakers did a mass walk-out, many of them stayed walked-out but continued to cooperate. This means that there are shamans (and lineages, fellowships, and societies of shamans) that are members of the Dreamspeakers but not members of the Council of Traditions. This external membership contains some very respected Masters, and while they're not hostile to the Trads, they don't offer them any help or support, either. If the Council pisses the Dreamspeakers off, there's a real chance they'll just fully secede as a large, potent Craft.

                                                        The Spheres
                                                        These are a conceptual framework that the traditions created so that they can discussed magic with each other without having to fully understand each others paradigms, nobody actually thinks they are casting a forces effect. A hermetic believes he is casting a Creo Ignem effect, a dreamspeaker is using a fire spirit and a SoE is using a 50GW plasma rifle. They only use the sphere forces to talk to each other about what they are doing. Hence it makes no sense to have a master of <Sphere> beyond the fact that certain mages are very good at certain stuff and spheres are how we talk about it. So a chemist SoE might be called a master of forces because of the explosions he can so easily and cheerfully create, but amongst the SoE, they'd have their own terms (chemist for instance). While a mage with forces 5 might be able to throw a fireball and a lightning bolt, his paradigm might actually only allow him to do one of those.

                                                            In my version of Mage, the Spheres are an innovation of the Traditions, and are used primarily by them: it's part and parcel of their philosophy. Individual Traditions generally have one or two Sphere variants representing special insights they have into the workings of those Spheres; but they never go beyond Sphere variants.

                                                            The Technocracy has its own set of Fields that are similar to the Spheres but distinct from them (Space, Time, Void; Energetics, Materials, Systems; Data, Mind, Utility); they don't have variants, as that goes against their “One Truth” philosophy.

                                                            Meanwhile, most of the Disparates have their own organizing principles: the Sisters of Hypollyta use the four Seasons while the Templars invoke Archangels and the Solificati wield alchemical processes, for instance. The Hollow Ones are an exception to this: they explicitly use the same Spheres as the Traditions, a holdover from their long association.
