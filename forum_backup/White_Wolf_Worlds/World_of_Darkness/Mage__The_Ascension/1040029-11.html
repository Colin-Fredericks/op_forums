    Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post

    Other game lines do not get to dictate rules to Mage, nor does Mage get to dictate terms to them. If Wraith says something that conflicts with Mage's setting or theme, Mage wins (for Mage games anyways). That's common sense. It's also stated explicitly.

    Subjective Reality, however it actually works, is one of the themes of Mage; it goes to the very heart of Mage's postmodernist bent. It does not play very well with other game lines or histories. Often those games have to contradict Mage just to maintain their own themes but that makes no difference to Mage. Cite Changeling or Werewolf all you want, they may even be informative, but if there is a contradiction, Mage wins.

    That doesn't mean that Mage has to be 100% Subjective Reality*, but it does mean that we can reject anything from other lines that say it can't be.
    Although (and I'm writing this as a shamelessly pro-crossover fan), when I have an explanation which doesn't contradict the other lines and one which does, I choose the former, because, well, Occam's razor. An explanation which have to ignore too much things from the wider WoD is less likely in my eyes than one that enables more things.

    Yes that approach comes from the viewpoint that the gamelines aren't existing in their own exclusive universe, but parts of the larger communal setting instead. I know, the preferences of authors on this was different, but I think the official approach to the setting was closer to mine, with the exclamation that you always exclude things if you want, or rather you don't have to use the other lines' books. Nevertheless, the majority books of every line were written with the other lines in mind and referred again and again, I think. For example, Wraith is being referred in the Euthanatos tradbook.

    If nothing worked, then let's think!

                        The Technocracy is an overwhelming presence in Mage; they've literaly built the modern world. In VtM and even WtA, at tops, outside the regular foes (Sabbat, Independent clans, anarchists, Camarilla, rivals inside the sect, or the Wyrm paws for Werewolf, and the eventual Weaver or spirit foe beside rival Garou or Fera), the most you could expect would be a mortal Hunter, or some Hunter organization like the inquisition. There is no vast conspiracy of Mages with vast resources on top of it's supernatural capacities hunting kindred or Garou in those games. Tipically at least. You can make it, of course, but that's not how the scenario truly works for those lines.

                        There is no such a thing as "Consensus" for the other lines either. Nor do they have subjective realities. In fact, there isn't even the effort about it. Werewolf has an Umbral landscape with an objective reality; no Horizon Realms on those. The Umbra for Mage can't even be the same, otherwise the game won't function too well.

                        The other splats don't present technology as inherently magic either; that's a Mage exclusively thing too. On contrary, technologies on many occasions are presented as anti-magic, as a "weaver-thing" that supress all the wonders of magic.

                        The other splats pay no concern about humans beliefs; it isn't important at all. To Mage, it is the most important thing.

                        To truly mix all splats... Well, first of all, you'd have to take many of the MAGE and put it above the rest. Meaning: yes, Hit Marks hunt down those sabbat cotteries when they find them. Yes, technology is magik. And yes, belief shapes reality. Without that overriding of the other splats, you're not really making crossovers with Mages, you're just putting humans with spheres as interesting NPCs or PCs.

                                                        It is not maimed, it is clarified. Subjective viewpoints can exist within an objective reality but objective perspectives cannot exist within a subjective reality. The nature of the subjective perspectives of Changelings, Fera, Kindred, KJ, Mage, Wraiths, etc can exist in an objective reality (much like the subjective perceptions of individual humans can exist within an objective world) because there are common foundations to reality. In a subjective reality, however, the powerful groups are more correct than the powerless groups because their perceptions are dominant.

                                                        In an objective reality, slavery is wrong and has always been wrong, regardless of the opinions of the powerful groups, because slavery oppresses human being with innate rights. In a subjective reality , however, slavery is right when the powerful groups say it is right and wrong when the powerful groups say it is wrong, and when the powerful groups say slavery is right, slaves are subhumans that have no rights. The objective reality just makes more sense, from an ethical (and a scientific viewpoint), than a subjective reality.
