        At the end of the day, I think it's as much about setting expectations for what a Storytelling game is "meant" to be as anything else.
        WoD began, at least in part, as a direct rebellion against the fantasy RPGs of the time that were more freeform board games than role-playing. Game rules as a lose facilitator for stories of personal exploration. Dark and edgy and punk, but also processing anxiety and trauma, exploring identity and boundaries in the safety of fiction.

        In that context, playing the role of a Nephandus is categorically unhealthy. Dark characters? Manipulative characters? Even outright evil characters? All in-bounds. Even the nastiest Tzimisce or Baali is a tool for exploring personal horror.

        But Nephandi really are meant to be something else. Mage's is the horror of being in a world too small for your ambitions. If you don't see why getting into the headspace of an ontological nihilist whose greatest ambition is manipulating all other people into hopelessness and self-destruction is a different level of darkness from a villain "just" trying to destroy the world or murder people or whatever... I envy you.

        Which is all to say, if you *don't* want to play Mage as that type of self-reflective game, that's fine! But I can't fault designers who intended to create a certain experience for not supporting the idea of playing a character that, if also represented as intended, would not be okay to experience that way.

                                                            This got me thinking. However improbable or difficult, would it at least be possible for a Mage, as part of an elaborate but genuinely altruistic scheme to save/rebuild the world, to go Nephandus for access to their world-ending power, if he thought another Mage he trusted implicitly could take advantage of the last nearly-formless moments of Reality to catalyze a new and better one?

                                                            Obviously his Avatar would have none of it, so it would probably take (at least) powerful Mind magic or the like to compartmentalize or excise the parts of him that knew it was an attempted double-cross. And because of that, the resulting Nephandus could *not* be counted on to pull punches or otherwise aid his trustee of his own volition. But maybe he could bind himself ahead of time in ways that would make him somewhat less efficient? What would it take to wipe the Avatar's memory?

                                                            For all purposes the Nephandus is still a Nephandus and will act, think, etc like one; you could say he's not even the same Mage, just one who has been manipulated(/sacrificed) by the Mage he used to be.
