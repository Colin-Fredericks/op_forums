    Microscopic Vision

    11-26-2024, 08:18 PM
    What would be involved in using magick to give oneself microscopic sight? I haven't been able to find an example of this in any of the source material available to me.

    This seems like it would fall under Correspondence, but all examples I've read involve shifting perceptions between locations, not altering the scale or focus of perception beyond innate capabilities.

    If a mage were to start looking at things on a sub-microscopic scale, that would probably involve a conjunctional effect based on the sort of thing you're looking at, as you're getting down to the nitty-gritty of quintessential patterns there (and how they perceive these things would depend upon their personal paradigm.)

          Truly microscopic vision would be Forces 2: you're just altering light (or others if you want to get smaller) waves like you would a set of lenses. That's what microscopes do. They adjust light to see just a small bit of area and magnify it.

          Life 1/Matter 1 could be a form of microscopic vision for techno-magic oriented mages, but it's less "see things like you would in a microscope" and more "how do filter detecting microscopic Patterns through that Focus."

              Perception effects can be skinned however is appropriate for the mage in question so as Heavy Arms indicated, you have to distinguish between the effect "manipulate the world to see things as if through a microscope" and "theme the perception effect as microscopic vision."

              A spell that actually works like a microscope is going to be a Forces 2 effect to manipulate the light, Life 3/4 to physically change the structure of the eye, or maybe Correspondence 3 to colocate perceptions in a clever way.

              A spell that is themed like a microscope is much more open and could include the Life 1/Matter 1 option. Correspondence 1 in theory should show everything in the area though I'm inclined to say that method will definitely need the Mind 1 option of processing multiple inputs.

                      These are all really good points!

                      I hadn't considered using a Forces effect to focus reflected light in order to provide a very close-up view of an object, but I see no reason why it wouldn't work, so long as it falls within the paradigm of the mage attempting it. Probably most conceptualizations of the nature of light would allow it, unless you're talking about settings in very early history. This is probably one of those uses of Forces which would require a certain amount of practice. beyond just understanding the basic idea, (I can see some damage being done to one's own eyes without a good feel for exactly what you're doing here.)

                      Enhancing one's own eyes to allow this sort of magnification would be the more difficult approach, magickally speaking, but I can see Progenitors (and some Etherites) with the required skill favoring this option. (Granted, either would probably prefer to use an actual microscope, but one doesn't always have one handy in the field.)

                      I would probably let someone do this with a Correspondence 2 effect, provided it falls into line with their own practices of magic. If a mage uses a telescope as a tool for Correspondence, for instance, the idea of exploiting the same concept "in reverse" seems fairly natural to me. A Virtual Adept who understands the world as a manifestation of information could probably justify filtering that to a much smaller scale for examination. To a Hermetic, it might simply be a matter of adjusting mathematics and symbols they use to provide this sort of focus, when making use of scrying practices.

                      When attempting to view things at molecular scales or lower, I would require an appropriate conjunctional pattern sensing effect, as that's getting down to the underling nature of things, and those subscribing to different paradigms are going to perceive these things differently. As brilliant as Epicurious and Lucretius were, and I'm convinced either or both were probably mages in the World of Darkness, not all paradigms take an atomist view of reality beyond a "practical" level. A Progenitor or Etherite might see DNA within a person's cells, but a Verbena or Dreamspeaker is likely to see a Life pattern represented quite differently.

                          If one works in fields where microscopy is common, it's a lot easier to have one on you than it used to be. Portable digital microscopes are pretty decent (at least equal to any standard compound microscope you'd find in most university non-specialty labs), and these days can Bluetooth right to your smartphone or laptop which helps a lot in portability because you cut out a lot of mass that way. Given what's commercially available, a Technocrat that does any sort of field work in biology/etc. would probably have a microscope in their kit these days. It's just not something most people ever really see... because most people aren't following around the folks that do stuff like fecal smears to monitor parasite loads of managed herds.

                          Not really insightful to the mage side of things, but it at least helps calibrate what sort of existing tech is out there for appropriate mages to consider using normal tech/enhanced tech over directly using magic.

                                          Oh! That's another possibility I hadn't thought of! Aside from certain paradigms adding a bit of Spirit to any sort of magic, which M20 deals with a bit, I can easily see a Verbenna or Dreakspeaker using some sort of rote to share the perceptions of a much smaller creature for this purpose. It would be somewhat like forming a mental connection with an eagle or hawk to view the landscape from above, but instead sharing the perceptions of a mouse, or even a fly. This might add some interesting twists to it, given that sight functions somewhat differently for other lifeforms, and that may not be exactly what a practitioner of this method is accustomed to. This might not necessarily be a disadvantage, as it might notice something which the mage otherwise wouldn't even think to look for, being more accustomed to its own perspective than the mage is. "Alright, we're looking for tiny fragments of metal.." "Hey, this coat smells good! I think there's some crumbs in the pockets!"

                                          Asking a spirit to share its perceptions with you might also be interesting, and might result in shared perceptions which are more or less than what the mage intended. Especially if a spirit is using its own abilities to grant a mage this sort of vision, that's going to be colored and nuanced in a manner particular to that spirit's perceptions of things.

                                              Originally posted by quelsec View Post

                                              Distorting proportions would be in Corespondence 4. Paradigm or practice should have nothing to do with that. Keep in mind a character may think he can do what in reality he cannot.
                                              You could use some variant of Data to make it lower.
                                              Wouldn't rank 4 Correspondence deal more with actually distorting the relationships between objects and spaces, as opposed to altering one's own perspective? I understand that opinions on the nitty-gritty of how space and perception work depend on the paradigm of the individual mage, and whether they believe that the world is a hologram/illusion/representation of pure information/etc. (I love that M20 goes into a lot of detail on this to let players fiddle highly divergent ideas about it into the framework of even a single Tradition.)

                                              What I've managed to glean is that the first rank of Correspondence allows the user to understand the relationships between objects and spaces, but they're not yet able to shift those perceptions outside what they think of as their own personal perspective relative to their own body.

                                              At the second rank, they've realized that perception is no longer necessarily bound to their own personal location, relative to anything else they have enough of a mental "hold" on to make that relative to. Thus, they can choose to shift that perception 20 feet above, relative to some particular space. It's very much an "I no longer need eyes to see" thing. (Replace "see" with any other sense, if the way this is written is to be accepted.) It doesn't seem to me that matters of scale really come into it at that point, as we are in fact no longer really talking about how the human eye works, the mage having moved beyond that sort of limitation.

                                              Granted, that sort of notion is going to come more easily to certain paradigms than others, and the tools one works with are tightly bound up with paradigm, especially if you're going with M20. If a mage uses something involving lenses as a tool for Correspondence workings, the idea of shifting the scale of perception will probably come to them easily. Not necessarily so for a different mage who likes to use flames or trance states for scrying practices.
