    To take a real-world example: let's pretend Christian tradition didn't prohibit suicide. Would you expect every Christian to spend a couple of days settling their spiritual and material affairs and then kill themselves? They would believe they were going to Heaven, the most perfect state they can achieve. Why wouldn't they hasten to it?

    I don't think they would. Even if someone believes in it, I don't think people are usually ready to rush headlong into such a fundamental change in their existence. You could call it fear or a reluctance to give up power; in either case, assuming a firm belief in their next life, the desire to delay would be irrational but I think very common.

                                                        Every World of Darkness series has it's themes.

                                                        In Vampire, you have blood-drinking abominations cursed by god dealing with the cut-throat politics of the immortal as they fight off their own feral nature to try and remain civilized. Hold onto the last dregs of their humanity, or alternatively, give that up for a civilized ideal closer to what their new reality asks of them.

                                                        In Werewolf, you have shapeshifting defenders of the natural world, fighting a two front war against the forces of modernity and entropy. The system is breaking down and the enemies are at the gates. What must you do, what sacrifices must you make to save the world?

                                                        Less sure on Wraith, Changeling, and Demon, but I'm sure someone can fill in those.

                                                        In Mage? You have ordinary men or women who gain an understanding of the universe so potent they can alter the fundamental laws of reality. What do they believe? What will they use that power for? And what happens when they disagree? The concept of mage is a fundamentally human concept. It's human history flanderized beyond all belief as men and women go to war time and time again over ideology and resources. Over religion and the lack thereof. Over ethics. Sure, you get cool powers. But so do the people who think your version of cool powers are powered by satan and you need to die. Mage has no morality stat, because to do so you would need to find one morality that all mages subscribe to. Which is quite obviously, impossible.
