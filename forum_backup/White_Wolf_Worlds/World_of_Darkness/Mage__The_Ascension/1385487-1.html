                                    I've never seen the Traditions as people "united to help mankind". I see them as individuals with private goals who have formed an alliance of convenience in order to stand against a common enemy. That alliance of convenience is a description of both the Council of Nine and each individual Tradition as well.

                                    And the definition of that alliance can change at any time. At least two Traditions were once members of the Technocracy, and their involvement is only because they became political dissidents within the Union.

                                    The Traditions certainly have a code of ethics, but I think it's just as effective as other professional code of ethics in Law, Medicine, or any other group. The code allows, justifies, and even requires certain actions that could be considered wrong by other people (use your legal skills so that murderers and rapists escape justice because everyone deserves a legal defense, or use your medical skills to save the lives of murderers and rapists). And the code is also easily ignored so that criminal practitioners use their skills for self-serving purposes and exploit people who come to them for help. Not to mention general sleazy people who may not commit any violations per the ethics code, but still seem pretty sleazy without it.

                                    Professional codes of ethics tend to be things designed to create pride within the organization that they have standards, and to serve as Marketing/PR to the outside world.

                                    Power tends to corrupt, and Magick gives people a lot of power.

                                    So I've never seen them as an organization of White Hats although certain individuals might be.

                                                Akin to what Black Fox just said, a large part of the morality question for Mage is emphasized by the thematic focus of 'Hubris'. Mages by definition are proud, driven, even arrogant. They are classic heroes and villains. Embodiments of Hubris.

                                                They alter circumstances and reality itself through the sheer force of willpower and wrestling the nature of it into the shape that they see fit. Damn those who disagree, will-workers are self-righteous. Some are truly righteous as well but they must all be unswervingly dedicated to their principles to do what they do.

                                                By their very nature, this means that many of them defy the restraints of conventional morality. What is the law of Essex County to someone who can bend the so-called laws of physics? Who cares if a few people's death pave the roads to utopia?

                                                Everything in the world becomes easier to justify when you are absolutely certain of your rightness. Then you take these people and find those with loosely similar opinions regarding reality and give them a loose title to unite them all into nine collectives.

                                                Then you take these nine collectives and gather them into one political body. Each head of the leviathan intent on reigning supreme and each writhing in a different direction with only the uncaring principles of Metareality and the dubiously moral 'Hero' to hold the sword and shield against it. Chaos bound together only by necessity and conceit.

                                                This is the Council of Traditions.

                                                        With all due respect, I tend to view the “held together only by necessity and conceit” is more of a Disparate Alliance thing than a Traditions thing. Yes, the Traditions do fight among themselves; but they've got their own version of a “shared truth” that binds them together, and actually facilitates bridge building among them: “We Are All Gods in Disguise”. When the first Council of Traditions established the shared lingo that allowed mages of nine wildly different traditions to get on the same page when discussing Magick, it established a common ground that let those mages work together with an ease that they had never been able to manage before. One of their strengths is that they're inherently “ecumenical”, with their shared belief that they're all practicing the same Art; they're just going about it in different ways.

                                                        They don't always live up to that ideal; but they try to.
