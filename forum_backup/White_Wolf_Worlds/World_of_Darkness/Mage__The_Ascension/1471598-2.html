                Originally posted by Aleph View Post
                Sorry for misinterpreting your intent.

                As a matter of Focus. Focus for mages rarely represents a lessened potential - certainly not the way not having the Yearning does for Sorcerers. Gutter Magick it's a no less valid road for Ascension, Power or whatever - your magic(k) can be every bit as potent as that of a High Wizard.
                Practitioners of Gutter Magic are looked down upon by practitioners of more defined arts; Gutter Magic is basically the M20 replacement for “you don't have a paradigm, and make do with whatever scraps you manage to find”. Basically, the earlier editions' Orphans. Likewise, the Hedge Magic of HH2, Kinfolk, and GH are all described in terms along the lines of not having a cohesive worldview and foraging for scraps.

                In terms of things like the Yearning or being initiated into the Otherworld, that's not really a thing in terms of game systems; it's part of the fluff about how sorcerers take the art seriously, and invest more into it than the dabblers in Hedge Magic such as hunters and Kinfolk. The only way that sorcerers are more powerful than hedge magicians in terms of game mechanics is that they're given an initial allotment of Paths, whereas the various hedge magicians have to buy their Paths with freebie points. So for the most part, the distinction between sorcerer and hedge magician is very similar to the distinction between mage and Orphan.

                S:PoP also has a section on “Making Sorcerers More Powerful” for use by Storytellers who don't want the sorcerers falling too far behind. There's not much to it (in a nutshell, “remove fast-casting penalty if you want faster sorcerers; remove the penalty for witnesses if you want sorcerers to be able to cast more freely; and only require the highest Aspect of a casting if you want sorcerers to be more powerful”); but the point is that there are tweaks that can be made to buff sorcerers if the Storyteller feels they need buffing; and if you want a mechanical distinction between the dabblers in Hedge Magic and the professional sorcerers, you can say that once a mortal has been initiated into sorcery, she gains access to the sorcery buffs.

                That said, I've been using “dabbler” not in the sense of “only has one or two dots”, but rather in the sense of “Hedge Magic isn't what the character is primarily about”. A vampire hunter is primarily about hunting vampires; he may learn a bit of Hedge Magic to that end. But even if he's invested five dots in, say, Divination, he's still a vampire hunter first and a Diviner second. Whereas a sorcerer who hunts vampires is a sorcerer first and foremost and a vampire hunter secondarily.


                On the digression to HtR: the Enemy Books were designed with a “there are more thing in heaven and on earth than are dreamt of in your philosophy” mindset: the vampires in The Nocturnal aren't necessarily the Kindred of VtM; the werewolves in The Moonstruck aren't necessarily the Garou of WtA; and the witches in The Spellbound aren't necessarily the Awakened of MtAs or the sorcerers of, well, Sorcerer. The idea in all cases wasn't just that they were presenting a stripped down version of the other gamelines, but also that they were open enough in their design that you could confound the players who were well versed in the associated game by having your monster do things that you wouldn't see happen in the other game.

                As such, I do not consider Hunter's Enemy books to be a valid source of information about the games that inspired them.

                            To me, the mechanical reason to prefer S:PoP over HHII+K:ABA+GH is that it's more comprehensive and more consistent. More comprehensive: there are Paths in S:PoP that don't have counterparts in any of the officially published X20 books, but do appear in previous editions of Sorcerer. Using the official X20 supplements doesn't fill these holes. More consistent: compare the Path of Divination as it appears in HHII, K:ABA, and GH. 'Nuff said.

                            What's happening here is that the Hedge Magic rules in X20 have been tailored for each gameline. HHII hedge magic was written with vampire hunters in mind; K:ABA hedge magic was written with kinfolk in mind; and GH hedge magic was written with ghost hunters in mind. They were not written with a common framework, as was the case of the original “Year of the Hunter” supplements that mostly riffed off of and added Paths to what was found in Ascension's Right Hand.

                            And that lack of consistency in the X20 lines is not an oversight or a mistake. During the KS for GH, I asked about the Path of Divination being classified as a Psychic Phenomenon instead of a Hedge Path, and was told that that was a deliberate design choice.

                            That's why I don't consider the Hedge Magic systems presented in HHII, K:ABA, and GH to be parts of a single Hedge Magic system, but rather three separate magic systems that have overlapping terminology. S:PoP doesn't “correct” this; it couldn't even if it wanted to. Instead, it presents sorcerers as Hedge Magic through the lens of Mage in exactly the same way that HHII presents Hedge Magic through the lens of Vampire and K:ABA presents Hedge Magic through the lens of Werewolf (and, despite official statements about it being a general WoD supplement, GH presents Hedge Magic through the lens of Wraith).

                            The thing is, “the lens of Mage” is one that puts Focus front and center, allowing you to present sorcerers with wildly different Practices; whereas HHII and K:ABA each focus primarily on what their respective hedge magicians can do and mostly don't bother dealing with how they go about doing it.

                                            Honestly kind of agree with the Original poster's assertion that the sources described should not be used as a formal S20 update.

                                            I disagree that Sorcerer: paths of power is a good alternative though. Spop reverently sticks with the "sorcerers are to mages what ghouls are to vampires" theme sorcerer revised invented, going so far as to make it so that mages are better at making sorcerer paths than Sorcerers are.

                                            It also fully embraces many of the nerfs WOD20 has for paths in non-sorcerer-centric books. Path of alchemy for example has many of the nerfs found in the HH2 version. In revised edition, the 5th level power in path of alchemy brought it in line with mummy and possessed, in that the maximum of a stat that a human could have with magic was 8(via possession, mummy magic, or copying powers via sorcerer alchemy) and that getting to the 9-10 range requires either becoming a full splat or infernal pacts. HH2 made sorcerers no longer in line with this and I find it strange that Spop embraces this.

                                            Spop also limits the ability for psychics to learn each sorcerer disciplines. It originally took 14 xp to learn a cross discipline power at first level to represent the effort it to one to learn something outside their comfort zone, but from then on all powers were their norm XP cost. In Spop the initial cost is removed, but psychics are barred from Ever learning rituals or paths that require rituals, Forever. The system just straight up punishes you for being psychic.

                                            The new paths listed in the book also don't seem to fit. Saturnal anima/saturnal manes stick out for just how specific they are and the fact that, unlike how they portray sorcerer paths, they only have 1 effect line each. I had to take multiple looks to makes sure that they were indeed their own individual sorcerer paths and not part of something else. I've also noticed they've done similar with other paths that used to have more effect variables in previous editions, which makes the "variable effect" system of Sorcerer: Revised that they copies whole-sale rather redundant or entirely nonfunctional depending on how you look at it.

                                            All in all, I don't recommend.
