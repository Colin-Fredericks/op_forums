                                    Originally posted by Aya Tari View Post
                                    Newton didn't understand the nature of gravity either, he just mathematically described it, so does he lose science points because he did not understand curved space?
                                    There is a tremendous difference between noting a natural thing and spitballing ideas about it or noting a natural thing, sitting down, figuring it out, testing your assumptions, and finding a mathematical proof that explains what you're seeing, discarding ideas that don't make the cut of evidence or that cannot be proofed out. One is, in essence, a wild guess (even if a very precise one) - the other involves at least a proto-scientific method.

                                    Let me put it this way. If I, the bizarre wizard man that I am, notice the sun rises and set, and determine that the sun is in fact a great hurtling fire that pursues the lake that comes out when it is hiding, and from there also deduce (quite logically, with that as a base parameter) that during the waning days the moon is evaporating as water exposed to flame does, and that these waters coalesce again during the waxing, and that therefore the moon is closer to the sun during the new moon than the full moon, and also determine that this is why the tides are higher in the full moon (as the moon, a giant ocean not having evaporated, is sympathetically linked to the seas of the earth), without repeatedly testing my ideas, am I doing science?

                                    If, however, I notice the sun rises and sets and a correlation with the tides, sit down, and try and figure it out - unsuccessfully, even, because let's say I don't comprehend gravity - and manage to determine a relationship between the heights of the tide and the stage of the moon, and then create a model that can predict that relationship and attempts to explain it, then repeatedly test that model to see if it holds up, am I doing science?

                                        Boy this thing went off the rails into one of the old Mage-forum-chestnuts.

                                        I'll reiterate something from previous threads about this stuff:

                                        Reality Zones are an invention of the Order of Reason. Before the rise of the Order of Reason - as seen in DA Mage - reality just was. Mages didn't think about Consensus or things like that. Nobody was fighting for how to shape reality itself. The "Consensus" of the time was essentially just the human experience of observing what M20 calls the Earthly Foundations (which is not something M20 invented by the way, such elements were discussed in previous books). Stuff falls down, there's a pattern of celestial movement, etc. and nobody's specific beliefs were powerful enough to actually do anything. The sun didn't orbit around the Earth in some places, while the Earth orbited around the sun in others. Much like we actually know now, they simply moved in relation to each other and everything else is a frame of reference.

                                        The Order of Reason were the ones that figured out Reality was malleable with the power of mass belief as they searched for ways to strike back at the entrenched magical societies that they saw as moral hazards. They worked to "break" the previous "natural" Reality by influencing beliefs to influence Reality via Consensus. This created Reality Zones as dominant local beliefs started to take hold (see: Sorcerer's Crusade) instead of the previous state of things. Then the Order of Reason began their efforts to unify Reality Zones through their methodology to create the modern nearly-global Consensus.

                                        -----------

                                        Also remember that due to the Mage metaphysics, the Anthropic Principle is an absolute necessity. The very idea of Earthly Foundations is to establish the setting elements necessary to justify the Anthropic Principle working.

                                            Yes, but it still stands, that the majority, or at least a significant part of how the world works is part of the Earthly Foundations. The Consensus and reality zones in general don't override those, instead they're adding another layer to them, another set of rules.

                                            Like, flying.

                                            Humans don't fly, that's a Foundation at this stage of our evolution.

                                            Although, other things are able to fly, and using the same laws, derived from the Foundations, humans could build machines that could fly. This is still not the product of the Consensus.

                                            However, the Consensus adds another layer, that says, because of millions of indoctrinated people, that flying is in fact the safest method of transport, so glitches occur least frequently. The consensus also makes modes of flying, like jetpacks less vulgar, because people got accustomed to the idea that machines and science could do crazy things for real and so, it works easier, despite that it contradicts the Foundations (read, basic physical laws). However, the average modern people would have a harder time believing the dude chanting magical words and then going airborne, or suddenly sprouting wings with a 6 meters span. The point is: planes don't fly because of the consensus and the World in general doesn't work only on the base of Consensus. Also, Consensus isn't omnipotent, it doesn't overrides the Foundations, it adds to it.
