            As point of order it would have to be three drinks over three nights, not three in one night. No matter how much vitae you drink if it's all in one night you're only one step blood bound. Three drinks over three nights is just how the magic works.

            The Tremere weakness in V20 is an alteration of their weakness in earlier editions where they were always considered to be one step blood bound to the Circle of Seven due to drinking from a cup of transubstantiated vitae either during or shortly after their embrace by rigidly observed clan protocol (in order to dodge it you had to buy a special clan merit). In V20 the weakness is thematically similar. The Tremere, much like the Assamites, have a lot of associated consumption imagery (even more than standard issue for vampires). The drinking of the potions that turned them into vampires, the devouring of the Salubri, the ritual of the Cup of the Seven which both cements loyalty and deliberately mirrors the origins of the Tremere and makes them "true" members rather than "just" vampires. As such their weakness reflects the perils of drinking too heavily.

                        As mentioned above one of the big things to remember is that the early Tremere, especially Tremere, Goratrix, and Etrius were all Mages for over a century before they 'embraced' themselves. Once they have firsthand experience with vampire blood and its magical properties became clear they quickly began adapting their former knowledge to create the Thaumaturgical Paths they became famous for. Currently Black Hand Guide to Tal'Mahe'Ra has the most recent rules for what happens when a mage is embraced AFAIK.

                        Yet their occult knowledge remains. Former sorcerers learn magical Disciplines faster than other Kindred. To reflect this in a system, calculate the total Experience value of the magus’ lost powers. She gains a rebate of half of this total to spend on Necromancy, Thaumaturgy, and other forms of blood magic. The vampire usually requires a teacher for anything learned out-of-Clan, but the Storyteller may make an exception if the Cainite used to practice a form of magic that strongly resembles an existing form of blood magic, or if the player and Storyteller develop a new Path to represent the fledgling’s unnatural insights. This may explain how sorcerers such as the Tremere rapidly developed sorcery powerful enough to disrupt the status quo.
                        So a Mage with multiple Pillars/Spheres between 3-5 dots is going to have a far more significant repertoire of mystical oomph than a normal neonate could ever dream of in the beginning. Add to this the fact that much of the House structure beneath them still had all the awakened tricks at their disposal it becomes easier to see how they survived. By the time the Order moved against them the Tremere would already have prepared knowing their enemy well since they were them. Additionally Tremere had just orchestrated the destruction of House Diedne only a few decades ago through sheer personal persuasion. The Order may quickly start looking grimly at the losses of trying to go after and destroy another House prepared to fight them. Add in the fact that magic was becoming less predictable and the Order pulling back and eventually ignoring the Tremere so long as the latter did the same also makes sense.

                        On the other side of the war bringing the awakened structure to fight the Omen War will pit the vampires against a foe whose abilities they do not understand. Once the Tremere convince the Ventrue to back them they can successfully stabilize since now they have a Tzimisce free region to move into and a potent ally to convince the other clans to just let the Tremere be.

                        On the point of magic becoming less reliable. Tremere, Goratrix, and Etrius had all surpassed a normal mortal lifespan thus magic was the ONLY reason they were alive. They find out magic is starting to fail (they dont have a rulebook to see its only getting more difficult instead they see effects that once worked now DONT) and they panic. How long until the potions and methods they use to extend their life fail. If they do fail will they age normally or will they simply die instantly as their true age catches up. That terror of thinking you could just up and die without notice drove Tremere to put both Etrius and Goratrix on their mission to find another out to death. When Goratrix came saying that he had found an answer that would ensure their life in perpetuity of course they jumped at it. Why take the risk of spending time studying the potion when you might DIE at any moment, especially since its coming from the person you specifically told to go find a solution and is now offering it?
