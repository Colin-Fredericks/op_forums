    Questions about abuse - Beginner Storyteller

    11-22-2017, 06:32 PM
    So, I write this because a friend of mine is having trouble in a game of his. I tried helping him, but I learned with the 3ed and he is using the m20 and one of his players is quite the powergamer. I'd like help with a few rules

    1-) Sanctum background. The player just put the cult background and sanctum plus wonder(prime 5) at maximum, so he'd stock up quintessence and always reduce the difficulty of magics inside the sanctum. With this, he is making effects with 50 successes or more. Now, in 3ed when the effect came out of the sanctum, it instantly was undone. There is nothing like this when we searched m20. We asked in some forums, and people told us to use the unbelief rule. But there are some problems
    A-) the player is making the pattern alteration that he causes stronger with quintessence, making it harder to dissolve trough unbelief
    B-) The unbelief rule is vague, at best.
    2-) There is no rule for permanent paradox in regards to creating armos with the life sphere, claws and so on. The player is doing just that. He made an armor with 50 successes with life 3. And some other colorful things. We are well aware of the rule about permanente paradox in regards to attributes, but there is just that. Nothing in regards to changing soaking(Like there was in verbena 3ed) and so on
    3-) The storyteller does not use the resonance rule because it is optional. We used ressonance in 3ed to limit getting quintessence with prime. After some time, it made you a beacon of supernatural energy. But since the ressonance rule is optional, how to deal with this?

    What is my friend, the storyteller from that game, missing?

      A few possibilities come to mind.

      Permanent Paradox

      Depending on whether they are making the changes to themselves or an object Permanent Paradox might occur (bolded for emphasis) pg 548

      Permanent Paradox generally comes through the Enhancement Background Trait, described in Chapter Six. Severe backlashes bestow such points as well, and certain alterations to a creature’s Pattern may do so too. Bygones and constructs often suffer from permanent Paradox points – a flaw that keeps certain Technocracy operatives or Nephandi confined to the Otherworlds. (See the HIT Marks in Appendix I and the Gilledians described in Chapter Five.) Essentially, a being with permanent Paradox is a walking reality crime – a violation of Earthly metaphysics whose own body is hazardous to her health.
      In the wonders section there is a spot about permanent Paradox and items pg 652

      Permanent Paradox
      On the flipside, certain Wonders ripple the Consensus simply by existing. The Enhancement Background (described in Chapter Six) invokes that kind of permanent Paradox, and certain other things do as well. For the most part, permanent Paradox comes about through certain lasting modifications: moving tattoos, glowing horns, and the like. In general, assume that a Wonder that can be turned off or set aside does not generate permanent Paradox. If a Wonder does involve permanent Paradox, add that Paradox to the wielder’s Quintessence/ Paradox wheel whenever he has that Wonder in his possession.
      So the character's super armor is clearly stronger than any terrestrial material and thus could fall afoul of having Permanent Paradox.

      Limiting Successes for Some Effects

      Below the chart on base damage and duration (pg 504) has the following suggestion

      To preserve game balance, the Storyteller may choose to cap damage at 20 health levels (10 successes, or nine successes for Forces Sphere attacks).
      This same idea could be used for effects that soak damage.

      Quiet

      Blatantly ignoring reality's boundaries repeatedly (such as making personal armor that is sturdier than mountains) could result in the Mage developing Quiet as they lose their grip on what things are supposed to be. pg 554

      The Storyteller makes some plans, based around a particular character’s personality and actions, and decides how this particular case of Quiet will manifest.
      When the character reaches a particular goal (say, 10 Paradox points), or endures a certain challenge to her sanity (reading a blasphemous tome, perhaps), the Storyteller begins slipping notes to the player, describing the delusions or suggesting irrational acts.
      The Enemy

      If the character had the idea its quite possible that the enemy (Technocrats, Nephandi, etc) have had the same idea. So he might have his super armor, but the Technocrats break out a high powered turbolaser to deal with it. Additionally such potent effects could very easily stand out to Awakened sense, at that number of successes possibly even Awareness might pick up on it.

                                      I need some things clarified:

                                      From the context it seems that this Chronicle has been going on for quite some time. Characters with Prime 5 are not possible under the normal character creation rules. At this point he is a Master, and should be capable of truly amazing feats. However, I could be interpreting this incorrectly and the Wonder is what is giving him access to all of this Quintessence. What specifically does the Wonder do? At 5 dots it probably has a few powers, but it should definitely have limits to what it is capable of. Wonders don't get access to the entire list of Sphere effects, just some specific rotes.

The ST needs to talk with the player and make sure that it's understood that this behavior is clearly taking advantage of the system and the ST, and it needs to stop in the name of fun and for the sake of the story.

                                              Just a thought here - but has your friend considered the rule for rituals involving brief rites, ceremonies, and great works (pg 541)? This kind of thing will make spells with 50 successes incredibly hard to achieve, limiting folks to 1-10 successes depending on their stamina capabilities.

                                              If that's not enough, it's also worth noting that while Sanctum does make the effects of a mage's powers coincidental, that's _only within the confines of reality within that location_. It's essentially a minor reality zone, usually attuned to the mage in question. Why this is important, is because it means that the mage will not be breaking any of the Foundations of the reality - and yes, some effects will still be vulgar. In no paradigm is your skin being able to shrug off a nuke without a scratch coincidental, and while you might be able to convince a cult that you are able to armor him up or what have you, some things will be beyond their belief. (...Also, some things will be beyond their time. Cultists are people too - if he abuses them and only uses them to benefit himself, that merit will dwindle.)

                                                      Hmmm....

                                                      I didn't notice any Avatar background mentioned there. That could be a problem for one. And the Wonder lets the character draw in quint from the universe, great, but where is he storing it? It's a separate use of Prime to utilize Tass an without an Avatar background he's going to struggle storing it in his pattern/using that quint for anything.

                                                      No sleep nor respite is asking for pattern bleed as is if he's just making it so he can soak unearthly levels of harm.
                                                      Armour with the Life Sphere? So he's either Increased his Physical Attributes OR he's wearing a created life form with Increased Physical Attributes (to whit Stamina) that he's also having to spend successes on to be able to soak lethal and maybe even Aggravated damage (both of which tend to come across as Vulgar when they are used, not cast and can also cause Bleed or Quiet). Plus there's the time taken to do this, what is the rest of the cabal up to while he's taking all this time to generate 50 successes? There's around probably around 20hrs of casting time there for an Arete 3 character (without knowing the paradigm), unless he's using magick to also make his cult immune to tiredness, distraction and food (which would also require the Mind sphere). If so then you have things like Domino Effect, or penalties for maintaining additional Magickal Effects.

                                                      The key problem though is seemingly the unwillingness of the ST to rule on these things, it's not even House Ruling but Mage requires the ST to take the examples of how things like Paradox, Pattern Bleed and Quiet function and apply them to a characters actions. You wont get a hard and fast on a lot of that because of the sheer scope and flexibility of the Magick that Mage uses.
