    Originally posted by Irioth View Post
    Sure, full agreement here. A necessary premise to this kind of analysis is deciding what one means to do with magic. Mine includes the ability to perform most kinds of common magic feats through the mystical equivalent of brute force with relative ease and safety. In my perspective, this a major and necessary component of 'true' or 'real' magic.
    I mean, one's perspective is one's perspective, but setting that kind of bar for magic to qualify as "real" or "true" is the one thing you've said here that I'm strongly inclined to disagree with. Magic is magic because it's magic, which is to say that whatever you are doing is achieved by paranormal/supernatural power, rather than obeying the laws of science as we understand them. How powerful or impressive the result isn't really what matters, but rather the method of attaining the result. Maybe it just comes from the shallow look I've taken at the subject of chaos magic, specifically the idea of magic as the ability to induce synchronicities, events that appear as mere coincidence but can be linked with the perception or will of the practitioner, but I've become less interested in "magic" as "a flashy way to blow stuff up" in recent years.

    You are of course free to prefer whatever type of magic you wish, but to try and set a definition for what qualifies as "real" or "true" seems to move beyond a statement of opinion or perspective, into a matter of fact.

    Originally posted by Irioth View Post
    Sure, but the reward is totally worth the risk from my perspective. I may point out that being able to harness a great deal of brute force at need and use it easily and efficiently oftentimes is an excellent way to deal with the problem of potential hostiles.
    Are you familiar with "The Order of the Stick," specifically the prequel comic "Start of Darkness"? It includes a page that really demonstrates this point in quite an entertaining fashion (alas I don't think I can find a copy floating around the internet). It is indeed a very functional solution in many cases, though subtlety and technique can be equally effective IMO. I must admit, as much as I admire subtlety, I often tend to resort to more brute-force solutions myself in practice, haha.

    Originally posted by Irioth View Post
    As far as I know, the Batini and the Valdaermen have Pillars that are specialized for travel or space manipulation and hence well suited for this kind of feat. The other Fellowships nowhere so much, although they may often access other means of quick and efficient travel, such as flight, shapeshifting, and/or weather control. Also please notice that traveling to the Umbra (and possibly using it as a means of quick travel) is a wholly different matter, and the other Fellowships often are proficient in it.
    I took a bit of time to look at the Valdaermen Runes, and I must admit they are rather impressive. They manage to mirror a lot of the more interesting and useful effects you can find among the Batini Pillars, while also including some of the abilities from Prime and Spirit that I really like as well. Which did remind me that, aside from the already mentioned lack of Prime abilities, the Batini not having any talent for accessing the Umbra is probably their other big shortcoming from my perspective. But Mind, Prime, Correspondence, and Time are probably my favourite Spheres, while Spirit is really cool but not quite one of my favourites despite its considerable utility. Given the opportunity I would of course want my character to be skilled at all types of magic, but that's not really feasible until you're getting into Master/Archmage territory, and until then you've got to choose priorities.

    Maybe I'll take another look at your topic about Dual Affiliations in DA Mage and consider which pairing might give me all my favourite toys lol.

        Well, I do not mean to dismiss or demean the validity of perspectives like yours. If subtlety and apparent coincidence work for you, they work, and good for you. I am happy things work well for you that way on your end. My description of brute-force magic being the only 'true' or 'real' kind for me is simply an attempt to describe how things register to me. I feel happy and fulfilled being able to do things this way, annoyed and frustrated having to do them the other way. The main exception is when an effect is subtle by its very nature or really works best that way, such as mental manipulation or fate/luck spells.

        I suppose it is no coincidence (heh) that two of my three most preferred Spheres (Forces, Life, and Mind), besides being some of the most powerful and versatile ones, are also among the ones that often work best this way. Mind is just as powerful and versatile but Exhibit A for the 'subtle by nature' exception. Entropy is usually next in the priority scale but a mixed bag for me. I fancy the direct death/destruction effects, and I find the fate/luck ones often rewarding and useful for personal benefits but not my preferred solution to problems if a simpler brute-force one exists. If it doesn't, the exception applies. As it concerns the necromancy stuff, either it is useful as a tool for immortality, or Spirit is usually a more versatile alternative, or it does not interest me. Correspondence, Prime, Matter, and Spirit are often useful in their niches but not my preferred solution if an alternative exists. MtA often makes Prime practically necessary to some serious degree, but the situation is more complex for DA. I have little overall use for Time, except as a tool for immortality.

        Sure. As I said, I find subtlety occasionally acceptable or useful when it is the natural way of doing things or the by far most convenient method, but I have little patience with it when a brute-force solution seems available and just as or even more effective. I tend to find manipulation a tiresome way of dealing with problems, and straightforward brute-force solutions more rewarding.

        I took a bit of time to look at the Valdaermen Runes, and I must admit they are rather impressive. They manage to mirror a lot of the more interesting and useful effects you can find among the Batini Pillars, while also including some of the abilities from Prime and Spirit that I really like as well.
        I won't deny the Valdaermen package has its selling points. My biggest gripe with it is the Foundation requirements, IMO one of the most inconvenient ones among the Fellowships, at least if you care for your ability to keep your body intact and have a decent lifestyle. I greatly appreciate the ability to withstand unavoidable hardship but denial or sacrifice for their own sake or even as a preferential way of (supposed) self-improvement register as off-putting and incomprehensible to me. Oddily enough, despite the Middle Ages having been the peak for often outlandish degrees of Christian ascetism, the Messianic Voices do not find themselves as burdened as the Valdaermen with too many explict requirements for sacrifice. I suppose it happens because to a large degree they are able to replace that with personal submission and abasement to their idea of the divine, which I find just as off-putting (also because the Middle Ages Christianity idea of God typically registers to me as the biggest tyrant and monster of them all).

        As a rule, another preference of mine about magic and mysticism is strong sympathy for the Left-Hand Path. In Mage paradigm terms, it typically plots to a mix of Order of Hermes (without unnecessary ritual), Verbena (without unnecessary sacrifice), Cult of Ecstasy (without hippie pacifism), and Taftani (the scorn for subtlety). In DA terms, it usually means OoH, Old Faith, or their mix.
