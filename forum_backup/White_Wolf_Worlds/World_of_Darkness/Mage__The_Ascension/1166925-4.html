    More like half a century, if you count its predeccesors. It means generations grown up without any wars within its borders and that we don't have to bother with paperwork within when traveling, etc. And I didn't say that that the EU will last, that's still up for grabs, neither did I suggested the same with the disparates. The thing is, it's an example that people could be willing to sit down and compromise, even after centuries upon centuries of grudges and after twice burning down the continent. It's possible. Yes, those old grievances and new interests might cause it to crumble, but seeing them making a starting attempt is not that stretching.
    But these groups, as written WON'T compromise. And the Book of Secrets rational for how to get groups of rabidly ethnocentric groups to function together are pretty much a recipe for disaster. These aren't grievances, they are core beliefs that don't allow for much bending before the group is going to break. Then you'll end up with, say, Taftani in the Disparates and other Taftani. Both of whom think of themselves as the REAL Taftani (and one of whom is more correct, and it's not the one in Alliance...)

    This isn't border crossing and work visas and cheeses, it's like Poland had slavery and Spain was all about the value of human freedom, that Italy thinks assassination is a legitimate way to handle political advancement while Denmark insists that all that matters is primogeniture in determining who should be leader (though those aren't opposites, man, will they EVER collide) or even just having France believing it is superior to all others and refusing to deal with the other nations unless they respected the innate superiority of the French. Then saying, "Here are the Ten Commandments" and having the Hague deal with all the conflicts by means of punitive justice with only one punishment available, execution.
