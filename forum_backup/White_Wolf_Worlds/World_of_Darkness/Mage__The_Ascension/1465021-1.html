    Originally posted by HorizonParty2021 View Post
    I think it's okay for a discussion to evolve out of one's personal preferences and head-cannon as a player, but I suspect that you are searching for a reason to play the Traditions just to get some varied play experience. I would not enjoy playing the Technocracy as written, for one reason: Room 101. I wouldn't want to storytell a Technocratic-Union-as-written campaign, because I would be faced with imposing Room 101 on imaginative, rebellious players, which gets into issues of player agency.

    If you enjoy being in lockstep with the Union's stated goals and following orders, then you see the glass half full where the Technocracy are concerned. A way of seeing the glass half full with Traditions is that You can be the Tradition Mage who pulls it all together. You can be the visionary leader who points to the distance, saying, "Follow me to...(use your imagination)".

    The M20 Character sheet lists: Name, Player, Chronicle, Nature, Demeanor, Essence, Affiliation, Sect, Concept, in that order for a reason. If you are substituting the Technocracy's well defined Timetable for in-depth freeform Seekings, you may be missing out on the opportunity to play in a faction that does not punish deviance from party line with torture and Conditioning.
    While I do understand where you're coming from, I think you're being a bit too rigid here. Yes, Room 101 exists. But they don't send every troublemaker there. It's reserved for the worst offenders, renegades and traitors. Nor does the Technocracy punish innovative and imaginative agents. They will punish those that are dangerously reckless or regularly disobedient but then so would any organization. And even then, Room 101 and the severe punishments are only for those morons that don't seem to get the memo that being a reckless jackass is a bad thing. Again, like any other organization would.

    If a ST of a Technocrat game is threatening PCs with Room 101 for attempting stray from their rails, that's a problem with the ST. Though if a PC is behaving badly, threatening them with Room 101 is valid.

    And Technocrats (as well as other Technomancers) do have Seekings, they just perceive and understand them differently from Mages. Just as Mages from different Traditions will perceive their own Seekings differently from those other Traditions. If they didn't, they wouldn't be able to advance their Enlightenment as other Willworkers do.

    Homo sapiens. What an inventive, invincible species. It's only a few million years since they crawled up out of the mud and learned to walk. Puny, defenceless bipeds. They've survived flood, famine and plague. They've survived cosmic wars and holocausts. And now, here they are, out among the stars, waiting to begin a new life. Ready to outsit eternity. They're indomitable. Indomitable.

            HorizonParty2021, the point is that the possibility of abuse doesn't mean abuse happens, or that it happens with the frequency you seem to expect. I've enjoyed playing characters with a long history with Room 101, but also enjoyed games where it is used sparingly, as the last resort for agents whose indiscipline have been costing lives time and again.

            Both readings are Union as written. Even because on big institutions a lot of things happen at middle management, so there is no mandatory reading of the institutional rules out or in character. At least not mandatory in a way that can be reliably enforced.

            Firanai, the Traditions are a defense alliance. They are working toward a greater vision: a world where you can follow your path for Enlightenment without a self-righteous jerk hunting you just out of spite for your personal world view. This is a vision in itself. Also they have individual Traditions and subdivisions carrying on with particular propositions in a way the Conventions can't, as defending diversity of ideas includes, by definition, that you'll have this diversity of ideas to follow or dispute.

            If you don't like it is ok. But in that case you must reframe your reasoning, because we already presented plans, vision, representations, strategy, context, and it so happens that you don't like them, which is very different from them not existing or being ill defined. As you put it in the OP, this is not the problem.

                    I ended up getting back to this a wee bit late, but I do have one other suggestion to offer. The Traditions are not like the Technocratic Union, where all of the Conventions are at least nominally on board with one big Plan for all of them. The Traditions don't have A Plan, they have Nine Plans, one for each Tradition. The Traditions as a collective exist to facilitate each of the individual Traditions pursuing their own agendas, cooperating and protecting each other, and arbitrating disputes (ideally, at least). Most of the plans of the Traditions as a whole will simply be the result of negotiating the best way to advance the common interests of the separate Traditions. If you want a deeper idea of "What is the game plan for the Traditions," you would probably have to study each Tradition individually rather than try and find a big answer that covers everything.

                    I haven't done a ton of digging into the individual Traditions, but one example might be the Virtual Adepts' focus on the Digital Web and Reality 2.0. That's *their* Plan.
