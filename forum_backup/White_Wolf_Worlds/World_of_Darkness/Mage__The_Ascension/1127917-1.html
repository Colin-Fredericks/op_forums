    One of the other pitfalls of Seekings, and one that I suspect has soured many a player on the subject, is that STs often run them as challenges (riddles, puzzles, etc) for the Players rather than challenges for the characters. To be fair that's a common ST mistake in many situations, but it is particularly problematic here. Seekings are supposed to be integral and defining parts of a character and as such they should be things the player has an overwhelming amount of input on. That's why my preferred method is to merely facilitate players describing the Seekings themselves.

            Well, something I've been part of that's along the lines of what Ramnesis posted that takes advantage of digital technology:

            The player writes up their Seeking, and the ST acts more like an editor (esp. content over copy in that regard). So yes, things like, "how does that make your character feel?" might be part of the redlining process, but you'd see far more, "how does that impact your character's views?" or whatever. The final product would go into a shared GoogleDoc that the other players can read at their leisure.

            It's not something I think would work for everyone (I know a lot of people that don't like doing solo writing things like that), but it's actually a pretty good way for player-facing Seeking construction that doesn't pull down group-time play.

                                My group is uneasy with the idea of Seekings so we've tried alternatives. To me, the key elements of a seeking are interfacing with the avatar and self-improvement. My ancillary goals were to include all the other players and either further the story or give a glimpse of another world.

                                Methods will vary depending on your view of the Avatar. Our group agrees that the Avatar may or may not be a soul but Avatars seem to be independent and are essential if not responsible for the ability to do Magic.


                                What we've tried:
                                *Avatar challenge - The avatar issues a challenge to the character that needs to be maintained during a story. If the avatar is advancing metaphysical advancement this could be barring access to an overused sphere or maybe not letting a character use higher dots of a sphere. I have a Chorister who's overly fond of "divine providence" in the form of Entropy and Time and he's restricted to each at level 1 until the end of the current story to get a fourth dot of Arete.

                                *Avatar quest - The player is given a kind of mission from their avatar. Finish business from a past live, carry out an activity that's particularly in line with one's essence, or simply a large undertaking to advance the avatar's agenda. This is similar to the symbol quest of a traditional Seeking but the cabal can cooperate. In a symbolic seeking a character may need to protect a unicorn as a symbol of purity but in an Avatar quest, the character need to protect an actual unicorn that's under threat. These take one session and can cover multiple Seekings.

                                *Avatar session - Avatars may have a life of their own when a character is sleeping. In an Avatar session, the players roleplay their Avatar doing Avatar stuff. The one we tried was the Avatar as an element in an Umbral Court. The Avatar settled a score and gained power. The other characters also participated as their Avatars. This makes Arete a reflection of the power of their Avatar moreso than wisdom.

                                *Flashbacks - This involves a character revising a choice that the character made that had large consequences which seemed unavoidable. One character has a personal ban against using life magic destructively due to the circumstances of their awakening. In one session for an hour we replayed the awakening and the character tried to find an alternative. It took two tries but the character found another way. The other players took on the roles of other characters present in the Awakening.

                                What we haven't tried:
                                *Analog to Vampire's Humanity - As the Mage gains more understanding their rule of magical ethics become stricter. The character must act in accordance with some metaphysical ethic to advance understanding. This isn't the same as a moral straight jacket but may be something like expanding ones tools of magic, investigating new paradigms and the like. We've not really found something we like with this method and failure would imply that arete could drop which seems terrifying.

                                *Teaching - This may not make immediate sense, but to raise one's arete one must shepard and teach another Mage to their level of Arete. I often don't understand something until I teach someone.

                                Avatar challenge has proven the easiest and Avatar quest has been used to great effect. The Avatar session was really weird which I enjoyed but I think some of the players were confused. Flashback worked in a pivotal case where a previous event was key to who the character was.
