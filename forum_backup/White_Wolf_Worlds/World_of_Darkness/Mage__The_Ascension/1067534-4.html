        54. The Nickel StreetCar Marauder. Back in the day, this being the early twentieth century when electricity was just being developed and many electrical entrepreneurs saw electric streetcar lines as a way to make money, there was a young man into streetcars. He failed, like most others in the Midwest (Wisconsin would be easy to do if you read Forrest McDonald's Let There Be Light but you could easily do Ilinois, Indiana, Ohio or other states or a foreign country). This young man went on to other endeavors, but was always angry that the city council, which granted streetcar franchises and set rates, would not negotiate in good faith for a nickel fair. A growing electric utility company bought him out, and used the electrical equipment to develop its electrical system before being merged with other companies into a giant electric holding company.

        The old man grew bitter as automobiles took over for street cars and his city became less important in the 1980s Rust Belt recessions. Yet he still kept the tickets, the flyers and other papers from his old streetcar company. He rarely talked about the company but died with this weight on his mind.

        His grandson, when going through the papers, and in a grieving state, saw the newspaper clippings about the rate fight. The city wanted more and more service, but wouldn't pay a nickel for it. A nickel was to the company, a fair fare. The grandson snapped, realizing that the world was unfair and the only way to restore justice to his city was to bring back his grandfather's dream. He decided to run to where the power grid was and in an accident, was electrocuted. The electrocution should have been fatal, but the grandson's Avatar awoke then and channeled the forces into the ground. The grandson of the streetcar man was now alive, Awakened, and seeking to create a world of streetcars and neighborhoods again.

        Now the grandson is going around town ramming into people like he is a streetcar saying, 'Pay the Nickel Fare.' Most people are confused, since other than the electrical wires, there is no evidence a streetcar was ever there. . . At a higher level of Quiet, streetcars and a pre-Great Depression city will emerge and those not paying the fare will be rammed by actual streetcars. ..

        Note: Without the Marauder flavor, this was one of several utility-related villains for a superhero game I ran. Nickelboy was one of the weaker villains, probably because streetcars aren't something most people I've met have much experience with.

                                        62) A gang of youths in identical hand-made masks have been causing minor mischief for a year now. The police find them troublesome, but ultimately a minor problem (especially in light of more serious police work that needs tending to).

                                        All this changes after Imbolc - one of the four seasonal Gaelic holidays - when the gang's activities began to take a darker turn. Their masks - previously mocking and playful faces with tongues sticking out - have morphed into sinister visages, with piercing eyes and toothy grins. Where before they would simply egg and toilet paper a house or throw fruit at stop signs, now they engage in targeted acts of property damage. Largely these acts are performed against religious buildings like churches and mosques, or against the homes of prominent moral figures in the community. Windows smashed, fires set, statues defiled, plagues defaced. There has been a definite edge of anti-religious animosity in the gang's activities, though they've also taken to vandalizing schools, clinics, homeless shelters, and community centers.

                                        Additionally, creatures have been disappearing from the city, only to be found dead days later. At first, it was just pets, or animals of local significance (like the ducks that make their home at the local high school's pond, which served as informal mascots for the institution). They were discovered strung up, their chests ripped open and hearts missing. Copies of the gang's signature mask were affixed to the faces of the animals as best could be managed, this time smeared with blood. These bodies - sacrifices, really - are accompanied by messages scrawled on nearby surfaces. Blasphemous messages, and inverted crosses and pentagrams.

                                        As summer gives way to autumn, however, the gang has upped their game. Their latest victim wasn't an animal, but a man. A homeless person, known to local police. For this reason, the authorities have made this gang of youths a high priority. While they can't rule out that the murder could be a copycat crime, investigators are moving forward with the idea that the gang has grown bolder and more extreme.

                                        There is a problem, however: the police are having trouble finding members of the group. There are, in fact, supernatural forces helping the gang evade capture. And if some of them are cornered, they will not hesitate to employ yet more occult power. This is because, unsurprisingly, this collection of youths have turned to Infernalism, and regularly summon their demonic patron. A patron who now demands greater offerings, both in the form of live sacrifices and in attacks against forces of goodness and progress.

                                        And the patron demands something extra special be done for Samhain - Halloween, when the borders between this world and the next are thin, and dark things have greater freedom.

                                            63) Invitations have been sent out to all the occultists in the city, from the entirely mundane dabblers, to Sorcerers (both solitary practitioners and members of other groups, like the Arcanum), to full mages of the Traditions and Disparates and Orphan scholars, and to vampiric blood magicians like the Tremere and even the lone Assamite Sorcerer. Obviously, the degree to which the sender is apparently aware of even low-key or secretive individuals is disturbing.

                                            The invitation is to an auction of occult items and texts. A list of items for sale was included with the invitation, and if even half the wares on offer are accurate, it's an event too tempting for any serious student of magic to pass up. In addition to common reference materials (that is, common among the secret world of real magic), there are a number of rare volumes, books containing detailed sketch copies of notable Solificati art (a group famous for hiding the alchemical knowledge, not in text, but in deeply symbolic paintings), at least two Grimoires and one known Primer, a not-insubstantial supply of rare spell components (pure pearls, powdered bone of murderer, wood taken from a tree struck by lightning; that sort of thing that ritualists and alchemists need for exotic spells), and several magical items (ranging from simple Trinkets and Charms to full Artifacts and one Talisman).

                                            While it could easily be a trap, several of the invited individuals will, begrudgingly, take the risk of attending (or at least sending proxies), and rumors circulate of this in the occult community. Meaning anyone who had reservations going forward must now weigh the risk of attending versus the cost of allowing other occultists - many their direct rivals - to get all the spoils for themselves. Others are quietly comparing notes with each other, and making deals ahead of time to buy the materials from those that do attend (at inflated prices, naturally).

                                            So what's really going on? More or less, the auction is on the level. Or as on the level as it can be when it's a Follower of Set running the show, selling off the effects of an incautious wizard who got too in debt to the serpents. Sure, the Setite also wants to use the event as an excuse to gather data on potential "future customers", take a dig at the local Tremere, and generally stir up the city's occult hornet's nest and revel in the chaos for fun and profit. But the Setite, being a businessman first and foremost, is going to run a more-or-less honest sale of goods. It's just good business. He's even sure that, as the date of the auction grows closer, his identity as the show-runner will get out. He's debating letting it slip early himself, just to watch the reaction.

                                            Now there's just the matter of the Technocracy, who naturally have also found out about the event. They are currently weighing the options about what, if anything, they want to do about it...

                                                            67) At the height of the Dark Ages, and into the Renaissance, when tales King Arthur and his knights abound, a rumor spreads in the seedy underbelly of Europe. Just as good, faithful folk spin Romantic morality tales of the Holy Grail, whispers pass between the vilest, most evil men of its antithesis. Any mention of it has entered into taboo in Europe on the level of infernalism, so naturally it is mainly infernalists and other evil men who discuss it, and only in the lowest tones. Low tones for a low topic. It is for this reason that, while the Holy Grail is legendary even in modern times, this unholy chalice is only remarked on in the most obscure and reviled tomes.

                                                            It is called the Black Grail. A "prophet" among infernalists - Gertard the Thrice Mad - spoke of it loudly and with great authority, until his loose tongue finally saw him burned by the Catholic Church for witchcraft and heresy. According to Gertard, the cup was fashioned from the skull of the first man to be damned to hell, and it caught the blood of Satan himself when the dark one saw fit to walk the earth in times long past. As rumors of the Black Grail's existence spread, alternate explanations for its appearance and origins popped up. There is now a steady trade among evil men - whether infernalist or merely depraved - of any scrap of knowledge pertaining to the Black Grail's location. For it is said that any water poured inside goes instantly stagnant, or that it turns into blood. And anyone who drinks it will obtain supreme power.

                                                            What that power is has been debated in lower circles for years. Some say it offers an unholy Investment, without a need to bargain or trade the soul. Others say it gives the drinker immortality. Others, still, maintain that the liquid drunk from the Black Grail is revelatory, gifting the drinker with great and terrible knowledge of magic. Some even say that, because the Black Grail is so hard to find, it surely will be the method whereby Satan finds his Antichrist. The hitch being that the Black Grail can only be found by one who displays such depravity - by one who so sullies their soul - that they are worthy of the title "Versus Christi". Only by those for whom atrocity is as natural as breathing.

                                                            And so, while good folk seek the Holy Grail, evil folk quest for its supposed opposite. Black knights and evil wizards scour the land, looking for any hint of the Black Grail. On the way, sinning and sinning, in hopes that they prove sinister enough to find it. Though the heyday of such quests is over, the modern day still sees the occasional vile scholar unearth mention of the object, and go looking for it.


                                                            Truth is, the Black Grail is and ever was a complete crock.

                                                            Oh sure, a demon summoned to speak of it will certainly talk at length about the legend. They WANT people to search for it, and sin greatly along the way. But if pressed - if compelled to speak true - they will admit to not knowing for sure if it actually exists, or if it ever existed. This is because, in reality, it never did.

                                                            At least, not at first. And not in the way its seekers think.

                                                            An angel - or a great spirit patterned after an angel - discovered the insane ramblings of Gertard the Thrice Mad. But that's all they were: the fever dream delusions of a lunatic. Still, the "prophecy" spread. It was too late to stop it. The Catholic Church made the topic verboten, and many moral Mages - of many different Crafts, Traditions, Fellowships, and Conventions - agreed to stymie such legends. All people of morality knew such rumors - even false ones - would only lead to great harm. But they could not be stopped entirely. This angel understood this fact well, and so decided to do the next best thing. It used the legends, and crafted its own Black Grail. The angel even used the skull of Gertard himself to fashion it, after his charred bones were consigned to an unmarked grave. And upon this Black Grail was laid a curse. A curse of death...or worse, of transformation into a beast that hunted other evil men. The latter was a move of redemption, to be played out until they'd removed enough evil from the world to pay for the drinker's own sins.

                                                            Which fate the one who drank from it suffered...was entirely up to them. A fact the angel explains to the one who finds the chalice, as they are locked in the hallucinatory throes after drinking. By then, it is too late to avert their doom; they made the choice to drink, adding the greatest of weight to the enchantment. The only recourse the user has is what form that doom will take. Death, or the path of redemption through hunting other evil things.

                                                            The Black Grail is not a reward. It is a Trap. A rumor bent towards good ends, by channeling the vilest of souls towards a quest that will either destroy them along the way, or destroy them upon completion, or reduce them to mere beasts that hunt their own kind. There were always going to be hunters of the Black Grail. Now, what they hunt is their own doom.
