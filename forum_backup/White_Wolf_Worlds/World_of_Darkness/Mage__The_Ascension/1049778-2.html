    Spirit 2 - Summon Spirit

    Now, that's a special case power. This, right here, is basically the power of Shamans. Almost everything Shamans will do will be right here.

    This is a VERY peculiar power, because it is absolutely inderect, and requires a LOT of things beyond a simple Paradigm/focus and Arete roll.

    You'll summon a spirit, and you'll have to bargain with him for his help. Those bargains may be very complex or very simple things; if you're a lazy ST like me hehe, you'll just ask for some quintessence tax and be done with it. However, asking favors are a VERY interesting way to deal with this, specially since you can get the smart-boy Shaman who thinks he can do everything with one single spell, but end up against the wall. The best way to use this is: spirits have allies and enemies too. So, if you bargain with spirits A, B and C, you'll gain the enmity of X, Y, Z. That's the price you pay for that power. So, you'll have to choose. Give the player a big list of possibilities - but, some things he would want... Well, sadly, he choose the wrong side.

    Also, spirits are assholes. Have the Shaman NEED a series of self-imposed flaws in exchange for all those wonderful paradox-free powers. So, never using anything artificially made, means that the Mage will basically be treated as a homeless person in any "civilized" community (cities and the like). It must be things that will have a serious impact, and the bigger the impact, the more power it potentially wields. Another option: follow the footsteps of Odin, and pluck one of your eyes away to "gain wisdom" (yuck!).

    Sacrifices little children! That's a good one... A good one that the Union don't understand... But thats why the Traditions fight for right? To preserve their freedom and way of life... Including the possibility of human sacrifices for the spirits to give you power.

    So... How about trying to give it a few mechanics?

    The way I do it: the powers spirits give are related to the level of "commitment" the Shaman has to pleasing them. So, I simply number the possible sacrifices from 1 to 5, and let the Mage ask for powers that could be mimic with spheres at maximum level of the level of his sacrifice. So, human sacrifice is a level 4 offering, while children sacrifice is the mighty level 5. So, a Shaman could sacrifice a child to ask for a Spirit to give him Primium (Matter 5), or the power to shapeshift into a real dragon (Life 5) or to go back in time (Time 5). Animal sacrifice won't work, unless the Mage raise the animal as a personal pet, in which case is a level 3 sacrifice. Offerings of quintessence are always level 2. Plucking your own eye is a permanent level 3 ofering, your hand or leg, or both eyes or your tongue is 4. Sacrificing your own children or loved one is a permanent level 5 sacrifice. (Meaning, you can gain one permanent "spiritual power-favor" free of paradox with the same effect as a power with maximum Sphere of 5. Are you THAT power hunger? Or THAT desperate? Or... Is your loved one AND you so aware of the dire situation you are, that you both know that this is THE ONLY WAY?).

    Services must be just as significant. What would that be, it's up to the ST, but hardly a service will be significant enough to earn an eternal permanent "favor" in the form of a charm from a spirit.

    A few ideas:
    -Cannibalism of the Dead (alone or in adition with sacrifices) - Level 4;
    -Consecration of the Dead (mummification technics*, proper burial, cremation, etc) - Level 3
    -Upholding the spirits ideals (Never refuse hospitality, never lie, never eat meat, poverty vote, chastidy, never refuse a challenge for a duel, never run from a fight, always trick your friends and enemies, never do an honest day of work and always steal what you need, and many others) Variable
    Fighting for the spirits Wars and enemies - Variable, according to the level of the enemy and commitment to the cause



    *not to confuse to the spell of Life from Mummy the Ressurection

                        I'll give some ideas of Spirit 2 summonings that may be useful, even if you're not a propor Shaman, and even if you're not willing to do bargain with the spiritual entities (or if you'll only go so far when bargaining)

                        Summon Banes Spirit 2
                        Banes are the entropic and evil spirits of the Wyrm, from the middle Umbra. Those beings can be a huge pain for the summoner if he isn't careful, but even so, they can be useful. They are very useful when you wanna to taint, desecrate or destroy an area; banes may even do it for "free" for you on some occasions. So, do you wanna kill a Verbena's sacred Tree? Do you wanna destroy the pure glade of some Dreamspeakers? A large group of Garou hiding in their Caern is causing you trouble? Well, those guys are here for you, and they'll charge you ALMOST nothing...
                        Oh, do you have nature spirits angry at you? (Probably for such acts as I state above) Well, fear not, banes are friends!
                        Just be careful when using those. Always weight the pros and cons before using banes... The ideal way to use them is the tactic of "summon and hide". Summon them at the doorstep of those damn (insert enemy here), and run away.
                        Beware of the Jhor that this effect causes thou.
                        But, on the up side, with more Jhor, the easier it is to use this effect... And... The "friendlier" the banes become... Isn't that lovely?
                        Now, if you're a Nephandi... To hell with the consequences! Just invite some thousands to live in your Sanctum, offer them some children and virgins sacrifices on regular bases, and have fun with a pletora of cool powers!
                        For, it is best to rule on hell... And fuck up the Earth

                        Summon Pattern Spiders - Spirit 2
                        Now, Weaver spirits aren't always so helpful as banes, but you can also make use of them. Bargaining with Weaver spirits can be a little harder; they can demand that you stop disrupting reality, for example (never use Vulgar Magik, Level 5 sacrifice). That's their most common request in exchange for permanent help; however, they can give minor concessions IF the Mage agrees to keep high amounts of Pattern (the Mage ressonance must have at least 2 dots on Pattern above Entropy or Dinamism).
                        Even without bargaining, those spiders can be summoned to strenghten the Gauntlet. They'll do their jobs automatically, as long as left alone. Clever Mages will summon pattern spiders in an area surrounding his chantry, to make harder the life of any spiritual invasor.
                        Those spiders are also great if you're having problems with banes or wyldlings... Did you summon a bane to devour the totem of some dreamspeaker, and now the banes are bothering you? Never fear! The Weaver is here! Summon as many of those spiders as you can, and they'll calcify the banes...
                        And you keep going attracting spiritual attention acting like this...
                        But who cares right? Spirits are just tools after all, and we dont ask the Hammer how he feals about hitting the nail, am I right or am I right?

                        Summon Wyldlings - Spirit 2
                        Annnnnd, of course, those. Forget bargaining with those, unless you're a Marauder. Just summon and run (actually, summon, and EVERYBODY runs).
                        Oh, and they'll do great against those pesk spiders trying to calcify you in the gauntlet for being a naughty boy.

                        Keep summoning all the representations of the Triad to have them fight one another... That's a sure way to make friends. But, once again, who gives a damn

                            Summon Minor Demons - Spirit 2
                            No, that's not The Fallen. Those are beings from the High Umbra, spirits of mischief and mayhem. Not banes either.
                            Demons are a diverse lot. Demonologist's manuals will describe countless types - Imps, Succubus, Incubbus, Rakshasas, Efrities, Yomi, Maeljins, Oni, and many, many others.
                            Summoning demmons WONT make you an infernalist right away, but, it is EXTREMELY shun on the Traditions, and forbatten by the Union. Demonology is something that can quickly award you a court trial on the Traditions, and a trip to 101 for the Union. However, a Mage can keep this kind of business on... Respectable levels, as to not draw attention. Some demonologists can get the respect of their peers however, if they show that their Arts are used to study and to better fight those creatures, not to serve and bargain. But, to gain respect as a demonologist, is not hard.
                            Demons are probably the kind of spirit more prone to bargaining. The most common and desired commodity is simple: the Mage's own soul. But, since not all Mages desire to sell their souls, demons are happy to bargain all sorts of "inofensive" things.
                            The things demons ask may seem ilogical (specially for those not used to those creatures and that believe in the myths of hell and that they are absolutely evil, sadistic, etc). A succubus, for example, may ask the Mage to have sex with her... And that's one that A LOT of Mages do... Because... Well... The power she offers me... So much... For so little? Besides, she is gorgeous... Hum... Why not right? It doesn't mean anything, I can delight myself once, it doesn't mean anything, I won't damn my soul just because of that...
                            Other demons simply may not care about corrupting that silly Mage at this moment... They have more pressing matters... "So puny mortal, help me destroy the cults of Tarshkar, the unholy, lord of the flies of the seventh hell, who dares oppose my rule over the feud of the frozen wastelands, and I'll grant you what your heart desires!". Demons fight other demons even more than they fight any other, and they will happly use Mages as pawns in their conflicts.
                            Smart demonologists will try to extract Intel about the many "demonic cultures". In a sense, Demonology is actually a specific kind of "sociology". Those Mages with a good amount of knowledge of the demonic courts and Realms, can play their rivalries for his benefit, being able to get many good bargains without losing his soul, or his free will.
                            That's still a very dangerous endeavor, no matter what.
                            As to what demons can offer, possibilities are huge. But, basically, Power. Knowledge. Influence. You name it.

                                Summon Angels (Celestials) Spirit 2
                                Despite the name, those are not only the winged humans from abhraamic lore; the title of "angels" is the most common, but the right name of their category is "Celestials". Those spirits from the High Umbra represent higher morals, positive emotions and things to aspire. Classic Christian archangels, Nordic Valkyries, greek Muses, servants from the chinese Celestial Bureoucracy and so.
                                When first summoned, this kind of spirit will almost certanly answer. They'll be openly hostile only if the Mage is utterly currupt (like the Nefandi). Each type of Celestial values a different set of atributes: muses value love and beauty, valkyries value bravery and honor, angels value empathy and piety, archangels value courage and duty, celestial bureoucrats value order and sense of duty, and so on.
                                Celestials will expect those seeking their services to live by their ideals; the Mage must be brave, must be humble, pious, must put the duty above his own personal wishes, must be charitable, live without vices, and many many more.
                                As for favors, the most usual ones will be spreading the celestials values, protecting those they value, and fight off their demonic enemies. More than simply destroying terrible monsters with horns, that mostly means fighting what they represent - fight against hate, jeaulousy, cowardice, vice. Each celestial, usually also have one specific sin that they despise - valkyries hate cowardice, angels are anatema to hate, archangels despise lies, and so forth.
                                Celestials are mostly helpful, but they expect the Mage to be able to deal with THEIR trials, rather than relying constantly on help. If the situation is dire, they won't refuse all the help they can handle, but they won't do all the job for the Mage. A Mage that cannot pull his weight may be pietyed, and, at best, the Celestials will send him to a purgatory-like realm, were he'll suffer, but will also be both safe and tought and made stronger. Celestials mostly use violence against the wicked, but a Mage that keeps summoning them for no good reason, may end up being cursed (losing all his money is a common thing).
                                And, of course, if you are being persecuted for a demon, you can get a little help for free...
                                Like the demons, the celestials have a tendency of atracting groups of cultists, that practice the so called "White Magic". It is said that, the old Order of Reason Gabrielites were one such groups, the biggest know, found for the worship of the powerful archangels Gabriel. Needless to say, celestial cultists are the greatest enemies and nemesis of infernalists.
                                Probably, the largest group of White Magic practicers are the Celestial Chorus, but Hermetics are also found of making alliances with this kind of spirits

                                        Enhanced Paper Cut - Matter 3

                                        By using the power to reshape materials - and, more importantly, to prevent them being reshaped - the user causes a piece of paper to resist bending, shearing, crumpling, or tearing. It will keep its shape, under stress, so long as the Duration lasts and those stresses are less than the Successes rolled for Structure. And since paper is thinner than most blades, it can serve as an effective and deadly cutting weapon. A simple playing card is like a knife, and far more easily carried or found without arousing suspicion. Using paper as a weapon may even be coincidental; paper cuts are a known phenomenon, though usually not deadly. Many industrious persons have turned paper into powerful weapons, however, and they didn't have recourse to magick.

                                        For this and other inventive control of paper, see Read Or Die.

                                                            Radio Sight - Forces 1
                                                            With this simple effect, the Mage can hear all radio frequencies around him. He can hear his favorite musics or watch TV directly inside his head.

                                                            Radio Telepathy - Forces 2
                                                            With this effect, the Mage can send sounds and images in the form of radio waves.

                                                            Two Mages using both effects can effectively have a telephatic communication without the use of Mind, and even better, they can communicate over large distances without the need of Correspondence (you can thank the technocratic Consensus for that).
                                                            However, despite all those obvious positive sides and easiness of access, this has the downside of letting anyone curious enough to hear the conversation. Maybe the Mages will wanna use some encrypting before using it.

                                                            Make sure to use apropriate wave lenghts to adjust them to the desired distances
