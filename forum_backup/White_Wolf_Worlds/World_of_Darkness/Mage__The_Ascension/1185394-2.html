    See, in my games the Disparates never existed (I don't recognize the Revised metaplot in my games) and the Hollow Ones still hang with the Traditions for the most part. I can understand their fascination with post-modernism and Chaos Magick (even if I myself do not care for the concept of post-modernism) but I always threw out the whole Goth angle that was played up about the Hollow Ones. It never really made sense to me other than the fact that it was White Wolf in the 90's and Goth was considered "cool" back then.

    My opinions on the Goth subculture are already well-established on this forum, so for the sake of civility I will not repeat them, but I'm going to take my personal issues with the Goth subculture aside and look at it from a purely in-game point of view.

    Why would an entire tradition focus their entire paradigm on the Gothic-Punk subculture? A subculture that has only been in existence since the mid-1970's at the absolute earliest. I get that the Hollow Ones are supposed to actually be Neo-Victorian romanticists and all that jazz, but Victorian romanticism and occultism doesn't really translate well into the contemporary Goth subculture of the 1980's and 1990's. At least not directly. Yes, Goth was influenced by the tropes of Victorian romanticism but only in a superficial way. And then the fact that the lore actually confirms that the Hollow Ones' paradigm is actually post-modernist in its approach, then I'm wondering why an entire tradition rooted in post-modernism and Chaos Magick would all uniformly be 1990's-era nihilistic Goth stereotypes? (Maybe it's my bad experiences with Mage LARP and online Mage games, but it seemed every Hollow Ones character I saw someone play was practically the same, so who knows?)

    Now, I have a fix for the Hollow Ones in my games. The Hollow Ones are still about Post-Modernism and Romanticism, but they aren't tied to any one particular subculture, but rather the collective consciousness of rebellious youth. So, you'd have everything from the 20th Century onward. Flappers, Beatniks, Greasers, Mods, Hippies, Bikers, Punks, Disco Dancers, Goths, Metalheads, and even more modern youth subcultures like Hipsters as well.

    See, the Hollow Ones don't believe in any one paradigm in particular. But if others believe in it, they can use it.

    Personally, I'd like to see some Anime Otaku/Weeaboo Hollow Ones as PC's or NPC's. It may seem ridiculous, but it's not any less ridiculous than nihilistic 90's Goths in a 2010's setting.

    EDIT-I understand that later on they tried to distance the Hollow Ones from the stereotype of them being exclusively Goths, but the fact that even to this day there are still Mage players and ST's who still insist on the Hollow Ones being solely a Gothic-Punk tradition and nothing else still irks me.
