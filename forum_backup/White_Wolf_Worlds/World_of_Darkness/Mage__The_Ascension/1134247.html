      I have a counter question:

      What would you trade with, in case of Angels? "I won't do anything bad, I promise" won't cut it.

      Angels are just as bound to their credo and the faith the Angel springs from as demons are bound to their corrupt deeds. And I think it can be said that 'Angels' don't just exist in the abrahamic origins, just as much as demons as evil entities don't. But let's stick with abrahamic for this exercize.

      So, what would an Angel want from one that in return? How much do you need to give in order to get the really good secrets? Be ever watchful of all your actions for the rest of your life, or risk an angel of wrath to descend for breaking your oath? Hunt down sinners (again, depending on the purview of the angel in question)? Sacrifice of something dear to you? The Bible is full of demands of sacrifice up to including your own offspring to show your faith. Angels are not happy campers. They are as much unfallen demons as demons are fallen angels. And they might demand something for simply calling them down.

      Angels vs. Demons is choosing a rock instead of a hard place. Both things aren't wise, and while a demon definitely will try to make a shady deal and answer your call, an Angel might potentially smite you for calling them down. And I think while you *might* be able to trick a Demon, *somehow* out of a deal, trying that with an Angel will definitely have big consequences - Demons are egoistic and work solo. Angels work together and as part of something greater. That's the biggest difference. And that Something Greater will hunt you down if you weasel your way out.

      At least that's all my interpretation.

                      What could a human offer an angel in exchange for services? To not sin? Being good is the bare minimum expected of humans. What sense does it make to reward someone for doing what they should have been doing in the first place?

                      Moreover, the relationship between mankind and heaven should be one of faith and prayer. Cutting deals is not how it works, because it's probably not proper to see one's relationship with the Almighty as a business transaction. God wants to be worshiped, while mercenary action implies that a human will go to the "competition" if they don't believe they are getting a satisfactory deal. And angels work for God (or at least that's the idea).

                      When a Chorister or Templar calls down an angel, they do it by preying for aid or instruction, with the Paradigmatic understanding that God allows this as He sees fit. No negotiation is going to go down, because either the mage convinces the angels of their need, or they don't. When a Wu Lung summons agents of the Celestial Bureaucracy, it's by observing all the proper procedures to demonstrate they have the Mandate of Heaven, and thus have the authority to order these celestial beings around (demonic ones, too). When a Hermetic calls down an angel, he has similarly done all the right rites to prove himself as having the authority of the divine (in essence, a Hermetic can order an angel around because he believes himself a god).

                      In all these cases, a mage doesn't necessarily strike bargains with angelic beings (real or generated by belief).
