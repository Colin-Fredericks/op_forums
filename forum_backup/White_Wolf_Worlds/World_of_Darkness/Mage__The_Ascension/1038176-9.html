            Originally posted by Aya Tari View Post
            But the Dreaming itself dissolves True Magick like acid because it can unravel Patterns. I am sort of confused why there is the insistence that there is nothing the Technocrats cannot beat. Every other supernatural factions faces unconquerable foes (the Antediluvians for the Kindred, the Wyrm for the Garou, etc). Why not just accept that the Dreaming is the natural and unconquerable antithesis to the Technocracy?
            For the simple reason that there is, quite explicitly, nothing that cannot theoretically be done with true magic. Even the limits that we use to play the game are psychological blocks on the part of the player characters, this is HOW magic is generally taught and how it is generally learned. And there are even examples within the rules themselves of things that violate those rules with no real explanation or reason. The flintlock pistol that shoots lightning but causes no paradox and requires no quintessence, for example. Mage is a game about infinite possibilities, and thus infinite mysteries. Nothing is really revealed to you beyond what one set of people BELIEVE about why things happen. How things work.

            The other splats are all about being this badass amongst a world of cardboard, but having some unconquerable foe that even you, in all your might cannot defeat.

            Mage is about being one of those suckers made of cardboard realizing that they CAN do something about their situation. They CAN beat anything and anyone, if they're only good enough. If they're clever enough. If they BELIEVE enough. Do they always succeed? No. They don't. But mage is about being something that fundementally does not follow the same rules as everyone else. Sometimes this puts them on the top of the heap. Other times it drops them to the bottom. Even the technocrats have their highs and lows. Which is why so much of their organization is invested in mortals. To hold the line while the enlightened agents are busy exploding due to the paradox of the latest HITmark.

            Mage is about being a weak frail bastard who refuses to stop because, against what everyone would tell them, there is hope of victory. About taking that hope and forging it into a sword and either going down in a blaze of glory, or bending all of reality to your will.

                                                        If putting Cold Iron into a bullet and firing it from a gun is too great an engineering challenge for Iteration X - a huge assumption - they could always go from the next best thing: cold iron-tipped crossbow bolts. It's not like such a technology has been lost over the ages; the Order of Reason was using them way back in the Sorcerer's Crusade. Crossbows, that is, not necessarily cold iron bolts. (Although, given that the Fey were considered credible threats by even "rationalists" at the time, they could very well have employed cold iron weapons during that era). You could also use air-pressure dart guns, with darts made or tipped in cold iron.


                                                        As for making Cold Iron weapons, it comes down to whether the definition is "has only been worked by hand hammering", or "has never been heated". If it's the latter case, Iteration X has a potent answer to shaping metals without heat: pneumatic presses. They can beat raw ore into shape through mechanical strength.


                                                        As for if the Union would ever try using Cold Iron, remember that part of the Technocracy's MO is finding ways to rationalize to themselves why such-and-such seemingly supernatural phenomenon can work under scientific principles. Obviously, the so-called "Fairies" are disrupted by the specific harmonic and chemical signature of iron ore, a property that is lost in purer forms of the metal or alloys.

                                                        It's bullshit reasoning, but like hell a Technocrat is going to eschew that explanation in favor of "because magic". They're scientists, dammit, and scientists are willing to go along with facts if the data supports them. Theories can come later, when they've had a chance to dissect the specimens post-mortem.
