                            I assume the primary point of joining the Traditions for young Mystics would be to actually be able to advance as Mages at a reasonable rate. I find the idea of Orphans reaching more than 2 (or maybe 3) in any Sphere (or equivalent, if we are assuming Orphans do not use Spheres) to be very much at odds with how the setting of MtA is outlined. The 1st edition Corebook Magick section has in-setting introductions by a Mage tutor welcoming an Initiate and talking about a particular Sphere a bit. The impression these parts give to me is that Magick requires rigorous study, extreme efforts of comprehension and will, considerable perception and outlook shifting, and lots of time and hard work. The Correspondence section says outright that many Mages never attain Correspondence 2 (I assume it refers to Mages who attempt to study Correspondence Magick). Libraries and mentors are mentioned very often, as are debts owed for Magickal tutelage. If raising magickal capabilities was that easy, then why are the majority of Tradition Mages willing to submit to the extensive and often harsh obligations of being Apprentices and following mentors?
                            Another reason to join Traditions (or other Mage factions) - becoming more skilled in Magick Effects and widening their repertoire of them. And learning Rotes.

                            Of course, some Orphans would be the exception to this and able to grow wiser, stronger, and more potent by themselves or with limited contact with other Mages. But I think that such Orphans are extremely rare.

                            I have to say that I find the idea of a sizeable number of Orphans kind of confusing. As far as I understand it, the vast majority of Mages are either former Acolytes and Consors, or were Sleepers who caught the attention of a Mage and were led into Awakening. A good example of this is in the first Verbenae Tradition book - the Verbenae kidnap several persons, introduce them to their Paradigm, history, and outlook, and then lead them to Awakening, and these new Mages join the Tradition straight away.

                                We're well beyond what the first edition had to say on the subject. These days, the amount of study that it takes to develop your Arete and Spheres depends to a large extent on your Focus: some, like those found among the Order of Hermes or Sons of Ether, rely heavily on study; others, like those featuring Shamanism or Ecstatics or Faith, place more weight on some sort of supernatural revelation, something that can't be taught. In M20 in particular (which is the context of this thread), the idea that Orphans have a rougher time of it exists in precisely one form: that the Practice of Gutter Magic is more common among Orphans than nearly any mage faction (with the Hollow Ones being the noodle exception, in that they embraced Gutter Magic and raised it to an art form). In supporting material for M20, we're given a number of sample mages; and in that sample, most have either never heard of the Traditions or have chosen not to join them. (The martial artist, in particular, specifically did not join the Akashayana, and has philosophical disagreements with them.)

                                And the “most mages are former consors or acolytes” thing is also, at best, a relic of the first edition that has largely been left by the wayside. The Hermetic effort to teach sorcery as a path toward Awakening is generally presented as a mixed bag, with no conclusive proof that it actually works well enough to be worth the effort. Primers are a thing; but they basically nudge someone who's already almost there across the starting line. The impression I get is that most Awakenings are spontaneous.

                                        Of the first three game-lines, Mage has one of the most versatile systems for creating the backstory of how the splat-character came to join.

                                        In Vampire, you sink your fangs into someone's body-part-you-prefer and turn them into what you are. Some guidance afterwards is helpful if you want this neonate to do you proud.

                                        In Werewolf, you produce offspring and keep an eye on the bloodline waiting for the recessive gene to manifest.

                                        The first is direct. The second is somewhat indirect.

                                        Mage can be anywhere in between. Mages try to propagate their ideas in sleeper society so that some will Awaken and be drawn toward their Paradigm (comparable to Werewolves mating and waiting). Mages can also take a direct approach, selecting a worthy sleeper and coaching them toward Awakening or even attempting to force an Awakening with drastic, Vulgar methods (comparable to Vampires). I don't care if one edition of MtA emphasizes one approach over the other. I am for all of these approaches being available to player backstory and in-game recruitment efforts.

                                                        No offense intended; but given the nature of this thread, that's a big oversight. Because Brucato has taken the stance that that's where the majority of Awakening's come from: not the actions of the Traditions/other mages, but spontaneous Awakenings.

                                                        The most that mages typically contribute is their efforts to seed the culture with ideas compatible with Awakening, putting the building blocks necessary for constructing a Focus that's rugged enough to be used for Willworking. But the actual spark that's needed to Awaken isn't something they can trigger, for the most part. And without a reliable means of triggering an Awakening, they have to disseminate their ideas as far and wide as possible in order to have enough of a chance of equipping someone to Awaken to make the effort worthwhile.

                                                        But the further you spread your ideas, the harder it is to monitor those who receive them and if you spread them far enough to have a decent chance of Awakening one mage, chances are really good that that new mage will Awaken somewhere that you're not watching. Because even the NWO doesn't have that extensive of a monitoring operation.

                                                        In short: if Embraces are direct and Garou breeding is somewhat indirect, Awakenings tend to be extremely indirect, to the point that drawing a connection between Mage efforts to encourage Awakenings and the Awakenings themselves is nearly impossible.
