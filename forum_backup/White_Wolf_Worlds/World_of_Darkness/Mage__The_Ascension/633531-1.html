        Originally posted by AgentCunningham View Post
        Aleph, when you have a spell that has no activation anymore, locked jn etc. you can't go around giving paradox, it's unfair. The caster did the extra success, difficulties for lenght, for the success etc.
        Actually, that's not entirely true. Or at least it hasn't always been. Some time ago, in an example of how Revised Paradox rules worked, Malcolm Sheppard gave an example of a fellow who was using a stealth/invisibility spell (via manipulating shadows) to climb down a wall. At the top, it was fine, there were shadows and it was dark. As he descended, search lights and such eventually grew too bright and the spell would have to be dropped unless the player went full on vulgar. He did, and took the paradox. (The exact post is here: http://shadownessence.se/forum/topic...-works/?page=1 It's still a helpful read, I think, even though Revised rules are no longer the default.)

        Now this version wasn't a published rule (closest comparison would be creator-written informal errata) and I honestly can't remember if a similar example made it into an actual sourcebook. This was also Revised Paradox, which does work differently from Mage 20, though whether it would in this particular area is unclear (I'm hoping the Sphere Guide will address it). But it does at least provide a sample situation for when environmental factors can change whether or not a spell is vulgar or coincidental while it has already been cast.

        I'll admit, something like 10 dragons already starts off so vulgar that I can't really imagine a situation where I feel it would be appropriate to add more paradox on top of it. But there is at least anecdotal precedent to justify the idea.

                        It occurs to me that while I don't have any objection to a storyteller embracing a player's very crazy idea and running with it, I think there's always something that needs to be considered. What about the other players in your game? Are THEY willing to deal with the consequences of this one guy's crazy actions? Will they still have fun when the dragons are consuming everything in sight or when the Technocracy goes full brunt against them? (Heck, even in a one-on-one game, the storyteller counts as a player, would YOU have fun running this game?)

                        As a storyteller, you really have the responsibility to try to be sure all the players are enjoying themselves and not to allow one person to ruin everyone else's fun. So if you allow something like the grand summoning of 12 golden dragons, then you really need to make sure your other players are on board.

                                        (This is directed toward Darth Revan's post above. I apologize - I hadn't seen AgentCunningham's or Ambrosia's posts or I would have included a quotation for clarity)

                                        That depends on how he buffs his strength, if he does it over 5, Mage 20 (see Quantumboost's post on the life rituals thread) seems to indicate that he'd keep some permanent paradox until the effect ceases. (Then it can be burned like a normal paradox point.) That's not really a terrible consequence. (You wouldn't accumulate more temp paradox on top, unless he does some additional working.) I admit, I've never used the possible Genetic Flaw alternative, so I'm not sure how that would work in game.

                                        651 of Mage 20 addresses Wonders and Paradox. It points out that a wonder gaining paradox is dependent on the coincidence/vulgar determination and the presence of witnesses at the time of its use. (I would read that qualification to mean that whether or not something is coincidental/vulgar could also be influenced by the context.). So if someone shoots from a distance and it's plausible that they missed, the silk shirt and hoodie would be fine and coincidental. If it's an unmistakable point blank shot in front of witnesses, I think you'd be getting paradox. It does appear clear from that section that the determination is made each time the wonder is used. I hope when the more involved Wonder rules that the book promises come out, we'll get a bit more detail about how to use this.

                                        The funny thing about an armored silk shirt is that you're less likely to get paradox if someone is shooting at you than if they tried to stab you or hit you with a bat. It's more plausible that someone could miss shooting, less so that a silk shirt would offer any sort of padding or insulation from a knife or blunt force. (...you might be able to argue that the blunt force could be mitigated by the attacker not hitting as hard as it looked, actually. The knife wound is trickier.)
