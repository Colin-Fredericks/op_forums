            Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
            This is something I've been pondering recently. If we could toss out the existing structure of the otherworlds and remake it from scratch, without having to worry too much about other game lines, how would you do it?

            For my own part I'd probably:

            - Move away from a clearly defined gauntlet.
            - Make the astral, spirit, and under worlds very different things and not just different flavors of umbra.
            - Move more of the strangeness into the physical world.
            - Make more room for realms that don't actually fit anywhere but exist anyways

            I haven't gotten much further than that. What would you do?
            This is for Mage, right? Not “The World of Darkness”; Mage.

            So we don't need to keep an Umbra that's consistent with the Garou, a Shadowlands that's consistent with Wraith, or a Dreaming that's consistent with Changeling; we can do our own thing without any concern about any of the other gamelines. Right?

            On the other hand, this is Mage, where a huge variety of belief systems coexist side by side; and ideally you can't pin down any one of them as being more true than any of the others. So chances are that if we can come up with something that satisfies Mage, it will naturally have something in it for the various other World of Darkness gamelines. That's not the goal; but it's likely to be the result.

            The following isn't a well-thought-out structure for the Otherworlds; it's more of a hodgepodge of ideas.

            I think what I'd do would probably be something like what Scion 2e does, with each Tradition, Convention, and Craft having a set of Reality Zones and Otherworlds of its own, in keeping with the cosmology favored by that Tradition. The Order of Hermes might have its Otherworlds organized into four planes of reality: Assiah (the Material), Yetzirah (the Astral), Briah (the Iconic), and Atziluth (the Spiritual). The Society of Ether would have the Hollow Earth and Etherspace. The Verbenae would have a Tree of Life, which its Nordic members would call Yggdrasil and who also identify as a bridge between the Nine Realms, of which Earth or Midgard is but one. The Virtual Adepts would have their Digital Web. Among the Traditions, the Akashayana would be the most likely to have Realms based on the human mind; and among the Conventions, the New World Order would be most likely to have access to psychic Realms. The Dreamspeakers, oddly, probably wouldn't have an Otherworld, per se; they'd deal with spirits that exist in and around this world. This in contrast to the Euthanatoi, who would be linked into the various Afterlives. And the Celestial Chorus would have access to Heaven and Hell.

            Getting to these Otherworlds would normally involve going to a suitable Reality Zone and bridging over to the Otherworld from there. Depending on the mage's Focus, it might be possible to “cross over” from other places; but even then, it would be easier to do so from an appropriate Reality Zone.

            Travel to Otherworlds wouldn't be the domain of any one Sphere; the Sphere you use would depend on the type of Otherworld you're going to. Correspondence can get a VA to the Digital Web, or a Void Engineer into Hyperspace; Entropy lets the Euthanatoi visit the Afterlives; Mind lets Akashics and NWO agents enter Mindscapes; Prime gives the Celestial Chorus access to Heaven and Hell. Forces and Matter could provide access to Elemental Planes. And Time could provide access to the past, the future, and alternate histories.

            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                Well, the "too humanistic" part is true. First of all, we need to take into consideration that animism is an anthropomorphization of nature. Therefore, every single mystical or religious idea in history is inevitably "human-centric". Because that's how the primitive and ancient humans tried to make sense of a senseless nature, by attributing human characteristics to it.

                                                Trying to get rid of such a "tendency" would funnily mean to move away from a "mystical" background and towards a more mechanicist viewpoint; in essence, it would be a sort of "scientificization" of the myths, where instead of having nature with human characteristics, they are viewed as impersonal forces.

                                                Sure thing, if the spirit world was real and approachable towards some sort of scientific method, that would probably be more like the answer; on top of our material nature that doesn't care about humans at all and that neither hates nor loves us but instead doesn't think or feel at all, and therefore wouldn't miss us if we were extinct, we then would have yet another layer of nature that is completely oblivious to us or towards itself.

                                                But that's not how mystical views are formed, since they are all of them absolutely human-centric.

                                                That however, was not behind the reasoning I used to adopt such a model - and neither does it represents the WoD Umbra.

                                                As I have already said ad nauseum, WoD is a heavily Gnostic/Hermetic setting. If the writers did so because they are secretly cultists gathering under auspicious moons in the middle of the night wearing cloaks and sacrificing animals to their hermetic Masters, or if the writers did so after extensive research into esoteric literature, which inevitably would end up ending into Gnostic/Theosophical/Hermetic literature, since it is an ancient, prolific and syncretic set of beliefs that have ended up "infecting" many other currents of theology and philosophy (for example, Immanuel Kant and Hegel works drip Gnosticism throughout their entire works), that is debatable. If I were to bet, I would say that the writers were searching esoteric literature and eventually ended up with a copy of the "Corpus Hermeticum" and from there on, everything they wrote started to be done following the mantra "As Above, So Bellow".

                                                Be that as it may, WoD is absolutely Gnostic/Hermetic throughout all of its pages, up to the point that Vampires were created by a being that can only be described as the evil Demiurge of Gnosticism.

                                                So, this idea that the Spirit Worlds function as the "human mind", is absolutely rooted under Gnostic/Hermetic thought. It is in the essence of "As Above, so Bellow" - which means at the end exactly that, the human mind is a reflection of "God's mind". Because, as above (God), so bellow (human).

                                                Hegelian dialectics for instance is purely Gnostic thinking. As is the immaterialism of Emmanuel Kant. It's the idea that EVERYTHING is just "God's Mind".

                                                In Gnostic mythology, although I'm heavily simplifying, first there was God, and God was just mind, it was pure intellect, pure mind.

                                                And, because everything was God's mind, everything that existed was essentially a byproduct of God's imagination.

                                                Yes, Gnosticism is solipsism (or distilled and twice pure narcissism) elevated to infinite.

                                                Eventually, God begun to mistake his imagination with reality, unable to understand that the things around himself/herself/itself were just products of its imagination, not the reality of things.

                                                Then God got "shattered", and each shatter became a "soul" - that's us. We are fragments of the divine.

                                                The bigger fragment, however, is called the "Demiurge", and is the God of the Bible (and plenty of other Gods from other religions), and the Demiurge is our enemy, he is our imprisoner. The Demiurge, being that he is a being with a larger portion of the fragments of the original God, enjoyed this fake reality created by the imagination of God, and seeks to rule over it, and the last thing that the Demiurge wants is for the other shards of God's soul to reunite and acknowledge that they are God and that everything that is isn't real, is just imagination. So, the Demiurge created the Material World as a prison for the shards of God, so that they can't awaken for the truth.

                                                Luckily for the Gnostics/Hermetics, they can "awaken" due to "Divine Revelation" (that's literally what the word "Gnosis" in Greek means, Divine Revelation. No, it is NOT "knowledge", but rather specifically "knowledge acquired by DIVINE REVELATION) and the guidance of their "Hermetic Masters", to which they claim that figures like Jesus or Buddha were just a few. And their purpose is to guide the whole of humanity towards a "collective awakening", upon which we would all come to the realization that we are in fact God, and then we would transcend the prison of the Demiurge.

                                                That's the origin of those concepts.

                                                As you can see, all this mythology seem to be an almost perfect fit for WoD - again, I believe that this has been accidental, but if you see primarily Mage and Demon, there's no denying, but even the others are pretty close to it.

                                                That's probably why the Umbra has such a configuration; I also have to say, there's many ideas present on the WoD Umbra that seem to be taken directly from the real life Corpus Hermeticum book. I have some knowledge about that, so I can say as much. Sure, there's some artistic and creative modifications, but it is pretty closely aligned with the real world Hermetic mythology. No doubt this has been one of the major inspirations for the games.
