    Anti-Revised Metaplot rules

    03-22-2022, 08:19 AM
    My overwhelming dislike (no Edition Warring intended, I am just trying to describe my purpose) of the MRev Metaplot and the playstyle that it favored had long since driven me to prefer optional and/or house rules and setting changes that may nullify its effects. Of late, I have grown wondering how best to apply them properly in fine detail.

    I fancy the notion of human-like supernaturals such as mages and changelings being as resilient to lethal damage as vampires and shapeshifters, and I am not entirely averse to the notion of the sturdiest mortals sharing in the benefit. However, I also like the idea of supernaturals, no matter their kind or physique, being more resilient than mortals by default, and grandmothers not being able to soak shotgun blasts. Therefore, I have tentatively thought of making Cinematic Damage the default, but modifying it to make all mages and changelings, and humans with Stamina 3+, able to soak lethal. Setting rationale is that Awakening or having an active fae soul strenghtens the body just as being a vampire or shapeshifter, if in a different way. Speaking in flavor and symbolic terms, all mages have Too Tough to Die at no cost in these terms.

    About Acclimation and Disembodiment, I dislike them just as much, but I find M20 treatment of them confusing. The book acknowledges Acclimation was caused and sustained by the Avatar Storm, and so was accelerated Disembodiment. However, it also retroactively grandfathers a lesser form of Disembodiment as always having existed. As far as I can remember, however, Disembodiment was never a thing in1st/2nd Edition. Therefore, I have tentatively thought of letting all mages, or at least the ones whose paradigm acknowledges the existence of the Otherworlds (mystics, Etherites, Virtual Adepts, Void Engineers, etc.), have Umbral Affinity at no cost. The mages whose paradigm makes the Umbra a big deal, as well as the ones with an inherent connection to the Umbra such as being a Natural Channel, Nephilim/Laham, Deathwalker, etc. also have that, plus the equivalent of Shapeshifter Kin (without the actual blood ties) at no cost. I am conflicted at doing away with Acclimation and Disembodiment entirely.

    The Avatar Storm never existed, or it was a brief and minor event. Its destructive effects get partially replaced by other setting changes, without restricting access to the Umbra. Speaking in flavor and symbolic terms, all mages have Stormwarden at no cost.

    Vulgar without Witnesses does not exist, immortality spells do not cause Permadox, and mages roll Arete + Sphere to cast magick as in DA:M and M: tSC. Setting rationale is the Consensus never got strong enough to enforce Paradox without the support of active and present Sleeper disbelief or the mage bungling the spell, or weaken magick casting.

    Consequently, immortality without Paradox trouble is the default state of sufficiently powerful and experienced mages, unless they somehow do not want to be or fail to avoid the hazards of their lifestyle. Elder mages play as relevant a role in supernatural society as elder vampires, but in both cases, other setting changes prevent supernatural society from getting too static.

    Opnions? Suggestions?

          Originally posted by Irioth View Post

The book acknowledges Acclimation was caused and sustained by the Avatar Storm, and so was accelerated Disembodiment. However, it also retroactively grandfathers a lesser form of Disembodiment as always having existed. As far as I can remember, however, Disembodiment was never a thing in1st/2nd Edition.
          M20 was written by the premier expert on the first two editions of Mage. If he says that there was a lesser version of Disembodiment before MRev, you can trust that there was. And in fact, there was:
          Disconnection: … Explorers without shapeshifter blood eventually lose their connection to the Earth if they stay in the Umbra for long. After a while (one to four months for most people, four months to a year for primal mysticks), they forget their lives on Earth and become spirits. Their physical bodies fall away (or die, in the case of astral travelers) and their consciousness shifts them into spirits which embody their true nature. This is a one-way process. … People who dwell in Horizon Realms don't suffer this effect, but gradually take on the aspects of the Realm in which they live, and they shape that Realm in return.

          Book of Worlds p.183

          I personally have some difficulty wrapping my head around the notion that “three months” (MRev) is less than “one to four months” (M2e); but there you go.


          The bottom line, as far as I'm concerned, is that the writer of M20 took great pains to highlight what the differences between M2 and MRev are, and to give you the choice between the two. If there's a lack of understanding of anything on his part, it was of some of the MRev material. You shouldn't need to house-rule M20 at all to get something that's in accordance with M2.

          And as Heavy Arms points out, a number of your musings here aren't really about “anti-Revised” rules; they're more “anti all-editions”. But, like I said in another thread, you do you.

          With that said, you might want to consider adopting a design concept from Mage: the Awakening 2e, namely the concept of Attainments. This idea is that as you progress through the Arcana (Awakening's counterpart to the Spheres), you gradually become more inherently magical, and develop some innate abilities that serve as permanent Effects. Some of these are just a case of formalizing particular rule quirks, such as needing one dot in an Arcanum in order to counterspell Effects created by that Arcanum, or the fact that two dots of Space (i.e., Correspondence) lets you use the Sympathetic Range chart. But others are more drastic: the first dot in an Arcanum grants Mage Sight, meaning that any supernal phenomena involving that Arcanum is mystically highlighted for you; no casting needed. And the second dot grants Mage Armor, providing Arcanum-appropriate protection.

          I could see implementing an M20 counterpart to this, where anyone with two dots of Life gets to soak lethal damage; although compared to the various benefits that Awakening's Attainments provide, that's actually quite mild.

          My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                          3) Two innate Purviews (their Pantheon Purview and one of their parent's Purviews) with their associated innate bonuses, and whatever Boons you take (nothing stops you from keeping your Boons within these two Purviews), and the ability to use them to create Marvels.

                                                          They can get more powers via Guides and Relics (and bonuses from Birthrights in general), but those powers are limited by access to them. Just like mage can't use a Wonder if it's not on hand. While Scion 2e provides considerable benefits to taking more Purviews and Boons through this method, they're not mandatory.

                                                          At Demigod, their Purviews get a huge upgrade (we already know what this looks like). The only think that would at all seem lacking to your interests in getting more innate Purviews since that seems to be saved for completely apotheosis. Dominion, Divine Dice, and Casual Miracles are all fairly big deals.

                                                          The only problem I can really see for your stated preferences is that Demigod keeps things to two innate Purviews, and the restrictions on which those are; but if you feel like Scion 2e demigods aren't somehow powerful enough, having them build up their innate Pureviews over the journey from Legend 5 to 9 instead of waiting for 9 to hit them with yet another massive jump in power isn't hard to do. It would pretty much necessitate moments like the MCU Thor reference as part of the apotheosis arc Demigods go through.
                                                          IIRC the one Pantheon Purview that had any interest for me was the Theoi one, since I love shapeshifting. Having to channel all of my powers through a single Purview or two is a serious red flag for me since another gaming thing I hate is power specialization and thematic constraint. As much as I fancy min-maxing in other matters, I love power variety, flexibility, and redundance. A character of mine tends to have at least a half-dozen broad power types, with at least half of being good for combat uses in different or redundant ways. I am rarely satisfied to throw fireballs, although it is almost a prerequisite; I want to use fireballs, lightning bolts, ice bolts, super-strong punches, and TK crushes or throws.

                                                          E.g. my ideal superhuman (assuming we can treat Scions as such) is OP, immortal, a flying brick, a generalist elementalist, an energy projector, a healer and/or a Wolvie-style regenerator, a telekinetic, and a shapeshifter. They are also able to do all of that more or less at leisure w/o any reliance on tools (except possibly for an additional power boost and/or finesse focus) or special activation conditions on a limited budget when going beyond a narrow thematic focus. Having to rely on a juice pool for any serious power use on equal terms for different abilities may or may not be acceptable depending on circumstances (i.e. size of the pool and difficulty to recharge).

                                                          If they were to be a God, and not so rarely have been in appropriate ttrpgs and cyoas, they would be the God of Strength/Might (or any other Domain necessary for all-around physical prowess), Life, Fire/Sun, and Sky/Weather. I may often add Water, Earth, Magic, Knowledge, Love/Lust, and/or Trickery to round up the portofolio and expand it beyond the core concept.

                                                          This rings somewhat hollow to me. You love M20 because... you can ignore so much of it instead of just playing games that actually suit the kind of power level you want to play? That's the point of investing in such a customization heavy system if you're only in it to build the same thing every time?
                                                          Well, what can I say? I suppose I am the gaming equivalent of those authors that prefer to create endless variations of the same few concepts and stories. I like developing a few optimized templates and concepts that reflect my preferences satisfactorily, then build endless variations of them in different games. I suppose it is the result of gaming often being a self-insert wish fulfilment for me, having a serious optimizer streak, and being a "my way or the highway" individualist.
