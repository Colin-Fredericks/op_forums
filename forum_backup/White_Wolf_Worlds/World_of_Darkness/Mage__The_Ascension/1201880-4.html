    Actually, yes, I can.

    I reject this idea entirely. If magic is the power to warp reality (which it seems to be in Mage), and it is governed by the strength of your imagination, then that would logically mean that magic has no limitations. As long as you believe in it, it becomes possible, regardless of the general consensus is to the contrary. In laymen's terms, you dictate the rules, which are as malleable as your own interpretation of them.
    Except that's not how it is in Mage: the Ascension. There are concrete laws of Reality created by the Consensus. That's why Reality works as it does. Magic, Enlightened Science, Willworking, whatever you wish to call it, is the bending or breaking of those laws to achieve something. And only the Awakened can truly do individually. For the Sleepers it is simply their collective subconscious will working together to establish the laws of the Consensus.

    Sure, you can get groups of Sleepers to agree on things contrary to those laws and create a bubble where those laws function that way but it will be small and only work in isolation as in the face of the greater collective of Sleepers, will bow to it.

    Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
    That's why for the longest time, I have considered magic and science to be different from each other. Science tries to apply rules and deductive reasoning to how things work. Magic chooses to ignore those laws and reasoning, making it beyond the realm of scientific explanation.
    Again, that's against Mage: the Ascension. Magic, Faith, Science, they're all one and the same. They are how mere mortals can comprehend the Reality-altering powers at their fingertips and are ultimately an incomplete understanding because to fully and truly understand it is to become... well God basically. To cease to be something mortals can comprehend and become something beyond.

    In Mage: the Ascension, everybody is right, everybody is wrong because none of them can have truly complete picture of everything.

    In fact, that's actually built into the gameplay. As a Mage gains more understanding, they start discarding Foci because they are beginning to realize that they don't need their Foci, that their Beliefs are flawed and incomplete.

    Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
    If they want an even playing field, then the Technocracy could easily settle for using pocket dimensions, and give everybody (or at least every mage) the freedom to create their own personalized universes. That way, everyone is happy without stepping on anybody's toes.


    But no, they don't trust us to wield that kind of power. And if they don't trust us, then why in the hell would we trust them? Because they claim to have the vision necessary to lead us into the future? Yeah, just how many tyrannical dictators have used that exact line of reasoning to justify their own regimes? And how often did such regimes end in disaster as a result of their leader's hubris?
    Except that won't work. As I said, for the Sleepers they affect the Consensus through their subconscious which includes their doubts and hesitations. One of the biggest problems any Mage group has in spreading their beliefs is that even if the Sleepers buy into it, many will privately or even subconsciously doubt it. The thought we've all had, 'It's too good to be true'. And that's all it takes for it to come apart or worse, cause Paradox for the poor Sleepers. In fact, that's why modern medical procedures and drugs don't always were or cause side effects, the Sleepers think it's too good to be true and the Consensus makes it so.

    Homo sapiens. What an inventive, invincible species. It's only a few million years since they crawled up out of the mud and learned to walk. Puny, defenceless bipeds. They've survived flood, famine and plague. They've survived cosmic wars and holocausts. And now, here they are, out among the stars, waiting to begin a new life. Ready to outsit eternity. One story in particular involved the PC's going to Japan, to hunt a Nephandus who they'd been after for some time, and they ended up working with the Technocracy to do it through said PC's connections. Which meant I ended up having to do my background reading on the Technocracy in Asia...throwing it all out because it was more or less garbage, and writing my own history.

                                                        Long story short, the real history of the collapse of the Tokugawa era was that the Tokugawa government was hopelessly indebted to the merchant caste, who was the lowest-status caste in Japanese feudal society. The samurai caste got around this by forgiving their own debts, and continuing to extort money from the merchants. Needless to say, the merchants didn't like this. So, when the Sat-Cho Alliance rose up against the Tokugawa government in the 1860's, the merchants backed them and the rest was history.

                                                        For my Mage game, I'd decided the mystics had backed the priesthood and samurai all this time. A foundation for the Order of Reason had already been built up among the merchant and artisan caste, led by Mitsui- and Sumitomo-affiliated mages who resented Tokugawa and mystic rule. During the Meiji Restoration and numerous samurai rebellions, this Japanese proto-OoR found itself at the top of the newly-forming pecking order, and Mitsui and Sumitomo were joined by Mitsubishi and Yasuda. When the Japanese government started really sending out citizens to study at Western universities and companies to learn and return with their knowledge, Zaibatsu-affiliated Mages went out too, to learn under the nascent Technocratic Union.

                                                        The Technocracy was entirely happy with this, because it was their non-imperialist inroad to Japan. So were the Japanese mages, since their regime was to Westernize, become economically and militarily powerful, and grow their sphere of interest to prevent further Western influence or control. During this time, the Zaibatsu were entirely independent from the Technocratic Union, but close enough to the Technocracy's own ideology and goals, and not European enough, to be worth assimilation or further attention.

                                                        Except, the Zaibatsu fucked up big time. The majority of their people, already being merchants and interested in business and economics, went to study under Exchequers. Of the minority, most of those went to study under the Electrodyne Engineers. A handful of others went to study elsewhere. When the Etherites abandoned the Technocratic Union, the Japanese Etherites largely remained because they were loyal to zaibatsu and country, not Convention; this drew (entirely warranted) questions from the Ivory Tower about the zaibatsus' true loyalties. driving a rift between the Zaibatsu and the larger Technocracy. But by then, Japan was almost entirely of, by, and for the Syndicate, albeit a ludicrously militarized and nationalist version of the Syndicate, wherein the Zaibatsu acted as miniature Conventions within the larger whole.

                                                        After WWII, the Technocracy stepped in, busted up the Zaibatsu, demilitarized the Japanese Syndicate along with the rest of the country, and split up/re-educated the Japanese mages to align to the contemporary Conventions and Methodologies. Of course, the Japanese Syndicate was too powerful and entrenched, and the measures taken too little, so the Japanese Syndicate reformed as the keiretsu, but this time around with just enough external oversight by other Conventions to stay under the radar.

                                                        So, basically, what I'd written was an example of a non-European collective of mages observing the Technocratic Union, learning and taking from them what would work respective to their own culture and societal goals, and creating their own vaguely Technocratic society of mages which stayed independent from the greater whole for nearly a century. And, when the Technocracy eventually intervened, they adapted enough to make the Technocracy happy, while maintaining enough independence to maintain their own group identity and pursue their own goals in alignment with the greater Technocratic vision.
