    What's the point of being Bad?

    08-07-2017, 04:57 AM
    Not talking about Nephandi-level bad or Infernally bad; these are complex questions.

    I'm talking about your average Mage. Ordinary Avatar. Doesn't want to destroy the world. Largely sane. Why would they want to sacrifice humans for their magic?

    Yes, humans are a source of Quintessence. So are Horses. Yes, humans can be used as a focus for Maleficia, Blood and Sacrifices etc. There are many alternatives to *actual human sacrifice*. Basically I have to wonder what the incentive is to be cruel or evil. Is there any mechanical advantage? Or is it hand-waved as "some people are evil and they don't need a mechanical advantage"? Because evil people IRL tend to get something from it, even in a very short-sighted and idiotic way.

    I've yet to find any real advantage to sacrificing someone, beyond the possibility that this may count as pulling on a Mythic Thread or using a Unique Instrument if the person is suitable; easily replicated. What if human sacrifice absorbed a bit of Paradox? The idea would be that their departing Quintessence would be partly consumed by the Paradox generated, possibly obliterating their soul altogether. So if the spell is Vulgar and Successful then it absorbs the one point of Paradox. If the spell is Vulgar and Botches then it absorbs Paradox so that the backlash is 3x Highest Level Sphere, which could be a significant reduction.

    Or you could significantly reduce how much Quintessence you can get from animals, using a Vampire-type justification; yes an Elephant might be HUGE and it might seem logical that they'd contain a lot of Blood/Quintessence, but they are less energy-rich than a human; their soul is on a similar level to that of a dog or monkey, they just have a much much larger body to go with it. Simple, unintelligent animals would have a Quintessence of 1, smart animals (octopi, dogs, monkeys, elephants, dolphins) would have a Quintessence of 3 due to a slightly greater state of self-awareness. Now one human sacrifice is three times as potent as a dog sacrifice, which is a big enough gap that a sociopath might prefer a human...

    Have I missed some advantage to virgin sacrifice? Does it need a mechanical incentive?

      "Being" evil is something that in my opinion happens in bad character writing, or wanting to be edgy. I prefer to see it as unempathetic towards another inidividuals, having a might is right mindset etc. and with the mindset comes the advantages.

      Amorality doesn't begin with human sacrifice, which is more at the "depraved" "apathetic" end of the spectrum. A moral character for example would prefer to defeat their enemies without defeat, fight them honorably etc. An amoral character could use poison gas, explosives, traps or leave mutilated corpses (or corpses in a generally bad shape, being cutin two for example) behind to disturb their enemies and weaken their morale.

      It only makes sense to act evil when you gain efficiency, or a psychologycal bonus from it.

      Even though I find the notion of sacrificing souls interisting, you have to think about what big of a crime this is. Normally, if you would sacrifice a human, you would burn up his bodily pattern and absorb his quintessence. If you would like to also render their soul, you would need in my guess at least Prime 4 and either spirit 3 or 4, depending on ST choice.

      Large Animals are used in sacrifices for thousands of years and an animal with somewhat the body mass of a human should give the same amount of quintessence as you are absorbing the live pattern. The benefit in human sacrifice could very well be in mythic threads, which is not to be underestimated as every difficulty reduction in an arete throw can be a significant boon. Also there is the notion that different kind of quintessence has different resonance. The essence gained from sacrificing humans would have entropic resonances, making them very usefull in destructive rituals, but less so in constructive ones.

      At last, I don't see any reason to lesser you paradox for human sacrifice as reality doesn't really care about what you do. If you burn the body and harness the essence with Prime 4, you can do it coincidentally, if you botch, you don't harness energy and the paradox is yours. If you would use Prime 5 to just "rip away their life force", no chance of this being not vulgar, unless you are in a special reality zone.

              You are right. Since all magic in Mage is really Chaos Magic deep inside, everyone who choses to use an evil paradigm is a tool. As is, admittedly, everyone who doesn't immediately believe in Chaos Magic.

              The two main categories of evil mages are nephandi and infernalists. Nephandi use evil paradigms because they are Always Chaotic Evil and evil is the whole point of being a Nephandi. They don't really need or have any reasons for using evil paradigms other than "I'm a Nephandi". Infernalists don't really need to use evil paradigms, but they usually do because it pleases their patrons. And pleased demons may just give you a load of investments without taking a chunk of your soul, so it all works out.

              "Hypernarrative" mumbo jumbo is basically useless. It's a -1 modifier to your spellcasting, and the maximum possible modifier is -3. You know what also gives modifiers? Spending additional turns gives -1 per turn. Using personalized instrument gives -1. Being near a Node and/or doing research gives -1 to -3. Pretty much any time you might use a sacrifice to justify "hypernarrative" you might as well just spend another minute and get full -3 modifier with no sacrifices.

                      Perhaps to make a point. For example a Verbena might us a Progenitor or an Iterator as a source of quintessence as payback for that same progenitor using one of their own to power one of their horrible machines.

                      Perhaps it's to kill without being wasteful. A dreamspeaker may consider their enemy to be perverting the power Mother Gaia has bestowed upon them, and seeks to redistribute this power by offering their enemy back into her embrace. Sacrifice her and allow the quintessence to be used for good, and thus their life would not have been wasted even if their living caused greater harm to Gaia.

                      Perhaps the sacrifice is part of a way to save them. A Euthanatos might think a ritual is the proper way to provide one with the Good Death. Offering their soul to Kali to ensure it is properly reincarnated. The Quintessence left behind then is to be used by the Euthanatos mage in question. It is the last remnants of their being and thus must be used in order to allow the mage's avatar to properly reincarnate without ties to the previously doomed existence.

                      There are loads of reasons a mage can incorporate ritual sacrifice into their crafts. You just need to think thematically rather than purely mechanically.

                                              In some cases, a particular paradigm allows for more potent effects if sacrifice is involved. There's a Prime 3 effect in the Verbena tradbook that goes beyond the typical quintessence you'd get, and says that if you sacrifice a willing Awakened, you can raise a Node's rating. A bunch of fiction in the book describes a use of that rote, to empower a faltering Node on a Verbena farm. The Verbena paradigm is all about blood and sacrifice and it makes sense for them.

                                              From a mechanical perspective, you could easily argue this: "Either Prime 3 allows you to sacrifice a willing Awakened to empower a Node or it doesn't. If it does, then you don't need to be Verbena to do it. If it doesn't, then the rote is broken and should include something else, like lots of Prime." That's all fair, but I would argue with both of those points.

                                              First, the Sphere system is a rough outline. Not everyone with Prime 3 can do everything listed under Prime 3, because they are limited by their paradigm - and no one has the paradigm of "whatever it says in the Mage corebook". So it is in theory possible that Prime 3 does allow that effect and nobody but the Verbena - or another group whose paradigm is equally based around sacrifice - can actually cast it.

                                              Second, yes, you could imagine replicating this effect outside of that rote with Prime 5. But it seems odd that to do such an effect, you'd need the ability to enchant items - but by the Sphere system you would, because Prime 5 requires Prime 4. Think about it like Dark Ages: Mage with the Pillars system - where each group had their own path of powers. Hermetics threw fireballs by command of elemental forces via the Forma of Vires. Old Faith witches drew out the strength of Summer to do the same, and Valdaermen used the Hjaldar rune. So Hermetics who could throw fireballs could also refract light, but the witches and the Valdaermen couldn't - which makes sense based on their paradigms. So I don't mind bending the Sphere rules, as if a Verbena's Prime sphere is a little like a "Verbena Prime" Pillar with slight variance from what, say, a Progenitor might do.

                                              To bring it back to your original question: some paradigms would require sacrifice to produce typical effects, and sometimes effects beyond the common use of those Sphere levels. A Hermetic might call that Prime 3 rote "evil" but a Verbena certainly wouldn't. Power in Mage is based in beliefs and if you believe you need to sacrifice to get that power, that is actually true for you.
