    Originally posted by ebakunin View Post
    In the past, Technocracy super-science has been rather straightforward, even if it's like Blade Runner or Star Trek. What if it becomes so complex that only Archmasters can understand it? Or better, only AIs (or the Incarna that is Computer). What if Technocrats lost control over technology?
    Originally posted by Aleph View Post
    What you describe it's the experience of every non-enligthened scientist on the Technocracy (citizens are handed pieces of hypertech not even the best of them can understand all the time).

    There's also rare stuff that the Technocrats use and can only be understood by Archmages, there's a neat example in the VE Convention book: A defect you can buy for Void Ships it's that the ship uses a core that was built by an Archmage and no ordinary mage understands how it works (so, if it breaks you're in trouble).
    I've been pondering a campaign setting (Or heck, theoretically even the wishful concept of WoD gamelines) that deals with a situation that would *perfectly* fit this; A Fallout / Mad Max / TriGun / Last Of Us / etc-esque time in the WoD that plays after a global apocalypse. Whatever that apocalypse might have taken form as.

    Warning: Long post ahead.



    Was it mundane war? Was it the Wyrm? A magickal accident, triggered by hubris? Nephandi? Did the Ratkin unleash the mother of plagues as a radical measure? Was the Wyrm somehow *beaten* and did Gaia, with the triat restored, decide to reset some things through natural desasters? Is everything a wasteland, or is everything being reclaimed by nature? A mix?

    Perhaps it's in the far enough future that people don't even quite remember and history meets fable in the minds of the surviving people. No matter what the origins are, mankind is back to smaller size.
    And as we are biologically prone to, we are back to more tribal communities and havens. People struggle and survive, some communities had early access to more technology and knowledge, some less so. But in general, things are pre-industrial-revolution as the means to mass-produce simply are gone, and some places are downright medieval.

    What does this mean for the supernaturals?
    Ignoring different effects depending on the nature of the apocalypse, it's clear that certain structures of the supernatural world also crumbled. large sects and groups crumbled or fractured. It's a time of schisms. And ,mirroring human society, more tribalism. Vampires are back to a more feudal state. 'Low' clans handled the apocalypse way easier than those used to luxuries and power. The Garou Nation, with the common big enemy having lost (or won), lost its reason d'etre and has broken up. Human communities are protected by certain gifted individuals able to deal with danger more than others (Hunters).

    And in general, as with human tribes and more separation due to lack of any long-distance communication goes, 'superstition', tribal habits and unique cultures form.


    Where does this leave the mages?
    Well. All it took was, in a twist of irony, the downfall of humanity to put the Traditions and the Technocracy on somewhat even ground once more.
    With the rise of belief in something other than technology, mysticism and shamanic culture is growing once more, mixed with the remnants of old technology still understood.
    The Technocracy, hit hardest due to the apocalypse happening during the Avatar Storm, lost an incredibly vast amount of knowledge. Not just Masters, but many high-profile mages are long gone in generations past.

    But the Technocracy does what it does best; It stays in the shadows, organizes in Fallout-Institute-like structures, and tries to protect humanity. With even the last obvious remnants of Control and the old higher-ups gone, the union has regrouped into units that are more shrouded in folklore mystery than even during their hayday of late 1900's conspiracies.

    However, with the loss of that vast amount of knowledge come attempts at understanding the height of Technocratic hypertech again - and a concentrated effort of pulling it away from normal society as much as possible. Mundane wandering nomads find strange old bunkers with 'Golden Age' technology that seems absolutely impossible to even comprehend, and strange sites of impossible materials become tales of legend. And often enough, traders in these ancient collections cross lines with strange people wielding even stranger weapony and cloth, seemingly hellbent on relieving them of those goods in a peaceful or hostile manner before disappearing again..


    Meanwhile, the lines of hostility or cooperation between the Traditions and Technocracy blur, depending on where you go. Open (or subtle) conflict in some areas meets subtle cooperation for the betterment of living in other communities. The Union perhaps even has become Union(s) where the "partylines" are at odds. And of course there are always renegades, establishing their own little territories..

    And the Umbral landscape? Oh boy.

                                                    No; as much as I love Awakening — and I do love it — keep it out of Ascension. At least, keep it out as The Fundamental Truth of the setting.

                                                    Frankly, Ascension needs to go more agnostic on fundamental truths rather than enshrining more things as such: my preference for M5 would be for it to explicitly present the “Reality is subjective and mages alter it through acts of Enlightened Will” as Tradition talking points rather than as The Ultimate Truth: the Traditions wholeheartedly believe it to be the Ultimate Truth; and the way it was presented as such in the earlier editions of the game can mostly be chalked up to the fact that (with the exception of M20) they were all written from the Traditions' perspective. With M5, it should be presented as a possible Ultimate Truth, and even one that has a lot going for it as evidenced by how well it has served the Traditions; but don't present it as the Ultimate Truth anymore; at the very least, let the Technocracy be advocates of a contending view of the nature of Reality that's just as valid as the Traditions' Metaphysics of Magick while being different enough from it that you don't have to classify all Technocrats as either liars or dupes either for knowing the Truth and pretending otherwise or for having the Truth concealed from them.

                                                    Definitely present both the Traditions and the Technocracy in the core book. Also present the Crafts — though I could do without the budding “Disparate Alliance”. And retire the term “Orphan” as anything other than a derogatory term for independent mages who have either made the choice not to sign up for any of the larger factions or who haven't come to the point where such a decision is even relevant: just refer to them as “Independents”. Definitely implement something like Chronicles of Darkness' Tiers system, with Independents being the primary focus of first-tier games; Crafts being the primary focus of second-tier games; and Traditions and Conventions being the primary focus of third-tier games. Which is not to say that you can't run a first- or second-tier game featuring the Traditions or a third-tier game featuring Crafts or Independents, etc.; just that the default assumption would be that if you're interested in the tier-one style of game, you're probably going to find Independent protagonists more interesting than members of any factions, and so on.

                                                    Continue using M20's Focus mechanics — though possibly change the terminology to match earlier editions; while it was a valiant effort, I think Phil has probably lost the battle to redefine terms. So what M20 calls “Focus” should be called “Paradigm”; what M20 calls “Paradigm” should be called “Theory”; “Practice” can remain the same; and what M20 calls “Instruments” goes back to being called “Foci”. (This last one I say with some chagrin, because I personally prefer “instruments”; but the consensus appears to disagree.) The main innovation from M20 that gets kept in M5 is the idea that Theory and Practice are presented separately from your chosen faction, just as Foci have always been: as in M20, a practitioner of Weird Science isn't assumed by default to be an Etherite; nor is a practitioner of High Ritual assumed to be a member of the order of Hermes. The converse is true, though: all Syndics practice the Art of Desire, and all Choristers practice Faith. Rather that having an Affinity Sphere, every Tradition, Convention, and Craft (save only for the Hollow Ones) has a Signature Practice. They just don't have any exclusivity with respect to said Practice (either in the sense of them practicing it exclusively or in the sense of them having exclusive access to it). Also replace the current “mystic vs. technomancer” divide with a discussion of internal vs. external paradigms: an internal paradigm is one that embraces the notion that magick is an extension of oneself and emphasizes the transcendence of foci; while an external paradigm is one centered around the notion that something about the world is magical and that magick consists of identifying and harnessing it, and emphasizes the mastery of foci. Mystical paradigms tend to be internal, and technomantic paradigms tend to be external; but exceptions exist on both sides: something akin to Mage: the Awakening's view of a Supernal World of Truth that defines the World of Phenomena would be one example of a mystical paradigm that's external; and the Virtual Adepts have an unusually high (for technomancers) number of internalists who view mages as the CPUs of Reality.

                                                    I'd like to see the Technocratic Sphere Variants folded back into the Nine Spheres, for simplicity's sake. Instead of Sphere Variants, allow certain paradigm- and/or faction-specific Merits that let you modify how some of the Spheres work. The Art of Desire should have a Merit that lets you use Prime to treat organized human activity like a Node; the Void Engineers' Signature Practice should allow them to use Spirit to more easily breech or reinforce the Gauntlet at the price of not being as good at summoning, binding, and warding E.D.E.s (their thing is crossing dimensions, not animism), and using distance-based Correspondence ranges instead of Sympathy-based ones (emphasizing the spatial aspects of Correspondence); the “Everything is Data” Theory should allow for a modifier to the Correspondence Sphere that makes it easier to use in relation to the digital world (including the use of something like Data's replacement for Correspondence ranges); “A Mechanistic Universe” might adjust Prime to be better at creating Wonders at the expense of its more “noumenous” applications being more difficult; and so on. This allows us to tailor the Spheres to individual Paradigms without having to write up entire Sphere variants, greatly cutting back on the word count while keeping the resulting flexibility. Note that this also involves scrapping the Qlippothic Spheres: if Nephandic magick differs from others' magick in terms of what they can do instead of “merely” what their Paradigm is, it can be handled through a Merit just like anyone else. Heck, even the Marauders' special relationship with Paradox could be handled in this manner.

                                                    Replace M20's three forms of Quiet that are merely an adaptation of MRev's Triat-inspired expansion of Resonance and Quiet with a four-fold set of Quiets representing possible personality pitfalls that a mage needs to be especially wary of: Madness and Denial stay; but instead of Morbidity, you also have Obsession (Morbidity being a particular case, specifically Obsession with Death) and Hubris. These correspond roughly with the Avatar Essences in the same way that the types of Resonance in M20 do: anyone can succumb to Hubris (or Madness, Denial, or Obsession, for that matter) just like anyone can acquire any kind of Resonance based on what actions they take; but the sorts of things Questing Avatars tend to push their mages to do tend to accrue Devotional Resonance and put them at a greater-than-normal risk of Hubris; a Dynamic Avatar frequently fosters activities that lead to Temperamental Resonance and a greater risk of Madness; a Pattern Avatar usually advocates actions that lead to Stabilizing Resonance and a greater risk of Denial; and a Primordial Essence often suggests actions leading to Elemental Resonance and a greater risk of Obsession. Ultimately, the kinds of Resonance and Quiet a mage faces depends on his own actions; but if roleplayed correctly, the mage's Avatar will have at least something to do with what those actions will be.

                                                    On the other hand, Synergy goes away. It was there in M20 mainly as a sop for MRev fans who wanted to keep the Dynamism/Stasis/Entropy triad that that edition pushed into the foreground; for M5, it's an unnecessary complication. M5 shouldn't be defined by the Triat the way the first three editions of the game were; to the extent that there's any overall structure to the metaphysics of the game, it should come from the four-fold Avatar Essences.

                                                    In terms of Metaplot elements, I'm ok with Brucato's “New Millennium” developments hinted at in M20 to start coming to fruition, for the most part (except for the Disparate Alliance, as I said before; I just don't buy it). Go ahead and start the Technocratic Civil War, with a potentially Technephandi-inspired takeover of Control followed by the exodus of Navalon and the potential birth of a new Order of Reason in opposition to it (with the latter patterned heavily after the Revised Convention Books while the former takes a darker turn). Navalon/the new Order of Reason should be considerably weaker than the Union, but still powerful enough that Backgrounds such as Requisitions remain viable for its members. The Union's traditional enemies should be split on how to react: some want to take advantage of the chaos to tear down both versions of the Technocracy; others want to help the Order of Reason on the theory that the enemy of one's enemy is a potential friend; and still others just want to sit on the sidelines eating popcorn as they watch the former bane of their existence self-destruct (the catch being that shrapnel from an explosion doesn't stop short of “the sidelines”: I could see the Union restarting the Pogrom, putting everyone in danger).
