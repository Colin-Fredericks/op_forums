    The Problem with Flexible Magick

    08-01-2017, 04:49 AM
    So in Mage you aren't just throwing around the kind of "rotes" that other Splats are stuck with. A Vampire uses Lure of Flames 5, and gets a very specific result using completely static rules.

    Mages, on the other hand, can do things their own way. You could set someone on fire, turn them into fire, teleport them into an umbral realm made entirely of the very concept of fire... if you have the Spheres, successes and bravery to do it (and face the consequences). This is cool in that it allows for truly Magickal events... but the problems start to pop up with Mechanics.

    Now Mage does have a lot of mechanical guidelines for damage, duration, range etc. but there are still problems. You can happily say that X Successes = Y Damage with a Forces effect, but many changes simply don't work that way. Turning someone's clothing into stone? How many successes are we talking? The ST could say anything from 3 to 5 I believe, depending on how they feel. Making the floor beneath someone into a liquid? Again, it's impressive, but the successes needed will vary.

    So it's not that Mage *lacks* rules, it's that the ST is forced to make decisions constantly and their opinions will vary from session to session, ST to ST, player to player. This isn't a bug, I'm guessing it's a feature.

    The problems arise when you realise that we have rules so that the ST's feelings don't shape the game too heavily. Implicitly or explicitly we have biases when it comes to our players and their actions. We'll feel differently to a player saying "I attempt to use Spirit and Prime to erase the Nexus Crawler from existence... basically I call upon God's aid in smiting this monstrosity and casting it out of his creation" than we'll feel when they say the same about a major NPC (or PC, but we'll get to that). Once it's someone we're invested in, we're more likely to um and ah and increase the successes needed, factor in the protections they have... see the problem here? PvP makes this into a NIGHTMARE. Luckily most of these effects where mechanics get blurry are going to be paradox-heavy, but that isn't going to matter while the scene is playing out, especially if one player is keen on eradicating the other for some reason.

    So, basically... have I missed something? Is this a legitimate problem you run into as STs and Players? I've only done a one-shot with a single player so my experience is limited.

                  Nope. Correspondence makes anyone not protected by wards (meaning most Mages most of the time) vulnerable. However, it better work on the first try because if your target has Correspondence as well you will be in for retaliation.

                  If this is a problem in your game it's can be easily dealt with, especially for "death" type effects. To outrightkill with magic effects, even heart attacks, the total heathlevels must be overcome (for balance sake at the very least) this will require at least 3 successes with Forces and 1 more for other spheres. This is in addition to successes needed for Corespondece range. This usually will require at least an extended roll. The target will be allowed an Awareness Roll (8 or higher) to notice magic being used against him as soon as the effect begins, giving a chance for defense. It may not seem like much but it's always been enough to discourage it in my games.

                  Also if your players like this sort of tactic see how they like it used on them. That should cure the problem almost immediately.
