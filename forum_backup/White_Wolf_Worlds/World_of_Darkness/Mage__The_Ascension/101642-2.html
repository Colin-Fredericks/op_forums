    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
    Changelings are an interesting case, in that they arguably are being edited out of existence by a hostile Consensus of humanity; that is, after all, a major theme of Changeling: the Dreaming: people by and large don’t believe in fairies anymore, and the fae are suffering for it. Put another way, Banality is arguably a manifestation of the Consensus — at least in part. The fae are probably unusually vulnerable to the Consensus due to the strong connection between the Dreaming and human dreams, which are themselves closely related to belief.
    Banality may be a manifestation of the consensus (sort of), but it is not a rejection of the fae directly; arguably that happened a long time ago. Instead it is more the nature of the current consensus is creating a hostile environment, much as would happen to Vampires or Werewolves if somehow the masses believed in an endless day or a world of silver grass. It's not quite the same thing as the consensus isn't actually removing people's ability to dream, but its a good approximation.

    To put it another way, the Fae arguably do have a higher metaphysical backing, it just happens that backing is vulnerable to something on earth. As it is pushed away, so are they.

        Seems that the general idea that the Consensus and Reality are two separate things does provide a good explanation for why the other major Supernaturals of the World of Darkness are still around for the most part.

        Even if you don't make that division we can explain the continued existence of the majority of Supernatural creatures thus:

        As Dataweaver mentions the Consensus has no impact on the Umbra (save for the bits it does I guess like the Dreaming) allows for at least a plausible explanation for Wraiths, Mummy's, Kuei-Jin, Werewolves and other Changing Breeds. Since they all have a link to or a "umbral" nature inherently.

        Changelings/Fae might have had the same type of immunity once upon a time, but as Ramnesis said, their metaphysical backing was vulnerable to something the Consensus can effect and therefore they are getting screwed over by it now.

        This still leaves Vampires and Demons as the odd ones out.

        Demons are sorta extra-planar (to borrow a D&D term) in that one could argue that have a link to a realm beyond the physical. Likewise they are very rooted within a human hosts soul (if we are talking about the Fallen) so the have an "anchor" to resist reality rejecting them.

        With Vampires you can as Ramnesis says say "God did it!" and that be the end of it if you want. Nothing else massively springs to mind about why Vampire don't suffer from it.

        However, one could argue that enough people still believe in Demons (it's a surprisingly common belief even among secular people) for them to have no problem with Consensus. Vampire saturate modern culture and have for about the last 100 years on and off more or less. So while people might not believe in them, their perfectly happy to think about and engage with the idea of vampires. Hence the Consensus leaves Vampire alone.

                NWoD lets you cross them and have them basically "shrug" at the fact that their metaphysics just don't cross over for the most part (or at least not in ways your average splat is aware of). They can point out odd parallels and coincidences and suck and dig into that if they want. Up the ST if there is anything too that or not.

                CWoD requires that an ST be selective about his use of fluff for all game lines involved. Likewise, if he decided to stick to the cannon meta stuff for say both Vampire and Mage, he's going to have to emphasize the few places they might cross to make it work. Straight out of the box one could argue that they simple don't work. I think the V20, W20, M20 and Wa20 new generation of CWoD books is making it easier by going with a more "decide" your own cannon approach to things.

                Personally, I'd never cross more then two game lines. Prior to digging into Mage in the last month I've been a pure Masquerade player pretty much for the last 10 years. Read up on Demon and Mummy a bit. Werewolf and Wraith even less and I think I've ever even picked up a Changeling the Dreaming book.

                In that whole time I've only ever crossed it over as an ST with Kindred of the East and bits of Werewolf (namely that they exist and hate Vampires/keep them in the cities), oh and used generic ghosts removed from any of the Wraith stuff. Never felt the game suffered for lacking content. Each of the CWoD lines is chock full of fluff, lore and unique stuff that gives your more the enough toys to fill a world.

                As a player I've been in Masquerade games that crossed over with but no more then the extent I've crossed it over in my games, Mage (there was a local Verbena, got killed by some Technocracy), Changeling (we had a Kaysid in the group), Wraith (there was a civil war in one of the underworld cities that a few dead characters got involved in off screen) and Kindred of the East. Some of these worked really well some not so much.

                My general rule is don't let the splats cross the streams (no Mage Vampires) and try and keep everything deeply personal for the PC's. With what I've learned about Mage I might cross over those two once I've got a copy of M20. However, I'd emphasize the points where the fluff cross-over e.g. the Tremere and Ta'Ma'Ra and I'd very much have my players either as all Vampires or all Mages.

                                        Nothing special. As most people have already said, when a vampire Embraces a mage you just wind up with a vampire. The Avatar goes bye-bye. That's it. You start from square one. I believe there were some (entirely optional) rules for an ST to allow players to liquidate the XP from their Spheres and use it to buy Disciplines, or possibly even Thaumaturgy Paths and Rituals. Certainly Sorcerers had that option back in second edition (ditto for Sorcerers who Awakened or somehow became wraiths). But for the most part, you just wind up with a neonate vamp.

                                        Of course, said vampire will retain all of the knowledge he had as a mage, meaning he knows all about the various factions, their metaphysics, who's who, where the chantries are, etc. He might not be able to work Sphere magick anymore, but that he still has the potential to be dangerous. He might even be able to eventually translate some of his former knowledge into new Rituals, a new Path or even an entirely new school of blood magic (see the aforementioned part about converting XP above). Personally I think that works better for sorcerers, psychics and other static, linear magics. Indeed, IIRC some of the later material for Dark Ages hinted that Tremere might have been a sorcerer rather than a true mage. Then again, the Tzimisce also claim that the Eldest was a true, Awakened mage (or at least they did back in second edition). Various other characters like al-Ashrad have been called magicians or sorcerers before their Embrace, but its never been explicitly stated if they were true mages, sorcerers or something else altogether.

                                        As for Liches, yeah, that's a Rote from Dead Magic. It's mostly a Etruscan and Greek thing so by the modern era Euthanatoi and maybe Hermetics are the most acquainted with it (and aren't inclined to share or practice their knowledge anyway), though the Chakravanti had developed their own version called Yamasattva. They aren't vampires or wraiths, they are mages who have discovered a special, magical state of undeath, though the results are not pleasant to say the least. They do lose their Avatar, and the ability to learn new magic, but otherwise they retain their old abilities. Presumably these are the same Liches mentioned in Dirty Secrets of the Black Hand, though we can't know for certain (DSOTBH was written for second edition, and included abominations, soul eaters, and other stuff that didn't really get used much afterwards; its entirely possible their liches were intended to be Embraced mages who retained true magick...).

                                        Intriguingly, various sources for both Vampire and Mage claimed the Nagaraja bloodline were the Idran, a fallen Chakravanti sect of necromancers who used a variant of the Yamasattva rote to transform themselves into a new lineage of vampires rather than liches. Of course, the Chakravanti (or at least the Idran) had contact with the Black Hand, and the Nagaraja bloodline was affiliated with that sect, so its also possible that the liches of the Tal'mahe'ra were just vanilla liches after all.

                                        However, that brings up another interesting point. Several sources have hinted at mages using magic to teach vampires. Veil of Night mentioned the Ahl-i-Batin teaching the Ashirra the rudiments of Sihr. DSOTBH had the Chakravanti (or at least the fallen Idran sect) and Verbena teaching the Tal'mahe'ra, even spawning the Rafastio family and the Nagaraja bloodline. Some second edition material had Native American Dreamspeakers associating with the Sabbat in the American Southwest, while Dark Ages material suggested House Tytalus helped the Tzimisce refine Koldunic Sorcery. While most of its just fluff, at least it gives some hint that the metaphysics of true magick might overlap with the principles of Thaumaturgy and other forms of blood magic, however slightly.
