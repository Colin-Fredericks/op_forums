                                For me, the most appealing aspect of the Disparates is their drive for authentic experience, and their unwillingness to compromise on that. Unlike the Traditions, they don't necessarily buy into the idea that “we're all doing the same thing”; to the extent they share a common view on why different magical beliefs work, it tends to be “agnostic”: “we know how our Magick works, and we know that other Magicks work, too; but we don't care why.” They're not trying for all-encompassing magical beliefs that explain anything and everything, including other magick; they just want to stay true to their own beliefs. The two biggest exceptions to this are the Batini (who would love nothing better than to get the Disparates to accept the same “it's all one and the same” view that the Traditions hold) and the Hollow Ones (who practice Gutter Magick and don't really have a unified set of Magickal beliefs that they pursue).

                                And the result is that the Disparate Alliance is an alliance of convenience, where each Craft is given some role to play in the overall organization and everyone is expected to stay in their own lanes and out of each other's ways. At this point, the only thing holding them together is their shared concern about Nephandi infiltration, adding a level of paranoia to them that's not particularly suited for long-term trust-building.

                                Another result of this “stay in your own lane” arrangement is that there isn't really any room in the Disparate Alliance for independent mages. The Hollow Ones are about as close as you get; and that's more a matter of the “whatever works” nature of their Gutter Magick than it is about actual independence.

                                    In terms of making the Magick system better, one thing I'd like to do would be to make it a bit more responsive to the mage's beliefs.

                                    I mentioned Prism of Focus before. One of the things that book does is to add structure to Paradigms by posing a series of questions for each Paradigm to answer, along with a set of possible answers for each (called Tenets): for instance, “How does the Universe Work?” (A Rational Universe; As Above, So Below; Fall from Grace; Reality is a Lie; The Divine is Real; Unfathomable Forces at Work); “Why can I use Magick?” (I am Chosen; I am not limited; I am special; I have greater understanding); and “What is the point of it all?” (Achieve Full Knowledge; Annihilation is Freedom; Build a Utopia; Existence is Meaningless; Merge with the Divine; Power is its own reward). One of the questions asked is called the Epistemological question: how is knowledge acquired? (Mystical Insights, Scientific Experimentation, Divine Revelations).

                                    If M30 includes something like this, it might have the mage's answer to this question influence how Magick works, possibly in a manner similar to the Technomancer/mystic divide in M20: If you choose Mystical Insights, you have the option to Work Without Focus; but your Arete is capped by your Willpower. If you choose Scientific Experimentation, you can't Work Without Focus, but you also don't face any restrictions on your use of Magick beyond the need for it to conform to your Practices. If you choose Divine Revelation, you tend to experience Wild Magick (representing whatever higher power you serve working through you); but you're susceptible to Faithlessness (failing to uphold your divine benefactor's will can result in access to your Magick being blocked until you atone).

                                    That won't work for every Tenet; it may not even work for most of them. But I could see something like this, if used sparingly, making it easier to represent seemingly contradictory belief systems in a common set of rules for Magick.
