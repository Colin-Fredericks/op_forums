            That's a complaint I can get behind. Resources is awkward in that regard, in that I can't think of any other Background where not having it puts you at a distinct disadvantage relative to the Hypothetical Average Person. One way to address this is to recalibrate Resources so that what's currently 2 dots becomes 0 dots. I’d keep 5 dots the same, spread what's currently 3 or 4 dots over the one-to-four for range (3 becomes 1 or 2; 4 becomes 3 or 4; and two new levels get interpolated), and replace what's currently 0 or 1 dot with a Flaw representing poverty. Another way to address it is to just give everyone two dots in Resources at character creation; though that runs into the problem of what to do if someone wants to play a hobo mage.

            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                        I don't know if you necessarily need to have Resources 0 represent rock-bottom, just precariousness. You can make just enough money to cover a small apartment in the worst part of town, or a room in someone else's place somewhere nicer, but you have no real savings for a rainy day, just one week of missed work away from losing it all. Maybe you have a car, but it's some clunky beater that could die anytime, and you don't have the money to fix it. That makes Resources 0 less of an active penalty and more of a "this isn't a great place to start, but it probably isn't going to active kneecap you if you're smart." Or you can simply embrace the hobo life if that's what you're going for.

                        Being unhoused and jobless against your will might be better represented as a genuine Flaw, even if it's just something that would indirectly impair your ability to get steady work which would then result in you not being able to keep a roof over your head.

                                    X Working Poor: you live paycheck to paycheck, and that's about it.
                                    • Slightly ahead of the game: you've got a small apartment and perhaps a cheap vehicle.
                                    •• Lower middle class: you've got a condo or apartment, plus a vehicle, some savings, and enough money for occasional indulgences.
                                    ••• Middle class with property: you either own or have decent equity in some property, plus a vehicle, investments, and savings. You could maintain a one-dot standard of living indefinitely.
                                    •••• Well-off: You own a large house, some property, and at least two vehicles, as well as notable levels of savings and investments. By local standards, you're a millionaire, and although that's not worth as much as it once was, it's nothing to sneeze at.
                                    ••••• Welcome to the 1%. As a multimillionaire, you've got substantial holdings, investments, and savings.
                                    Implicit in this is that if you don't have even one dot, you don't even have an apartment. Unless you're living at a friend's or family's house, you're homeless. That, IMHO, ought to be a Flaw; even at zero dots of Resources, a character ought to have enough to have a place to stay. Basically, “you've got a small apartment and perhaps a cheap vehicle” doesn't strike me as “slightly ahead of the game”; it feels more like “the working poor”. Ditto for the descriptions given for two, three, and four dots: the labels work fairly well; but the descriptions of what those labels supposedly mean feel like they ought to go with the next ranking down. Except for Well-off: the first part (owning a large house, some property, and at least two vehicles, as well as notable levels of savings and investment) feels more like Middle class with property, whereas the second part (“by local standards, you're a millionaire, and although that's not worth as much as it once was, it's nothing to sneeze at”) feels appropriate to “Well-off”.

                                    This comes from personal experience: I have a friend who owns a house that eight people can live in comfortably (two stories and a basement, with three bedrooms upstairs, one downstairs, another room converted into a bedroom downstairs, and the basement converted into effectively a luxury apartment), and two cars; and he's got decent levels of savings and investments — all the earmarks of what Resources is calling Well-off, except that he's far from being a millionaire. He feels more like Middle class with property. (Part of that is that of the right people living there, one is his wife and four are his children.)

                                    Likewise, my own situation is what I would call “working poor”: I'm renting a room (in effect, an apartment), and I have access to a vehicle for use on the job; with luck, I'll be in a position to buy a cheap vehicle for myself in about six months or so. I would consider my current situation to be the bottom of the Resources chart, the X; and the car won't change that.

                                    Six months ago, I was staying in a shelter, because things got really bad for me; without the shelter, I would have been living on the streets. It was worse than “working poor”.

                                    That said, I also knew a guy who was living on the streets; and as bad as things were for me in that shelter, they were far worse for him. So perhaps the “sub-X” should come in at least two levels: homeless (you're somehow able to find a room to sleep in every night; but it's an ongoing challenge, and there's no guarantee that it will be the same room) and destitute (if you're lucky, you're pitching a tent in the local park; if not, you're sleeping in a cardboard box).

                                    Before things fell apart for me and landed me in that shelter, I was living in an apartment, with a vehicle of my own, some savings, and enough money for the occasional indulgence — and I didn't feel “lower middle class”; I felt “slightly ahead of the game”.


                                    So if I were reworking the Resources table, I'd bump X down to a Flaw (possibly with two levels: homeless and destitute), • down to X, •• down to •, ••• down to ••, and the first part of •••• down to •••. Then I'd flesh out •••• with something better than what I moved down to •••, but still not good enough to qualify for •••••'s “Welcome to the 1%”. I'd keep the labels the same: X is Working Poor, • is Slightly Ahead of the Game, •• is Lower middle class, ••• is Middle class with property (or perhaps Upper middle class), •••• is Well-off, and ••••• is “Welcome to the 1%”. But what they mean would shift downward.

                                                Something that would be reasonable/likely/logical for a Mage character would be resources that don't seem to make sense.
