                Originally posted by quelsec View Post
                You mistake the mastery of the known paradigm with sphere mastery. One is practical the other one phylosophical.
                Mastery of the known paradigm is what defines an Archmaster; Sphere mastery is what defines an Exemplar. Ascension is achieved by moving beyond any one paradigm; at least, according to Masters of the Art in particular, and earlier editions of Mage in general.

                Originally posted by Lord Revan View Post
                Dataweaver Banu_Saulot Sometimes ascension is described as the classic transcendence: free yourself from selfish thoughts and materialism/greed.

                But then they say that ascension is a personal thing that depends on your paradigm, so it isn't necessarily the classic one? In theory one could ascend by means of violence and aggressiveness.
                Oh, certainly; different mages use different paths to reach Ascension. But if the older material has any validity, all of those Paths eventually broaden as the mage pushes beyond her paradigm's limits. If it always works within your paradigm, that's only because your paradigm gets redefined in increasingly broad terms. But it's also valid to view the final stages of the Path to Ascension as developing a flexibility of thought, an open-mindedness if you will, that lets you go beyond your paradigm. It's what growing beyond the tools is about: the gradual realization that your Magick comes from within, and that the instruments of your practice are merely aids to be used or discarded as you see fit.

                My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                    I used to argue this a long time ago, but that's taking a game mechanic and turning it into an in game metaphysic. It also leads to some strange consequences, like mages abandoning their beliefs and morals as they rise in Arete.

                                                    Also, there's a common trope in fantasy and sci-fi about the characters getting so good at their at their skills they no longer need the same rigid steps they used to. Sometimes this is them growing beyond their previous paradigm, but often this is just because the steps were just crutches to make magic easier or easier to understand.

                                                    And this is the direction M20 took. Mages aren't abandoning foci, they are internalizing them. The oracle stops needing drugs to get into an altered mental state not because he doesn't get into an altered mental state, but because he doesn't need to use drugs to get there anymore. The martial artist doesn't need complicated forms to direct the chi flow in his body because he can do that directly, but his paradigm is still based around chi flow.
