    The Limitations of a Paradigm

    05-21-2020, 01:49 PM
    For the last little while I have had an idea for a Hollower Chaos Magician on the back-burner, as a potential PC in a game of M:tAs or maybe as a self-insert NPC if I'm running the game instead. I've had a lot of fun working on ideas for his Focus/Paradigm, but there's a particular question that I haven't been able to resolve, and I was hoping for some input from people with more experience. The basic premise of the Paradigm is pretty Purple; everything is some form of Energy (Prime), and everything is connected (Correspondence); Magic(k) is learning how to use the Energy of Belief/Will (Mind) to manipulate Prime, using Correspondence connections to 'direct' or 'focus' it.

    While that very rough outline provides a Paradigm that is capable of almost anything, the particular details of his specific arcane theories end up creating some constraints. In his conception, Physical and Mental/Spiritual qualities are distinct "modes" or "states" of Prime, with direction interaction between the two being complicated and typically mediated by acting upon the more foundational Prime. I imagine Mind being his "dominant" Sphere because Mind acting directly upon Mind makes sense to him, while a lot of his other Effects would involve Entropy (manipulating chance through subtle manipulation of the underlying Prime). But I imagine his Paradigm having a lot of trouble with Vulgar physical effects, especially related to Forces and Matter; I don't see this guy tossing lightning bolts at people or turning lead into gold. Similarly, while I imagine Correspondence playing a huge part in his Magical Theory, the more Vulgar aspects of the Sphere (teleportation, warping space) don't seem to mesh well with the concept.

    My question is, do you think that hard limits on the kinds of Effects a Mage can learn to cast within their Paradigm are a good thing?

    Should things like Teleportation or Fireballs be impossible for him unless he hypothetically manages to Ascend?

    Or is it more appropriate that his Paradigm should, eventually, be able to accommodate these Effects?

    The question is relevant mostly in the consideration of the relationship between his Instruments and Effects. I'm wondering if I need to consider which/how many Instruments he might need to perform such Effects, compared to other Spheres which come more naturally to him. My original plan was that these kinds of Effects would require multiple Instruments and considerably more prep time for him to achieve, and reaching the point where he can just "will" such Effects into being would take much longer. Obviously the Spheres he actually learns will place practical limitations on what he can achieve in-game, but this question is about what is theoretically possible.

          Originally posted by Kharnov View Post
          My question is, do you think that hard limits on the kinds of Effects a Mage can learn to cast within their Paradigm are a good thing?
          By definition, Paradigm imposed limits are going to be soft. Very rarely they're hard (mystic vs. technomancers being an example of a hard limitation tied to the Focus)

          "I don't see my mage doing X" it's a perfectly reasonable tought (and does limit what you can do)...but afterwards you could always discover or imagine a way of doing X that makes perfect sense for your mage. So it's better not to see these Effects as being "off bounds", you've just yet to imagine how to do them within your Paradigm

          Should things like Teleportation or Fireballs be impossible for him unless he hypothetically manages to Ascend?
          If I had a character with such a Paradigm, I would say that my mage uses his application of Will to affect the Energy surrounding a target, transforming it into fire. Fire it's just an expression of Prime like everything else, hence there should be a way to "get" from Prime-energy to more mundane fire. If I can alter the first, I should be able to get the latter. Easy peasy

          Teleportation would be more esoteric on the worldview I'm thinking off. I could explain it as altering the way my Energy/Pattern relates to other stuff - if part of my Focus it's "everything it's connected", I should be able to change the way these connections interact (as I'm doing magick here, not merely describing how things factually work). If I can do that, I can decide to interact with my room from anywhere. From that to teleportation it's semantics: I choose to interac "totally" with my room, with my whole body.

          In general I think you should try to be creative to explain all kinds of effects. Only don't do them if you really think it doesn't mesh with the style of your character.
          My experience in Mage it's that I can *explain* almost every effect within my paradigm, but I can't always *do* everything because the way my character would do that it's too complex/impractical/doesn't have the right tools (the bane of technomancers :P ) . The Instrument it's usually more limiting that the Paradigm

          I'm a firm believer that *anything* should be at least theoretically possible for any Mage (well, almost anything). The particulars are up to you, but I think that approach it's perfectly sensible.

              Are you asking me to provide an overview here to help readers resolve the question, or to go through each Sphere and consider it personally?

              If you're simply asking me to consider things from that perspective to find my answer, I already have, and the resulting uncertainty is what I'm hoping to get help with :P

              I haven't figured everything out yet, but I can provide a rough outline of my ideas below, if it would help?

              The simpler answer is that his Paradigm can conceive of the theoretical way to make it happen, but contains other constraints that might make such things practically impossible for him.

              Roughly how I'm conceiving things is that all magick starts with Mind; either Mind energy acts on other Mind energies (producing Mind effects), or acts on Prime to make other things possible (all Prime is interconnected = Correspondence; subtle alterations to the underlying Prime manipulating chance = Entropy). But to make the kind of large-scale alterations to physical phenomena produced by higher-level Matter and Forces Effects, or the more Vulgar applications of Time and Correspondence, requires a lot more raw 'power' than the subtle 'pokes' that are employed to achieve Entropy Effects. Spirit is kind of a funny thing, in that I imagine him not having it a part of his Paradigm initially, but being very easy to 'fit in' once he is exposed to the concepts.

              I suppose my question breaks down to which of three options is the best fit for the setting, or the most interesting:

              a) Accepting the Limits of the Paradigm: certain kinds of Effects will remain impossible unless/until he Ascends, because his Paradigm simply cannot accept that he can generate/wield/whatever the power necessary for Vulgar Pattern Effects.

              b) Shedding (the limits of) The Paradigm: his Paradigm grows more Purple, freeing him from the constraints that make certain kinds of will-working more difficult; this essentially takes the form of realizing that his beliefs about how his Paradigm works are wrong in some way.

              c) Deepening the Paradigm: rather than becoming more Purple in the sense of "growing out" of the weaknesses; he never stops believing that certain types of Effects are more difficult, but instead comes to believe that his own growing power/knowledge makes things possible that previously weren't.

              In a way, I'm wondering which of these answers is the most likely to be true within the "canon" of the setting? Using the Traditions as an example, some seem really well-equipped to include almost any kind of Sphere or Effect (e.g. the Hermetics), while others seem like they might have some serious limitations built in (e.g. Akashic alchemist?). Every option seems feasible, but I'm wondering what people feel about the flavour of each option?

                  In my opinion, how hard the paradigm limit is should be more dependent on the mage's personality and how rigid or creative the person's thinking than anything else. Sure there are tons of ways to explain something, but would the person focus on what he or she understands, or add to their understanding and continually modify their mental model of magick and reality, or are they the type that can change their mind or believe in multiple conflicting models of understanding?

                  As something of a mental exercise, I once tried to figure out how a specific akashic character I made with the idea of "harmony of mind body and spirit to access the macrocosm through mastery of the macrocosm" as a source of magick would work.

                  Naturally, the mage had mind, spirit, and life.

                  The mage would be inclined to develop mind up to 4 for astral travel, life 3 for enhanced physical capabilities, and spirit 3 to gain mastery of the spiritual self before even considering branching out to other spheres.

                  At that point an understanding of the astral and the akashic records as something connected to one's self and to all other things could lead to an understanding of correspondence, entropy, and time, whilst a more comprehensive look at what binds the mind, body, and spirit together would lead to a greater understanding of prime. Matter and forces would probably remain elusive unless some experience highlighted their link to the microcosm of one's own mind body and spirit, perhaps by repeated interaction with the body's use of force on matter.

                  Most likely, the character with mind 4, life 3, and spirit 3 would then either branch to correspondence 3 and entropy 3, or prime 5, or matter 2 and forces 3, or else simply bring life mind and spirit to 5 each. Then the character would probably alternate between Arete increase and branching out to increase mastery of spheres.

                  This would be different for other characters but I'm guessing will follow a similar idea of development based on how the character thinks.

                  Edit: I meant access of the macrocosm through the mastery of microcosm up there. Lol

                                                      It sounds like a pretty neat idea, Spacecat, but you will need to work within the framework of a somewhat-consistent set of Instruments and Practices; it might be a bit unfair if you just find some movie or TV show where wizards are depicted throwing fireballs willy-nilly and go "I can do that!" Like starting off with some sort of ritual centred around the media in question before being able to emulate more powerful Effects depicted within it.

                                                      Originally posted by Muad'Dib View Post
                                                      (I'm sorry that I can't answer your post thoroughly, but I don't currently have some matters I need to sort out)
                                                      I'd like to know about why you think the Mind kind of Magick should be at the top of this diamond/pyramid. This idea/concept seems to be interesting.
                                                      Understanding that what I'm describing is meant to be his Paradigm, and not a theory about how all Mage magic should work, I'd be glad to try and explain

                                                      Mind is at the top of the pyramid for two reasons. The first is that "the top of the pyramid" isn't necessarily meant to say that Mind is significantly more powerful or more important than the other Spheres; it's simply understood to be more complex, within his framework. The four "Basic Spheres" are like atoms, the "Advanced Spheres" are like molecules, and Mind is like a cell.

                                                      Of course, I will now contradict myself by admitting that Mind *is* especially important within his Paradigm, as the "starting place" of magic. In his Paradigm, Mind is necessary for two things; first, it provides the understanding necessary to comprehend *how* to perform magic, so he views it as closely tied to Awakening; secondly, he views magic as the process of converting mental/emotional energy into other forms of power to achieve the desired end, generally by using mental energy to subtly influence the underlying Prime. It's a little bit influenced by a layman's understanding of quantum physics, that perception has the power to tangibly influence outcomes.

                                                      That's also why Matter and Forces are such difficult Spheres for him; compare getting a bunch of gold by tricking a person into giving it to you, or being able to subtly rig the rules of a game of chance, versus just thinking really hard at a rock to turn it into gold. Obviously, the fact that other Mages can do such things will eventually get across the possibility to him, but "getting it" will be more difficult for him I imagine.

                                                      Also, just reflecting on this question has been helpful, as I realized there's at least a bit of a complication or shortcoming with my model: framing Mind as a combination of Life and Entropy comes with a bit of an issue when spirit-entities can have Minds without falling into the Life category. I have an answer; since Spirit is virtually everywhere within his pyramid/diamond model, it makes sense that it can roughly "fill in" for a lot of different things, like Life, when needed for a given Pattern.
