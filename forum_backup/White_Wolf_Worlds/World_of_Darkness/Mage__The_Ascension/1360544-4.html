    Iteration X

    It can't be bargained with. It can't be reasoned with. It can't feel pity, or remorse, or fear. And it will not stop, ever, until you are dead.
    - Kyle Reese, The Terminator (1984)

    In the ancient lands of Khem during the reign of Pharaoh Djoser (2630BC), the exalted Imhotep of Memphis transcribed the Sacred and Most Secret Scrolls of Ptah, revealing the cornerstones of creation, the sacred geometry of the universe, and architecture of the Eternal Monolith. His disciples became the Brotherhood of the Rule, devoted to the doctrine that any and everything within creation can be measured and quantified, and therefore controlled and perfected. Eventually, these teachings would reach the Roman Republic and Empire, where the teaching of Ars Praeclarus was codified by the Collegium Praecepti.
    By the Dark Ages, the doctrine diverged into three strains. The Collegium Praecepti continued within the lands of the Byzantines, while in the Muslim regions it inspired the Muktashfu al Daw' (Discoverers of Light). And within Charlemagne's Holy Roman Empire, Wulfgang Reisman founded the Craftmasons. While the Discoverers would join the Web of Faith, many of them eventually being largely subsumed by both the Ahl I Batin's University of Light and the Celestial Chorus's Alexandrian Society, the Craftmasons and Collegium would eventually reunify as one of the founders of the Order of Reason. (They would also see the defection to their ranks of a number of younger members of House Verditus.)
    ​The dawn of the Industrial Revolution and early theories of Socialism led to a revolution within the ranks of the artificers as well. They reorganized themselves as the International Brotherhood of Mechanists, devoted to constructing a well-ordered and egalitarian industrial utopia. As part of this, they helped found the Enlightened Society of Reason in 1851, and developed a number of steam powered and electrical machines, clockwork biomechanics, and analytical engines.
    With the 20th century, the Mechanists devoted themselves fully to industrialized warfare, social planning, and brutal repression in the quest to create the New Man and Utopian State. When the Society was reorganized as the Technocratic Union after WW2, they also upgraded themselves into Iteration X. As such, they continue to work toward their technological utopia via the military-industrial complex, Information Age warfare, the merging of man with machine. They strive to perfect humanity and society through the use of strict social order, material humanism and atheism, collectivism, indoctrination, cybernetic conversion, dependency on advanced technology, and the all powerful State.
    Iteration X has three methodologies. Biomechanics seeks the perfection of the human form through the fusion of man and machine via cybernetics and bionics. Management seeks the perfection of human society through orderly regulation, efficiency, and advanced technology. Statistics seeks the perfection of reality itself through advanced mathematics, data quantification, and predictive sciences.
    Among their forces are Exterminators (cyborg assassins able to pass as humans), War Machines (high power robotic shock troops with heavy weapons), Fixers (damage control specialists trained in maintenance, field medicine, cover up, and assassination), Inspectors (relentless detectives, trouble shooters and secret police), Programers (instructors who re-educate citizens in the ways of conformity and obedience), Weathermen (specialists in probability analysis tasked with identifying and eliminating future problems), Librarians (walking information systems who collect and process all available data on assigned subjects), Cyphers (deep undercover agents with reprogrammable minds), Komrads (gangs of engineered faceless cannon fodder), Drones (synthetic automatons simulating living creatures to act as spies and assassins), and Black Ice (savage security programs which patrol Technocracy controlled areas of the Digital Web).
    Iteration X strongholds include Laboratories, Workshops, Factories, and Think Tanks, as well as Gulags, depending on their focus.
    The ultimate end of Iteration X Conditioning is The Machine. The longer you are processed, the more you become less of a person and more an unthinking cog in an emotionless and amoral system.

        The Progenitors

        We shall conquer and beat the Nations of the Earth into the dust and reforge them in our self wrought Image; the Final Society, a new humanity without weakness or mercy, hard and pure. Our descendants will walk the hillside of that future, innocent beneath the stars, with no more between them and their naked will than a wolf has. THEN there will be Gods in the Earth.
        - SM Stirling, The Domination of the Draka

        In the Fourth Age of Man (the Grecian Bronze Age), the Golden Doctrine of Apollon Phoebus was given to his son Asklepios, revealing the secrets of disease and healing, animal husbandry, metamorphosis, and divination so that he could recreate the Golden Race of Kronus. His disciples and followers created the Golden Circle of Asklepios, and their experiments and activities created countless hybrid beasts of legend, as well as there greatest creation, the race of super soldiers known as the Myrmidon.
        While the Golden Circle faded away by the Roman Empire, their secrets persisted in the Dark Ages and into the Renaissance. It was this time that saw the birth of the Solificati, the Crowned Ones, devoted to the use of alchemy and other arts to achieve spiritual and physical perfection, including immortality. With the Convention of the White Tower in the 14th century, the Solificati saw a schism, with the majority joining the Order of Reason and a misguided minority trying and failing to ally with the Mystic Traditions.
        The end of the Enlightenment included the Promethean Plague, a flood of alchemically created artificial human-like monsters who ran amok. The already scattered and disorganized Solificati collapsed. In their place, at the 1851 creation of the Enlightened Society of Reason came the Eugenitors. Their focus was the improvement of the human race - indeed, the creation of a race of over-men - through the use of controlled breeding, public hygiene, nutrition, enforced sterilization, and experimental surgeries.
        With the reorganization into the Technocratic Union, they became the Progenitors. They embraced the new fields of genetics as well as the growing area of pharmacology. Their ideal is an organized and biologically perfect society of those engineered for their selected roles. To this end they advocate the use of genetic engineering, population control, utilitarian bioethics, chemical control of emotion and personality, and biological warfare.
        The Progenitors have three methodologies. Genengineering focuses on human perfection via internal methods, including genetic modification and surgery. Pharmacopics focuses on human perfection via external methods, including drugs and nutrition. And Purification focuses on human perfection via social methods, including sanitation and doctrine.
        Among their forces are Alphas (genetically modified over-men with enhanced strength, intellect and health; the Convention's leadership), Betas (genetically modified assistants and aides designed for competence, obedience, and loyalty to the Greater Good), Delphians (experimental psychic diviners created through genetic engineering and designer drugs), Omegas (mindlessly obedient vat-grown clones used for simple labor and as cannon fodder), Watchdogs (designer animal security agents with heightened intelligence and loyalty), Tracers (bioenhanced hunters, both animal and humans, with enhanced senses and predatory instincts), Vectors (specialists in the distribution of biological agents, drugs, and propaganda to the masses), Lures (recruitment specialists designed for physical attractiveness, social grace, and pheromone production), Sweeps (telepathic security agents used for interrogation, counter intelligence, and loyalty enforcement), Sanitizers (clean up specialists trained in hazmat safety, quarantine procedures, cover ups, monster hunting, and assassination), Victors (super soldiers created through a mix of breeding, gene therapies, and drugs, equipped with advanced weapons), and Bio Beasts (vat grown monsters mixing multiple lifeforms used as shock troops and terror weapons).
        Progenitor strongholds include Laboratories, Factories, Farms, Theaters, Arks, and Hives.
        The ultimate end of Progenitor indoctrination and improvements is The Master Race. They are no longer human, and look upon the masses the way humans do cattle and other livestock.

                            The Syndicate

                            So, this will require a little bit of a prelude to explain a few things.
                            First, while there is a tendency to associate the Syndicate with the idea of capitalism and its alleged evils or shortcomings, this really isn't the case. What people are thinking about when they mention these things is called Crony Capitalism, in which the business class and political class collude with each other in the form of regulations, corporate welfare, and other forms of state intervention (usually in return for large campaign donations, positions on boards after leaving government service, and other "gifts") to stifle competition, corner markets, and get around the laws that apply to "the little people". At the least invasive end of the spectrum are things like trade associations or set asides for preferred groups, but at the other end are countries where it's impossible to do any kind of business without taking on high ranking government officials as business partners or even majority owners (China is pretty notorious for this).
                            Second, when I started looking at how the Syndicate operate, I decided that it was almost certain that they've always gotten ahead by cheating. They game the system, not just with money, but with Magic. And that means that, in addition to the usual sort of mesmerism and other aspects of Ars Cupidae you see with the High Guild in the Swashbucklers' Handbook, they can also directly manipulate Fate (which I use in place of Entropy), both their own and others. In fact, they can directly steal the good fortune and future potential of other people. But as part of their MO, they offer people money and other favors, but if they can't pay the Syndicate back, then they lose what they put up as collateral - their potential for the future. The members of the Syndicate take this potential for future success and use it for their own gain. (This is one of those instances where, like in the M:SC Order of Reason sourcebook, there is a difference between the lower ranks who are using "advanced science" Procedures/Rotes and the upper levels who are perfectly aware that they are altering Reality with abilities mundane people can never possess.)
                            And just to note, part of this concept came from an old Twilight Zone episode, "Person or Persons Unknown", but last year I came across an anime called C:The Money of Soul and Possibility Control that perfectly exemplified what I was trying to do with this. I really recommend it.

                            And with that ...

                            And, for an instant, she stared directly into those soft blue eyes and knew, with an instinctive mammalian certainty, that the exceedingly rich were no longer even remotely human.
                            - William Gibson, Zero History

                            During the Roman Republic, there was a mystery cult, The Fraternal Order of Plutus, devoted to a spirit known as the Pratonus Plouton. Members of the cult achieved great wealth as patrons, censors, and publicans, both through their personal connections, the fortune granted by their god, and the use of the arts of divination and necromancy. They used their money and influence to create an orderly society which was overseen by and primarily benefited the wealthy elite.
                            The rise of Christianity and the fall of Rome let do the dissolution of the cult as a whole, but its traditions continued to be passed down, often with their spiritual patrons held to be angels of the material world. While many would have associations with the Hermetic House of Jerbiton, they constantly refused any greater involvement with the Order of Hermes, finding the medieval Craftmasons more in line with their thinking. By the High Middle Ages, the various inheritors of the cult had reincorporated themselves as the Grand Guild of the Golden Pyramid, involved in Europe's great trading cities and networks. It was here that they helped found the Order of Reason, and would be one of its strongest and most consistent pillars throughout the Renaissance and Enlightenment. They eagerly financed the Age of Discovery and the pillage of the New World, encouraged the creation of royal monopolies such as the British and Dutch East India Companies, and profited from the use of indentured servitude and slavery.
                            In 1851, with the formation of the Enlightened Society of Reason, leaders of the Guild reincorporated themselves as the Invisible Exchequer. As captains of industry and champions of imperialism, they enjoyed an era of monopolies and a growing regulatory state's power to crush their competition. After the World Wars and Great Depression, with its war profiteering and command & control economies, they joined the other Conventions in updating themselves as the Syndicate. They thrive in the modern world of crony capitalism, insider trading, market manipulation, and a moneyed elite who are clearly above the law. Their nigh-inconceivable levels of wealth allow them to headhunt and groom talent, equip themselves with the most bleeding edge of technology, and buy the hearts and minds of the masses.
                            The Syndicate has three methodologies. Investments advance the interests of the elite through resource management, recruitment, patronage, training and indoctrination. Adjustments advance the interests of the elite through diplomacy and negotiation, spin doctoring, blackmail, assassination, and the direct manipulation of Fate. Special Projects advance the interests of the elite through security, debt collection, espionage, experimental research, and the repossession of forfeited collateral/futures.
                            Among their forces are Assets (mundane people in debt to the Syndicate paying their way with service and favors), Patrons (managers of mundane assets and investments, cultivating people, places and projects for potential future profit), Providers (specialists in logistics and the management and distribution of equipment, funding and information), Boosters (elite Providers trained in the collection, manipulation, and distribution of Quintessence), Visionaries (specialists trained in probability analysis, event forecasting, and strategic planning), Salesmen (field agents trained in the arts of desire and diplomacy, handling recruitment, indoctrination and propaganda), Spin Doctors (elite Salesmen trained indirect fortune manipulation and mental operations), Headhunters (field agents trained for troubleshooting, tracking, kidnapping and assassination), Spectres (field agents trained in espionage, infiltration, and undercover work), White Knights (elite combat units used as security and strike teams, with the best equipment and training money can buy), Repo Men (elite agents who specialize in debt collection through the use of organ harvesting, life erasure, and removal of potential/future and even self), and Hollow Men (Assets who have defaulted on their debts, their entire future and even sense of self removed to serve as disposable servants and canon fodder).
                            Syndicate strongholds include Offices, Tradehouses, Showrooms, Vaults, and Retreats.
                            The ultimate end of Syndicate indoctrination, privilege and adjustments is to become one of The Elite, far above the common masses and their petty concerns or emotions.

                                    Primal Utility would probably be a specialty of Quantum Science based on human perception and the Observer Effect as it applies to the values people put on people, places, and objects actually causing changes to Reality on a quantum (ie magical) level. And it would be part of what the Investments methodology handle, and what the Boosters are specifically trained in.
                                    I tend to have Fate focus on the ideas of probability, fortune, and destiny. If you default to this version of the Syndicate, they repossess whatever good things they gave you (money, fame, fortune, a political career, your spouse and kids), but seize whatever future destiny you might've had that you put up as collateral for the "loan".
                                    Since I adapted Awakening's Sphere set up (Knowing, Compelling, Ruling, etc.), they can do a fair amount of stuff with just levels 1 - 3 (basic manipulation of probability, manipulate the strengths and weaknesses of people and objects, create chains of interactions between multiple targets), but at level 4 and 5, they can radically alter or distort how things or circumstances grow or change and completely create or destroy someone's destiny.
                                    While Probability Science/Fate is their primary Science (with the ability to recognize potential winners and losers and start making sure that you're one of the winners being part of their initial indoctrination and training; ie PS/Fate 1 as their free starting Sphere), they also work a lot with Mind (almost rivaling the NWO with it), Prime, Time, and Connection. Life also plays a part, usually completely focused on the self (biofeedback, diet and exercise regimes, and other methods of life extension). Matter usually doesn't get much above 1 (being able to judge the make and value of objects), and Forces and Spirit usually end up being Equipment/Talismans purchased from the other Conventions (except for certain people in Special Projects who work in those areas).

                                    By the by, my default set of Mage backgrounds (which I limit to 10) are Allies, Contacts, Followers, Influence, Library, Mentor, Resources, Sanctum, Status, and Talisman. These are changed for Technocrat characters as Associates, Favors, Staff, Leverage, Training, Patron, Backing, Lab, Rank, and Equipment, all to reflect how I have Union culture, politics, and organization work.

                                    As for the Conventions and their various Spheres/Sciences, they generally break down as:
                                    • NWO: Mind (primary), followed mainly by Connection and Time. Life, Prime and Spirit.
                                    • Iteration X: Matter (primary), followed closely by Forces and Life. Mind, Time and Connection.
                                    • Progenitors: Life (primary), followed mostly by Mind and Matter. Prime and Spirit (almost entirely xenobiology)
                                    • Syndicate: Fate (primary), follow closely by Mind and Prime. Time, Connection and Life.
                                    • Void Engineers: Spirit (primary), followed closely by Connection and Time. Mind, Forces and Life.
                                    With defectors, NWO usually ends up in Virtual Adepts (but sometimes Order of Hermes, Celestial Chorus or Akashic Brotherhood), ItX in Virtual Adepts (and sometimes Sons of Ether), Progenitors in Virtual Adepts or Ether (and sometimes Verbena or rarely Ecstatics), Syndicate in Virtual Adepts (and sometimes the Hermetic House Jerbiton), and Void Engineers in Sons of Ether (and sometimes Dreamspeakers).

                                                The Hollow Ones

                                                This is a Dark Ride.
                                                - sign hung in John Hemingway's office, The John Larroquette Show

                                                So, to be perfectly honest, the Hollow Ones kind of bugged me at first. This was the summer or early fall of 1993 when Mage 1st ed originally came out, and they initially came across as something included just to remind everyone that this game was supposed to be set in the same world as Vampire, and so naturally had a group that seemed to embody every single annoying aspect of a certain segment of Anne Rice fandom and Francis Ford Coppola's Dracula including a "Mina is Vlad's reincarnated true love" romance angle. Their expansion in World of Darkness: Outcasts and Orphans' Survival Guide helped a little, but still skewed a bit toward style over substance, and didn't differentiate enough between the Hollowers and Orphans in general IMO. But at some point, they gradually grew on me. Part of it was that I knew a number of Goths over that decade, and certain presentations of the somewhere-between-Craft-and-Tradition explained them in a way that made sense and helped me understand how their philosophy and world view fit into not-necessarily-Goth subcultures (including the metal, horror, and indie comic scenes I was familiar with personally). And because I was able to find some many nooks and crannies of the world that they seemed to fit in, I was always kind of disappointed that they didn't seem to get the full expansion of internal factions and subfactions that other groups did. So I ended up making my own.

                                                The Unseen Society
                                                Sometimes seen as the upper class or staid elders of the Hollow Ones, there are references to them as far back as the early 19th century. They tend to be intellectuals and scholarly types who value their privacy as they delve into the realms of arcane lore, forbidden knowledge, and hidden history. Their mystical stylings tend toward high ritual, spiritualism, and science.
                                                • Grimm's Children: A loose collective of folklorists, historians, and storytellers constantly on the hunt for stories both old and new which serve to illuminate the true nature of the World of Darkness. They pour over uncensored fairy tails, urban legends, and ghost stories, seeking the power and lore hidden within our modern mythology, finding potential spells, forgotten rituals, and strange spirits. Many of them specialize in local history, urban exploration, true crime and even gossip as they compete to see who can find the purist or most morbid version of a tale. Given their interests and activities, it's not uncommon for members to end up entangled in the machinations of the Fair Folk.
                                                • The School of the Dark Arts: Dating back at least to Regency England or pre-Revolutionary France, they are an elitist society of aesthetics with an appreciation for works of art considered dark, gothic, and morbid. Members enjoy collecting, discussing, and even creating works that shock and offend conventional sensibilities; However, banal and predictable attempts at blasphemy - a crucifix in urine or a statue of the Virgin Mary covered in animal dung, for example - are met only with distain and mockery. In addition to their artistic activities, members also engage in Luciferian Satanism, chaos magic, demonology, and tantra.
                                                • The Eternal Mystery: A collection of mostly amateur detectives and investigators devoted to solving the ultimate mysteries of death and the afterlife. Members often insert themselves into mundane murder investigations, especially cold cases, while also looking into hauntings and other Fortean events and actively debunking spiritualist frauds. Their tools include forensic science, parapsychology, and various forms of necromancy. Many are skilled exorcists and spirit mediums.
                                                • The Spider's Web: Technomancers who augment their computer and engineering skills with eclectic magical styles. They specialize in exploring the mundane Deep Web, the mystical Digital Web, and the underworld's Dark Web. Their hardware frequently incorporates crystals, relics, and bound spirits, while their software is often coded with arcane symbols and languages. Members know each other largely online, and gain prestige by uncovering hidden secrets, unlocking closed doors, and advancing the art of cyber-technomagic.
                                                • The Councilors: An oddity among the Hollow Ones, they are the political faction that wish to make the group a more organized force within the World of Darkness. Some go so far as to aspire to become the tenth Tradition and take a much more prominent role in the Ascension War. Those that take this path often work to learn all they can of Awakened history and society, going out of their way to join Tradition cabals or chantries. They often specialize in both defensive magics and Ars Cupidea.

                                                (Concepts: Walking encyclopedia of horror stories, Professional spirit medium, Collector of cursed objects, Cynical Satanic shock rocker, High-tech necromancer, Would be diplomat to the Traditions, True Crime writer specializing in cults, Alice in a Dark Wonderland.)

                                                    The Danse Macabre
                                                    What most people think of when they think of the Hollow Ones. The most social of the group, revelers in the dark underground scenes of the counterculture at least as far back as the early 20th century and the Lost Generation. Evolving among Bohemians, Beatniks, Punks, and Goths, the majority desire to live free and dark until they leave behind a pretty corpse. Their mystical style tends heavily toward Ars Cupidea augmented with spiritualism and techno-magic tricks.
                                                    • The Succubus Club: Perhaps the most conforming to the Hollow One stereotype, their name derives from the infamous Chicago night club. They tend to mimic the fashions of various iterations of vampire styles, from the classically Gothic to the modern leather divas. Striving to be as sensual and seductive as possible, augmenting their Ars Cupidea with blood magic and demonology.
                                                    • The Nightingales: Sirens of the night letting their dark souls shine through their music and performance. [Yes, marvel at my purple prose.] Members focus their magic through voice, dance, and musical instruments augmented with rituals and superstition. Their melodies and lyrics work their way into listeners minds like mnemonic viruses, manipulating emotions, dreams, and life itself. They seem evenly divided between those who join to form groups vs those who front acolytes so as not to share the spotlight.
                                                    • The Midnight Riders: Diehard adherents of rockabilly and gothabilly, as well as its associated hot rod and motorcycle cultures. They roam the highways and byways in and between cities, seeking thrills and chills, often chasing urban legends and hoping to become one themselves. Their vehicles are typically augmented with various charms and even spiritual possession or awakening, while their bodies are covered with tattoos incorporating hidden mystical symbols.
                                                    • The Empty Heart: Serial heartbreakers who master Ars Cupidea for dark ends, they are cruel and cynical anti-Cupids who hold out the promise of love only to rip it away at a moment calculated to cause maximum pain and sorrow. Motivated by a cynicism often born of either personal heartbreak or innate cruelty, they claim that they only show others the true harsh nature of reality. It's not uncommon for members to also master forms of alchemy, divination, and charm making, both classical and techno mystical.
                                                    • Klubkroft: Originating in Germany's underground club scenes, technomages specializing in mass rituals disguised as raves, performance art, or flash mobs. Using psychotropic drugs, subliminal light shows, and subsonic frequencies mixed with spirit summonings, vision quests, and ritual sex, they try to shift the perspectives of attendees and performers alike. While this typically done to raise psychic awareness or support for social causes, events sometimes devolve into sessions of mass hypnosis or violent riots.
                                                    (Concepts: Singer with a voice that will break your heart and enslave your soul, Gothloli anti-Cupid, Dark-eyed fortune teller effecting an air of exotic mystery, Rave organizer with a hidden agenda, Last of the true old school punks, Leather-clad bondage queen, Drag Queen of the Damned, Byronic vampire hunter.)

                                                    The Darklings
                                                    The most radical of the Hollow Ones, those who pursue lifestyles and paths of power far outside the mainstream of magic and mysticism. Prone to nihilism and self destruction, their quest for dark enlightenment often involves the realms of necromancy, blood magic and violence. They tend not to play well with others.
                                                    • The Dead Kids: Necromancers who strive to keep at least one foot in the grave, walking among and even mimicking the dead. They dress in funeral garb and surround themselves with symbols of death, mourning, and decay while conducing seances, offering themselves up for possession, and raising corpses as walking dead. Many also delve into the dark arts of necrosynthesis. [See the revised Euthanatos Tradition book.]
                                                    • The Horror Show: Fans of horror and splatter punk, self-styled freaks and monsters striving to master the powers of fear, revulsion and violence. Members use body modification and shapeshifting to reform their bodies into nightmarish insanities while also crafting poetically sadistic traps and mind-bending labyrinths for their prey. While many claim to be acting out a form of primal justice, punishing the guilty and abusing the abusers, others are more interested in simply avenging perceived personal slights or even terror for terror's sake.
                                                    • The Razors: A cliquish cult of blood mages who channel their own personal pain and shed blood into mystical power. By mastering the mystical potential of their own blood, members are able to work powerful effects of blood sympathy to cast curses or enchantments, and even turn their own blood into deadly weapons or homunculi. Some go as far as to perform blood sacrifices for powerful magic rituals or summonings.
                                                    • The Bahari: Also known as the Children of Lilith, this secretive cult of the Dark Mother is one of the more organized factions of Hollow Ones. Teaching a doctrine of enlightenment through pain, members work to shock the sensibilities and cause anguish among those they wish to "teach", including themselves. They also work to balance creation with destruction, taking up such crafts as gardening or art, often with a dark bent including poisonous plants or grotesque imagery. Their group rituals tend involve inflicting each other with physical and emotional pain, followed by sharing what insights they've gained.
                                                    (Concepts: Disciple of the Dark Mother, Masked slasher out to punished everyone who made his life a living hell, Young man in love with a ghost, Mentally unstable lord of nightmares, Perfect poison flower, Body-mod fanatic experimenting with grotesque homemade cybernetics, Virtuoso director of bleeding edge guerrilla horror films acclaimed for their realism, Abuse survivor turning her pain into power.)

                                                        Of course, M20 differentiated the Hollow Ones from Orphans in a very different way: it moves the Orphans away from being ad-hoc magicians with at best a makeshift paradigm and turned them into mages who differed from those of the Traditions and Crafts only in the sense that they don't have memberships in any secret societies. This left the Hollow Ones as the advocates for what previous editions had presented as “Orphan Magic” — the ones who take the premise of “We're All Gods in Disguise” to its logical conclusion and argue that the philosophical debates that bog everyone else down are ultimately pointless, and the differences that the various groups tout about each other are essentially window dressing.

                                                            My head canon (and this is largely based in M20):

                                                            Fed up at constantly having their own concerns and needs ignored by the larger council, the Dreamspeakers withdraw from the Council of Nine (in our own game the events of Standing Rock served as the straw that broke the camel's back). And since they were largely made up of various diverse cultural groups that were really own combined in one tradition because many of the more...eurocentric traditions kinda shoved them together, refusing to recognize the diversity of the groups beyond "deals with spirits", they officially disbanded.

                                                            That's the official story at least. Unofficially a significant portion of the tradition actually remained together, secretly entering into membership talks with the Disparate Alliance. How they even knew of the existence of the Alliance isn't exactly clear.

                                                            The Seat of Spirit wouldn't exactly remain empty for long however, for soon after the Dreamspeakers resign the Council, the Void Engineers defect, bringing grave news. The majority of the Technocracy is completely under the control of Nephandi infiltrators.
