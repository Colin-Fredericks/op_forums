    For the idea of gaining additional dots in Strength for the Faith user, the easiest and most straightforward WOULD just be to use Life 3. You're just using the armor as an Instrument to explain the situational buff to your stats. The other option would probably be to use Forces 3 (maybe with Prime 2) to manipulate and boost the power behind your movements, so you get an equivalent number of dots to Strength-based dice pools.     I just have a minor disagreement here about the use of Forces. In this case, it would be Forces 2. Forces 2 is what regulates the intensity of any given energy type. If you want to amplify or damper any sort of energy, that's Forces 2. Therefore, for "Strength", the intent being clearly to amplify the amount of energy that the body can apply - so for example, if I try to lift something heavy, then I'll want Forces to give a little extra energy for that effort, but also would I want a little bit extra energy for when I'm punching someone's face - then all of that would be Forces 2.

    I know why people gets confused, and that's because we think with a real world physics's mind. We are thinking that, in this case, by adding energy to the system we are "creating energy out of nothing". And such assertion would be right for the real world, but is not the case for Mage the Ascension. The "intensity" of energy in MtA is not a matter of increasing electrons to a system, thus requiring the creation of extra stuff. "Intensity" instead is just a matter of perception. And "intensity" is controlled entirely by Forces 2, not 3.

    What Forces 3 thus is to change one type of energy into another, it's the "alchemical transmutation" of Forces. Thus, it is changing movement into electricity for instance.

    And, if you're invoking fireballs from the ether, then you need Prime 2 and a single point of Quint, because you're summoning fireballs out of nothing, thus you're transmuting Quint into fire energy. But Forces 3 is always and exclusively only transmutation, remember that.
    ​​​

But an Effect alone wouldn't make YOU stronger (wouldn't give you more dots to the dice pool).
    Matter 3 could change your Egyptian cotton suit into Kevlar, but you require Matter 5 to change properties rather than just transmuting *existing* materials.

    Besides, there's nothing really that Matter can do other than giving extra armor soaking dice.

    Yes, but that would be like Wolverine-ing into the fight. In game mechanics, not different from adding damage such as a sword would.

    Yeah, but what would that "machinery" actually do? Is it mundane or magical "machinery"?                                             Found what I was looking for in regards to augmenting physical attributes using hypertech.

                                            The Iteration X convention book has some systems advice on this, and refers heavily to the Enhancement background when it comes to cybernetics, which differs somewhat in M20. The latter is not for the faint of heart systems-wise. Using hypertech doesn't dodge the permanent Paradox which comes of using magick to enhance Attributes to "unnatural" levels, and there are some very nasty things Life adepts can do to those who graft mechanisms onto their bodies, provided they sufficiently understand what they're dealing with. Those primium countermeasures the Conventions developed are the main defense cyborgs have against being hit with magickally induced rejection of their own implants and grafted prosthetics, and if those fail it can get pretty grisly.

                                            In the realm of stuff like armored suits, there are some good examples given in the Iteration X book, and it'd be pretty easy to work off those in order to come up with variants. That material was written for Revised, so some tweaking might be necessary to get it to fit with M20 rules.

                                            In crossover games involving wraiths, Artificers are probably the deadliest threat those using Technocracy hypertech of any sort can come up against. The paradigms, methods and tools of the Conventions provide for no particular defense against the Inhabit Arcanos. On the other hand, such restless would we well warned not to try the same tricks against Tradition technomancers. There are some pretty entertaining examples of what can go wrong when a wraith tries to Inhabit mad Science devices..
