                        Entropy in the game deals with decay, causing, halting or reversing it. We can go for a physics definition of entropy to explain decay, but that goes beyond the scope of the game for now. The thing is, by a common understanding of decay instead of the scientific definition of entropy, we're talking about "natural" decay, the slow "degradation" of a Pattern over time, instead of overt transformations through sudden interactions. I know this is nonsensical from a physics standpoint, but it is a necessary understanding for the game.

                        With that, and re-reading, Entropy 3 alone can't revert damage on Matter. It affects Matter Patterns by affecting their decay on whatever form it takes on them, but it can't overtly repair damage. Would adding Matter 2 to it solve this? I don't know, depends on the Paradigm of the Mage, because "grabbing" the Pattern to target the individual fate of its components (like in a Quantum Healing) would conceivably be a Matter effect. That way, healing a Vampire with Entropy 3 would require all three Spheres discussed.

                        But Entropy 4 adds another layer. It stands from level 3 on two things. One, it overtly affects Life Patterns on this regard, which certainly gives credence to your understanding. If it can affect Life at 4 and Matter at 3, it doesn't have any missing piece of the puzzle for Vampires. The second distinction, though, is that it overtly states that it heals. It simply does, and Aggravated, nonetheless.

                        Going with this piece alone I'd say it doesn't have any reason to not heal damage as per the Base Damage or Duration chart, or very close to it. While the chart explicit says it applies just to healing from the Life Sphere, it is the default reference for any effect that is explicitly meant to deal or heal damage within the rules. We're not talking "makeshift" healing anymore.

                        How to explain that? By myself I wouldn't say that Entropy can really mend things, but "damage" is a rather abstract concept. Cutting off your arm causes damage, but lacking the arm isn't the damage itself, never having it back won't keep you from completely healing. In a sense, damage isn't about how much you're wounded or chipped, but how much those wounds get you from a break down, which is an Entropic event. So healing with Entropy can be understood as reinforcing you against decay enough to keep that wound or missing piece from causing a "catastrophic failure".

                        ​In any case, I'd say that the correct answer to LokiTX is that, by RAW, Entropy 4 is enough to heal any of their friends.

                            The point I guess is that not only do I think using Entropy 4 to heal a vampire is indeed an acceptable interpretation of RAW, but there is already sufficient penalty in place over the alternatives for anyone who plans to use it that way.

A newborn baby was once found alive in a trash bin with no less than 26 knife cuts in the body, pale due to blood loss, but alive. This is life.

                                                    Entropic healing would deal with that. You're not bleeding so much anymore. The vessels that were about to rupture from stress are now starting to get better. Your immune system is picking the right anti-bodies quickly by dumb luck. The many changes inside your body that are what can actually kill you are going in a favorable route. Also, your body is strengthened by the spell, it isn't just a matter of what your current wounds are doing to you right now, but also that they don't mean new wounds will have an easier time killing you anymore, either.

                                                    At the end of the day, 4 damage won't kill you, but it will make the next 4 kill you. If your body is strengthened enough for that to not happen, that's "healing".

                                                    But I do agree that, at the very least, an Entropy healing would rely on the spell duration to some degree.
