    Null Traditions

    03-02-2018, 10:12 PM
    So, do I still need to put up a spoiler warning for CB: Void Engineers? I guess I'll say it anyway: spoilers. Kinda.

    So, Threat Null is what happens when a Technocrat gets trapped on the wrong side of the Gauntlet and turns into a half-techno-spirit half-human monster, exaggerating the worst, or at least most inhuman, aspects of their respective Conventions. The Void Engineers themselves have conspicuously not been seen among the numbers of Threat Null, likely because their exaggerated versions would likely just go explore in the middle of nowhere and leave everybody else alone.

    My thought was this: Threat Null is an excellent plot device in many ways, not least because it allows us to see the potentially harmful consequences of the various Conventions ideals taken to their logical extreme. It's an opportunity for soul-searching and questioning how far you should go to attain your ideals (if you don't get instantly mindjacked by the Agents during your first encounter, obviously).

    So here's the million dollar question: Why not the Traditions? Sure, they're "the good guys", but, taken to their logical conclusion, their philosophies can be just as harmful as those of the Technocracy. Threat Null represents the Mage divorced from humanity and driven to monstrosity on the strength of their own vision, a very real threat to those with the power to reshape the universe in their image.

    Presented for your approval (or, y'know, inevitable scorn and derision, but whatever), the Null Traditions:

    The Akashic Brotherhood, on the far side of the Gauntlet, found their inner peace and shed their fragile mortality to become the Manasputra. Inhuman and detached in their endless enlightenment, the Manasputra are the least likely of the fallen or void adapted Traditions to attempt to breach the gauntlet or influence the Sleepers and, as such, are mostly harmless. Those who mistake their inaction for compassion, however, will find themselves quickly and dispassionately eliminated when they attempt to seek wisdom from these masters. The Warring Fists believe that an individual should find their own path and the Manasputra are willing to make examples of those who seek enlightenment from without and not within.

    Ever seeking the face of the One, perhaps the Celestial Chorus found what they were looking for in the depths of the Umbra. All heretical thoughts burned from their mind, the Archons serve their higher purpose with newfound resolve and unity. They seek to strike at the heart of corruption until its tendrils retract from the minds of God's children. Any unclean thought, any belief that questions the absolute supremacy of the One is scorched with holy fire along with the rest of the apostate's mind, until such time as the Archons see fit to teach them in the ways of true obedience to the almighty.

    The wild and wondrous sensations of the Umbra can have disastrous consequences on those who embrace them too fully. Such was the fate of the Dements, once members of the Cult of Ecstacy who have lost themselves to sensation. Nothing exists that cannot be touched with your hands or felt with your heart. Without tactile reminders that others are people of their own, the Dements continue their life's work of transcendence through sensation, without the constraints of "respect for life" and "empathy" to hold them back. Perception is reality and they perceive you as just another object.

    Perhaps through their familiarity with the Umbra or their closeness to the spirits, no Null Dreamspeakers have been documented by Umbral travelers. Perhaps a Dreamspeaker, shorn of their humanity and pushed to the limits of their philosophy, is just a spirit.

    How many broken lives does a monster touch? How many paths to darkness begin with a light brutally snuffed out? How many of the abused become abusers? In all its beauty, the Umbra is a place of deep and abiding corruption. The Euthanatos try to rescue what they can and redeem wherever the possibility rears its head. The Reapers, however, recognize this for the foolish idealism that it is. The Tellurian is sick and cannot be cured until every last drop of taint has been scoured away. Cold and dispassionate, the Reapers don't even deign to feel contempt as their scythes fall on those begging to be spared, those claiming to be victims. Their memories of cruelty are all that is needed to plant the seed of corruption; therefore, they are guilty The Euthanatos excise the cancer in the body, while the Reapers amputate the limb. Better safe than sorry.

    The Umbra holds more secrets than the totality of humanity could ever possibly document. The masters of secrets, the Order of Hermes, feel at once inspired and terrified by the sheer scale of the knowledge they will never live to obtain. Left to their own devices, forgotten among so much lore, the Hermetics were given an infinity in which to perfect their methods of learning and cataloging all the nameless wonders in the cosmos. In the pages of the dustiest of occult tomes, the Grigori were born. The Grigori are all secrets and all secrets are of the Grigori. They watch all events, even those they cannot possibly know, gathering secrets upon secrets for seemingly no reason other than because they can. They manipulate and politic, making power plays and using fronts in hollow imitations of the men and women they once were, having long forgotten the purpose of their endless strings of betrayal and blackmail. If indeed, there ever was one at all.

    The Sons of Ether are their vision. They have had time to think, and they've decided that human evolution was too slow. Human society too stagnant. Human technology too crude. The Sleepers need enlightened masters of Science to shepherd them to a glorious future. The problem is, no two Etherites can even come close to agreeing what that means. The same is true for the illuminated few who persisted beyond the Gauntlet, the Illuminati. Perhaps they would be a real threat to the Sleepers if they would stop trying to kill eachother over which twisted, alien vision of transhumanism was the correct one. One part feudal lord, one part comic book supervillain, the Illuminati can agree on only one thing, and that thing is how much they hate eachother.

    Many Verbena fear that the modern world has no place for them. Others say that they have no use for the modern world. In the primal wilds of the Umbra, these practitioners have had an epiphany: if human society has no place for them, they must not be human. They must be, in their own words, Predators. Raging against humanity for its crimes, real and imagined, the Predators lay blame not just at the feet of the clever monkey and his tools, but at his very mind. Intellect and consciousness separates us from the Earth and Her sublime rhythms. You will be made to forget your filthy tools, to love in the wilds and submit to the will of the pack, or you are simply prey.

    The infinite variety of reality beyond ours is astounding. Some people want to constrict that? Some people want to force you to obey their structure? That's the thought process of many Virtual Adepts, their forms long vanished into a cloud of 1's and 0's. To achieve the world of absolute freedom they desire, the Anons tear down the halls of order, one brick at a time. Health insurance provider? Hack their accounts and drain them into bankruptcy. Military bases? Goodbye soldiers, hello splatters on the wall, courtesy of their own automated turrets. Traffic slows to a halt. Schools close indefinitely. Planes crash into the ocean and hospital patients are quietly snuffed out. One by one, the pillars of society are crushed, until all are free to dance on their ruins.
    Thoughts?

                      My submission to the original thread on Threat Null way back when...

                      Nirvana: The Self is an illusion. These agents of enlightenment will free you of deception and ignorance. Their light removes all boundary between the consciousness and the external world. Harm to one thing afflicts everything, all hidden truths exposed, emotions and agendas melt together between enemies and friends alike. None meet them and return unchanged, and many never reclaim an individual identity to speak of, becoming part of their egoless light from their gazing third-eyes.

                      Archons: Agents of the Source - or so they claim. Messengers and Avengers of Divine Commandments. Deliverers of Terrible Miracles and Divine Judgment. Usually appearing as angelic visions of primal flame.

                      Liberalines: Chaotic forces of freedom from all bonds of restraint - be it authority, actual imprisonment, your own moral restraints, or the laws of physics. Rarely does the same one appear the same twice, as bizarre form modification through ecstatic self-mutilation is a norm.

                      The Emissaries: Half-human, half-spirit hybrids, they wish to bridge the gap not only between humans and spirits, but to merge the Umbra with Earth, replacing the entire world of physicality with the symbolic mechanics of the deepest Umbral realms. Instead of keeping the peace between humans and spirits as they once did, they find it more effective to simple combine the two. [credit to face_p0lluti0n for this idea]

                      Moroi: Voormas & his ilk. These macabre liches carry the Maelstrom of the Low Umbra in their wake, and will bring the Underworld to the living and the rest of the Umbra to end the boundary of death all together. [I know this is an inversion of what the Euthanatos actually believe; but the extreme of that (ending the world to turn the Cycle on a cosmic scale) is what the Nephandi already have covered - so the traditional Necromancer shtick of Voormas fits well enough here. Of course Senex is still human and upholds the actual Euthanatos ideals against Voormas]

                      Exarchs: “Will-Workers” taken to the megalomaniacal extreme. Would be God-Kings who burn with elemental power, bending the Umbra to their will as personal spirit courts. Regal Exemplars of Hubris and Arete (in the Greek sense) who wear their own authority in crowns of lightning.

                      The Pattern: Strange observers, slightly off from normal human, but often still supremely innocuous. The universe is their science experiment. Diversely strange phenomena and their often fatal results are inflicted and studied. Everyone on a void ship switches bodies suddenly. Space warps into an endless loop trapping subway riders inside while their train infinitely accelerates. A spontaneous pregnancy results in a hyper-evolved homo-neandrathalus child. All in the name of science.

                      The Sacrifice: Blood-Soaked vassals of nature itself. Animal-skulled heads, antlers, cloaks of sea-brined hands, endless bleeding runes, etc etc mark the chthonic atavists of the Sacrifice. The cycle of nature will be upheld, and the blood of those who would hold themselves apart from its tole (like all human civilization) will grease the turn.

                      Singularity: solipsistic utopias exist in the virtual realities of the Digital Web. Like fairy tales of old, one must resist the temptations they offer, and the wiles of the bodiless simulators as well, or disappear into a personal paradise never to return.


                      Note that these groups are not united, sometimes not even within themselves (they don't have Control and Conditioning like the Union did), and are not a universal fate of all Tradition Mages who became Spirits after the Avatar Storm. But those hubristic Masters who used Horizon to escape humanity into their own paradigmatic worldview without the pluralism that is the Traditions' greatest strength... this is what happened to them.

                      Sphinx, sending the Rogue Council broadcast might be another faction of Umbra-adapted Tradition Mages made up of some who united across paradigmatic lines and became something else in their transition entirely.

                                                          Originally posted by The Nilbog View Post
                                                          The KaLuon? It's a reasonable theory. By the same token, though, I do kinda' want to imagine that there are just actual aliens in Mage.
                                                          Aliens in Mage have shifted a lot over the editions. 1st Edition had a general concept that if the Void Engineers paradigm were to win out, the many masked Umbrood would permanently become Aliens, but it also had some truly weird entities, such as the Zigg'raugglurr, who didn't seem to quite fit the Umbrood-to-Aliens framework. The 1st Void Engineer book had LERMU (grey alien) forms created by the Progenitors, and filled with Umbrood spirits by the Void Engineer Dimensional Scientists, although the Revised Book characterized LERMUs as humans genetically modified to survive the Void. Book of Worlds and one of the Book of Madness editions introduced Deep Universe squid aliens for a faction of the Nephandi to serve - although this was sort of retconned by M20's idea that even those entities were a mask for the Absolute.
                                                          The Ka Luon were in the Time of Judgement Ascension book and a few other places, and appeared to be genuine extra-solar beings who generated their own reality zones and never absorbed paradox. They were going to appear in the Revised Void Engineers book, but ended up being cut due to wordcount.
                                                          I want to say that there was an alien autopsy that sort of crossed over with Demon: the Fallen in the Revised book The Bitter Road.

                                                          I think the core question about aliens in Mage is whether outer space is merely a realm shaped by the dreams and beliefs of the Earthly Consensus, or if that's only true to an extent. 1st Edition leaned hard on the idea that all quintessence originates from Earth, but other editions seemed to imply that there really might be other planets with their own civilizations not dreamed into existence by earthers.

                                                          I think if I ran a far future Mage game, I would shamelessly steal the Doyen, the Chromatics, the Qin, and the Coalition from Aeon.
