        Originally posted by Niza View Post

        Thank you thank you thank you! Slowly its coming together. Can you help me out with Defining The Abyss?
        Mmmm, crossover cosmology.

        So, "the Abyss" is one of the more problematic aspects of crossover cosmology for the Old World of Darkness because it has specific connotations within different game lines that do not (necessarily) match up:

        In Vampire, "the Abyss" was historically treated mostly as a piece of lore and power-source for the Obtenebration Discipline of the Lasombra Clan; while the magical practitioners of Abyss Mysticism had an intensely nihilistic "return to the pre-Creation void" philosophy going on, the cosmology-lite nature of Vampire means it gets relatively little solid fleshing-out. At least until V5 where they have linked it more explicitly with Oblivion IIRC.

        In Werewolf, "the Abyss" is a Near Realm associated with death, destruction, and lost things. While definitely part of the Middle Umbra, it has enough of a connection the the Low Umbra that a bunch of wraiths seem to have found their way there.

        In Demon, "the Abyss" is defined predominantly as a featureless void at the edge or bottom of Creation. It is pure Nothingness, or the edge of it at least. It does seem to be "adjacent" to the Lower Umbra in a cosmological sense, since I recall an account of a Fallen mentioning tormenting the souls of the dead. But depending on who you ask, the reliability of the memories of the Fallen is questionable.

        Where cosmological questions in the World of Darkness get really tricky is that the precise answer to the question of "how are all of these things related" is going to vary greatly depending on what kind of story you want to tell, and which particular "facts" you choose to regard as canon as a result. To use an example, a chronicle that ultimately wishes to affirm an Abrahamic religious worldview is likely going to have a very different cosmology from one which chooses to subvert or deconstruct it. A game that favours Werewolf mythology will probably come up with different answers than a game that favours Demon mythology.

        To go back to the example of the Abyss, I have not entirely settled all of my answers of how I want to handle it in my stories. But I do tend to favour an approach that has them connected to some extent, rather than completely different things which just coincidentally have the same name. Similar to Zones, "the Abyss" is a feature of the Tellurian that crosses the boundaries of the High, Middle, and Low Umbras in my personal canon.

                                I once came up with a visualization for how you might conceptualize the Dark Umbra.

                                Imagine a large soda bottle uncapped, inverted, and filled with a boiling, churning, chunky liquid. Soup is probably safest. The liquid is spiraling out the hole, but the chunks are caught in the tube, clogging it. The soup is the Tempest. The clog is the Labyrinth. The hole is Oblivion. Now take a map of the world and stick it to the inside surface of the bottle. That's the Shadowlands. Take some other smaller maps of fallen cities and imaginary lands and dump them into the soup to swirl around. Those are stable places in the Tempest like Stygia and the Far Shores. Take a bunch of cut outs of roads and rivers and attach them with one end to the Shadowlands and one end to one of the stable islands. They will writhe in the soup, but their endpoints remain attached to the same places. These are Byways.

                                It's great for the Dark Umbra, not so good for others. Does anyone have any good visualizations for them?

                                            The planets in space once were quite different than they are today. They were, as old tales and myths described them, mystic and alien worlds, back in the time when travelling into the umbra literally was doable by jumping into an airship in the Rennaisance and flying up. There was no 'classic space'.
                                            The spread of the Technocracy's reality, combined with the 'formatting' of space by the Void Engineers, essentially pushed the normal, scientific cold concept of space outward - essentially expanding 'normal' reality - and the Umbral representations of those planets got pushed behind the expanding gauntlet, leaving cold dead rocks and gas giants to the observer.

                                            This area ranges up to our solar system's asteroid belt, which is the 'second horizon' that leads into the Deep Umbra.

                                            Now, yes, the planets - at least their true selves beyond the gauntlet, on the Umbral side of things, are the Shard Realms of <insert sphere here>, while the shadow they cast on Earth's gauntlet are the 'Shade Realms of <insert sphere here>'. The Shade Realms are easier to reach due to the closer 'proximity' and also tend to be less...hazardous than the full blown Shard Realms.

                                            Of note is that there is no SR Prime. Some claim that it's the Sun, but other theories are that SR Prime is actually Earth - and since it was observed that each Shard Realm seems to be a gateway to a whole universe-like representation of that sphere, that our universe is actually the Prime-universe connected to SR Prime (Be it Sol or Earth - each would make sense, giving Sol's energy, and Earth's Prime nodes).

                                            An if this sounds confusing, welcome to Mage cosmology. It's an awesome ride.
                                            I suggest reading Beyond The Barriers and Infinite Tapestry. Both are really really good books about Mage cosmology, one 2nd, one Revised, each with kind of a different focus on things.
