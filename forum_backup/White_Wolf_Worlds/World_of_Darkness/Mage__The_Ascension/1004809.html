    Indirect Magickal Damage

    11-18-2016, 01:22 AM
    RAW has rules for obvious applications of magickal force (fireballs, lightning, telekinetic missiles, Prime augmented strikes, direct Life or Entropy attacks, etc), that are either performed on the target, done over an area, or aimed. When it comes to Damage, we consult the damage and duration chart, soak rolls as needed. So far so good.

    But what about all the specific ways that Mages can harm their opponent? Effects wherein the harm a character experiences is a side benefit of making the magick happen? Using Matter to turn the air into acid or a flammable gas (that is then ignited through mundane means). Prime used to conjure heavy objects over a person's head, or liters of water inside their lungs. Correspondence teleporting a target 100 meters in the air, so that they potentially fall to their deaths. Spirit forcing a target over the Gauntlet and into a Penumbra area with ephemeral environmental hazards. Forces altering gravity so a person flies sideways onto sharp objects or surfaces. Anything having to do with Radiation. A whole lot of edge cases that are entirely viable options (albeit usually Vulgar), yet the books don't really have rules for. Or the rules that exist need to be bent and twisted so they apply.

    Short of just rolling damage anyway (which smacks of RBD, and we've had this discussion before), how do you all handle this kind of thing? Have you any stories that feature interesting (and possibly amusing) applications of Sphere powers? And are there any other fringe cases I haven't already covered, but which would apply?

                      Standard WoD falling damage is a joke. An insane, utter joke.

                      A human falling 80 feet will be incapacitated. For a single day. If they don't soak some of the bashing damage. Roflmayo.

                      EDIT:
                      Which is why I once wrote a houserule:

                      For reference, a person falling from 30 feet (10 meters) equals getting hit by a MAC truck at ~30 mph (50 km/h). Except the MAC truck is planet sized.
                      In the default WoD rules this supposedly results in having to soak 3 whole bashing damage. Golfclap.
                      Scarily enough, after 9 feet (3 meters) you're already going 17 mph (28km/h).
                      There is a reason we instinctively are scared of jumping down even moderate heights.

                      So, new rules, with a concrete floor as default:

                      1) At 3 feet (1 meter), nothing bad happens. Let's not overdo it.
                      2) For every 3 feet (1 meter) after that, falling damage increases by +1 lethal damage
                      3) The damage gets reduced through a successful Dexterity+Athletics roll, pre-soak.
                      de
                      *) Uncontrolled/sudden falling doubles the amount of damage
                      *) A botch in the Dexterity+Athletics roll doubles the amount of damage. Yes, those two things stack.
                      *) Softer ground, soft obstacles slowing the fall etc. can turn the damage to bashing and/or reduce it further. Uneven ground, rocks, falling into a pile of scissors, etc. make things worse. Ask your ST for details.
                      EDIT2:

                      This rule could also be inverted and modified to calculate the damage from falling (accordingly heavy or sharp) objects. Because physics don't care much if you fall head first onto a bowling-ball, or a bowling-ball falls onto your head (aside of air-friction and inertia differences, but let's not make things too complex..)

                              Problem is that if you want a system for every kind of edge case, you're going to be spending more time looking up systems than actually playing out the scenario.

                              For example, if you want a system for objects falling on people, what are the things you're going to want to consider? Relative mass? Velocity? Hardness of the material? How sharp it is? Whether it lands on their head? How soft the ground is (on a scale of Water to diamond)...

                              That's a lot of variables to keep in mind for something which only takes a few seconds of in game time. Combat already takes up a huge amount of time as it is. Just think about it in terms of Health levels lost. So if you expect this sort of damage to injure them, its 3 dice, and if you expected it to cripple them, then 6 dice. Keep it simple and maintain the flow of the game.

                              As for a Nuke going off, unless they're doing some sort of magic to protect themselves, have all people in the immediate blast radius as "dead, very very dead". Anyone in the secondary radius will instead have radiation poisoning, which you can easily adapt the disease rules to fit (having them get gradually increasing bouts of damage each day for as long as they are poisoned, you can start with bashing or move straight to lethal depending on how severe the initial dose was).

                                          Really, the falling character should roll Dexterity + Athletics at the moment of impact, to simulate their efforts to roll with the landing and disperse force less harmfully. Each success reduces the difficulty of the resulting soak roll by -1, or alternatively just negates health levels of damage before soak. It depends on how charitable the storyteller wants to be. Obviously a person without Athletics shouldn't be able to perform this maneuver. Still, once it gets to the lethal damage range, the character relies on negating the force of the fall (unless they can soak lethal because of armor/magic).
