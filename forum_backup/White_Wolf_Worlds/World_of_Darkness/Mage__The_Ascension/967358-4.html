                                                In science and philosophy, the burden of proof falls on the more extraordinary claim. Which is more extraordinary claim? That our memories were created by experience, our species by evolution, our world by geology? Or that some bearded fellow (or a sentient lasagna or whatever) created the world last Tuesday?

                                                The problem with any Creation myth, whether it has the trappings of religion or science, is that they all require extraordinary claims that are impossible to proven experimentally. The religious ones that depend on dieties can at least be justified as symbolic explanations of reality derived from the life experiences of ancient peoples and, if considered logically, are relatively harmless and may be beautifully poetic. The scientific ones that depend on exotic substances are just confusing and poorly justified, as we have not been able to find one microgram of dark matter or generate one erg of dark energy.

                                                Logic suggests that there was no Beginning, because there would have to be a Before, and that there will be no End, because there will have to be an After. Until we can generate experimental proof for the extraordinary claims of any Creation myth, they should all be treated with skepticism. The majority of the religious ones can be rejected out of hand though, as we have experimental evidence or experiential evidence that contradicts them. There are no oceans of milk or water beyond the clouds, the world is not composed of a handful of elements, and humans are genetically derived from more than two people.
