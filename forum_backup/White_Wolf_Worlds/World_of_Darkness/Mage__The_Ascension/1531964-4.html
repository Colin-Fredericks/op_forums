            I have not actually encountered any Mage players who really hold that view. Pretty much everyone I have played with was greatly invested in the Paradigms of their characters. The problem is really in the core rules themselves. There is a real disconnect between the core principles of Mage and the cosmology that the game proffers up. At its core, Mage is a game about playing miracle-workers engaging in a philosophical war over reality. All of the cosmology sprinkled on top is in fact unnecessary, and can very much detract from the themes of the game. It is easy enough for the savvy player to grok Mage, and not pay too much attention to how the books actually describe it, but why would one write the books in such a manner in the first place?

            I think it is easier to get the point across with an example. If your mage has the Paradigm of a standard western ceremonial magician, then almost none of the setting's cosmology will work for them. The High Umbra will be the astral plane. There will probably not be a strict delineation between the Low Umbra and various heavenly afterlives. The Avatar will not be susceptible to Gilgul. The Pure Ones will likely be emanations of God. Avatar Essences will simply not fit into the Paradigm.

            In order to ensure that such a character would fit with the setting, compromises would have to be made, and that would damage the integrity of the character. The game is about playing will-workers with strong positions on reality. If one cannot reasonably play a character of a given Paradigm, then there is a real problem.

                        For what it's worth, the trio I suggested was motivated by the “Science, Faith, and Magick” line that appeared on the cover of Mage: the Sorcerers Crusade. That said, I'm not opposed to diversifying beyond that, if a good example of a fourth option presents itself. Note, though, that these three cover a lot of ground on their own; and something to the effect of “scientific mystic/techgnosi” (a mechanism allowing you to hybridize them) potentially covers even more ground. Your “Touched by Fate” suggestion is basically a hybrid between Scientific Experimentation and Divine Revelation: if Fate/the Force/the Cosmos really is impersonal, but there's still a principle that you must live by in order to retain your Magick, that principle can in theory be analyzed like a set of scientific axioms.

                        I don't know if I'd do the “hybrid learning styles” as a Merit, the way M20 does with technomancers and mystics; I'd prefer not to, as I prefer options such as these to be built around trade-offs and freely selectable by the player, as opposed to being something that the player has to decide on the basis of a point budget. But if the hybridizing results in the strengths of the “parent tenets” covering for each others' weaknesses, resulting in something that's objectively better, then making it Merit-based would be warranted.

                        Also, note that my original notion was focused on one particular set of tenets from Prism of Focus: the Epistemological Tenets, which are centered around how knowledge is acquired. Those aren't the only tenets presented there; they're just the ones that I have ready answers as to how mechanics might be attached. Another set of tenets had to do with the openness of your Paradigm: it may be Closed, Hierarchical, or Open. A Closed Paradigm is one that requires that everything be explainable through your worldview; a Hierarchical Paradigm assumes that your own Paradigm is better than the alternatives, but that the alternatives aren't wrong per se; and an Open Paradigm is one where the mage's Paradigm is viewed “merely” as being the best choice for him; and that others have to find their own truth. I could see that somehow playing into the system for Quiet — how your character is likely to react when his Paradigm clashes with the world around him — with Closed Paradigms making you more susceptible to Denial (or whatever its M30 equivalent is) while Open Paradigms make you more susceptible to the M30 version of Madness. And Hierarchical either doesn't show a bias either way or it biases in a third direction (not Morbidity; if there's a third type of Quiet associated with the Openness of your Paradigm, I'd be inclined to compare it to Hubris).

                        As well, individual Practices in Prism of Focus are given a Benefit and a Weakness: for Gutter Magick, the Benefit is that they're masters of doing more with less: while other Practices tend to be rather particular about what Resonances they can use in a given Effect, a Gutter Mage can put in a bit of extra work (take a bit more time) to leverage an otherwise irrelevant Resonance trait through some cobbled-together reasoning; and the Weakness is that they done get to use Unique Instruments.

                        My point in bringing this up is that not everything in terms of customizing how Magick works needs to be done in the same way. In particular, not everything is a “fourth option” to Science, Faith, or Magick. Some things, while valid reasons to customize the rules, are independent factors.

                        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                    Have I mentioned Resonance yet? I'd like to see it more thoroughly integrated into the rules for Paradox as well as regular Magick. For example: instead of splitting Quiet into multiple types, go back to the 1e and 2e notion that Quiet is Quiet; but: the form that your Quiet takes is influenced by your Resonance. And the four flavors of Resonance found in M20 stay: each type of Resonance, in addition to its specific type, is either Devotional, Elemental, Stabilizing, or Temperamental. When you've succumbed to Quiet and you're under the influence of a Temperamental Resonance, the result is akin to what MRev and M20 call Madness; when you're under the influence of a Stabilizing Resonance, your Quiet takes a form that could be characterized as what M20 calls Denial; when you're under the influence of an Elemental Resonance, your Quiet takes a form of a sort of obsession with whatever the element is; so if you're under the influence of Jhor (that is, the Elemental Resonance of Death), your Quiet takes a form of an obsession with death (M20's Morbidity). And if you're under the influence of a Devotional Resonance, your Quiet takes the form of a self-righteousness — a hubris where you believe that in the service to your cause, you can do no wrong.

                                    Quiet itself is “merely” a mixture of you seeing things that aren't there and/or exaggerating the importance of things that are, and not seeing things that are there and/or downplaying their importance. Different so-called “types” of Quiet are merely the result effect of emphasizing one of these effects over the others, as influenced by your Resonance.

                                    And other kinds of Paradox similarly get informed by your Resonance.

                                                That has several benefits, getting away from miniscule dice pools not being the only one - for instance, you would give preference to "stick to your better tricks", since even if you're rolling a "spell" that only requires lvl 1 on a Sphere, being a Master at that Sphere makes you better at it than someone taking their first lessons on it - heck, an Arete 3 Mage with Mind 3 would have the same dice pool than an Arete 5 Mage with Mind 1... Being that "Arete" would then become "raw power", but the Sphere levels would translate more towards "Experience in the field".

                                                With such a system implemented, then it becomes pretty straightforward to use "Magik Enhancing Abilities" or "Abilities Enhancing Magik": whenever you're using Magik with a skill, instead of adding Spheres to the Arete roll, you add your skill level.

                                                    A. Types of Paradigm:

                                                    1- Divine Revelation;

                                                    2- Materialistic/Mechanicist (Scientific Method);

                                                    3- Mystical;

                                                    4- Fate (? - other, or simply "Unknown" or even "Unknowable")


                                                    B. Source of Power:

                                                    1- Internal (willwork, "power of the soul", "individual Godhood" and so on)

                                                    2- External (Magical principles of the Cosmos, Scientific Discovery, Gifts from Spirits, Gifts from God(s) )


                                                    C. Openess of Paradigm:

                                                    1- Closed (only my view is right, whatever anybody else does follow the same principles as what I do, they just don't comprehend it the way I do) - susceptible to Denial

                                                    2- Hierarchical (there are many ways to do Magik, but my way is BETTER) - susceptible to Hubris

                                                    3- Open (there's many ways to do Magik, none are necessarily better than the others, my way is simply better for me, but everybody must find their own Truth) - susceptible to Madness


                                                    D. Usage of Foci:

                                                    1- Strict: cannot do Magik without some Focus (which needs not to be some technomancy toy of the latest generation or the most precise sacred geometry, even a Techieboy can accomplish many feats by meditation, hiper memorization, hypnotic advanced techniques and so forth; but they are absolutely necessary)

                                                    2- Optional: Foci help to concentrate the Will / concentrate the Magical energies of the Cosmos / attract the will of the Spirits/God(s), but it is not indispensable, it is simply easier. I can see "Psyonics" having this particular worldview, where at most they simply place two fingers in each side of their heads and just think about what they want to happen and contract their sphincters.



                                                    Now, with this simple table we could basically create any kind of Paradigm possible (I think). We just need to mix the items from each, for example, unionist techies would probably be A. 2 (Materialist/Mechanicist), B. 2 (External Sources), C. 1 (Closed) and D. 1 (Strict Foci)

                                                    A Gnostic (Theosophical) Choristers would be A. 1 (Divine Revelation), B. 1 (Internal Source), C. 1 (Closed) and D. 2 (Optional Foci), while a Psychic would could be A. 4 (Fate or simply "Unknown") but it could even be 2 (Materialistic/Mechanicist), B. 1 most likely, but a Nephandi could very well believe that his "psychic powers" are a "curse from Cthulhu-esque-thing from the other side", and then it could very well be 2. C. could either be 1 (Closed) or 3 (Open), if the Psychic either believes that "all those so called "Mages" are just simply Psychics, the fools!", or it could be 3 Open, but in this case he would believe "I'm a Psychic, but those other guys are actually doing "Magic", whatever that is", in which case he believes his powers come from "the power of the (his) mind", but that a Hermetic for example draws his powers from the "Magic Mists" or whatever; so this individual would actually believe in several different sources of power, rather than believing that "everybody is a "Mage" ". That's also an interesting view; an "Awaken" with an Open Paradigm can believe that some people are gifted by God, some are gifted by Spirits, others just do Science and yet others draw mystical energies from "the primordial Mists", while he himself is an X-Men Professor Xavier type. I mean, Dr Strange and Professor Xavier would NEVER tell each other "you know what, we just do the same thing all over, but I call it "Magic" while you call it "psyonics", but it's actually all the same". In an Open Paradigm, that most likely DOESN'T HAPPEN. And finally for our Psychic, D. 2 (optional Foci), yes there are some funny songs he can chant to help him concentrate, but he just really needs to press his head and contract his sphincter really hard for his "powers" to work.

                                                    That table also gives us a better vision as to what we want our Cosmology to be; I would get away with Avatars completely in here; maybe replace it for some other mechanics.

                                                    And each one of these items can have interesting mechanics to it - for example, "Divine Revelation" requires strict adherence to certain precepts; that's a limitation, but on the other hand it allows you to do things that others cannot (like somebody said somewhere, you don't need to know about Blacksmithing to ask for your God(s) or the Spirits to give you a "Sword to smite my enemies). A Materialistic/Mechanicist view would be less malleable, but have far more reliable and replicable results; a strict need for Foci would force you to always use something, but on the other hand, your effects are easier; and many many other ideas that I still haven't concocted (but that I expect that your minds can help me in expanding those ideas)

                                                            Originally posted by Magus Maximus View Post
                                                            A. Types of Paradigm:

                                                            1- Divine Revelation;

                                                            2- Materialistic/Mechanicist (Scientific Method);

                                                            3- Mystical;

                                                            4- Fate (? - other, or simply "Unknown" or even "Unknowable")


                                                            B. Source of Power:

                                                            1- Internal (willwork, "power of the soul", "individual Godhood" and so on)

                                                            2- External (Magical principles of the Cosmos, Scientific Discovery, Gifts from Spirits, Gifts from God(s) )


                                                            C. Openess of Paradigm:

                                                            1- Closed (only my view is right, whatever anybody else does follow the same principles as what I do, they just don't comprehend it the way I do) - susceptible to Denial

                                                            2- Hierarchical (there are many ways to do Magik, but my way is BETTER) - susceptible to Hubris

                                                            3- Open (there's many ways to do Magik, none are necessarily better than the others, my way is simply better for me, but everybody must find their own Truth) - susceptible to Madness


                                                            D. Usage of Foci:

                                                            1- Strict: cannot do Magik without some Focus (which needs not to be some technomancy toy of the latest generation or the most precise sacred geometry, even a Techieboy can accomplish many feats by meditation, hiper memorization, hypnotic advanced techniques and so forth; but they are absolutely necessary)

                                                            2- Optional: Foci help to concentrate the Will / concentrate the Magical energies of the Cosmos / attract the will of the Spirits/God(s), but it is not indispensable, it is simply easier. I can see "Psyonics" having this particular worldview, where at most they simply place two fingers in each side of their heads and just think about what they want to happen and contract their sphincters.



                                                            Now, with this simple table we could basically create any kind of Paradigm possible (I think). We just need to mix the items from each, for example, unionist techies would probably be A. 2 (Materialist/Mechanicist), B. 2 (External Sources), C. 1 (Closed) and D. 1 (Strict Foci)

                                                            A Gnostic (Theosophical) Choristers would be A. 1 (Divine Revelation), B. 1 (Internal Source), C. 1 (Closed) and D. 2 (Optional Foci), while a Psychic would could be A. 4 (Fate or simply "Unknown") but it could even be 2 (Materialistic/Mechanicist), B. 1 most likely, but a Nephandi could very well believe that his "psychic powers" are a "curse from Cthulhu-esque-thing from the other side", and then it could very well be 2. C. could either be 1 (Closed) or 3 (Open), if the Psychic either believes that "all those so called "Mages" are just simply Psychics, the fools!", or it could be 3 Open, but in this case he would believe "I'm a Psychic, but those other guys are actually doing "Magic", whatever that is", in which case he believes his powers come from "the power of the (his) mind", but that a Hermetic for example draws his powers from the "Magic Mists" or whatever; so this individual would actually believe in several different sources of power, rather than believing that "everybody is a "Mage" ". That's also an interesting view; an "Awaken" with an Open Paradigm can believe that some people are gifted by God, some are gifted by Spirits, others just do Science and yet others draw mystical energies from "the primordial Mists", while he himself is an X-Men Professor Xavier type. I mean, Dr Strange and Professor Xavier would NEVER tell each other "you know what, we just do the same thing all over, but I call it "Magic" while you call it "psyonics", but it's actually all the same". In an Open Paradigm, that most likely DOESN'T HAPPEN. And finally for our Psychic, D. 2 (optional Foci), yes there are some funny songs he can chant to help him concentrate, but he just really needs to press his head and contract his sphincter really hard for his "powers" to work.

                                                            That table also gives us a better vision as to what we want our Cosmology to be; I would get away with Avatars completely in here; maybe replace it for some other mechanics.

                                                            And each one of these items can have interesting mechanics to it - for example, "Divine Revelation" requires strict adherence to certain precepts; that's a limitation, but on the other hand it allows you to do things that others cannot (like somebody said somewhere, you don't need to know about Blacksmithing to ask for your God(s) or the Spirits to give you a "Sword to smite my enemies). A Materialistic/Mechanicist view would be less malleable, but have far more reliable and replicable results; a strict need for Foci would force you to always use something, but on the other hand, your effects are easier; and many many other ideas that I still haven't concocted (but that I expect that your minds can help me in expanding those ideas)
