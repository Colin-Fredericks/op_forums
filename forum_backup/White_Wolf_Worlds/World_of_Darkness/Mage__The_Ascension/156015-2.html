                I know this isn't exactly the topic at hand, but this annoys me a lot.

                While I love using conservative numbers for the supernatural beings in my chronicles, such as 1 mage for 250 thousand people, because this provokes more political bickering, territorialism and reasons for the players to unite in a group; I have to agree with the guy who gave the example of Canada, that this also creates some strange asymmetries.
                A country like Switzerland, Israel or Austria, averaging a population of 8 million, would have... 32 mages at best. You either have supernatural beings concentrated in megalopoli like New York, Shanghai and Moscow or you have them occupying a huge sparse area, completely isolated from each other, like feudal dukes.

                I can see this making sense with vampires, 90% of their after lives are dedicated to securing food source and engaging in political confrontations; they also don't have mobility because of the sun.
                But mages? It feel incoherent and I always need to fill my chronicles with side characters, crafts, sorcerers and hedge wizards, which lead the mages to act more as a privileged elite, as enlightened despots, than... Mages.

                                                            I generally like the percentage breakdowns presented in this thread, but have some issues that I would like to raise for discussion.

                                                            Firstly, I have a query about numbers relating to Crafts that are clearly rooted in a specific culture pertaining to an ethnic group. In this instance, I'm thinking of the Kopa Loei. (1) There are currently just under 700k people in the entirety of Polynesia (irrespective of ethnicity). (2) With 1 Mage/250k people, there might be 3 awakened, total, in this entire group of islands. (Yes, I'm aware that there are plenty of Polynesian background people living outside the islands. Even if we said the equivalent population was outside the islands, it'd only be 6 awakened.) 3) M20 indicates that a typical Kopa Loei group is between 2-20 members.

                                                            Are there just more Kopa Loei as a percentage of the population of Polynesia? Alternatively, are there a disproportionate number of people outside "Polynesian" culture (I balk at this term, as Maori culture is quite distinct from Hawaiian or Samoan or Tahitian and so on) that are Kopa Loei? If so...isn't this cultural appropriation? As a person living in the Pacific Rim, the grouping of all these different cultural group under one "Craft" is only slightly better than the original "Dreamspeakers".

                                                            Next, I'm skeptical about the OoH having the highest percentage of Mages, particularly in contrast to traditions like the VA. What's the justification for these percentage splits? I'd have thought that VA would be on the up, particularly considering the pervasiveness of the Web (since Revised came out). I think it might be good to revisit these percentages, and also indicate which way the trend is with regards to numbers. That is, which traditions are increasing in membership, and which are not.

                                                            Finally, separately, can someone please explain why the Wu Lung are experiencing reduced numbers (M20) to the extent that they've relaxed their "all-male" and "Han Chinese" standard? Surely in mainland China, with a population of 1.4 billion will have at least a few Wu Leng? That gives us 5400 awakened in China...even if only 1% are Wu Leng, that's still 54 purely in mainland China. I'd hazard there'd be a few more....
