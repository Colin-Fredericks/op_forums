    Someone else can check the other books.
    It may be of interest that in MotA restoring temporary Willpower it's one of the functions of ArchMind. This is described as "refresh Willpower fortifying his mental Pattern". This requires expenditure of Quintessence, but I don't think this is a call-out to other Spheres, it could also be as how Life requires Quint to heal Aggravated damage.

    On the opposite end, a counter-argument for Willpower being ruled by Spirit it's that beings with the Souless Defect can have Willpower ... Now, agreed that Mage it's iffy with the whole spiritual side of humans, calling the Avatar soul albeit one would think there should be more to it ... but Hit Marks are robots, created w/o Spirit AFAIK, and they have Willpower. Now, one could say this is just for the purpose of mechanics, but (unless they changed something in the new Guide to the Technocracy) there aren't any rules about such construct players not having Willpower. And I couldn't find any on Gods & Monsters, if anything they're even more vague on the subject of how willfull are intelligent constructs.

    Overall, it does seem to me that Willpower it's more Mind than Spirit.

    Of course, this whole subject is quite unexplored, but I haven't found proof of it being the other way.

    Interestingly, on the subject that combines Spirit and Mind 5, ArchMind does some stuff that should absolutely be coupled with Spirit for the rules to make any sense - but that should arguably be possible at these levels with both spheres: Interacting with the "mind" of an Avatar. In concrete, this allows to access the Backgrounds of Past Lives and Dream, that are, if I understand correctly, function of what information your Avatar reveals to you trough different means (Past Lives it's accessing memories of previous holders of the Avatar).

            Originally posted by Ramnesis View Post
            Enchant Objects (Enchant Patterns)

            The mage can also channel quintessence into objects to improve them in some metaphysical way. Improved weapons can hurt spirits and inflict aggravated damage. Other things improved in this way can instead soak aggravated damage and spirit attacks.
            Iirc, there is a text in M20 with an example of Prime reinforcing a device that isn't about soaking/damaging. Like, making a machine just run better. I don't know exactly how this would translate into game mechanics, though, but it seems very useful.



            Body of Light (Body of Light)


            A mage can use Prime 2 to project a hologram like replica of his body. M20 is not as clear as previous editions about what this is supposed to be used for. There are, however, a few places in M20 that use this as the base for other effects, most prominently as a basis for a more robust astral form than normal projection. Note that as written this only lets a mage make a projection of themeselves, which is a break from previous editions. Given that this is seemingly the basis for some illusion spells, though, this likely isn't an intended restriction.[/quote]

                    Note that A Rich Bastard's Guide to Magick adds a massive capability to Prime 1: the ability to access Primal Ventures, in very much the same way that Primal Utility can. And Lore of the Traditions generalizes the concept beyond financial Ventures to practically any collective human endeavor, with its Stable Wellsprings (which are basically Primal Ventures stripped of the economic terminology). I'm going to use "Stable Wellspring" to refer to both concepts, as it's the most general and arguably subsumed the notion of Primal Ventures. And you should arguably be able to target temporary Wellsprings as well; consider a Cultist using the Practice of Bardism on the audience of her rock concert to treat the enthusiastic participants as a temporary Wellspring…

                    This addition has major ramifications, not the least of which being that it arguably becomes possible to target a Stable Wellspring with other Magick. Once you've done the due diligence to be able to access a Stable Wellspring (which generally involves contributing to it in some way), why shouldn't you be able to target the entire Stable Wellspring with other Effects as if it was a single thing? Go Big or Go Home.

                    This potentially steps on the toes of Correspondence, in a big way. Because targeting a Wellspring is very similar to targeting everyone who meets a particular criterion, regardless of where they are or how many of them there are. The good news is that it includes something roughly analogous to needing to find a Sympathetic connection to your target(s) — namely, the aforementioned need to contribute to it. But once you've done that? Boy howdy, can you do a lot. You could, for instance, arguably use Forces to set fire to every participant in the Wellspring — something which arguably would only be prevented by the fact that you'd end up targeting yourself as well. But what about using Life to heal every participant? Or any other beneficial Effect.

                    Even with the rules a written, you can tank the Wellspring by overdrawing from it. This is analogous to something that normally requires the highest level of Entropy: futzing with the efficiency of an abstract system, in a way that causes it to break down.

                    I'm thinking that much of this functionality should be relocated to Correspondence, with "access Stable Wellspring" being a conjunctional Correspondence/Prime Effect, rather than simply a Prime Effect. Arguably, the ability to target everyone associated with the Stable Wellspring should require Correspondence 5, being analogous to Co-Location; and the "contribute to the Wellspring" part of it is simply the bog standard "establish a Sympathetic connection to it". Though maybe it should only be Correspondence 4, since you're not actually stacking locations?

                    But then there's the matter of Entropy, which also has the ability to target distributed systems… Should it maybe be involved in Effects such as this? Maybe Entropy, rather than Prime, is what's needed to treat an organization as a single thing… But should that be in addition to, or instead of, requiring Correspondence?

                    That said, I could be persuaded that Prime is always needed to target a Wellspring on the basis that it largely amounts to a far more effective counterpart to "affecting multiple targets" in much the same way that Correspondence is a far more effective counterpart to "affecting targets at range". The usual rules for hitting multiple targets doesn't increase nearly as rapidly as the number of participants in a Wellspring does, as five ranks in a Wellspring has the potential to affect people all over the world, with a population comparable to a nation. I don't object to that being possible; but I don't think five dots in a single Sphere should be enough to do it, especially when that Sphere is already being used to overcome range. Tying it to Prime would also let you argue that most such Effects should arguably cost Quintessence; and the larger the Wellspring, the more Quintessence it should cost to affect it. So maybe Correspondence 5/Prime 2, a Sympathetic connection, and a Quintessence cost equal to the dot rating of the Wellspring to affect all of its participants as of they were a single thing.

                    Or maybe not; I don't know. There's a lot of interesting possibilities to consider, but also a lot of room for abuse.

                    Thoughts?

                    (PS: there's also the matter of how this plays into the optional Management and Human Resources rule in M20 on page 595.)

                                Primal Utility is better at creating Wonders: it only needs three dots to create a Device, as opposed to the four dots that Prime needs to make a Talisman.

                                As well, while it's never explicitly said that you need Primal Utility for this, the Technocracy knows of a way to set up a facility which lets you create Devices or Grimoires without having to expend personal Utility to make the rolls, or to expend a point of Willpower to complete the process; though you still need a source of Utility to integrate into the Device or Grimoire. My guess is that this "Enlightened Science assembly line" requires Primal Utility 5 to set up; though I'm open to arguments that it only requires 4 dots. Either way, it is something that the Technocracy still has a monopoly on.

                                And M20 Sorcerer leverages this a step further by saying that mages can create Hedge Paths using the rules for creating Talismans. A logical extrapolation of this is that Technocrats get to create Paths the same way they create Devices, which means that they need only three dots in Primal Utility to do so, and can also set up "factories" for doing so. By all rights, Technocrats ought to dominate Sorcery as a result of this.
