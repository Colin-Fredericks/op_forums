                    Originally posted by HorizonParty2021 View Post

                    The player comes up with a character concept where his character has been reincarnated. His previous incarnation was a legendary Mystic Mage. He fully recalls the world as it was before guns were introduced to the Consensus. He sees the modern world as an illusion. He takes Time 3 and Forces 3. It's an original Paradigm. Why should the player not be able to do this if they have put careful thought into this concept? And, if the effort to create an original Paradigm doesn't merit some bending of the rules, why is Purple Paradigm a pejorative term? It's like, "Be original... Oh, you can't do that because rules... Oh, you just want to conform to the rules so you can get on with playing? - PURPLE!"
                    The "purple paradigm' is not a pejorative for using the game rules. It is a pejorative for treating the game rules as an actual paradigm or as an in world metaphysics rather than a gaming abstraction. It applies when people make their character have a paradigm like "The world is made up of nine spheres and if I know enough about them I can make things happen" and then using that to try and bypass elements like instruments or paradox. It also was a major source of contention in the Revised era because lots of people assumed that the "purple paradigm" was a universal thing mages started defaulting to as they gained Arete and shed instruments. M20 is a little clearer on what shedding instruments means.

                    The example character you gave works as an original paradigm (I think. I'm presuming he's essentially using the paradigm of his past self, whatever that is). The player can do that. He still needs to have the right spheres for the effects he wants because he's still playing the same game as everyone else. He still gets hit with paradox too (when appropriate), because like many (most?) mages his paradigm does not play well with the consensus.

A lot of her spells are self-targeted and Coincidental, a few being Vulgar Without Witnesses.

They are opting for a game where using Techno-Magick is the default and doesn't require any mental gymnastics to create an effect. Paradigm fits Consensus. Also, I am not talking about Hyper-Technology. Tradition Mages in populated cities have to act like Technocracy Mages in terms of the effects they are creating in order to avoid Paradox.

                        The example character you gave works as an original paradigm (I think. I'm presuming he's essentially using the paradigm of his past self, whatever that is).                         "using the paradigm of his past self" is what I meant, correct. It's not a character I would try to play or slip past the storyteller to get away with Consensus-breaking effects. A Paradox-ridden NPC maybe.

                        The degree to which I enforce Paradigm and Focus requirements and how is influenced by some IRL factors that I suspect differ from most people here. I am not part of any local gaming club. The people I play with were new to both Mage and RP, generally, when I introduced the game. If I had tried to sell Mage while suggesting that you have to pass an essay-exam on Paradigm in order to begin play, it would have been a tough sell. My approach is less "front end" and puts more emphasis on Vision for the World.

                        For comparison, let's say you have a character, beginning as a sleeper, who has an optimistic vision for the world. They believe that it is possible to change the world for the better in the near future. They Awaken and are recruited into the Euthanatos Tradition and learn about The Wheel of Ages. Their Mentor teaches them that they are currently in an Age Darkness, soon to be followed by an Age of Sorrows. This aspect of their Paradigm will significantly influence what they can do with Magick and how they cast. The character might have a surprisingly optimistic Vision for the World that somehow fits their Tradition (accelerate the wheel to skip the next age), an aspect of their Personal Paradigm. In standard play, the emphasis is on the current metaphysical understanding of reality, not the vision toward which they are trying to influence the world.

                        By making Vision for the World as important as Paradigm Metaphysics, I allowed the development of each character's Paradigm to be extended over the course of the whole Chronicle, rather than compacted into an awkward essay-exam that they must pass before the end of session zero. The vision of an ideal world that the character is trying to push into the Consensus shapes the type of Magick they can do. I will be getting more into Resonance effects as the campaign rolls forward. We all started with some amount of Arcane for survivability's sake, but will be exploring the benefits of high personal Resonance in the context of Paradigm.

                            First let me clarify that I see nothing wrong with your play-style or take on Paradigm. I'm just talking rules.

                            Originally posted by HorizonParty2021 View Post
                            ​The conflict is right there in what you just said: YOUR (player's) Paradigm states what Magick can do... Versus: No Paradigm can ignore the Consensus...
                            It seems that you mixed up a little what is the Consensus with what is your Paradigm. And maybe the focus of the misunderstanding is the Paradox.

                            Paradox isn't anti-magic. Paradox isn't the impossibility of magic. Casting a fireball will incur Paradox, but the fireball is still cast. It is supported by the caster Paradigm, but not by the Consensus, and the Consensus punish you for breaking HIS Paradigm.

                            So the idea of a character that brings his Paradigm from their past life is absolutely valid, but in no way that implies immunity to current Consensus, the Consensus will keep punishing them for breaking its perceived rules.

                            No Paradigm includes the idea of Paradox, they just try to understand it. The Paradigm states that reality works in a given way (it doesn't) and magic is the Mage using the laws of this theory of reality to do stuff. But the Consensus is the Paradigm of the entire mass of humanity, and it is a vengeful Mage. It makes its own magic all the time, we just call it natural laws, but it doesn't use countermagic, instead it retaliates with Paradox. Something that is by nature absent in the original theory of the Mage, creating an unpredicted consequence.

                            The Mage can have absolutely any explanation on their Paradigm of why they aren't prone to Paradox. They still are.

                            Originally posted by HorizonParty2021 View Post
                            While in the city, he uses Correspondence and Forces to make an area around him operate under the physical laws that function in the Primal reality Zone, where guns don't work.
                            First it would be an effect, not a Paradigm. And second, if they could really pull out that effect (which I think would be more complex than just two Spheres and would at least use Prime, not Forces), then yes, that would work precisely like that, so what? It would still not be a consequence of their Paradigm, but of a legal Effect.

                            And just to be sure, the idea of a Paradigm using indirect explanations to sidestep the rules was already covered by talks on Spiritual Paradigms, 'cause they're this kind of situation and are actually quite common. A shaman may explain every effect as talking to the spirits to get results, but isn't excused to make everything just with Spirit. If the shaman asks the spirits to create a plastic bag out of thin air they need the same level of Matter as anyone making the same, but probably explain out their mastery of Matter as understanding the spirits of physical things. And despite spirits not being subject to Paradox, the shaman is and it is just as incomprehensible to them.
