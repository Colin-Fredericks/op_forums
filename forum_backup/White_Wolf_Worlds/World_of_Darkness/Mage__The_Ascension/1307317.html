      Bodywork is direct manipulation of the target's body through either external treatments, or through rigorous physical regimens. Manipulating chi might be part of the Focus for the character, but there is a significant difference between doing daily meditations using specific body postures to control your chi flows, and calling on your internal energy by concentrating (which would be the energy focus).

      In some Focuses, bodywork and energy are closely relate (Akashics being the most common group). In others it will be completely different. A Progenitor might use bodywork that looks more like a finally honed exercise routine, with biofeedback monitor and similar efforts, while they would be using energy to manipulate bio-electricity to help their neurons fire faster.

      With thought forms, the power of the thought form is directly related to the importance of the form. A quick thing you just concentrate on for a moment is a very weak thought form that you have spent little time structuring your mind. Spending an hour contemplating the concept of the American Dream, will do you better.

      In general though, remember that Instruments are important, but are designed to be flexible (at least until you get to personalized and unique instruments, but those should be specified enough to avoid any confusion). It's OK if bodywork and energy have a fuzzy line between them.

              I think you're getting too hung up on the "visual" of the instrument rather than the nature of why they work to help a mage channel power. Bodywork vs. meditation is bodywork manipulates the body to influence the mind, where meditation hones the mind to control the body (besides all the other things you can do with them). You might not be able to tell the difference by watching two different mages, but that's going to happen with any largely internal focused Instrument. But this is why you start with Paradigm, then a Practice, then your Instruments. Instruments are about translating belief into power with method, since you already know the belief and method, you're picking instruments that suit that. How it looks to other people isn't important.

              To repeat: it's OK for there to be fuzzy lines between instruments.

                  Yes the groups of instruments are just overarching classifications. They aren't the be all and end all. A lightning-infused Etherite might use their "personal electromagnetic biopulse" to fuel effects, and call that an Energy instrument.

                  An important feature to keep in mind is that the player should have a rough idea of which instruments link to which effects; there has to be a logical connection within the practice. Turning on an electrical device using Energy would make sense for such an Etherite, as would manipulating Resonance or burning someone by touch. However they would be far less likely to alter someone's memory using their personal static aura...

                  Then again they might still use Energy for such complex effects, but mix in Lab Gear; they will use micro-electric pulses to various parts of the cortex to wipe and alter memories, for example.

                                  Indeed. Parading, Practice and Instrument are separated only as a didactic tool to better explain and model the Focus. But in truth none of this existsin a vacuum, what would allow a Player (and ultimately the ST) to determine if it's correct to say you're applying meditation or energy would be the Practice first and the Paradigm second. You need the whole of the Focus to see how these things break down, and only then you can say if these overlap or not.

                                  With that in mind, it may be a pertinet question to ask the player what uses do each Instrument have. For instance, if an Akashick wants to have Martial Arts as Instrument, and also Energy (channeled trough Martial Arts), one could argue these overlap. The player would then need to explain what kind of magick uses Energy and what kind of magick uses MA alone, in order to justify taking them as different Instruments.

                                  This is entirely doable...Maybe the MA discipline the body to do stuff beyond it's normal capacity (loads of Life and Mind), and moving Energy arround (most of Prime) it's an extension of that Practice that's very related, but not always needed to do magick(tm). But if all that MA can do it's imagined by the player as Chi or viceversa, then there IS an overlap.

                                  If there is a low level of overlap, for instance Energy channeling being done trough MA alone, I would suggest not allowing to drop Energy before droping MA (otherwise you would need to ask the player to either do MA anyway to channel Energy or discover another way to channel it. Otherwise he can channel Energy doing nothing as well as ever...and would be droping two Instruments at the price of one). Remember that the order of the droping it's decided by the ST.

                                  And about Energy being always a part of an Instrument...It's true that Energy it's the perfect Instrument to combine with prety much any other instrument. However, note that while the player needs to explain how he channels energy, if Energy alone it's what's important then ANY way of channeling that energy would count. The ST needs some way to determine when you're "energized", but ultimately it's the "energy use" rather than the physical action what counts.

                                  Most of this it's entirely narrative/interpretative, and thus it requires to have a certain level of trust on the player. If the player it's roleplaying, he or she ain't going to use Meditation when Energy was what most strongly resonates with the Effect. If the player it's going to try and game every part of the system, then Mage it's going to break. This game it's not known for it's resistance to powergaming, and this section in particular just isn't built to restrict the player.

                                          To give a more concrete example, one of my mages uses a lot of art in his magic, but only has one instance of "Artwork" as an instrument. He uses Craftwork as his main Practice, coupled with a bit of Faith, because Creation is Innately Divine and Alive, and his art is devotional to his gods.

                                          So:

                                          Artwork - this is something of a foundational Instrument (though not his personal one) that is probably going to end up dropped close to last, but primarily used when his faith doesn't dictate a specific aspect to his effort, and he's free to personally interpret his work as an act of intimate creativity. (Linked to the Art ability)

                                          Blood - His gods ain't the nicest around... and sometimes they need sacrifices. This is essential for anything that involves spending/imbuing/etc. quintessence, for him.

                                          Elements - Extremely important for impacting patterns, he invokes the gods that govern those patterns - or the changes he's going to make to them - in his art. If a particular plant happens to be sacred to a specific goddess, it would be worked directly into the art of his magic. (Linked to Craft)

                                          Language - Prayers are, of course, an element of faith, and a sacred language to use in his art, and is a way to bridge divides between both different mundane things by call on their hidden divinity, and bridging the divide between the realms of the gods and the mundane. (Linked to Expression)

                                          Music - A flip side to Elements, if he wants to call on the ephemeral associations of the gods, he plies them with music. It doesn't require specific words unless appropriately combining Language, which is generally going to mean he's doing something big. (Linked to Art or Esoterica)

                                          Ordeals - Tthere's blood, but blood can come from other things, while personal sacrifice through endurance goes a step farther to both connect with hidden mystic knowledge and the flows of time, as well as being essential - if a bit baked in - to any larger ritual.

                                          Symbols - Of course, somewhat undeniably, all of his magic comes down to using art as a symbolic representation. But specific direct symbols have specific direct meanings that aren't as fluid and experiential as art in general. If Artwork is him at his most creative with his magic, symbols are him at his most traditions, as a symbol or array of them must be precisely crafted and arranged to achieve a single result. Using divine symbols especially means going past art-as-supplication and into grabbing the living breathing blood of the world and channeling/changing it to his needs, as his personal Instruments within the larger common ones. (Linked to Occult or Esoterica)

                                          Now, are there other options I probably could have used for some of them? Yes. Language instead of Prayer is close, but I opted for language because he creates as he uses Language, not simple recites a remembered prayer and the artistic act is empowered by his mastery over words; the gods listen because of how good he is, not because of how nicely he asks.

                                          But in general, I'm hoping this example demonstrates how Practice and Paradigm make each of these Instruments come alive, and it should be pretty easy to figure out which one he'd use for most effects. Even if, externally, it might be hard to tell the difference on which Instrument is the one at the center of what he's doing (since he tends to combine them a lot anyway).
