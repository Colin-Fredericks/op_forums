            Oh dear. That's a silly statement. Of course game rules can be hazy and absurd. Real world laws are hazy and absurd sometimes and they actually matter. The problem is that rules are written by humans, and we're all a bit flawed sometimes.

            One of the things that made World of Darkness special to me when I played originally, and now, was that unlike some other games, there was an open acknowledgement at the beginning of each book that the rules weren't going to cover every eventuality or suit every playstyle. That the creators of the games expected players to modify, replace and discard rules at will. That was an amazing idea to me, and really emphasized to me that these are games that are, first and foremost, about everyone having fun.

            They gave us the Golden Rule, which is reprinted in every core rulebook, and I suspect every supplemental book as well, which supersedes every other rule in the games (including all of the helpful charts): "The rules are what you want them to be." (Page 384 in M20.) I'd recommend checking out page 22 and 23 for help with the sandbox nature of the game.

                                    The chart does not take into account every eventuality, and occasionally contradicts other parts of the text. One example is Wonders. By way the chart reads, a Wonder should only incur Paradox on creation. However, the Wonders and Paradox section later in the book expressly contradicts this, and addresses circumstances where use of the Wonder can cause Paradox. There is nothing in the book that says, "in case of contradiction, the chart wins out." The fact of the matter is that an individual storyteller must decide which rule is appropriate for her game.

                                    (Another example of something the chart doesn't address is p. 533's Unbelief. It is clear from reading that section that Unbelief will still be in effect after initial creation. There isn't a precise rule in that section, however, so that's something a storyteller needs to decide. Basically, if the charts covered everything needed to run the Mage game, M20 would be 40 pages rather than 600+)

                                    Mage 20's section on magick (specifically spheres and Paradox) is much smaller than most of the other editions' sections. This isn't a bad thing as they still give us enough to go on. However, there will be a Guide to the Spheres posted soon. That book may resolve a lot of lingering questions/contradictions. Though, in the grand tradition of Mage the Ascension, I'm sure it will introduce even more contradictions and loopholes.

                                    Mage 20 is a wonderful, magnificent edition of Mage the Ascension, but like every edition of Mage the Ascension, it has its flaws and loopholes. And storytellers will find their own way to deal with them. If you ask the forum a hypothetical involving a potential loophole, as in this thread, you should not be surprised that experienced Storytellers already have their own ways of plugging the loophole (or, per their reading of the rules, don't see the loophole as existing at all.)
