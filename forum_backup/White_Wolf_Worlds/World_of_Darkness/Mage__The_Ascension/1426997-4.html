                First edition had 15 supplements; 3 were devoted to the Technocracy (Iteration X, Progenitors, NWO), 3 to Technomancers (Digital Web, VAs, and Sons of Ether), and 2 to mystical Traditions (Verbena and Akashic Brotherhood).

                Second Edition had 21 supplements; 3 were devoted to the Technocracy (Void Engineers, Syndicate, and Guide to the Technocracy), 2 were devoted to Technomancers (Digital Web 2.0 and Technomancers Toybox), 5 to Traditions, and 1 to Disparates (the Book of Crafts).

                Revised Edition had 30 supplements, including 5 Convention Books (4 of which were published a decade later), 2 Technomancer books (the revised VA and Etherite Tradition Books), and 8 Tradition Books (including the Guide to the Traditions). 5 books could kinda sorta be seen as Disparate books if you squint (Dead Magic I and II, Dragons of the East, Lost Paths, and Hollow Ones), but MRev was big on the notion of eliminating the Crafts as something separate, for the most part opting to fold them into the Traditions.

                All told: from 1993 to 2004, the Technocracy got 7 books (five “Convention Books”, the Guide to the Technocracy, and one Revised Convention Book), while the Traditions got 19 or 20 (the original 9 Tradition Books, the Guide to the Traditions, and 9 or 10 revised Tradition Books, depending on how you count the Hollow Ones) and the Disparates got upward of 5 or 6, despite being given the shaft throughout most of that period. If you include everything up to the year before M20 came out, that count for the Technocracy bumps up to 11; still far short of what the Traditions got.

                M20 doesn't yet have any books out for the Technocracy, though it's going to have one (Technocracy Reloaded) and one Technomancer book (TR's companion book). Granted, there aren't going to be equivalent books about the Traditions or the Disparates; but that's not out of a lack of desire to do them: everything that's currently in the pipeline for Mage has been in there for a while, and it appears that the new White Wolf hasn't been authorizing anything new. In particular, Brucato wanted to do a Disparate Alliance book, but was shot down by White Wolf. Basically, it looks right now like M20 is on its last gasp publication-wise as White Wolf is looking to clear the decks for M5.

                Heck, even the fact that TR got a Kickstarter could be viewed as Onyx Path attempting to breathe some life into the M20 publication line, as opposed to being about wanting to single out the Technocracy as more important than anyone else. From what I understand, Victorian Mage will be getting a similar treatment.

                As for the fanbase: don't confuse a vocal minority for a majority. Conversely, note that for the most part, the closest thing to a defense of the Technocracy has been “shades of gray”, not “Technocrats are the good guys”. There has been one post of the latter here, and a broad consensus that there's no merit to that claim.

                Finally: if you don't like how much oxygen this is getting, stop giving it oxygen. It's perfectly valid to simply ignore a thread that you don't like.                             Those decisions were made years ago when the environment was different. I have no doubt that the original plan was to release the key core books first (that is, HDYDT, [b]Book of Secrets [/by, and Gods and Monsters, which together consist of “most of what Brucato wanted to put into M20, but couldn't find the room”), followed by faction-oriented books written in the order of most needed to least needed (where the ranking was according to Brucato's judgment of needs), and a number of other projects that the Mage team has always wanted to write but never quite got around to interspersed. Thus, Victorian Mage and the supplement about money magic, the name of which currently escapes me.

                            In terms of the faction books, here's what I think was the rationale for each:

                            Book of the Fallen goes first, because the existing material on the Nephandi is limited to just the two editions of the Book of Madness, both of which are too “comic book evil” for Brucato's liking. That needed to be rectified ASAP, so it went first.

                            Technocracy Reloaded came next because while there was a significant body of material available for Technocrats, most of it was getting increasingly outdated (everything but the last four Convention Books) and/or didn't align all that well with M20.

                            The Disparates were going to be next, because they desperately needed more material. I think the only reason why the Technocracy went first was because the Technocrats are one of the more visible factions in the game while the Disparates are largely hidden.

                            By contrast, the Traditions went last because, well, there are already 19 books written specifically about them; and unlike the Technocracy stuff, that material isn't particularly problematic. I'm sure they would have gotten an M20 supplement eventually; but there just want as much urgency for such as the was for the other factions.

                            Heck, for all I know the M20 version of Guide to the Traditions likely would have come out after the M20 edition of the Book of Madness (which Phil has days that he wanted to do just as soon as he could figure out how to do it properly).

                            The problem is that the last three of these (Disparates, Traditions, and Marauders) weren't in the pipeline when White Wolf decided to stop authorizing new X20 material.
