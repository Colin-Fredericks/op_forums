        I agree that Extatics would loose a lot if they became a practice. More so, the Traditions would loose a lot: There needs to be a faction focused on the passion and the irrational - in a sense they're the most direct enemies the Technocratical Paradigm has within the Traditions: The ones claiming absolute freedom from the world shakes, the anty-vulcans, the ones claiming pure reason and logic it's fundamentally wrong. They're the spirit of the Traditions (hence it's very appropriate that Sh'zar was the one that founded the Traditions in the first place). They're the ones who balance the OoH more nuanced take on mysticism.

        In fact, I would propose to make the Akashics a faction of the Extatics, and use the Extatics as the core to build the new Traditions:

        Extatics would be the faction who catters to irrational and passional magick all arround the world. Be it about the extremes of human experience or the void of meditation. Rather than having a Paradigm or a etnicity (like the Akashics have) they would be a more overaching group where every deeply irrational, paradoxical, passional, Paradigm it's welcomed.

        Meanwhile the OoH I would redefine as the group that catters ritual mages all around the world. Again, it wouldn't be so much an ethnicity or Paradigm as a certain approach to magick: A more rational and nuanced approach than the Cult, but deeply mystical nevertheless. Parts of every Tradition could end here.

        And those 2 would be the heart of the Traditions, the "declaration of intent" if you want, although not the only members.

        The Chakravanti already are decoupled from Paradigm, being about Deat and the police/judges of the Traditions.

        The core of the Dreamspeakers and A LOT of others could be reworked as the Shadow Talkers: a group focused on the Umbra that is all about interacting and getting power from otherwordly entities (for instance, an ufologist could easily end here if he keeps close encounters with EDE).

        A New Order of Reason could take the torch of Reason (Etherites/VA) as Trippy proposes, but I wouldn't make the VE join them.

        I would reverse the roles Trippy proposed for the Technocracy and make the Syndicate fold into the NWO. Because while money and the system are important, the Technocracy don't work as interesting antagonists for unafiliated mages without the concept of Control. I would also keep the Void Engineers as part of the Technocracy because the protection of earth deserves a group. That would make the Techncoracy a group of 3.

        Batini, Choristers, Verbena could form a faction that centers arround the concept of Faith, which it's also an important element of Mage (belief it's power, but it can be rational or irrational).

        That with Rogue, Disparate, Marauders and Nephandi makes 13 "Clans" (although personally I don't see much need to keep with Vampire numerology)

                            I am pretty excited for the prospect of M5. I think my biggest fear is that they will dumb down the idea of consensual reality and the flexibility of the Spheres. These are always the hardest things for new players to wrap their head around, but for me they are what make Ascension my all-time favourite game. I think there is definitely potential to use a Hunger-esque system for Paradox, and I really liked the suggestions that Trippy made a few pages ago.

                            As for the fluff, I don't mind some changes. The Union has been on the brink of civil war for a while now, so it wouldn't surprise me if they didn't exist in the same configuration moving forward. Reading this thread, I see some people bristle at the notion of treating the Traditions in a more negative light. Your game is your game, but I always treated both sides as being morally complex, neither good or bad. The primary theme of Mage, from a player perspective (and of course, in my opinion), is hubris, and that should affect every faction.

                            Nor am I overly concerned about injecting politics into the game, because they have always been there to begin with. Mage has always been about the battle of ideas, and if it needs to update itself a little bit to feel relevant to today, I see no issue with that. And honestly, the furor over the politics of V5 (amusingly accused of fascism by some, and social justice politics by others) is overblown to my reading. I don't really think there is anything to be concerned with. M20 is pretty much my perfect rendition of Mage, and I don't really see the value in a new edition that doesn't do things at least a little differently.
