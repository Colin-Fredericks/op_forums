    Game Reasons for the Different Types of Umbral Travel

    04-17-2026, 10:25 AM
    I have issues with the way Otherworlds travel works in Mage, and I'm considering reworking it. Two big ones are:

    1) Mage is vague and contradictory about key issues.

    2) There are seemingly arbitrary difficulties for certain realms, and these are usually handled by heaping on Spheres rather than something more story driven.

    But before I can start addressing those issues, I really need to consider why they are there in the first place. Specifically from a gaming standpoint. To give a prime example: the Underworld. The Shadowlands are sometimes referred to as part of the Penumbra, because they behave like it, right up until a mage wants to visit, at which point they are something else. Mages suddenly need a new method to get there.

    Now we could get into the metaphysics of it, or the publishing history of it, but right now I am more interested in the gaming aspect. What happens if mages can access the Shadowlands as easily as they can access the Penumbra? Does it dramatically change Mage? Also, is the goal here to make the journey more difficult, or to restrict it to more experienced mages?

    I have similar questions about other aspects of Otherworlds mages have difficulty with, such as the deeper parts of the Underworld and the Dreaming in general, so feel free to comment about those as well.

                  One way to reconcile this is to use the Union's methodology. Each "level" and "section" of the Umbra has a unique vibrational frequency, and going from one to another requires different ways to facilitate this. For instance, the Shadowlands could have a frequency of say (-1,-1) using Cartesian coordinates, while deeper levels of the Underworld would have larger numbers (e.g. -2s for the Tempest and -3/4s for the Labyrinth). Similarly, the Penumbra of the Garou would be more (+1, -1), the Astral Penumbra (+1,+1) and the Near Dreaming (-1,+1), and so on.

                  As each level has a different rule set that governs what goes on within, then it stands to reason that each level needs slightly different ways to access. For instance, one must be 'dead' to enter any part of the Underworld. Being temporarily 'dead' counts, as well as being undead such as a vampire. The Garou workaround is to be entirely spirit, which doesn't completely violate the rules. The rules for the Penumbra, and the rest of the Middle Umbra, is that one must have a 'living spark,' as well as a spirit form. This makes it fairly easy for mages and Garou, but vamps need to 'borrow' the spark from something else to enter.

                  I've been coming up with a new way for organizing the entire Otherworlds for a while now, and the above just grazes what I've been working on. One day I might post something, but I'm afraid it will just get too big. It includes a few new ideas, plus a different view of the history of the WOD, but it's working out. So far.

                                      So I have two options that I'm looking at with the Penumbra. One is just having separate but constantly intermingling penumbras. The other is using the Periphery to provide a very thin area on the other side of the gauntlet where they all intermingle. M20 uses the Periphery as a purely perceptual thing on the physical side of the gauntlet, but other sources mix it in with the penumbra. Here are the writups I did for them as I was working through the ideas.

                                      Current way
                                      We have the physical world, then a perceptual Periphery where people start getting senses of the umbra. Then there is the gauntlet and the shroud. Crossing the gauntlet leads to the Penumbra, which is a spiritual realm that looks mostly like the physical world. This is single place that can be seen through lenses corresponding to the high, mid, and low umbras.

                                      Crossing the Shroud is the Shadowlands, which is a spiritual realm that looks mostly like the physical world. This may or may not be part of the Low Umbra or it may be a second Penumbra. It looks very much like the Penumbra, but it has ghosts.

                                      From the Penumbra, travellers of the right state can push into the High and Middle Umbras properly. From the Shadowlands, travellers who are dead or dead adjacent can push into (or further into) the Underworld.

                                      Despite having an area that looks mostly like the physical world, the Dreaming doesn't have a Penumbral Videre or a Penumbra. Anything acting like the Penumbra is fully in the Dreaming.


                                      Prospect #1
                                      We have the physical world, which is separated from the rest by a Gauntlet. Sometimes sensations do seep through. Then there is the Gauntlet/Shroud, which separates the Physical from the Penumbral. There are multiple Penumbras, at least one for each of the four Otherworlds, and it is rumored there are others.

                                      Travel across the Gauntlet will usually lead to the Penumbra that most resonates with that location in the Physical world and with the crosser's situation. In a Penumbra, travellers can, with effort, see into other Penumbra that have enough connection with the current location. Travel between different Penumbra is possible, though often difficult, unpredictable, or unreliable. It is much easier if the traveller is intending to go to the Penumbra that best matches his state (i.e. Astral Travellers have the easiest time going to the Astral Penumbra).

                                      The Shadowlands are the Penumbra associated with the Underworld. Travellers don't have to be dead or dead adjacent to get there, but it is much easier if they are. The Dreaming also has a Penumbra, though the easiest ways of getting to the Dreaming tend to bypass it.

                                      Travel into the Otherworlds proper requires being in the Penumbra of the right type and, often, being in the right state. Astral travellers can't push into the Spirit Wilds, and spirit travellers can only get into the Vulgate of the Astral.


                                      Prospect #2
                                      We have the physical world, which is separated from the rest by a Gauntlet. Sometimes sensations do seep through. Then there is the Gauntlet/Shroud, which separates the Physical from the Penumbral. The shallowest layer of the Penumbral is called the Periphery and it is the closest reflection of the physical.

                                      Travel across the Gauntlet leads to the Periphery. One of the Penumbra will usually influence the look of the Periphery, as will a travellers Videre. From the Periphery travellers can, with effort, see partway into any Penumbras that have enough connection with the current location, though some Penumbra are naturally harder than others. It is difficult to stay within the Periphery unless actively trying, most travel will naturally take the traveller to the most appropriate Penumbra for that location and the travellers state. There are multiple Penumbras, at least one for each of the four Otherworlds, and it is rumored there are others.

                                      The Shadowlands are the Penumbra associated with the Underworld. Travellers don't have to be dead or dead adjacent to get there, but it is much easier if they are. The Dreaming also has a Penumbra, though the easiest ways of getting to the Dreaming tend to bypass it.

                                      Travel between different Penumbra is possible, though unreliable. It is much easier if the traveller is intending to go to the Penumbra that best matches his state (i.e. Astral Travellers have the easiest time going to the Astral Penumbra).

                                      Travel into the Otherworlds proper requires being in the Penumbra of the right type and, often, being in the right state. Astral travellers can't push into the Spirit Wilds, and spirit travellers can only get into the Vulgate of the Astral.

                                      The advantage of using the Periphery is it keeps what is just on the other side of the Gauntlet consistent with what we already see. It is, however, more complicated. Thoughts?

                                          I think you have first to ponder how this affects Paradigm. Among the reasons Mage does this the wonky way it does, is the fact it must let an individual Mage think whatever about how other worlds work and be validated by personal Magic.

                                          Also, I have to correct you on one thing: the Dreaming does NOT have a Penumbra. There's nothing like it in Changeling.

                                          Chimerical reality is a superposition over the Material. Chimeras and dream stuff are all around, all the time, at least to some extent in areas that don't have too high Banality. Those things are invisible and intangible to Material beings, but the reverse isn't true. They're fully in the Material.

                                          So when Changelings interact with each other in Fae form and with weird vehicles, wondrous clothes and whatnot while in an environment like the Material... It is, indeed, the Material, and people passing by will see them, just not as Fae (usually). That's why a Changeling that's fighting a Dragon in a parking lot will appear to a passerby to be playing or hallucinating. The Changeling, their equipment and the Dragon are all in the Material.

                                          The first level of the Dreaming, the Near Dreaming, already departs from our reality. It can have some geographical correspondence, but it's not much, it looks more like a Middle Umbra Realm that casually maps over Material reality for inter travel purposes.

                                          My suggestion for the rest is to take a cue from CoD: there's only one Penumbra, but three "Frequencies". While they interact with the same environment, inhabitants only interact with others in their own frequency. An object or Mage traveler can exist as environment or may narrow to one Frequency.

                                          Don't think you should limit to which Umbra the traveler goes by default. Instead, passages to them are naturally in their native Frequency only, but supernatural means of travel or detection may be able to bypass this.

                                          How to integrate the Underworld: put ghosts everywhere. Mage avoided to have much to interact with in the Penumbra because reasons but you can just put them there. The Shroud is the Gauntlet and that's that, their Frequency is the most populated by far as a matter of fact. I don't think traveling further into it should be in fact harder than traveling further anywhere.

                                          Our last challenge is the Dreaming. It's usually declared a Zone that touch the Umbra all over the place without belonging anywhere. This has a good reason: everyone dreams. But at the same time, the relationship between the Dreaming of Changeling and the dreamscapes of the sleepers has always been muddy...

                                          What if Dream and Dreaming are not the same place? The Dream Realm is made up of infinite Dreamscapes by the denizens of everywhere, with some pseudo-reality between them made of traveler expectations, artificial inter zones and Dreamscape spillover, all accumulated over time. This is the actual Zone that permeates all other realities.

                                          From the Dream Realm one can enter the realm of the Dreaming similarly to how one can enter an Umbra from the Penumbra. This is not just a deeper portion of Dream, though. It's the realm of Glamour, a substance related to creativity and some mental states, and inhabited by the Fae. It's relationship to the Dream is intuitive to some, but is actually incidental, I'd say almost arbitrary to those who understand real dreams.

                                          Your biggest challenge, though, won't be to get any of those to make sense. How to reconcile the Traditional Umbra structure with Everett Volumes?

                                              Changeling is my weak point in all this.                                               Actually, the full idea, which I don’t think I’ve put in this thread was: Accessing a Penumbral Vidare depends on two major elements. The first is location. Every place in the physical world resonates with one (actually more than one) of the penumbral frequencies. Most commonly this is the Spiritus, followed by Astral, then the Mortem, then possibly the Oneiric (maybe going to those correspondences in the Near). Crossing the gauntlet at these points leads to that Penumbra.

The Penumbral frequencies sometimes overlap so some locations in the Penumbra lend themselves to transitioning to a different frequency while others do not, so transitioning between levels often requires journeying to find one of these interference points.

                                              I’m also toying around with the idea that some frequencies have such a dominant connection to the physical that it is much harder to cross back over to the physical from a different frequency. The only problem is this would be best represented by having different frequencies have different gauntlet strengths and that’s more numbers to keep track of.

                                              The other element is metaphysical state. The above assumes the caster is a standard human/mage, but some creatures and states are far more drawn to one of the frequencies. A ghost, if they could cross the gauntlet, would find that most physical places resonate with the Mortem with only a few resonating with any other frequency, for example. Mages can take advantage of this by changing their metaphysical state. Projecting their minds makes accessing the Astral easier, projecting as ghosts makes the Mortem easier, etc.

                                              Natural inclination may still play a small role, but it is tiny compared to the former two.

                                              I’d also point out that while this does assume a Dreaming Penumbra, I considered it to be by far the most difficult to access and would rarely be a reliable way of getting into the Dreaming.

                                                      I ran into this as part of a crossover game, and settled on something very similar to 'frequencies' with Penumbrae each for high, middle, and low Umbra. Again, this is for a crossover, but the easiest way to make sense of it for me was to
                                                      1) tie Gauntlet strength to the type of Penumbra it borders
                                                      2) let the Essence of the one who steps sideways determine the 'default' Penumbra

                                                      It clicked when I tried to reconcile how Gauntlet strength is different for a Werespider and a Garou, when they nominally enter through the same Gauntlet. The reason is of course because one has Weaver (Pattern) and the other has Wyld (Dynamism) Essence, but that difference makes only sense when they enter the Penumbra through different Gauntlets.

                                                      So why not have a Gauntlet each for three distinct Penumbrae? I didn't see any metaphysical reason why stepping into the high umbra should not be easier in places of mind (Pattern) such at studies, libraries, theaters and so on. And honestly, it feels more consistent when each umbral realm has its own barrier that resonates with one of the Essences. The standard Gauntlet ratings make sense for the Spirit Wilds, but why would the Vulgate be easier to reach from a primal place?

                                                      Dreams are a special case with special rules though. I'm still figuring out how to fit them into this larger picture, but the Essence idea still works to some degree.

                                                          One of the things this does it turn the Penumbra into a liminal space, or I suppose I should say, it highlights that it already was one. That actually helps a lot because it gives room for the various Umbra to be their own things.

                                                          Despite my attempts to fit the Dreaming into being a type of Umbra I actually don't like thinking of each of the realms as a different flavors of the same thing. The Penumbra, as an entryway and part of all three made them seem that way. On the other hand, if the Penumbra is a transitory space that exists between all (or at least many) of the various realms, that helps keep them as distinct worlds.

                                                          It also opens the possibility of there being other more esoteric realms that can be accessed that are not part of the big 3 (or 4).
