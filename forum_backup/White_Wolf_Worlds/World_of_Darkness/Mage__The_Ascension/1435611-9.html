            The Victorian Age is a grim time, and the Chorus Celestial and Order of Hermes (at the very least) are given just as much grief for thier complicity in European Imperialism.

            The Order/Union of this era as a whole represents probably the absolute nadir of their morality. No matter how you slice it, the dominant powers of the age were definitely in bed with industry and finance, and were busy committing probably the highest density of wanton atrocity against civilians ever seen in human history.

            Reading the writeups for the Blocs and Conventions, the Celestial Masters, Electrodyne Engineers, Analytical Reckoners, International Brotherhood of Machinists, and Hippocratic Circle are all portrayed as having several great virtues and ways in which they could actually start *fighting* oppression, besides contributing largely to benign pursuits like infrastructure and medicine, instead of the bloody-handed Void Seekers or the rapacious Invisible Exchequers. Not to mention the Blue Cog Cognate and the contacts of the Children of Kabezya-Mpungu, which paint a picture of the Order containing quite a bit more ethical complexity than one might initially assume.

            Individuals and even whole groups can do real good, and the book acknowledged this, but the Order as a larger entity has grown cruel and monstrous. You can give in and become a monster, you can fight from within, or you can break away, but you simply cannot remain ignorant forever. This isn't a moratorium on PC's from the Conventions, it's an ultimatum: if you want to rescue the dreams of True Science from the jaws of Empire, you're probably gonna have to kill the management. And that's something Union fans have been well aware of since 2e.
