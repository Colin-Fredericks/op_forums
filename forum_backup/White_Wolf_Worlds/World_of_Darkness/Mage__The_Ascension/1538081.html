      Fight smarter, not harder.

      This is something most Mage players understand, but it's something that shows up in fiction as well. Often the best way to defeat an opponent isn't to overpower them with overwhelming magical force, but instead to find the vulnerability in their defences or understanding, and exploit that. A good way to do this is to try and found out the Spheres that an opponent isn't able to use, and try to use those against them.

      That being said, it can also be fun if you can manage to pull together the right amount of brute force to just overwhelm an opponent who wasn't prepared for that kind of confrontation, either. But a smart Mage probably shouldn't just be throwing potent spells at a target and hoping for the best; if something isn't expected to solve the problem, it should be helping to set up the final victory somehow.

      From the perspective of a Storyteller, it's probably a good idea to give yourself a certain amount of leeway to have a villain say "I thought of that!" even when you, the Storyteller, didn't. You probably want to have an idea of what the chinks in their armour should be, and be willing to reward an especially creative idea, but a Big Bad level villain probably shouldn't be taken out by a relatively mundane transmutation that you, a regular human being with a busy life to worry about, just didn't consider in advance.

              On another point, Mages, like most people, don't want to die. Unless they're a fanatic, really desperate, or just so arrogant they can't imagine losing, most Mages should have a plan to "hit the bricks" if the fight turns against them. Since they're Awakened, they have the benefit of altering Reality itself to effect their escape. Whether it's a Rote they try to cast, or some kind of Wonder that can do it for them (Artifact, Talisman, one-use Charm, etc.). In addition to mundane ways, like mapping exits ahead of time, having friends with a waiting van/helicopter (very likely with Technocrats), or a flashbang or smoke grenade they can drop.

              What magical escape tricks a Mage can employ comes down to what Spheres they possess, though with Wonders this requirement can be dodged if they acquired it from someone else.

              Correspondence: Corr 3 (Teleport) and Corr 4 (open gate) are standard and obvious tricks for getting out of Dodge. A master could contract, stretch, or fold space to make them cover more ground or make it harder to be followed. But even just Corr 2 could be employed to fetch pre-prepared mundane tools, like the aforementioned flashbang or smoke grenades. Conversely, Corr 4 could be used to bring allies TO the NPC, who could then distract the PCs while their foe retreats. Corr 2 to create a ward that makes crossing harder, or Corr 4 to outright Ban pursuit.

              Entropy: Ent 2/3 could manipulate fate to make a mundane chase sequence "coincidentally" turn in their favor, with lots of little happenings that help them and foil their foes. If the NPC retreats to a car, they could use Ent 3 to make the PCs' choice of vehicle not work. Entropy 4 can lay a nasty curse on a PC, forcing the Cabal to either stop to unweave the enchantment or negotiate the NPC's escape in return for removing it. And Ent 5 can just make the Cabal forget they were even fighting, as the NPC has decayed their memory of the situation.

              Forces: Flight, conjuring darkness/light, invisibility, create barriers by exploding buildings, literal firewalls, whirlwinds, fog, levitating the opposition, turning into wind or light or electricity, increased velocity, gravity manipulation, radioactive barrier, etc. There should be no shortage of fun and flavorful ways a Forces user could escape a fight. It's more a function of what the Mage is about and how they operate, since the one Sphere carries so many different potential tools.

              Life: Life 2/3 to heal or gain damage resistance, and just tank hits while they run away. Life 3 to boost Dex or Stamina, or gain animal traits to traverse the environment (climbing, water breathing, squeezing into tight spaces, etc). Life 4 to turn into a fast, swimming, or flight capable animal; or give opponents traits that make them slower, like clubbed feet or stiff joints. Life 5 can just turn foes into harmless animals, so the NPC can walk away (or kill them at their leisure). Life 3 + Prime 2 to create plantlife or bug swarms to block pursuers. Life 4 + Prime 2 to create temporary attack beasts, to distract the Cabal.

              Matter: Matt 2 to turn barriers or ground into soft clay. Matt 3 to create barriers, or create holes in the environment (then repair them to prevent following). Matt 2 to turn the gunpowder in the Cabal's weapons into inert sand, so they can't shoot when the NPC runs away. Matt 3 to make their clothes more durable and just tank hits. Matt 4 to turn any random large object into an escape vehicle. Matt 3 to make the path super smooth and slippery. Matt 2 to turn the air behind them into poison gas. Etc.

              Mind: Mind 2 to read the Cabal's thoughts, to make dodging or pursuit easier to avoid. Mind 2 + Corr 2 to contact nearby help (high levels to force people to come help). Mind 2 to make PCs afraid enough to freeze/flee or angry enough to be tricked into a trap. Mind 3/4 to manipulate PCs into not fighting. Mind 3 to create illusory barriers, threats, or false NPCs. Various levels of Mind to put the Cabal to sleep, or make them see their greatest fear. Mind 4 to make the PCs forget.

              Prime: Prime 2 to just bribe the PCs with Quintessence. Prime 3 to blast barriers with pure magical force, or Prime 5 to unmake them. Prime 5 to negate Paradox that might bring them an early end. Various levels of Prime to create anti-magic.

              Spirit: Spirit 3 to Step Sideways is always a classic. Spirit 2 to convince local Umbrood to help out, and reduce the Gauntlet rating so they can use powers easier. Spirit 4 to summon Umbrood allies to distract the PCs. Spirit 2/3/4 to harm the spirit allies of PCs. And of course many spirit allies, previously hired, could help effect the Mage's escape in some way.

              Time: Time 3 to speed up escape or slow down pursuers. Time 4 to step outside of time, allowing for effortless withdrawal. Time 3/4 to age pursuers, making them slower. Some level of Time to reset an unfavorable turn or start running earlier. Time 2 to predict when a fight turns against them and, again, bail early. Or, if you the Storyteller want to be REALLY cheeky, retcon the entire fight by saying it was the Mage using Time 2 to see the future. And then the NPC either goes into the fight with better knowledge of all the Cabal's tricks, or doesn't show up at all.

                  For the sake of not making the encounter unfair, you might limit these kinds of retroactive countermeasures to only a certain number for that fight. (They have only so many agents waiting in the wings, they have only so much Tass to brew up specific Charms, they can only carry so many objects on their person, they can only have so many Effects running, etc.).

                  Effectively the Mage equivalent of a boss monster's Legendary Resistances per day from D&D. The NPC gets 3 "Outs" per fight, to keep the encounter from ending prematurely. After that, though, the character has to rely on their wits, spells, and resources on hand.
                  I have a house rule about "sensory rolls". I use it to any sort of effects for "information". That, of course, includes all uses of level 1 Spheres, but also includes things like Time 2 and sometimes Corr 2, but I also use it for Auspex Vampiric Discipline and similar powers.

                  The rule is very simple, each successes the player has in the sensorial effect, allows the player to ask one question about the target, or to retain a "lock on effect" to detect any posterior changes.

                  So, for example, a Life 1 effect could be used to ask "What is it?". "Human." "How strong?". "VERY strong". "Any weaknesses?" "Yes, a fragile ankle". "And with my 4th and final success, I want to keep it locked for any alterations".

                  That rule also plays well enough for Time 2, since Time 2 isn't very exact in it's visions, and it is also oftentimes confuse and cryptic in the visions. Therefore, instead of trapping yourself into a corner, instead you give off some "potential tips" for your players to work with.

                  The same works for Entropy 1 too, and likewise do wonders. Instead of giving off everything in a silver plate for the players, give them a list of potential options to work with. It also engages the players to be creative in requesting the proper questions; sure, using Entropy 1 and simply asking "any weaknesses?" will be the most common, but situational awareness will become crucial, for example, using Entropy 1 and asking "what are the best exit routes from this room?" "There's the window behind you or the door to the floor above, but not the door to the floor bellow". Or Time 2 "what might happen if I step outside the window?" "Agents will try to shoot you from the windows above while you run out through the emergency stairs out the windows". "What will happen if I try to escape by the door to the floor bellow?" "A full squad is coming from the elevator and will storm through that door any moment now".

                  That way, the player can get valuable tips, but they have to have the necessary luck in the dice and the smart to now what to ask, based on the descriptions of the scene. In the above case for example, let's say that the player only asked about the door to the floor above, where there are just 2 agents waiting. Then, because they think there's 2 agents above, they decide to go down, only to be ambushed by a full squad.

                  Also, notice how Time 2 and Entropy 1 would work vastly differently here. In the above scene, Entropy 1 would give the best options, but the players wouldn't be any wiser about what awaits them behind each door; they know that going above is the best option, but they don't know that there would be 2 agents there, despite it being the best option (going down would be hell). But, with Time 2, the player would know there's 2 agents above, but NOT that this is better than the full Squad coming up from the elevator and stairs.

                  Likewise, I allow the same rule to be used by my NPCs. In the case of Time 2, each success gives my NPC one relevant information about the players actions. Entropy 1 is one relevant information about possible outcomes, and so on, so forth.

                  For example, a villain NPC using Time 2 against a Player also using Time 2, would see that the players decided to go up, "spending" one of the questions to ask "are the players moving up or down", and then he decides to order his squad in the elevator to go to the up floor, instead of the floor where the players are. If the Player with Time 2 still has successes to "spend" on further questions, he could ask "where's the elevator going to?", since he previously saw the squad going up on it, and now his vision would have changed, with them moving towards 2 floors above, and then the party decides to go over by the window; then the NPC with Time 2, if he still has successes left, could ask "after moving to the floor above, where will they go?", and he sees that the players are going out the window, and he now makes a forced stop at the elevator, and his soldiers get out immediately to try to reach them at the windows.

                  Therefore, you can resolve such conflicts as either "whoever has more successes will give the last laugh", but it can also lend itself to a position upon which no further future scrying shenanigans will improve things (after the villain jumps out of the elevator and the players go out the window, further future knowledge won't improve the odds for neither side).

                  I hope this helps

                      Yes. Mages are the wild cards of 1.000 tricks from WoD. That's why they are deemed as the "most powerful spat", regardless of either or not that's true. Mages are insanely capable and unpredictable, even if you do know what "Spheres" they possess.

                      Clever Mages will try to stay ahead of their enemies, that means working with extensive Intel and planning.

                      In a "real life simulation" environment, you won't ever find 2 Mages going 1 vs 1 in a ring (nor a Mage and <insert your favorite supernatural monster here>).

                      Granted, the Mage's enemies will likewise be doing the same, so this quickly turns into a game of cat and mouse between the two. A Vampire hunter Mage won't come up into the Elysium and challenge the vampiric Prince to a duel.

                      Now, Vampires, Werewolves, Changelings and Boogiemen also have their bags of tricks and modus operandi, but they don't possess the nearly limitless capabilities of Mages.

                      So, before any real confrontation, the wannabe combatants will engage in extensive espionage and counter espionage, and lay plans and counter plans, and sometimes abandoning entire missions when those espionage efforts are discovered.

                      The first line of combat thus begins with the defense mechanisms a Mage lays out in their lair (Chantry or Amalgam). A Mage's home/laboratory/sanctum will have plenty of defense systems, both of the mundane and magical keep kinds.

                      This means that, when "playing defense" you'll have the "home turf" advantage; when "playing offensive" thou, it will depend on how extensively you've gathered Intel upon your target, what kinds of counter plans you've laid out and how well you can execute those.

                      I'd say that, most of the times, either the so called "assault team" just gets fried out instantly as soon as they step in a trap they didn't previously know about, or they execute a mission so well that their target won't even realize that he is dead.

                      Therefore, there's plenty to be said about kinds of territorial and personal defense mechanisms effects and mundane measures, as well as plenty of effects to facilitate gathering Intel and infiltrate places. I'll see if I can throw a few bits about those too. Counterintelligence too is fundamental (Entropy 1 and Time 2 for example may be a Mage's best tools to know when someone is at their trail, and how to not only make them lose his track, but also reverse the tables upon his adversaries)

                      Correspondence 1: have you all seen Batman: The Dark Knight? Well, that "Sonar Vision Device" that Batman uses is basically it. Yes, you can see through walls and other obstacles with it, just like Superman does (don't use this to look at women without their clothes kids!). That alone by itself already is super powerful and super useful. With this alone you can look at every single hall, doors, corridors, windows, secret passages, architectural designs, plumbing and electric system of ANY installation.
                      This seems great, but it's not that much awesome when I put some of the countermeasures; however, the Boogiemen of the dark don't have those same capabilities, therefore this is the perfect tool for infiltrating a vampiric nest for example.
                      With Correspondence 1 you can also pinpoint locations. Many people don't understand just exactly how OP this is. I use this in conjunction with Prime 1 to create "tracking devices". With Prime 1 I change the resonance of any object (even rocks can work, although, as always, remember the rules for Foci, Paradigm and Resonance) to that of the Mage, or I just use it to "memorize" the resonance of the object. This works best if it's something that can be divided in 2 parts (a crystal broken in half for instance). Or, if you have an object imbued with the resonance of the target (having a sample of blood from the target will turn this effect into almost trivial).
                      Afterwards, you can always target you target using Correspondence 1.
                      Therefore, a simple Correspondence 1 Prime 1 can create a powerful tracking device that is completely undetectable because it's not (necessarily, again, Paradigm) an electronic device.
                      You can also use this to pinpoint the location of specific patterns; for instance, are you trying to prospect a land in the hopes of finding iron for mining? Very well, Correspondence 1, Matter 1 will tell you exactly what is the plot of land that you want to buy for maximum profitability.

                      Correspondence 2 is like the device used by Batman to track the Joker, using all of Gotham's phones to generate images of the entire city, allowing him to spy on millions of people simultaneously. INSANELY POWERFUL STUFF.
                      Just add Entropy 1 so that you can use this for scrying for "targets of interest". That's what Batman did: his device was spying on millions of people at once (Correspondence 2), but since he had a sample from Joker's voice, he could use it to pinpoint his exact location, dismissing all of the "background noise" (Entropy 1).
                      You can also combine this with other sensory spheres, just like with Correspondence 1, with the difference that this allows you to do it at a distance. Do you want to map all the areas with highest crime in your city? Correspondence 2, Entropy 1. Do you want to scry groups of refugees crossing the border? Correspondence 2, Life 1. And so on.

                      Infiltration: Obviously, Correspondence 3 allows you to enter your enemy's Sanctum directly, in any room of your chosing. Corr 4 allows you to bring your friends.

                      Countermeasures: Correspondence 2 can block scrying from would be invaders. This... Pretty much invalides EVERYTHING ABOVE in Intel Gathering.
                      Well... Almost everything. Because, you see, if you're looking at something with Correspondence 1 or 2 and all of a sudden you "see" a "black spot", well... You might not know what lies inside that place, but at least you'll know that THERE'S SOMETHING JUICE INSIDE (aka: MAGES).
                      A more refined countermeasure would involve using Correspondence 4 in order to distort the spatial perceptions of the place. With Corr 4 you can create a "distorted space" upon which would be scryers would see something else entirely different from what actually is inside a place.
                      Correspondence 4 can also ban unauthorized access from would be teleporting invaders.
                      This means that Correspondence 4 is the bare minimum to any Chantry/Lab/Sanctum worth its salt.
                      So, just to clarify: anyone using Corr 2 to try to see inside a "Magik fortress" protected with Corr 4, will see walls and halls and doors alright, but if they rely on that information alone, they'll have a very unpleasant surprise upon entering the facility...

It will work somewhat similar to the Arcanum background, upon which any spy will simply "forget" to look into that particular place, or won't pay attention to it. For instance, a Mage scrying an entire city looking for the technocratic lab will simply "forget" to scry the old glass factory, because it simply "never crossed his mind to do so").

                      During a Fight: With Mind 3 you could use a sort of "Mental Blast" to utterly confuse targets beyond their ability to operate, by throwing Spacial Senses in distorted ways. Combining Mind 3 with Corr 2, you could make the target see and hear many things simultaneously, including upsidedown and backwards, rendering him nothing short to absolutely stunned.

Go SEVERELY Looney Tunes in here, with your portable holes that send enemies to their certain death 1.000 kilometers (or miles) into the atmosphere (unless they can fly using Forces 2, sprout wings using Life 3, use Corr 3 to teleport back, use Entropy 2 to land on a lake, Spirit 2 to beg a bird spirit for salvation, Matter 2 to turn the soil into gelatin or some other hundreds of absurd options that Mages are capable of).
                      My favorite is to put the entrance to the portal/gate bellow the target, and the exit of it immediately above. Enjoy the show of watching your target falling at maximum speed, constantly being accelerated by gravity, without actually moving from his place. When you finally get bored, close the gate and watch his body exploding like a comet on terminal speed, or let him burn the air around
                      Corr 4 can also punch from a distance, including the "punch of one thousand strikes", upon which the Mage can punch a target several times from different angles and in different spots with every single blow he deals - AT A DISTANCE.
                      But my favorite fighting technic used Corr 5 to become invulnerable to... Well, to everything actually (except for Magikal attacks). Using Corr 5, bullets, swords and punches will just pass by your body as if you were incorporeal... Your own punches however, will still hurt just the same.
                      But even better than that... Make your target turn "incorporeal" with Correspondence 5 and... Disappear in the ground, being dragged to the center of the Earth. Let the high temperature deal with him, in a gruesome death.

However, locations that were properly fortified with Corr 4 may not allow such luxury. Some defenses may instead have an anti magic "field" created with Prime, but that would hinder much more than just your ability to teleport.
                      In many instances, Corr vs Corr may end up in a fight of successes between two Mages (or between a Mage and static defenses)

                              Intel Gathering (Entropy - Continuation)
                              With Entropy 1 you can also identify "juicy targets" for information acquisition. Think of it as a sort of "Supernatural Intuition". For instance, Entropy 1 may tell you that the librarian woman may hold a key to the Intel about the Amalgam you're looking for.                               Countermeasures
                              Entropy 1 is as good to gather information as it is to protect it. Find the weak links in your organization; Entropy 1 will tell you who are the "Exceptional Citizens" who are more prone to being sloppy or traitorous (Entropy 1 is every HRs wet dream!)
                              It will also tell you when there's something odd going on, something that doesn't add up. Did the old librarian request a copy of the old testament in ancient Aramaic? Throw Entropy 1 to it, and you'll sense that there's some odd sequence of events unfolding here, which only Entropy 1 can pierce together.
                              Entropy 3 will obviously keep all the gadgets in your sanctum working at 110% efficiency.
                              Entropy 1 can also be thrown into an "alarm system" against intruders (need a Trigger Effect)
                              And finally, Entropy 5 will be your best friend in concealing your home, operating like an "Arcanum background". That's probably the best defense that money can buy to a Mage so blessed.

                              During a fight
                              Entropy 1 can help spot weaknesses that can be exploited, as well as hidden threats (like an enemy hidden)
                              Entropy 2 will basically function as "Magic helping skills", adding dice to your rolls. You can also use it to subtract dices from your enemies' actions
                              Entropy 3 is like Bluecho described. Guns will malfunction, locks will jam, cars will not start. Here a bit of creativity may do wonders - you can throw in an Entropy 1 effect during a car chase for instance, before using Entropy 3, so that you'll know the best moment to use Entropy 3 to make a tire blow. Combine this Entropy 1 with Time 1 and you'll know with precision when to do that, so that when you do blow the enemy's car tires with Entropy 3 they lose control of the vehicle at the precise moment a high speed truck blows them into pieces.
                              Of course, your own Entropy 3 is also useful to prevent your enemies from blowing your own vehicle tires, in an opposing anti-magic effect.
                              Entropy 4 is direct damage, the old school "Heart Attack!"

                              Escaping
                              Entropy 1 will tell you the best escape routes. Just trust your supernatural guts.
                              Entropy 2 will make your enemies trip all over like a bunch of Looney Tunes jesters, giving you time to escape.
                              Entropy 2 can also give you that tiny little edge at crucial moments - the proverbial "leap of faith" here becomes much more literal when you're trying to frenetically jump over rooftops (think of Neo fleeing for his life from Agent Smith in Matrix)
                              Like I said above, Entropy 3 is awesome when both chasing enemies or when trying to escape during a frenetic car chase
                              And Entropy 5 can make pursuers loose your track. Let's say for instance that you get some distance from your pursuers and you turn left at the corner. Throw Entropy 5, and they'll turn right. You're now safe.


                              Forces: Flight, conjuring darkness/light, invisibility, create barriers by exploding buildings, literal firewalls, whirlwinds, fog, levitating the opposition, turning into wind or light or electricity, increased velocity, gravity manipulation, radioactive barrier, etc. There should be no shortage of fun and flavorful ways a Forces user could escape a fight. It's more a function of what the Mage is about and how they operate, since the one Sphere carries so many different potential tools.
                              Yes, that's pretty much it for Forces. What little I could add is using Forces to create visual or auditive illusions (sort like holograms and audio records) to protect your base and confuse attackers; Forces can also be used for Intel Gathering (the proverbial "X-Ray vision", or "heat vision" or "ultraviolet vision" and the "night googles"). People disregard how much useful Forces can be to manipulate the senses. Not only can you turn invisible, but you can also become completely silent. You can also blind enemies creating a "fog of shadow" in front of their eyes; you can also turn them deaf or mute. Those uses of Forces can very well be much more powerful than fireballs or fancy lightning bolts. Besides, those only require Forces 2, and your increased senses only Forces 1. Being able to fight in the dark against a blind and deaf enemy is basically an easy win.
                              Forces 1 can also allow you to feel vibration or movement. That makes for impressive detection systems against intruders. Think the Marvel superhero Daredevil here; you'll be able to perceive EVERYTHING around you (including above and bellow) with that, even if you both your eyes and ears have been destroyed (also, with Forces 1 you can still hear or see, even if you were born deaf or blind).
                              You can also fancy yourself a nice radar/sonar "vision" with just Forces 1.

                              Forces 1 is INSANELY POWERFUL.



                              Life: Life 2/3 to heal or gain damage resistance, and just tank hits while they run away. Life 3 to boost Dex or Stamina, or gain animal traits to traverse the environment (climbing, water breathing, squeezing into tight spaces, etc). Life 4 to turn into a fast, swimming, or flight capable animal; or give opponents traits that make them slower, like clubbed feet or stiff joints. Life 5 can just turn foes into harmless animals, so the NPC can walk away (or kill them at their leisure). Life 3 + Prime 2 to create plantlife or bug swarms to block pursuers. Life 4 + Prime 2 to create temporary attack beasts, to distract the Cabal.
                              Yeah. Life is very powerful, but in my opinion not as OP as the 3 previous Spheres, at least not for such direct confrontations. What I can add is that Life 1 is a powerful sensorial effect; since most of the threats that you'll have to worry about will be living things (like guards and other Mages), it is insanely powerful to always know the movement patterns that those living patterns have inside of enemy territory.
                              Life can Str, Dex and Sta, but it can also boost Appearance, which is useful for seduction attempts, and making Intel Gathering so much easier and sweet.
                              Your senses can also be improved - eyes like an eagle, ears like a fox, smell like a hound. Never ignore just how powerful having senses beyond human limitations really is.
                              Just one correction, controlling plant life or bugs is Life 2, not 3. And if you're in a florest, you don't need to conjure plants adding Prime 2 (and 1 Quint point), you can just control the plants around.
                              You can also use Life to "juice up" some of your "living defenses".
                              Getting an oak that attacks intruders like in Harry Potter is a little silly... But would work.
                              Better perhaps to just breed bioengineered "war dogs".
                              Rats and birds can make up for awesome spies. So can ants and bees; you can even modify them to be intelligent.
                              You can also use "chemical communication" like ants do, sharing secret comms with a partner with at least Life 1
                              Having a sense of Discriminatory Smell like a hound is absolutely OP. With that you can find even invisible foes or disguised foes.
                              With Life 3 you can also disguise your smell. Pretty useful indeed against enemies hunting you with dogs, or against enemies with the same capabilities (like other Mages with Life, Werebeasts or Vampires with Auspex)

                              ​​​​​


                              Matter: Matt 2 to turn barriers or ground into soft clay. Matt 3 to create barriers, or create holes in the environment (then repair them to prevent following). Matt 2 to turn the gunpowder in the Cabal's weapons into inert sand, so they can't shoot when the NPC runs away. Matt 3 to make their clothes more durable and just tank hits. Matt 4 to turn any random large object into an escape vehicle. Matt 3 to make the path super smooth and slippery. Matt 2 to turn the air behind them into poison gas. Etc.
                              My players had the annoying habit of turning the floor bellow enemies into NAPALM. Annoying pricks.

                              Matter 1 can also be used as a Discriminatory Smell sense like hounds do. Just like with Life, this is awesomely useful.

                              Matter 1 can also identify individuals wearing concealed guns or concealed gadgets. This may save your life.

                              Matter 1 can also allow you to map the electrical grid and plumbing system of an installation. You can also map the structural design of a building using Matter 1 too. Therefore, that's another powerful Intel Gathering tool.

                              Matter 2 can also purify the air against poisonous gases. When the SWAT throws suffocating gas, laugh at them with Matter 2.

                              Matter 3 can also be used to make anything that you use to hurt others, hurt more. Turn your bullets into penetrating bullets, increase the edge of your sword or put spikes into your knockles.
                              Alternatively (or in addition), Matter 2 can turn your lead bullets into depleted uranium or into TNT, increasing their damage or turning ordinary ammo into incendiary ammo.

                              And with Matter 5, throw "portable black holes" into your enemies - or as the superstitious mystics call them, "spheres of annihilation"


                              Mind: Mind 2 to read the Cabal's thoughts, to make dodging or pursuit easier to avoid. Mind 2 + Corr 2 to contact nearby help (high levels to force people to come help). Mind 2 to make PCs afraid enough to freeze/flee or angry enough to be tricked into a trap. Mind 3/4 to manipulate PCs into not fighting. Mind 3 to create illusory barriers, threats, or false NPCs. Various levels of Mind to put the Cabal to sleep, or make them see their greatest fear. Mind 4 to make the PCs forget.
                              Again, we must never underestimate the power of sensory capabilities. Mind 1 allows you to detect minds close by, as well as their general dispositions. That alone is absolutely incredible when trying to infiltrate a place. It can also be used to detect intruders. It also shows you how receptive a potential target for Intel Gathering can be to persuasion, seduction or intimidation attempts (and which one would be more effective).

                              And of course, for Intel Gathering, Mind 4 to mind read a target is the best and as direct as possible.

                              Astral travel with Mind 4 is also great for espionage (but Mind 1 can detect those astral travellers, beware)
