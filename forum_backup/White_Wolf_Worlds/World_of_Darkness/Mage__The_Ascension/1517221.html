      Avatars are wholly in the storyteller's purview. They can do anything and everything as long as it's in the service of the path to Ascension. The ones that can manifest can just become physical in addition to doing things.                                                   ​​​​​Ultimately, the only thing certain about the Avatar is that it's what gives the mage his Magick. As I showed before, it doesn't even have to be part of the mage, nor does it have to have a will of its own; though admittedly, both of those options are exceptions rather than the rule where traditional mages are concerned. And with those kinds of exceptions being possible, the idea that your Avatar is a long-dead mage is very much possible.

                                                  Bear in mind that it's more likely that your mage simply inherited their Avatar from a long-dead mage; but if you want to say that the previous mage joined with their Avatar when they died, and that the Avatar now is the previous mage, then go for it!

                                                  System-wise, you might consider taking the Mentor Background to represent your Avatar — although like the Ally Background, this combined with Manifest Avatar would essentially make your Avatar a separate character. Still, that separate character need not be physical; and while it might be capable of interacting with others besides you, it doesn't have to be. Or it could simply choose not to.

                                                  Regardless, this would make the Avatar more significant than a mysterious figure that shows up primarily during Seekings, and would warrant the Manifest Avatar Merit. On the other hand, if you're fine with your Avatar mainly showing up in Seekings and only occasionally showing up otherwise, then you need neither Manifest Avatar nor Mentor.

                                                          Generally true. But Mage is full of odd corner cases, and Lord Revan's character could easily be one such case. Again, the only thing that's absolutely true about the Avatar is that it's what allows the mage to use Magick; everything else about it has exceptions. And in particular, the write-up for Manifest Avatar outright says that with that Merit, the Avatar is a person in and of itself, not merely a piece of the mage.
                                                          For most mages, the Avatar remains a mysterious figure, goading them from the sidelines and appearing primarily within a Seeking or during other moments of intense stress. For you, however, the Avatar is a vibrant presence in your life, as real to you as anyone else. Essentially a character in its own right, this manifested Avatar interacts with you on almost a daily basis. In certain situations, it might interact in other people, as manifestly real as any other person in your world.

                                                          The Manifest Avatar Merit embodies the Avatar as a full character under the Storyteller's control. On its own, this Merit reflects that Avatar as a person who only the associated mage can see, hear, and interact with on a physical level; in conjunction with the Background: Allies, however, the Avatar becomes a character that everyone can see, hear, and feel.
                                                          Book of Secrets p.71

                                                          Remember that one thing about Mage is that it strives as much as possible to avoid saying that “X is false; but your mage believes it anyway”; even in the case of Quiet (which, to be clear, is not what I'm arguing this “Avatar is a character” stuff is), the things the mage believes are true if only in the sense of being hobgoblins or a Mindscape that the mage interacts with. If there's a way for something to be true in your case, even if not in general, then Mage tends to go for that over “you believe it, but you're wrong”. And that's why the Manifest Avatar Merit is a thing.
