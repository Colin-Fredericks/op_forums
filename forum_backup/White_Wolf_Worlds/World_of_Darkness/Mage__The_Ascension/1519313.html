    Spheres as Instruments

    08-01-2023, 01:39 AM
    In other threads, there has been some definite opinions expressed against the way that some effects/rotes have spheres added to them for seemingly no reason - the most prominent example, I believe, being Spirit, which even has a sidebar A Bit of Spirit? in How Do You Do That? about adding Spirit to effects just to represent their spiritual nature. "What does each sphere do?" some forum members ask, and rightly so.

    But I was thinking, what if there was some small thing that these additions did do, that could help us differentiate the style of the magic? (Which in turn helps us tell the story of the characters.) So...

    What if certain Spheres themselves could act as an instrument, or allow access to an instrument that would otherwise be unavailable?

    Take Spirit, for example. A Bit of Spirit? (if used) says that Spirit 2 is necessary to invoke spirits in order to achieve other effects. (To invoke spirits of decay to invoke an Entropy effect.) What if we just said that "invoke a spirit" is itself an instrument?

    Not that there is much in the way of firm rules around what instruments are required to produce an effect, of course. Each mage has a base of seven instruments, and gradually sets them aside one by one as Arete climbs, but they aren't directly linked to particular effects (although I would say they should be linked to a mage's particular implementation of a rote - a mage who uses Voice and Vocalizations to cast a rote should have to fall back on an improvised effect, at best, to achieve the same thing when rendered mute). There's no minimum number of instruments to be used to produce any effect. So counting use of the Spirit sphere to "invoke the spirits" as an instrument doesn't have a direct effect. It's really just asking the ST for a little extra leniency when evaluating whether a mage's use of instruments is good enough.

    And like any other instruments, there should be circumstances in which it is unavailable. If the Gauntlet is particularly thick or under the influence of an Avatar Storm, or maybe if an effect has cut an area off from the spirit world, or the like. Or maybe for some reason the spirits occasionally just say no - if you're relying on the spirits to do the work for you, you probably need to put some effort in to stay in their good graces!

    Also, it's probably not good enough as the only instrument to invoke a Spirit 2+ effect, at least in many cases. It is not the intent to make the use of (other) instruments redundant for Spirit 2+ effects.

    Similarly, I could see Mind giving access to a "power of the mind" instrument for psychic characters - the kind who simply think about something happening, and it does.

    And possibly also Life for those characters who draw on "lifeforce" to achieve effects - especially when using sacrifice to power an effect, so that a mage might simply draw lifeforce from a chicken directly without using a knife to cut its throat. (But no using this offensively against a target who can defend themselves - if you want an actual damaging effect, you need to cast that yourself. If you would need to roll to see if you can dispatch your sacrifice with a knife, you can't substitute use of Life sphere to perform the sacrifice.)

    Thoughts? I don't know myself whether I would or will use these ideas, but there might be concepts of interest.

      At the very least, this Instrument would be awkward when it comes to working genuine Spirit Effects. “I want to Step Sideways, which is Spirit ••• [or whatever]; so I'll use my Spirit Sphere as one of the Instruments.” And Surpassing the Instrument would be especially weird under those circumstances: “I've dispensed with the need for Spirit, so I'll take a –1 to my difficulty for using it with this Spirit ••• Effect.”

      Honestly, I don't have a problem with “A Bit of Spirit” as written: if I as a player wasn't to say that my character also needs to add Spirit • or •• to all of his workings that don't already involve Spirit — oh, and by the way, my character already has Spirit •• — what's the harm? The difficulty of the casting doesn't change; there's no Quintessence cost that's charged for having a Conjunctional Effect; and since my character already has the two dots of Spirit on the character sheet, it's not like requiring him to include them in a non-Spirit Effect will limit him in any way. (And no, I don't buy that there will be situations where requiring the inclusion of Spirit •• will make the casting impossible.)

      So from a strictly practical perspective, it strikes me as a great big “meh”. I wouldn't do it, myself; but I see no harm in doing it if you want to. And I see no point in complaining about it; it simply isn't a big enough deal to worry about.

      From a conceptual point of view, on the other hand… the heart of the problem with “A Bit of Spirit” is that it conflates Sphere requirements with Focus requirements. And that muddied thinking has the potential to cause me to throw Spirit •• into a Rote write-up when my character goes to share his magical knowledge with others. Which isn't appropriate.

      My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                      I'm not sure why that's "round about."                       My point is, that what you're suggesting isn't really making the Spheres potential Instruments, but rather specific effects the Spheres cover being Instruments. And if those effects are so important to a mage, they're going to be using those effects all the time (or get access to them in other ways), not just taking on some meaningless dots to a casting to represent that effect being put into the casting as an Instrument. Having the effect up separately is also very practical because it means you're taking out steps to cast quickly should the need arise, or lets you do things like rolling an Abilities Enhancing Magick check to invoke the spirits for the benefits of that.

                      And as I said, I'm not opposed to the concept of Instruments that need magic to use, but that's not making Spheres themselves Instruments, which doesn't really solve why people don't like the idea of adding a few dots in a Sphere to lots of your effects as that sidebar proposes.

                                                          Ultimately, the problem is that it's leaning into something that goes against the core point of the design of the game (which some people like because it's also adding flavor).

                                                          Mage's magic system is supposed to be flexible enough where any effect you can think of is possible. It's also supposed to be flexible enough that wildly different styles of magic can all use it coherently. When a mage wants to summon a fireball out of nothing and blast someone with it, it's a Forces 3/Prime 2 effect. It's that for a Hermetic using an incantation of flames, an Etherite firing flame-gun, or an Akashic pulling an anime style fire attack on someone. All of those differences in approach are what M20 combined into the Focus concept. Focus is what's supposed to change between different mages that do things differently. The Spheres to pull off an effect are supposed to be the same (as clarified by M20 stating firmly that it's a PBD system).

                                                          When Focus starts changing what Spheres are needed to pull off an effect, it's moving away from the idea of the magic system working the same for everyone on a mechanical level. When you remove that barrier, you take away any reason to say some Spheres should be able to act this way, while other's can't. Sure, you don't have to let people do it, but it just comes off as being arbitrary and controlling to say that my reality hacker can't add Forces to everything to manipulate the electronic code of the universe, while Mind gets to do more to make psychic-Focus character feel more mechanically special. You've eliminated any rational argument against making it the same for all Spheres..
