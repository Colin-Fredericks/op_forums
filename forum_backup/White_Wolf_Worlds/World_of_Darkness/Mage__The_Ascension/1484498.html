    breaking down Ward effects by spheres

    06-20-2022, 07:38 AM
    I was looking at HDYDT’s warding rules the other day, and I never appreciated just how strict it is with its sphere requirements.

    The example on Dante’s computer is just awful, makes wards look like a complete impossibility in most games.

    What I usually try to do with HDYDT rotes is break them down into their components and imagine what the effect would look like without certain parts.

    This is what I’ve come up with, I’d welcome any feedback.

    Wards need Corr 2 to reduce successes and corr 4 to strengthen space sufficiently to keep things out.

    Ok, I can get behind that. I’d also add that Corr 3 would block people from teleporting in just as it can prevent people from scrying in.

    On it’s own, corr 4 would simply strengthen space for everyone and everything entering it. Great if you want an invisible door, not so great if you want to go through it without dispelling it.

    Either spheres can be added to specify either exceptions or targets, and the rank of the sphere determines the complexity of the objective.

    Matter 2 specifies types of matter patterns, base elements as broad as metal to as specific as lead. 3 refers to shapes and objects, and 4 for more complicated devices which may contain several other pattern spheres within it such as electronic devices. The increased rank only adds specificity, a ward against metal by all accounts would affect metal guns, bullets, and phones with metal components.

    Life 2 would block simple organisms, specify insects, plants, bacteria. Life 3 for complex life forms, animals, fish, cats, humans, and yourself. Life 4 for specific individuals, providing the ward has a way to identify them, either by corr connection or by use of other identifiers. E.g. “the man with the golden tooth” can be Life 4 Matter 3. Substituting Life 3 expands this to all humans, while matter 2 would make it against any “man that has gold”.

    Forces 2 is already capable of providing a barrier against other forces on a a small scale, bur unlike a ward, it works by providing the opposite energy, cold to counter heat, or positive charge to counter negative charge. Or more mystically, ground energies to counter air energies (against electricity) and water energies to counter fire energies (cold against fire). A ward instead thickens space itself to make the force’s activity weaker or incapable of travel.

    Unlike Life and Matter, corr 4 wards do not intrinsically block forces effects. If they did then all thickened space would appear dark. Forces therefore is required to block things like light, fire, airflow, or even gravity in an area.

    Forces 2 can allow your ward to have a similar protection to a force field, but Forces 3 is needed to specify an objective which cannot pass thickened space. Forces 4 and 5 instead of offering specificity, enhance that quantity of power the ward would be able to protect against. Forces 3 would do well against flaming arrows or cast fireballs, Forces 4 would protect against a raging fire, while Forces 5 could keep an area free from damage even as it was surrounded by magma

    Mind 2 to make an objective of those who have a specific resonance on them or are feeling a specific emotion, mind 3 for specific thoughts, can also be used to make a password, and Mind 4 to affect those with specific memories. Combining Mind 4 with Life 4 can thereby specify a specific person without need of corr connections.

    Corr connections can be used to specify objects as well, such as a specific gun to which you have the fired bullet. This too is an alternative to using higher ranks of spheres for similar specificity

    If corr connections are used, corr must be at the same level at the heighest sphere used in the effect as usual. Otherwise there should be no problem making a corr 2 minor ward against guns, for example. (Unless casting the ward from a distance)

    Mastery in any one sphere allows you to be infinitely specific within the purview of your ward.

    Without quintessence, the ward will be very weak. Each success will be depleted for every strike made against the ward. Quintessence instead enables the ward to stand firm against repeated attacks. Prime 2 is sufficient to fuel the ward with your personal quintessence, through Prime 3 lets you channel quintessence from others, tass, peirapts, what have you, and Prime 4, from the energies nascent in the room.
    If the periapt is on your person, it counts as a part of your own quintessence and thus prime 2 is sufficient.

    If enough pawns of tass are used as a core component of the ritual, as in the tass itself it a material component in building the ward, then the need for Prime 2 may be bypassed entirely to making a strong major or minor ward.
    Likewise creating the ward around a periapt has a similar effect, with the added flexibility of being able to add or remove quintessence to the ward, strengthening it further or temporarily weakening it.

    Portable wards need to be locked in to a pattern as usual. Matter 2 for simple objects like coins, gems, wooden runes, matter 3 for clothing, and most mechanical mechanisms, matter 4 for more complex mechanisms. Dante could have saved himself a lot of trouble by putting the ward on single plastic key of the keyboard to make it matter 2, and then putting that key into the laptop. Of course if the key is removed, it no longer protects the computer.

    Locking into the pattern indefinitely makes the item a trinket, though turning it into an artifact makes the effect of the ward permanent, and offers you the option to turn it off and onn, which is less possible with a trinket.

                                  Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                  The reason I asked about it is because that particular supplement is a compilation of rotes from every edition of Mage. It isn't really a how do you do that book; but considering that the one thing for spheres that earlier editions of the game had that M20 lacks is sample effects, EG plugs that hold nicely.
                                  I took a better look today.

                                  Pros:
                                  • Basically kept every rote as close as possible from their original incarnations, so each sample is just as good as it was before, for whatever that means;
                                  • The thematic groupings are good. Not astoundingly good, but more than enough to make searching effects be reasonably intuitive;
                                  • Original references are clearly depicted under each name, with book and page, so looking the original for comparison is as easy as it could be;
                                  • Since the descriptive text was also kept mostly intact, whenever the rote had a good explanation on what it does and how, and why it has its requirements, this explanation is kept intact.


                                  Cons:
                                  • No further exploration is given at any time, of any nature. There are explanations for the grouping, but nothing for the actual rotes beyond what they had anyway;
                                  • Lack of explanations means that rotes with obscure effects or weird Sphere requirements remains just like that, and some are really bad at this;
                                  • More concerning, quite a few had text that depended on context given by their original books, now the text is out of context. The groupings explanations sometimes help, sometimes don't;
                                  • Heft price for the final product, $15 for a compilation with minimal writing work on it. The layout is decent enough, the editing is good, and it was a deep research for a single guy, but still not enough to justify the price.


                                  Final rating: 3 out of 5. It does the job and is a must have for anyone new to the game, but it could be a lot better on this regard, specially for the price. Were it better written or significantly cheaper I would certainly recommend the purchase for everyone, as it stand I don't think it is worth it for anyone who managed some 5+ years of Mage STing without it, specially if you have the original books.

                                      1)I know it's from HDYDT, but Corr. 2 "wards" reducing successes ...

                                      I'm always interested in mechanics that do something with dice other than " +/-3 => diff ", I would like to be able to use the Sphere System to interact with every aspect of the "simulation" - only being unable when there's very good reason - so I like the principle of it but ... I have problems with seeing this as a Correspondence thing at all.

                                      It creates "uncomfortable space" where "you're not welcomed". That doesn't sound like connections/space magick to me . I would buy it making movment slower, which would perhaps make penalties for some actions, like making "difficult terrain" or something. But because of how Correspondence works, *that* actually could require 4+ (3+ for one person).

                                      What I would allow Corr 2 it's to be the basis for making a "magick trap". An area that, when entered, unleashes some spell on the infractors (which could be specified just as with other) that, unlike normal locked effects, isn't always active but only actives when the "barrier" it's breached. Kinda like a Time-locked effect, but space locked instead.

                                      But, by itself, other than warding *against Correspondence*, I don't like it as a basis for Ward/Bans. At least not by itself (Like, one could make a similar effect using Entropy to "hex" the intruders, or Mind to make a royal pain for them to be there).

                                      2) I'm not sold on Corr. 4 not blocking Forces.I think that Corr 4 Wards, what they do, it's altering space. And it would be seen like it should be seen depending on how this it's done.

                                      If it's just a literal "thikening" well, maybe it's not opaque because Ligth can traverse it anyway just because it travels very, very, fast. Even if it takes only a fraction of a second for ligth to get to you, that still could be a very far distance - one that even a sniper rifle could be unable to reach, or take enough time to dodge or "grab" the bullet (with protection). It could make a "shimmering, mirrage effect" if it slows ligth for long enough

                                      If it's "negating travel", then it would be opaque

                                      I always liked one effect of Corr. 4, the "Seal of Hermes", that it's used to take a creature "outside of space" (used against beings too tough to be fireballed). It doesn't create an opaque space, nor does the creature just stops moving - instead the creature just "vanishes". It's teleported "nowhere". The effect it's specifically creating a space outside of space that can't be seen nor interacted with. This is an example of a "spatial interaction"...So, if it's like the Seal of Hermes (to do it over an area you may need Corr 5), the person just "vanishes" (or, rather, only for the stuff the Ward protects against)

                                      3) Again, a legacy of HDYDT: I don't like the Quintessence tax. Not even if it's just "adding Prime". I don't like it "for the principle of it".

                                      I could see expending one dot (but no Prime) for a Permanent one, and of course WonderWard would cost like any Wonder, but my Wards are otherwise "free of charge". It's enough for me with many being Vulgar on "muggle space", and requiring a ritual to put into place.

                                          Agree with Aleph's points, but I would add some of mine.

                                          On Corr 4 not barring light, not only there's the matter of light speed being so hugely high that a thick ward can still let it pass. If light do take 1/100th of a second to cross the Ward, it's a 300 Km (186 miles) of compressed space. Most non-artillery bullets travel up to 4 Km and a few go as far as 9 Km under exceptionally favorable circumstances, we can safely assume most things just can't traverse the ward in any meaningful way. There's also Paradigm, the Mage may simply not think light behaves like that and wouldn't be hindered by space. That's too minor a pass to be worth being so harsh on the description of the effect.

                                          But besides that, I think some of the secondary Spheres you went a little overboard on how high to require them. It is already a Corr effect, why call for up to 4 levels of another sphere just to better identify the target when the main effect is Corr alone? Seriously, for most of those I don't think more than level 1 should be needed unless the Ward itself has some further transformation effect. Even if convinced that higher levels may be warranted, I hardly think that it makes sense to demand as high as 4 levels of a secondary Sphere that's effectively adding nothing to the final effect but identification.

                                          Maybe if the Ward conveys another Corr property to the target, such high levels would be warranted. Maybe the warded-off object can actually be taken into the area, but there it can't move without help, maybe it is displaced from one side of the warded area to another, or the effect not only identifies the object, but analyzes its structure completely and let the Mage decide on the spot what to let pass or not, so the definition has to change during the effect. Why would otherwise you need more than Matter 1? I think establishing with care what the ward does to the things that meet it is a generally good idea and justifies some of your higher level requirements, but just identifying the target can generally take less (Mind being the exception, more or less).
