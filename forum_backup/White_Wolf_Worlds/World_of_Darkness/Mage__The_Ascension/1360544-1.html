        Regarding the Avatar, I've never really liked that name, as I don't think fits all that well (IMO at least), but I've also never been able to come up with a suitable replacement term for it. I've considered Daemon and Genius, among others, but none of them seem to fit what I'm looking for. But that aside:

        I treat the Avatar as an outside influence, a shard of semi-aware primal energy that bonds with a mage's soul (or psyche or whatever term you want to use) during the Awakening. There seems to be a limited number of them, and no one is born with them. They can carry over memories from one incarnation to another, so that some Awakened are gifted/cursed/annoyed with visions of "past lives", but it's the Avatar's previous incarnation, not the person's. (Although cultures that believe in reincarnation believe that some Avatars seek out the same reincarnated soul over and over again.) It is also a popular theory that the Avatars are fragments of the beings that once oversaw the Tapestry and the functioning of reality itself in the period before creation was shattered into it's current state. Some think that each being may have fractured into four parts, one of each of the Essences, but there's no way to really know for sure.

        I also borrowed the idea of Auras for the four Essences. This is both a personal aura for each of the Awakened, as well as frequently adding a resonance to their magic. (I never really got into Revised Resonance system very much.) Archetypes also seem to be common among the four types.

        Dynamic - affiliated with the forces of creation and chaos, the element of fire, the gall humour, the season of Spring, the Eastern direction, the Sea, the suit of Wands, and the future.
        Aura: Energy and Drive
        Archetypes: The Advocate, The Fool, The Rebel, The Wanderer, The Warrior

        Pattern - affiliated with the forces of order and stasis, the element of earth, the phlegm humour, the season of Winter, the Western direction, the Land, the suit of Coins, and the present.
        Aura: Calm and Stability
        Archetypes: The Builder, The Defender, The Judge, The Teacher, The Watcher

        Primordial - affiliated with the forces of change and destruction, the element of water, the bile humour, the season of Autumn, the Southern direction, the Underworld, the suit of Swords, and the past.
        Aura: Eeriness and Loss
        Archetypes: The Destroyer, The Enforcer, The Hermit, The Madman, The Purifier

        Questing - affiliated with the forces of balance and vision, the element of air, the sanguine humour, the season of Summer, the Northern direction, the Heavens, the suit of Cups, and infinity.
        Aura: Focus and Dedication
        Archetypes: The Creator, The Crusader, The Emissary, The Healer, The Seeker

            One side effect of my games being earth centered, and also locally focused (at least to start with), is that there's a much stronger sense of local politics.
            Above the Cabal and Chantry level, I have Councils. These function as the leadership of a city or other geographical region's Awakened population (mainly that of Tradition members, but it may sometimes speak for others). A Council is made up of representatives of the area's Chantries, although in instances where a old and powerful Chantry dominates an area, it may also dominate the Council. The Council's main duties are to set and enforce local Protocols (the rules and regulations members of the Traditions live under, which vary from place to place), managing the local Ascension War, and handling diplomacy with outsiders.
            There's also a number of titles/positions that are part of the local structure. A Custos is a Mage charged with guarding and defending a local Chantry, Council, or individual or object of importance from mystical threat. (This can include anything from legendary Artifacts or gateways into the Umbra to a mundane individual who is believed to be Fated to play some important role in human destiny.)
            A Deacon is someone who assists Council members in their duties, often while being groomed for future leadership positions. Though some develop an overinflated sense of self-importance and become annoying antagonists for PC cabals.
            A Gardener is a Mage tasked with the care and preservation of a Wellspring, a place where Quintessence naturally bubbles up into the world, providing a valuable resource for a Chantry or Council.
            A Herald is a diplomat among the Traditions, usually in service to a Chantry or Council, but potentially also to a major Tradition faction, the Grand Council, or some regional collective (the Web of Faith, for instance).
            A Questor is a Mage tasked with the investigation of a specific event, individual or object. This frequently involves investigations of corruption, murder, or treason, as well as tracking down lost talismans, grimoires, or persons.
            A Rogue is a Tradition mage who has been expelled or ostracized from Mage society, usually as a result of protocol violation or political conflict.
            A Veilkeeper is a Mage tasked with maintaining the Veil of Secrecy, upholding the safety and security of Chantries and Cabals from mundane or Technocratic discovery, and protecting mundane bystanders from the dangers of the Awakened world.

            On a larger level, I borrowed the factions from Mage The Awakened for subsections within the Traditions.
            The Adamantine Arrow are those that focus on carrying out the Ascension War against both the Technocracy and the Nephandi.
            The Mysterium are scholars and seekers trying to develop a better understanding of reality, the world, and its hidden secrets.
            The Silver Ladder are those who focus on developing power and influence within the mundane world and its various sociopolitical power structures, as well as Tradition politics.
            The Guardians of the Veil are those who focus on upholding the Veil of Secrecy, and to a lesser degree the other protocols, protecting Tradition secrets from enemies and those who might be endangered by them.
            And the Rogue Council is a loose collection of various Rogues, Orphans and other Mages who pursue a number of purposes, including trying to recruit Orphans and Crafts into the Ascension War, protecting Orphans from the Ascension War, trying to reform or replace the existing Grand Council, turn themselves into a forth faction of the Ascension War, and others.

            There's also various regional factions, such as the Web of Faith and its counterparts in India, Africa, and other places.

                                    Another side effect of my focus on local issues was that I had to figure out just how the Traditions organize themselves at a local level. I wanted the various members of each Tradition to still come together and focus on the aspects of the Tradition, its paradigm and culture, even if they were scattered across multiple Cabals and Chantries. The places this happens can be Chantries that happen to cater to a single Tradition, or just the private sanctum and property of one of the Tradition's local masters.

                                    Members of the Akashic Brotherhood come together in local schools, which can be anything from martial arts dojos or religious temples to dance studios and private social clubs. Here they engage in meditation, martial arts practice, crafts, philosophical debate and other facets of the Eight Fold Way of Do.

                                    The Celestial Chorus fellowship in prayer, debate, and activism as a congregation. The nature of the group is typically decided by the ranking elders, with more traditional groups favoring ritual masses while less conservative ones tend toward simple group meetings and studies.

                                    Local Cultists favor informal gatherings hosted by area Masters, in which members engage in philosophical discussion, sensual exploration, and shared vision quests in an effort to feel Lakashim and reach Ananda. The host will typically decide on the cultural parameters and methods of exploration.

                                    Neighboring Dreamspeakers form medicine societies (or its equivalent term in other continents) to preserve and pass on sacred knowledge, resolve internal political problems, and foster a stronger sense of community among its members. While these societies have traditionally reflected the dominant local cultures, modern migrations and upheavals leave many areas having to balance multiple, sometimes conflicting, cultural needs.

                                    Euthanatos often meet in secrecy, hidden from those who might misunderstand their ways. The meeting site and group rituals typically reflect the tastes and traditions of the eldest Master or the majority of Thanatoics present. Temples, caves, and private drawing rooms are the most common locations. These courts offer mystical and ethical instruction, and well as discussion and debate on issues that impact the Great Wheel (including assassinations).

                                    The Order of Hermes is the most hierarchal of the Traditions, with formal meeting Chantries, mandatory monthly meetings, official rules of etiquette and order, and a tightly controlled agenda of lectures, debates, duels, and official declarations.

                                    Etherites communicate, debate, and feud with each other in the pages of their private journal and newsletter Paradigma. On a more local level, individuals will host parties and symposiums to show off their latest inventions, argue over competing theories, debate the events of the day, and make efforts at united political maneuvers.

                                    Local Verbena gather in covens, preferably consisting of a sacred number of members (3, 7, 9, or 13), in order to conduct sacred rituals, pass on ancient lore, and tend to the local Tree of Life. They also attempt to resolve internal disputes, plan for mutual protection, and coordinate future social and political activities.

                                    Almost all Virtual Adept interaction takes place online, either in the mundane deep web or the mystical Digital Web. Real world "meat" meetings tend toward a level of paranoia reminiscent of Cold War spycraft. Most groups run as democratic meritocracies, but they are frequently prone to passionate arguments and rampant egotism.

                                    Local Hollow Ones tend to congregate regularly at whatever nightclub, bookstore, coffee shop, or other hangout that is currently in favor. There they jockey mercilessly for status and prestige while claiming not to be interested in such things. On occasion, issues of actual importance may be settled.

                                                A lot of this developed just from the question of "what to Mages do during an average week?" Even D&D characters have downtime a life outside of the adventuring party. All those backgrounds like Allies, Contacts, Mentor, Resources, Sanctum, and the like have to correspond to a person, place, or thing in the immediate setting. Lone Wolves, with no connections or ties to anyone or anything, work much better in fiction (the Jack Reacher novels, for example) than they do in an RPG. A bunch of people who have no lives outside their little group and the adventures they go on tend to be pretty boring, at least in my opinion. The more plugged in my characters are to the world immediately around them, the more skin they have in the game as far as the Ascension War and other parts of the setting goes. I like dealing with things like what happens when a Mage tries to balance being a Mage with also being a spouse and parent, or trying to be an active part of their community. These also put a more personal face on the Ascension War. If you have kids, the idea of them being co-opted by the Technocracy, either just from mundane compulsory state education, or being actively brainwashed and turned against you, can be utterly terrifying.

                                                And on the subject of backgrounds, I tried to pare them down to just ten, largely for the same reason I did with Abilities. I want those starting points to go as far as possible and make for characters with a lot of background ties. I have five mundane ones (that are roughly the same for all my games), Allies, Contacts, Followers, Influence, and Resources, and then five specific to the supernatural subculture, Library, Mentor, Sanctum, Status (a measure of one's place within the supernatural subculture's political sphere), and Talisman. For the Chantry, if the Cabal has one, I do it as a pooled background, with one point per person in the Cabal (and additional points if they choose to spend them on it), that can be spent on size, security, location, and other features.

                                                        I also use something along the lines of the Gnosis paradigm (where the power stat represents a deep understanding of the mysteries of the world); but I call it Enlightenment, and present it as an alternative to Arete rather than a replacement for it. There are tangible differences between a mage with Enlightenment and a mage with Arete: those with Enlightenment don't get to Work Without Tools or Surpass their Instruments, but get to select Instruments that they master at the same rate that mages with Arete choose to surpass them; a mastered Instrument is just like a surpassed Instrument except that you can't do without it. On the other hand, their Enlightenment isn't capped by Willpower; they don't need to expend Willpower or personal Quintessence for the act of creating a Device/Talisman or Principia/Grimoire the way that mages with Arete do; and they also have a slightly easier time teaching magical tricks to Sleepers who have a compatible Focus (the Technocracy's Extraordinary Citizens and their less tech-focused counterparts).

                                                        Technocrats are, almost to a man, Enlightened rather than Awakened (i.e., have Enlightenment rather than Arete); the handful of exceptions are most likely to be found among the Order of Reason Syndicate's SPD (see my earlier post for how I've changed it), and even there they're a minority. But Enlightenment isn't exclusive to the Union, or even to technomancers; though it's more common among technomancers than it is among mystics. The Traditions heavily lean toward Arete, with the exceptions of the Etherites and Virtual Adepts. Disparates vary; though they tend to lean toward Arete.

                                                        I haven't actually done this; but I've considered making Faith a third variant of Arete, granting both the benefits and drawbacks of the True Faith Merit as well as the benefits and drawbacks of Arete. I haven't implemented it yet because I'm still not fully comfortable with the implementation.

                                                        I also don't use Sorcery in my games. Instead, acolytes and extraordinary citizens can be taught individual rotes resulting in something along the lines of the sorcerers found in Gods and Monsters. As mentioned above, the Enlightened have a slight edge in training their adepts (with an emphasis on “slight”).

                                                        ---

                                                        The main motivation for this is that I don't like the implicit “the Technocracy isn't just dangerous; they're also fundamentally deluded and/or deceitful about the nature of reality”. This split between mechanically distinct Arete and Enlightenment (and maybe Faith) is an attempt to address that: the Enlightened see a universe with an underlying Truth to it, and their Science is an extrapolation of that Truth: careful use of their Instruments to exploit edge cases of how the world works lets them achieve results that seem impossible. And because of the benefits Enlightenment provides that Arete doesn't, a strong case can be made that they very well might be right. It's far from a sure thing, mind you; it's also possible that they're wrong and that the Traditions' view that We're all Gods in Disguise is right. And it's also possible that they're both wrong, like the tale of the Blind Men and the Elephant. I make a point of not answering that question — not for the players, and not even for myself.

                                                        In a similar way, I make a point not to define precisely what an Avatar is, beyond a few characteristics it has. Further definition is left to each mage's Focus. This includes whether or not an Avatar is inherently part of a mage's soul or is something that gets grafted on when the mage Awakens/Enlightens/whatever. I personally assume that it's always there, just Sleeping until the Mage Awakens; but that's because of my view of what the Consensus is.

                                                        That said, I like the alignments of various Natures to different Essences. It's a less radical version of something that I've been considering for a while now: jettisoning Nature and Demeanor (which is essentially a Vampire thing, given its heavy emphasis on intrigue) in favor of something more in tune with the nature of Mage. Werewolf and Changeling each have their own examples of how to replace Nature and Demeanor with something else. So far, my thoughts for Mage have been to replace the Nature-and-Demeanor system with a Paradigm-and-Essence system, where your choice of (M20-style) Paradigm earns you Willpower whenever you actively affirm the philosophy that the Paradigm represents; and your Essence likewise provides some sort of reward when you act in accord with it.

                                                        In this regard, Essence might become a bit like Werewolf's Auspices, where every Garou regains Willpower in a manner consistent with one of the five Auspices. That said, there's something to be said for giving each Essence numerous Archetypes that replace Nature.

                                                        As for the relationship between Essence and Resonance: I use the M20 system of Essence briefly sketched in M20 and detailed in Book of Secrets, with the four types of Resonance loosely coupled to Essence: Questing Avatars tend to push for actions that lead to Devotional Resonance; Primordial Avatars favor activities that generate Elemental Resonance; Pattern Avatars tend toward Stabilizing Resonance; and Dynamic Avatars foster Temperamental Resonance. (Note: these are loose associations. The mechanism, such as it is, is that your Avatar attempts to influence you toward certain kinds of actions — see the last paragraph about replacing Nature and Demeanor with something at least partially Essence-based — and those actions tend to produce the corresponding type of Resonance. For instance: if you have a Questing Avatar, you'll be getting Willpower rewards whenever your actions generate Devotional Resonance.)

                                                        In keeping with this, I also use a different breakdown for Quiet: a four-way breakdown that aligns with the four Essences instead of a three-way breakdown aligning with the Metaphysical Trinity. This lets me factor the different types of Resonance into the rules for what kind of Quiet you experience more easily. You still have Denial and Madness, which correspond to Stabilizing and Temperamental Resonance, respectively; but Morbidity goes away. Instead, Elemental Resonance (such as Jhor) tends to promote Obsession (including, but not limited to, obsession with death: Morbidity is a subset of Obsession) while Devotional Resonance tends to promote Hubris.

                                                        And just like there are Marauders who have succumbed to Madness and a recent phenomenon of “Marauders” who have succumbed to Denial (more or less the Negative Men from Book of Secrets, though I tweak them slightly), it's possible that there are “Marauders” who have succumbed to Obsession or Hubris. Possible, I say, because I haven't yet worked out the appropriate mechanics for them; so they're still largely hypothetical.
