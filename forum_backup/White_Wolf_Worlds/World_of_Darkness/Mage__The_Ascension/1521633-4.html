                Since the difficulty of evaluating whether a maneuver is worth using in a given circumstance has been mentioned in this thread, for everyone’s convenience, here’s an Anydice program to run the numbers for you: https://anydice.com/program/31a9c

                It only does one comparison at a time (varying with the number of dice in the relevant attack pool and Strength) because looping over scenarios got to be too long to run on the Anydice server. There is it set up for comparing Punch and Kick, but it’s easy enough to modify the last few lines for other damaging maneuvers. The “wod” functions generally should be useful for calculations over a broad range of scenarios. (0 successes is an ordinary failure, -1 successes is a botch,)

                It’s set to one of the turning points - 8 dice, 3 strength - where using Kick, which players would normally assume increases damage, actually reduces mean damage. Just change the obvious variables near the bottom to try it for different scenarios.

                            Imagine this scenario:

                            you have strength 3 brawl 3

                            yourboponent has been knocked down by someone else, so they are imobalised, they have used up their dice and so cannot dodge.

                            now, you could roll one attack with your full pool, or you could roll 6 attacks with a pool of 1 each

                            let’s say you roll. 3, 4, 5, 6, 4 8

                            the diff to punch is 4, and to kick is 5, due to them being knocked down.

                            as one attack, this roll would net you 5 successes for a punch, which mean 4+3 damage dice, a total of 7, and 3 for a kick, giving you 2+3+1 = 6 damage dice

                            however, if these were six separate attacks, then you would get a damage roll for each one separately.

                            for the punch that is:
                            3x5 = 15
                            for the kick that is:
                            (3+1)x4 = 16

                            reducing the difficulty by a further -2 prior tovthis roll would instead let you receive max potential. For the punch this is 18 damage, while the max potential for the kick is 24 damage. While the risk of botching is exactly the same for both.

                            Thing thing about rolling only one die is that a difference of difficulty of 1 only matters 10% of the time. If you get 8,8,8,2,3,2, then it’s 9 for the punch and 12 for the kick, a 33% increase in damage output.

                            in this scenario of diff 4 vs 5, each roll has a 60% chance of being better for kicking, a 10% chance of being better for punching, and a 30% chance of being the same for either choice.
