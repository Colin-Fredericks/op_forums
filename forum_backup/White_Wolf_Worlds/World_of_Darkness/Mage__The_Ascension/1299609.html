    M20 Brawl vs Martial Arts

    03-25-2019, 11:41 AM
    Greetings,

    I would like to know what is the difference between certain secondary abilities and the main one, my best example is Brawl vs Martial Arts... What would mean to have low or zero Brawl and high Martial Arts?

    My understanding is that Brawl is needed for basic fighting, but Martial Arts gives you extra options... So for my example Brawl 0, Martial Arts 2, does this person know how to spin kick, fly kick, but not how to punch?

    I am using M20.

    Thanks,

      Thanks,
      Brawl is a talent. With Brawl 0 you can just roll your Dexterity (say, 5) and get good results.

      Martial Arts is a skill. This means that if you don't have it you'll be a +1 difficulty as a minimum (unless you have the Jack of All Trades Merit). Knowledges are more extreme, demanding 1 dot in them to even make the roll (if you have Law 0 then if you attempt to convince a judge of something in a court case you won't have any clue what you're doing because you don't understand the rules or the terms of art, so you have to make different rolls which rely on abilities you can use).

      Martial Arts includes punching, but only if the martial art in question involves punching naturally.

      Brawl gives you access to Dirty Fighting maneuvers, while Martial Arts gives you access to Martial Arts maneuvers.

      Brawl is surrounded by talents that you might feel you need to invest dots in, and the Primary/Secondary/Tertiary setup means you might prefer putting dots in MA or Brawl depending on where you feel you need more dots between Skills and Talents.

      As for why you would have Martial Arts 2 and Brawl 0... as a kid I never fought anyone, and would have no idea how to fight someone. But I did study judo, both throws and wrestling. Therefore my ability would be best represented with Martial Arts 1 (Judo) and Brawl 0.

          Baring with me with the caveat that I greatly dislike the decision for M20 to have Brawl and Martial Arts (and Do):

          The point of having Brawl and Martial Arts is to try to reinforce a purely narrative distinction between 'kinds' of fighters. Brawl based fighters are supposed to be about learning from dangerous experiences on the streets, in underground fighting rings, and similarly unregulated and unsupervised settings. Martial Arts based fighters are supposed to be about people that study formal schools of fighting.

          Remember that MA has lots of assumed access to the general close combat maneuvers that's in the individual write-ups even if not on the summary chart. So, basically, a Brawl 0, Martial Arts 2 character loses out on a few tricks because those don't get taught in formal systems, but can generally do whatever a Brawl user can do.

                          Originally posted by Lord Revan View Post
                          I don't mind the Brawl/Martial Arts thing, because they indeed are very different, including the mindset and the fact that one is a Skill and other a Talent.

                          What I think is worse is Occult/Esoterica Knowledge. I still don't get why would a character pick Occult, even after reading all the books explanations and FAQs. Occult is just mysticism as seen by the masses, even stated as "wrong" mysticism. Why would you want to have a "wrong" knowledge??
                          Occult isn't JUST mysticism. It's a blanket term for knowledge of the Occult aspects of the world. You would roll Occult for things like secret societies, various supernatural creatures, occult histories, and even conspiracy theories.

                          Esoterica is meant to cover specific, technical knowledge. It exists to show how well the Mage can apply her mystical principles. But a character wouldn't be able to roll it to know about fairies, cults, or the histories of major figures or groups. That's what Occult is for, because Occult is the measure of one's knowledge of hidden things. (Obviously there are various Lore specialties that give more specific knowledge. Occult's utility is in it being a grab-bag of material from a wide cross-section of hidden knowledge; you trade the possibility of having "wrong" knowledge, for the chance of only needing to invest points in one Knowledge).

                          Moreover, the fact that Occult is separate from Esoterica is a positive because it's easier to justify, from a role-playing perspective. Anyone could have studied general occultism, through books or documentaries or folk knowledge. That's why Occult is a Knowledge available to all splats, regardless of their esoteric persuasion. Esoterica can be available to common people, but it depends on the Specialty you're talking about. If a character understands Demonology - to an actionable extent - you, the player, have to explain where that knowledge was gleaned. Especially since practical, actionable Demonology is a controversial, guarded knowledge base among people familiar enough with the subject to know it isn't just BS. Even if the explanation for the character's knowledge amounts to "found a book in their grandfather's attic"; that book creates its own set of potential plot threads or dangers to the character.

                                      Originally posted by Lord Revan View Post
                                      About Esoterica, does the mage must pick a specialization even if his score is below 4? And can he use it with "abilities enhancing magick" rule making both rolls (ability and Arete) in the same turn?
                                      With some abilities you pick your "spec" from the get-go. This happens with Art, Martial Arts, Crafts, Esoterica etc. However you don't get double 10s from it. It's more a case of recording on your sheet what these dots mean and what you can actually use them for, or what it looks like when you use them. So it could be Art (Singing), Martial Arts (Judo), Crafts (Figures/Dolls) and Esoterica (Symbolism), but by the same measure it could be Art (Calligraphy), Martial Arts (Karate), Crafts (Woodworking) and Esoterica (Yoga). When you hit 4 you can then use the well-trained craftsman option to buy another ability for 4 EXP essentially. So you have Art 4 (Singing) and you spend 4 EXP, and now you have Art 4 (Singing and Piano). Of course the book makes it clear that you have to devote time to this.

                                      You make the abilities enhancing magick roll the turn before you do your Arete roll, unless you're able to take multiple actions for some reason (like Time 3 acceleration). Only one Arete roll per turn, ever, though.

                                          Originally posted by Lord Revan View Post
                                          I don't mind the Brawl/Martial Arts thing, because they indeed are very different, including the mindset and the fact that one is a Skill and other a Talent.

                                          What I think is worse is Occult/Esoterica Knowledge. I still don't get why would a character pick Occult, even after reading all the books explanations and FAQs. Occult is just mysticism as seen by the masses, even stated as "wrong" mysticism. Why would you want to have a "wrong" knowledge??
                                          Esoterica is more useful for a Mage who is performing their Practice. But Occult is incredibly useful when it comes to certain practices, since understanding what sleepers believe about the occult world is very important. The occult rating gives you more opportunities to leverage human belief. The Mythic Threads / Hypernarrative modifiers, for example, come from doing things in a way that match the ideas sleepers have on some really deep level (using sunlight to purge away the darkness, exorcising someone using holy water, defeating a monster with a Big Fucking Gun at the most awesome and impressive moment possible).

                                          So while Esoterica is more foundational (unless you're using Science instead), Occult is still important since it helps you manage a cult, understand the ways sleepers think, and also gives a broad set of knowledge which may include plenty of truths and half-truths for you to investigate.

                                          Here's a good example; a Mage has Occult 3, and has a faerie problem. They know from their reading that in legend Fae are harmed by iron, and in some tales only cold iron. They try it out on a true fae, and it works! But even if it didn't work (modern Changelings don't have the flaw, but I believe the true fae in Arcadia do), they'd still have a Mythic Thread to pull on because of it; cold iron will make a good material to mix in with your focus when fighting a fae of some kind. In a similar vein a Mage with occult 1 might "know" that a stake in the heart will kill a vampire. They're wrong, but still largely right, and the result is a staked vampire (assuming they're strong enough to ram a stake inside an angry vampire).

                                          Occult

                                          Aren’t mages occult by their very nature? Yes and no. This Knowledge represents a generalized comprehension of the mystic world as most Sleepers understand it; in short, then, such knowledge is often misleading, incomplete, or flat-out wrong. A decent rating in this Trait (three dots or higher) helps you sort the truth from the bullshit… but by then, you’ve also begun to understand how deep the occult rabbit-hole really goes. “Occult” means hidden for very good reasons, and even the most skillful occultists often don’t know nearly as much as they think they do. Being a mage does not automatically confer accurate understanding of other practices – in fact, it often muddies the waters even more! Still, this Ability grants you a decent overview of mystic practices, philosophies, lore, history, arcane cultures, and so-called secrets. Of course, they’re all secrets by definition… or at least that’s the theory, anyway.

                                          • Dabbler: You think you know who Crowley was, and you have some New Age books on your shelves.
                                          •• Student: Some of the stuff you’ve heard or read is actually true.
                                          ••• Scholar: You’ve got first-hand experience in the world of shadows.
                                          •••• Professor: Study and experience have taught you a respectable amount of genuine lore.
                                          ••••• Master: Knowledgeable parties respect your accomplishments and consider you an authority in your own right.

                                          So that's far from useless, and it brings something to the table. It's not as good as Esoterica or Science, but Esoterica and Science are THE Mage abilities, arguably, with almost any Mage benefiting enormously from having them, or being unable to work at all without them (in the case of the Minimal Abilities rule). There will always be an elite set of abilities like Art, Martial Arts, Computer, Technology, Esoterica and Science because practices rotate around them.

                                          And as a minor aside, Occult is sometimes rolled in the Umbra, since superstitions and myths can be a decent rudder there.

                                                  Originally posted by Aknorals View Post
                                                  As pointed out Brawl lets you use Dirty Fighting, whereas Martial Arts lets you use martial arts techniques.
                                                  Also, AFAIK, natural weapons like bite/gore/claw are only covered under Brawl.
                                                  That's a very good point; if a Mage with Brawl 5 suddenly sprouts fangs/claws/horns because of magick then they'll be able to use them to best advantage immediately. A Mage with Martial Arts 5 would struggle to use them effectively because MA is so deliberate and well trained; it's very much a Skill and not a Talent.

                                                  With that said, need drives invention. If a person has horns/claws/fangs then they absolutely will develop a martial art style that suits this new natural appendage. Lots of martial arts already have "animal styles" which you could build off of on a thematic level. Furthermore, indian martial arts already includes a practice dedicated to the use of a small "claw" weapon that sits in your palm and forms an easily concealed weapon; this style of MA would be a Melee skill, but studying the rules of that style you could definitely learn to use your own claws as though they were a weapon (best represented by MA). So if someone with MA develops fangs/claws/horns (best represented under the Advantages section in M20) then they can invest time and effort in developing their own MA, or style of MA, to cover it.

                                                  Do, I should point out, lets you use a coin as a weapon if you really want to (with the 1000 weapons ability), so the idea of making your claws into a weapon is quite in-line with it. Furthermore, Do makes your attacks Lethal automatically (if you want them to be) regardless. So integrating claws into Do would be a matter of practice, as Do is all about learning to harness your own natural state, find some harmony, rather than simply learn a set of rules about fighting.

                                                          There's a good Hunter flaw in Hunters Hunted ll (I believe) which represents never having been in a *real* fight before. Gives you a risk of seizing up, running away etc. depending on Courage and Willpower rolls.

                                                          Personally I like this idea that Mages are, outside of special training and certain associations with spirits and conditioning by certain movements, not suited to combat the way other nightfolk are. Mages are scared of getting hurt, Mages get PTSD, Mages struggle to stop thinking about the terrible things they did, or saw others do.                                                           Of course when mages stop being scared of violence, and start taking risks in fights, things can get very very messy. It's kind of boring to have all these abilities and just get scared every time there's a fight. And if someone prefers to RP self-preservation and a very timid fighting style then that's also okay.
