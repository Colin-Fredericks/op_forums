                If I had to bring MtAs into the present, I'd probably iterate on the idea I had earlier for a post-Avatar Storm Technocracy being on the backfoot. While also using an idea I posited back in the thread about custom Crafts (which I sort of jumped off into specifically the "Sons of Liberty").

                Namely that in the wake of the Avatar Storm and the literal loss of Control, the Technocracy lost the reins it had over global society. It's state apparatuses, its economic systems, its information networks, and its culture. The Technocracy never completely controlled any of these things, of course. But they could guide them. Use all manner of levers and tools to nudge our world in a direction of the Union's liking. And, importantly for our purposes, moderate forces that could throw their Neo-Liberal hegemony out of "proper" alignment.

                With the loss of Control, the systems put in place to direct society were broken. Their ties to every strata of the Capitalist system severed, their assets cut off and gone to seed. Knowledge of even what all those assets and systems were was lost. In much the way that "client Crafts" slipped the leash of the Union, so did its assets embedded in government, corporation, media, and the tech sector. As the Technocracy struggled to get its own house(s) in order, a process of several years, the global edifice they helped build kept running without them. Unconstrained, unguided, and unmoderated.

                Fascism, always a movement festering in the underbelly of Conservativism, was a beast leashed by Sleeper and Enlightened forces alike. Used as a solid base that could be manipulated through dog whistles and propaganda to maintain the Status Quo, and occasionally act as a bludgeon forces that would threaten the Neo-Liberal hegemony. Fascism is and always has been, after all, Capitalism's immune response against Socialism.

                Now, the Technocracy's ability to restrain both the worst excesses of Capitalism and the spread of Fascism were compromised. The beast they thought they could control slipped its tether, and started feasting. The Syndicate could not or didn't want to prevent Capitalism from exploiting people in new and creative ways (enabled by technology), beyond what it previously allowed. And Fascism preyed upon the growing discontent within people at their tightening economic bonds, providing no end of scapegoats for the problems directly caused by corporate greed and control.

                Moreover, in a time of increased social media dominance and consolidation, the Technocracy wasn't there to stop algorithms from blindly pointing users towards radicalizing content. YouTube offering via Autoplay all manner of videos decrying the scientific establishment and fomenting belief in conspiracy theories of an often supernatural bent. Something the Union would never have permitted, and might otherwise have prevented by simply using their agents embedded in YouTube to tweak the algorithms away from such content. Such tools were outside the Technocracy's reach, allowing reactionary movements to snowball through blind systems of profit optimization and engagement farming.

                The resulting reactionary movements - such as Gamergate - redpilled whole generations of people into not only believing unscientific, unsecular worldviews, but galvanized them into harassment campaigns and outright mass violence. (Nephandi weren't responsible for this, of course, but they could have empowered certain personalities or signal-boosted radicalizing messages further. Perhaps been in key places to nudge individuals into lone wolf violence, that in turn inspired more. But simply funneling money to right-wing grifters and ideologues is more than sufficient to allow a Fascist movement to snowball, even without dark magick.)

                All while corporate interests, unfettered even by the true believers among the Syndicate, pushed Capitalism harder. More deregulation, more propaganda, more disinformation, more exploitation. Billionaires became richer, more powerful, more deranged, and more immune to being affected by Union assets that remained (simply by virtue of their popular visibility; could the same forces that keep presidents and prime ministers from being influenced also shield figures like Elon Musk?). Instead being guided by their own greed, and the same mundane radicalizing forces that play on the common people. Technofetishists no longer seek to further actual innovation (sanctioned and scheduled by the Technocracy, of course), but by any grifters promising impossible technology at unrealistic rates of return. Enamored by thoughts of unlimited growth, greater control of consumers and workers, and the promise of leaving the dying planet behind for off-world colonies. Distractions from the very real problems they allowed to creep in, if not outright enabled to exist, especially Climate Change.

                In the end, what are we left with? Well, the rise of Fascist movements, the mass disillusionment of the populace in the promise of Neo-Liberal policies and systems, a growing leftist movement to foster collective power (and a blunt-force state crackdown on same, as the illusion of representation is eroded away to reveal a violent, repressive totalitarianism, even among the liberal centrists). And the mass rejection of Science and Progress, in favor of reactionary mysticism and religious fanaticism.

                The world of Mage in the modern day is one typified by the Technocracy struggling to regain the reins, and the Traditions struggling to fight against the hateful ideology worming its way into their movements.

                This must be what it felt like to be a Mage in 1930s Europe, with the PREVIOUS rise of Fascism. Except with more nukes, and no one in control.

                        To be specific, I think one the big issue of that is not so much the core setting, but what it became post 1st edition.

                        Mafe begin with a paradox of difficult to reconcile concepts. "a world like our own" "a world where reality is decided by consensus" and " a world where magic and the supernatural are hidden".

                                            Within the history of the setting, the Consensus being possibly unnatural isn't something new, if we take into account how Paradox works.

                                            At first, no force countered Awakened magic whatsoever. We know this because the rise of new, unforeseen limitations on Magic is a plot point for the dawn of the Dark Ages and the formation of the Order of Hermes. It is also the whole point behind House Tremere experimenting with vampiric blood, and eventually turning into vampires.

                                            Even then, at first the only thing that Mages have to be worried about is Backlash, a problem arising only when they botch on spellcasting, which is how things work in Mage Dark Ages. Only by the time of Sorcerer's Crusade, already at the beginning of the Renaissance, does we have actual talkings about a Consensus, and the Scourge appears. Even then, it works very differently still from Paradox. By then, it is still defined mostly locally, and a global Consensus is more theory than anything.

                                            Paradox as we know it has a precise date of birth: 14 June, 1645, at the Battle of Naseby. The theory goes that it was the result of the Scourge changing under the pressure of too much vulgar magic. And even then, the strength of Paradox changes over time, reaching its apex at the end of the 90's.

                                            That's just to show that, as far as metaphysics goes, the setting doesn't really relies on the Consensus being a natural or ever-present phenomenon. We know that people's beliefs shape reality in some way, but a lot of our assumptions about how it work derive from something that hasn't always been there, and in fact that could be linked to beliefs only from the 15th century onwards.

                                            It becomes even more problematic when we start to examine how many things go unexplained by the Consensus. It is blatantly clear that a fundament for reality exists, as a lot of things are real despite general belief, and that goes from monsters to diseases. In fact, it is even more clear given that the Technocracy's influence over the population was greatly overrated. People not only still believe in magic, they still do so in droves and even in the most secular environments.

                                            Originally posted by Nyremne View Post
                                            if we were to built a world where the consensus is an aberration, how can it be replaced without making it look like a massive retcon, so to speak?
                                            My idea, at least, isn't to replace the Consensus (at least not at first), but to have it weakening in much the same sense it has strengthened before. The idea isn't to retroactively replace the Consensus with something else, but to play its inconsistencies and, at first, to simply recognize the whole Revised Era rules for Paradox while dialing back to 2nd Edition Paradox.

                                            So, while it is maybe a bold move lorewise, as far as mechanics and storytelling goes, it's actually quite simple. I may come to use even less strict Paradox rules at some point, but that's about it.

                                                This could be a really fun avenue to explore in the "reality is breaking down" or "consensus is shifting" storyline, I think. MRev and M20 were already gesturing vaguely in that direction by indicating that some in Traditions and the Technocracy as a whole that Sorcerers (or their equivalents) are at parity or even outnumber "True Magic" wielders.

                                                So instead of (or even alongside) balkanized reality zone neighborhoods you get a sudden explosion in sorcerers across all walks of life. Not just jewish mysticism in Midwood and voudu in Flatbush but even more unexpected places. An EMT lays on hands to stop the bleeding on a accident and finds himself using the path of Healing. The bored bartender unknowing working Herbalism or Alchemy. A street performer with Facination. A cab driver makes his usual morning prayer to Ganesha, remover of obstacles and suddenly finds himself working the path of Conveyance.

                                                (insert your own local reality zones and sorcerers here. I'm Brooklyn NYC based and am writing what I know).

                                                The themes such a story point would serve would be a counter to magical hubris. A reminder that the "little people" matter. In fact, right now they are where the real action is. And, perhaps, that is where the real action always was.

                                                Plotwise, there are a variety of reasons this could be happening. One of the more hopeful interpretations would be that a distracted (threat null) or reformed (post civil war) technocracy has loosened the reins on enforcing conformity and therefore the consensus. Heterodox belief flourishes not only within communities but within individuals as well. And again, perhaps it was always this way. Most people muddle through their day with a mixture of faith in a higher power and a belief that science will ensure their car turns on and their aspirin works the way its supposed to. Very few people in the modern world actually are creationists or anti-theists. All it took was the Technocracy to take its foot of the gas pedal in the form of the Pogram and the Timetable.

                                                Perhaps it was never a "Consensus of Apathy" at all. Maybe it was just the birth pains of a "Consensus of Pluralism" being born.

                                                Or there is a Nephandic weakening of reality causing this. The new sorcerers are a symptom of this problem, not the cause. Nevertheless they could be targeted by factions who do not realize this and think by reducing the number of sorcerers they can slow or reverse the deterioration of reality. Or we could use this to explore the "evil always plants the seeds of its own demise" trope as sorcerers are able to band together and strike back at the Nepahndi. These practitioners, ignored and belittled in the greater Ascencion War may be able to win a victory on par or greater than the victory won by the Council and Union combined at the end or WWII.

                                                In any of these scenarios there will be a lot going on as all factions maneuver in response to this sudden flourishing. A goldrush or a bloodbath depending on where you stand. Each one has a target on their back to be killed, convinced, suborned, recruited, or abducted. Even the best intentions can seem horribly disruptive to a normal person.

                                                Take our EMT suddenly working the Path of Healing. The Progentior's note her technical expertise and level head in stressful situations. They want her for field work. The Celestial Chorus notes she was raised Catholic, still prays to St. Michael, and her abuela was a consor for a Chorister during the 70's. They want her in a Chantry as a medic and caregiver for those on the violent fronts of the Ascension War.

                                                What does she want? She wants a good union job with decent benefits. She wants her partner, their kid, her abuela and her whole extended family and friend group not to to be at risk of being murdered by any number of the vast ancient magical conspiracies that are now SEVERELY complicating her life.
