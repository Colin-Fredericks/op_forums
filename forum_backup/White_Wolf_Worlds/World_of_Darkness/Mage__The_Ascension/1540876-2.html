    It's actually quite simple to prioritize if you take into account what I said earlier: some will interact with you and others will just observe and take care of their business. So you deal with the first group and keep the second group under observation. This is far from ideal, but the situation in question is also far from ideal in many ways. If it sounds desperate, it's because it is.

                    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                    Yes, but how to properly prioritize when you know nothing?

                    Remember, in this setup they're completely in the dark about how this world works. They don't think about those splats as "vampires, fairies, werewolves, etc" in the same sense a native would, or a player, or even how they would think of RDs in their own reality. Instead, they're all "natives".

                    So they don't have a parameter to say which is a priority and which is not. And the many distinctions between those entities and their apparent counterparts in WoD would just reinforce that such judgements aren't safe.
                    Exactly. Not only that, the Technocrats probably would even mistakenly confuse this new "universe" with some weird Deep Umbra realm or some weird Paradox Realm. As soon as they understand that Kansas has gone bye bye (which would be really quick), they would stop making assumptions about their surrounding reality.

                    As a matter of fact, that may even lead them to NOT prioritize "the mission" - or even to totally discard it. That would greatly depend on what they assume that this reality is. Are these "humans" or "sleepers" around them really "humans"? If they try to detect the Avatars of said humans and don't find any, would they still consider these to be "the masses" that need protection? Or are all the billions of inhabitants of this reality a threat to the "Frontlines" (their "base Earth") and therefore prone to extermination?

                    The answers to these questions may pose them as "folks' heroes" or harbingers of doom.

                    Since there's a possibility that they see everything in this reality as "ultraterrestrials" (just like extraterrestrials, but instead of being beings from other planets, it's beings from other dimensions), they may have no problem working with any kinds of local "Reality Deviants", both towards making alliances and also brainwashing them.

                    And in such case, their main priority besides growing in power in order to survive, would probably be "research a way to go back home", rather than trying to "protect the Masses" (which they might not even recognize as being properly humans to begin with)

                    And yes, I agree with Monteparnas that them and the God Machine would probably form the biggest antagonistic tensions in the setting.

                    Speaking of which, I just remembered, how will their Spirit Sphere (Dimensional Science) work here? Will it give them access to the Supernal? Will it work on the native spirits? Does it also works on the spirits of the dead? (Ghosts). Remember, they don't have a "Death Sphere", and their "Dimensional Science" should at least in theory work for anything that is not in the Material Plane.

                    How exactly will that work thou? That's one crucial point

                                        Coming in late, but:

                                        How will any of their Spheres work? They're in a universe with completely different metaphysics, where actual reality-warping Magick isn't a thing outside of the mythical acts of Imperium performed by Archmasters. It's entirely possible that their Spheres and Hedge Paths don't work in the Chronicles of Darkness. And if they do work, they don't necessarily work the same way.

                                        Break out the Mage Translation Guide, everyone; we're gonna need it here. Granted, it's somewhat out of date, because it was translating between Mage: the Ascension Revised Edition and Mage: the Awakening first edition; and both gamelines have moved on to new editions since then. But it's still mostly relevant.

                                        My gut instinct would be to define two kinds of mages for this: the Supernal Mages are the ones native to the Chronicles of Darkness, Awakened by the Watchtowers to manipulate the Supernal symbols in the Fallen World; and the Phenomenal Mages who hail from the World of Darkness. Phenomenal Mages don't Awaken to Watchtowers, and aren't split up into Paths; instead, each Phenomenal Mage is uniquely Awakened by a resident deep within her Oneiros formally known as her Genius. Her Magick isn't performed by manipulating Supernal symbols, but by directly shaping the Phenomenal World.

                                        The Technocrats would be Phenomenal Mages, and conceptually would use Spheres instead of Arcana. This would include Entropy instead of Death and Fate, and a Spirit Sphere that's capable of reaching any of the Otherworlds in the cosmology rather than being limited to the Shadow.

                                        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
