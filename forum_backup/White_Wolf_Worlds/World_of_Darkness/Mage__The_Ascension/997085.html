    Some questions on blurry rules

    11-02-2016, 07:55 AM
    Hi guys, i just wanted to ask u and have ur opinion regarding the messi rules found in the 20th anniversary edition (bear in mind that english is not my first language so i apologize in advance)

    I think i understand the basic rules for instant casting and effects that come into play right away- a fireball inflicts damage according to the chart, and a healing spells heals "x" damage.

    I also understand the mechanics for rituals and extended casting and the dividing successes option-

    However i have some doubts

    1) if i'm a life mage shapechanger, and say i want to change my appearence to look like another person or i want to grow fangs and claws, i need life 3 and three successes.
    Do the total amount of successes count toward the duration too? Say i roll three successes with my arete roll, do i transform into the new form for one entire day since i have three successes?

    2) Same thing regarding mind control. If i roll three successes and say to someone i've maybe already influenced or is my ally "protect me!" and i score three successes, does that mean that he will protect me for the entire day? (according to the chart and the duration)

    3) Same question for the physical boost of attributes- if i score 3 successes on my arete roll, and i boost my dexterity by three points, will this change last for an entire day or do i need to spend more successes beyond the basic successes for extended duration? (rules for extended casting and instant seems to blur here)

    ----it seems to me that UNLESS u use the extended casting and optional dividing success rule spells that require three successes or more will basically last for days or even the entire story by default when u score a good arete roll. Am i missing something here?

    4)If i use time acceleration and score 5 successes on my arete roll, i will have 2 extra actions every turn for six months? (5 successes, duration chart)-

    ----On adjusting the arete difficulty roll.

    it says that the cap for every modifier cannot exceed the -3, howerver there is a smal paragraph that explain that by spending extra quintessence its possible to cancel out extra modifier for the difficulty on the casting- so for example:

    5)if i want to travel back in time one or two rounds (vulgar without witnesses), i add +3 to the standard difficulty, raising the difficulty up to 10. Can i spend 3 points of quintessence to cancel out that +3 for time rewinding and THEN use a focus or something else to lower the difficulty even more?
    for example i set a small ritual or use my unique and focused instrument, lowering the difficulty to -3, and then i spend 3 quintessence points to cancel the +3 difficulty to the time rewind factor?

    6) time travel also seems like a bitch to understand to me. If i go back in time or rewind an entire scene do my character have memories of what had just happened in the present/future or does he need mind 2 to store the memory and change the scene? If so, if my mage doesn't have mind sphere dots, do we simply re-replay the entire scene and nothing changes? (since he doesn't know or remember which choices he made in the future and cannot change the time flow)


    Thanks in advance if u try to clear up this mess for me, i'm trying to setup a mage campaign for beginner players, and i dont want to mess it up ^^ its a problem if even the storyteller doesn't have clear ideas on the rules and the setting.

      On questions 1 to 4 you are right. You count your total successes once against the task at, and once again against the duration. This is true if:
      a) you do not do damage(or healing)
      b) you do not use the optipnal divided successes rule

      Regarding question 4)
      You are right, but you age 18 months also.

      5) yes this is possible (and even encouraged to have a chance at some difficult feats)
      Your difficulty can not deviate by more than 3 from the base difficulty.
      Example:
      You use Forces 2 to turn invisible while no sleeper is watching you.
      Base difficulty is 2 (Forces rank used) + 4 (vulgar without witnesses) = 6
      Now we assume you are under pressure and have to fast-cast (+1)
      We assume further that you have major trouble with your avatar (+3)
      There is a mage casting antimagick with 3 successes (+3)

      Final difficulty in this case would be 9 and not 6+1+3+3=13 because the cap is at +3 and 6+3=9.

      Now for some modifiers in your favour.

      Same situation as aboove plus
      - you fortunately have Avatar 5 and spend 5 quintessence (-5)
      - there is a node nearby with resonances perfectly with your spell (-3)
      - you use your unique and personal invisibility cloak (-2)

      Now we have 6+1+3+3-5-3-2=3

      Even if you would use abilities enhancing magick the difficulty wouldn't go lower.

      The Time Travel is indeed very blurry.

      Of the top of my head:
      If you use Time alone then you keep your conditions (wounds, knowledge, paradox) from before the spell.
      Other spheres come in handy if you don't want damage to travel with you.

      So, this Zen Master walks up to a hot dog stand and says: "Make me one with everything!"

                                              First it requires Time 3 to step oneself back. Second if they are going back the need Life 3 Mind 1 to recall the now future events they are leaving otherwise they take the same actions they took before. Additionally effects that turn back time are always vulgar and suffer an additional +3 difficulty (maximum difficulty being 10 or 9 and requiring additional successes if using the threshold option) additionally it is a Difficult Feat to step out of time requiring three successes; every two successes allow the Mage to rewind one turn however each turn rewound adds a layer (multiplier) of Paradox (ex going back 3 turns means getting 3 times the Paradox incurred by the effect and even on a success vulgar magic incurs 1 paradox which would then become 3 if the Mage went back 3 turns).

                                              If your mage successfully goes back (with memory of the future events through Life 3 Mind 1) time will pick up and follow its path as it did previously until such a point that the time traveling Mage does something then the others will react to the new events breaking the previous timeline. So if the friend died during those turns the Mage could try to do something different to prevent it (for example if the friend is shot and killed the Mage could go back and attack the shooters before they could take the shot).

                                              Now if you wanted to have the Mage 'reboot' the dead friend back to life by rewinding body to a state from turns ago it requires Life 5 Mind 4 Time 3 due to working the intricacies of patterns and consciousness and added three additional successes required over any others (so over the 3 for the feat difficulty and the 2+ for rewinding the appropriate number of turns) for affecting another target.

                                                          I used to play a Time mage and this sort of stuff happened almost constantly.
                                                          Under Time 3 it specifically mentions requiring Life 3 Mind 1 to recall the events of the future they just came from. M20 pg 523
                                                          Combining this Rank with other Spheres, the mage can affect other Patterns (Forces, Life, or Matter 2), cast Effects across distance and time (Correspondence 3), move back in time while recalling events from the future she just left (Life 3/ Mind 1), or even invoke multiple probabilities (Entropy 3). Again, such attempts are Paradox magnets with awful longterm consequences.
                                                          And relevant parts from M20 How Do You Do That pg 111
                                                          Time Loop: In this case, the mage turns time back upon itself within a certain area of effect. Story-wise, you might see Lee Ann cut a complex glyph in the air with her lotus sword; everything within the room stops moving except for Lee Ann herself, and then it all “rewinds” to a given point in time while Lee Ann changes the actions she would have taken otherwise. In game terms, time loops count as Magickal Feats of Time, with bigger reversals becoming bigger feats. Repeated time-loops suffer the Repeated Time Effects penalty described above.

                                                          Reboot: By adding Life 3 and Mind 1 to the Time 3 Time Warp, the mage can send herself back a few turns (or other increments of time) with full memories of what had happened before. In this case, the rest of the world moves exactly as it had moved before until and unless the mage changes the course of its actions. If, for example, Lee Ann goes back to stab a cultist before he fires at her friend Kyle, then the cultist (and Kyle) respond to her “rebooted” actions.
