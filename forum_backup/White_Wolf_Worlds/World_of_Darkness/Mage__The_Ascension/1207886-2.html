        Originally posted by Ambrosia View Post
        Also, how well your Mage is at postcongition depends on how the mage does it. Is it an Ecstatic relying on visions that might be hazy, or more symbolic than actually literal? That's a good way to spice up the postcongition process with small puzzles and things to make sense of, as an ST, instead of a literal picture.

        Is it an Etherite, using an Ether-disruption-calculator to reconstruct the motions of the Ether in the room in the past? They might get a sort of Daredevilish echo-location view of the room of the past - good enough to conclude what happened, but possibly not good enough to make out important details - what was the color of the clothes and skin of the person who was there? Is the detail of their face fine-grained enough? Was there something burning that might not show up on the this transtemporal echolocator? To interpret sound waves that happened back then might need an enhancement to the Ether-disruption-calculator that the Etherite still might need to devise...

        I highly encourage STs to actually make looking into the past an exciting thing that leads on a plot to actually get a clear image, instead of just serving it outright, if it is viable with the Mage's paradigm and tools. Not to avoid the image, but making it more fun and creative to get there.

        Sometimes it's good to look at roleplaying sessions like Episodes in a series. On tonights episode! Will Detective Justin Time manage to glimpse into the past of this gruesome place, before the killer an escape? STAY TUNED.
        Exactly. By allowing a PC to have Time 2, the Storyteller has tacitly accepted that pre-cognition and post-cognition are not only possible, but are active elements of the game. So the best way to deal with this is to work WITH that fact, and structure mysteries around the assumption that the players can look backwards to see what happened.

        This might happen through the quality of the post-cognition being colored by the character's Focus. Indeed, it certainly should be, to reinforce the themes intrinsic to the character. The Storyteller could also work to make the events a character could see NOT tell the whole story, even if the vision was clear.

        If a killer murdered someone, they may have moved the body, meaning the "crime scene" may not be where the murder happened. Alternatively, the killer could have been killing from afar (with, say, a sniper rifle or assassin). Or wearing a mask. This last one is especially likely, as any killer would have mundane reason to hide their identity beneath a mask, if they were planning to commit a crime in a location they couldn't fully control. Even if the Mage had Life 1, allowing them to know the killer's physiology, it may not be a person they are acquainted with, forcing them to do additional investigation. Or the killer might be one of a set of twins or triplets, whose bodies/life patterns were close enough to make it difficult for the mage to know for sure which one they were looking at.

        The point isn't to invalidate Time senses. The point is to assume from the outset that Time is one of the ways a mage character can gather information, and set the mystery up accordingly. No one source of info is going to unravel an entire mystery in a normal setting, after all, so a Mage mystery should be no different. The PC should be able to derive some clue from post-cognition, yes, but no more than they could with other senses, magickal or otherwise. They'll still need to do legwork, to find more pieces to the puzzle.

        Remember, also, that the identity of the killer, for example, doesn't need to BE the mystery. It's only part of the problem, and perhaps not even the most difficult to derive. The mystery might, instead, be in why the killer did it, and where they'll strike again. On another note, remember that "post-cognitive visions" won't stand up in court. If the PCs want to deliver the killer to justice, they'll either need to gather more concrete evidence, trick the killer into confessing, or get close enough to the killer to met out "street justice", depending on what the PCs want. Any one of which could be far more involved than just finding out who it is.

                    On the subject of Time, I'd also like to point out that, if conditions are right, post-cognitive senses can get a PC INTO trouble as much as solve their problems.

                    One idea is that a particularly knowledgeable assailant may know that certain Awakened investigators could look backwards through time. Knowing this, they may opt to stage a fake crime, for the benefit of post-cogs (and probably for mundane investigators too, since they could be misled by convincing fake evidence). Crimes committed among the Awakened are particularly prone to this, as Mages tend to tackle mysteries using their advanced senses. So creating a smokescreen or ruse could distract mage investigations, either letting the perpetrator get off scot free, or else buying them enough time to achieve some other objective.

                    A different idea I came up with involved subverting the assumption that a post-cog is SAFE in their past sight.

                    Say a mage - an Ecstatic, probably - tokes up around a murder scene to see what happened. In their cannibus vision, they see hazy scenes of a killer murdering the victim. The killer's identity is obscured by symbolism, but a good Enigmas roll could help decipher the riddle. More to the point, after the killer does the deed, he stops to perform a summoning ritual. Turns out the killer is an Infernalist, who is cashing in the murder to his patron for some reward. You know, as you do.

                    For this one, I chose the Prince of Nightmares (Book of Madness, p. 90-91) for the patron, but it could be anyone. Just that if the mage had Spirit 1 or Mind 1 (or neither, if the Prince decided to just Materialize), they could see the patron appear, whispering to the infernalist. So far, so standard. But in this case, I'd also silently roll the Prince's Perception/Gnosis. If he made it - despite the vision being of a past long gone - the Prince would look at the Ecstatic, with burning nightmare eyes.

                    What follows would be a tense scene, where the Prince of Nightmares - this ancient demonic entity - starts to approach the mage, his hand batting away the smoke that clouded the space between past and present. As if the Prince could notice the spy through time (I chose the Prince of Nightmares because a being of dreams/nightmares might act outside the regular flow of time), yet needed to approach for a closer look. Now the player is presented with a choice: keep watching in the hopes of gathering info, or cut off the Effect before the Prince got a good look at the character?

                    At the very least, the infernalist's patron is aware of someone who knows about them, and will doubtless inform his pawn of the danger. A certain amount of the element of surprise is lost to the PCs. If the Ecstatic remains too long, the Prince might see who they really are. This might involve attacking or just touching the character through the mists of time, dealing bashing damage either way. Or it might result in the Ecstatic finding the Prince has taken an interest, and starts visiting the mage in their own dreams. The demon comes bearing offers of power, knowledge, or worldly wealth, if the character would only enter into a pact...

                                            Originally posted by PixelPuzzler View Post
                                            So time 2 gets mentioned a lot, but what can you do with time 3 that isn't the extremely well known "add or remove extra actions" ability, that is honestly probably best served as being a wonder for you, as it'll cost less for similar or greater effect depending on how you invest?
                                            The ability to speed or slow targets isn't limited to the caster (or to their enemy's person). It can also be used on objects. On machines, it can even be coincidental. After all, it's less noticeable if a mechanical device functions faster or slower than it normally should, at least compared to organic creatures.

                                            For instance, I can totally see a Virtual Adept rote built around "overclocking" their computers by selectively surrounding them with a field of accelerated time, making them process information far faster. Reversing that, by contrast, could curse an enemy's computer with unbearable slowdown.

                                            It could work for simpler machines, like guns. A normal pistol or revolver could fire a three shot burst in one turn. Instead of giving yourself more actions, you could try accelerating the time specifically on the gun, making it's three shot burst into a six shot burst. Only people who are REALLY familiar with firearms should be able to notice that it's firing faster than it should. And that could just come down to some home modifications.


                                            Here's another thing: Time 3 doesn't just affect the rate of time's passage, but also it's order. Time 3 can be used to turn time back, by individual turns. Which can be helpful for undoing massive damage to oneself, one's allies, broken objects, expended ammunition (with the help of Correspondence and Matter, probably), recently alerted guards, explosions, etc.

                                            Sure, it's Vulgar as hell. But it can be really handy if there's an occurrence you'd rather didn't occur. Even something as simple as saying the wrong thing in a conversation.

And honestly, at that point you could simply perform a horribly vulgar Life 3 effect (3 + 1/2 + 1) and attempt to heal the damage itself.

                                                If you just wanted to push your current Pattern back in time a round or two, that would only require Time 3, but it would be equally difficult.
