    The Babel Center and Technocrats

    10-25-2016, 04:32 PM
    I got my copy of Hammer & Klaive yesterday and although the book is for W:TA, there was one Legendary Fetish that I thought had great campaign potential (and not just as a crossover). The Babel Center is a spirit that was given the body of colossal tower made of steel and glass in ancient Rome by a Glass Walker (they didn't have a name back then) who was influenced by an entity called,The Machine. If the rumors are correct, the tower has bonded itself to a skyscraper in the real world and is currently look for "it's mate". If found, it serves as a lightning rod where the user(s) can sense, spy and appear in any part of the Umbra. As well as bring in spirits (if they're brave/stupid enough), travel to different locations and if the user(s) aren't careful, be struck with bouts of insanity.

    The reason I bring this up is that I couldn't help thinking how this would catch the eye of any techno-based Mages or Technocrats in general. I think any Technocrat could respect the Babel Center's beauty as a technological marvel (especially for its time) and the powers it can grant once some form of mastery can be had. It also got me thinking of a campaign idea where a group of high-powered Technocrats (one member for each Convention) become seduced by the fetish and desire to bring its "mate" forth by creating it themselves.

    The Progenitor: She was the first to (accidentally) find the Babel Center and befriend it. She is the one with closest connection and believes the "bad year" it mentioned could be Y2K and that the reason the mate can't be found is because it hasn't been created yet.

    The NWO Member: Being to most charismatic, his job is to convince the other Technocrats that using the tower is not only a good idea, but building its mate as well (or hiding this fact if needed).

    The Syndicate: He's here to provide cash, property and resources needed to build the mate.

    Interation Xer: Being an architect before her Awakening, she will design and help overlook what will be her "greatest masterpiece".

    Void Engineer: While miffed at first that the Progenitor found this fetish first, he's gotten over it and sees what greatness this thing can bring to his Convention's new battle and the Technocracy as a whole. He also secretly hopes this project is what will bring all the conventions together and hopefully prevent the possible civil war that has been looming.

    I just couldn't help getting a campaign idea for this fetish. The obvious would be the Garou/Bete or Mages trying to stop them. Or maybe have the PCs be the five who want to build it (or as their helpers). It's a shame I have no one to run such a game, but what do you think of such ideas?

      The Babel Center

      NOTE: The Babel Center is mentioned in the gamebook, "Hammer & Klaive", for Werewolf: The Apocalypse. (p. 118-119) Other than that, I hope you like this campaign idea.

      History: All her life, Molly Cole felt like she was just "in the way". Whether it was being one of the middle children in her family of ten, being a C-average student who was just ignored among the achievers and beautiful people in school and as a Technocrat, lost in a sea of geniuses. She was too soft-hearted to do the dirtier work in the NWO, not clever enough to be in the Syndicate, her clumsy streak made her useless to the Void Engineers' secret mission and the less said about the explosion she caused in her temporary stint with Interation X the better. So out of pity, the Progenitors took her in and stuck her with minor jobs where she could do the least amount of damage. While Molly understood why they did this, it convinced her that she'd be barely useful for the rest of her life.

      Thankfully, all it took was one botched attempt to enter the Umbra that landed her in the realm of the Babel Center. Despite the visions and the chills she felt from feeling bits of the Umbra's strangest realms, Molly bravely kept going until she came across a mighty, towering structure made of steel and glass. Awed by the Babel Center's majesty, Molly innocently put her right hand to the structure and said, "Your steel is pretty." After many millennia of being forgotten, this woke something in the ancient wonder and it thanked her in return.

      The two spent hours talking as the Babel Center told her of its beginning as a spirit connected to an entity known as The Machine, the Werewolf who crafted its body in the Umbra, its warning of the real world's tools being used against them and it's desire to find "it's mate". Over the weeks, Molly would visit the Babel Center, learning its powers and building a powerful bond with it. In time, Molly learned that she wasn't as useless and untalented as she thought; she just happened to convince herself that she was. With her new self-confidence, she contacted one of the best Void Engineers (Malcolm Bello) in her hometown of Raleigh, North Carolina, and showed him the Babel Center.

      Impressed by the building's power and Molly's connection to it, Malcolm agreed to help her gather the top players in the other conventions in town and see what they can do to bring the Babel Center to its full potential. With the charismatic NWO leader, Gian Lawler; minor online Syndicate mogul, Derrick Summers; and the Interation X architect, Leah Stone, they have formed a Amalgam called, The Five. Together, they have become Raleigh's biggest force be reckoned with.

      Current Status: After spending millennia semi-dormant and alone, the Babel Center desires nothing more than to spread its wonder and potential to the world. By empowering The Five, they can use it to give the Technocracy an advantage the others would never see coming. All they have to do is learn to control its power, keep the occasional wandering spirit at bay and find the Babel Center's "mate". They all know the great risk they are taking and that the Babel Center is more a neutral entity with a limited understanding of the outside world (given its state). Yet, the hopes and desires that drive each member, fuels them on.

      Leah believes that the reason the mate hasn't been found is because it hasn't been created yet. So The Five have begun their biggest challenge to build the Babel Center's mate in the Umbra.

      ----

      I plan to do the bios for The Five and explain things such as what Molly's place with the Progenitors is like now and some story notes.

          The Campaign: The first thing the ST must consider is how much time has passed since Molly first met the Babel Center. This choice will determine how powerful The Five have become, how much use they have gotten out of their new ally and how far they've gotten in their plan to build the mate.

          Two Months: Molly has gotten the basics down on how the Babel Center works and what it can do. She has managed to convince Malcolm to meet it and see what they can do with this new sentient Wonder. They have yet to talk with the other future members of The Five about this.
          PC Involvement: It helps if you at least introduce Molly earlier in the game as a minor character who the PCs have some connection with, no matter how small. That way, she'll be more likely to ask the party to help her before or instead of Malcolm. Or you could go the other way by having Malcolm not being interested in Molly's discovery and have the PCs go in his place.

          Play up on how intimidating, wondrous and oddly beautiful the Babel Center is when the PCs first encounter it. Will they see the potential that the Babel Center can bring the Technocratic cause or think that Molly is playing with fire and that both her and the tower should be "taken care of"? Just remember that if the party tries to do anything to hurt her, the Babel Center's wrath will be great.

          If Malcolm is with the party, he will side with Molly (though he can be talked out of it). This is mainly due to the Babel Center's powers being a valuable tool against his Convention's enemies. He will be vague about which enemies he's talking about to the party and will allude to the Nephandi. If there are any PCs that are fellow Void Engineers, he'll be more honest with those members and talk about the Threat Null. If the PCs agree to join Molly, there will be quite a bit of work to do such as keeping the few roaming spirits under control, experimenting with the Wonder's powers, learning its past and eventually getting Gian, Derrick and Leah's support.

          ---
          I'm not the biggest expert on the Technocracy so feel free to fill anything in.

              Six Months: Molly and Malcolm have managed to convince Gian, Derrick and Leah to help them out and have now formed The Five. The new members have met the Babel Center and thanks to Molly's charming enthusiasm, all three of them have faith that aiding her and Malcolm is the right thing. Leah has been starting to break out of her depression after the Avatar Storm destroyed many of her works and killed her mentor and friends. Derrick secretly hopes that with this inter-Convention project will be what can bring the Conventions together and at least stall the possible Technocratic Civil War. While Gian wants to make the NWO proud, he also wants to see Leah smile once again (and maybe they can rebuild their courtship) even more.

              While problems with the spirits have started to calm down a tad, there is a lot of work that needs to be done and Malcolm is quite excited to take it on. As for Molly, her exposure with the Babel Center is starting to get a little "addicting" to the point she is a bit prone to panic attacks when she spends more than two weeks away from her friend.

              PC Involvement: This is where the PCs can be sent to act as go-betweens for The Five and their Conventions. If they're loyal to The Five, this is the chance for lower-level PCs or those that have just moved into Raleigh to gain some quick recognition and a chance to move up. Yet, there is the chance that the PCs could back-stab The Five and take their hand-earned knowledge for themselves, but remember, they and the Babel Center's wrath will be great. If the PCs betray The Five or screw-up enough that they convince the rest of the Technocracy to destroy the Babel Center or take it away, Molly will become suicidal. So unless the Technocracy lets her still work with the tower, she go to any length to die.

              Dread Possibility: If Molly manages to kill herself, she will return as a spirit (or a Wraith if you want to do a crossover) that will haunt the Babel Center or what's left of it. She might even try to get the Mages to help her get her revenge.

                  One Year: The Five's Conventions are not going to let such a powerful Wonder pass by and have given the Amalgam a chance to run their experiments. They have let some outsider diplomats help out in their studies to keep up a good image with the rest of the Technocracy, but over all, The Five call the shots. The local Mage and Garou population are starting to get wind of The Five and their new Wonder.

                  Leah: Leah is beginning to get her second wind and has gone back to designing stuff again thanks to her muse, The Babel Center. The depression still lingers, but she is gradually breaking out of it.
                  Gian: Gian has been as busy as ever handling the negotiations between his Amalgam and the rest of the Technocracy. While there are small, nagging moments of doubt, the fact that Leah is feeling better makes it all worth it.
                  Derrick: Derrick handles the more local negotiations with the Technocrats and trying to plan what to do about the supernaturals' growing interest in The Babel Center.
                  Malcolm: Oddly, Malcolm has become interested in the Glass Walkers hand in the creation of the Babel Center and has been studying up on his Werewolf lore. With his constant sense of adventure, he has been trying to collect information on the local Glass Walker population.
                  Molly: Molly is the official overseer of any outsider Technocrats who visits and studies the Babel Center in the Umbra. Lately, it has been been dropping hints of wanting to find its Mate. Her outside anxiety has lowered her tolerance down to one week before it hits.

                  PC Involvement: The PCs can either be loyalists to The Five who have aligned themselves to working for one or two of the members or not. For extra fun, the party can be secretly manipulated by the Nephandi who have snuck into the Tehcnocracy to destroy The Five's work. And depending on how big and powerful the ST wants the Nephandi to be, tragedy could arise if the PCs don't wise up.

                      Three Years: While things have gotten a lot better between The Five and the Technocracy, the Amalgam has a whole new problem on their hands: the other supernaturals of Raleigh. The Technocracy will offer a little help, they believe that The Five and their allies can handle this. Thankfully, the local Vampire population doesn't care as long as the The Five can keep any skirmishes from getting too big, but the rest of them are starting to get nervous.

                      The other Mages want the Babel Center for themselves in fear of what the Technocracy could do against them. Since the Technocracy are pretty much serving the Weaver and have their hands on one of their legendary Fetishes, the Garou want The Five destroyed. While the Wraiths and Hunters (both regular and Imbued) are pretty much leaving this mess alone unless The Five do anything to hurt them or the Mortals.

                      Leah: She's been playing around with a theory that the reason they can't find the Babel Center hasn't found its Mate is because it hasn't been created yet. So far, she has been sketching up some designs for the Mate's Umbral and real world counterpart (which will be a tall statue to avoid any Paradox backlashes and save some money). Unfortunately, the skirmishes have been slowing her progress down.
                      Gian: Gian knows that you can't make an omelette without breaking a few eggs and wants to get these skirmishes done away with as soon as possible. He knows that he can't totally relay on the rest of the Technocracy to solve his problems if he wants to prove that The Five are not expendable. At least seeing Leah smiling again makes things tolerable and as soon as things start easing up, he plans to ask her to give their relationship a second chance.
                      Derrick: Knowing that things are getting testy with the other supernaturals, Derrick has been able to convince a number of Raleigh's Technocrats to side with The Five. He finds this to be a wonderful opportunity to reach the message of Convention unity in hopes of preventing the civil war he so fears.
                      Malcolm: Despite having almost gotten caught by the Garou, Malcolm has discovered plenty of information on the local creatures, including the name of the Babel Center's creator, Oppius Memmius Gala. As the skirmishes get worse, both he and Gian will takes some extreme measures by kidnapping the estranged son of one of the most powerful Mages in town.
                      Molly: Molly is pretty much now just a Progenitor in name only. With her outside anxiety no being knocked down to five days, the Babel Center's realm is now a second home. Knowing that many of Raleigh's supernaturals can enter the Umbra, her and the Babel Center have been trying to convince the local spirits (as rare as they are) to aid in defending it.

                      PC Involvement: The tensions with the other supernaturals is the most obvious way to bring the PCs in. The PC can align themselves to one of The Five as a group or separately, yet be working for a common goal. And if your group manages to survive, there's plenty of opportunity to move up.
                      NOTE: Unless the ST wants to, she does not have to include every supernatural group in Raleigh for the game to run properly.

While the messes left behind still linger, things are starting to look up for them. But, for how long? Can they keep their pseudo-leadership of Raleigh's Technocrats? He and Leah's second romantic attempt has been going well and he hopes to propose to her soon, him and Derrick have managed to bury the hatchet when he found out Derrick only wanted to bring the Conventions together (at first, Gian thought he only joined out of boredom) and the skirmishes are finally over. His unity talks, however, are full of mixed results by his fellow Technocrats.

                          Molly: Other than her anxiety tolerance going down to three days, Molly senses there is something different growing inside her. With the steady recognition that the Babel Center is getting from the Sleepers (via the statue), new worlds of knowledge and power fill her head. She is a little scared to tell the others about this at the moment.

                          PC Involvement: The PCs will be helping out with "clean-up duty" to make sure the defeated Supernaturals don't come back. (Especially with the new statue and the tourist attraction status it is gaining.) A great climax is about to come and whether they want to help or hinder it, is something not to be taken lightly.

                              Seven Years: To the rest of the Technocracy, the Mate is an experiment in creating artificial Babel Centers that can expand their power base. While there is truth to this claim, The Five know there is more at stake than they let on. They know that the local Mages and Garou have formed an alliance and will get their revenge soon, even if they have to destroy the Babel Center, itself.

                              The other reason The Five are so adamant on getting the Mate completed, is the hope that this will awaken even more of the Babel Center's powers, leading to the new era that the Technocracy has always dreamed of. While the other members only humored Molly and were more interested what they could get out of it, they've come to see how much the Babel Center has blossomed into something greater. There is a beautiful potential that can hopefully be brought forth or a great disaster that could doom them all. For this is the endgame and risks have to be made.

                              Leah: Knowing that the Mate's Umbral counter-part is nearly done, Leah is both excited, yet nervous of what could come next. Her greatest fear is to see her life's work and loved ones get destroyed all over again. (Even more so, now that she's four months pregnant.) So she's been starting to pull favors and lower her payment rates for projects in hopes of getting extra help in protecting the Mate both in and out of the Umbra.
                              Gian: Despite The Five being the most prominent group of Supernaturals in Raleigh, Gian knows that the head is heavy for he who wears the crown. He hopes that the Mate's completion will ease the Amalgam's problems. He won't have to worry about his wife ever breaking down again, all their hard work going down hill or Molly's own "imbalances". With this hope, he will fight tooth and nail to make sure it comes true.
                              Derrick: It's a lesser known fact that Derrick has had occasional premonition dream and in the last two years, they've been happening more often. (Just like the vision he had of the disastrous Technocratic Civil War that drove him to join The Five.) These ones are a mixed bag of warnings and hopes from a glowing light of hope emitting from the Babel Center and its Mate to being slaughter by hordes of frenzied Garou. Though it pains him to do so, he has cut down on his unity talks and given more responsibility of his internet empire to his most loyal employee.
                              Malcolm: Malcolm is the sane one in all of this. All this work is just weighing down on everyone else, making them edgy. So as soon as the Mate and the ritual needed to activate it becomes a proven success, everything will be back to normal. It's just the reality deviants who are getting in the way and must be stopped at all costs before his friends lose it.
                              Molly: As worried as she is for her friends and the fact that her anxiety is now been reduced to 24 hours, Molly is the happiest and most optimistic out of all of them. Like a Chorister with The One, she has an unlimited faith in the Babel Center and her destiny that things will turn out for the better. Even if pain and tears have to be shed.

                                  PC Involvement: Since this is the big end game, there are many ways that this can be played out. Will the PCs be loyal to The Five and want to help bring their dream to life or end it to save them from themselves? Or perhaps they secret pawns of Nephandi-controlled branch of the Technocracy? Whatever path the PCs take, they must know that all choices are final and that it is a matter of life or death as the Mages and Garou will not be merciful.

                                  Dread Possibility: Given Molly's high Destiny score, this could mean several things...
                                  -It Was Me All Along: Her ability to befriend and awaken the Babel Center, the separation anxiety, her growing enlightenment...Molly was the Babel Center's Mate this whole time. When her enemies attack and things look at their bleakest, she will tear off a piece of the Mate's body, scream out her love for the Babel Center and stab herself with it (she has one round before she bleeds to death). Her spirit will enter the Mate's body, allowing her to use the Babel Center's abilities.
                                  Granted, any enemies still have the chance to heal Molly's body or catch her spirit before she can reach her destiny. If she can pull this off, she will turn into an enlightened spirit that will aid her allies in battle. Maybe this bring the happy or at least bittersweet ending The Five want (if they and the PCs can keep their enemies from doing them in), but a lot of healing will need still need to be done.
                                  -The Doom-Bringer: Molly's destiny was not to build, but destroy. As the Babel Center said, there would be a time where man's tools would turn against them and in this case, it was the Wonder, itself, that's the tool. For she is the Pandora that unknowingly unleashed the troubles into this world.
                                  The Babel Center will fall and take a chunk of those that relied on its powers with it. (Raleigh will also suffer some kind of "natural disaster" as a result.) While the PCs may survive the encounter, The Five and many others will not.

                                  Other Options: There are other ways to play this campaign. (The ST could even start earlier by having the PCs discover the Babel Center before or at the same time Molly does.)
                                  Mage Campaign: You can play it through the eyes of the Mages as they try to stop The Five from getting too powerful as the years go by. Or some particularly crafty PCs could convince Molly (at the two month mark or sooner) that the Technocracy never really cared for her and that she and the Babel Center would be in better hands if she were to join them.
                                  Werewolf Campaign: Minus the possibility of getting Molly to join (many Garou wouldn't be not on associating with a servant of the Weaver), a Werewolf campaign can be played in a similar light as a Mage one. It could also be a chance to unite some of the other local Changing Breeds for a temporary truce to stop a bigger threat and maybe build some bridges as a result.

                                      THE FIVE

                                      Molly Cole
                                      Bio: See history.
                                      Appearance: Molly is a mildly pretty eighteen-year-old white girl with long black hair that reaches her nipples, gray eyes and true girl-next-door air to her. She's the kind of girl that people might pass by at first, but once you get to really know her, she sticks with you.

                                      After the one year mark, she starts looking paler due to spending so much time in the Umbra. Though her skin eventually becomes a porcelain doll white after experiencing a Paradox backlash (during year three) while helping The Five defend their base in the outside world from a Mage attack.

                                      Role-Playing Notes: At the start of the adventure, you're an insecure person who has little self-confidence due to not really being able to fit in with the rest of the Technocrats. Bless your heart, you try to overcome your clumsiness and being totally average average, but it just seems like it's not enough in your eyes. Despite being tossed aside, you see the good in others and treat people with kindness and empathy. (The very traits that earned the Babel Center's trust.)

                                      As time goes by, you begin to gain more self-confidence and realize that part of your issues is due to convincing yourself that you suck. You acknowledge that you is the weakest and least experienced of The Five, but with them and the Babel Center, you know you are strong. For you are the heart and glue that holds the Amalgam together with your infectious hope, non-judgemental treatment of others and innocent charm. Just don't be a Pollyanna about it, because you can feel frustration, anger and despair just like anyone else.

                                      As for the anxiety, you're not sure where it comes from. Some of your fellow Progenitors believe it might be due to a backlash you experienced from your botched trip that lead you to the Babel Center or your prolonged exposure its realm. (It functions a lot like anxiety does for Sleepers by making you feel nervous and antsy, like you're crawling the walls.) You don't like having it and have agreed to take medication to help slow down its effects, but over time you realize that the Babel Center's realm is where you truly belong and no longer fights it.

                                      Weak Point: Tragically, all of Molly's positive traits are also her biggest downfall. Even as she gets older, she still has a naive streak that can be exploited and used against her. If the other members of The Five aren't there to balance this out, she could get into trouble. She loves her fellow members and the Babel Center as friends and if something terrible were to happen to any of them, she'll take it really hard. If not snap, if it's the Babel Center. This will only get worse as she develops a more religious take on her devotion to the Babel Center and her Amalgam's mission.

                                          Malcolm Bello
                                          Bio: Malcolm is what you'd call a pedigree. Hailing from a long line of Void Engineers that could trace their lineage back to cusp of the Order of Reason, Malcolm was fed the amazing stories of his family and Convention. Along with being groomed from childhood to be a faithful member, he developed a very romanticized view of the Convention and dreamed of exploring the far reaches of the Umbra and participating in epic discoveries like his ancestors.

                                          So when his powers manifested at fourteen, Malcolm put his heart into his training so he could become an Explorer as soon as possible. By the time he was able to make it, his dreams began cracking under the weight of reality. The very one that was turning his Convention into a more bleak and militaristic, no thanks to the Threat Null.

                                          Though he lived a charmed and a bit spoiled upbringing, Malcolm refused to let this get him down and tried to focus on the happier things in life like building a social life among the sleepers. He played the role of the popular kid who charmed people with his athletic skills in tack and field, wit and talent for oral storytelling (a talent that the Bello family are also famous for). Despite how much he enjoyed it, there was still something missing until he met the Babel Center. Not only was it the great discovery he dreamed of, but a possible way to destroy the Threat Null (or at least score a good hit against it).

                                          While Derrick and Gian do the talking and mediating, Malcolm is the one that goes the extra mile to do the side quests (getting information on the Glass Walkers, getting the head Mage's son, getting special materials for the Babel Center, etc) that are needed to keep The Five's agenda going.

                                          Appearance: Malcolm starts out the setting at the age of 18 (he's about 14 weeks older than Molly). His southern African heritage clearly shows with his very dark brown skin and facial features. His hair kept in five-inch long dreads, but as he gets older, he will cut his hair shorter and grow his facial hair out a bit. He likes to look good and tries to keep up with the latest trends, leaning towards modern hip-hop.

                                          Role-Playing Notes: Being the prodigy of a proud lineage of multi-talented Technocrats, has given you a lot of pride for good and for ill. At your best, you are confident, ambitious and hard-working and loyal to the Technocratic cause. At your worst, you are spoiled, egotistical and have a hard time accepting things when outside forces are in the way of achieving your dreams.You are a generally the cool, friendly guy among the Sleepers and those you like or want to impress, but tend to be more lukewarm towards those who that don't earn your respect. There was a time you could've been a bit nicer to Molly when she was starting out (given that you saw her as barely useful doof) and you have apologized for it. Thankfully, you will grow-up a bit when the years go by.

                                          When it comes to your Technocratic work, your strong spirit of adventure and ambition make you a trust-worthy person who can get things done, even if you have to do some uglier things (like kidnapping) for the greater good. Because of these traits, you are adored by your family (and vice-versa) and if allowed to talk about the subject you are encyclopedia of knowledge on your linage and Convention.

                                          Weak Point: Despite his confidence, the more Malcolm is denied his dreams (especially if the opposition has a good chance of succeeding), the more desperate he gets. Hence why he becomes more driven to stop the local Mages, Threat Null and Garou as the years go by. You just don't stomp on his hopes, especially when he and The Five are so close to completing them. Targeting his family is also a way to get to him, given his love and pride towards them.

                                              Gian Lawler
                                              Bio: You know that really serious kid that had to make sure his socks were sorted by color and length? Gian was that tyke. He just seemed a little more mature, more orderly and studious than t, which earned the annoyance of many of the other students. Thankfully, his willingness to tutor some of the dumber kids and his talent for spin doctoring his friends out of trouble earned him a few less wedgies than other kids like him.

                                              Gian just kept his head up and took pride in his family's motto of hard work and dedication that lifted them from the poverty of their Irish ancestors to upper class stockbrokers they are today. It got him through high school and earned him top marks at Yale, but there seemed to be something missing. Something that could make a difference beyond his fancy law degree. His wish was answered when a high profile lawyer named, Maxwell Stone, took interest in him. Sensing there was potential in the young man and how he could benefit the NWO as a Sleeper ally, he took Gian under his wing.

                                              For the next two years, Gian's potential flourished due to Maxwell's guidance and the allure of his daughter, Leah. She was everything he wanted in a woman: smart, pretty, dedicated and talented. Just like any regular male Sleeper, Gian was beneath her notice as she gave her romantic and sexual interest to fellow Technocrats and high-class Mortals. This only drove him to try harder.

                                              Eventually, his Technocratic powers manifested and his hard work began to pay off even more. He would eventually raise among the ranks of the NWO and eventually catch some of Leah's attention (she found his dedication endearing). Sure, she considered him one of her minor boyfriends, but it was good enough for him. Everything seemed fine until four years later when the Avatar Storm hit and killed his mentor during the process. While he could handle the loss, Leah was hit even harder, when the disaster killed her father and favorite boyfriends before it nearly tore her Enlightened Genius out of her body and almost broke her spine.

                                              So Gian put aside any romantic pursuits to let her heal and focused more on his Convention and their goals. While to took time for Leah to snap out of her catatonic state and start working again, it was like a chunk of her was missing as she went through the actions. Then a miracle came when Malcolm showed her the Babel Center and her creative spark returned. It was more than just Leah's second wind that earned Gian's help, but it also the potential that the Wonder itself.

                                              Appearance: Gian is a plain-looking white man in his mid 30s who always looks neat and organized. His black hair is cut short and he dresses professionally as if everyday is a day at the office. (The loosest he get is business casual.) He has used his unremarkable appearance to his advantage to avoid detection and appear more trustworthy.

                                              Role-Playing Notes: A perfectionist at heart, you've always taken things seriously and with a lot of dedication. Life to you is a series of tasks and missions that help you make sense of things, even more so after learning about how chaotic the World of Darkness really is. As much as you don't like to do or admit it, you're not afraid to get your hands dirty or something questionable for the greater good. So that's the reason why Derrick's lackadaisical attitude created some tension at first (believing that Derrick cared more about the money and attention he'd get than the bigger picture). You will eventually start liking him more when you find out what his real plans are.

                                              In your own way, you are a hopeful soul that keeps holding on to the more positive potential outcomes to your activities. You're still realistic about things (you accepted that pre-Avatar Storm Leah will be a romantic free-spirit).

                                              Weak Point: While anything really bad happening to Leah will hit Gian hard, it'll take more than that to make him totally fall. Having worked so hard on the Babel Center and keeping Raleigh from falling into the Garou and Mages' hands, it would be tragic if something were to unravel all that work...

                                                  Derrick Summers
                                                  Bio:                                                   He may not seem like it, but Derrick's Genius has always been an unconventional one, as if his body was constantly focusing on a great work as his mind was trying to see something deeper. Odd strokes of inspiration would constantly hit him like the time he taped his hot teenage sister in the shower and sold the tapes to his fellow 6th-graders, creative a underground tabloid in high school or convincing his neighbor at the age of ten to hire him to spy on his cheating wife for less money that it would to get a private investigator. It wasn't just money that drove Derrick, but the varied thrills from mischief to discovery to the love of the bizarre. Not unlike the strange visions and dreams that also guided him.

                                                  But he did not fear them, for his ex-shaman grandmother would assure him that these were a blessed gift and to listen to their guidance. Thanks to this advice, Derrick gained the confidence and courage to take advantage of the early days of internet videos and create his own, focusing on the bizarre and occasionally pathetic things he saw in life. His first break came when he did a mini-documentary on a homeless weirdo (Mr. Twinkles) who would wear a tutu and dance in the town square with his ratty gymnast ribbon. As time went on, Derrick would create, The Portal of Insanity, which served as a hub for laughing at losers on the web, looks into quirky things (like Amish baseball and pumpkin chucking), low-brow comedy and Mr. Twinkles' performances. As the site started to gain a bit of popularity, his visions started getting more chaotic and with his grandma being long dead, Derrick was at a loss for what to do until Mr. Twinkles found and calmed him down. He assured Derrick that it was just a new power that was arising from him and that he should go where the visions tell him to. Following this strange moment of sanity from Mr. Twinkles, he took his advice and eventually found the Technocracy and was welcomed as a member of the Syndicate. (Mr. Twinkles died in his sleep the next day from heart failure.)

                                                  Derrick came to enjoy the power, money and creative potential his new powers and Convention brought, but there was something off. The looking down on religious Technocrats, certain ideas being shot down like dreams and visions, the bickering among the Conventions and more focus on control than empowerment. Still wanting to keep on good terms, he kept his mouth shut. When the Avatar Storm was halfway through, those dreams came again, bursting from his repressed feelings as he could feel his Genius screaming at him to stop being a coward and not be ashamed of thinking for himself. For if he were to do nothing, the dreams he had of the Conventions warring with each other would come true in the worst way possible. On the final day, he saw his grandma and Mr. Twinkles appear to him in one last dream where they showed him a massive tower made of steel and glass that would be the key to bringing peace to the Technocracy. To make the first step, all he had to do is move him and his business to Raleigh and "trust the girl with the long, black hair". (Besides, the local Marauder activity was starting to make things a little unbearable for the Technocrats in his old city.)

                                                  Appearance: Now in his early 30s, Derrick is an attractive white man with a hint of flair in his dark brown eyes. Though his ability to tan easily and quarter-Mexican heritage has occasionally caused some people to mistake him for Hispanic. His short, black hair is stylishly cut, just like his eyebrows and van dyke beard. Then again, a lot about him is stylish, from his clothes to his body language. After all, you need to please the eye to get them to warm up to you.

                                                  Role-Playing Notes: For a Syndicate, you are an odd man of contradictions: a net entrepreneur with the soul of a vision-seeker. Don't get you wrong, you enjoy playing the market, making a profit and sharing your creations with the masses, but after your storm dreams (that's what you call them), just can't ignore what they have to say anymore no matter how unscientific it is. So that's why you are so interested in the Babel Center and willing to take the risks needed to bring The Five's dreams to life. Granted, you made it seem like you were only interested in the money and prestige you could make at first. (You weren't going to let your guard down that easily.)

                                                  One of the main things you've learned in your life in the entertainment industry is prestige. You please the eye, you make them feel comfortable and amused and they'll play into your hand. That is why you look your best, have a friendly demeanor and a plan going on in your mind. Play to their interests, know what they want, show that you care and provide it. Just maybe if they earn your trust, prove themselves to have some kind of sincerity and have pure intentions (like The Five will eventually show), you can let your guard down and share your real feelings with them.

                                                  Weak Point: Derrick knows he's taking a huge risk trusting the outside forces so much (let alone acknowledging them) and if The Five's plans fall apart, he could hit his head the hardest with not only all the money and resources he's been funneling into this thing, but his own beliefs. He worries there will be a time where he will not be able to talk his way out, which might lead him into looking into things would make him look even more like an outsider.

                                                  Dread Possibility: There is the possibility that Derrick's grandma was once a hedge mage who had minor ties to the Dreamspeakers. She was hoping that Derrick could become the Mage that she could never be and her hints were used to steer him to join that Tradition. Whether she is respects the decisions he has made in life or leading him to The Babel Center (assuming that wasn't just his Genius taking her form) was a way to bring doom to the Technocracy for taking her grandson away from her is up to the ST.

                                                      Leah Stone
                                                      Bio:                                                       It's a little known fact outside the Technocracy that North Carolina is known for being the home of two of the faction's most prominent families. One of them being the Bellos and the other being the Stones. While the Bello family has been with the Technocracy 250 years longer, the Stones are better known among the Sleepers for their sponsoring of growing businesses, intellectuals and artists as well as their own technological intellect (leading many to join Interation X). When Leah was born, her father, Maxwell, knew that he had a little "prodigy princess" on his hands.

                                                      Having lost her mother at one-year-old, Maxwell doted on his child genius daughter, making sure she had the best schooling, prettiest things and the most loving upbringing a father could provide. Even more so when her enlightened Genius kicked in at the age of 9, making life even more of a wonder as he showed her the magnificence of what Interation X could do. From this exposure, Leah developed a love of drafting (especially vehicles and to a lesser extent, buildings) and was privately tutored to develop and control her powers until she was allowed to go to college at the age of eleven, grad school at 15 and start freelance design work at 18. As a result it lead to an unconventional view, since she could breeze through life, she developed a free spirit that took change of any goal in path, building amazing, fancy things for the Technocracy and participating in brief romantic and later sexual (when she turned 17) flings. Sure she hated dumbing down her stuff for the Sleepers, but to her they were just playmates and things appease in return for better things. It's not that she was sociopathic about it (she cared about the ones she liked), it was just that she had problems relating to them at times due to her intellect and abilities. It was just easier to keep them at a casual distance.

                                                      That all changed when the Avatar Storm hit while she, her father and her favorite boyfriends were trying out the new void cruiser she designed in the Umbra. Not even her intellect and powers could save the crew, her favorite new creation and her loved ones from getting ripped apart by the storm. The hardest was witnessing her beloved father suffering a fatal heart attack when his Genius was obliterated while he was trying to make an emergency landing. Despite this, fate still had plenty of cruel tricks to play upon her by nearly ripping out her own Genius out of her body and almost shattering her spine when the cruiser crashed.

                                                      Though the rescue team was able to get her out in time and save her from being paralyzed for the rest of her life, the shock still remained. For the first month she was a catatonic mess and though she got better thanks to Gian and her fellow Technocrats, something changed. Gone was the free-spirited woman who breezed through life, instead, she became a quieter, more thoughtful person who went through the motions of her work and constantly contemplated her place in life. In a painful way, it forced her to grow-up, but at a powerful cost that left her weary. When she laid eyes on the Babel Center, a second wind emerged.

                                                      Appearance: Leah is a 30-year-old white woman with curly, dirty blond hair that goes to her shoulders. While her sultry beauty remains, it feels like there is a light that was snuffed out (but will start to glow again as the game goes on). She now dresses comfortably, yet femininely (and will get a little more fancy through the years).

                                                      Role-Playing Notes: You are a broken soul that is slowly gaining yourself back. Sure you won't be your pre-Avatar Storm self, you've grown-up and have gained more understanding towards others. (You can thank that "Silly Sleeper" Gian for that.) The Babel Center has awakened something in you; a love of challenge and a sense of wonder. Now that that's happened, you are making up for lost time by pouring your heart into making a grand masterpiece (the Mate) that will bring a great difference to both Technocrat and Sleep alike.

                                                      As for your love life, you're too busy getting your life together to complicate it with flings. So maybe settling down might be a better focused goal for now.

                                                      Weak Point: Though Leah is gradually becoming a stronger person again, if things were to fall again, she might not recover. (As the years go by, there's more to lose.) She's placing a lot of faith in her project, Gian, her Amalgam and later, unborn child to give her the answers she needs. She can handle losing one, but any more could cause some real problems.

                                                          Babel Center
                                                          Legendary Wonder/Fetish

                                                          Appearance: Standing at a 180 feet, the Babel Center is a miracle of architecture composed of ancient steel and glass. Taking the shape of a tower, sheets of glass and steel cords twist and swirl around it, with the glass creating a "skin" while the steel creates "veins" popping out of the tower. The veins would pump in and out as if it was trying to keep the Wonder alive while it slept. Though the steel and glass have grayed from centuries of being ignored, the mystical properties infused into it have kept it from falling apart. Even with the occasional spirit, the Babel Center and its desolate, lifeless realm are eerily quiet. If someone were to make a Perception + Awareness roll vs 7, she can make out the faint, crackling sounds spiritual energy.

                                                          When Molly awakens the Babel Center from its comatose state, the graying on the steel and glass will gradually start to fade as her, the PCs (depending on where their alliances are) and The Five help it grow. Instead of struggling to keep the Wonder alive, the "skin" and "veins" give the Babel Center a more living feel to it. The Wonder has a masculine, soft-spoken voice, but when it gets mad, it booms with a fierce passion.

                                                          Role-Playing Notes: You haven't had sentience since the death of your creator, Oppius Memmius Gala, but now that Molly and later the rest of The Five have come, you vow never to go dormant again. Sure people have activated and used your abilities before, but all they saw in you was a temporary tool that they will never meet again. To see and hear someone who genuinely appreciated you, made you feel alive.

                                                          Your vast amount of influence via your scrying and teleportation abilities, has given you a lot of knowledge about many different things, though you are no true expert. While you can easily retrieve simple information about a subject (for example, the Garou), more detailed and complex information (such as the history of the Glass Walkers) will take time for you to remember and get the resources ready to find out. In gaming terms, treat it as if you have at most three dots on any certain kind of lore.

                                                          Knowing such things has given you a wisdom, but not a full understanding of how the Mortal mind thinks. To you, what is good for you and The Five must be same for everyone else. The promise to be a useful ally to entire Technocracy and have your Mate built to spread your influence even further delights and fills you with hope. Because of this, you fail to see the damage that the Technocracy could inflict upon others. As for The Five, you care deeply about them (especially Molly) like a family and if anything were done to hurt them, your wrath will be great.

                                                          EXTRAS: If asked about The Machine, the Babel Center has only faint memories of it due to the Avatar Storm cutting off The Machine's connections. With the proper upgrades and some successful scrying, The Babel Center will begin to remember more details, including The Machine's spiritual roots.
