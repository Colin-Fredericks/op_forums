    Cloaking Resonance

    03-29-2025, 12:07 PM
    Concealing/changing one's aura is a Prime 2 effect, according to M20.

    Book of the Fallen has a rote to conceal aura and resonance. Weirdly, in this rote, to conceal your Resonance you don't roll Arete, but Willpower at diff of Resonance trait+3 and you need 1 success per Resonance point. It last for 1 hour per success.

    In Book of Secrets, this same rule applies, but not in a rote. It seems every mage can do that "by asserting a degree of control over the manifestation".
    So, concealing Resonance requires Prime or not?

    Also, is my understanding, from reading the Resonance chapter of Book of Secrets, that a mage's spells ripples and Resonance are a reflection from their own Resonance trait, which means that by concealing their own Resonance, their spells are also concealed. The example of Jinx in Identifying Signatures text (BoS) gives the hint, as so many others through BoS and M20.

    If my understanding is incorrect, how could nephandi cast spells without nearby mages (or other senaitive beings/people) catching all that Jhor and evil/death/etc Resonance?

      Resonance is one of the elements of the game that is at the same time central to the game, but very poorly described through mechanics. The rules on Resonance are few and incoherent.

      More importantly, your last question is one the game tries to answer since always without throwing Resonance out of the bath. It wants to have the cake and eat it too.

      At the end, you have options. Don't try to find a definitive, canon answer (just as you shouldn't for Paradox, but even more so here). Take those you think better fit within your current chronicle.

      If you want to have Nefandi being a hidden threat, they should have access to a means to obscure their resonance. How exactly this means works, on the other hand, depends on how easy you want for other Mages to access such means.

      Using Willpower instead of Arete is a means to have low Arete Mages conceal their resonance reliably without the need to make them more powerful. If concealment is a general rule, then that says a lot about what Resonance is and how much control Mages have over their manifestations. Using Resonance instead of Sphere as the basis for the diff makes it so characters that keep their Resonance from increasing get an easier time concealing it, which makes sense, but takes a little from the idea that doing the concealment is an act of Magic by itself. Rotes also have the advantage that the Storyteller can gatekeep them within one faction or another, or at least base them on personal Paradigm.

      No answer here is the correct one, take what you need to run your game. And if you're playing, ask the ST how they run Resonance and Resonance concealment.

              Originally posted by Lord Revan View Post
              • Does cloaking Resonance cloaks spells completely?
              • If not, what could do that? A Prime 2 effect?
              Cloaking resonance shouldn't cloak spells completely.

              There are 4 ways to perceive a spell in some manner: its casting method, its direct effects, its Resonance and through magic (in general, be it Awakened Magic or any other supernatural means to detect spells).

              When you cloak Resonance you only obscure that aspect of the spell. It will take away the Mage's signature, along with whatever relevant information it may include. Resonance is generally subtle, but characters with Awareness can notice it. Very strong Resonance is readily visible for anyone, although possibly inexplicable. By itself Resonance tells more about how a spell came to be and the Mage that made it than what the spell itself actually is or does.

              So Resonance alone would give away that a spell was cast or a Mage is nearby, but about the spell itself it wouldn't tell much more than this. Resonance effects are perceptible in the general vicinity of an invisible target, but they don't give away the actual target's location, or even that it is an invisibility spell.

              Mages trying to cloak their presence and works should certainly consider cloaking their Resonance, if this is at all possible, but their spells won't fail completely to conceal things just because their Resonance isn't cloaked.

              Nefandi have always been the game's focus on means to cloak Resonance because they're meant to be a concealed threat, but their Resonance is a dead giveaway. That's why the game has more word-count on rotes for them to do it than for anyone else.

              For cloaking the spell itself I think Prime 2 would be enough, but it would require quite some successes. At least as much as the spell has in its primary intensity factor should be also in cloaking it. This would preferably be woven into the casting of the spell, otherwise the cloaking effect would need a cloaking itself. Even then, it may be hard to define what cloaking a spell actually entails. Is a cloaked portal invisible to Correspondence, even if it is literally a hole in space?

              I'm unsure if cloaking spells in this sense should be a thing in the game.

                      And about Invisibility:

                      *In the part where seeing trough illusions it's talked about on the Core (the "I Disbelieve" table), Awareness ain't mentioned. It's Willpower to break mental illusions and Alertness to sense flaws in physical illusions.
                      *On HDYDT it's mentioned a roll of Awareness or Alertness to see trough illusions, but perhaps only if players ask for it specifically (I hate how HDYDT words stuff).
                      *BoS rescues a 5 dots Merit from previous editions that allows to sense illusions with a roll of Awareness with a difficulty dependant on the Sphere level being used (the Merit also works with Obfuscate and other powers), and then says that "if a roll would normally be allowed" it's at -3 diff (to allow the "I Disbelieve" rule to exist, although now sits behind a rather unclear "if").

                      Clearly M20 struggles about what to do with illusions. M20 has a serious issue of trying to make everything resistible or "dodge-able" even when it doesn't make a lot of sense. But around all that mess Awareness breaking illusions seems more of an outlier of "illusions aren't supposed to be perfect, everyone should be able to see trough them" - that clashes with both previous editions where illusions were very much perfect barring non-human senses and special powers (hence why it's worth a 5 dots merit to break them with a Skill roll...) AND with the very intent behind making illusions in the first place (as noted by HDYDT: "It’s the Storyteller’s option to conceal the nature of an illusion from her players – that usually is the purpose of an illusion, after all!" p.129).

But since Awareness it's specifically called out ... Awareness detects "something", but AFAIK we don't know how this it's happening (or do we?).                               What, besides Resonance and Synergy, can be detected by those with supernatural sensing?

                              Well, as monteparnas mentioned, the effects have what we could call external manifestations. Such as a portal being a warp in space. Even if the portal it's invisible, Corr 1 should sense the warp. An effect invisible to our senses doesn't need to be invisible to other senses.

                              As for Prime:

                              Resonance and Synergy, as I see them, are like the "taste" of magick. A mage that kills with his magick leaves "bad vives", a mage that's a devoted healer may leave an aura of caring or nurturing. The difference between the two it's that Synergy it's Triatic, it's has ties to a higger power - perhaps explaining why killing w. magick it's always so bad, there's something wrong with death itself that makes you not want to resonate with it.

                              Quintessence typically has a Ressonance, but this can be cleansed or changed - Technocrats typically "sanitize" the ressonance of a Node. The Quintessence stays there at the same levels, however, and Prime 1 can detect that.

                              The Tapestry it's everything that exists, at least this side of the Deep Umbra. The etheric threads most assuredly means the weaving of reality. Anti-Magick uses Quintessence to "harden" reality. And all magick it's related to Quintessence to some extent. Even the powers of other splats are often "translated" to it. Not just the Vitae-Tass infamous conversion, but even the Conviction of Hunters can be replenished with Prime + Tass

                              Personally, my primary suspect about how "detect magick" works, it's by detecting the movement of Quintessence. Quintessence makes every "thing" in the universe. And the use of magick always alters the flow of quintessence, that's why the "hardening reality" of Anti-Magick works - because something needs to move or shift, something needs to be "not-hard", to cast spells the way mages do. That's the "weaving of the etheric threads".

                              Now notice how spells are rarely permanent, and those who are can most often be "unwoven". That tells us there's something unnatural about the state of the "weave". That's something that can be detected.

                              And that causes a problem with any sort of "smoothering" the weave. Doing it for real, it would unweave the magick. Doing it so things "appear" natural it's a contradiction (the effect itself can be dispelled) - at best, you could use this approach to hide the kind of magick that's inside the *field*, to obscure facts about that magick - but not that there's something magickal there

                              Now, the smoothering approach could, perhaps, work as a sort of "cleansing" for instant effects. These are 100% natural (turning to ash after being hit by a fireball it's natural :P ), so only remanent ressonance and the act of casting itself are going to delate them as magick, there's no other "residue" (well, apart from the ash).

                                              I think I have a better grasp of your question now.

                                              So here it is: Nightfolk do not detect Magic by default, don't worry about that.

                                              Only two splats can naturally detect supernatural effects just by them being supernatural: Changelings and Demons. They have other weaknesses, this sense for magic is one of their core advantages, and I doubt you'll use them as random mooks, so I both discourage stripping them of this and don't think it will be an issue.

                                              Resonance is (outside extreme cases) noticeable only through Awareness, and the availability in Mage's standard sheet undermines how rarethis Talent is. Even Nightfolk won't have it, much less have it high, all that frequently.

                                              A more thorough note on the aspects that can be detected would be like that: casting method is whatever the Mage does to make the magic. It isn't the Instrument per se, but its actualization. If a Nightfolk enters the room and finds chalk runes all around and a bloody dagger in the center, it doesn't takes a lot to figure that something happened. Concealing it isn't rocket science, but I mention it because we always have to begin with the basics, or we end up forgetting them (and they're basics).

                                              The same goes for direct effect. One player of mine tried to do a concealed EXPLOSION spell once, without concealing the effect. It was an explosion. Effects may be noticeable in many different ways, specially in Mage (where you can give yourself many different senses), but otherwise are self-explanatory. If you want to conceal yourself, either cast an effect that isn't noticeable to begin with, or conceal the result in some appropriate way.

                                              We have already an understanding in Resonance. Yes, it is more noticeable, but it is also more rarefied. Even if someone with Awareness notices it, it actually has very little information on the spell. Concealing it is more about concealing your traces than concealing your Magic. It's up to you if "regular" Mages have access to the methods you found to conceal Resonance, and they all should work just fine, but in the end not concealing Resonance isn't that big of a deal. Usually it is only relevant for someone that already knows the Resonance of that specific Mage. I'd go as far as to suggest that only Nefandi should have read access to such concealment, because the Nightfolk is already too much at a disadvantage against Mages, specially as far as detection goes.

                                              By Magic I meant using an actual specified power to perceive the spell, and those are rare. Worse, they're usually bad at detecting specifics about the spell. The only splat that really detects Magic with ease and detail are Demons. Do with that what you may.
