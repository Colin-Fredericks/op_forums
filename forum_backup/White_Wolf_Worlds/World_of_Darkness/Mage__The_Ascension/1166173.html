              I usually consider the Rotes like foci: something the Mage learns as a beginner, tying his belief into established rituals that give him enough confidence to pull an effect out. Through the Epiphanies (or in-game events, like improvising something without time or materials for the proper ritual) he gradually learns that whatever constraint he's putting on his reality is unnecessary, so as his Arete raises his need for Rotes lessens and he becomes more adept at improvised casting.
              My beginner mages (players) usually can only cast through Rotes, while the more experienced ones know they can bend reality even without them but still feel the safety of established procedures (therefore the usual -1 diff keeps existing). It depends from character to character though.

              Similar argument for the Technocracy, but I stress even more the need for proper Rotes and Foci there since their Technology paradigm is supposed to be more rigid than the Tradition occult pastiche.

                      Rotes I feel are important in Mage. Of all things they show what a Paradigm can do and how it works, fast casting and being able to act in the moment is great and certainly an important factor but Rotes are where aspects of the game really get to shine. If you take a look at a bunch of existing Rotes (outside of the default sphere effects) you'll see several that appear more "powerful" than what you get off of the Damage/Duration/Distance charts, this I believe is deliberately so. Many Rotes have a time factor within them, specific foci that have to be used and so forth and something as a Rote really should be easier and more effectual in it's purpose than something pulled together in the moment.

                      In my games I have the -1 Difficulty for Rotes as well as the +1 difficulty for Fast Casting and the space in between for doing stuff you know how to do but have not taken te time to refine into a Rote (or cannot perform the rote for). I also let my players get a little more Bang for their Buck if they take time to create Rotes.

                              The first question to answer is, "What aspect of magic do I want rotes to exemplify in my game?" They could be signature spells ("Steve's Amazing Laser Punch"), in which case a -1 difficulty makes sense as they're built to perfectly fit their caster's paradigm and are cast regularly. In that case they should also be cheap to buy, because they're more roleplay-based than anything else. It fits a truly reality-warping game because they're based solely on YOU and what your paradigm is. Angie's not going to beg Steve to teach her his Laser Punch because she would need the right Sphere mix anyway, and with that Sphere mix, she can come up with Angie's Amazing Laser Punch much more easily than learning Steve's, and they'll do the same thing.

                              Alternatively, you might want to have a role for "ancient, powerful magic" in your game. In Mage proper, there's very little reason to go digging in ruins for some dusty spellbook, or to learn the long-forgotten ritual of whatever, because you can do literally everything those effects did simply by wanting to and having the right Spheres. Indeed, the only groups that dig for ancient knowledge, like the Hermetics, do so because their paradigm demands it - they think they need to adhere to the ancient formulae, so they do. So, absent a purely Hermetic game, you could make rotes into rules-stretching effects that cannot be replicated on one's own. So rotes become unique things with unique rules.

                              As an example - can anyone with Entropy 4 / Life 2 / Spirit 3 and a decent explanation in their own paradigm go on an Agama-style sojourn in the Low Umbra? Or is that something that only the Euthanatos and their Euthanatos-style ritual can accomplish, thus requiring anyone interested in such a journey to go curry favor with Euthanatoi and learn their paradigm?

                              As always, the rules as written contradict themselves: the 1xp per rote implies the former path, while many printed rotes break the Sphere rules in a way that implies the latter. So we get to decide for ourselves and decide what to keep and what to throw.

                                              I think a little more guidance with Rotes would be a neat idea, however it's not 'Shoring up casting with bells and whistles' it's "Employing the Laws of Reality a given character knows to the greatest of effect". Just going "I have the right Arete, Spheres and can blag what I'm doing with whats at hand" isn't really using the concept of Paradigm, well it is but it's not how more successful Mages are likely to act. Sure a Hermetic mage may be able to etch the Sign or Mars in the air with a burning stick while speaking three secret words to draw fire from the air and have their enemy combust (as we all know a simple Forces 2 style attack), but a Hermetic mage who has taken the time to acquire a specific wand, keeps it inside a specially treated sheath with gold and red threads all woven in the pattern of Mars Ascending the Heavens and reconsecrates it's power every fortnight is probably employing the secrets of the Order of Hermes in a more successful way.

                                              The first method of course uses the Damage and Duration charts, doesn't have any Fast Casting penalty because it's a 'basic sphere effect' and can benefit from something like Ritual Casting (Ability to enhance Arete roll) if the ST deems so as well as extended casting rolls etc.

                                              The second might look a little like the following:
                                              Bolt of Mars' Wrath (Forces 2)
                                              Any novice of the Order of Hermes can turn their knowledge of Forces to brutal effect but those who study the best ways to use Magick in battle (typically those of House Flambeau) study a little deeper to that desire. The mage must procure a wand cut from a lightning struck tree or one that has survived a great fire, they can acquire this themselves as a branch or piece of burnt wood and shape it into their own wand (Dex + Crafts Diff 6). On to this wand must be written or etched enochian words for "Lighting, Wrath and Fire" while it is held for a full day and a full night by the caster within a sacred pentagram (Wits + Ritual/Rites?Appropriate skill for your chronicle roll, Diff 7. A Complete Success (3) or more on this roll lowers the difficulty of the NEXT casting of Bolt of Mars' Wrath by -1). This must be performed once every 12 days or a new wand will be required for the Rote. The wand itself must be kept in a sheath that is marked fore and back with the Sign of Mars and is made of red silk, whenever it is not in use, failure to do so for more than a few minutes will also rob the Rote of it's power.

                                              The target of a successful Bolt of Mars' Wrath is set on fire suffering damage as calculated by the Dam&Dur chart plus two additional dice. In addition regardless of the subjects actions they will suffer 3 dice of fire damage at the end of their next turn (this includes complete submersion).

                                                  Wow, some good stuff in this thread since I last checked it!

                                                  I think the "rotes as ancient magick" is fairly well supported in the setting, what with the idea of certain rituals having been sort of etched into consensual reality through a long history of frequent use, which is why the linear magick of sorcerers works (and doesn't typically incur Paradox). In fact as I've been reading Sorcerer Revised, it occurs to me that one might use the paths and rituals from Sorcerer as rotes in Mage and then either reward them with a -1 difficulty reduction or even say that they don't yield Paradox on a successful casting (might be a little too powerful, I'd have to try it in play).

                                                  It seems to me that these two possible interpretations of rotes are actually fully compatible: they can be signature spells that reduce difficulty by 1 or just remove the fast-casting penalty, or they can be well-established mostly-consensual rituals that don't give Paradox - both types of rote could be used in the same chronicle. As for the third idea of allowing rotes to have greater effects than an improvised spell with the same spheres... it could work if the price is still 1 XP to buy it, but I feel like Mages are quite powerful enough with their current sphere rank limitations

                                                  What are your thoughts on using Sorcerer rituals as rotes? At least as a narrative conceit - you could still let players invent their own rotes or throw some at them that you've made up yourself, and just say that those are ancient rituals that are also available to sorcerers. Does that seem like an interesting interpretation?

                                                          Even if the basic premise of "Rotes are just plain BETTER the all other magic" (arguable and the weight of what rules there are bend away from that), we need a LOT more guidance than we get.

                                                          How do you learn that Rote? Does it cost XP? Can a PC come up with such a Rote? Is there an benefit to being a Rote-heavy caster that can be offset by something else that a Rote-FREE caster gets? (Like, say, your Hermetic is weighed down with a shit ton of these Super-Effect Rotes, what about the Ecstatic that pretty much ALWAYS makes up every effect on the spot entirely dependent on the particular circumstances of the situation at hand.

                                                          I don't disagree with the basic premise to some degree. That's sort of how most of the Rotes I've ever written up as a Mage ST have kinda sorta gone (though I usually base what they can do much more concretely on what can be achieved with the constituent Spheres than in making them sort of spells that just do stuff, kinda like Vampire Thaumaturgic Rituals). If you are going to run Rotes this way (and why not? JUST NEED SOME RULES/GUIDANCE ABOUT IT....) I'd probably add in that a botch/Paradox tends to result in a consistent bad result effect. Effectively, Rotes are sort of "grooves" in the surface of Consensual Reality. They work they do because the focused action and intent along with repetition has "worn the groove" and allows for the magic to flow in a particular way.... So there would be additional sections of the write-up. What the associated Paradox would be. But also, the Practice required would be needed, along with the Instruments. Even if you belong to the Sect that uses a Rote, if you aren't doing magic in a particular way, you can't lean on the work of your predecessors to "get in the groove".

                                                          The silly example of this might be something like an Iteration-X Procedure that has actually become consensual since the 1920s (so, really an IBM Procedure) :
                                                          Make Toast (Matter 2)
                                                          With construction of the appropriate mechanism, the Scientist is able to turn bread into toast.
                                                          Practice: Craftwork, Hypertech?(well, it was back in the 1920's... in which case, maybe Weird Science?)
                                                          Instruments: Devices & Mechanisms, Energy (specifically "heat" of some sort)
                                                          Paradox: At low levels, a botched casting roll to Make Toast will result in burned toast. Higher levels of Paradox will generally result in the device catching fire and harming the mage, or in creating dangerous electrical shocks.

                                                          So, not just any technomancer can learn this Rote. It would take one with the specified Practices and Instruments.
