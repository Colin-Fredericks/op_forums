        By all means, we know the literal interpretation isn't the intended result, it's just an observation of how the rules sometimes say stupid things because the authors didn't knew what they were talking about.

        The problems are two-fold, though. First, this does results in eventual conflicts because many players and STs have a reasonable grasp of those concepts, and modern internet exacerbates this aspect.

        Second, and specially in this case, it creates limits based on misconceptions of how things work. Lets take Uranium as an example: it doesn't dish enough ionizing radiation to be an immediate hazard without ridiculous amounts of material and highly advanced prep. Even as ammunition it is useless for anything less than modern heavy artillery. It's just a heavier steel, hard to work, crappy for most jobs, and whose only other regular use is to die glass. It isn't even expensive (if natural. Enriched it is expensive as hell).

        Oh, by the way, Uranium doesn't glow green in black light because of radiation. All versions (enriched, depleted and natural) glow just the same. This glow is a chemical property of the material that shouldn't require Forces, unless you think that making any glowing toy requires Forces.

        Why Mages can't create Uranium? Because the setting buys into a mythos around radiation and atomic fuel that still lingered in the 90's. It sees radiation as "special" in an undefined way. But the result is that while your Mage needs Matter 5 to create some green dye for glass, they only need Matter 3 and some Google-Fu to replicate the technology that can actually turn ugly rocks, beach sand and water into a Hydrogen Bomb.
