    What's a good plot hook for a group that's never played Mage before?

    12-06-2022, 05:45 AM
    Mage is... a lot. As far as I understand it you can literally do anything, which leaves me overwhelmed. We think about playing it, but I'm the only one vaguely familiar with it past "real world magic, consensus of reality, technocracy vs traditions," and I have zero ideas on how to introduce the setting to them. The Awakening seems like a good start, but I can't seem to wrap my head around the concept of how one even starts that process (I've read the books, I still seem to struggle with how they trigger or how it changes your perspective so much you can bend reality to your will). I've got a Tremere nerd, so I suspect the Order of Hermes is going to be relevant to any of my hypothetical games.

          I think you should do a Sesion 0 (talk about the game, maybe build the PCs). This I think it's important for any game, really, but more so to introduce a setting.

          Talk about mage on loose terms, what you expect of the game, what they expect of the game. If you want to be the storyteller, I imagine you have some ideas of the kind of story you want to tell, or the kind of game you want to play. Players can use that information as fuel to inspire characters, and you can use their feedback to inform the setting.

          About Awakening:

          A very simplistic approach of "how they trigger it" and "how it changes your perspective so much" it's to say they Awake when they're faced with an obviously supernatural threat, that breaks their preconception of living in a "normal" world, and they need a miracle to survive. It happens a lot of times in the books.

          If you do this, don't mind a lot abut what Spheres they have (in fact, it may be that you don't know yet) - they do Wild Magick, and what they need or wish to happen, happens. If the priest tries to repel a vampire with a cross, the vampire gets repelled.
          This magick isn't really controlled by the character, nor the player, though. Also, it's legit to cause an Effect they wouldn't be able to replicate as starting characters, so feel free to exaggerate and/or cause collaterals, have fun with it :P

          Most of the time Awakening isn't really such a sudden thing, but a progressive revelation. Like having the nagging suspicion that the WoD it's not as mundane as people believes. Or like being initiated on an esoteric order, and practice until you "get" it. That last one a common experience among Hermetics (about half hermetics were part of the Order, as normal human Acolites or Sorcerers, prior to Awakening).

          In any case, the moment of Awakening it's a good time for characters to enter in contact with their Avatars (oh, btw, have your players take the Avatar Background at least at 1. It's really usefull). For a game so overcomplicated, remember that as an ST that you have an NPC attached to each PC, that's guiding them towards Ascencion. Avatars should be enigmatic, they're not Mentors, but it's still a tool in your belt. You can offer a glimpse of what magick it's trough them.

          Note: Most mages don't believe they can "bend reality to their will". Check the chapters abot Paradigm and stuff. They understand magick trough those lenses.

          You can think about at least Paradigm before Awakening. People have beliefs about how the world works. Knowing what they are, you can use them to color the Awakening. Awakening can cause a paradigm shift , if the player wants, but even then it could be useful to at least gloss over about what they believed prior to Awakening. The metagame idea here it's to have more tools, and to involve the players

              Honestly, I'd not focus on their awakenings beyond maybe 15 minutes in the starter session. Start with something simple they can bite into. Maybe they are in a bar waiting for their mentors to discuss grownup things when a shovel party happens by, or they have all been caught by MiB's for "reeducation" and need to band together to run away and find their own path.

              Just keep it simple, scale it down and let them explore it on their own, don't overwhelm them with the lore, the mysticism and the exploration of self. Sell them on their characters and the system then build up into the rest.

              What doesn't kill you, makes you... stranger.

                  Mostly in regard to "how it changes your perspective so much you can bend reality to your will" part of your post, but might be also relevant to your post overally:

                  I suggest you consider that when a woman or a man (or a girl or a boy) becomes a Mage (whether a Tradition Mage or a Technocrat) she or he gains new outlook, thinking styles and methods, and also considerations in regard to feelings and emotions. These are largely drawn and defined in regard to her specific Paradigm, particular mystical and occult outlooks, and various long-term goals and ambitions. And all of this is influenced and keyed to the particular faction, and other organisations, cults, and groups - and also relations, like the student-mentor relation, or a relation between lovers, or the bond between members of the crafts guild, or loyalty between sworn companions.

                  As such new considerations in regards to analysis, overall thinking, and emotions (both perceiving and comprehending), as well as new view points and outlook, and also of course casting Magick Effects (or performing Techomagic Feats or Hyperscience Effects) can be easily understood and analyzed, as they are real and observable, as well as distinctly keyed after various themes and ideas of particular groups and organizations, as well as events and particular experiences that a Mage goes through as nights, weeks, and also months pass through.

                  This also goes for understanding and discussions in-setting by a particular Mage player character and her or his group of fellows / colleagues / friends. Various books, tomes, outlines / notes and even specially placed and influenced newspaper writings, and periodical / magazine articles could also help a young Mage - or a group of Mages, like a woman and a man who as a pair a group of Verbenae Apprentices.
                  This would involve dealing with adjusting to new outlooks and viewpoints, and also having a positive and optimistic attitude in regard to experiencing in interesting new ways various feelings and emotions. Another factor that Tradition Mages and young Technocrats can depend on is a definite improvement to their material conditions and wealth that also includes their style, attitude, and preferences.

                          For me, the absolutely best way to "understand" the awakening is by watching the movie Matrix. It also helps to set to stage for this crazy "technomantic" concoction that is Mage the Ascension, thus already helping to take your and theirs minds from a "ponty hat Merlin type" mindset of what a "Mage" could be, and thus be ready to exploit its full potential. You could even do it EXACTLY (or more or less like) in the Matrix movie, and instead of having them awakening after seeing a werewolf rampanging on the streets, or suddenly having an "Eureka!" moment while studying to the test of quantum physics at the university, you could simply say that they "always felt there was something... odd with the world. Something wrong". And one day, a legendary outlaw/terrorist shows up at their doorsteps saying "come with me, if you wanna live". And have those clueless guys make a run for their lives while they are crazily running away from an Agent just like those from Matrix, including jumping from rooftops and dodging bullets, or some crazy Terminator android pursuing them. After that, have them recruited into the Virtual Adepts, in a heavy "technomancer" type of Mage, and from there you expand upon the game's concepts.

                          As for their actual "awakening", which is what triggers the shadowye organization which they'll soon learn to fear called "The Tecnocracy", you can say that they begin to perceive "glitches", incompreensible and unexplanable inconsistencies within the world, and conections between things. Briefly explain the 9 spheres before starting, just saying what each one covers, and let your players choose 2 of their liking. After that, try to explain those "glitches" - for example, if a player liked the idea of having power over minds, describe to him how he's starting to see a pattern of behavior in every being, as if they were all either connected, or have some sort of "pre-programming', and that people are more like automaton robots rather than free willed beings; describe also how in the middle of a crowd, they feel strange intuitions; have your player even prevent a robbery, without fully understanding exactly how he knows what he knows, except that he does know what he knows; then have him finally waking up to the deceiptful cheating liar his girlfriend, or mother/father or false best friend, not fully understanding how he now sees it, but that he clearly sees it now beyond any doubts; than make it all get progressively worse, make the guy have vivid nightmares, only for his mother/girlfriend/brother (whatever really) to make up and say "I had the strangest dream..." and have it be THE SAME nightmare the soon-to-be Mage have. And then describe how he hears his father saying "dumbass", and when he asks "what did you say?" His father (brother/friend/whatever) say "I didnt say anything".

                          And then have the thoughts of people around him start to flow more and more, to the point he can no longer control it, and his own mind becomes a whirlwind of thoughts constantly banging him literally in the head. And have people's moods change crazily around him, almost without control: a couple kissing start fighting, he enter his living room where his sister is watching a popcorn comedy, and suddenly bursts into tears, and let the tension build up to the point the character starts believing he is actually becoming insane.

                          If they are to have spirit sphere, have them see shadows with the corner of their eyes, which of course wont be there when they turn to look. Sometimes they see a monster sucking the spine of some guy on the streets, but when he looks at it, there's nothing there. Let them have crazy dreams of Cthulhu-esque monstrosities and impossible beauties, make them hear voices, when the wind blows it carries single words with it, soft and gentle like "north", "cold", "free". Nothing that makes sense. Or, if they touch the ground barefooted, they hear "strong" with a very vibrant voice.

                          If any of them chooses correspondence, make them see things far away. Turn their sight upside down for a few seconds. Describe how a car accident happened hundreds of meters away (or hundreds of yards) but they are seeing it as if they were just next to it. Then make them see dozens of things at the same time. Describe how they are actually having a 360 degree sight, and they are perceiving things on their backs... And then make it all worse, to make them see even with their closed eyes! They can barely sleep, because they cant shut their sights to everything in front of them, on their sides, on their backs, ABOVE them and BELLOW too!

                          If it's life, it could begin after months of praying and crying over his mom with terminal cancer. After months and months awake, he developed such a connection to her.... Until she finally died. In that moment, you felt. You felt the heart stopping. The blood freezing. And the brain shutting up.
                          Afterwards, you began to feel your own heart. Pulsing like a drum. Impossible to sleep, why is it beating so hard? It feels as if it's about to explode from your chest. And then, you start to feel it... Tum tum... Tum tum... This time however, it's not yours. You have the feeling you can almost see everybody's heart pulsating. It almost looks like everybody's heart is about to explode. Then you hear a ZZZZZZZ as hard as an helicopter... You close your eyes and shut your eyers, and it gets worse, because in the dark of your closed eyes, you see a fly, pulsating in green energy, and the people around you, you see clearly all their veins, as if looking in a cartonish X-Ray. You run, scared, and place your hand on a tree to take your breath, and then you feel it... You can feel water flowing up from the soil and going to each leaf, and you feel each root of the plant slowly growing as if they were your own... And then you start feeling the rats walking at the sewers bellow the asphalt, and there's two of them having sex in the middle of the filth, and you can feel the beasts orgasms, which makes you puck in disgust.

                          And so on, so forth.

                          When those crazy sensations reach their climax, that's when some mysterious hooded person shows up doing the old Arnold's "come with me if you wanna live", followed suit by some crazy Agent/Terminator dispensing furious wrath all around.

                              The Matrix trilogy is the perfect movie to truly understand the game Mage (yes, imagine that, for Mage! For pointy hats Merlins!) in so many senses... Not only is perhaps the best awaking analogy, but it also has the best analogy for what the Technocracy represents (for non technos of course) in the form of the Agents of the Matrix, but also of the capabilities that are possible for Mages (you can bend the rules, but not break them), but even an idea of what Ascension represents (in the figure of Neo as "The One").

                              But there's more, one masterfull detail that eluded me for years. It has to do with the colors of the movie.

                              Now you see, when they are inside of the Matrix, everything has a GREEN tone about it on the background. Thus, Green represents THE WORLD OF THE MIND.

                              When they are in Scion (aka "the real world"), the color is BLUE. Thus, Blue represents THE WORLD OF MATTER (the Material world).

                              And finally, when Neo looks at Seraphy, the Guardian of the Oracle, he sees it as a YELLOW being.... Now, yellow is a color that didnt appear before. Neo sees it again after getting blinded, he sees yellow yet again when Agent Smith "posseses" one guy in the real world, and then he sees the machines as yellow in the machine-city, with his blinded eyes.
                              Thus, yellow represents THE SPIRIT WORLD.

                              I recommend you to watch Matrix before playing Mage for the first time, in order to get in the "feeling" of what this game can be.
