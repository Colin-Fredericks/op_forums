        Originally posted by Saikou View Post
        How does M20 divvy up successes?
        M20 is like the older Second Edition, or Sorceror's Crusade - You still need to divide successes between Damage and Duration if you want *both* of them to apply at once - but aside of that the default rule is that the *total* successes count for everything. It really simplifies things, and makes Mages with low Arete scores not need tons of rolls to pull off everyday things. Of course there are still certain minimum amounts of successes to be reached, depending on the magnitude of the feat.


        The Revised "Divided Successes" system is an optional rule that you can either still optionally use, enforce, or the ST can decide to mix and match - for example, they can still demand one extra success per extra target on top of the rest of the successes, but leave the rest as is. But the default rule is as above.

                    Originally posted by Lord Revan View Post
                    Sure, its a Forces 2 effect. How exactly this would work is never stated in raw, but you could make it as soak dices, or reducing damage from the opponent. There are lots of ways, actually.

                    Also, the shield would probably push everytjing that goes around the mage, depending on how it ws made, which could be quite annoying
                    I've always ruled it depends on paradigm and personality of the mage in question.

                    A Tytalan NPC in a previous Mage game had a straight-up Parma Magicka style ablative barrier with auto-soak levels, but had to be restored via Arete rolls as it ablated. In other words, she had magickal reactive armor. But, her preference was to hit hard and hit fast, and be able to soak up individual huge hits that may or may not happen before she had the chance to lay her opponent out...or her personal favorite trick, tie in Mind Effects to compel her enemies to surrender, having seen her negate those massive hits. Vulgar, but effective.

                    Meanwhile, her Flambeau NPC best friend preferred shields (Forces 2/Prime 3) which siphoned away the Forces Pattern of incoming attacks as ambient Quintessence, which she could then throw back at her targets. It didn't negate the attack, thereby keeping it coincidental (important for a Flambeau who needed the ability to skimp on Paradox she could instead build up through attacking), but it did jack up the difficulty of damage rolls made against her and allowed her to use "ambient Quintessence" to fuel magic without fucking up the surrounding environment (at least, due to loss of Quintessence).

                    In the same chronicle, a Euthanatos PC used a combined Entropy/Forces Effect to subtly bleed off the kinetic energy of attacks while increasing the probability the attacker would miss. Sure, either sphere independently could work...but Forces, unless one spends valuable successes, can only target one type of Forces Pattern at a time, and Entropy only goes so far against area-affecting attacks. Not to mention, depending on what is being employed against the mage, either sphere independently could invoke vulgarity; both at the same time, not necessarily so much. In any case, it was a flat difficulty hike to attack him.

                    The VA PC used a Forces/Correspondence/Time Effect a time or two to imperceptibly dilate the space around him, causing the attack to move "further", giving him a dodge difficulty reduction (the Forces component was to allow the Effect to be selective against what triggered it).

                    Although, the nastiest and most inventive one employed in that chronicle was a Forces 2/Life 3 Effect which bled kinetic energy away from the attack to strengthen the mage's Life Pattern where the attack would hit. It provided soak difficulty reduction relative to the strength of the incoming hit, -1 for every two levels of damage dealt (to the usual minimum of 3). Which meant it was pretty much always coincidental, especially since it didn't negatively impact the mage's ability to interact with their surrounding world.
