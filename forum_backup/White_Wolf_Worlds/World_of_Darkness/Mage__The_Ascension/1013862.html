    Help : How to roleplay Seekings ?

    12-07-2016, 02:26 PM
    Hi everyone !

    This week, I will roleplay Seekings for the first time, so I'm not sure how to do it and I would be happy to get your experience on the matter. Do you describe a scene ? Do you make the player part of the description, giving enigmas or asking them to make choices ? Do you play it only narratively ? Do you do it in private with the player or do you let everyone share the experience (I will to it that way I think).

    Well, thank you for your answers !

      When I have moments where it is personal to a player and the other PCs will not be there. I take a note out of the VtR2e book for feeding. I let the other players come up with OR I will give them NPCs to be a part of the scene.

      It all depends on what your planning on how you want to go about doing it, but normally for me I ask "Is this something I have a plan for." if so, then I create NPCs and give them some history and it will also have "Make sure you express these things before your characters screen time ends." If it is something I don't have a plan for, I let PCs just come up with what they think will fit well in the scene. Every now and then I will step in to help nudge the supporting players when I see a good opportunity.

      If there are no plans on the seeking to have beings that can communicate (humans, spirits, scary things...) then I still let them watch.

                  There's a lot of good stuff for seekings in the Revisded Storyteller's Handbook.

                  The things I try to consider for seekings are:

                  Essence: This will be the biggest determining factor for the motive behind, and the overall feel of the seeking.
                  Paradigm: This will determine more or less what kind of imagery will appear in the seeking, as well as dictate how the mage may have one.
                  Nature and Demeanor: Good to look for ideas regarding flaws that the avatar would have problems with.
                  Spheres: Which spheres the mage is looking to grow or uses most heavily/neglects is always good seeking material.
                  Flaws: This stuff is great for symbolism, but can also become the focus of the seeking in question.
                  In Game Performance: Do they tend to over use a particular kind of magic? Do they disregard their 3 points of Prime? Are they keen to go on their own? Do they rely on one of their backgrounds too much?
                  Backstory: How the mage awakened and what kind of seekings they've had before can be a good place to build from.

                  With all this in mind, I go through the following questions to think up a good Seeking.

                  1. What doe the avatar want to teach the mage?

                  2. How would their build the seeking? (i.e. would they make a new world, adapt the mundane world, have it be a dream, make it like a spirit quest...)

                  3. What relationship does that avatar have with the mage, and how does that reflect here?

                  4. What will the ultimate choice be, and how many ways are there to succeed or fail?

                      Our experience over time has varied widely.

                      In the current game, there is enough going on that the added complication of having an invisible, inscrutable bitchy "friend"/boss just wasn't worth it. Effectively, either there are no Avatars or they are as integrated as they were back in the Dark Ages. (Take your pic, as either one doesn't create material difference in the game proper.) The Avatar Background is explicitly the same as Font. One player wanted a "standard Avatar", I had her invest in Totem to mimic the overall narrative feel, up to and including having said Totem show up and have an integral role in moving up in Arete.

                      I know! Sacrilege! Mage without Avatars! Well... sometimes Avatars actually get in the way, so we wanted to try it out. It's actually working REALLY well. Enough so that Avatars as described will only be a factor in future Mage games if it's appropriate to the type of experience we're going for.

                      There are still "Seekings" of a sort. Each character has specific goals and plot points that are important to their advancement. It's my job to set it up so that they get the breadcrumbs to end up in the right place at the right time with enough XP to have a chance to do the right thing and hit that quantum jump in potential. So far, that's gone really well too. 3 characters of 6 have had their "Seekings" Though the session focuses on a character and his part in the ongoing plot as well his own particular journey, everyone else gets to be involved playing their own characters. The character doing the Seeking gets the opportunity to use their magic to do the plot-critical relevant actions and there is much catharsis to be had. Afterwards, some aspect of what just happened crystalizes as their goal for their next personal story arc leading them to the next situation where they can gain Arete. Effectively the "Seeking" is really the entire time the character is at that dot of Arete, with a punch at the end.

                      In the past, we've gone the whole ten yards and I've recast the entire game to one degree or another for a session to do an Arete Run. Minimum, it's like Ramnesis suggests and it's a sort of reverse Wraith Harrowing. A dreamscape where the Avatar (usually played by me) is the director and Chorus with the other players acting out the important roles. Sometimes those roles have been versions of their characters aspected to deal with whatever role they have in the passion play. Just as often they play other relevant parts. (Even though they don't get to play their characters, they are okay with it, as they know that it will be their turn at some point... so, no need to try to carve out private one on one time).

                      This has varied widely in how much effort goes into it. At the lower end, the player saw and interacted with various alternate versions of himself in mirrors scattered throughout an empty house (that was important in his backstory). The other "hims" were based on prior decisions that went the other way. That one was inter-cut with scenes of the actual game going on.... which led to the question of "Where the Hell was Gavin? Why couldn't we contact him?" and the standing statement that, when characters "disappear" on these ethereal kinds of Seekings, they have "Gone to Florida", as that was the character's answer when asked where he was.

                      On the other end, I effectively rewrote pretty much all the fluff of Mage. It was set (possibly?) in a space station above a devastated Earth abandoned to the "Forces of Darkness" where a refugee population of humans and supernaturals, led by various groups of mages, had colonized the solar system. The Traditions, Technocracy, other supernaturals all went into the mix-master, resulting in new Sects - The New Covenants (sorta like the Traditions, only not), the Combine (mostly from the Union), the Court of 10,000 Fixed Stars and the X<Alt>8sh0n (both of which were their own things). His Seeking involved interacting with representatives of the 4 sects (cue the other players) and coming to some conclusion about which one he was going to hand the keys to the future they desired. His Avatar rating was relatively low, so his Avatar was something along the lines of "The Signal in the Noise". The other PCs and I were mostly "the Noise" though we each had some aspect of an assistant in determining what "the Signal" might be, the decision was the latest iteration of him "Finding the Signal". There were several options with several possible outcomes.... some of which resulted in him not gaining the dot in Arete and starting over.... (That has always been a factor in our Seekings. They aren't guaranteed to work out. And, on a few occasions, they haven't.)

                      All that for 1 session.

                              I honestly think the Avatar is the single most useful thing in Mage for directing the players.
                              You want them to look for something specific? Have their avatar highlight where to look.
                              You want to guide them away from the purple paradigm? Have their avatar get annoyed if they stray too far from their practice.
                              You want to railroad a few things without convoluted reasons? Have the avatar guide them where you need them to be.

                              It's such a useful tool for the ST. I can't imagine running a game as free form and open to abuse as mage without it.

                                      To say that, as a story teller, you don't like the Avatar because it ruins the fun for the player may say a lot more about how much you trust yourself with such a tool than it says about the tool itself.

                                      Personally, the first thing I like to do after Character Creation is figure out my player's avatars. I like to figure out what they are like, how they may appear and influence the player's mages, and what kind of conflicts that would cause.

                                      It's a pretty cool way to allow the player to talk allowed the internal conflicts that go on in their Mage's mind. The avatars will expect much from their mage, and the differences between their expectations and the mage's own wishes is one of the most interesting sources of conflict in the game.

                                      As a mechanic, as said above, it's brilliantly useful for gently nudging the players towards some of the stuff you prepared, and drawing their attention to key objects or people.

                                              If you read my previous posts in this very thread, you can see that I have used Avatars to varying degrees over 25 years + of playing Mage in all four editions. My opinion is informed not only by high minded philosophical debates on this and previous forums (or what passes for it...), very often by by people who love Mage conceptually but have never had the chance to actually PLAY it, but also those two decades of practical hands-on hip-deep experience running and playing the game. And all but the current game with Avatars in some form or another (even as what Avatars even are and how they work has radically changed from book to book, much less edition to edition).

                                              My point based on your post was that the aspects you love about Avatars are the very same aspects that can make them complete crap when in the hands of a poor or inexperienced ST. All of those things? If they are eliminating aspects of player choice about their characters that haven't been subject to prior negotiation and agreement, then they are outright awful. If they are being used because the ST is having to hit the players over the head with the intended plot, there's often a problem with the plot - the players are less interested in it than in pursuing different objectives, the linkages between scenes are too weak for the players to discern, etc. All too often, those are not, in fact, brilliant ways to initiate character action. They are stop-gaps or leading players around by the noses. That's not brilliant ST. Sometimes is lazy or simply bad ST.

                                              There are just as many problems with the entire set-up of Avatars as there are benefits from using it. There's a reason that Dark Ages doesn't have them. The idea of Avatars as walking, talking, interactive ur-persons, while very pertinent for some Foci and whatever the "uber-Foci embraced by each Sect that makes them a Sect" can be called (that used to be one slice of the definition of the vagueness of "Paradigm", but it's a concept that has gotten lost for lack of a name in M20.... The Hermetics are definitely spot on about some things), is pretty much total crap for others. It's a REALLY bad fit to force some types of characters to depend on Harvey for their even having the ability to alter reality. It doesn't fit into the way they Awakening, their Focus, their Seekings, etc. It's 100% forcing an ill-fitting game concept onto a character and straight-jacketing pretty much every thing about their character that is magical.

                                              That's why the Guide to the Technocracy spent a lot of time showing several different ways that the Union minimizes and marginalizes their Geniuses. That's why the types of Avatar you are talking about used to require a Merit. They weren't the default. That's why there was another Merit to buy the chance to have the Avatar not be this other thing you ever had to really deal with. That's why the description of the ways Avatars manifested based on Essence wasn't always, or even often, about a person, particularly one that the mage dealt with regularly. It could just as easily be a noise or a book or a vague feeling or the way events happened around the character, etc.

                                              Avatars are what you make of them. And you can make absolutely nothing of them at all and have a Mage game that may be "ideologically impure" but it can be just as strong, perhaps even stronger and better for some chronicle ideas, than having them. You can also go completely the other way and have Avatars be even more intrinsic and involved than your method (How about setting up a collaberative "Avatar-Guiding" system based around something like Shadow-Guiding from Wraith? Why not?) However, those are very particular games with a very particular feel.

                                              The NWO is my current game is MUCH better off not having to deal with/not deal with a Genius, particularly of your invisible, bossy and maybe-potentially-helpful-if they-feel-like-it friend variety. The perhaps-a-Son-of-Ether and the Hermetic could probably have them and still function as is, but it would be something grafted on that doesn't need to be there for the characters to function and would probably get in the way of the way the characters have been played to date. The Cultist and the Verbena could integrate such an Avatar best, particularly the Cultist, but they have other things going on, so, though it might add, it would take time away from some of their other concerns. The pyromaniac Akashic former Kopa Loei has a Totem that does what you're talking about, only not very often... And it works out great for that character. THAT character.

                                                          Arguably that's part of the nature of the Avatar. Some mages have it so strong that they literally can't go a day without them coming in and rearanging their life, others wonder if they have an avatar at all.

                                                          The game I'm currently running has avatars used quite extensively for various things.

                                                          One has a Primordial avatar which is the spirit of Excalibur, who berates their mage often for choosing to avoid conflict and battle. The player then has to question, do they try to apease their avatar, or do they stay true to themselves? Is there a away to do both?

                                                          One has a Dynamic avatar which comes up in the form of music, shapes in their cigarette smoke. But sometimes it's just intuitions and feelings, for example the sudden instinct to look out the window just in time to see a group of technocrats approach the building, or a burning desire to return to the scene of the crime just to give it a second look. Problem is that because of the dynamic nature of their avatar, the question on whether or not all these intuitions add up to anything or not is always present.

                                                          One has a Questing avatar which if pushing them to pursue what happened to their parents, regardless of whether it helps them on their current goal or not. The player then has to choose whether to follow this push and satisfy their personal curiosity, or ignore their avatar and stay on course with what they are trying to do.


                                                          I use them to add an extra personal level to each player, and provide for them a conflict which may get in the way of the events of the story, or even lead them completely down the wrong path. The avatar may wear the clothes of a spiritual guide, but it is just as self serving as it is insightful.

                                                          Another thing that the Avatar allows is for Pathos towards Widderslainte. In fact, the entire tragedy of a widderslainte falls apart without recognising the avatar.
