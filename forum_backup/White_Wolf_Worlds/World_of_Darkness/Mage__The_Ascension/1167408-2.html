        Well.... The Nine Traditons, historically, haven't been. So, if HAS to be 9, then the Traditions aren't it. Typically the number is 8. Seats are not occupied for centuries at a time. The Solificati slunk away almost right after the Convocation.and there aren't any real contenders to sit in the Seat of Matter for a very long time. The number did jump back to 9 for the decades after the Etherites joined up. But then the Batini didn't so much tip over the table and leave in a huff as they just stopped showing up in such a subtle way that it really took awhile for everyone to catch on that no one had been sitting in the Seat of Correspondence for a while. The whole "NINE Traditions" thing was 1457-1470, 1904 - 1922 & 1961 onwards. That's roughly 80 total years out of 550.

        So, taking a bunch of random factoids from various places doesn't pan out on the ground. It seems more like post hoc rationales tacked onto, perhaps, the Order of Hermes putting their particular take on that out there. (Perhaps, once Baldric La Salle realized he wasn't going to be adding a slew of new Houses, he went for the "The Order of Hermes started with 9 and that was a good number. so let's do that" but, even then, that seems like adding significance to something circumstantia from seven centuries beforehand. It could have been 8 Houses if Tremere and Tytalus's Master had pushed for her own House with her apprentices in place instead of setting them up on their own. Or 10, if Trianoma hadn't stepped back into Bonisagus's shadow. Or 7/8 if Diedne had told the Order to go screw themselves because they didn't need to join their little club. And so on.)

        Also, if we are going to play numerological games, it has been put out there in the material more than once that the number 9 is also seen as being incomplete. Ten is 1 is Unity. The Nine Traditions mystically have always been pining for that 10th Seat, 10th Sphere, 10th member of the family.

                                        While I don't think retconning in a merger is hard to do, having them merge in the "now" of the setting would be a fairly bigger deal compared to having them always been that way as initially suggested.

                                        One of the big reasons is what I generally refer to the "scientist vs. engineer" split. The predominant mentality of the Etherites is science. That is, the rational expansion of their understanding of the universe, from which they can create functional models, from which they can do stuff. The predominant mentality of the Adepts is engineering. They take an existing model, and expand that, and themselves, and then what they can do, with it.

                                        Asking the VAs to operate in the SoE's academia and formalities would be very difficult. Asking the SoE's to operate in the VA's individualistic and anachristic social structures would likewise not work well. Neither group is going to react to, "The Ahl-i-Batin want back in, so make room and combine," well, and would probably both respond with, "they're religious mages, why don't they just join the Chorus. The Chorus could use some better representation in their ranks from the Arab world anyway."

                                        It also presupposes that the Virtual Adepts aren't going to evolve with the times as younger Adepts adjust their technology to what's cutting edge now instead of what was cutting edge 20 years ago. The idea that the cyberpunk genre is so firmly rooted in the 80s that a group inspired by it can't be adapted to confront the technological realities of the 21st century is kind of ridiculous as the genre has itself updated and adapted up until this day. Not every cyberpunk genre piece goes for the retro style of the late 80s and early 90s There's also plenty of genre entries that hold up today with minimal alterations (ex: Ghost in the Shell is still as relevant as ever, and however you may feel about the live action movie, it does show that GitS' approach to cyberpunk doesn't feel out dated or retro even today).
