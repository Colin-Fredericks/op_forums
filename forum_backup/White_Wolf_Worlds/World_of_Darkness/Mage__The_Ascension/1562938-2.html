    Basically that.

    Merely as a new Sphere level and regular rules, I'd say it's ridiculous. But as a more involved system where an entire story is built around it, potentially involving lots of people, we can have a lot of leeway without breaking the game or its assumptions: Archmastery is fundamentally distinct from regular Magic, and the effects are by definition possible, despite powerful.

    Lesser effects can happen a number of times in a game with intense, but shortish stories, while world-changing ones would take most of the remaining chronicle, making the disposition of the group to commit to the story a natural check on abuse.

            But, in any case Mythic Thread it's already a force that runs contrary to the "common sense" of Reality Zones and such. A consensus within the Consensus, or otherwise a form of "reality" that doesn't answer to the rest of reality - like a under current within the current of a river.

                Mythic Threads aren't necessarily the only thing Archmastery can do. In this tangent the main point is Magic Through Stories, Effects whose Magic is in the quest to produce them, not in a Ritual roll. That's why I'd lump RZs and Avatars into it rather than just run Mythic Threads/Cosmological Constants/Earthly Foundations.

                I'd slightly counter the notion of MTs running against a RZ by stripping factual effect from in-game theory:

                Some phenomena seem to exist independently from local reality. The Curse of vampires is one of those, but so is Gravity. It's unknown if they share nature/cause, or if different reasons can exist for something to transcend local reality.

                What we know is that there's a Global Plastic Reality (which we commonly term Consensus), a Local Plastic Exception (the Reality Zone) and Non-Plastic Phenomena (which we have 3 names for with theoretical distinctions baked in).

                All that said, most Effects for Archmastery will squarely deal with the third, but dealing with the other two isn't necessarily out of reach, especially when we consider that history within the setting has shown that current theories are either flawed or incomplete: the cause of the Consensus may not really be any consensus or human belief at all, and this ambiguity must remain.

                For Reality Zones, Archmastery can serve as a framework for altering them even if not being their cause. It can also be one possible cause, so a single Mage establishing a house-size RZ isn't incompatible with an Archmaster making a Town-size RZ. This isn't a statement on the nature or source of RZs, but a tool to model in the rules the process of establishing a large RZ or Realm (we have suitable rules for minor ones, but they scale poorly).

                Let me see if I can find and get Imperial Mysteries, if anyone can summarize the rules here it would be nice. For now, what can we use as the fundamental units of story and effect for this rule?

                    It's been a while since I read Imperial Mysteries, but as I recall there's a setting element and a metaphysical element that make archmagic what it is. The metaphysical element is an extrapolation of the Arcane XP system. The learning and discovery that makes Arcane XP what it is can actually be turned directly into fuel or the workings of archmagic. But at such rarified levels it can't be just be any experience, it has to be specific ones. Archmages are advanced enough in their craft that they know what will get them what they need, but it is often incredibly esoteric stuff like the sound of a heart breaking written for a four string quartet, or the specific DNA sequence of a frog in suspended animation in a Siberian glacier, etc.

                    The setting element is a cold war/detente among the various archmage groups that keeps them relatively subtle. Ostensibly they are supposed to not rock the boat and keep the world the way it is. In practice, all of them are working both to advance their agendas in the world and to cast world shaking spells that will change everything and propel them further on the path to the Supernal, they just have to be careful and discreet. What this means for magic is that they can't spend much time looking for these unique spell components in the world and instead have to farm that out to younger mages. Likewise other tasks they might need, such as moving ley lines and changing resonances (I forget if thats an Awakening thing) are likewise better farmed out.

                    The point is that the spell is broken down and major parts of the effort are taken out of the hands of a dice roll and into the hands of a story that more standard level characters can participate in. And while the specific ideas like Arcane experience don't have a counterpart in Ascension, the idea of searching for key information, specific favors and agreements, vital and unique components, and shifting resonance can.

                        There are basically three parts to it, as I remember:

                        1. A system that lets you spend Arcane Experience on the casting of a spell to “supercharge” it, gaining access to drastically improved Imperial Spell Factors such as number of targets, range, duration, intensity, etc. Basically, gives the Archmaster a way to flex on something regular mages can do.

                        2. Imperial Practices. Awakening uses “Practices” as part of its Arcanum system, where a Practice is a generalized answer to “what can you do with X dots in an Arcanum?” which then gets specialized by the choice of Arcanum.

                        For the sixth dot, the Practice (I forget its name) allows you to imbue the spell with a sort of intelligence, allowing it to respond to changes of circumstances without having to be “programmed”.

                        For the seventh dot, there are two Practices: Entities and Excision which do opposite things (kind of like “Make” and “Unmake”), both of which involve altering the nature of a supernatural template as it manifests in the subject of the spell. Cast this on a vampire, and you can add or remove features to it from “what does it mean to be a vampire?” Or even to flat-out add or remove the template, turning an ordinary mortal into a vampire or vice versa. And to drive the point home, it goes on to list which Arcana are needed to me with each gameline's template.

                        For the eighth dot, you get to me with the environment in a similar way, adjusting what amounts to the fundamental laws of reality within the area affected by the spell.

                        I forget what the Imperial Practice for the ninth dot was; but I seem to recall it being something to the effect of being able to simulate the achievement of Ascension.

                        3. Acts of Imperium. This is where the “story generation engine” comes in: the Archmaster defines one change that he wants to make too the fundamental nature of reality; he is given a list of tasks (ranging from “legendary feat” to “do the impossible”) that he's supposed to complete in order to achieve his ultimate goal; he's encouraged to recruit agents (such as regular mages) to help him complete those tasks, which are designed to create the perception that what he wants to achieve has been achieved.

                        Then, after they are achieved, he enters Supernal Reality and engages in an Act of Imperium to shift the Supernal World to match the changes he's brought about in the Phenomenal World. This isn't a solo Act: other Supernal beings join in, either to support his effort or to interfere with it, and a “bidding war” begins between the Archmaster and his allies on the one hand, and those who oppose his goal on the other. If the Archmaster loses, his attempt to alter reality fails; I believe he also risks utter annihilation should he fail badly enough, where he's erased so thoroughly that it's as if he never existed in the first place.

                        If he succeeds, he makes the changes that he's arranged to simulate to become real, fundamentally altering the nature of reality and achieving Ascension, becoming a Supernal god.

                        Other parts of the book address, in turn, what it takes to become an Archmaster (answer: you must build what amounts to your own personal Watchtower, creating a “site” in Supernal Reality that defines your essential nature and what your magic is like, replacing your former Path with a fully personalized Golden Road), and what you do after you achieve Archmastery (answer: you right in the Ascension War).

                        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                            So, breaking it down we have:

                            1. A system that uses the regular rules to make specific things otherwise outside the scope of regular rules. The "traditional" approach to Archmastery.

                            1a. Said system recognizes those limits piecemeal and tackle each directly: Adaptive spells -> Splat Building -> Reality Zone -> Something.

                            I'm curious to know more about the "Spell AI" feat, less so but still about the last "Something" in the end, as those may be worth replicating.

                            2. A system that let you burn XP to scale up regular factors. I'm not a fan of burning XP, but scaling up factors will be a thing.

                            2a. This is where Earthbound uses Background and, IIRC, Faith. I think Quintessence in large quantities can be a substitute for XP with a caveat: instead of relying just in Quint economy we could work out how channeling so much of the stuff affects the Mage and the area.

                            3. A system that enacts change in a global scale and, if successful, the character "wins the game", and where we find the Story-fueled Magic we're after.

                            3a. This one seems at first to go a little too far for our goals, although I'd like some examples to be sure of how it works. It may not be as setting breaking as I think.

                                                        We're starting to gather some elements we can use as the "cost" of Archmastery Effects:

                                                        - Entire Nodes,
                                                        - Large quantities of Tass,
                                                        - McGuffing ingredients,
                                                        - Unique Instrument,
                                                        - Terrain preparation,
                                                        - Field research.

                                                        I'll add to the list:
                                                        - Painstakingly specific Resonance,
                                                        - Witnesses,
                                                        - Integration.

                                                        My thoughts on this:

                                                        1. We can impose specificity at this level. This effect won't require Quintessence, it will require Nodes, Tass and Quint from Avatars in a non interchangeable way.

                                                        2. Resonance has to be perfected from start to end, in such a way that collecting an ingredient can't be just about getting it, but getting it in a way that reinforces the Resonance in the right direction. Nodes, Tass, ingredient, Instruments, Location, Execution, even witnesses must all fit the spell.

                                                        3. There must be some way to quantify the more ephemeral ingredients and/or research. I think we can add an Arcane Experience system where the character is awarded similarly to XP until they have all the ingredients.

                                                        4. Instruments and rituals shouldn't be possible to ditch in this kind of effect. They're part of the Resonance requirement, not just Paradigm.

                                                        5. Field Research may use the same Arcane Experience system, or may be measured as a number of phenomena to be observed.

                                                        6. Witnesses add another layer of reasons to have others making stuff for you. It doesn't need to be witnesses to the final casting, but to any momentous step of the process. That way even seemingly unrelated events can add up to the total witness count.

                                                        7. Integration requires that all the elements fit formally in function, not just Resonance. The final ritual feels as a coherent process that will cause the effect rather than just things lumped together arbitrarily. I'd personally say that the archetypes convened by the casting must also fit the Effect.

                                                        The idea here is that the requirements work as drive for the Archmage to elaborate adventures and seek help, and also as a clear map of when requirements are achieved. It's not just a lot of things to gather, but things that are disputed, must be gathered in the right circumstances, arranged to have the right witnesses, and can't be easily substituted.

                                                        I'd also say that what sets an actual Archmage apart when using this system is how much they decide how to approach the whole thing. For a PC, it means the ST won't devise a story, but rather run the scenario the player built.
