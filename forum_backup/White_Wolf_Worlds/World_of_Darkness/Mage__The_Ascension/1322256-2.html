    Originally posted by Czernobog View Post
    How much of that stuff from Ascension is even canon anymore?
    Well, note that in my first paragraph I was just talking about stuff from Lost Paths rather than Ascension, and that's where I saw the info about the Entelechy as a being from the "last Great Cycle" which had been banished from the Tellurian "thousands of eons" before. M20 repeats the basic story of the Entelechy's return being the origin of Ahl-i-Batan souls on p. 202, although it just says the Entelechy was "comprised of the Ascended souls of mages who lived in an age before this one" without specifically saying how ancient it is or anything about a Great Cycle. The notion of a Great Cycle is mentioned on p. 156 in the Euthanatos section but it doesn't elaborate too much on what this means, so it's not clear if they're still talking about very long historical cycles of universal creation and destruction (which the revised Euthanatos tradition book talked about on p. 76-77, using the Hindu idea of Kalpas) or if they're exclusively talking about the cycles of death and rebirth for individuals.

    Aside from the question of universal cycles, since the OP was asking about anything before the fall of Rome, M20 does include an outline of history on p. 121-123 which goes back a lot further, naming various ages prior to the "High Mythic Ages" after the fall of classical civilizations. And depending on what you want to take as canon, a bunch of the revised Tradition books have information about their history stretching back to before the fall of Rome.

        Note that when Exalted first came out, a lot of effort was made to present it as the ancient prehistory of the World of Darkness, and not just in Exalted; the references JesseM cites are among the examples going the other way around.             In addition to the Exalted connection, I think some of the stuff about the Great Cycle in the Revised era may have been because they wanted to highlight the idea of some kind of impending apocalypse--I don't know exactly when they made the decision to give the Mage universe a definite wrap-up with the Ascension scenario, but even before that they may have wanted to have more of a sense of the world being on its last legs (p. 263 the Revised core book talks about this aspect of the setting, saying 'Millennial fever has struck the World of Darkness, hard, and it’s tough to ignore the portents that the End Times are near'). This seems especially likely in the section I mentioned on p. 76-77 of the revised Euthanatos book, which talks about the Hindu belief that we're in the Kali Yuga (the last age before the death and rebirth of the universe) and then goes on to say "If the Kali Yuga follows the natural rhythm of the Cycle, it will conclude at the end of the 4300th Century. ... Unfortunately, the Wheel turners suspect that the Age of Kali has fallen from its predetermined course. Nuclear weapons, pollution and turmoil in the Underworld all point to a premature apocalypse — the destruction of the Wheel rather than its natural rotation."

            Also, the section on p. 12 of Lost Paths where they introduce the Entelechy said it was made up of prehistoric mages whose role in the last Great Cycle was "to guide the dominant species to its destiny", which sounds like a kind of foreshadowing of Psychopomps and the Telos sphere in Ascension (or may have at least been part of the inspiration for a later decision to make the Entelechy play a big role in the story of their re-emergence).

                “The end is near” was built into the World of Darkness right from the beginning back in 1991 when Vampire: the Masquerade was first published. The first edition that didn't have an apocalyptic scenario in its immediate future was the 20th Anniversary Editions; and even they tend toward a “teetering on the brink” attitude — that said, “the world could end if we screw this up” is a damn sight better than “the world will end, and soon” of the earlier editions.

                    Sure, in Vampire and Werewolf the "end is near" theme was always very prominent, but the different game lines weren't that closely integrated and I think this was less of a theme in Mage before Revised.
