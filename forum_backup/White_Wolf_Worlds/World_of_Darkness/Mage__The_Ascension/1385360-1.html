            Consider the possibility that chemical and nuclear transformations might both require Conjunctional use of Energy and Matter, because both are nearly always either endothermic or exothermic, with the former turning energy into matter while the latter turns matter into energy.
            Yeah well, in a sense that much is true in a physical sense, but then we would get in a problem about how to divide the game mechanics in sensible "packages". Because, if we go that way, then "Biology" is also just Chemistry ("biochemistry", but that's just a specialization of chemistry) which then it's just actually how energy behaves; in the end, "Matter" is actually just "solid energy". Atoms are made of Protons, that require particles like Quarks and gluons and neutrinos and whatnot, and many of those actually have ZERO mass, because what gives mass is a very specific particle called the "Higgs Boson", thus many of those subatomic particles are actually PURE ENERGY, then if we go into String Theory, than everything gets even worse, because even deeper than those known particles, everything in the Universe would be literally just "vibrating strings", pure energy, so there really is no matter at all, just energy in fact, vibrating in different frequencies thus creating different stuff...

            Thus we could just say that everything in the "Frontline" "Material Plane" is controlled by a single "Uber Sphere" (the "Material Sphere"?)

            Now, this actually ties up well to something that I already wanted to mention coincidentally. It's the phenomenon called "Emergent Patterns". And that's the reason why the study of Physics is separated from Chemistry and those 2 from Biology, and that's also why we don't try to understand Psychology in terms of Quantum Physics.

            In a very succinct manner, Emergent Patterns means that "the whole is bigger than the sum of its parts".

            Example: let's go full Jack the Ripper and "disembowel" a person with medical precision.

            Let's remove the brain, the spinal cord, the heart, liver, lungs, stomach, intestine, kidneys, muscles, bones and every other organ of a person - even preserving all blood and all fluids of him.

            Now, if I dump it all into a chest, will a human being come out of it?

            Of course not. Because a human being is more than just a sum of a bunch of organs.

            Likewise, if I disassemble all cells from somebody's eyes and then dump them into a petri dish, will I have an eye back?

            Obviously not.

            In the same manner, atoms are more than just a collection of protons and neutrons and electrons, and molecules are more than just a collection of atoms. If I take 4 atoms of Hidrogen I don't have a Helium, I'll have instead 2 molecules of Hidrogen gas.

            In Emergent Patterns it matters quite a lot how the individual constituents of a pattern are arranged, rather than just what those are.

            If I take 6 atoms of Carbon and Oxygen and 12 of Hidrogen I will NOT have a molecule of glucose, unless they are organized in the proper structure that Glucose has.

            There's a phenomenon in chemistry called "Isomery" that is exactly about that - molecules with the same kinds and numbers of atoms but different structures, and thus they behave entirely differently.

            This phenomenon holds true for basically anything imaginable in the Universe. Our conscience is more than just the sum of our neurons, but rather an Emergent Pattern derived from their configuration; human societies and cultures are more than the sum of its individuals, but rather the result of how they interact with each other. Galaxies are more than just the sum of their individual stars, but rather how they interact with each other in their very specific distribution.

            This also means that the entire universe is absolutely interconnected like an infinite cosmic web (like I said, I'm an adherent of Superdeterminism). The tiniest particle influences and is at the same time influenced by the entire Universe (the gravity of the farthest galaxy from us still influence us, and our own gravity also influences the farthest away galaxy).

            Therefore yes, chemical reactions are indeed exchanges of energy, but that's what the study of chemistry entails already. Therefore I think that the Sphere of Matter should deal with that exact kind of energy exchange (as opposed for example from the energy of photons or kinetic or thermal energy which is energy from the movement of molecules). Therefore, I don't think it's necessary to add Energy into it. Moreover, there wouldn't really be anything that Matter would be capable to do by its own if that was the case.

                                    If I may:

                                    In terms of “pedantic and boring groundwork”, Einstein's General Theory of Relativity is a theory of gravity, that expresses it in terms of a curved spacetime geometry. Surprisingly, most of that curvature in practice is in the dimension of time: it turns out that when there's a differential in how fast time flows, trajectories curve toward the region where time is flowing faster. This would argue for the possibility of having Time cover gravity as well. Note that it doesn't take much: Earth's surface gravity is caused by a differential in the flow of time so small that it takes careful examination of highly precise atomic clocks to measure it, making it a significant factor in our GPS network which involves placing highly precise atomic clocks in orbit, where the flow of time hasn't been altered by gravity nearly as much as it has been on the planet's surface.

                                    Space would also have some limited influence here, as large differentials in motion tend to generate a “frame dragging effect” that similarly alters trajectories to equalize the disparate motions. This is most notably seen in the case of a rapidly spinning black hole (as in, rotation near the speed of light), where trajectories approaching its surface find themselves forced to circle around the black hole; and in some cases, this space curvature is so severe that the “direction of time” tilts sideways into what would normally be a direction of space, and the timeline of whatever follows that trajectory ends up doing an “oroboros”, circling around the spinning black hole until the path's future encounters its past and the snake eats its tail.

                                    The point is that the Enlightened Science notion of Time and Space isn't the classic view of everything matching forward through Time at a single, steady pace, with Space being a featureless background. Motion is the interplay between Space and Time, and gravity is what happens when that interplay gets distorted. This is why I think that motion should be governed by Space, and gravity should perhaps be governed by Time.

                                    Where energy and momentum come in is that they're what traditionally cause the spacetime distortions. I agree now that motion and gravity shouldn't be removed from the domain of Energy; but they should also be covered by Space and perhaps Time as well.
