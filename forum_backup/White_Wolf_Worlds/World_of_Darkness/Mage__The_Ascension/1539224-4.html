            You don't need a new faction to represent this; you just need the existing Dissidents in the Sons of Ether. The extreme versions of this would likely succumb to Quiet, and possibly go so far as to become Marauders.

            As for the original contradiction that you pointed out: my own take on that is to lean into Earthly Foundations, which were originally presented as “those boundaries between Coincidental and Vulgar that the Consensus finds nearly impossible to change” and later got recontextualized as “the default nature of Reality, and what it reverts to whenever the influence of the Consensus is removed”. To an Enlightened Scientist, Effects that are consistent with Earthly Foundations are in a sense “more true” than Effects that aren't; and as a result, they try to build on those Foundations, preferring to craft Effects that are Coincidental no matter what the Consensus says because they're in accord with the Earthly Foundations. When M20 talks about Technocrats having the Coincidental Edge, that's what they're talking about; and when Technocrats talk about discovering objective truths, that's also what they're talking about: an “objective truth” is something consistent with the Earthly Foundations and therefore always Coincidental.

            Likewise, something is “objectively false” when it's always Vulgar no matter what the Consensus believes; and Enlightened Scientists try to avoid those kinds of Effects.

            Where things get messy if that most Effects are neither: whether they're Vulgar or Coincidental depends entirely on the local Consensus. But even here, Enlightened Scientists have a working theory that not all possible Reality Zones are created equal: even when a particular Effect can be rendered Coincidental or Vulgar by the Consensus, it may be easier to get the Consensus to accept some Effects than others; and part of what makes Enlightened Scientists “scientists” is that they seek to discover what Effects the Consensus is prone to accept and which ones the Consensus is prone to reject; and then they try to develop theories to predict whether or not a given Effect is more likely to be accepted by the Consensus. Technocratic Reality Zones are the result of centuries of this admittedly weird application of the Scientific Method to mold the Consensus into a form that's as fully compatible with the Earthly Foundations as possible and as accepting of as wide a range of Effects as possible at the same time. Essentially, these tendencies of what the Consensus will accept or reject are put on a spectrum between the extremes defined by the Earthly Foundations, with things that the Consensus easily accepts being “objectively mostly true” and thought of as being “near” the ideal of full compatibility with an Earthly Foundation, and vice versa with things that it's nearly impossible to get the Consensus to accept.

            (This is actually something that belongs in my Technocratic Fields thread; but I'm bringing it up here to give you a sense of how I attempt to resolve the “Technocrats are doing Science in a world where Reality is flexible” dilemma.)

            For the purpose of this thread, I'm thinking that there's should probably be a faction of Enlightened Scientists in the same sense as the Technocrats are, but who reject the Technocracy idea that technical experts should decide how everyone lives their lives. Setting aside the more mystical takes on the VAs (who I'll refer to as the Mercurial Elite from now on, to distinguish them from their more Scientifically grounded brothers), the VAs are actually a solid example of this: they strive to keep their Science grounded in mathematical “Earthly Foundations” as per my rant above; but they're anti-authoritarian, about as far from “Technocrat” as you get. To the extent that the surviving Craftmasons are still Scientists, they may also fall into this group: their American Experiment was an attempt to create a governmental system that's run bottom-up rather than top-down, making it largely anti-Technocratic. (Or maybe I'm misunderstanding the Masons?)

            This “anti-Technocracy” would be the go-to for those who want to play Enlightened Scientists but don't want to play Technocrats. In that regard, they're very similar to M20's Navalon, but without the Camelot imagery or the British roots.

            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
