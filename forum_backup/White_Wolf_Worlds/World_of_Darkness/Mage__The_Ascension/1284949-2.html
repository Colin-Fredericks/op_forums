    Your comment on teleportation reminds me of some fun with altered internal dimensions once. The characters were entering a house they knew was spacially screwed up, so one of them had the bright idea to bring a rope and tie it to the handle of the front door in case they got lost. The thing was, I had already decided that I was going to roll to find out which room they ended up in every single time they passed through a door. The rope didn't make a lot of sense in that scenario, but instead of telling them that and coming up with some kind of BS reason why they'd know it didn't work I decided to toy with them.

    After a little while they entered a room they'd never been in and saw a rope stretched between two doors. Concerned they started following their rope back and sure enough it took them back through a different path than the way they had come (I don't remember if it took them to rooms they hadn't been in, but it could have). It was also far, far longer than it was when they tied it to the door. As they progressed they kept seeing the rope in increasingly odd paths through the rooms, and by the end they were having to crawl through a virtual spider web created by their own nigh infinitely long rope.

    This wasn't a Mage game, but it is the kind of thing I would consider pulling in the right circumstances in a Mage game. Specifically in a permissive but volatile consensus.

            On reflection I've decided that the setting would benefit from a significant tweak to the EXP system in Mage. Split EXP into Mundane and Arcane Experience.

            In the normal system I'd award 4 points of EXP after a session to all PCs (I don't like the systems in WoD where you give different EXP to different PCs), which they can spend on anything they like. The worry is that they'd tend to invest in Spheres, Arete and the "cool" backgrounds while neglecting their Attributes and Abilities.

            In this system I'd award 5 or 6 EXP after a session, split between Mundane (used to upgrade Attributes, Abilities, Willpower, purchase most Merits) and Arcane (used to upgrade Spheres, Arete, mystical backgrounds like Demesne, Resonance/Synergy, purchase supernatural merits...).

            This allows me to award more EXP without the characters growing as Mages too quickly. It also sticks to a core theme of Mage; magick is who you are, not just what spells you can do. A Mage's powers are supported and informed by their Attributes and Abilities.

            It also means PCs don't feel hampered when they invest EXP in stuff like Cooking and Theology. They have Mundane EXP that they need to use on something like that after all.

            Furthermore it allows me more fine-tuning control. I can decide that the PCs are too advanced in Spheres for their age and start to limit Arcane EXP to 1 point per session until some in-game time has passed. This way they continue to improve without being on a constant track to being archmages.

            e.g Finn gets 6 EXP after a session. 4 is Mundane and 2 is Arcane. They spend the 4 EXP raising two of their Abilities (Enigmas and Esoterica, describing how they spend time studying their notes from lessons on puzzle solving and magickal theory) and they save the remaining 2, since they don't have enough EXP to raise their Arete yet. Alternatively, they could spend that 2 EXP upgrading their Familiar Background rating. They describe cultivating new flowers rich in various types of nectar, to feed the hive and help it grow.

                            Originally posted by Muad'Dib View Post
                            So characters at the school are less likely to accumulate Paradox Points due to using Magick ? Is this comparable or similar to how Sanctums of Mages work in regard to this ?
                            So the school does have Sanctums of varying power. Naturally for a Sanctum to maintain its power it has to be at least somewhat private, so the common learning areas aren't Sanctums. The private Dojo of the Akashyana gym teacher where he inducts students into studying Do, however, is.

                            The reason students tend to escape Paradox in the setting is that there are zero Witnesses in the entire academy grounds, and the local consensus is very permissive. As long as what you're doing makes sense within your own paradigm, you'll remain Coincidental essentially. You're wandering around a place where the response to pulling out a fireball is "that's dangerous" not "that's impossible!". However if a person is just making up random bullshit (I, a naturalistic Verbena, am going to give this guy rabies through a voicemail message) then it will tend towards vulgarity.

                            And of course, effects that are always Vulgar will still be Vulgar here.

                            But the setting reduces the odds of accumulating Paradox quite considerably, is the take-home message.

                            The after-effects of Resonance and Synergy however are another story. And if you are messing up local reality (even coincidentally) the "Groundskeeper" may want to have a stern word with you.

                                A problem occurred to me; all the PCs are Arete 1 and may be stuck there for a while. Level 1 spheres mixed with Abilities are great for problem solving, BUT people come along to a magic setting expecting to be able to command magic.

                                So I've decided to add a new feature to the school.

                                High in the castle's graceful towers, among the spires of House Corona, there is a strange little storeroom holding a reclusive Mage. Samuel Hurt, master wandmaker. When first year students visit him he pairs them with an object that will act both as a channel for their powers (a Unique instrument) and as their first way of using advanced Magick.

                                A Hermetic might leave with a wand that allows them to control one of the elemental forces, for example, with a Forces 2 effect bound into the Wand.

                                All of the Wonders he makes are Hermetic naturally, and he's one of the most well-renowned creators of these wands and staves. If a student enters who is difficult to match with anything in his stock, he'll disappear through a door leading to a corridor (Corridor? We're at the top of a tower...), only to return seconds or hours later holding what the student needs.

                                For example, for my Entropic Computer Expert he'll return with a sleek metal remote with a Data 2 effect that allows the bearer to forge a connection with something for the purposes of using their Abilities or Spheres. For example it could connect her to a computer to interact with it with Computer/Technology, or to cause havoc with Entropy 2 when she develops it.

                                The idea is that the kids get a hold of something special to them early on that lets them do proper magickal stuff. Furthermore as they grow they will leave these objects behind, or find new and cool ways of using them (that flame control effect is much scarier when the wielder has Arete 5 after all).

                                p.s. "Samuel Hurt" is a reference to John Hurt, who portrayed Olivander. It is in no way a reference to the infamous WoD Gary Stu by a similar name.

                                                            I've decided to add a new (very minor) faction to the Mage setting, loosely based off of a vivacious faction of do-gooders.

                                                            This post will have huge show/book-ruining spoilers for the Series of Unfortunate Events lore, so don't read this until after you've binge-watched the show I suppose?

                                                            This highly secretive organization (who refer to themselves as Librarians) spans the Traditions, Technocracy and the Crafts. It is made up of individuals who are devoted to understanding history, literature, philosophy and so on. Valued skills include Research, Library Science, Esoterica and Occult, Science, Law, Medicine, Academics, Subterfuge, Empathy, Meditation...

                                                            The mission statement of the organization is to form a cautious coalition between the wider sects through back channels. It has its origins within the Virtual Adepts; a small number of them valued freedom of information above all and only saw a peaceful future for humanity in a scenario where the Technocracy and Traditions were both reformed. As these Adepts were new members of the Traditions and old members of the Technocracy, this meant that a delicate operation of connection could begin. Thankfully some premeditation on the part of these proto-librarians meant that some of them had remained loyal to the Technocracy. The vague end-game of the Librarians is a world where mysticism and technology exist in some sort of harmony, with wisdom from all practices being available to future generations of Mages.

                                                            The Librarians have been successful in slowly growing their ranks, developing sophisticated code behaviours and phrases, avoiding detection, and recommending some really amazing books. They haven't been successful in ending the Ascension War. However, if a setting includes a movement pushing for a "Kinder, Gentler Technocracy" then this group is partially responsible.

                                                            Although it wasn't their intention, the Librarians have fostered an environment within their society where they are perfectly placed to develop their Arete and become potent Mages. The Librarians treasure books and therefore have access to a large body of Grimoires of all kinds. Librarians tend to be recruited from positions of lore-keeping, custodianship and so on, so the network is a powerful hub of excellence.

                                                            A member of this faction has met one of the PCs, and is very very slowly drip-feeding them the ideals of the Librarians. She has provided him with a book and tasked him with finding the young Mage who will really benefit from reading it (another PC... a very simple puzzle since the book is full of fairytales on plants, and one of the PCs is a mystic focused on herbalism).
