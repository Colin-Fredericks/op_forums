    Of course I understand the powergaming issues. My assertion was that putting a lid on said powergaming was the only real reason the extra rules exist. That is, the only reason besides the in-universe hand waves, which were not terribly convincing.

    And I understand that powergaming is not terribly good for a game. But that's why Storytellers need to communicate with their players on the matter...as well as play out the Mage character's addiction in a serious manner. As I've stated, such a PC is a junkie. Even if they don't have a regnant, they are still slaves to the thirst. Storytellers need to hammer this home to the player. And the players need to recognize the double-edged sword they are running with. If either of these are impossible, then the Storyteller needs to avoid giving the player the opportunity to find vampires and easily drink from them.

    Plus, I already presented an alternative rule for Ghoul Mages: they cannot gain more Arete so long as they are Ghouls. They are so addicted to the Red Stuff that they lose sight of Ascension, and become so full of Stasis that they can't advance. This is at once much easier to arbitrate - you either can go on Seekings or you can't - and forces the player to choose whether they want to play a Mage or a Ghoul. Because if you have to rely on Disciplines for power, you can't advance in Magick, and vice versa. And Disciplines are much less flexible, especially at the low levels that Ghouls can use.

    If the Storyteller still thinks the Discipline thing is too easy, they can mandate that the Mage needs to spend blood points for every use, even if the Discipline doesn't require it. Then they need to go out hunting for vampires...or get a vampire sugar daddy. These create story possibilities, and present the character with a series of hard choices.

                (Widderslainte bearing an Avatar of one of the original Tremeres would be quite the snowflake to play as good-aligned Technocrat!
                As a Syndic, the goal would be to earn $$$ of the vampires fad and turn it into Quintessence with Primal Utility to, for a start, produce synthetic-but-tasty blood, and market it properly to the potential customers.)

                Similar case could be with Nagaraja. Some could be protecting and nurturing an Avatar in hope that one day at least their hunger for flesh might be lifted, somehow.
                In DA Mage, it specifically states that the "quarrelsome invisible friend" Avatars of Renaissance and later mages were integrated and not really separate beings. So, either they were separate beings and just weren't being chatty assholes who can decide when and how "their" humans use the Avatar's magic juice or the whole pre-modern phenomenon of having a separate Avatar just wasn't a thing AT ALL back when Tremere and his buddies were doing their immortality thing.

                In either case, it seems like a major roadblock to that story as far as the in-setting material goes. You can, of course, completely ignore it. I know I have. I ran a game once where the implicit conceit was "Avatars = souls" and one of the characters was the reincarnation of Tytalus. It led to a very interesting scene when the mages showed up in Vienna that involved a conversation with his "brother in Art", aka Tremere-by-proxy (or, more appropriately, some of the attention of Tremere was briefly directed his way).

                But under the "orthodoxy" it would be very convoluted for Etrius, et al. to chase down an Avatar through the ages something they never were aware that they had or needed, and, upon losing, didn't really notice overmuch (sure THAT kind of magic wasn't working anymore for the newly-vamped, but they put all their accumen and knowledge to work and were able to create THIS kind of magic over here pretty damn fast.). Why wouldn't the leading Tremere consider this whole hysterical Avatar thing to represent a weakness of Will by modern mages? They have to invent some kind of imaginary playmate to handle the cognitive dissonance that being a mage creates in their mind.

                                        Er... 'working on a very complicated and difficult problem and after working through it coming to an epiphany' is very likely to be... just the form that Technocratic Seekings take.

That's not just Technocrats and not all Technocrats - Traditions Mages sometimes have Circumspect Avatars, and Demented Eidolon and Inner Knight are a Flaw and a Merit for a reason. Sometimes your muse is another person who just shows up from time to time, and sometimes it's your hat that you have to show off every time you cast a spell.

                                        Hell, it might just be that the only reason all these things are thought of as the same is because basically all of them agree there's something that lets them do all this crazy shit and decided to agree to the polite fiction that they have enough idea of what it is to stick a label on it.
