      As a general rule I assume you'd need to understand a culture's categories to answer that question. A West African "Sphere" system would likely involve either four or sixteen because those numbers symbolize totality and completion. Thus the importance of the Goddess Oshun being symbolized by five and seventeen. Oshun is the unknowns that secretly power and complete the world.

      Five tends to be an important number in East Asian countries. Eight is vastly important in Taoist lands especially China and Korea.

      So, find the important numbers, then find out how they categorize.

                              Also, one of the great pitfalls of Mage is blurring the line between the spheres as a a game mechanic and the spheres as an in universe system. The mechanics are a gaming abstraction. They don't say anything about the metaphysics of Mage or the characters in game unless you want them to. Every character can be played using the mechanical sphere system, even those which would, in universe, never consider describing their practices using the Traditions sphere descriptions.

                              Of course you can choose to have paradigm and practice affect the game mechanics if you want to, but it isn't mandatory. Let the decision be based on the needs of your game and players.

                                              I mean yes, the Spheres were very obviously intended as mere abstractions. And the game works better overrall when players and Storytellers treat it that way.

                                              But it's untrue to say the Spheres have no significance in-universe. They very much do, if for no reason than because a sufficient number of Mages have been using the Spheres as a theoretical framework for centuries. The Umbra reflects human belief, and how they conceptualize the world. In the Umbra, the planets in our solar system are tied directly to the Spheres. Pluto's spiritual counterpart IS ALSO the Shard Realm of Entropy. Mars's spiritual counterpart IS ALSO the Shard Realm of Forces.

                                              Should it be that way? Probably not, since it's very gamey. But Mage is all about how peoples' shared narratives alter how Reality functions. Even if most Mage factions prefer to order their world in their own esoteric ways, that they use the Spheres as an inter-faction framework for discussing Magick means those concepts have a weight all their own. To the point even the Technocracy grudgingly uses the same Spheres (barring Technocratic variants, like Primal Utility or Dimensional Science, of course).

                                                  To be fair, the reason why the Technocratic variants exist is because using the Spheres as written for the Technocracy doesn't quite work. Almost; but not quite. Thus, you get Dimensional Science's easily penetrated Gauntlet in a place where the Gauntlet would normally be at its thickest (and in the Revised Convention Book, a restriction in focus when dealing with spirits that still lets you attack them, capture them, and drive them away, but has nothing for more benign interactions with them) and Primal Utility's removal of “glowy Effects” (for lack of a better name) in exchange for having an easier time making Wonders and, when first published, the unique capability to tap into Primal Ventures. Even now, Technocrats have a unique capability to make Devices without having to invest personal Quintessence or Willpower into the effort, something that (like Dimensional Science's ability to penetrate the Gauntlet where it really shouldn't be able to) is restricted to specialized Technocratic infrastructure.

                                                  There's precedent for tweaking the Spheres to produce variants that don't quite work the same as the default write-ups, normally in the form of adding capabilities or making existing capabilities easier to access, but always in exchange for removing other capabilities or making other capabilities harder to access. A shift in capabilities rather than an expansion of capabilities.

                                                  And I personally find it ironic that the only examples we have of this belong to the Technocracy. Why can't we facilitate the use of Necromancy by saying that, e.g., the Euthanatoi have developed a variant of Entropy that makes much more of the things we associate with Necromancy available with just that Sphere variant? I once went through a thought experiment where I considered each of the Traditions with the idea that they might have some unique insights into one or two of the Spheres that let them do things with it that the strict write-ups of those Spheres don't permit. It seemed like a very Traditions-like thing to do.

                                                  With that said, my current view on the subject is to lean into Minor Spheres, like what quelsec briefly suggested earlier. The one catch is that the closest thing we have to an official endorsement of this idea is the Revised era's Storyteller's Handbook; and that was explicitly a book about hacking the rules and setting. There's a wonderful STV product (Book of Fragments) that presents a robust system for Minor Spheres which I heartily endorse; but it is a STV product, and not something officially endorsed.

                                                  Sphere Variants and Minor Spheres have the advantage that you can devise one of them at a time, in a vacuum; and even then, it requires significant effort. What Bernese asked for here is a whole different organizing principle for the Spheres. That's a much bigger ask. Frankly, the strongest argument I've seen for sticking to the Nine Spheres as written is that the work in writing them up has already been done. From a strictly practical viewpoint: for all their faults, they're something that we already have.

                                                  My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
