                                        I wouldn't bother with a Sphere tax; as I said in my last post, those don't do anything if you already have the Sphere in question.

                                        I do agree that Psionics is a broken Practice if you approach it as “concentrate and things happen”. That's not a Practice; that's Working Without Focus, and costs a point of Willpower and +3 difficulty. Bear with me a moment:

                                        I've mentioned before that I like to generalize the “Takes Extra Time” Magickal Modifier to serve as a “uses onerous instrument” modifier: it may be onerous because it takes longer to use, or because it's not particularly mobile (cauldrons vs. broomsticks, for example), or for other reasons. But whatever the reason happens to be, if the instrument that you're using is particularly inconvenient to use, it's worth -1 to -3 difficulty on the Arete roll. The motivation for this is primarily a gamist one: why would a player use an onerous Instrument if there's no benefit for doing so? And the answer is that there is a benefit to doing so, though it's an indirect one: some Instruments are more effective than others, which is represented by them granting a negative Magickal Modifier; but these more effective Instruments also tend to come with drawbacks. Thus, they're “more onerous” to use them typical Instruments. If this wasn't so, then these more effective Instruments would be used by everyone, for everything. Mages aren't idiots.

                                        But conversely, I tend to adapt the “Working Without Focus” mechanic to the opposite effect: if the instrument that you're using is exceptionally convenient, to the point that it's almost as if you're not using an Instrument at all, then by a similar line of reasoning, they should come with a “difficulty tax” of up to +3 difficulty; and for the most extreme ones, possibly even a “Willpower tax”.

                                        So you want your psychic to just go around wielding Forces through simple concentration? That's +3 Difficulty and 1 WP, just like if you're trying to Work Without Focus — because you basically are. Or, you could put in the work to devise a set of Instruments for your Psionics that aren't “just concentrate, and it happens” — say, a reliance on crystals or dowsing rods — and you get to bypass those prices, or at least mitigate them.

                                                            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                                            I'm of two minds on this: on the one hand, I sometimes wonder if Mage wouldn't be better off using a fundamentally different game engine that's even more lightweight and freeform than the Storyteller System: something like Chad Underkoffler's Prose Descriptive Qualities (PDQ) system (e.g., Truth & Justice) or Fred Hicks' Fate system. Basically, take the open nature of the Spheres and apply it to everything.

                                                            On the other hand, I'm not sure that the problem with Mage is that it's overly complicated so much as it's under-defined. There's a lot of stuff, especially in the Magick system, that relies on the Storyteller making everything up on his own, as opposed to making that an option. It could also do with better organization; but let me focus on the “more definition” for now.
                                                            It definitely appears underdefined in some areas, in the sense that there are implicit assumptions that are used freely without ever saying why. There seems to be an unstated rule that Life is inherently harder to target than Matter, for instance, to the point that targeting people requires adding Life while targeting objects does not require adding Matter. However, I don't think that's an indication that the magic system needs more definition. I largely think the hidden assumptions are in the way and most of the 'problems' they address are better handled with other aspects of the system like success requirements, skill checks, and difficulty modifiers.

                                                            I have considered porting some WoD games over to Fate in the past, but I haven't considered it for Mage. Fate works best when you can make the system say something about the nature of the world, so I'd need to see what tweaks promote a world of Reality on the Brink.
