            I think where a lot of people have problems with a fully malleable reality is that they don't see guard rails stopping the setting from sliding into utter unrecognizability. On the other hand, some see a tendency for the consensus to reinforce the status quo. For the former, they need to add those guard rails. For the latter, the setting will remain as we expect it barring a major push.

            Notably, because this is a shared setting for use at the gaming table, neither side is wrong. The gaming world usually tends towards the expectations of the people at the table
            .

            On another note, the question between Influencing Reality and Defining Reality is actually two partially dependent questions:

            1) Is the human belief/will the only thing pushing on reality?

            2) Is all of reality malleable or just some of it?

            I think that's an important distinction because both 'defining' and 'influencing' can have multiple meanings.

            For instance, I tend towards the idea that all of reality is up for grabs, but humanity isn't the only thing doing the grabbing. I'd say reality is defined by belief/will but that's not limited to humans. Another person might say everything is up for grabs but because humanity isn't the sole factor, human belief only influences reality. Different terms, but we mean very similar things. On the other hand, a third person might say much of reality is set and can't be changed at all, so belief/will influence reality. That's the same term as person 2, but with a very different meaning.

            These are also arguments mages have in world, and though they don't have a similar influence on the outcome, the same terminology problems arise. Indeed one of the problems of the Ascension War is that the Technocracy and Traditions use different terms for the same things sometimes and similar terms with different meanings at others.

                    Cosmological Constants, as they were described in MRev, are basically “the metaphysics of the other gamelines aren't particularly susceptible to the Consensus.” The examples given for Cosmological Constants were things like “the Triat enforces Werewolf's cosmology, and something like the Abrahamic God enforces Vampire's metaphysics; so don't bother trying to get rid of vampires and werewolves by convincing the Masses that they don't exist.” This notion is similar to M20's Earthly Foundations, save only for the fact that the latter appears to be more focused on maintaining a consistent mundane reality.

                    MRev also featured Historical Inertia, which M20 belatedly addresses in Victorian Mage, when it takes about what happens when a Territory (that era's term for Reality Zone) gets depopulated: the Territory gradually drifts back toward the Earthly Foundations, but maintains whatever laws of reality the former Consensus imposed on it for a time after the Consensus is removed.

                            Originally posted by mark View Post

                            I am certain Mrevised regarding cosmological constants also said "The earth is round, fire was always hot, 2+3= 5. bacteria were probably not made up by committee, etc) Much of this was in the core rules.
                            I don't specifically remember those all being cosmological constants, but even so, those are very different statements and they address different things.

                            Changing 2+3 to 4 is matter of sense vs nonsense. Either we are redefining the word 'four,' to mean 'five' which is not a reality change, or we are saying something meaningless like "square circle". It's not really a limit on the power of the Consensus to say it can't do that, just like it's not a limit to say it can't flibble wurble bing.

                            Fire being hot is more of a mythic/narrative thing. It's not that we can't think up a world where that doesn't happen, but so much of culture and experience and storytelling is based around fire and the nature of fire. That Cosmological Constant is preserving a form of narrative weight.

                            Bacteria as a Cosmological Constant is establishing the explanation as a CC rather than the phenomenon. That is, there's a good case for disease itself being a CC, but bacteria are a modern, largely imperceptible explanation for disease. They don't need to be entrenched as a foundational principle of reality at all and there are plenty of fantasy worlds where diseases are caused by humors or spirits. Not to mention, as Dataweaver has said on a few occasions, if you can only see something with scientific instruments, it may just be part of the focus rather than part of the Tapestry.

                            Meanwhile, the Round World CC is arbitrary. Not only are flat worlds quite common in fiction (just look at Exalted) but Sorcerers Crusade suggests a nonlinear world where the same path doesn't always lead to the same place, and that's an even stranger possibility.

                                                    My view is to define the Consensus specifically as humanity's influence on the nature of reality, instead of being “any and all sentient entities that influence reality”. Which isn't to say that the Consensus is the only thing that influences reality; rather, I want a term that I can use to describe “what the people in this town believe” as distinct from “what the people in this town, as well as the spirits in this town and whatever other potential influencers that may or may not exist, believe”. For those mages who believe that everything's subjective once you've included all of the influencers, the word for the latter is “Reality”; but it's one of those things where the distinction between “what is innately true” vs. “what is true because that's what the Weaver believes” is moot.
