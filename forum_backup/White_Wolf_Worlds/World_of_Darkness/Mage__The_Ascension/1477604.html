    Dramatic Spells to Conceal or Give Cover to ally's Stealthy Magic

    03-28-2022, 10:19 PM
    (Questions at the bottom)

    I want too get some perspectives on a strategic concept in Mage, having to do with detectability of Magic and simultaneous (same round) or timed casting. To explore this, a hypothetical scenario requires three characters.

    Character 1: Ben is a Progenitor - Genegineer with ------------Life 4, Matter 3, Forces 3

    Character 2: Lisa is a NWO Operative with -----------------------Mind 3, Entropy 3, Time 3

    Character 3: Zylus is a Hermetic from house Flambeau with--Life 3, Forces 3, Mind 3, Entropy 3

    Zylus burned a Technomancer alive and now Ben and Lisa are pissed. They don't just want to see him dead, they want to see the Chantry who still harbor him suffer some of the same pain. They have tracked him to his oceanfront property, where he sits on a lawn chair sipping Southern Comfort. It's a humid, slightly cloudy day and after a bit of hiking, the Technomancer duo have found a position within line of site of their target who is unaware of them. Having surveilled Zylus, read reports on him and studied his sphere-marks, they know that he has two of the three Spheres that each of them has.

    Lisa has a Procedure ready to use on him, using all of her Spheres at their highest rank. It makes use of Time to discharge the effect slowly, over an extended duration, to add to its subtlety, hopefully reducing its detectability. If successful, the effect will be that Zylus will experience a series of social mishaps (Entropy) that unravel his relationships with Chantry mages and push him toward violent conflict with them (Mind). She is relying on Ben to distract Zylus with an attention-grabbing, dramatic effect.

    Ben, using all of his Spheres to full capacity, creates an effect that targets low-lying clouds. It turns existing air into nutrients used by cloud-born microbes to multiply and accelerate the condensation process that leads to rain. The effect is short acting, but intense, hopefully increasing its detectability. On top of that, Ben spends 3 Quintessence on the effect, hopefully giving it even more detectability. The purpose is not only to piss on Zylus' vacation, but to focus his perceptual abilities on the effect so much that Lisa's Techno-Curse goes undetected.

    Ben discharges the cloud effect at an initiative score of 3. Lisa has an initiative score of 4, but intends to withhold her action until Zylus has used his action. Zylus also scores 3 on initiative and his Sphere sensory abilities are active (because if you burn a Technomancer alive...). If Zylus falls for the cloud trick, he will try to use a Spell to counteract it, fearing it is the precursor to a lightning attack. Similarly, he could try to counteract Lisa's curse if he detected it in time. In both cases it isn't counter-magic, but a defensive spell using two of the three Spheres being used against him.


    Questions:

    Would Ben's cloud spell actually improve Lisa's chance to curse Zylus undetected?

    If, instead of waiting for Zylus, Lisa cast at the same time as the other two, would her Curse go undetected?

    Alternatively, if we give Ben Prime 2 and he cast the effect while Zylus was sleeping, fueling it so it becomes a constant, lasting distraction, would that improve the chances of Lisa, arriving after Zylus is awake, to Curse him undetected?

    Thank you for passing time with me in conversation. My Hacks.

      I would think that all the cases would justify a severe increase in the number of successes needed for Conscience to detect it. Prime 1 could be similarly fooled. Probably woudln't survive a dedicated Prime 2 scan, that can discern the Spheres present on a magical working.

      In all cases, the disadvantage exists as long as the "noisy" spell it's active.

      Note that being affected by an obvious magical attack from a source that you coudln't detect ain't conducent to *not* being alert. The stage for Zylus to ignore the second spell should include some sort of constant way to keep diverting his attention. For instance, if he's forced to erect some lasting magical defenses of his own because he can counter the "attack", but can't know the source and it keeps comming, it could keep passive Conscience from noticing something else it's going on.

      Depending on how perceptive (and understandably paranoid) it's Zylus and his comarades, this could be overkill. Not all Tradition Chantries exert the same control over their mages, as not all mages like to be controled. A subtle spell may pass undetected, if the mage it's expected to carry some benefitial spells on his person. Buffs and protections ain't rare, and some may get angry if there's askance about their magical protections...which could be aggravated by the spell itself causing to misinterpret the inquiry. At that point, the ball it's in Zylus own Perception and countermeasures (and how bad can things go before the Chantry starts to get suspicious)

      By being blatant you have to count on the Chantry and Zylus being proactive and trying to pool it's resources to understand what's going on from day 1. An invisible attacker targeting an active member represents danger for the whole Chantry. You have to trust they ain't going to have the power to detect the source of the attack, nor unravel the ruse.

      Other than that, "social mishaps that unravel his relationships with Chantry mages" could be stereotypical Hermetic's second name.

              Being from house Flambeau, I think Zylus may be more likely to see through the ostentatious display of force, because he would have faced such displays many times over from his own peers.

              However, the real answer to the question depends entirely on Lisa. In this case, it seems that Lisa has been trained quite well for the purpose of using, even a subtle distraction, to it's full advantage, by realizing when Zylus is most distracted and timing her attack to land in that interval.

              Let's say that Ben chooses to make the big splashy distraction, if only for an instant. Zylus realizes that this is big splashy forces magic and starts acting to defend himself, but Lisa knows that Zylus will start by defending himself from "the enemy in front of him", the unknown person who is obviously using some sort of matter and forces rote. In other words, while Zylus may have all his senses "up", he's going to be focusing on senses that don't apply to Lisa's procedure. Lisa strikes with her own procedure while Zylus is still busy initiating his defenses against Ben's seeded cloud, or perhaps looking for the person responsible for that magic. Lisa avoids detection because she isn't actually involved with what Zylus is focusing on. Now Zylus will certainly notice that Magic is being used and he might even discover Ben, but Lisa and her procedure are relatively safe, because she timed her attack properly.

              Let's say Ben just seeds the clouds enough to get a gentle rain. Zylus, who is used to big flashy attacks, will likely be more concerned about getting out of the rain and not letting his drink get too watered down. Lisa times her procedure to strike as Zylus is feeling the rain gently striking his skin and he is fussing with his drink, probably covering it with his newspaper. What's one more gentle strike compared to the myriad of raindrops? Even if Zylus is immediately suspicious of the gentle rain, the result wouldn't be different than it was in the first scenario.. Ben may become discovered, but Lisa's training (or maybe the well developed sense of timing comes from some other factor?) allows her to time her attack well and fly under the radar.
