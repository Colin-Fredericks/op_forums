            Arguing from rote precedent should be understood to be shaky - beyond edition rules differences, the writers never agree on which determinism is in play. I also think that the tendency to strip examples down into mechanisms is misleading. We shouldn't understand that the ability to turn into a fire-breathing tiger means you can use Matter to create Wonders, even if the situations can be argued to be analogous. It's frustrating, but Mage's magic system isn't particularly systematic.

            That said, the Time 4 (+Entropy 3 optional) rule is old and not well-loved. If you're getting into the legalistic weeds to try and find ways not to do it, just ask your ST to drop it.

            For your specific cites, Gift of Prana isn't really a contingent effect, it uses Prime's ability to enchant living beings to cause the subject's body to be a temporary healing Wonder. Encrypt just looks to be a basic error - the presence of Time 3 is nonsensical, as that level has no bearing on the effect. It's either supposed to be Time 4 or Time 0, by Revised rule (barring one extremely obscure alternative.)

            (Locking is to anchor an effect from a Sphere onto a Pattern governed by a different Sphere, so I don't think you could or would Life-lock a Life effect.)

                    The Locking section in M20 says that same-Pattern effects will often follow their original targets without locking, and only proposes locking as a solution for cross-Sphere working. Do we think it should take Life 2 to maintain a one-dot Life Sense effect without penalties?

                    In M20, the following do not apply the juggling penalty:

                    "an Effect that has a Time-based trigger,
                    one which has been locked into another Pattern, or one that
                    has been cast but whose duration has not yet expired, does not
                    count toward that total. If Lee Ann enchants a guy, and if –
                    thanks to the number of successes rolled – that enchantment
                    lasts for a week after they part company, then Lee Ann does
                    not have to concentrate on the Effect in order to keep it going.
                    If she wishes to keep the Effect in motion, however, then it
                    counts against the number of Effects that character can employ
                    at the same time."

                    Which appears to mean that it's actually not just any effect with a Duration - it's one that's being kept in some more active state than merely "going", though the text doesn't clarify what "keep in motion" means in this instance. It may mean that we're supposed to differentiate between "a spell which makes someone happy for a week" and "a spell which lets me alter someone's mood on command for a week."

                                    When I said I don't think generalizing from rote precedent was fruitful, it wasn't a request for more rote precedent.

                                    Prime enchantment of living beings has unspecified benefits beyond the agg damage standard (consider Rubbing the Bones or whatever), which Prana seems to rely on - fittingly for our discussion, I don't think you could generalize from Prana into a non-Life effect. That's the sort of nuance that I feel gets discarded when you imagine that a specific case can be reduced to a series of necessary mechanisms which can be separated from it and applied elsewhere.

                                    Let me cut to the chase here:

                                    HDYDT suggests you can use Life, Forces, and Prime to turn into a fire-breathing tiger. Turning into a tiger is Life 5 (dissimilar mass and size), and creating fire is Forces 3/Prime 2.

                                    You seem to be concluding from this example that one could, for example, turn a stick into a wand of lightning bolts using Matter 2 (turn a stick into a stick) and Forces 3 / Prime 2.

                                    I am of the opinion that you probably are not intended to be able to move from the specific to the general in this fashion.

                                    Effects are not "static" but they generally seem to be "singular", in the sense that the effect has the traits needed to be the effect specified. A ward's behavior "changes" because the desired effect is necessarily selective, for instance. A flight spell lets you change direction and so on because, well, that's the common-sense expectation of flight. Ascension's magic tends to use degree of success, rather than a mechanical complex of Practices (see Awakening), to differentiate between "complex" and "basic" outcomes.

                                    Now, you can argue that "I want to create fire on command by pointing my finger" is a singular effect, and if that's cool with your group go for it. In most cases, it seems as though Ascension operates on the notion that you'd just cast the effect each time you wanted to use it - you necessarily have the capacity to, and unlike Awakening there's no inherent mana cost to use magick.

                                    The intended function of Time/Entropy contingency appears to be based on the idea that, barring effects for which it is obviously necessary, contingent or delayed outcomes require other Spheres, as the delayed (or contingent) release is an embellishment on the singular effect and not an intrinsic trait of the spell.

                                    No amount of "well this effect seems to allow it without Time" is, to me, useful evidence here, because I think the premise underlying that type of argument is faulty.

                                            The horrible truth is that all I can offer are, effectively, my table rules - a lot of this stuff is negotiated at the table, ranging from the vulgarity of effects to how complicated an effect is, or even how many Sphere dots it requires. I'll try to be supportive, but allow me to reiterate: This isn't stuff we have hard rules on, and I don't think we ever will. I can only offer interpretations.

                                            First example: Projecting emotions is Mind 2. No need to lock if the area is intended to be stationary. Probably would be an "ongoing" effect, causing the juggling penalty, if the influence remains in place and would affect people entering the area after the mage has left - that's based on my read of the section I quoted earlier in the thread.

                                            Second example: By HDYDT's overlong section on wards, this would be Corr 4 (the ward), Matter 2 (locked to a passageway), Life or Mind ~3 (sleep effect), and probably Forces to detect the password. With Mind 4, you could have the ward target selected types of mind - this would mean that people who know the password would not be affected, and would not have to say it aloud, which would allow you to drop the Forces requirement. Obviously 10+ dots of Spheres is a lot to achieve this relatively unambitious effect, but near as I can tell that's how it runs by the book in M20. I'd do it differently at my table.

                                            The alternative is to say "well it's like a ward, but it's not a ward-ward" which is going to require your ST to agree that "a spell to protect a region that has an effect on people who trespass" is meaningfully distinct from "a ward." A nonselective sleep spell locked to a doorway is like Mind or Life 3/Matter 2.

                                            Third example: Uncontrolled cosmetic alteration of another person? Life 3, probably. You don't have to lock Life to Life, as I've said above.

                                            "Cause his hair to change color the next time he takes a shower" seems to be Life 3/Time 4/Entropy 3 in M20.

                                            Fourth example: Locking allows an effect to travel along with a nonstandard Pattern. Paralysis is some sort of Life or Mind or etc, probably in the 3-4 dot range.

                                            Locking it to the flashlight with Matter would presumably make it affect anyone touching the flashlight, or have the effect radiate in all directions from the flashlight. Locking it to the flashlight's beam with Forces could cause it to work on whoever it was directed at, but turning the flashlight off would, in my estimation, end the effect - the Pattern carrying the spell would be gone.

                                            Gift of Prana leverages some of the nonmechanical "benefits" of Prime enchantment of living bodies, so you might build effects which are analogous - but because the effect of Prime on living beings is mostly, like, a rush of energy or improved well-being, most of what you'd want to do could be done with Life directly, or maybe some very minor mood-based Mind stuff. There are old effects which suggest you can use Prime's effect on living bodies to cause bliss, improve health, correct imbalance, or inflict pain. As an example, I'd allow this:
                                            The Corrective Lotus Fist (Life 3/Prime 3): By striking key acupoints corresponding to the water system within a subject's body, Akashic masters enchants them to protect against toxic effects. The enchantment costs 1 quint (as standard for enchant life) and uses Life to attempt to countermand drugs or poisons the subject is exposed to. Though this has practical applications, it is most often used on disciples - wayward apprentices that sneak out of the temple to drink find themselves sobering up at impossible speed. The more exacting Akashic masters opt not to provide protection against the rapid-onset hangover which results.

                                                    My rule of thumb here is:

                                                    1.) "Is the effect always on, even if its effects are not currently relevant."

                                                    Consider a Mind spell which makes it impossible to talk about Dave. It's not contingent or delayed, even if it's necessarily selective. Or a Life-locked Forces effect which softens someone's falls, which just isn't doing something while they aren't falling.

                                                    2.) "Is the supposed trigger an inherent quality of the effect, or a convenience to delay the effect until desired?"

                                                    Consider the difference between a Life-locked Forces effect which amplifies the subject's shouts into a deafening sonic blast, and a Time-delayed Entropy-triggered Corr spell which teleports the mage home when they shout. This is another example of what I mean about not being able to project forward "precedent" from one case to another. Identical activation cases may not require identical Sphere use.

                                                    I think your notion of using an conjunctional Sphere to "detect" a trigger is reaching - I don't you've done anything to support it, and the very fact that it does almostcompletely invalidate Time triggers would suggest it to be suspect.

                                                    What is your take on pattern bleeding and permadox in those effects, like the Life ones I gave?
